Ordered octahedral pages for a tree of actual crossing circles

1 November 2026. Private topological derivation.

The statement below concerns the original augmented link exterior, before any auxiliary component is filled. It does not infer a topological identification from the factorization of a state sum or of a volume. The ingredients are the literal four-endpoint operations, the closed bowtie projection cut, and the original disk-face identifications.

1. The actual ordered object and the statement

At each vertex v of a finite tree put one original two-strand crossing circle coupon P_v. Its retained crossing is R^{epsilon_v}, with epsilon_v in {-1,0,1}; its auxiliary component bounds the original twice-punctured disk. Every edge is an actual quarter-turn between two ordered product junctions. There are no product junctions without their own original circle. Terminal circles are included as vertices of degree one. The tree has at least one edge.

The product order, the four endpoint frames, every rotation of a child ball, and every original disk orientation are part of the data. A representative recursive description has Wv=Pv∏w child of vT(Ww). W_v=P_v\prod_{w\text{ child of }v}T(W_w). \tag{1} The product here is in its specified order. At the closing sphere one may use the actual collar move to expose a root circle. A double turn then leaves its actual half-turn decoration on the affected subtree; it is not replaced by the scalar identity T^2=1. The relevant moves are the literal closed-turn and cyclic collar isotopies in notes/actual_conway_net_twist_volume.md, equations (14)–(18). Their four collar sectors, and any reversal of the child order, are retained. Thus (1), with those transported frames, describes the original all-circle ordered operation tree. It does not introduce a commutativity move on open two-tangles.

For clarity, the literal root adapter is C(h0T(E))=C((TP0)T(E))≃C(P0T2(E))=C(P0τ(E)). C\!\left(h_0T(E)\right) =C\!\left((TP_0)T(E)\right) \simeq C\!\left(P_0\,T^2(E)\right) =C\!\left(P_0\,\tau(E)\right). \tag{1a} Here τ\tau is the actual half-turn of the ball. If E=L1⋯LkE=L_1\cdots L_k, then τ(E)=τ(Lk)⋯τ(L1)\tau(E)=\tau(L_k)\cdots\tau(L_1), and τ(TW)=Tτ(W)\tau(TW)=T\tau(W). Thus the adapter reverses the displayed root order and transports every child frame; it does not suppress this rotation. The two-leaf case of (1a) is the base used below.

More generally the original circle coupon can occur between two ordered lists of child factors in a nonroot junction. Keep that position: Wv=(∏w∈Iv−T(Ww))Pv(∏w∈Iv+T(Ww)). W_v=\left(\prod_{w\in I_v^-}T(W_w)\right)P_v \left(\prod_{w\in I_v^+}T(W_w)\right). \tag{1b} The lists record the cut at the parent edge in the original cyclic order. The left-insertion version of the calculation below handles this form without moving P_v through a child. Reassociation of a stack and transport of genuine turn-chain rotations put the literal circle-only tree in this form. At the root only the actual cyclic closed-collar move is used to choose its cutting position.

Write E for the number of tree edges and V=E+1 for the number of circles. The exterior admits the following labeled cut:

  • There is one pair of octahedral balls per original tree edge.
  • At a circle v, its incident edge blocks are joined in the original cyclic order. There are d_v disk-side pages, and exactly one of their cyclic seams retains the original disk identification and its epsilon_v crossing. All other page seams are the untwisted labeled identity seams supplied by the ordered insertion.
  • Cutting open those distinguished disk seams and the projection faces gives two balls. Each is a tree of E octahedra attached along E-1 triangular faces. Its remaining boundary has exactly the two original shaded triangles of every crossing circle and the original white face cycles.

Here an octahedron means the explicitly labeled topological cell. The positive angular shapes for these cells, if desired, are a separate geometric input. In particular no convexity claim is needed for the union of several cells.

The two orientations are opposite, as required for joining oriented balls. The old face disappears. The second triangle in (7) is exactly the new v face in (3), and the last two are exactly the new w faces. The other shaded face of v is untouched.

The four white triangles are the complementary octahedral faces. Along the three edges of the glued triangle, their unions with the old white pieces are precisely the new white regions. One can verify this without a drawing: at each of B,C and O_v remove the two glued shaded sectors and concatenate the remaining sectors; their order is exactly the planar strip substitution (5). The new vertices A,D carry the new identity strips in that same order. Thus every remaining shaded sector and every cyclic vertex order agrees with the literal compiler. The white facial walks therefore agree as well.

Perform the same attachment in the other projection copy. This adds exactly one two-octahedron edge block and introduces one new original circle. It neither identifies two old circles nor inserts a circle at the attachment seam.

The calculation also proves the inverse operation: remove a terminal edge block, restore the deleted triangular face, and undo (5). The original diagram loses that terminal child and otherwise retains its actual strand connections and order.

The two formulas are direct applications of the same lower-output/upper-input stack rule; neither uses a rotation identity for the value of a coupon.

4. Why this covers every ordered tree

Start with one actual tree edge. The two-leaf matching gives exactly the octahedral boundary (7), in both projection copies. This is the labeled two-circle block of notes/actual_reflected_two_circle_octahedral_base.md, after the recorded endpoint-frame rotation. Its proof is a disk/projection cut, not an equality of two scalar sources.

For a general tree, choose a terminal vertex as root and start with its unique incident edge. Expose that root by the actual closed-collar moves from section 1, retaining all transported frames and orders. The initial root then has no further children to insert; at the other endpoint the only existing edge is its parent edge. At each vertex add the children on the right of P_v in reverse final order, always immediately after P_v. Add the children on its left in their final order, always immediately before P_v. This is a construction of the specified word, not a permutation of an existing word: successive insertions produce exactly the two ordered lists in (1b), including the special case (1). A new child initially has only its own coupon; its children are subsequently inserted inside its own ball. Its actual quarter-turn carries the whole local insertion, including the two projection sides and all four endpoint labels. Any previously recorded half-turn or input/output reversal similarly carries the complete insertion and its order. It does not remove the frame decoration.

Induction now gives exactly the original recursive word and its full boundary ribbon graph. Every step glues a ball along a triangular disk, so each projection copy stays a ball. There are E-1 attachments after the initial octahedron. The shaded face count is 4E−2(E−1)=2V. 4E-2(E-1)=2V. \tag{8} The count is only a consistency check: the insertion proof has already identified each of these two faces with its particular original circle and each main-segment vertex with its literal pair.

The equality of oriented ribbon graphs gives a label-preserving homeomorphism of the final boundary spheres. Extend it over the balls by a ball extension. All original white and disk-face pairing maps are matched, hence the quotient is the original link exterior. This is stronger than an identification of the unmarked manifold: the main arcs, individual auxiliary circles, disk sides, and their specified collar paths are preserved.

The extra triangular interfaces provide the promised pages. In the whole exterior an upper interface triangle and its lower partner form a two-triangle, thrice-punctured sphere. The ends are the same original circle and its two physical main punctures. Its compactification is a twice-punctured spanning disk for that circle. These pages are obtained from the explicit cell subdivision and the preceding homeomorphism; we did not assume that a single original disk cut somehow creates all d_v pages. The insertion order places the pages consecutively in exactly the original cyclic order at that circle. There are d_v-1 new seams there, plus the original distinguished disk seam.

5. Retained half twists and reversed disk frames

First perform the projection construction with the disk cuts open. A retained crossing changes their regluing, not the half-segment compiler of the open cut. At each v keep its original distinguished seam throughout all insertions. Its original disk map is used once, after the untwisted page attachments. Thus no crossing is distributed among several edges, and no crossing is silently added at a new seam.

For epsilon_v=0 the original pure disk map pairs the two sides within the same projection copy. For epsilon_v=+1 or -1 use its actual odd pairings: the punctures exchange and the two projection copies are permuted as in the original A,B disk maps. The signs +1 and -1 have their distinct primitive meridian words even though their endpoint permutation has the same parity. The explicit collar permutation is also recorded in notes/actual_balanced_octahedral_belt_stack_volume.md, section 3a. A full twist changes the peripheral marking and must not be discarded simply because its endpoint permutation is trivial.

A physical rotation or reflection of a disk frame transports its puncture labels, its two pages, and its chosen meridian and longitude together. Apply the insertion table in that transported frame. In particular a reversal that exchanges the projection copies is applied to both halves of every incident block. There is no use of an arbitrary puncture permutation justified only by the rigidity of an abstract thrice-punctured sphere.

These conventions cover the original signed and reversed diagrams. The local geometric block may be relabeled by these same actual rotations. Its shape formula is not used to determine the topology.

6. Exact primitive peripheral concatenation

Fix one auxiliary circle v and orient its original disk-framed longitude lambda_v and meridian mu_v as in the original marked leaf theorem. After the page cut, a small cusp torus of v consists of d_v annular sectors, one from each incident edge block. Every page boundary on this torus is the same primitive disk-longitude class. A small meridian crosses these sectors once in their cyclic order. The insertion (5) subdivides one such transverse passage into two consecutive passages; it neither winds the meridian a second time nor changes its longitude class.

This can also be read as a face-word statement. Each insertion replaces one original disk-face passage by two passages separated by the new triangular interface. In the final word all internal interface changes of coordinates occur in inverse consecutive pairs. After eliminating them, the retained word is the original disk meridian. Its ordered factors are one endpoint passage for each incident block, followed at the distinguished seam by the single original half-twist correction. This is a statement about the actual marked loop before evaluating its holonomy.

7. Scope

This proof applies to the actual ordered circle-only tree, with all its inherited frames and disk collars. It does not assert such a cut for an arbitrary scalar tensor network, for arbitrary branching of six independent boundary slots, or for a graph with cycles. It uses neither commutativity of pair-algebra polynomials nor equality of source actions. No auxiliary component is filled or deleted in the topological identification.

The new content is the explicit terminal substitution (5), its ribbon-face realization (7), and the induced primitive path concatenation. The local positive angular chart, global existence of the charge path, and the final complete-filling certificate are separate inputs.
