A=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\alpha\\0&\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix}, \quad B=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{-1}&0\\\beta&\lambda\end{pmatrix}, \quad K=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\beta&-\alpha/\lambda\\-\beta\lambda&0\end{pmatrix}, \quad\det K=-\alpha\beta. \tag{48} Consequently (Ka)⊗(Kb)det⁡K=−λ−1(A−1b)⊗a. \frac{(Ka)\otimes(Kb)}{\det K} =-\lambda^{-1}(A^{-1}b)\otimes a. \tag{49} Likewise ((Ha)⊗(Hb))/det⁡H=−λ(Ab)⊗a((Ha)\otimes(Hb))/\det H=-\lambda(Ab)\otimes a for the ABAB convention. These are exact vector-tensor identities. At λ=M−1\lambda=M^{-1}, the raw factor in (49) is −M-M; its reciprocal is −M−1-M^{-1}. A use in a dual or inverse comparison must specify which of those factors it requires.

The equal-eigenvalue hypothesis cannot be removed. If instead Aa=λaAa=\lambda a and Bb=λ−1bBb=\lambda^{-1}b, then A=(λα0λ−1),B=(λ0βλ−1),K=(αβ+λ−2−1−αλ−βλλ2−1). A=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\alpha\\0&\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix}, \quad B=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\\beta&\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix}, \quad K=\begin{pmatrix} \alpha\beta+\lambda^{-2}-1&-\alpha\lambda\\ -\beta\lambda&\lambda^2-1 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{50} In particular Kb=(−αλ,λ2−1)𝗍Kb=(-\alpha\lambda,\lambda^2-1)^{\mathsf t}, which is not on the line of aa for λ2≠1\lambda^2\ne1.

For the native pair Ag,BgA_{\mathrm g},B_{\mathrm g}, the actual ideal corner flags at R,0R,0 have eigenvalues M−1,MM^{-1},M, respectively. This follows from their native derivatives M2,M−2M^2,M^{-2}. Thus (50), not automatically (48), describes those two decorated ideal vertices.

The natural decorated version of (46) is (A,B;ℓA,ℓB)↦(A−1BA,A;A−1ℓB,ℓA). (A,B;\ell_A,\ell_B) \longmapsto (A^{-1}BA,A;\ A^{-1}\ell_B,\ell_A). \tag{51} If the selected eigenvalues agree, KK carries the two old lines to the two lines displayed in (51). If they are opposite, it carries the opposite eigenline of each input puncture to the displayed output line. Indeed KAK−1=A−1BAKAK^{-1}=A^{-1}BA and KBK−1=AKBK^{-1}=A, so the eigenvalue of each transported line is unchanged; the desired output line instead carries the other input’s selected eigenvalue.

This gives a precise prescription for a decorated collar: use the actual Hurwitz representation and develop the output triangles from the naturally transported flags in (51). When the two selected eigenvalues differ, this cannot be replaced by applying KK to the two old ideal vertices and keeping the neighboring white charts unchanged. On the marked eigenvalue cover the alternative eigenlines vary holomorphically and coalesce at M=1M=1, which is why the distinction disappears at the complete point.

To finish an arbitrary oriented-tree theorem, the physical endpoint types must specify which eigenflags occur on each side of the braid mapping cylinder, and the adjacent white maps must be recomputed from those output flags. The unframed Hurwitz identity alone does not establish that final decorated sewing or its global preferred-longitude factor.
