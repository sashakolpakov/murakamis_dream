6. Exceptional specialized fibers

The irreducibility proof works after specializing M=M0M=M_0 whenever t(M0)≠0t(M_0)\ne0, including M0=±1M_0=\pm1. At M0=±iM_0=\pm i, however, t=0t=0 and He=(Qv−1)2(Qw−1)2 H_e=(Q_v-1)^2(Q_w-1)^2 \tag{13} is a square. Those specialized fibers split. This does not affect the generic constants theorem, but it prevents using generic-fiber irreducibility to assert regularity at these two values.

A separate regularity argument may work on a component through a generic QQ-point there. It must exhibit a point where the actual geometric ratio and its inverse are regular and nonzero. The present proof neither supplies nor rules out that point.
