5. The opposite-eigenflag qualification

The two eigenlines coalesce at the parabolic point, so the complete figure alone cannot distinguish the operations.

6. Representation gluing followed by global decoration

The following construction keeps that distinction explicit. First perform the actual collar gluing at the level of representations; then select the geometric eigenflags on the resulting original link group.

The half-twist is the actual mapping cylinder of the two-punctured disk, fixing its outer boundary. Its two boundary generator tuples are related by (8). If CC is the pure boundary intertwiner, replacing it by CHϵCH^\epsilon, with the side and composition order fixed by (14), supplies the new intertwiner. Apply the operation in every original circle’s transported puncture frame. The other cyclic page seams remain those of section 2. At this stage no assertion is made that the old pure ideal vertices still provide the correct decoration.

For each resulting main component, choose its actual positive meridian and its M−1M^{-1} eigenflag. At a corner whose native loop is the inverse, take the corresponding MM eigenflag. Transport these choices by the original paths in the completed cut. Peripheral commutation makes the choice independent of a peripheral path. Keep the already selected disk-longitude flag on each auxiliary circle.

These choices are holomorphic near the complete structure. Develop the original topological ideal triangulation from these global flags and the constructed representation. The map of each triangle is the unique map of its transported ordered triple.
