The sign of the preferred longitude from a Seifert surface

1 November 2026. Private derivation.

This note fixes the last sign after a squared longitude identity. It uses the actual preferred boundary curve and a spin lift of the geometric representation. It does not choose a square root to match a source formula, and it does not infer a finite eigenvalue identity from a cusp derivative.

1. A frame calculation on a once-bordered surface

Let SS be a compact connected oriented surface of genus gg with one boundary component. Embed it in an oriented three-manifold which has a spin structure. Choose a smooth oriented tangent frame (e1,e2)(e_1,e_2) on SS, and complete it by the positive unit normal ν\nu. Such a tangent frame exists because a surface with boundary has trivial tangent bundle.

Pulling back the ambient spin double cover along (e1,e2,ν)(e_1,e_2,\nu) gives a double cover of SS. Its monodromy is a homomorphism H1(S;ℤ/2)→ℤ/2H_1(S;\mathbb Z/2)\to\mathbb Z/2. Since the single oriented boundary is zero in this homology, the lift of this chosen frame closes after one boundary circuit.

Now replace the first tangent vector on the boundary by its positive unit tangent tt, and the second by the corresponding inward tangent normal nn. Relative to (e1,e2)(e_1,e_2), the frame (t,n)(t,n) has winding number χ(S)=1−2g. \chi(S)=1-2g. \tag{1} This is the boundary form of the index formula: extend the outward boundary normal to a tangent vector field with isolated zeros. The sum of its indices is χ(S)\chi(S); measured in the global tangent frame, it is the degree of its boundary direction. The boundary tangent differs from that normal by a fixed quarter turn, which does not change the degree.

A full 2π2\pi rotation in the tangent plane is the nontrivial loop of the oriented three-dimensional frame group. It therefore changes the sign of a spin lift. Formula (1) is odd. It follows that the boundary-adapted frame (t,n,ν) (t,n,\nu) \tag{2} has an antiperiodic lift: after one boundary circuit its lift returns to the other sheet.

No choice of spin structure changes this conclusion. The only possible change was the monodromy of the first global frame, and that monodromy vanishes on the boundary.

2. Application to a complete hyperbolic knot cusp

Let YY be an oriented complete finite-volume hyperbolic knot complement. Take a sufficiently far cusp torus TT. Its preferred longitude λ\lambda is primitive and bounds a Seifert surface in the truncated complement. Arrange the boundary of the surface to be the straight Euclidean representative of λ\lambda on TT, and arrange its collar to meet TT orthogonally. These changes are made inside the ordinary product collar of the boundary torus.

Along that boundary, (2) is a translation-invariant horospherical frame, up to one constant rotation: tt is the constant longitude direction, nn is the radial direction, and ν\nu is the constant perpendicular direction in the horosphere. Thus Section 1 says that the translation-invariant horospherical frame has antiperiodic spin holonomy along λ\lambda.

For precision, identify the oriented frame bundle of hyperbolic three-space with PSL2(ℂ)\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C), and its spin cover with SL2(ℂ)\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C). Conjugate the cusp fixed point to infinity. If the longitude translation is zλ≠0z_\lambda\ne0, the path of translation-invariant horospherical frames is represented by U(t)=(1tzλ01),0≤t≤1. U(t)= \begin{pmatrix}1&t z_\lambda\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad 0\le t\le1. \tag{3} This is the lift starting at the identity. Here translation-invariant refers to the cusp’s Euclidean translation group; no assertion of Levi-Civita parallel transport along a horocycle in hyperbolic three-space is needed. For a determinant-one holonomy lift ρ̃\widetilde\rho, the endpoint of (3) closes in the quotient spin bundle precisely when ρ̃(λ)=U(1)\widetilde\rho(\lambda)=U(1). It returns to the opposite sheet precisely when ρ̃(λ)=−U(1)\widetilde\rho(\lambda)=-U(1). The antiperiodicity just proved therefore gives ρ̃(λ)=−(1zλ01),tr⁡ρ̃(λ)=−2. \boxed{\displaystyle \widetilde\rho(\lambda)= -\begin{pmatrix}1&z_\lambda\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \operatorname{tr}\widetilde\rho(\lambda)=-2.} \tag{4} Reversing the longitude replaces this matrix by its inverse and does not change the sign. Any two determinant-one lifts differ by a character to {±1}\{\pm1\}, and the preferred longitude is zero in the knot complement’s first homology, so (4) is independent of that choice as well.

This proof only uses a spin structure associated with a lift of the geometric holonomy. Existence of such a lift is the usual spin property of oriented three-manifolds; the sign calculation itself is the explicit surface-frame argument above.

3. Compatibility with the original augmented representation

Return to the original all-circle construction. Write μK\mu_K for its main meridian and μv\mu_v for its original auxiliary meridians. The original circles are pairwise unlinked. If ℓv\ell_v is the linking number of circle vv with the main knot and pvp_v is its integral filling coefficient, the fixed main-boundary word which becomes the preferred filled longitude is λfill=λKμK∑vpvℓv2. \lambda_{\mathrm{fill}} =\lambda_K\mu_K^{\sum_vp_v\ell_v^2}. \tag{5} In the augmented exterior its first homology class is [λfill]=∑vℓv[μv]+∑vpvℓv2[μK]. [\lambda_{\mathrm{fill}}] =\sum_v\ell_v[\mu_v] +\sum_vp_v\ell_v^2[\mu_K]. \tag{6} The original crossing disk is punctured by exactly two main strands, so ℓv∈{0,2,−2}. \ell_v\in\{0,2,-2\}. \tag{7} Consequently (6) vanishes modulo two.

At an actual complete filled knot point, pull back any determinant-one lift of its complete holonomy to the augmented group. The local face construction gives another lift of the same projective representation. Their ratio is a {±1}\{\pm1\}-valued character: both project to the same irreducible projective representation after one fixed conjugation. By (6)–(7), that character is one on λfill\lambda_{\mathrm{fill}}. Thus the original face-map lift has exactly the same longitude sign as the filled lift in (4).

This step is useful because the original matrices of an auxiliary filling slope may be −I-I rather than II. One need not assume that the particular auxiliary lifts descend to the filled group. Their possible discrepancy is a character and cannot change (5).

4. Fixing a holomorphic squared longitude identity

Suppose a connected germ of the actual geometric representation, with its original selected main flag and fixed filling equations, has already supplied Lgeom2=M2a(D)∏e(M2−Xe1−M2Xe)2. L_{\mathrm{geom}}^2 =M^{2a(D)} \prod_e\left(\frac{M^2-X_e}{1-M^2X_e}\right)^2. \tag{8} Assume the factors are holomorphic units on that germ, and define the longitude coordinate as the inverse eigenvalue on the source M−1M^{-1} meridian line. The ratio of the two sides before squaring is then a holomorphic function taking values in {1,−1}\{1,-1\}; it is constant on the connected germ.

At the actual complete filled point, M=1M=1. Each edge factor in (8) is one, since the geometric wire does not have Xe=1X_e=1. The inverse selected longitude eigenvalue is −1-1 by Sections 2–3. Therefore the constant is negative: Lgeom=−Ma(D)∏eM2−Xe1−M2Xe. \boxed{\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{geom}} =-M^{a(D)} \prod_e\frac{M^2-X_e}{1-M^2X_e}.} \tag{9} The conclusion requires the exact squared identity as input. The complete-value sign fixes only its remaining two-valued ambiguity; it is not being used to infer the dependence on MM or on any auxiliary parameter.
