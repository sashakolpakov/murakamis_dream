Generic reconstruction at every nonzero main eigenvalue

1 November 2026. Private algebraic derivation.

The purpose of this note is narrower than a global positive-shape theorem. It shows that the actual marked representation constructed from the edge characters and the original page/collar gluing has a regular generic point over every fixed M0≠0M_0\ne0. In particular the trace-zero values M0=±iM_0=\pm i do not force a pole of a main peripheral eigenvalue ratio. The native octahedral ideal subdivision does degenerate there, but the rational representation does not.

The local matrix input is the character adapter, sections 2–4 and 7. The actual ordered gluing input is the page and collar construction, including its full boundary tuples and the distinction between unframed gluing and subsequent eigenflag decoration.

3. Sewing finitely many blocks uses a rational finite atlas

The ordered page construction glues two boundary pairs whose matrices are explicit rational functions of the edge variables. Their identification is the actual one in the native table; where a loop is inverted or its order reversed, that operation is included before asking for an intertwiner.

After those marked identifications, the two ordered boundary pairs have the same three traces and are irreducible by (12). Their intertwiner is the solution line of Ga=a′G,Gb=b′G. G a=a'G,\qquad G b=b'G. \tag{14} The solution line is one-dimensional, and every nonzero solution is invertible. Indeed a nontrivial kernel or image would be an invariant line for one of the two irreducible pairs. Existence can be proved without using ideal vertices. For an irreducible pair the four matrices I,a,b,abI,a,b,ab span Mat⁡2\operatorname{Mat}_2. Their multiplication table is determined by the common trace tt and product trace pp: a2=ta−I,b2=tb−I,ab+ba=t(a+b)+(p−t2)I. a^2=ta-I,\quad b^2=tb-I,\quad ab+ba=t(a+b)+(p-t^2)I. \tag{14a} The same table for the primed pair gives an isomorphism of the two full matrix algebras, hence conjugation by an invertible matrix. Equivalently put both pairs in the explicit form (8); irreducibility ensures that the two selected generator flags are distinct and that the off-diagonal entry used to normalize the frame is nonzero. Either proof retains the ordered generators, not just the individual conjugacy classes.

Equation (14) is a finite linear system with rational entries. Choose a nonzero maximal minor and normalize one coordinate on its one-dimensional kernel. This gives a rational intertwiner regular in a neighborhood of that point. No single choice of minor has to work at all parameters. The finitely many choices form a rational atlas; the resulting representation and peripheral eigenvalue ratios are independent of the scalar normalization of an intertwiner.

Start with one edge block. The actual page insertion joins each new block to the preceding union along one marked disk side. Its attaching graph is a tree of blocks, so this inductively constructs a representation of the entire page-cut object with all matrices regular. The distinguished pure or odd seam at each circle is then installed using (10), (13), and the two full generator conjugacies of its actual mapping cylinder. Those formulas preserve its outer disk product. Van Kampen therefore gives the representation of the original augmented exterior. No arbitrary pants permutation or commutativity of tangles is used.

For any fixed finite tree, intersecting the finitely many necessary open charts gives a nonempty open set over the fixed M0M_0. Equivalently, at any good point one may choose each nonzero intertwiner minor in succession, obtaining a regular neighborhood of that point. This proves existence of the required actual rational PGL2\mathrm{PGL}_2 reconstruction at every nonzero main parameter.

This step requires the actual marked boundary conjugacies, not just a formal family of characters. They were identified with the original cut in the page/collar proof and remain rational identities on the present open sets.

5. A regular nonzero main eigenvalue ratio

Every main peripheral word is a fixed finite product of the regular invertible group matrices just constructed. Hence its matrix and inverse are regular at the chosen point. The main meridian is noncentral.

There is one important trace-zero detail. Commutation in PGL2\mathrm{PGL}_2 alone would allow a longitude to exchange the two eigenlines of a trace-zero meridian. Use the matrices on the local section through nearby MM. Normalize the meridian μ\mu to determinant one. Its projective commutation with a regular invertible longitude representative LL gives LμL−1=κμL\mu L^{-1}=\kappa\mu, where κ2=1\kappa^2=1 by determinants. At the nearby parameters with M+M−1≠0M+M^{-1}\ne0, taking traces gives κ=1\kappa=1. Consequently the exact matrix identity Lμ=μL. L\mu=\mu L. \tag{16a} extends to the trace-zero point. The undesired anticommuting component is not part of this continued representation. This also follows as a rational identity on the integral generic source family.

At M0≠±1M_0\ne\pm1, choose its specified eigenline locally; its eigenvalue on a commuting peripheral word is finite and nonzero. The ratio of the two peripheral eigenvalues is therefore a regular unit.

The same conclusion holds at M0=±1M_0=\pm1, without diagonalizing the meridian. In a frame in which its upper-right entry is a unit, a commuting matrix LL has the form L=aI+bμ. L=aI+b\mu. \tag{17} Here b=L12/μ12b=L_{12}/\mu_{12} and a=L11−bμ11a=L_{11}-b\mu_{11} are regular. Its two selected eigenvalues are a+bMa+bM and a+bM−1a+bM^{-1}. Both are nonzero at the point because det⁡L≠0\det L\ne0. Their ratio is therefore regular and nonzero through the parabolic value. Scalar changes of the representative LL cancel from the ratio.

The selected meridian eigenline itself is regular as well: in this frame it is spanned by (μ12,M−μ11)(\mu_{12},M-\mu_{11}), or by (μ12,M−1−μ11)(\mu_{12},M^{-1}-\mu_{11}) for the other selection. The first coordinate is a unit, including at M=±1M=\pm1. Thus coalescence of the two flags does not create a pole in the marked eigenvalue ratio.

This directly applies to the square of a determinant-one preferred-longitude eigenvalue, with the chosen inverse convention if necessary. It does not require choosing a global square root of a projective matrix determinant. Every factor (7), and every framing monomial in MM, is likewise a unit at the point.
