# Quantitative circle-tree publication: first-correction dependency

This read concerns the canonical publication checkout and the ten files recorded in `md-all-circle-public-docs-final-read.json`. No repository file was changed by this read.

The master theorem equations (21) and (24) have a justified O(N^-2) remainder. The relevant input is the grouped-wire packet, Section 5 equations (25)–(27), Section 6 insertion rule, and the conditional combined-contour corollary (36) in Section 8. The base interpolated integrand is independent of the moving main parameter after the recorded exact scalars are removed. Its only residual parameter dependence is the product of edge insertions. Each insertion is 1 minus pi*s^2*cot(pi*x)/N plus a uniform O(N^-2) error, with fixed derivatives on a smaller complex regular tube.

The new quantitative analytic note supplies the required combined contour without relying on large reciprocal slopes: Section 5 provides a uniform nondegenerate complex wire saddle and exponentially small finite-grid currents on the explicit angular disk. Section 7 provides the nondegenerate first joint angular saddle. Sections 8–9 prove that all other charges, endpoint connectors and discarded source terms are exponentially smaller than its action. For the retained contour, normalized insertion of a fixed holomorphic function evaluates that function at the saddle with error O(1/N). The linear Gaussian term vanishes; its pairing with the cubic phase and the quadratic insertion term both cost O(1/N). Multiplying this error by the explicit 1/N in the edge correction yields O(N^-2). The common uncomputed first saddle coefficient cancels in the ratio. A separate second-order expansion of the whole source amplitude is therefore unnecessary.

The exact framing factor is then expanded only after retaining it in the master equation (21). With z=2*pi*i*s, the coefficient is the preferred cusp shape divided by 8*pi*i, as in equation (24). The geometric identification uses the independently proved preferred-cusp continuation, rather than identifying a cusp from the scalar expansion. Bounds are uniform on every fixed R<1; derivatives require a smaller disk. No claim is made at the ordinary resonances or for growing trees or crossing powers.

The new criterion is an additional sufficient class, and the existing complete and moving long-twist theorems for every fixed strict tree remain unchanged in the guide.
