Geometric continuation beyond long auxiliary fillings

1 November 2026. Private research note; no repository files are changed.

The long-filling argument uses two different facts. The explicit source equations give an actual marked representation, while a separate local geometric construction turns a representation sufficiently close to the complete one into a complete metric on the filled topology. The first fact continues as rational identities wherever its charts are defined. The second does not follow from those identities alone.

This note establishes an exact obstruction to using the original real angle chamber as a global filling domain. It then gives a finite geometric certificate which does imply completion without a closeness hypothesis. The certificate uses actual cells and actual logarithmic link periods, rather than assuming that a hyperbolic holonomy exists. It also distinguishes the absolute volume calibration from the nondegeneracy and quantum dominance questions.

The inputs are the explicit original maps and primitive curves in notes/actual_conway_general_mask_marked_filling.md, Sections 3–7; the local core/link construction in notes/general_source_regulator_hyperbolic_equality.md, Sections 2–7; the absolute fundamental-chain regulator in notes/general_peripheral_representation_volume_bound.md, Sections 1–2; and the exact Fourier/filling dictionary in notes/actual_main_meridian_joint_filling_transform.md, Section 6.

1. What proximity supplies in the existing proof

The explicit white and shaded matrices give the original group relations, not a global embedding of their deformed images. In particular the deformed white pieces can cease to be coplanar. An arbitrary solution of the rational edge relations is therefore an actual representation but is not yet a geometric metric.

The completion proof near the complete representation uses proximity in three separate places.

  1. Equivariant averages on a compact core and its collars remain orientation-preserving local diffeomorphisms by a uniform positive Jacobian bound.
  2. The affine development of each cusp link is close to its complete translation development. At an axis-type cusp the second common fixed point escapes every developed fundamental polygon.
  3. The actual periods of the logarithm of that link development tend to zero on the fixed primitive generators. Consequently they equal the chosen small multiplier logarithms, with no added integral multiples of 2πi2\pi i.

The last assertion is proved on the fixed generators before they are multiplied by the filling coefficients. Thus it is uniform over long, independently chosen slopes. None of these three assertions follows merely from a small angular saddle in the reduced Fourier integral.

4. A finite certificate for completion without proximity

The following is a sufficient condition which can be checked on finite geometric data. It applies to an arbitrary original compact core with torus ends, including one obtained from the marked Conway cut.

Choose a finite geodesic tetrahedral subdivision of the original topological core and its ideal ends. Finite interior vertices are allowed. This avoids an assumption that an arbitrarily prescribed face triangulation extends using ideal vertices only. Keep the actual face identifications and primitive peripheral curves.

A certificate consists of the following data and checks.

  1. Each tetrahedron is realized as a nondegenerate, positively oriented hyperbolic tetrahedron. Its ordered faces are identified by the prescribed orientation-compatible isometries. Ideal shapes may be checked by positive imaginary parts in their stated order; tetrahedra with finite vertices can be checked by the signs of their hyperboloid determinants and their nondegenerate face data.
  2. Every interior edge has total dihedral angle exactly 2π2\pi, and its full face-transition word is the identity. The original combinatorial links are circles at edges, spheres at finite vertices, and tori at the ideal ends. These are topological checks on the chosen subdivision, not assertions about holonomy.
  3. On an unfilled torus, the developed similarity triangles have translation holonomy with two real-linearly independent primitive periods. On a filled torus, the affine peripheral group has a common second fixed point qq, distinct from the selected ideal flag at infinity. Every triangle of one finite developed fundamental link region, including its boundary, misses qq.
  4. Continue log⁡(z−q)\log(z-q) along the two prescribed primitive generator paths in that link, and compute their actual periods Pμ,PλP_\mu,P_\lambda. For the chosen primitive filling slope, verify the unexponentiated identity aPμ+bPλ=2πiη,η=±1. aP_\mu+bP_\lambda=2\pi i\eta, \qquad \eta=\pm1. \tag{11}

The triangle avoidance in item 3 is a finite plane-triangle test. The periods in item 4 can be computed as finite increments of the logarithm along edges, retaining the winding integers. Exact algebraic equalities and strict inequalities may instead be verified by certified interval bounds and exact arguments. A nonzero multiplier alone is not the period check.

In fact the avoidance test is redundant once the link has been proved to be a nonsingular similarity torus. Here is a direct reason. In a developed affine coordinate, the vector field (z−q)∂z(z-q)\partial_z is preserved by every transition z↦az+bz\mapsto az+b fixing qq, since az+b−q=a(z−q)az+b-q=a(z-q). It therefore descends to a smooth vector field on the torus. Every zero has index +1+1: the development is an orientation-preserving local coordinate, and near a zero this is the radial vector field. The sum of the indices on a torus is zero. One can see that without a metric theorem by choosing the usual periodic trivialization of its tangent bundle: the opposite sides of a fundamental polygon cancel in the winding number of the vector-field components. A finite collection of positive-index zeros is therefore impossible. The development misses qq everywhere. Thus item 3 may instead require only the actual nonsingular similarity link and its nontranslation peripheral holonomy; the explicit triangle test is a convenient independent finite check.

Completion theorem. A certificate with these properties gives a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric on the original specified Dehn filling. It uses no proximity to a complete representation.

First, the positive tetrahedra and their face isometries give a local hyperbolic metric away from the edges and vertices. The full edge word and total angle 2π2\pi remove both a longitudinal mismatch and a cone singularity. At a finite vertex the spherical triangle links form a smooth curvature-one metric on a topological sphere. Its compact local spherical development covers the standard sphere; since the target sphere is simply connected it is an isometry. The finite vertices are therefore nonsingular as well.

At an ideal vertex, sufficiently high horospherical sections of the incident tetrahedra are Euclidean triangles. Their induced transition maps are the prescribed positive similarities. Edge angle sums remove the singularities at triangle vertices. They therefore give a nonsingular similarity structure on the compact cusp torus, with a developing local diffeomorphism dd on its universal cover. No injectivity of the global three-dimensional development has yet been assumed.

For an axis-type end, all peripheral maps fix qq. The finite avoidance check and equivariance imply that the entire image of dd misses qq. On the simply connected cover set f=log⁡(d−q). f=\log(d-q). \tag{12} Its real differential is invertible. The Euclidean metric |df|2|df|^2 is invariant under the peripheral deck group and descends to a nonsingular metric on the compact torus. It is complete. The lifted local isometry ff is consequently a covering onto ℂ\mathbb C: every finite Euclidean segment lifts by completeness. Since ℂ\mathbb C is simply connected, ff is a diffeomorphism. Its periods form a real rank-two lattice, and the periods on the actual marked paths are precisely the computed Pμ,PλP_\mu,P_\lambda.

In the frame with selected flag infinity, the actual geometric end contains all sufficiently high vertical rays above each link triangle. These fit to an equivariant expression (d(w),etb(w))(d(w),e^t b(w)), with b>0b>0 and b(w+γ)=|aγ|b(w)b(w+\gamma)=|a_\gamma|b(w). At an unfilled end the translation link similarly develops onto ℂ\mathbb C, and its vertical end contains a full horoball quotient. It is a complete finite-volume cusp. The certificate can be applied to a new subdivision or to an explicitly continued one. It does not claim that the original ideal pyramids remain positive at short fillings, or that every hyperbolic filling has already been certified in this way.

5. Why multiplier equations do not replace the link certificate

There is an elementary local model for the missing integer. Fix L>0L>0 and an integer k≥2k\ge2. On the universal cover of a torus choose a complex coordinate with period lattice ⟨L,2πik⟩ \langle L,2\pi ik\rangle \tag{15} and similarity development d(f)=efd(f)=e^f. The two affine holonomies are dilation by eLe^L and the identity. The local development is positive and avoids zero. Its actual primitive angular period, however, is 2πik2\pi ik. Formula (14) completes to a tube with cone angle 2πk2\pi k, not a smooth solid torus.

One could write 2πi2\pi i as a logarithm of the identity holonomy and falsely satisfy a unit algebraic log equation. That logarithm is not the actual period of this marked link. This model shows exactly why an exponentiated gluing solution, or arbitrary choices of logarithms at one solution, do not suffice. It is not asserted to be a particular Conway counterexample.

The same distinction explains the usefulness of the small-neighborhood argument: near completeness it proves automatically that the selected small multiplier logs equal the actual periods. Outside that neighborhood the equality must be supplied by continuation with winding control or by the finite link certificate.

6. The absolute action can be continued without a hidden volume constant

Let the rational original face maps and all selected peripheral flags continue along a path from the complete structure, avoiding undefined charts. Retain the same original oriented fundamental chain, the same primitive paths, and continuous lifts of all required logarithms. Work on this marked logarithmic cover, not merely on the projected character variety. The source identity on that cover is dG=12∑iVidUi,ℑG(0)=Vol⁡(Y). dG=\tfrac12\sum_iV_i\,dU_i, \qquad \Im G(0)=\operatorname{Vol}(Y). \tag{16} The original ideal-chain boundary cancellation continues as a rational identity; its Bloch–Wigner differential is dVol⁡(ρ)=12∑i(ℜVidℑUi−ℜUidℑVi). d\operatorname{Vol}(\rho) =\tfrac12\sum_i (\Re V_i\,d\Im U_i-\Re U_i\,d\Im V_i). \tag{17} The absolute representation volume here is that of the same marked fundamental class. It can be computed using a signed straightened chain even at intermediate parameters where no positive metric has been established. It is not a new primitive chosen at each point.

Differentiating (16) gives, along every such path, Vol⁡(ρ)=ℑG−12∑iℜUiℑVi. \operatorname{Vol}(\rho) =\Im G-\tfrac12\sum_i\Re U_i\Im V_i. \tag{18} The initial value fixes the constant. Thus a geometric certificate does calibrate a continued selected action, provided that its logarithmic lift and original fundamental chain are identified. A certificate at an isolated solution cannot by itself select among differently normalized actions: adding an imaginary constant to a local primitive preserves all its critical equations and changes the proposed volume. The complete anchor and the continued boundary term are substantive data.

7. Positivity of cells and nondegeneracy of the source are different checks

For real positive tetrahedral angles, the volume Hessian is negative definite on each angle plane. With linear edge constraints and an injective retained-wire map, the restricted sum has a negative definite Hessian. This is the original complete optimization argument. It gives uniqueness and analytic response on its strict real domain.

Primitive filling equations are complex and are not those same linear angle constraints. Section 2 shows that their smooth solutions cannot lie in the original real pyramid chamber. The complex Hessian must therefore be checked on the continued branch; positivity of a metric realized by a different cellulation does not identify that Hessian.

Even the local leaf action supplies a simple warning. For g(x,t)=J(2x)−2J(x)−J(x−t)−J(x+t), g(x,t)=J(2x)-2J(x)-J(x-t)-J(x+t), its analytic second derivative is gxx=4πcot⁡(2πx)−2πcot⁡(πx)−πcot⁡π(x−t)−πcot⁡π(x+t). g_{xx}=4\pi\cot(2\pi x)-2\pi\cot(\pi x) -\pi\cot\pi(x-t)-\pi\cot\pi(x+t). \tag{21} At (x,t)=(1/4,1/2)(x,t)=(1/4,1/2), all the sine arguments defining this local analytic chart are nonzero, yet gxx=0g_{xx}=0. This is not a claimed glued filling solution. It proves that the negative real-chamber Hessian does not persist throughout the regular complex leaf chart merely because its logarithms are defined.

A nonperturbative application should consequently certify both the geometric data of Section 4 and the determinant bounds for its actual continued source saddle. Neither assertion follows from the other.

8. Present conclusion and the remaining constructive question

There is a concrete nonperturbative geometric theorem: the finite positive-cell and primitive-log-link certificate implies smooth completion and, with the continued marked action, absolute volume calibration. It permits short slopes whenever its checks succeed. It has not yet been established uniformly for a new family of short Conway fillings.

The normalized cusp formula (10) makes high-degree circle junctions a natural test. It proves that a bounded physical power can correspond to a large normalized slope. Equations (6)–(7), however, show that the required logarithmic branch is not the old small complete branch. To obtain a genuine new family, one must construct a finite positive subdivision along that branch, or exhibit a new geometric subdivision at its endpoint together with the continued log-link periods. The analytic inverse-curvature criterion alone does not do this.

No conclusion about short-slope quantum dominance follows from this note. That requires the separate source, tail, noncancellation and primitive-lattice estimates at the same continued saddle.
