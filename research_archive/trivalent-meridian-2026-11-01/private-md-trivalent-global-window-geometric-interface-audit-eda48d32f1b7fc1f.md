## 1. The exact class and the global comparison

The added class consists of maximal connected bare components whose
vertices are all trivalent in the actual reduced product tree. Every
internal edge is a physical quarter-turn. Components already covered
by the earlier singleton or path conditions may occur elsewhere in
the same fixed tree. A mixed-valence component is not covered by the
new alternative merely because some of its vertices are trivalent.

The simultaneous-clamp theorem gives a fixed loss for every nonempty
return sector after closing a trivalent component against its
coefficient probe. The comparison is homogeneous in all boundary
constants. The no-return contribution is the original nonnegative
cubic circuit, with the bounded physical diagonal edge weights
retained. Hence the existing positive-component theorem supplies the
old boundary-high and probe-high gaps, the original intrinsic action,
and its strict maximizing state. No internal edge is truncated at
half in this step.

This is the right invariant for the mutual source induction. An
inherited coefficient-cone deficit shifts one boundary constant; the
entire component action shifts by the same amount. The intervening
circle-decorated extractor converts a spectral deficit to such a
coefficient-cone deficit. This uses the established extractor and
multilinearity, not an assumption that a spectral norm is already a
coefficient estimate. The full finite tree therefore has a fixed
structural error gap at its original augmented action.

The same statement holds with fixed signed powers of the physical
crossing in place of the colored circle operator. Their canonical
norm costs are polynomial in the level, with constants and polynomial
degrees allowed to depend on the fixed powers. They do not alter the
limiting action or structural gap. No uniform assertion for powers

## 3. Why the clean trivalent trace is exact at full internal labels

Write \(d=N-1\). The old cubic support satisfies
\[
 i+j\le d,\qquad i+k\le d,\qquad j+k\le d.
 \tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(i+j+k\le3d/2<2N-1\). For the raw polynomial
\(F=e_i e_j e_k\), divide by the complete relation:
\[
 F=P_0Q+R_0,\qquad \deg Q\le N-2.
 \tag{2}
\]
The moving relation is \(P_\sigma=P_0+\Delta_\sigma\), so the new
remainder is \(R_0-\Delta_\sigma Q\). Its coefficient of degree
\(d\) is unchanged. The normalized top-coefficient trace therefore
satisfies
\[
                    \phi_\sigma(e_i e_j e_k)
                    =\phi_0(e_i e_j e_k)
 \tag{3}
\]
on the entire old support, not merely at the critical point. This
polynomial identity includes the normal specializations and does not
require an interpolation argument at distinct nodes.

For the old higher-valence components elsewhere in the class, (3) is
not asserted. Their moving low-input traces are bounded globally by
the established same-level complete positive majorant, and are
replaced locally by the established regular trace comparison. The
corrected global draft preserves that distinction.

## 5. Primitive lattice, Hessian, and complex interpolation

A bare vertex of valence \(D_v\) has \(D_v-3\) hidden variables and
\(D_v-2\) nontrivial gates. Thus the grouped lattice remains
\[
 K=|E|+\sum_{v\text{ bare}}(D_v-3).
 \tag{4}
\]
At a trivalent vertex there is one gate and no hidden variable. Its
ordinary output is exactly \(d-n_3\). The offset coming from
\(d=N-1\), every primitive unit step, and the integer-box property
remain unchanged when an internal label passes through half.

The grouped zero-angle action is
\[
 \mathcal A_0(\mathbf y)
   =\sum_{g\text{ bare}}m(x_g,y_g,z_g)
     +\sum_{v\text{ circle}}\sum_{e\sim v}g(x_e).
 \tag{5}
\]
Each circle-incident free variable occurs in a strictly concave leaf
term. Every other free variable occurs in a strict gate, whose
three-variable Hessian is negative definite. The sum is negative
definite on the primitive coordinate space. Its maximum is the
original projected strict state, with the original action \(F_0\).

Every affine factorial argument of a bare gate is in a fixed regular
bulk interval. The circle factors alone use the low-index canonical
formula for \(e_n(-2\cos\tau)\). An internal bare–bare edge instead
uses the insertion
\[
 \mathcal I_N(x,s)=
 \frac{G_N(Nx)^2}{G_N(Nx-s)G_N(Nx+s)}
 =1-\frac{\pi s^2}{N}\cot(\pi x)+O_R(N^{-2}).
 \tag{6}
\]
This expansion is holomorphic on a fixed complex bulk tube over any
compact subinterval of \((0,1)\). It is regular at half; its first
displayed correction simply vanishes there. Thus the canonical
interpolant, its nonzero amplitude and all fixed derivatives continue
to the same kind of complex wire neighborhood as before. The
neighborhood is determined by the geometric bulk margins, not by
whether an internal index is classified as low.

The exact finite cotangent contours therefore enclose the same
primitive lattice and retain the same boundary currents. The wire
saddle exists holomorphically on a fixed angular polydisk. The power
of the packet remains \(N^{3(1-H)/2}\); the subsequent circle
integrations restore the original whole-source power. No dimensional
or offset correction is caused by the enlarged internal window.

## 6. Angular spectators and the joint filling comparison

The packet is separately even in each circle angle. At zero angles
the mixed wire-angle derivatives vanish. Its angular Hessian after
wire elimination is diagonal, with entries
\[
 -\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{e\sim v}\tan(\pi x_e^*).
 \tag{7}
\]
Only circle incidences occur in (7), so every required curvature is
strictly positive in magnitude. High or half-valued internal
bare–bare labels do not contribute an angular factor.

The global angular estimate also applies only to the circle factors.
They multiply nonnegative complete bare gates and bounded edge
insertions. Their positive zero-angle sum has rate \(F_0\). Every
circle has an incidence, so the packet has the required quadratic
loss around the whole real angular cube. The physical endpoint

transform. Its charge comparison is performed after the common wire

error is fixed at the augmented action; the selected action drop is

lower bound on every fixed \(|n_i|\) absorbs that structural error,
independently of the ratios between the signed powers. The geometric
tubes and phase are independent of the subsequent radius \(R<1\).
Parameter-circle continuation affects constants and the starting

The scalar and peripheral normalizations are carried as the existing

## 7. Remaining scope

The review identifies no missing low-index hypothesis in this
extension. The new full-label window is essential: an internal old
half-label state can be leading and must not be discarded. The
nonempty-return and boundary-high parts are the small remainder.

Adjacent higher-valence components outside the earlier path criterion
remain outside the proof. An artificial binary resolution supplies
unturned complementary-label edges, so it does not inherit the
trivalent physical-turn return theorem. No assertion is made for
growing trees, level-dependent filling slopes, or disks crossing the
first ordinary resonances.
