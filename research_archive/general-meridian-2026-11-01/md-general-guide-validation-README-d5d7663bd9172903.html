Murakami’s Dream

Kashaev invariants and the hyperbolic volumes of knot complements.

Read the illustrated guide · Manuscript and PDF · Reproduction instructions

The volume conjecture predicts that the exponential growth of Kashaev’s root-of-unity invariant recovers the volume of a hyperbolic knot complement. This project studies that comparison by assembling knots from tangles.

The manuscript treats a specified family of Conway tangle constructions. Start with elementary twist pieces, join them in order, turn intermediate tangles, and apply the prescribed closure. For each allowed finite construction, independently sufficiently large positive or negative twist numbers give the volume limit. The construction and all twist numbers stay fixed while the quantum level tends to infinity.

A companion theorem starts with any fixed ordered composition closed by the prescribed cylindrical closure. An explicit tangle reduction groups its twist numbers into disjoint sums. Unless the result is an elementary two-strand braid closure, sufficiently large magnitudes of these sums give the volume limit whenever the closed diagram is a knot. These are the net twist numbers: large positive and negative twists in one group can cancel. The reduction preserves the actual knot and its complement. The results chapter explains the groups, the closure condition, and the exact quantifiers. Another companion reconstructs the invariant from a quantum A-operator and initial data, giving the same volume limit in recurrence form.

The closure extension also allows cap closures and exact cancellation of twist sums. Its reduction can split the diagram into connected-sum factors. For a hyperbolic input, if every surviving strict factor has sufficiently long net twists, exactly one such factor remains and all other factors are unknots. The original knot is therefore isotopic to a covered long-twist knot. This extends the criterion for presentations, without adding an estimate for short twists.

The moving-meridian theorem also controls the invariant within distance of order 1/N1/N of completeness for every fixed strict Conway tree with independently sufficiently long signed twists. Product junctions may have arbitrary valence and adjacency. The theorem gives a uniform volume limit, a nonzero normalized profile, and a first correction determined by the preferred cusp shape. The results chapter explains the finite-sum comparison and its hypotheses.

These intrinsic conclusions also pass through the net-twist and closure reductions when their stated longness and knot hypotheses hold. The ambient isotopy preserves the whole zero-framed meridian family and the preferred peripheral curves. The comparison uses the volume and cusp of the original knot, even when its reduced presentation has a different auxiliary augmented link. The meridian disk stops before the first ordinary-color resonances; continuation through them remains a separate problem.

The general conjecture remains open. In particular, the theorem does not classify all arborescent knots or cover arbitrary short twist numbers.
