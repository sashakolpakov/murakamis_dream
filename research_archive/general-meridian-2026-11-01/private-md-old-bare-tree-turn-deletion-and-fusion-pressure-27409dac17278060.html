Deleting quarter-turn edges in the old positive pressure

Private proof, 1 November 2026. The comparison retains entire neighboring profiles and all correlations between their incidences. It does not identify the links before and after deletion.

1. The original tree and its complete contraction

Let 𝒢\mathcal G be a finite connected ordered tree of bare product junctions. Each actual junction has full valence at least three, counting external incidences, and every internal edge is a physical quarter-turn. External branches carry the old rigid profiles g(a)+cjg(a)+c_j, 0≤a≤1/20\le a\le1/2, with their original constants. Write F(𝒢)F(\mathcal G) for the full old positive maximum, retaining the hidden wires in the original ordered product resolutions.

At level NN, use the fixed complete Newton basis e0,…,ede_0,\ldots,e_d, d=N−1d=N-1, with the complete Frobenius functional ϕ0\phi_0. Its exact metric and turn are ϕ0(eiej)=δi+j,d,T0(ei)=ed−i. \phi_0(e_i e_j)=\delta_{i+j,d},\qquad T_0(e_i)=e_{d-i}. \tag{1} These are identities of the full complete algebra, including the first normals at repeated nodes. A covariant whole-junction tensor is ϕ0(∏enj)\phi_0(\prod e_{n_j}). Contracting a physical turn identifies equal covariant labels at its two ends. An unturned contraction identifies complementary covariant labels.

For a subset SS of the original internal edges, remove the quarter-turn from every edge in SS, and fuse the resulting unturned product connections. Denote the resulting ordered tree by 𝒢/S\mathcal G/S. All physical edges outside SS remain quarter-turns. The external branches and constants are unchanged.

Theorem. For every finite edge set SS, F(𝒢/S)≤F(𝒢). F(\mathcal G/S)\le F(\mathcal G). \tag{2} This holds without a matching assumption on SS. Each fused vertex still has valence at least three and the new positive tree has its original strict maximum. For a single deleted edge, equality holds if at least one of its two rooted sides is a single trivalent junction with two rigid leaf branches. If neither side has that form, the inequality is strict.

2. Exact fusion and the inherited order

The basis dual to eie_i under (1) is ed−ie_{d-i}. Thus, for any two complete polynomials A,BA,B, ∑i=0dϕ0(Aei)ϕ0(ed−iB)=ϕ0(AB). \sum_{i=0}^d\phi_0(Ae_i)\phi_0(e_{d-i}B)=\phi_0(AB). \tag{3} Indeed expand A=∑iϕ0(Aei)ed−iA=\sum_i\phi_0(Ae_i)e_{d-i} and apply ϕ0(⋅B)\phi_0(\,\cdot\,B). This proves (3) in the complete algebra itself, and makes no distinct-node specialization. Applying it to the factors at two neighboring junctions fuses precisely their complementary-index connection into one whole-junction tensor.

In particular, changing one endpoint of an equal-index connection from ii to d−id-i changes that connection into the unturned pairing in (3). If both endpoints were reflected, the connection would still have equal labels. For the one-high application below, each selected edge is reflected at exactly one endpoint.

The order on a fused vertex is the induced ribbon order. Concretely, take a small oriented regular neighborhood of the two vertex disks and their connecting band, remove that internal band from the boundary list, and read the remaining half-edges along the resulting boundary. No permutation of attached branches is performed. With a root chosen outside this band, this operation is just substitution of the child’s ordered product into its position in the parent’s ordered product. Associativity flattens that unturned insertion. The unchanged external branches keep their orders, rotations and attachments.

Formula (3) proves the positive tensor identity in this induced order. Its symmetry does not serve as a premise for an isotopy of arbitrary tangles. Deleting a physical quarter-turn generally changes the diagram; the theorem compares its positive pressure with that of the original diagram.

If vertices of valences D1,D2≥3D_1,D_2\ge3 fuse, the new valence is D1+D2−2≥4D_1+D_2-2\ge4. More generally a connected fused set KK has valence 2+∑v∈K(Dv−2)≥3. 2+\sum_{v\in K}(D_v-2)\ge3. \tag{4} Because the original graph is a tree, contraction creates neither a cycle nor a duplicated branch. The induced order is also independent of the order in which the edges of a fixed fused set are contracted: it is the boundary order of that same regular neighborhood.

The closed old pressure of each fused hub is the maximum over its prescribed positive binary resolution. The fixed-label arity lemma constructs this resolution whenever its external labels have pair sums below one and total above one. For example assigning 3/83/8 to all physical incidences is strictly feasible at every fused hub. Hidden unturned wires are then constructed by that lemma, not assigned 3/83/8. The usual barrier and negative-Hessian arguments give a unique strict maximum of the fused tree. Thus every stage of the deletion process remains in the old strict pressure class.

3. The exact one-edge pressure comparison

Cut one physical edge in a tree of this class. Root each remaining side at that incidence. It is an unturned product of at least two inputs: an external branch is rigid type LL, and a branch through another hub is the physical turn of an unturned type-AA product, hence again type AA. The two rooted profiles, denoted fA(b),fB(b)f_A(b),f_B(b), retain all their original subtrees and constants. They are continuous on [0,1][0,1], analytic and strictly concave on (0,1)(0,1), and have their unique strict constrained states.

The proper product-response inequality gives fX(b)≥fX(1−b),1/2≤b≤1,X∈{A,B}. f_X(b)\ge f_X(1-b),\qquad 1/2\le b\le1, \quad X\in\{A,B\}. \tag{5} For clarity, this is the original unturned-product inequality, not an inequality for an arbitrary top-turned profile. At the last binary product its positive response satisfies Y2=(B1−B)(B2−B)≤(X−B)2,X2+Y2−B2=1,B<Bi≤X. Y^2=(B_1-B)(B_2-B)\le(X-B)^2, \qquad X^2+Y^2-B^2=1, \qquad B<B_i\le X. \tag{6} Consequently X≥(B+B2+2)/2X\ge(B+\sqrt{B^2+2})/2, so fX′(b)≥f2′(b)f_X'(b)\ge f_2'(b), where the two-leaf profile f2f_2 is symmetric about half. Integrating from 1−b1-b to bb proves (5). The comparison is strict for b>1/2b>1/2 if the last product has a proper composite child. A rooted side fails to have such a child exactly when it is one trivalent hub with two rigid leaf inputs; then its profile is f2+cf_2+c, exactly symmetric. The strict endpoint case follows by continuity and the strictly increasing difference fX(b)−fX(1−b)f_X(b)-f_X(1-b) for b>1/2b>1/2.

For the physical turn the covariant labels are equal, so both ordinary outputs agree. For the deleted turn they are complementary. The two complete pressures are therefore exactly Fturn=max0≤b≤1{fA(b)+fB(b)},Fdeleted=max0≤b≤1{fA(b)+fB(1−b)}. \begin{split} F_{\mathrm{turn}}&=\max_{0\le b\le1}\{f_A(b)+f_B(b)\},\\ F_{\mathrm{deleted}}&=\max_{0\le b\le1} \{f_A(b)+f_B(1-b)\}. \end{split} \tag{7} These identities include all internal maximizations; no branch is replaced by an independent capped norm budget.

If b≥1/2b\ge1/2, the deleted summand is bounded by fA(b)+fB(b)f_A(b)+f_B(b), using (5) for BB. If b≤1/2b\le1/2, it is bounded by fA(1−b)+fB(1−b)f_A(1-b)+f_B(1-b), using (5) for AA. Thus in both cases it is bounded by a feasible matched summand with ordinary output max⁡(b,1−b)\max(b,1-b). Maximizing proves Fdeleted≤Fturn. F_{\mathrm{deleted}}\le F_{\mathrm{turn}}. \tag{8}

If fB=f2+cf_B=f_2+c, the two summands in (7) agree identically; if fA=f2+cf_A=f_2+c, substitute b↦1−bb\mapsto1-b. Hence one symmetric side gives equality. If neither side is symmetric, the pointwise comparison is strict away from half. At half, both derivatives fA′(1/2),fB′(1/2)f_A'(1/2),f_B'(1/2) are strictly positive, so the matched summand is not maximal there. Compactness now gives a strict inequality of maxima. This proves the equality assertion.

4. Any finite family of deleted turns

Delete the edges in SS successively. After each deletion, (3) fuses the affected ordered products. Equations (4) and the strict witness show that the remaining graph is again a tree in the same class, with precisely the same external rigid profiles and constants. Every remaining edge is still a physical quarter-turn. Therefore (8) applies anew, to the actual profiles of that current tree. It yields F(𝒢/S)≤F(𝒢/S′)≤F(𝒢)(S′⊂S). F(\mathcal G/S)\le F(\mathcal G/S')\le F(\mathcal G) \qquad(S'\subset S). \tag{9} The profiles may change after fusion; they are never replaced by the previous profiles without recomputation. This is why the argument preserves the correlations between several reflected incidences, unlike an iteration of a fixed multi-boundary pressure with other marks held in the wrong response class.

The identity (3) also shows that a restricted reflected sector is bounded by the full positive contraction of 𝒢/S\mathcal G/S. The hidden complementary labels are summed in the fused tensor; discarding their original band restrictions only enlarges that positive upper bound. Equations (2) and (9) then compare its rate with the original F(𝒢)F(\mathcal G).

5. The one-high and several-high sector graph

Here is the precise graph bookkeeping needed for the moving-source application. Assume the local Fourier difference bound has replaced a selected moving tensor with exactly one high incidence by its old tensor with that incidence reflected. All its other incidences are low. Actual physical turn types prevent two moving equal-color vertices from being adjacent.

An internal reflected incidence therefore meets a constant old vertex. A nonzero old whole tensor has at most one strictly high incidence, by its complete pairwise support. Two selected reflected edges cannot meet at that old vertex. Nor can two meet at a selected one-high moving vertex. Thus the selected internal edges form a matching. Each connects a selected moving vertex and an old vertex; both endpoints have no other high exterior incidence.

Fuse these edges using (3). Each fused hub has only low exterior incidences in the original sector, and full valence at least four. In particular it creates no new high marked interface when other moving vertices with at least two high incidences are subsequently removed. Those latter vertices are never fused. They remain pairwise nonadjacent: no contraction joins two of their vertices, and a fused vertex is still retained between any such vertices which shared its endpoints as neighbors.

The remaining old regions therefore still have at most one high marked incidence per actual old hub. Their low arms retain the full old correlations. The separate several-high comparison may attach one gg profile on each low arm and reinsert its half-cardinal old hub, consuming that profile once. It applies in this fused tree with its actual valences and inherited orders. Its strict boundary gap is measured below F(𝒢/S)F(\mathcal G/S), which is at most the original F(𝒢)F(\mathcal G) by (2).

If the one reflected high incidence is an original external leaf, there is no internal edge to fuse. Its original high coefficient cone has rate cjc_j. After reflection its low label is rr; the old rigid comparison leaf has rate g(r)+cjg(r)+c_j. Introducing that old leaf is an upper bound because g(r)≥0g(r)\ge0, and the exact loss g(r)g(r) must be retained if a strict estimate is needed. The original constant is unchanged. This case is distinct from internal turn deletion.

These observations supply the positive-pressure bookkeeping, not the local Fourier difference estimate or its uniform error. In particular pressure equality can occur in (8), so a strict analytic gap cannot be claimed from deletion alone. The local Fourier-tail loss or an original strict boundary separation must provide it.

6. Which geometric action is the target

The original marked geometry identifies F(𝒢)F(\mathcal G) with the complete geometric action of the original strict diagram. The fused tree is a positive comparison calculation. It need not describe the same link, have the same peripheral marking, or have the same augmented complement. This proof uses only its strict pressure and the inequality (2).

Consequently a completed moving-source estimate must continue to use the original primitive lattice, actual circle colors, marked crossing powers and filling action when it invokes the existing source and filling theorems. The final knot-complement volume is that of the original marked filled diagram. No deleted diagram’s volume is substituted for it.
