Two preliminary facts for joint boundary reflection

Private note, 1 November 2026. The action inequality remains open here.

1. The reflected boundary is feasible

Let the marked covariant labels satisfy zi∈[1/2,1]z_i\in[1/2,1], with at most one marked incidence at each actual whole product hub. Write ri=1−zi∈[0,1/2]r_i=1-z_i\in[0,1/2]. Assign half to every other physical internal edge and every ordinary leaf incidence. At each hub of full valence D≥3D\ge3, all pair sums are at most one and the total sum is at least one. These are exactly the closed whole-hub support conditions. The fixed-association interval lemma then supplies all hidden unturned wires. Thus the reflected vector is feasible, independently of whether the original vector was feasible.

If every ri>0r_i>0, choose the free physical labels to be 1/2−ϵ1/2-\epsilon, with one sufficiently small positive ϵ\epsilon. At a marked trivalent hub the total is 1+ri−2ϵ>11+r_i-2\epsilon>1; at higher-valence or unmarked hubs the total also stays above one. Every pair sum is strictly below one. This constructs a strict reflected state in every original binary resolution. The case of a zero reflected mark is covered by closed support and continuity.

2. Marked constant vertices need not have the same tree parity

Put A=Vx⊗VyA=V_x\otimes V_y and B=Vy⊗VxB=V_y\otimes V_x. The six actual quarter-turn types include End⁡(A)→End⁡(B),End⁡(B)→End⁡(A),Hom⁡(A,B)→End⁡(Vx⊗Vx),Hom⁡(B,A)→End⁡(Vy⊗Vy). \begin{array}{rcl} \operatorname{End}(A)&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{End}(B),\\ \operatorname{End}(B)&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{End}(A),\\ \operatorname{Hom}(A,B)&\longrightarrow& \operatorname{End}(V_x\otimes V_x),\\ \operatorname{Hom}(B,A)&\longrightarrow& \operatorname{End}(V_y\otimes V_y). \end{array} \tag{1} The first two arrows stay in a constant opposite-color quotient; the last two lead to a moving equal-color quotient.

Take two old constant trivalent hubs joined by the first physical turn in (1). At the first hub, use an End⁡(A)\operatorname{End}(A) incidence for this internal edge and the other two incidences of types Hom⁡(A,B)\operatorname{Hom}(A,B) and Hom⁡(B,A)\operatorname{Hom}(B,A). Their compatible cyclic product is an endomorphism of AA. Designate one Hom incidence as marked and the other as an ordinary leaf. At the second hub use the reversed orders: the internal incidence is End⁡(B)\operatorname{End}(B), and its two other incidences are Hom⁡(B,A)\operatorname{Hom}(B,A) and Hom⁡(A,B)\operatorname{Hom}(A,B). Its compatible cyclic product is an endomorphism of BB.

Each marked Hom incidence can be joined by its physical turn to a moving equal-color hub, by the last two arrows in (1). Each ordinary Hom leaf is also a legitimate turned integral crossing: it is the turn of an equal-color crossing with the corresponding order. This produces the typed pattern moving−−constant−−constant−−moving, \text{moving}\;--\;\text{constant}\;--\; \text{constant}\;--\;\text{moving}, \tag{2} with one ordinary mixed-Hom leaf at each middle vertex. All three displayed connections are physical quarter-turns. Scalar carriers are the prescribed ones and do not change these domains and codomains.

The two marked old vertices are adjacent and hence have opposite parity in the bipartition of the old region. A whole constant product hub does not have one incidence type which can be iterated uniformly around all of its other incidences: compatible products may contain both opposite-End and mixed-Hom factors. Therefore the four-cycle for an individual turn does not imply that all constant vertices adjacent to moving vertices lie in one graph bipartition.

This is a typed local construction, not an assertion that every choice of exterior closures is a knot. The domains and ordinary leaf types required by the proposed old-region pressure comparison are already realized here, so a same-bipartition restriction cannot be imposed on those regions from the six-type turn table alone.
