Full internal labels at a two-junction component, and a coupled clamping question

Private derivation, 1 November 2026. No repository source is changed.

The present component theorem cuts every retained edge at one half. That cutoff cannot be kept for arbitrary bare components: a genuine two-junction component already has its complete maximum on the cutoff. Keeping that internal label gives an exact Gram identity and a source theorem for this smallest adjacent component, with no boundary-count hypothesis. The last sections isolate a possible general comparison and an exact obstruction to its coordinatewise version.

5. Why the internal half cutoff fails, and the correct local window

The positive complete internal law in (11) has its maximum at k/N=1/2k/N=1/2. Its covariant edge label is r=d−kr=d-k, so it too tends to one half. Choose supported indices with r>MNr>M_N and r/N→1/2r/N\to1/2. Strict approximation of the two original pair fibers gives the same limiting action Φpair\Phi_{\mathrm{pair}} on these individual terms. Hence the complete internal-high sector has no fixed exponential deficit. This is an exact obstruction to extending the published all-retained-low invariant unchanged.

It causes no singularity in the actual local saddle. At every strict old cubic vertex, the three pair sums are less than one, so its total normalized degree is less than 3/23/2. On a fixed sufficiently small original maximizing window, the raw polynomial degree is therefore less than 2N−12N-1. One division by P0P_0 then has quotient degree at most N−2N-2, and ϕσ(eiejek)=ϕ0(eiejek) \phi_\sigma(e_i e_j e_k)=\phi_0(e_i e_j e_k) \tag{23} exactly on that window, even if one of its indices is above MNM_N.

Only circle-incident labels must stay in a compact low interval for the circle trace and angular Gaussian. The internal bare–bare label may cross one half while remaining in a compact subset of (0,1)(0,1). Its exact edge interpolation and bulk expansion remain regular there: HN(s)WNx(s)=GN(Nx)2GN(Nx−s)GN(Nx+s)=1−πs2Ncot⁡(πx)+OR(N−2). \frac{H_N(s)}{W_{Nx}(s)} =\frac{G_N(Nx)^2}{G_N(Nx-s)G_N(Nx+s)} =1-\frac{\pi s^2}{N}\cot(\pi x)+O_R(N^{-2}). \tag{24} The first equality at nonintegral NxNx defines the canonical interpolation. Original strict gate arguments stay positive at this half-label state. Thus the finite primitive window and strict Hessian can be treated by the same grouped contour construction, retaining this full internal coordinate. This paragraph identifies the local interface; it does not assert an independently assembled filled theorem for the enlarged class.

6. Actual typing isolates moving return vertices

The six-type physical turn table has the four-cycle End⁡(++)→Hom⁡(−+,+−)→End⁡(−−)→Hom⁡(+−,−+)→End⁡(++), \operatorname{End}(++)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(-+,+-)\longrightarrow \operatorname{End}(--)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(+-,-+)\longrightarrow \operatorname{End}(++), \tag{25} and interchanges the two opposite-color endomorphism spaces. Only the equal-color endomorphism spaces have the moving relation; all four opposite-order spaces have the constant relation. At a lowered product vertex, compatibility makes its factors share that Casimir class. Hence every original physical turn edge has at least one constant-relation endpoint: moving vertices form an independent set.

This applies to the original reduced junction tree, not to unturned edges introduced in an artificial binary resolution of a high-valence product. In a trivalent physical-turn tree, distinct return vertices therefore cannot be adjacent. Their high incidences end in old vertices, which have at most one high incidence each. This is a useful extra restriction on the return sectors. It does not by itself control the pressures of the old regions joining several return vertices.

7. A coupled pressure obstruction and the remaining general lemma

For a general trivalent component, remove its return vertices and consider one connected remaining old region. Its boundary can have several prescribed high labels z1,…,zrz_1,\ldots,z_r. Let Ψ(z1,…,zr)\Psi(z_1,\ldots,z_r) be its actual old constrained pressure, including every ordinary leaf profile and constant. A sufficient simultaneous-clamping statement would be Ψ(z1,…,zr)≤Ψ(1/2,…,1/2)(zj≥1/2). \Psi(z_1,\ldots,z_r)\le\Psi(1/2,\ldots,1/2) \quad(z_j\ge1/2). \tag{26} If (26) held, all regions could be reoptimized simultaneously at the half interfaces, without adding independent caps. Each return vertex would be replaced by an old vertex with two or three half incidences; its old action is nonnegative whereas its return action is nonpositive. The resulting feasible old state has a saturated pair-sum inequality. The original strict maximum then gives a uniform positive boundary gap. This is only a conditional global argument: (26) has not been established here.

The tempting coordinatewise strengthening of (26) is false even for a genuine positive old region. Take two old trivalent vertices joined by label uu, each with one physical pure leaf, and prescribe the remaining boundary labels z,wz,w. Its coupled pressure is Ψ(z,w)=maxa,b,u{g(a)+g(b)+t(z,a,u)+t(w,b,u)},a,b∈[0,1/2], \Psi(z,w)=\max_{a,b,u} \{g(a)+g(b)+t(z,a,u)+t(w,b,u)\}, \quad a,b\in[0,1/2], \tag{27} with the two original old support domains. At w=1w=1, the second vertex forces b=u=0b=u=0. The first forces a=1−za=1-z, and both cubic actions are zero. Thus Ψ(z,1)=g(1−z),Ψ(3/4,1)=G0>0=Ψ(1/2,1). \Psi(z,1)=g(1-z),\qquad \Psi(3/4,1)=G_0>0=\Psi(1/2,1). \tag{28} This also gives an interior failure. Put w=1−ϵw=1-\epsilon, u=b=3ϵ/4u=b=3\epsilon/4, and a=1−z−3ϵ/8a=1-z-3\epsilon/8. For z=1/2z=1/2 or 3/43/4, all old gate inequalities and both leaf ranges are strict for sufficiently small ϵ>0\epsilon>0, and the action tends to g(1−z)g(1-z). Compactness gives the matching upper limit, since every limiting feasible state at w=1w=1 is the forced state above. Consequently Ψ(3/4,w)>Ψ(1/2,w)\Psi(3/4,w)>\Psi(1/2,w) for all sufficiently large w<1w<1.

Thus the derivative bound for a one-output complete branch cannot be applied one coordinate at a time to a region with several prescribed interfaces. The simultaneous inequality (26) is not contradicted by (28), because it also changes ww. Since the old joint pressure is concave, one possible proof of (26) would be a nonpositive supporting gradient at the all-half boundary. Strict angle feasibility at the all-half assignment is elementary here: each old vertex has at most one marked high-boundary incidence. Set those incidences to 1/21/2, and all other internal and ordinary-leaf labels to 3/83/8. An unmarked cubic has total 9/89/8 and pair sums 3/43/4; a marked one has total 5/45/4 and largest pair sum 7/87/8. Both are strict. What is not yet proved is that the constrained maximizer is strict and supplies positive boundary multipliers. Establishing those properties and the supporting gradient is a new geometric obligation; they do not follow solely from this feasible angle assignment or from the existing one-output probe theorem.

For higher-valence bare vertices, even the trivalent physical-edge support reduction needs further work: a raw low product can have a nonzero return trace without a high exterior label. The regular low-window Fourier comparison remains available, but it is not a global high-sector theorem. No general arbitrary-component moving-meridian bound or volume equality is claimed here.
