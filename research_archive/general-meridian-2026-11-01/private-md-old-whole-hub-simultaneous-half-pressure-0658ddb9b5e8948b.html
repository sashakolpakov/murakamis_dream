Half-boundary pressure comparison for complete whole-hub regions

Private proof, 1 November 2026. This note concerns the old positive source only. It does not estimate moving higher-valence trace terms.

The simultaneous-half comparison extends from trivalent old regions to arbitrary finite trees of old product hubs of valence at least three. The strict witness is constructed on the physical edges first and then extended through each ordered binary resolution. The artificial unturned edges do not receive the physical-edge label 3/83/8.

1. The pressure and the statement

Use the original positive Newton product action m(x,y,b)=J(x+y)+J(b)−J(x)−J(y)−J(b−x)−J(b−y)−J(x+y−b),J(u)=∫0ulog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt, \begin{split} m(x,y,b)={}&J(x+y)+J(b)-J(x)-J(y)\\ &-J(b-x)-J(b-y)-J(x+y-b),\qquad J(u)=\int_0^u\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt, \end{split} \tag{1} on max⁡(x,y)≤b≤x+y≤1\max(x,y)\le b\le x+y\le1, and the pure boundary action g(a)=J(2a)−4J(a),0≤a≤1/2. g(a)=J(2a)-4J(a),\qquad 0\le a\le1/2. \tag{2} Consider a finite connected tree of old whole-hub tensors. A hub has full valence Dv≥3D_v\ge3, counting its external incidences. Each hub-to-hub edge is a physical quarter-turn and identifies equal covariant labels. At an ordinary external incidence put the free profile g(a)+cg(a)+c. A marked incidence has a prescribed covariant label ziz_i and contributes no variable boundary action. Assume that each hub has at most one marked incidence and that there is at least one marked incidence in the region. With no marked incidence there is no boundary comparison to make.

Resolve each old hub by its original ordered positive binary products, retaining their hidden variables and the exact final complementary pairing. The artificial internal edges are unturned; their covariant labels are complementary. Equivalently, after rooting the region, their ordinary output and input coordinates agree without a turn. Let Ψ(𝒛)\Psi(\mathbf z) be the maximum of the sum of (1) and all ordinary boundary profiles, over this full compact wire polytope.

For changing the root, one can keep the very same resolved tree. The cubic action t(x,y,z)=m(x,y,1−z)t(x,y,z)=m(x,y,1-z) is symmetric in its three covariant labels. An unturned edge has complementary labels at its ends, whereas a physical-turn edge has equal labels. These relations remain the same when the direction of recursive evaluation reverses. Thus rooting at another marked terminal is an exact reexpression of the same real action and constraints.

The positive associative identities also imply that this boundary pressure is independent of the chosen resolution. This is an equality of the old finite positive contraction and its coefficient action; it is not an assertion about isotopies of arbitrary tangles. The fixed-label support and resolution statement is proved in notes/actual_conway_reduced_hub_source_normal_form.md, Section 2.

2. A strict witness in every prescribed resolution

For positive input labels a1,…,aka_1,\ldots,a_k, the exact strict product lemma is ai+aj<1(i≠j),maxiai<b<min⁡(1,∑iai). a_i+a_j<1\quad(i\ne j),\qquad \max_i a_i<b<\min\left(1,\sum_i a_i\right). \tag{5} These conditions are necessary and sufficient for strict intermediate gates with output bb, for every fixed binary association.

Here is the constructive part of that lemma. For a child set SS, write LS=max⁡i∈SaiL_S=\max_{i\in S}a_i and US=min⁡(1,∑i∈Sai)U_S=\min(1,\sum_{i\in S}a_i). A nonsingleton child may take any value in (LS,US)(L_S,U_S); a singleton takes its prescribed label. At a parent with target bb, restrict both child values below bb. Their lower sum is less than one, by the pair condition, and their upper sum is greater than bb, by the total condition. Choose a sum strictly between bb and one. If both children are singletons, their fixed sum is already in this interval. The two chosen child values are less than bb, so the parent is strict; repeat down the fixed binary tree. This constructs every hidden wire.

For a whole hub with external covariant labels a1,…,aDa_1,\ldots,a_D, exposing incidence DD makes the ordinary product output b=1−aDb=1-a_D. Thus (5) is equivalent to ai+aj<1(i≠j),∑i=1Dai>1. a_i+a_j<1\quad(i\ne j),\qquad \sum_{i=1}^D a_i>1. \tag{6} It proves strict feasibility in every prescribed resolution of the closed hub, with only the final forced flat pairing omitted.

Now set every marked physical label to half and every other physical edge or ordinary boundary label to 3/83/8. At an unmarked hub all pair sums are 3/43/4, and its total is at least 9/89/8. At a marked hub the largest pair sum is 7/87/8, and its total is at least 5/45/4. Hence (6) holds at every hub. Apply the constructive lemma inside each hub independently, keeping the already assigned physical edge labels. It supplies a global strict state in the fixed original resolution. The same inequalities have positive margins under small independent changes of all marked labels, so the feasible boundary domain contains an open neighborhood of 𝒛0\mathbf z_0.

This is the point at which the higher-valence argument differs from assigning 3/83/8 to an artificially resolved trivalent graph. Only physical edges receive that assignment. Hidden ordinary output wires are constructed from (5); complementary covariant coordinates on unturned edges are then automatic.

3. The constrained maximum is strict and analytic

Root the whole region at a marked incidence. A local hub is an ordered product of its other inputs with ordinary output equal to one minus its parent covariant label. The binary gates inside it are unturned. At a physical edge the actual turn supplies the complement before the next hub. Marked terminals other than the root remain fixed at half, without a leaf volume atom.

Apply the original nonvertex-flat barrier to the segment toward the strict witness. An atom (0,a,1−a)(0,a,1-a), 0<a<10<a<1, has inward derivative +∞+\infty; positive atoms and vertex-flat atoms have finite derivatives. No negative infinite term can cancel it.

An ordinary product output zero forces its two inputs to zero. An output one either gives two interior inputs summing to one, hence a nonvertex-flat atom, or has an endpoint input through which the descent continues. A physical turn exchanges zero and one; an unturned artificial edge leaves that ordinary endpoint unchanged. The descent strictly follows child edges in the finite resolved tree. It cannot end at a fixed half terminal or at the fixed root output. It must reach an ordinary leaf endpoint or a nonvertex-flat interior pair. An ordinary leaf at either zero or half supplies its own nonvertex-flat atom. Thus all variable wires and ordinary leaves are interior at the maximum.

With these wires interior, the original gate-face check applies unchanged: setting any of b−x,b−y,x+y−b,1−x−yb-x,b-y,x+y-b,1-x-y to zero gives a nonvertex-flat tetrahedron. Every retained gate is therefore strict. This argument is exactly the old boundary proof in notes/actual_conway_boundary_volume_relation.md, Section 4, with fixed half terminals added and both kinds of internal edge kept explicit.

The sum of the strict gate Hessians is negative definite on all resolved free coordinates; each variable occurs at a gate, and its three-label angle map has no kernel. Ordinary leaf Hessians reinforce this. The same statement holds when the marked boundary labels are allowed to vary: each of them also occurs at a gate. Consequently the constrained optimizer is unique, its implicit continuation is analytic, and the Schur complement giving the Hessian of Ψ\Psi is negative definite near 𝒛0\mathbf z_0. Compactness keeps this local critical point as the global constrained maximum near the all-half boundary.

5. Simultaneous comparison and its strictness

The joint pressure is concave: combine feasible maximizing resolved states by convex interpolation. Its supporting plane at 𝒛0\mathbf z_0, together with (3), gives (4).

This also applies at a closed feasible endpoint. For 𝒗=𝒛−𝒛0≥0\mathbf v=\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0\ge0, concavity gives Ψ(𝒛)−Ψ(𝒛0)≤Ψ(𝒛0+t𝒗)−Ψ(𝒛0)t. \Psi(\mathbf z)-\Psi(\mathbf z_0) \le\frac{\Psi(\mathbf z_0+t\mathbf v)-\Psi(\mathbf z_0)}t. \tag{11} For small positive tt, the right side is evaluated on the analytic strict branch. Its limit is ∇Ψ(𝒛0)⋅𝒗≤0\nabla\Psi(\mathbf z_0)\cdot\mathbf v \le0, proving the endpoint inequality without differentiating a degenerate optimizer. If 𝒗≠0\mathbf v\ne0, the negative definite Hessian gives a strictly smaller right side for sufficiently small positive tt. This proves the strict assertion in the theorem.

The gradient is in fact strictly negative at a marked incidence unless the whole region is one trivalent hub with two ordinary leaf inputs. In every other case the final binary product has a proper composite child, so one bound Bi≤XB_i\le X in (10) is strict. In the exceptional two-leaf product the derivative at half is zero, while the strict inequality in (4) away from half still follows from (11).

6. What this does not prove about moving higher-valence hubs

The theorem is a geometric inequality for the old positive pressure, with every hidden resolution variable retained. It can compare old regions with several high boundary labels without assigning them independent capped volumes. The requirement of at most one marked incidence per actual hub is essential to the strict all-half witness: two prescribed half incidences at the same hub already saturate one pair inequality.

This does not supply the missing moving-hub comparison. A moving higher-valence trace can have additional terms with every exterior index low. An artificial unturned resolution can also have adjacent moving additional terms, with complementary labels on their common edge. Such terms do not furnish the two-high/one-low pattern used by the trivalent replacement argument. Nothing above discards them or adds a replacement leaf profile to each of their arms.

Accordingly no new full-source, meridian-profile, or filled-volume class follows from this old-pressure theorem alone. The remaining problem is a whole-hub moving estimate compatible with this coupled old pressure.
