Physical edge labels at the complete maximum of an old bare tree

Private proof, 1 November 2026. This is an old positive-pressure statement. It supplies a regular-window fact and makes no estimate for additional moving-quotient trace terms.

1. Statement and conventions

Let a finite connected tree have actual bare product hubs of full valence at least three. Its hub-to-hub edges are physical quarter-turns. Every external incidence carries a rigid boundary profile g(a)+cg(a)+c, with 0≤a≤1/20\le a\le1/2, where g(a)=J(2a)−4J(a),J(u)=∫0ulog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt. g(a)=J(2a)-4J(a),\qquad J(u)=\int_0^u\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt. \tag{1} The constants are retained. Resolve each hub by its original ordered positive binary products; artificial edges inside this resolution are unturned and have complementary covariant end labels. Physical quarter-turn edges have equal covariant end labels.

The original complete positive contraction has a unique strict maximum. At that maximum, every physical hub-to-hub covariant label zez_e satisfies 0<ze≤1/2. 0<z_e\le1/2. \tag{2} Equality occurs if and only if the entire component consists of two trivalent hubs, each with two external rigid leaf incidences. In that exception the single internal label is exactly half. In every other component, all physical internal labels are strictly below half.

The statement allows arbitrary fixed valences and arbitrary fixed additive constants. It concerns physical edges only: hidden unturned-resolution labels are not required to be below half.

2. What remains after an actual edge is cut

Cut a physical edge and use its incidence as the root on each side. At a root hub of full valence DD, there are D−1≥2D-1\ge2 ordered inputs. An external input is a rigid leaf of type LL. An input coming from a child hub is the actual quarter-turn of that child’s unturned product. By induction, the child product has type AA, with full strict ordinary output interval (0,1)(0,1), and its turn again has type AA. Thus each rooted side is an unturned product of at least two L/AL/A inputs. This statement retains the actual order and every physical turn; it is not an inference from commutativity of a scalar trace.

Let fA(b)f_A(b) and fB(b)f_B(b) be the two rooted positive profiles at their ordinary outputs. They retain their full subtrees and all hidden product variables. The strict support, endpoint barrier and negative Hessian arguments give continuous profiles on [0,1][0,1], analytic strictly concave profiles on (0,1)(0,1), and unique strict constrained optimizers there. These are the original profile results for unturned products with L/AL/A children; their hypotheses have just been checked recursively for these particular sides.

The covariant label zz of the cut edge is the same at both ends. The ordinary output of each rooted side is therefore 1−z1-z. The exact optimized cut action is 𝒫(z)=fA(1−z)+fB(1−z). \mathcal P(z)=f_A(1-z)+f_B(1-z). \tag{3} No external leaf or cap is inserted in (3). Maximizing this action and then the two conditional subtrees recovers the original whole positive contraction.

4. Maximizing the physical edge

For z>1/2z>1/2, the ordinary output 1−z1-z lies below half. Thus 𝒫′(z)=−fA′(1−z)−fB′(1−z)<0. \mathcal P'(z) =-f_A'(1-z)-f_B'(1-z)<0. \tag{8} No strict interior maximum can occur there. At z=1/2z=1/2, the same derivative is strictly negative unless both rooted sides are single trivalent hubs with two rigid leaves. In that nonexceptional case, moving zz slightly below half increases the action, so its unique maximum lies below half.

If both sides are the exceptional two-leaf product, then 𝒫(z)=2f2(1−z)+c\mathcal P(z)=2f_2(1-z)+c. The profile f2f_2 is strictly concave and symmetric under b↦1−bb\mapsto1-b, with derivative zero at half, so its unique maximum is exactly z=1/2z=1/2. This also proves the asserted converse and the complete equality classification. The original strict maximum keeps the label away from zero in every case.

Apply this argument to each physical edge of the same whole optimizer. The conditional profiles in (3) are exact, so the separate edge arguments do not replace different neighbors by independent capped maxima. This proves (2) simultaneously.

For any fixed nonexceptional tree, finiteness now gives a compact window in which all physical internal labels and all original leaf labels stay in (0,1/2)(0,1/2). The hidden unturned product labels and all factorial arguments retain their original strict bulk windows. In the exceptional two-hub tree, its physical internal label stays in a compact interval inside (0,1)(0,1) containing half; it need not be forced to one side in a complex continuation.

This is only a statement about the original positive optimizer and its local continuation. It neither excludes moving additional terms away from that window nor supplies a bound for their total contribution. In particular it does not repair the valence-nine pointwise moving/complete trace comparison.
