Bounded carries, signed moments, and the missing normal-family estimate

31 October 2026. Private continuation only. The input is the exact filter in /private/tmp/md-main-meridian-finite-source-and-regular-filter-inventory.md, together with the existing regular-period and scaled-profile notes. No repository or public file is changed.

This report proves a finite signed-moment sufficient condition for local boundedness of the actual regular microscopic profile. It also shows that bounded carry width, the complete scalar rate, the actual root operator and affine source, all color data, and all Mellin quantum jets do not alone imply that boundedness. The counterexample is a comparison family, not a second actual knot invariant: it does not preserve the literal main-meridian finite-cut quantum-normal lift.

1. The all-integer filter and the carry decomposition

Fix a knot and a braid presentation. Let qN=eπi/Nq_N=e^{\pi i/N}, QN=qN2Q_N=q_N^2, Y=M2Y=M^2, and let fN(Y)f_N(Y) be its actual reduced ADO Laurent polynomial. Write KN=fN(1)K_N=f_N(1). For the knot class under consideration, the proved scalar theorem gives

N−1log⁡|KN|→v>0. N^{-1}\log|K_N|\longrightarrow v>0. \tag{1}

In particular KN≠0K_N\ne0 for all sufficiently large NN. All following normalized statements refer to those levels.

Let INI_N interpolate at the nontrivial NNth roots into degree at most N−2N-2, and put

SN(Y)=YN−1Y−1,PN=INfN,RN(Y)=fN(Y)−PN(Y)SN(Y). S_N(Y)=\frac{Y^N-1}{Y-1},\qquad P_N=I_Nf_N,\qquad R_N(Y)=\frac{f_N(Y)-P_N(Y)}{S_N(Y)}. \tag{2}

This is the original ordinary-color regularization. To check its monomial rule, divide an arbitrary integer exponent as ℓ=kN+t\ell=kN+t, 0≤t<N0\le t<N, and write U=YNU=Y^N. On the interpolation points YℓY^\ell equals YtY^t. The interpolant is YtY^t if t≤N−2t\le N-2, and is YN−1−SNY^{N-1}-S_N if t=N−1t=N-1. Therefore

Yℓ−IN(Yℓ)SN(Y)=Yt(Y−1)Gk(U)+𝟏t=N−1,Gk(U)=Uk−1U−1. \frac{Y^\ell-I_N(Y^\ell)}{S_N(Y)} =Y^t(Y-1)G_k(U)+\mathbf1_{t=N-1},\qquad G_k(U)=\frac{U^k-1}{U-1}. \tag{3}

For negative kk, the exact Laurent formula is Gk=−∑j=k−1UjG_k=-\sum_{j=k}^{-1}U^j. Thus, for example, 𝒟N(Y−1)=Y−1\mathcal D_N(Y^{-1})=Y^{-1} and 𝒟N(Y−N)=−Y−N(Y−1)\mathcal D_N(Y^{-N})=-Y^{-N}(Y-1). No sampled zero is divided in this argument.

Write the finite carry decomposition uniquely as

fN(Y)=∑k∈𝒦YkNpk,N(Y),deg⁡pk,N<N, f_N(Y)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}Y^{kN}p_{k,N}(Y),\qquad \deg p_{k,N}<N, \tag{4}

padding unused carries with zero. The exact cut-source degree bound allows one fixed finite set 𝒦⊂ℤ\mathcal K\subset\mathbb Z for all NN. If the braid has bb strands and LL crossings, its exponents obey

−(N−1)(L+b−1)2≤ℓ≤(N−1)(L−b+1)2. -\frac{(N-1)(L+b-1)}2\le\ell \le\frac{(N-1)(L-b+1)}2. \tag{5}

This remains true before cancellations in the finite q-binomial expanded cut state. It does not bound the absolute values of its signed coefficients relative to KNK_N.

At every root point, (4) has the same values as pΣ,N=∑kpk,Np_{\Sigma,N}=\sum_kp_{k,N}, whose degree is below NN. Interpolating those values gives the exact identities

∑kpk,N(Y)=PN(Y)+RN(1)SN(Y), \sum_kp_{k,N}(Y)=P_N(Y)+R_N(1)S_N(Y), \tag{6}

RN(Y)=RN(1)+(Y−1)∑kGk(YN)pk,N(Y). \boxed{\quad R_N(Y)=R_N(1)+(Y-1) \sum_kG_k(Y^N)p_{k,N}(Y). \quad} \tag{7}

For (6), the difference from PNP_N is a constant times SNS_N; its leading coefficient is the sum of the YN−1Y^{N-1} carry coefficients, which is RN(1)R_N(1) by (3). Subtract (6) from (4), use Uk−1=(U−1)Gk(U)U^k-1=(U-1)G_k(U), and divide by SNS_N to obtain (7). These formulas retain all negative and positive carries.

2. A finite set of quantitative signed-moment estimates suffices

Use the microscopic coordinate Y=ez/NY=e^{z/N} and the actual centered regular profile

ρN(z)=eαNzNRN(ez/N)KN,αN=N−12N. \rho_N(z)=e^{\alpha_Nz}\frac{NR_N(e^{z/N})}{K_N}, \qquad \alpha_N=\frac{N-1}{2N}. \tag{8}

Define ak,N(z)=pk,N(ez/N)/KNa_{k,N}(z)=p_{k,N}(e^{z/N})/K_N and cN=NRN(1)/KNc_N=NR_N(1)/K_N. If s=|𝒦|s=|\mathcal K|, define the signed carry moments

Mj,N(z)=∑k∈𝒦kjak,N(z),0≤j<s. M_{j,N}(z)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}k^j a_{k,N}(z), \qquad 0\le j<s. \tag{9}

They are central-carry moments, not derivatives in the quantum parameter and not absolute coefficient moments. Their definition uses the unique Euclidean carry representatives in (4).

The zeroth moment is already controlled. Set πN(z)=PN(ez/N)/KN\pi_N(z)=P_N(e^{z/N})/K_N and HN(z)=sinh⁡(z/2)/(Nsinh⁡(z/(2N)))H_N(z)=\sinh(z/2)/(N\sinh(z/(2N))), with its entire removable values. Equation (6) gives

M0,N(z)=πN(z)+cNeαNzHN(z). M_{0,N}(z)=\pi_N(z)+c_Ne^{\alpha_Nz}H_N(z). \tag{10}

The ordinary-color bounds and (1) imply πN→0\pi_N\to0 locally uniformly with every fixed derivative and cN→1c_N\to1. Thus M0,NM_{0,N} is locally uniformly bounded and converges to (ez−1)/z(e^z-1)/z, with value 11 at zero.

Signed-moment criterion. If the remaining s−1s-1 functions Mj,NM_{j,N}, 1≤j<s1\le j<s, are locally uniformly bounded on a fixed complex disk, then so is ρN\rho_N there. Only the number of these estimates, not their truth, follows from the finite carry width.

For the proof, the fixed matrix Vj,k=kjV_{j,k}=k^j is an invertible Vandermonde matrix. Hence the ak,Na_{k,N} are fixed linear combinations of the moments, with coefficients independent of NN. Equation (7) reads

ρN(z)=eαNz[cN+N(ez/N−1)∑kGk(ez)ak,N(z)]. \rho_N(z)=e^{\alpha_Nz}\left[ c_N+N(e^{z/N}-1) \sum_kG_k(e^z)a_{k,N}(z)\right]. \tag{11}

On |z|≤R|z|\le R, the finitely many functions Gk(ez)G_k(e^z) are bounded uniformly, including negative kk and ez=1e^z=1, and N|ez/N−1|≤ReR/2N|e^{z/N}-1|\le R e^{R/2} for N≥2N\ge2. This proves the criterion with an explicit constant depending only on RR and V−1V^{-1}. Cauchy’s inequality then bounds every fixed derivative on a smaller disk.

If the moments converge locally uniformly, the same fixed inverse gives limits aka_k, and (11) gives the actual limit formula

ρ(z)=ez/2[1+z∑kGk(ez)ak(z)],∑kak(z)=ez−1z. \rho(z)=e^{z/2}\left[1+z\sum_kG_k(e^z)a_k(z)\right], \qquad \sum_ka_k(z)=\frac{e^z-1}{z}. \tag{12}

Weyl symmetry makes any actual resulting limit even, as in the existing scaled-profile theorem. Neither boundedness nor convergence of the nonzero carry moments has been established by the scalar volume theorem.

A still weaker exact target is the single signed carry combination ∑kGk(ez)ak,N(z)\sum_kG_k(e^z)a_{k,N}(z). Its local boundedness is equivalent to that of ρN\rho_N: (11) proves one direction, and division on a fixed surrounding circle followed by the maximum principle proves the other, since N(ez/N−1)/zN(e^{z/N}-1)/z is uniformly a unit on each fixed disk for large NN. The moment criterion is stronger but splits this target into a fixed finite number of explicitly defined signed estimates. Values of those moments only at z=0z=0 are not the asserted disk estimates.

3. Algebraic uniqueness does not bound these moments

The existing central-freedom construction can be sharpened into an explicit failure of local boundedness while retaining all color data and their Mellin quantum jets. Let aNa_N be any scalar and define the comparison family

hN(U)=1+aN(U+U−1−2),f̃N(Y)=hN(YN)fN(Y). h_N(U)=1+a_N(U+U^{-1}-2),\qquad \widetilde f_N(Y)=h_N(Y^N)f_N(Y). \tag{13}

The multiplier has hN(1)=1h_N(1)=1 and is reciprocal in UU. It is never the zero Laurent polynomial. Since every root shift fixes UU, it commutes with every specified root difference operator. Thus f̃N\widetilde f_N and fNf_N have the same entire all-level annihilator ideal, meaning the fixed Ore operators evaluated at the roots qNq_N, not a supplied off-root finite-cut lift. The reverse inclusion follows as well: if an operator kills f̃N\widetilde f_N, its product with the nonzero hNh_N shows that it kills fNf_N as a rational function at that level. No operator is reconstructed from altered scalar values.

Every ordinary and complete orbit value is unchanged. Therefore the fixed-color functions Cr(q)C_r(q), their generic recurrence, their finite-start Mellin source, and all their quantum jets are unchanged. The actual interpolant PNP_N is unchanged. The new regular remainder is exactly

R̃N=hNRN+(hN−1)pN,pN=PN/SN. \widetilde R_N=h_NR_N+(h_N-1)p_N, \qquad p_N=P_N/S_N. \tag{14}

This is Laurent polynomial, because (hN−1)/(U−1)(h_N-1)/(U-1) is Laurent polynomial. If ANRN=bNA_NR_N=b_N and ANpN=−bNA_Np_N=-b_N are the original rootwise affine equations, then ANR̃N=hNbN−(hN−1)bN=bNA_N\widetilde R_N=h_Nb_N-(h_N-1)b_N=b_N exactly. The ordinary source, including all its coefficients, is retained.

Since hN(U)−1h_N(U)-1 vanishes to order two at U=1U=1, (13) even preserves the first meridian jet of fNf_N at every root orbit point. In particular it preserves RNR_N at every one of those points, the complete derivative of RNR_N, and the entire marked ramified normal fibre (a0,b0)(a_0,b_0) of (Y−1)fN(Y-1)f_N. These observations do not extend to its whole central profile or to the same off-root main-meridian quantum-normal lift.

To compute the profile change, put gN(z)=fN(ez/N)/KNg_N(z)=f_N(e^{z/N})/K_N. The exact scaled identity is gN=HNρN+πNg_N=H_N\rho_N+\pi_N. Here gN(0)=1g_N(0)=1, HN(0)=1H_N(0)=1, and HN′(0)=0H_N'(0)=0. Equation (13) gives g̃N(z)=[1+aN(ez+e−z−2)]gN(z)\widetilde g_N(z)=[1+a_N(e^z+e^{-z}-2)]g_N(z), while πN\pi_N is unchanged. Differentiating twice at zero yields

ρ̃N″(0)=ρN″(0)+2aN. \boxed{\quad \widetilde\rho_N''(0)=\rho_N''(0)+2a_N. \quad} \tag{15}

The equal complete values and first derivatives ensure that no other term survives in this difference. Equivalently, divide g̃N−gN\widetilde g_N-g_N by the same unit HNH_N near zero.

Choose

aN=N−12ρN″(0). a_N=N-\tfrac12\rho_N''(0). \tag{16}

Then ρ̃N″(0)=2N\widetilde\rho_N''(0)=2N. Cauchy’s derivative estimate proves that the modified profiles cannot be locally uniformly bounded on any disk centered at zero. For actual cyclotomic coefficients these aNa_N are algebraic scalars; no transcendental growth factor is needed for the example. Finitely many small levels can be left unchanged.

The carry width in (13) grows by only two: multiplication by U+U−1−2U+U^{-1}-2 shifts the original carries by −1,0,1-1,0,1. Thus a fixed finite carry bound does not prevent the example. Even the coarse explicit degree enclosure (5) cannot by itself exclude it. If desired, choose a fixed braid presentation with two inserted cancelling crossing pairs. Its crossing count rises by four, so each side of (5) acquires 2(N−1)2(N-1) of slack, at least NN for every N≥2N\ge2. The comparison polynomial (13) then fits that same presentation’s enclosure. This changes no knot, color value, or invariant; it only shows the limitation of the enclosure as a proposed uniqueness/conditioning hypothesis.

The example is not claimed to be the actual finite-cut state of that presentation. Its coefficients do not preserve the literal crossing weights or the supplied whole main-meridian preoperator and normal source. Those are precisely the data a successful analytic transport must still use. The example refutes an inference from the weaker listed algebraic data, not a theorem about the fully specified knot family.

4. Why the existing quantum-jet theorem does not repair the estimate

The bounded-jet Mellin theorem fixes the rational function ZN(X)Z_N(X) from the actual color recurrence, its prefix, pole lattice, and a bounded number of quantum jet levels. The families in (13) have exactly the same Cr(q)C_r(q), so they produce the same ZNZ_N and pass all of those tests. They are not competing Mellin solutions. They differ in the unsampled central meridian direction, and (15) makes that difference quantitatively visible.

This agrees with the earlier polarization calculation: rational main-meridian functions vary YNY^N with TN=1T^N=1, whereas the rational Mellin realization varies XNX^N with SXN=1S_X^N=1. Their finite Fourier correspondence retains the orbit and residue numerator, not the whole main-meridian normal profile.

The source-corrected main quantum-normal theorem can prohibit nonconstant central multipliers when its selected symbol is nonzero and both sections lift through the same whole preoperator. That theorem uses different information from the color/Mellin jets just discussed. Our comparison family does not assert such a lift. Even a uniqueness theorem for a fully specified lift would still require quantitative conditioning to turn an approximate or estimated source into uniform bounds for (9).

The finite-carry formulation therefore leaves a concrete analytic target: bound the actual signed functions Mj,NM_{j,N} on a fixed microscopic disk, or directly bound the single combination in (11), with constants relative to KNK_N. Termwise polynomial filter bounds or an absolute cut-state sum do not give this relative control. No such estimate is inferred here.

5. A quantitative normal-connection criterion

The source-corrected connection does give a useful conditional route, provided its actual marked lattice is controlled uniformly. Here is a precise elementary version. For all sufficiently large NN, suppose that on a fixed microscopic disk |z|<R|z|<R the actual source-normal section has coordinates vN(z)v_N(z) in a holomorphic frame of rank at most a fixed rr. Require that these are frames of the supplied meridian source problem, with its original normalization, and that the connection equation and the scalar observation take the form

vN′(z)=AN(z)vN(z),ρN(z)=ℓN(z)vN(z)+eN(z). v_N'(z)=A_N(z)v_N(z),\qquad \rho_N(z)=\ell_N(z)v_N(z)+e_N(z). \tag{17}

Assume on that disk, in fixed compatible norms,

∥AN∥≤C,∥ℓN∥≤C,∥eN∥≤C,∥vN(0)∥≤C. \|A_N\|\le C,\qquad \|\ell_N\|\le C,\qquad \|e_N\|\le C,\qquad \|v_N(0)\|\le C. \tag{18}

Then the profiles are locally uniformly bounded, with ∥ρN(z)∥≤C2eC|z|+C\|\rho_N(z)\|\le C^2 e^{C|z|}+C. Indeed integrate the holomorphic linear equation along the radial segment from zero to zz and apply the elementary integral Gronwall inequality. The bound is independent of NN and the actual rank. Cauchy’s inequality gives all fixed scalar derivative bounds on smaller disks. This proves normality, not uniqueness of a subsequential limit; convergence of ANA_N, the observation and the normalized initial vector would also give convergence by the same integral equation.

For the source-corrected construction, an invertible kernel-to-cokernel symbol in uniformly controlled lattice frames converts its first-order equation into (17). The coordinate U=ezU=e^z absorbs the U∂UU\partial_U derivative. One must bound the inverse symbol, the remaining connection/source matrices, the scalar observation and the normalized initial vector; all of these enter (18). An arbitrary singular change of frame can destroy these bounds, which is why the actual marked lattice is part of the criterion. A single nonzero selected symbol is enough for the central-uniqueness argument but is not, by itself, the stated matrix inverse estimate. The Mellin observable cannot be substituted for this meridian lattice without the unproved source-retaining comparison.

Thus (18) is one concrete operator-side sufficient condition, while (9) supplies a direct finite-source condition. Neither is an automatic consequence of algebraic uniqueness, bounded quantum-jet order, or the scalar volume limit.
