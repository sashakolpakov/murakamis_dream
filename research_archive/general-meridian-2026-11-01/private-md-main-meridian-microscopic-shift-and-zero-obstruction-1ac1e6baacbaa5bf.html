The actual complete-point shift ratio and nearby zeros

31 October 2026. Private mathematical continuation. This strengthens the already recorded obstruction to a uniform main-meridian WKB expression for the reduced ADO state. It uses the now-proved positive volume rate, the exact ordinary-color anchor, and an explicit degree bound from the original finite crossing matrices. It does not construct a replacement knot invariant or identify a classical A-polynomial factor.

1. The state and the exact microscopic shift

Fix a zero-framed knot K for which the actual all-level theorem gives

qN=eπi/N,1Nlog⁡|KN(K)|→v=V2π>0. q_N=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad \frac1N\log|K_N(K)|\longrightarrow v=\frac{V}{2\pi}>0. \tag{1} This includes the covered strict long-twist knots and the knots obtained from them by the ordered net-weight normalization. The knot and all integer twist weights are fixed before N grows.

Let F_N(M) be the actual reduced literature ADO state, in the marked coordinate

x=qNα,M=−qNx. x=q_N^\alpha,\qquad M=-q_Nx. \tag{2} The actual quotient-color theorem states

FN(−qNj)=Cj(qN),1≤j≤N,FN(1)=KN(K),C1(q)=1. F_N(-q_N^j)=C_j(q_N),\quad 1\le j\le N,\qquad F_N(1)=K_N(K),\qquad C_1(q)=1. \tag{3} Here F_N is the reduced, zero-framed state. It is not an unnormalized state, and no crossing Cartan or framing monomial is omitted from it.

The source proof in notes/even_meridian_norm_descent.md, Sections 1–2, gives, at every level and both parities,

FN(−M)=FN(M). F_N(-M)=F_N(M). \tag{4} For specificity, its exact insertion is a function of Y=M^2, and its N-fold cyclic norm is one. The residual framing exponent there is κD=w−b+1\kappa_D=w-b+1, which is even for a b-strand braid closing to a knot: its permutation has sign (−1)b−1=(−1)w(-1)^{b-1}=(-1)^w. Thus the framing norm qN−NκD=(−1)κDq_N^{-N\kappa_D}=(-1)^{\kappa_D} equals one. Alternatively, the source’s exponent of x is even term by term after the conserved position coboundary is summed. This is the precise reason that (4) has no additional sign. It applies to the zero-framed reduced state, not to the full modified state, whose parity is different at even N.

Combining (3)–(4) proves the actual identity

FN(qN)=FN(−qN)=1,FN(qN)FN(1)=1KN(K). F_N(q_N)=F_N(-q_N)=1, \qquad \frac{F_N(q_N)}{F_N(1)}=\frac1{K_N(K)}. \tag{5} The denominator is nonzero for all sufficiently large N by (1). Hence

limN→∞1Nlog⁡|FN(qN)FN(1)|=−v. \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N \log\left|\frac{F_N(q_N)}{F_N(1)}\right|=-v. \tag{6} The shift in (5) is the genuine meridian shift M->q_N M. In squared coordinates, writing fN(Y)=FN(M)f_N(Y)=F_N(M), it is Y->Q_NY with QN=qN2Q_N=q_N^2, and fN(QN)=1f_N(Q_N)=1. There is no parity exception.

The qualitative failure of a uniform nonzero WKB amplitude across these two points was already explained in regularized_kashaev_period.md, Section 7, and formal_a_curve_period_torsor.md, Section 3. Equation (6) supplies its exact exponential rate for the knots now covered by the volume theorem. Sections 2–3 give a further quantitative consequence for the zeros of the actual Laurent polynomial.

2. An explicit linear Laurent-degree bound

Choose any fixed braid presentation of K with b strands and L crossings. Let L_+ and L_- count its positive and negative crossings, so L=L++L−L=L_++L_- and w=L+−L−w=L_+-L_-. Put d=N-1.

Use precisely the finite lift of finite_sum_annihilator.md, Section 1. After removing the common Cartan scalar, the positive crossing is R̃=DΘ\widetilde R=D\Theta, where the diagonal D has x-power x−a−bx^{-a-b}. A nonzero summand of Θ\Theta shifts an input (a,b) to (a-k,b+k), with

0≤a,b≤d,0≤k≤min⁡(a,d−b). 0\le a,b\le d,\qquad 0\le k\le\min(a,d-b). \tag{7} The product of k unrolled E factors has Laurent x-exponents between -k and k. The diagonal sees the same conserved sum a+b before and after the shift. Thus every positive crossing entry has exponents in

[−a−b−k,−a−b+k]⊂[−2d,0]. [-a-b-k,-a-b+k]\subset[-2d,0]. \tag{8} Indeed a+b+k<=2d follows from k<=d-b, and -a-b+k<=0 follows from k<=a. The actual inverse is Θ−1D−1\Theta^{-1}D^{-1}, with the same allowed shifts and the same x-degree range of its E factors. Every negative crossing entry therefore has exponents in

[a+b−k,a+b+k]⊂[0,2d]. [a+b-k,a+b+k]\subset[0,2d]. \tag{9} Permutation and transpose conventions only permute these entries. There is no denominator depending on x in this matrix formula: the factorial denominators depend on q and are nonzero at q_N. Products, sums and the finite trace cannot enlarge the resulting exponent interval.

The residual zero-framing factor is x^(wd). Each of the b-1 pivotal traces contributes the same x-power x^(-d); its remaining dependence on the head index is a power of q. Thus the full fixed shift is x^(d(w-b+1)). Combining it with (8)–(9) puts the support of F_N in

[−d(L+b−1),d(L−b+1)]. [-d(L+b-1),\ d(L-b+1)]. \tag{10} The substitution x=-q_N^{-1}M multiplies coefficients by nonzero scalars and leaves this exponent interval unchanged. The removed common Cartan was already canceled by the exact zero-framing normalization; it must not be inserted a second time into (10).

Let m_N and n_N be the least and greatest actual nonzero Laurent exponents after any cancellations. Then

DN=nN−mN≤2L(N−1). D_N=n_N-m_N\le 2L(N-1). \tag{11} This bound is for the actual reduced state and holds at every level. The number of state-sum terms need not be bounded to prove it. By (4), all actual exponents are even, so the corresponding Laurent spread in Y=M^2 obeys

DNY≤L(N−1). D_N^{Y}\le L(N-1). \tag{12} For a positive-volume knot L>0. Also D_N>0 for all sufficiently large N: a Laurent monomial has equal modulus at 1 and q_N and cannot satisfy (1) and (5).

3. Actual zeros approach the complete point at distance O(1/N)

Define

δN=|qN−1|=2sin⁡π2N,rN=min⁡{|z−1|:FN(z)=0,z≠0}. \delta_N=|q_N-1|=2\sin\frac{\pi}{2N},\qquad r_N=\min\{|z-1|: F_N(z)=0,\ z\ne0\}. \tag{13} For sufficiently large N the zero set in (13) is nonempty. Indeed PN(M)=M−mNFN(M)P_N(M)=M^{-m_N}F_N(M) is a degree-D_N polynomial with nonzero constant term and D_N>0. Neither 1 nor q_N is a root.

Factor P_N with multiplicities as aN∏j=1DN(M−zj)a_N\prod_{j=1}^{D_N}(M-z_j). Since |q_N|=1, the Laurent monomial has exactly unit modulus between the two evaluation points. If r_N>delta_N, the triangle inequality gives

log⁡|FN(qN)FN(1)|=∑j=1DNlog⁡|qN−zj||1−zj|≥DNlog⁡(1−δNrN). \begin{aligned} \log\left|\frac{F_N(q_N)}{F_N(1)}\right| &=\sum_{j=1}^{D_N}\log\frac{|q_N-z_j|}{|1-z_j|}\\ &\ge D_N\log\left(1-\frac{\delta_N}{r_N}\right). \end{aligned} \tag{14} There is no generic-position or simple-root assumption. By (5), the left side is -log|K_N|. Solving (14) yields

rN≤δN1−|KN|−1/DN≤δN1−|KN|−1/[2L(N−1)]. r_N\le \frac{\delta_N}{1-|K_N|^{-1/D_N}} \le\frac{\delta_N}{1-|K_N|^{-1/[2L(N-1)]}}. \tag{15} If r_N<=delta_N, the second bound in (15) holds automatically, because |K_N|>1 for large N. Thus it is an unconditional eventual bound. Using (1), (15) implies

limsupN→∞NrN≤π1−exp⁡(−V/(4πL)). \limsup_{N\to\infty} N r_N \le \frac{\pi}{1-\exp(-V/(4\pi L))}. \tag{16} This is a proof about zeros of the actual knot state, not an artificial family having the same scalar growth.

The exact squared-meridian descent improves the displayed constant. Apply the same argument to f_N(Y), the point Q_N, and the degree bound (12). A zero ζN\zeta_N satisfies

limsup⁡N|ζN−1|≤2π1−exp⁡(−V/(2πL)). \limsup N|\zeta_N-1| \le\frac{2\pi}{1-\exp(-V/(2\pi L))}. \tag{17} Choose its square root z_N near 1. Evenness says that this is a zero of F_N, and |zN−1|=|ζN−1|/|zN+1||z_N-1|=|\zeta_N-1|/|z_N+1|, with |zN+1|→2|z_N+1|\to2. Consequently

limsupN→∞NrN≤π1−exp⁡(−V/(2πL)). \boxed{\displaystyle \limsup_{N\to\infty} N r_N \le \frac{\pi}{1-\exp(-V/(2\pi L))}.} \tag{18} Any fixed braid presentation gives a valid L in these bounds; the proof does not require a minimal-crossing presentation. The statements do not count the zeros in this shrinking disk, describe their limiting angles, or identify their algebraic factors with the classical A-polynomial.

4. What this rules out, including the full normalization

There cannot be a fixed complex neighborhood U of M=1 on which every sufficiently large F_N is nonvanishing. Thus the unrevised sufficient ratio premise in Section 3 of the private main-meridian interface note, when U contains the complete point, is impossible for the actual reduced state with positive volume. Already (5)–(6) exclude a nonzero finite limit for its one-step ratio at 1. The zero theorem strengthens that pointwise contradiction to failure of any fixed zero-free neighborhood. The same conditional ratio lemma remains correct on another domain; its premise at the complete point must not be assumed for F_N.

A scalar normalization independent of M does not change the ratio in (5) or any zero. Nor does a fixed rational/algebraic gauge that is a holomorphic nonzero unit on a common neighborhood of (q,M)=(1,1): its ratio at the two points tends to one, and it cannot remove the zeros in (18). A gauge with moving zeros or poles requires its whole lattice and normalization to be retained; it is not covered by this unit assertion.

In particular the actual full modified factor is not such a unit:

dN(M)=ηNM−M−1MN−M−N,ηN=iN+3,dN(1)=ηN/N. d_N(M)=\eta_N\frac{M-M^{-1}}{M^N-M^{-N}},\qquad \eta_N=i^{N+3},\qquad d_N(1)=\eta_N/N. \tag{19} At M=q_N it has a simple pole. Since F_N(q_N)=1, that pole is not canceled in 𝒩N=dNFN\mathcal N_N=d_NF_N. Its exact residue is

Res⁡M=qN𝒩N(M)=ηN(1−qN2)2N≠0. \operatorname{Res}_{M=q_N}\mathcal N_N(M) =\frac{\eta_N(1-q_N^2)}{2N}\ne0. \tag{20} Indeed the denominator derivative at q_N is -2N q_N^{-1}. This works at every N>=2 and both parities. The complete value remains ηNKN/N\eta_N K_N/N, finite and exponentially large. Thus the full-state normalization does not furnish a uniformly holomorphic nonzero scalar packet across the complete point and its first shifted point either. Its shift quotient is meromorphic, with a pole at M=1 for each sufficiently large level. This matches the exact gauge −qN−1(qN2M2−1)/(M2−1)-q_N^{-1}(q_N^2M^2-1)/(M^2-1); no sign was discarded.

5. The regularized affine state is the remaining candidate

The existing regular-period theorem supplies a canonical correction, not an arbitrary scalar normalization. With Y=M^2, let P_N^ord be the unique degree-at-most-N-2 polynomial matching the ordinary orbit values at Q_N^j, 1<=j<N. Then

SN(Y)=YN−1Y−1,RNreg(Y)=fN(Y)−PNord(Y)SN(Y). S_N(Y)=\frac{Y^N-1}{Y-1},\qquad R_N^{reg}(Y)=\frac{f_N(Y)-P_N^{ord}(Y)}{S_N(Y)}. \tag{21} This is a Laurent polynomial. The ordinary-color bound gives PNord(1)/N=OK(Nb−1)P_N^{ord}(1)/N=O_K(N^{b-1}), and hence

RNreg(1)=KN/N−PNord(1)/N,1Nlog⁡|RNreg(1)|→v. R_N^{reg}(1)=K_N/N-P_N^{ord}(1)/N, \qquad \frac1N\log|R_N^{reg}(1)|\longrightarrow v. \tag{22} There is no fixed ordinary-color anchor for R_N^reg(Q_N): it depends on meridian derivatives of the original f_N and P_N^ord. The exact obstruction (5) has therefore not been transferred to this regular state. Neither has a uniform shift-ratio theorem been proved for it.

A specified all-level annihilator gives the existing rootwise affine recurrence ANRNreg=bNA_N R_N^{reg}=b_N, after its recorded rational gauge and denominator clearing. Its source b_N has fixed Laurent support and polynomially bounded coefficients. A sufficient next statement would be a fixed-domain, nonzero geometric shift-ratio limit for this actual regular state, together with a locally uniform exponential lower bound making b_N/R_N^reg tend to zero. Dividing the finite affine equation by R_N^reg would then give its homogeneous classical symbol on the limiting ratio. The preferred longitude, gauge and source would still have to be identified. Equation (22) alone supplies none of those neighborhood estimates.

This continuation preserves the complete scalar volume theorem and the pointed quantum A-period/Gram formulations. It corrects the choice of candidate for a complete-point scalar WKB or shift-ratio theorem: the actual reduced F_N has an exponential one-step drop and nearby zeros; the actual full state has nearby poles. Their canonical regular affine component is a different, explicitly sourced object.
