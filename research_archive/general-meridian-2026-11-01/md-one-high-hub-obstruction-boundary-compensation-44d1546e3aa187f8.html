3. What changes for a neighboring nonrigid branch

If the high incidence comes from an adjacent completed bare region rather than an L module, its boundary action is not generally the high constant in (3). For example, attaching an old two-leaf product across the physical turn gives the original boundary profile fhh(1−u) f_{hh}(1-u) \tag{8} at covariant high label uu, with all its own ordinary leaf contributions retained. It cannot be replaced by its independently maximized norm pressure without accounting for the shared boundary condition. Equations (4)–(7) do not make that replacement.

The coupled complete-pressure comparison in /private/tmp/md-old-whole-hub-simultaneous-half-pressure.md does apply to such an old region if all its marked high boundaries are treated jointly. Its hypotheses are: actual hubs have full valence at least three; internal edges are physical turns; at most one marked incidence occurs at each hub; the other boundaries carry the original g+cg+c profiles. This supplies a valid coupled tool for a neighboring complete region, but does not yet show how to replace the valence-nine moving tensor by a complete tensor with compatible boundary profiles. The pointwise inequality has been disproved; the actual coupled geometric comparison remains a different question.
