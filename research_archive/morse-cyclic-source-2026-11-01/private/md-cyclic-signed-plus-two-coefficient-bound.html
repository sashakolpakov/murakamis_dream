A polynomial bound for every original face coefficient at the positive spectral endpoint

At the real spectral endpoint +2+2, every coefficient of the full original resolvent face polynomial is bounded by 2N32N^3. This includes the interior entries, both one-sided parts and the CROSS part, at both level parities. The proof retains the cancellation between one-step and two-step paths. It does not replace their signed continuant by its absolute path expansion.

Consequently, for any fixed marked diagram with rr such boxes, the full shared source, its CROSS part, and their difference are polynomially bounded at the single spectral tuple (2,…,2)(2,\ldots,2). This is an endpoint statement, not a uniform bound on the spectral cube and not a filling-volume theorem.

6. Whole shared contractions and scope

For a fixed marked source with rr original boxes, there are at most N3N^3 monomials in each local homogeneous polynomial of degree dd in four faces. The full original shared coefficient is a sum of at most N3rN^{3r} monomial selections. Equation (26) therefore yields |SNfull(2,…,2)|≤2rN6r,|SNCROSS(2,…,2)|≤2rN6r,|DN(2,…,2)|≤2r+1N6r. \begin{split} |S_N^{\mathrm{full}}(2,\ldots,2)| &\le2^rN^{6r},\\ |S_N^{\mathrm{CROSS}}(2,\ldots,2)| &\le2^rN^{6r},\\ |D_N(2,\ldots,2)|&\le2^{r+1}N^{6r}. \end{split} \tag{29} The CROSS projection, or any fixed LEFT/CROSS/RIGHT assignment, selects original input/output intervals, hence a subset of these monomial coefficients. This justifies the same estimate without introducing absolute values inside the local continuants.

The same argument using (28d) gives sup𝝈∈[c0,2]r|DN(𝝈)|≤2r+1N7r, \sup_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in[c_0,2]^r}|D_N(\boldsymbol\sigma)| \le2^{r+1}N^{7r}, \tag{30} and half that displayed bound for the full or CROSS source separately. All protected targets, shared ordinary targets, and the original mark are imposed before these estimates are used. They add no quantum dimension or Cartan correction. They do not estimate mixed tuples elsewhere in the fixed spectral cube, a neighborhood uniformly large enough for filling, or the supremum required for the undecorated bipyramid theorem.
