Endpoint locality and the original filling functional

Why endpoint concentration does not localize the original filling functional

1 November 2026. Private proof and source-interface review. The release f2076a1b is unchanged.

The positive endpoint theorem and its small-polydisk extension are genuine source estimates on their stated original certified flat-plat class. They do not, by themselves, localize the finite filling functional to that polydisk. This note proves that limitation inside the exact degree and node-vanishing space of a one-sided source. The obstruction persists for every fixed odd signed twist power, however large that power is. It invalidates an inference from the spectral-polydisk estimate, degree, divisor, and Hermite functional alone. It does not by itself invalidate a theorem using a small positive Fejér reference mass before the shared coefficient is taken.

This is not a counterexample to a closed knot source or its volume limit. The test polynomial below is a bounded linear combination of actual local one-sided diagonal coefficients. It has not been realized as the one shared coefficient of a fixed closed diagram. Thus a useful theorem for the actual shared source must exploit a restriction beyond the local degree, divisor, endpoint estimate, and finite functional norm.

1. The source statements that can and cannot be combined

The exact homogeneous source and its central/left/right partition retain the original four-face order, ordinary degree targets d=N−1d=N-1, and two protected zero targets. The whole complementary polynomial is

𝒟N=SN,full−SN,C. \mathcal D_N=S_{N,\mathrm{full}}-S_{N,\mathrm C}. \tag{1}

It has degree at most dd in each spectral variable and vanishes on the product of all spectral node sets. This is value vanishing; repeated-node derivatives generally survive. Applying the exact functional in each variable inserts the actual signed powers. The finite supremum theorem controls this operation on the whole real spectral cube. It does not replace that cube by a neighborhood of the negative endpoint.

For the original certified flat plats in actual_fejer_unique_geometric_maximizer.md, the positive endpoint entropy has a unique geometric maximizer, separated from every one-sided support. actual_fejer_spectral_polydisk_complement.md then proves a strict bound for (1) when every spectral variable lies sufficiently close to −2-2, including all fixed endpoint derivatives. Both notes explicitly retain the remaining filling-cycle problem. Neither statement alone supplies a bound on all the other spectral nodes used by the exact functional.

The general sector-cover method instead bounds the whole real spectral cube before applying the functional. Its sufficient hypothesis is a strict shared-radius certificate for every relevant genuine noncentral string. The 54-region example in actual_singleton_sector_envelope_obstruction.md disproves the universal existence of this certificate for arbitrary simple matched-sphere Morse sources: one genuine singleton sector, nonzero at all sufficiently large even levels, has envelope capacity greater than the complete action by 1/51/5. That example does not contradict the earlier flat-plat endpoint theorem. It also does not furnish a signed-source or filled-invariant lower bound.

Appendix Q is a closure theorem for sources already possessing an unabridged cover. Its insistence on including locally allowed strings whose old whole coefficients happen to vanish is essential: gluing can change that global support. It does not assert that every source has a cover. There is consequently no logical route from Q alone to a universal noncentral gap.

2. The exact one-variable functional

Set N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, m=⌊N/2⌋m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor, and

χN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,ca=2cos⁡ϕa,ϕa=(2a+1)πN,0≤a<m. \chi_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\qquad c_a=2\cos\phi_a,\qquad \phi_a=\frac{(2a+1)\pi}{N},\quad 0\le a<m. \tag{2}

The cac_a are distinct double roots. When NN is odd, −2-2 is the additional simple root. Write

BN(z)=∏a=0m−1(z−ca),ZN(z)={BN(z),N=2m,(z+2)BN(z),N=2m+1.χN=ZNBN. B_N(z)=\prod_{a=0}^{m-1}(z-c_a),\qquad Z_N(z)=\begin{cases}B_N(z),&N=2m,\\ (z+2)B_N(z),&N=2m+1. \end{cases} \qquad \chi_N=Z_NB_N. \tag{3}

Every left or right source entry is divisible by ZNZ_N. In particular the exact one-sided scalar space is contained in, and its diagonal entries span, ZNℂ[z]≤m−1Z_N\mathbb C[z]_{\le m-1}. No repeated-root normal has been removed by taking this space.

For a polynomial HH vanishing at all nodes, the original full-Hermite functional reduces to

ℒNnH=1N2∑a=0m−1(4−ca2)fn(ca)H′(ca), \mathcal L_N^nH= \frac1{N^2}\sum_{a=0}^{m-1} (4-c_a^2)f_n(c_a)H'(c_a), \tag{4}

where the omitted simple-root value is zero and the crossing multiplier retains its original lift:

fn(2cos⁡ϕ)=qnd2/4exp⁡(inN(ϕ−π)24π),q=eπi/N. f_n(2\cos\phi)= q^{nd^2/4}\exp\!\left(\frac{inN(\phi-\pi)^2}{4\pi}\right), \qquad q=e^{\pi i/N}. \tag{5}

These are the formulas in actual_uniform_filling_supremum_bound.md, equations (10), (13), and (22). The derivative of fnf_n disappears in (4) because the numerator value is zero, not because the crossing phase has been replaced by its absolute value. All values fn(ca)f_n(c_a) have modulus one, at both parities and at composite levels.

3. A polynomial in the actual one-sided divisor space

Define

HN(z)=ZN(z)(z+24)m−1. H_N(z)=Z_N(z)\left(\frac{z+2}{4}\right)^{m-1}. \tag{6}

Its degree is exactly dd. It vanishes at every spectral node. At the endpoint −2-2, its vanishing order is m−1m-1 for even levels and mm for odd levels. Hence every fixed finite list of endpoint derivatives is identically zero for all sufficiently large NN.

The elementary Chebyshev identities are

B2m(2cos⁡ϕ)=2cos⁡(mϕ),B2m+1(2cos⁡ϕ)=cos⁡((m+12)ϕ)cos⁡(ϕ/2)=Um(cos⁡ϕ)−Um−1(cos⁡ϕ),Z2m+1(2cos⁡ϕ)=4cos⁡(ϕ/2)cos⁡((m+12)ϕ). \begin{aligned} B_{2m}(2\cos\phi)&=2\cos(m\phi),\\ B_{2m+1}(2\cos\phi) &=\frac{\cos((m+\tfrac12)\phi)}{\cos(\phi/2)} =U_m(\cos\phi)-U_{m-1}(\cos\phi),\\ Z_{2m+1}(2\cos\phi) &=4\cos(\phi/2)\cos((m+\tfrac12)\phi). \end{aligned} \tag{7}

The quotient in the second line is its polynomial continuation at ϕ=π\phi=\pi. Consequently

∥HN∥[−2,2]≤4. \|H_N\|_{[-2,2]}\le4. \tag{8}

There is also a fixed complex neighborhood on which this bounded polynomial is exponentially small. For 0<r≤10<r\le1, put

ρr=2+r+r(4+r)2,θr=log⁡ρr. \rho_r=\frac{2+r+\sqrt{r(4+r)}}2, \qquad \theta_r=\log\rho_r. \tag{9}

If |z+2|≤r|z+2|\le r, choose ww with z=w+w−1z=w+w^{-1} and |w|≥1|w|\ge1. The identity

|z−2|+|z+2|=2(|w|+|w|−1)≤4+2r. \lvert z-2\rvert+\lvert z+2\rvert =2(\lvert w\rvert+\lvert w\rvert^{-1})\le4+2r. \tag{10}

implies |w|≤ρr|w|\le\rho_r. Using B2m=wm+w−mB_{2m}=w^m+w^{-m}, and using the m+1m+1 and mm terms in the two polynomials Um,Um−1U_m,U_{m-1} for odd levels, gives

sup|z+2|≤r|HN(z)|≤max⁡(2,Nr)ρrm(r/4)m−1. \sup_{|z+2|\le r}|H_N(z)| \le \max(2,Nr)\rho_r^m(r/4)^{m-1}. \tag{11}

For example, at r=1r=1, ρ1=(3+5)/2<4\rho_1=(3+\sqrt5)/2<4. Therefore the right side is exp⁡(−γN+O(log⁡N))\exp(-\gamma N+O(\log N)), where

γ=12log⁡83+5>0. \gamma=\frac12\log\frac{8}{3+\sqrt5}>0. \tag{12}

The same upper bound holds on every smaller fixed polydisk after taking products of these one-variable polynomials.

4. This is a bounded combination of literal source coefficients

The original homogeneous face formula gives, for 0≤a<m0\le a<m,

[x0bacd−ay0]HV,L(x,b,c,y;z)=χN(z)z−ca. [x^0b^ac^{d-a}y^0]H_{V,\mathrm L}(x,b,c,y;z) =\frac{\chi_N(z)}{z-c_a}. \tag{13}

Indeed the associated path has identical initial and final row aa; it uses no lowering step and leaves every other diagonal row unused. Thus (13) includes the full unused-row product and the same scalar normalization as the original resolvent numerator. It is not a spectral interpolant substituted for that numerator.

Partial fractions, or Lagrange interpolation of a polynomial of degree m−1m-1, now give the exact identity

HN(z)=∑a=0m−1αN,aχN(z)z−ca,αN,a=((ca+2)/4)m−1BN′(ca). H_N(z)=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{N,a} \frac{\chi_N(z)}{z-c_a},\qquad \alpha_{N,a}=\frac{((c_a+2)/4)^{m-1}}{B_N'(c_a)}. \tag{14}

The derivatives in (7) yield

αN,a={2N(−1)asin⁡ϕacos⁡N−2(ϕa/2),N even,4N(−1)asin⁡ϕacos⁡N−2(ϕa/2),N odd. \alpha_{N,a}= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac2N(-1)^a\sin\phi_a \cos^{N-2}(\phi_a/2),&N\text{ even},\\[3pt] \displaystyle\frac4N(-1)^a\sin\phi_a \cos^{N-2}(\phi_a/2),&N\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \tag{15}

In particular ∑a|αN,a|≤2\sum_a|\alpha_{N,a}|\le2. The coefficients are level dependent and alternate in sign; they are not asserted to be produced by one fixed exterior diagram. Their bounded total mass shows that the obstruction does not arise from exponentially large external scalar weights.

The exact resolvent/Hermite identity sends each polynomial in (13) to fn(ca)f_n(c_a). Equivalently, (4) and the Chebyshev double-root normalization give

ℒNnHN=∑aαN,afn(ca). \mathcal L_N^nH_N=\sum_a\alpha_{N,a}f_n(c_a). \tag{16}

This is where the retained normal matters. Replacing the numerator by its zero values would incorrectly make (16) zero.

5. Every fixed odd twist power detects it at polynomial size

Let n∈2ℤ+1n\in2\mathbb Z+1 be fixed, positive or negative. Define a unit phase and a parity factor by

ξN,n=qnd2/4exp⁡(inNπ4−inπ2),κN={2,N even,4,N odd. \xi_{N,n}=q^{nd^2/4} \exp\!\left(\frac{inN\pi}{4}-\frac{in\pi}{2}\right), \qquad \kappa_N=\begin{cases}2,&N\text{ even},\\4,&N\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \tag{17}

At the actual midpoint grid, the linear phase in (5) obeys e−inNϕa/2=(−1)ae−inπ/2e^{-inN\phi_a/2}=(-1)^ae^{-in\pi/2}. It therefore cancels the alternating sign in (15), exactly, before any estimate. Equations (15)–(16) become

ℒNnHN=ξN,nκNN∑a=0m−1sin⁡ϕacos⁡N−2(ϕa/2)exp⁡(inNϕa24π). \mathcal L_N^nH_N= \xi_{N,n}\frac{\kappa_N}{N} \sum_{a=0}^{m-1}\sin\phi_a\, \cos^{N-2}(\phi_a/2) \exp\!\left(\frac{inN\phi_a^2}{4\pi}\right). \tag{18}

The sum has the nonzero limit

∑a=0m−1sin⁡ϕacos⁡N−2(ϕa/2)einNϕa2/(4π)→2π−2in. \sum_{a=0}^{m-1}\sin\phi_a\, \cos^{N-2}(\phi_a/2)e^{inN\phi_a^2/(4\pi)} \longrightarrow \frac{2}{\pi-2in}. \tag{19}

Here is a direct justification. Put ta=Nϕat_a=\sqrt N\phi_a; their spacing is 2π/N2\pi/\sqrt N, with the first point at half that spacing. On every bounded tt-interval,

Nsin⁡(t/N)cos⁡N−2(t/(2N))eint2/(4π)→texp⁡[−(1/8−in/(4π))t2] \sqrt N\sin(t/\sqrt N) \cos^{N-2}(t/(2\sqrt N)) e^{int^2/(4\pi)} \longrightarrow t\exp[-(1/8-in/(4\pi))t^2] \tag{20}

uniformly. For N≥4N\ge4, 0≤t<πN0\le t<\pi\sqrt N, the absolute value of the left side is at most te−t2/16t e^{-t^2/16}: use sin⁡x≤x\sin x\le x, log⁡cos⁡(x/2)≤−x2/8\log\cos(x/2)\le-x^2/8, and N−2≥N/2N-2\ge N/2. Its lattice tails are uniformly summable, as follows by comparing the eventually decreasing Gaussian bound with its integral. Midpoint Riemann sums therefore converge to

12π∫0∞texp⁡[−(1/8−in/(4π))t2]dt=2π−2in. \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^\infty t\exp[-(1/8-in/(4\pi))t^2]\,dt =\frac{2}{\pi-2in}. \tag{21}

This proves (19) without an exchange of an infinite Fourier charge sum. It follows that, separately at either parity,

ℒNnHN=ξN,nN(2κNπ−2in+o(1)),limN→∞1Nlog⁡|ℒNnHN|=0. \mathcal L_N^nH_N =\frac{\xi_{N,n}}N \left(\frac{2\kappa_N}{\pi-2in}+o(1)\right), \qquad \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|\mathcal L_N^nH_N|=0. \tag{22}

The contribution in (19) comes from ϕ=O(N−1/2)\phi=O(N^{-1/2}), hence from z=2−O(N−1)z=2-O(N^{-1}), at the opposite end of the spectrum from the controlled polydisk. For any fixed closed neighborhood of −2-2 separated from 22, its restricted derivative-node sum in (18) is exponentially small: on that part of the grid cos⁡(ϕ/2)\cos(\phi/2) is bounded strictly below one. The odd simple-node value is zero throughout.

6. Exact obstruction and the remaining source question

There cannot be a bound

|ℒNnH|≤eo(N)sup|z+2|≤1|H(z)| |\mathcal L_N^nH| \le e^{o(N)}\sup_{|z+2|\le1}|H(z)| \tag{23}

valid for every H∈ZNℂ[z]≤m−1H\in Z_N\mathbb C[z]_{\le m-1}, even with the added assumptions ∥H∥[−2,2]≤4\|H\|_{[-2,2]}\le4 and vanishing of every fixed number of endpoint derivatives. The polynomials (6), with (11) and (22), disprove it for every fixed odd signed nn. The same conclusion holds for any smaller fixed endpoint disk.

Thus the finite filling norm, the true one-sided divisor, repeated-node normal recovery, and endpoint concentration do not automatically justify discarding the rest of the spectral grid. Increasing a fixed twist power does not fix this general functional issue. The actual midpoint phases can reinforce an alternating one-sided combination at the opposite endpoint.

There is an additional distinction between spectral locality and the stronger positive endpoint comparison. The smallness of (6) on the endpoint disk results from cancellation in (14). It does not imply a small sum of the corresponding positive Fejér coefficients. Therefore this example alone does not disprove a source-specific argument using that positive reference before taking the shared coefficient.

A universal coefficientwise comparison with that reference nevertheless fails, by a different exact example already present in the Fejér entropy sign calculation. At N=2mN=2m, let 0≤j≤m−10\le j\le m-1, and let vN,j(z)v_{N,j}(z) be the literal left coefficient of xjcd−jx^jc^{d-j}. Put

sr=2sin⁡(πr/N),ar=4cos⁡2(π(r+12)/N). s_r=2\sin(\pi r/N),\qquad a_r=4\cos^2(\pi(r+\tfrac12)/N). \tag{24}

The single all-xx path and its unused rows give its positive reference coefficient exactly:

pN,j=|vN,j(−2)|=4a0∏r=1jsr2ar. p_{N,j}=|v_{N,j}(-2)| =\frac4{a_0}\prod_{r=1}^j\frac{s_r^2}{a_r}. \tag{25}

For the original RR or R−1R^{-1}, equation (10) of the homogeneous source formula instead gives

|ℒN±1vN,j|=NSjSd−j=1,Sk=∏r=1k2sin⁡(πr/N). |\mathcal L_N^{\pm1}v_{N,j}| =\frac{N}{S_jS_{d-j}}=1, \qquad S_k=\prod_{r=1}^k2\sin(\pi r/N). \tag{26}

Here SjSd−j=Sd=NS_jS_{d-j}=S_d=N, and the omitted original lift is a unit phase. Since this face monomial is strictly one-sided, no central or right contribution can change (26). If j/N→u∈(0,1/2)j/N\to u\in(0,1/2), (25) has rate 2∫0ulog⁡tan⁡(πt)dt<02\int_0^u\log\tan(\pi t)\,dt<0. Thus no subexponential coefficientwise filling cost relative to the positive endpoint reference holds for all powers. The two powers in (26) are not independently long; this observation does not decide a statement that uses long powers together with additional closed-source constraints.

The missing step for an actual closed source remains more specific: prove a bound for its shared complementary polynomial on the rest of the grid, or prove cancellation of its exact weighted normal sum with all original face targets still imposed. The bounded combinations (14) have not been shown to arise from those targets. They therefore rule out a generic locality inference, but do not rule out a source-specific coherent estimate. No claim about hyperbolic volume follows from this counterexample in either direction.

It also records a short high-precision check of the nonzero constant in (19). These finite computations do not prove the uniform norm or asymptotic; their proofs are (7)–(22). The only quantum inputs are the written homogeneous source definition, the one-sided path partition, and the complete Hermite functional with its original Gaussian lift.
