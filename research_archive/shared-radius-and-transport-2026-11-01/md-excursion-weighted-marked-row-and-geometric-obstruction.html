Quantitative control of the protected row and the remaining excursion bound

The finite excursion reduction preserves the original full-minus-CROSS source, but nilpotence alone does not bound an excursion’s size. This note supplies a whole-real-spectral-interval bound for the complete non-CROSS part of the protected marked row, with all boundary loads retained. It also gives a single-low-Newton-profile bound for every one-sided north coefficient and an exact polynomial bound for one fully coupled two-step return block.

The natural local weighting by the complete circle parameters does not turn every bad step into a contraction. We give its explicit failure at the actual marked quartet and distinguish that failure from the still-open estimate on a complete excursion.

The previously proved positive Cauchy capacity obstruction already excludes a different, coarser remedy: replacing every full local polynomial by its unconditioned positive value at one shared set of radii cannot give the sharp complete rate at an unbalanced packing. It does not exclude the conditional matrix estimate sought here.

Thus the next useful estimate must concern the signed matrix K(z)K(z) in a controlled good-state boundary norm, or its actual contractions with the good-state transfer. A small norm for the nilpotent bad block by itself is not such an estimate.

8. Relation to the existing source estimates and remaining scope

The complete positive endpoint and its small complex polydisk complement have already been proved under their stated original-source hypotheses. They are not reproved here. In particular the existing flat-plat theorems cannot be silently applied to an arbitrary matched-sphere Morse presentation without the additional source and geometry adapter.

The all-CROSS cofactor bounds already control their original spectral strip and angular torus, retaining the protected cofactor cases. Those results cannot be substituted for the unused prefix and suffix factors of a general one-sided interval. Formula (7) is a special exact cancellation of those factors at the protected first row; (17) states precisely what remains for the other north rows.

Absolute estimates of individual complementary-source tree channels can exceed the geometric rate, even after their forced full contractions. Therefore no termwise tree-channel estimate is used in deriving (13) or (22).

All displayed bounds preserve the original root polynomials and their spectral normals. They may be differentiated in the spectral variables with the usual fixed-degree polynomial costs. They cannot be differentiated in qq while retaining the same root cutoff and omitted endpoint arrows. No whole-cube bound for the full sewn difference, and no new filled-knot volume theorem, follows from the present local and boundary estimates.
