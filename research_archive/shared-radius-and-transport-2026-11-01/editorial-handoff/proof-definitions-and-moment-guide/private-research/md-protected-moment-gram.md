# A positive moment form for the original protected source

The protected one-sided source has alternating coefficients at the spectral value \(2\). Taking their absolute values loses information that no positive diagonal Cauchy weight can restore. This note constructs a different positive form: its off-diagonal entries retain the interference between different original degrees. The construction and the resulting finite identities are proved below. A volume bound for this new energy is not proved here.

## 1. The coefficients and the original pairing

Fix an integer \(N\ge2\), and write \(d=N-1\), \(m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), and \(M=m-1\). Set
\[
 \theta=\pi/N,\qquad c_r=2\cos((2r+1)\theta),\qquad
 S_j=\prod_{r=1}^j2\sin(r\theta),\qquad S_0=1.
\]
In the original ordered face quartet \((x,b,c,y)\), set the protected faces \(b=y=0\). The left part of the polynomial numerator is
\[
 H_L(x,0,c,0;\sigma)=\sum_{j=0}^{M}v_j(\sigma)x^jc^{d-j},\qquad
 v_j(\sigma)=(-1)^jS_j^2\prod_{r=j+1}^{d}(\sigma-c_r).
 \tag{1}
\]
All unused spectral rows are present. The same coefficient formula makes sense for every \(0\le j\le d\), including the central part. Define
\[
 U_j=(-1)^jv_j(2)>0,\qquad
 U_0=\csc^2\frac{\pi}{2N},\qquad
 h_j=\frac{U_j}{U_0}
   =\prod_{r=1}^j\frac{\sin^2(r\theta)}{\sin^2((r+1/2)\theta)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(h_0=1\). These are elements of the real cyclotomic field generated by \(2\cos(\pi/N)\).

After the other original face targets have been extracted, let the spectator polynomial be
\[
 B(x,c)=c^dB(x/c),\qquad B(z)=\sum_{k=0}^d b_kz^k.
\]
Its coefficients can still depend on every other spectral variable. The original shared coefficient, with no changed target or new mark, is
\[
 [x^dc^d]H_L(x,0,c,0;2)B(x,c)
       =\sum_{j=0}^{M}(-1)^jU_jb_{d-j}.
 \tag{3}
\]

## 2. Positivity of the moment matrices

For every pair of nonnegative integers \(s,k\) with \(s+2k\le d\), the matrix
\[
              H^{(s,k)}=(h_{s+i+j})_{0\le i,j\le k}
 \tag{4}
\]
is positive definite. Thus these are genuine positive moment forms, not entrywise positive matrices whose quadratic forms have been assumed positive.

Here is a proof using ordinary beta integrals and Euler's sine product. If \(X\) has beta parameters \(a,b>0\), its probability density on \((0,1)\) is
\[
 \frac{x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}}{\mathrm B(a,b)},\qquad
 \mathrm B(a,b)=\int_0^1x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}\,dx.
\]
For an integer \(j\ge0\), direct integration gives
\[
 \mathbb E X^j=\prod_{r=1}^j\frac{a+r-1}{a+b+r-1}.
\]
If \(j<a\), the same calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb E X^{-j}=\prod_{r=1}^j\frac{a+b-r}{a-r}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Take independent variables with distributions
\[
 X_0\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1,1/2),\quad
 A_\ell\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\ell N+1,1/2),\quad
 B_\ell\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\ell N-1/2,1/2).
\]
For a finite integer \(K\ge1\), let
\[
 W_K=X_0\prod_{\ell=1}^{K}\frac{A_\ell}{B_\ell},\qquad
 T_K=W_K\widetilde W_K,
\]
where the tilde denotes an independent copy. Every moment needed below exists: \(j\le d<N-1/2\) is below the first inverse-beta singularity. Equation (5) yields
\[
 \mathbb E T_K^j
 =\left\{\prod_{r=1}^j\frac{r}{r+1/2}
   \prod_{\ell=1}^{K}\prod_{r=1}^j
   \frac{1-r^2/(\ell N)^2}{1-(r+1/2)^2/(\ell N)^2}\right\}^{\!2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Euler's product for sine shows that (6) tends to \(h_j\). For fixed \(N,j\) the logarithms of the factors indexed by \(\ell\) are \(O_{N,j}(\ell^{-2})\), so the limit exists, is finite, and is strictly positive.

For strict positivity, keep \(X_0\widetilde X_0\) separate from the remaining factors. Its moment matrix
\[
 B^{(s,k)}_{ij}=\mathbb E(X_0\widetilde X_0)^{s+i+j}
\]
is positive definite: for a nonzero polynomial \(p\) of degree at most \(k\), the integral of \(t^s|p(t)|^2\) against this distribution is positive. The distribution gives positive mass to every nonempty interval in \((0,1)\), whereas \(p\) has only finitely many roots there.

Let \(R_K^{(s,k)}\) be the moment matrix of the remaining product of beta ratios. Independence makes the matrix of (6) the entrywise product \(B^{(s,k)}\circ R_K^{(s,k)}\). The matrices \(R_K^{(s,k)}\) are positive semidefinite and converge entrywise to a positive semidefinite matrix \(R^{(s,k)}\) with strictly positive diagonal. Consequently
\[
 H^{(s,k)}=B^{(s,k)}\circ R^{(s,k)}
    \ \ge\ \lambda_{\min}(B^{(s,k)})\,
                         \operatorname{diag}(R^{(s,k)})>0.
 \tag{7}
\]
The inequality uses the elementary Schur product fact: if two matrices are Gram matrices, their entrywise product is the Gram matrix of the tensor products of the corresponding vectors. Apply it to \(B^{(s,k)}-\lambda_{\min}(B^{(s,k)})I\) and \(R^{(s,k)}\). This proves (4).

Only finite beta products were used. There is no interchange of an infinite random product with an unbounded integrand. The finite matrix limit suffices.

## 3. An exact finite positive quadrature

The proof above also leads to a finite algebraic evaluation of every polynomial of degree at most \(d\). Write \(\mathcal M_N(t^j)=h_j\), extending linearly on that space. The following constructions realize \(\mathcal M_N\) by positive weights at real algebraic points.

First suppose \(N=2m\). Set
\[
 H=(h_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<m},\qquad
 K=(h_{i+j+1})_{0\le i,j<m},\qquad A=H^{-1}K.
 \tag{8}
\]
Both \(H\) and \(K\) are positive definite by (4). In the inner product \(\langle u,v\rangle_H=u^*Hv\), the matrix \(A\) is self-adjoint and positive. If \(e_0,\ldots,e_{m-1}\) is the coordinate basis, its columns give
\[
                  Ae_i=e_{i+1}\quad(0\le i<m-1).
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(e_0\) is cyclic. A self-adjoint matrix with a cyclic vector has distinct eigenvalues, and the projection of that vector onto every eigenspace is nonzero. Denote the eigenvalues by \(t_1,\ldots,t_m>0\), and the squared \(H\)-lengths of these projections by \(w_1,\ldots,w_m>0\). Their sum is \(h_0=1\).

For \(0\le j\le2m-2\), split \(j=a+b\) with \(a,b\le m-1\). Self-adjointness and (9) give
\[
 \langle e_0,A^je_0\rangle_H
       =\langle e_a,e_b\rangle_H=h_j.
\]
For \(j=2m-1\), the corresponding expression is
\(\langle e_{m-1},Ae_{m-1}\rangle_H=K_{m-1,m-1}=h_{2m-1}\).
The spectral decomposition therefore proves
\[
       \mathcal M_N(P)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}w_\ell P(t_\ell)
                   \qquad(\deg P\le d).
 \tag{10}
\]
The nodes are the roots of \(\det(K-tH)\). The weights can be computed from its spectral projectors. All entries, nodes and weights are algebraic. No ordering of the nodes is needed to define the sum.

For \(N=2m+1\), apply the same construction to the shifted functional \(\mathcal M_N(tP)\). Use
\[
 H'=(h_{i+j+1})_{0\le i,j<m},\qquad
 K'=(h_{i+j+2})_{0\le i,j<m}.
\]
This gives \(m\) distinct positive nodes \(t_\ell\) and positive weights \(\widetilde w_\ell\) reproducing the shifted moments through degree \(2m-1\). For a polynomial \(P\) of degree at most \(2m\), write \(P(t)=P(0)+tQ(t)\). Then
\[
 \mathcal M_N(P)=w_0P(0)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}w_\ell P(t_\ell),
 \qquad
 w_\ell=\widetilde w_\ell/t_\ell,\quad
 w_0=1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}w_\ell.
 \tag{11}
\]
The remaining weight is strictly positive. Indeed let \(p(t)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}(t-t_\ell)\). Positivity of \(H^{(0,m)}\) gives \(\mathcal M_N(p^2)>0\); equation (11) says this equals \(w_0p(0)^2\). Since every \(t_\ell>0\), \(p(0)\ne0\). Hence \(w_0>0\). This is an exact quadrature with \(m+1\) nodes, including zero.

## 4. A Cauchy inequality that retains the alternating degrees

Put
\[
 p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{M}(-1)^jb_{d-j}t^j,\qquad
 \mathcal E_N(B)=\mathcal M_N(|p|^2)
   =\sum_{i,j=0}^{M}(-1)^{i+j}h_{i+j}
                          \overline{b_{d-i}}b_{d-j}.
 \tag{12}
\]
For real \(t\), \(|p(t)|^2\) means \(p(t)\overline{p(t)}\). Its degree is \(2M\le d\), so the functional and quadrature above apply. The energy is positive for every nonzero retained coefficient vector. Since \(\mathcal M_N(1)=1\), Cauchy in (10) or (11) gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{j=0}^{M}(-1)^jU_jb_{d-j}\right|^2
                   \le U_0^2\mathcal E_N(B)
                   \le N^4\mathcal E_N(B).
 \tag{13}
\]
The last inequality uses \(\sin(\pi/(2N))\ge1/N\). The original pairing has thus been bounded by an explicitly positive, non-diagonal energy with a polynomial closure cost. The entries with \(i\ne j\) are essential. Removing them would give a different inequality and discard the cancellation again.

Neither (12) nor (13) bounds this energy by hyperbolic volume. It is a new exact object on which such a comparison can be attempted. Its spectator coefficients remain those of the original shared source, with all the other face targets already imposed.

## 5. The spectral interval is a further question

At a general real spectral value define the real vector
\[
 g_j(\sigma)=\frac{(-1)^jv_j(\sigma)}{U_0},\quad
 H_L=(h_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le M},\quad
 \kappa_N(\sigma)=g(\sigma)^TH_L^{-1}g(\sigma).
 \tag{14}
\]
Positive definiteness makes this a well-defined nonnegative polynomial in \(\sigma\). The exact coefficient pairing and finite-dimensional Cauchy imply
\[
 \left|\sum_{j=0}^{M}v_j(\sigma)b_{d-j}\right|^2
         \le U_0^2\kappa_N(\sigma)\mathcal E_N(B).
 \tag{15}
\]
At \(\sigma=2\), \(g=(h_0,\ldots,h_M)^T=H_Le_0\), so \(\kappa_N(2)=1\) exactly. A uniform polynomial bound for \(\kappa_N\) on \([-2,2]\) would extend the polynomial closure cost to that interval. Such a bound is not proved in this note. In particular, positivity of \(H_L\) alone would not prove it: the inverse of a positive matrix may have exponentially large norm.

There are therefore two separate remaining estimates: the spectral dual norm in (14), and the energy in (12) for the actual spectator. The latter still has to be compared with the original geometric action. A finite quadrature identity, however exact, does not replace either estimate or prove a new knot-volume equality.

## 6. Sources and comparison with established constructions

The source coefficients (1) are the original one-step protected specialization of the homogeneous Casimir numerator. The exact spectral supremum and its boundary-current equation are proved in the companion protected-coefficient note. They are not imported from a positive tetrahedral model.

The classical beta integral used in (5) is [DLMF 5.12.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.12#E1); Euler's sine product is [DLMF 4.22.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.22#E1). The moment products have a structural resemblance to [Ostrovsky's Barnes beta distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.4422), where Mellin transforms are built from multiple-gamma ratios. No equivalence with one of those distributions is asserted or used. The positivity proof here is the explicit finite-beta calculation (5)–(7).

Equations (8)–(11) are the usual finite moment-matrix mechanism behind Gaussian and Gauss–Radau quadrature. Their proofs are included so that only the original moments, the spectral theorem for a positive finite matrix, and the stated polynomial degree are needed. The quadrature supplies an algebraic evaluation of the original protected pairing; it does not define a new topological invariant.
