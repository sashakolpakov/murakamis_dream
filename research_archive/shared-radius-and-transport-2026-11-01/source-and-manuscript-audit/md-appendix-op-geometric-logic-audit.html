1. Exact input and output types

The input to the geometric part of Appendix O is:

  1. A finite simple triangulated oriented sphere with at least four vertices; a perfect matching of the cubic dual; a nonmatching marked edge; and the actual cyclic frame of every matched quadrilateral.
  2. The inverse-cut diagram in S3S^3, or an existing diagram whose labeled cut is this data. The local disk maps and their original twice-punctured disk collars are retained.
  3. A fixed retained half-twist vector ϵe∈{−1,0,1}\epsilon_e\in\{-1,0,1\}. The original terminal graph determines the number of main components; a knot conclusion requires it to be connected.
  4. Integers pep_e, fixed before the quantum level, with ne=ϵe−2pen_e=\epsilon_e-2p_e independently sufficiently long. The threshold depends on the complete fixed diagram and frames.

The general Morse source identity alone allows more combinatorics, but the current geometry theorem uses the simple-sphere input above. There is no transfer to an arbitrary tensor graph, a non-simple cellulation, or an arbitrary auxiliary representation.

The geometric output is a complete finite-volume metric on the original auxiliary filling, with every main cusp complete. The knot case is the original S3\K𝒑S^3\setminus K_{\boldsymbol p}. The analytic output in Appendix O is only the nonzero all-central contribution. Appendix P adds the separate weak-balance or quantified remainder hypothesis to conclude the full-invariant limit.

In particular, neither “central” nor “geometric contribution” means that its finite coefficient is a separate topological invariant.

6. What Appendix P adds

P assumes the actual complete weak-balance inequalities. Its power-flattened weights are one common Cauchy radius vector and need not be another circle packing. It derives a strict real-cube bound for the entire difference between full and central sources.

The result is restricted to weak balance or P’s quantified extension, an original knot parity, and fixed independently long powers. It says nothing universal about unchanged simple spheres that fail those criteria.
