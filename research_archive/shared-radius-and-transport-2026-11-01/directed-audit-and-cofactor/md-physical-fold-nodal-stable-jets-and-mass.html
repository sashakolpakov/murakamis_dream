Physical-fold stable jets

Physical-fold stable atoms for the original central values and first normals

This note gives exact finite expansions at both level parities. Every value and first spectral derivative of the original central polynomial on the real spectral interval is a signed sum of nonnegative monomials times principal positive determinants. The total absolute polynomial mass is bounded coefficientwise by a polynomial in the level times the actual maximal physical folded source. The same bound therefore survives the original common face coefficient extraction. The expansion does not concentrate its principal-rank deficits: even a first normal at the maximal fold involves every available size. No neighboring-spectrum cancellation or filling theorem follows from the mass bound alone.

5. The original shared target and the exact rank bookkeeping

Take a fixed finite collection of original central ports, with at most one spectral derivative per port, at arbitrary real spectral nodes. Substitute the expansions above before taking any absolute value. Multiply the atoms with the original positive stable spectator factors, identify the actual shared face variables, set the original protected variables to zero, and extract exactly the original face-target multi-index. Stability is preserved by these operations, or the coefficient is identically zero. Omit only such exact zero atoms. If two boundary variables remain, every nonzero extracted atom is a nonnegative homogeneous stable binary polynomial of the original boundary degree.

The coefficientwise inequalities (21), (23) imply that the total absolute extracted mass, evaluated at any fixed positive boundary values, is at most a polynomial in NN times the corresponding extracted maximal-fold reference. This remains true with common face variables: it is one coefficientwise comparison followed by one common extraction, not independent extraction at each port. Fixed original unit phases multiply scalar coefficients; no phase of a local face polynomial is replaced by an independent choice. Signed spectators not themselves expanded into controlled stable atoms are outside this assertion.

At an odd port the atom using Δa(b)Δa′(c)\Delta_a(b)\Delta_{a'}(c) has principal deletion rank r=2J−a−a′. r=2J-a-a'.\tag{24} Its outer monomial compensates the lost degree, including the explicit xyxy in the skipping term. At an even port the first term retains the common factor AA and has the same rank. The second term deletes that additional one-dimensional positive pencil and compensates by xyxy; its rank is r=2J−a−a′+1. r=2J-a-a'+1.\tag{25} Ranks add over ports. The separately proved monotone coefficient-path theorem applies to nonzero extracted atoms with these ranks; it does not require a feasible intermediate coefficient to be assumed. This gives root-count control by rr, but does not make rr small.

6. What the polynomial mass does and does not resolve

The first derivatives in (17) normally have contributions from every node, even at u=u*u=u_*. Thus high deficits of order NN occur already at first derivative order. Polynomial total mass does not imply a sublinear-rank concentration, nor a weighted mass estimate in which spectral perturbation order controls principal rank. Such a conclusion would require an additional estimate after the actual shared extraction.

The original far-node cofactor estimate gives an exponential local loss at positive nonsingleton face triples when a/N≤1/2−δa/N\le1/2-\delta. Its reference u=0u=0 can be replaced by u*u_* at bounded cost. For even levels the positive path expansion gives Rj(b;0)≼CNRj(b;u*),CN=∏r<Jarar−aJ=2cot⁡(θ/2)N<4π<2. R_j(b;0)\preceq C_N R_j(b;u_*),\qquad C_N=\prod_{r<J}\frac{a_r}{a_r-a_J} =\frac{2\cot(\theta/2)}{N}<\frac4\pi<2. \tag{26} Each path uses a subset of these distinct diagonal factors. The product follows from ∏r=0N/2−1ar=2\prod_{r=0}^{N/2-1}a_r=2 and SN−2=N/(2sin⁡θ)S_{N-2}=N/(2\sin\theta). At odd levels the two references coincide. A local weighted loss is not automatically a relative loss after coefficient extraction: the extracted reference requires its own matching lower asymptotic, and a loss tending to zero with δ\delta need not exceed a separate cap budget. No such inequality is claimed here.

There is also an exact obstruction to dispensing with signed atoms. For J≥2J\ge2, at a=J−2a=J-2, RJ(b;uJ−2)=S2J−4sJ−12sJ2ΔJ−2(x,b,y)[x2+(2−γ)xy+y2],γ=1−sJ−2/sJ∈(0,1]. R_J(b;u_{J-2})=S_{2J-4}s_{J-1}^2s_J^2\Delta_{J-2}(x,b,y) [x^2+(2-\gamma)xy+y^2],\qquad \gamma=1-s_{J-2}/s_J\in(0,1].\tag{27} The quadratic has nonreal ratio roots and is not real stable. Choose a ratio root with argument in (−π,π)(-\pi,\pi), then choose x,yx,y in the upper half-plane with that ratio; the principal determinant remains nonzero when bb is also in that half-plane. These nodes approach u*u_* at order N−2N^{-2}. Thus the full cofactor cannot have a positive semidefinite linear face-pencil representation on a fixed physical neighborhood at all levels. This does not obstruct the signed stable expansion proved above, a low-defect indefinite representation, or a correlated cap estimate.
