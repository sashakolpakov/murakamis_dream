## Mathematical conclusion

No essential correction is needed. The new section proves arbitrarily large **recorded marked diagrams** with unabridged shared-radius certificates but without weak balance at that retained mark. It does not prove that their unmarked knots have no other covered presentation or mark.

The forty-branch certificate is unabridged. The first fifteen branches correspond to every nonempty subset of RIGHT labels at the four regions `2,4,7,12`; the remaining twenty-five branches choose a surviving one-sided anchor when none of those four regions is RIGHT. The verifier constructs this expected list symbolically and compares every stored branch to it. Its `!R` evaluation omits a protected-overloaded LEFT, so the branch at region 12 means only the locally surviving labels. No omission based on a global coefficient cancellation is used. The recorded gap is greater than `0.04`.

The anchor certificate is also being used with the correct narrower scope. Its anchor is RIGHT at region 12, with quartet `(0,11,16,14)`. Its only spectator masses different from one occur at regions `2,4,7`. At each such region the certificate uses a convex combination of its two complete triangle rows and its input-middle pure allocation. Thus its same rational flow is bounded by the unrestricted local maximum only at those three regions; every other spectator needs only its complete rate. All original ordinary targets cancel exactly. The anchor's boundary allocation is a convex combination of the two actual RIGHT comparison branches and has zero entropy. These facts prove the lower bound for the restricted function in (29), not merely for the larger original anchored function.

The recorded interval values give flow entropy greater than `19.95975965735` and complete action upper bound less than `19.92324700353`. Their difference is greater than `0.025`. If every region in `J={2,4,7,12}` were weak-balanced at completeness, the three spectator maxima would equal their complete rates, and the RIGHT anchor rate would be at most its complete rate. This would contradict (29). In particular the argument locates at least one failed comparison inside `J`; it does not assert which member fails or that all four fail.

The other marked-edge triangle is the output triangle `(16,17,0)` at region 15, quartet `(16,14,0,17)`. The first attachment therefore removes no candidate in `J`. Subsequent attachments take place on the newly appended marked-edge triangle. None changes the four original ordered quartets. The complete-action gluing identity from the earlier audit preserves the entire old complete vector up to one common scale. Since a balance difference is invariant under that scale, whichever comparison in `J` fails continues to fail.

For the tetrahedral input, at ordinary radii `B=C=1`, each ordinary face receives a singleton load `1/2` and two loads `1/4`. These are the complete stationary equations. Each of its two complete crossing rates is `2 f(1/4)>0`, while its positive-middle logarithms are zero. Its complete action is therefore `4 f(1/4)`, and it is strictly balanced. Each attachment adds one region and this complete action. The displayed formulas `R_r=17+r` and `Phi_r=Phi_17+4r f(1/4)` have the correct normalization.

The full-volume conclusion still invokes the proved closure theorem, the output diagram's knot-producing parity condition, and the long fixed signed filling theorem. The finite runner is not used to establish those analytic or geometric facts.

## Runner: what its checks establish

`check_sphere` verifies four distinct vertices at each quartet, distinct unordered triangles, two incident triangles at every edge, connected degree-two link graphs at every vertex, connected face adjacency, and Euler characteristic two. These are the finite incidence conditions for a connected closed triangulated sphere, excluding pinched vertices. It then checks distinct matching edges, that each matching edge pairs exactly its own two triangles, the retained protected nonmatching edge, and equality of ordinary-vertex and region counts.

Both branches of `attach_tetrahedron` have the claimed literal meaning. When the input middle is protected, the old triangle `(u,v,B)` is replaced by `(u,z,B)`, `(v,B,z)`, `(v,z,u)`. When the output middle is protected, `(u,B,v)` is replaced by `(u,B,z)`, `(v,u,z)`, `(v,z,B)`. Here `z` is the fresh vertex. In either case the three new oriented triangles have exactly the old oriented boundary; this is a stellar subdivision with the indicated new matching pair. The implementation retains the other old triangle and returns the index of the newly appended region for the next attachment.

The runner asserts at every step that the next region is outside `J` and that all four candidate ordered quartets are literally unchanged. It checks region count `17+n` and records every finite stage. Its default eight attachments end with 25 regions, 27 vertices, 50 triangles and 75 edges. I replayed these incidence checks independently from the imported functions and separately checked the two exact oriented-boundary identities. Both passed.

The runner re-executes the two interval verifiers with the same Python interpreter.
