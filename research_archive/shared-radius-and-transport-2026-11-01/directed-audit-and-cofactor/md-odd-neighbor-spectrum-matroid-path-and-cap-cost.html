Shared coefficient paths and low-rank transport of the cap estimate

This note proves a finite support and root-count theorem for an actual product of positive-semidefinite determinant pencils. It preserves the original shared face extraction, including repeated occurrences of a face and all monomial target shifts. It does not assert that an arbitrary physical spectral perturbation has the required signed determinant decomposition. That remains a separate source-identity problem.

1. Exact polynomial setting

Let the ordinary face variables be L1,…,LsL_1,\ldots,L_s, and retain two boundary variables A,CA,C. Introduce insertion variables t1,…,tkt_1,\ldots,t_k. Consider

F(A,C,𝑳,𝒕)=c0∏b=1Bdet⁡(AMb,A+CMb,C+∑fLfMb,f+∑jtjVb,j),c0>0, F(A,C,\boldsymbol L,\boldsymbol t) =c_0\prod_{b=1}^B\det\!\left( A M_{b,A}+C M_{b,C}+\sum_f L_fM_{b,f} +\sum_jt_jV_{b,j}\right),\qquad c_0>0, \tag{1}

where every coefficient matrix is Hermitian positive semidefinite and all matrices are finite. Fixed positive scalar factors are retained in c0c_0. A fixed monomial factor may be included by adjoining one-dimensional diagonal blocks. Protected zero faces are set to zero by removing their matrices; they are never replaced by positive weights.

Assume the product is nonzero. The sum of each block’s coefficient matrices is then positive definite. Thus (1) is real stable: when all variables have positive imaginary part, the imaginary part of each pencil is positive definite, precluding a kernel vector. A block-diagonal matrix combines the product into one determinant of size DD, the total degree of FF. The coefficients of FF are nonnegative, as also follows from the Cauchy–Binet expansion below.

Fix the original target vector 𝑲\boldsymbol K, and put

where all entries are nonnegative integers, 𝝁≤𝑲\boldsymbol\mu\le\boldsymbol K, and Q≠0Q\ne0. 2. Nonzero determinant coefficients: an elementary linear selection lemma

Factor each coefficient matrix of the combined pencil as UiUi*U_iU_i^*. Give every column of UiU_i the color ii. For variables ziz_i, Cauchy–Binet gives

det⁡(∑iziUiUi*)=∑S column subset|S|=D|det⁡US|2∏e∈Szcol⁡(e). \det\Bigl(\sum_i z_iU_iU_i^*\Bigr) =\sum_{\substack{S\text{ column subset}\ |S|=D}} |\det U_S|^2\prod_{e\in S}z_{\operatorname{col}(e)}. \tag{4}

Thus an integer color vector xx, with |x|=D|x|=D, has a positive coefficient if and only if one can choose a column basis with exactly xix_i columns of each color. Write

r(I)=dim⁡span⁡{columns whose colors lie in I}. r(I)=\dim\operatorname{span}\{\text{columns whose colors lie in }I\}. \tag{5}

The exact criterion is

xi≥0,|x|=D,x(I):=∑i∈Ixi≤r(I)for every color subset I. x_i\ge0,\qquad |x|=D,\qquad x(I):=\sum_{i\in I}x_i\le r(I) \quad\text{for every color subset }I. \tag{6}

Necessity is immediate. Here is a proof of sufficiency which does not assume a support theorem for stable polynomials.

First prove the following linear Hall statement by induction. For finite vector sets E1,…,EnE_1,\ldots,E_n, one can choose independent representatives ej∈Eje_j\in E_j if every subfamily indexed by II spans dimension at least |I||I|. For n=1n=1, choose a nonzero vector. If some nonempty proper subfamily is tight, let WW be its span. Induction chooses representatives spanning WW. In the quotient by WW, every subfamily of the remaining sets still spans at least its cardinality: apply the original inequality to its union with the tight family, and subtract dim⁡W\dim W. Induction in the quotient completes the selection. If no nonempty proper family is tight, choose any nonzero vector from the last set and quotient by its span. Every subfamily of the remaining n−1n-1 sets originally had at least one unit of dimension slack, so the quotient still satisfies the required inequality. Induction again completes the selection.

Now repeat the set of columns of color ii exactly xix_i times. For any collection of these copies, let II be its set of colors. The number of copies in the collection is at most x(I)≤r(I)x(I)\le r(I), so the linear Hall hypothesis holds. The resulting DD independent representatives are a basis and have the desired counts. This proves (6).

The rank function is submodular:

r(I)+r(J)≥r(I∪J)+r(I∩J). r(I)+r(J)\ge r(I\cup J)+r(I\cap J). \tag{7}

Indeed the span for I∩JI\cap J is contained in the intersection of the two spans, while the span for I∪JI\cup J is their sum. Consequently the union and intersection of two sets tight for a feasible xx are also tight: combine (7), modularity of xx, and both lower bounds r≥xr\ge x.

3. A monotone nonzero path preserving the shared targets

Let x,yx,y satisfy (6), and choose any color ii with xi>yix_i>y_i. There exists a deficient color jj, meaning xj<yjx_j<y_j, such that

also satisfies (6). To prove this, suppose every deficient jj is blocked. A violation after (8) can only occur on a set IjI_j containing jj and excluding ii, and integrality forces that set to be tight for xx. Their union II is tight, contains every deficient color and excludes ii. On the complementary set there are no deficient colors and there is the strictly excessive color ii, so

contradicting feasibility of yy. This proves the one-step exchange. Repeating it constructs a path changing only coordinates where the endpoints differ, monotonically toward the endpoint, with every intermediate coefficient positive.

Apply this result only for support purposes after substituting A=C=ZA=C=Z in (1). This merges the two boundary column colors. It does not merge the two variables in either final binary polynomial. Their nonvanishing is detected faithfully because the coefficients are nonnegative. The endpoint color-count vectors are

Each removes one unit either from an ordinary face target or from the boundary degree and adds one insertion unit. At every stage take the original shared coefficient with the current targets and insertion multiplicities, and multiply by the accumulated compensating boundary monomial. The initial and final polynomials are exactly (2) and (3), including their original scalar factors and multiplicities.

4. One swap changes the root count by at most one

Coefficient extraction of a real-stable polynomial is real stable or zero. This follows by differentiation in the extracted variables and then specialization to zero. Differentiation preserves stability by the elementary logarithmic-derivative proof of the one-variable Gauss–Lucas property; real specialization follows by a limit from the upper half-plane. Positive monomial multiplication preserves stability as well.

For an ordinary-face step, after the other fixed extractions, the two binary polynomials are proportional by positive constants to

p=[LKt0]F̃,q=[LK−1t1]F̃,h=[LK−1t0]F̃. p=[L^Kt^0]\widetilde F,\qquad q=[L^{K-1}t^1]\widetilde F, \qquad h=[L^{K-1}t^0]\widetilde F. \tag{11}

If insertion multiplicities have already been extracted, the constants in (11) are the relevant positive differentiation factorials and do not affect roots.

First suppose h≠0h\ne0. Then pp and qq each interlace the same degree-one-higher binary polynomial hh. To verify this precisely, fix C=1C=1, differentiate the required number of times in the auxiliary variable, and consider AA in the upper half-plane. Every auxiliary-variable root ζ\zeta lies in the closed lower half-plane. The ratio of the first auxiliary coefficient to its constant coefficient is therefore

∑ζ−1ζ, \sum_\zeta\frac{-1}{\zeta}, \tag{12}

with nonpositive imaginary part. It follows that the ratio p/hp/h, and likewise q/hq/h, has nonpositive imaginary part on the upper half-plane. A real proper rational function with this property has only simple real poles with nonnegative residues; a higher-order real pole would give both signs of imaginary part on small upper semicircles. Its partial fraction expansion is then strictly decreasing between consecutive poles, which proves interlacing, including common roots. For degree drops at infinity, first substitute A↦A+δCA\mapsto A+\delta C, C↦C+δAC\mapsto C+\delta A, with 0<δ<10<\delta<1, and then let δ↓0\delta\downarrow0. This keeps stability and makes the leading and constant coefficients positive. Repeated roots follow by the same limiting/interlacing argument.

The case h=0h=0 cannot simply be discarded. After the appropriate derivatives, the lowest nonzero total degree in (L,t)(L,t) is one, and positive rescaling of these two variables followed by a limit gives a stable polynomial of the form

aLp+btq,a,b>0. a Lp+b tq,\qquad a,b>0. \tag{13}

For fixed upper-half-plane A,CA,C, stability in L,tL,t forces q/pq/p to be a nonnegative real number. Otherwise some upper-half-plane tt makes −btq/(ap)-btq/(ap) lie in the upper half-plane as well, giving a zero. Since p,qp,q are nonzero stable polynomials, their ratio is holomorphic there. A holomorphic function taking only real values on the connected domain is constant. Hence p,qp,q are positive scalar multiples and have identical roots.

For a boundary-removal step, the unmultiplied child interlaces the parent and has degree one less. The compensating AA or CC factor adds one endpoint root. Any common previously accumulated boundary monomial adds the same roots to both sides.

Write every degree-dd nonzero binary polynomial in positive normalization as

P(A,C)P(1,1)=∏j=1d((1−λj)C+λjA),0≤λj≤1. \frac{P(A,C)}{P(1,1)} =\prod_{j=1}^d((1-\lambda_j)C+\lambda_j A), \qquad 0\le\lambda_j\le1. \tag{14}

The endpoints λ=0,1\lambda=0,1 record factors C,AC,A. Interlacing, including the case (13), proves that the unnormalized root distribution functions of successive degree-dd polynomials differ by at most one. The path from Section 3 therefore proves

sup0≤s≤1|#{j:λjP≤s}−#{j:λjQ≤s}|≤r. \sup_{0\le s\le1} \left|\#\{j:\lambda_j^P\le s\} -\#\{j:\lambda_j^Q\le s\}\right|\le r. \tag{15}

No nonzero intermediate coefficient or nonzero common interlacer has been assumed.

7. What is established for the original odd endpoint exterior

At odd levels every unchanged central endpoint coupon is exactly N2det⁡(bI+xH−/2+yH+/2)det⁡(cI+xH−/2+yH+/2)N^2\det(bI+xH_-/2+yH_+/2)\det(cI+xH_-/2+yH_+/2). Their product is (1), with the shared face variables identified before extraction and the original scalar N2(R−1)N^{2(R-1)} retained when a cap is removed. Extracting the other R−2R-2 ordinary targets gives the original binary degree dd, not a sum of independently optimized local degrees. Therefore every nonzero positive-PSD compression piece with compensating original face monomials satisfies (15)–(27), with no additional support-path hypothesis.

The argument does not assert that the actual varying-spectrum central source is such a decomposition with a controlled value of WW. Constructing it, bounding its total positive mass after the same extraction, and handling any macroscopic-rank tail remain separate obligations. Local physical nonstability makes that distinction necessary: a single neighboring central coupon is not itself stable throughout any one-sided endpoint neighborhood at all large odd levels.

The spectral order of a term must not be confused with its compression rank. The original skipping term usm2xyDbDcu s_m^2xyD_bD_c has order one in uu but removes two determinant degrees. A spectral series application must prove a relation such as rα≤Jkαr_\alpha\le Jk_\alpha, with a fixed JJ, between rank and perturbation order; it cannot silently set J=1J=1.

The present theorem supplies a finite transport tool for a proposed signed decomposition. It proves no new volume family, no even-level determinant identity, and no global remainder estimate without those missing source inputs.
