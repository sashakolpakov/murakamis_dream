A singleton triangle has zero Hessian. The ordinary graph obtained by deleting the adjacent
protected vertices u, v is connected: reroute paths through the cyclic link of u, then through
the connected link of v with u removed. Every surviving edge has positive conductance in the
displayed sum. Thus the full Hessian has exactly the constant kernel. The angle sums around
finite centers have zero first variation for a linearized circle-pattern solution, so all logarithmic
radius variations are one common scale.
Each of the two triangles adjacent to uv contains a finite circle tangent to both fixed lines.
Its radius is 1/2, so its scale variation is zero. All radius variations are therefore zero.
With radii fixed, each finite center triangle has one infinitesimal Euclidean motion. A
one-line triangle has only a horizontal translation. Removing the two triangles at uv leaves a
triangulated disk with connected triangle adjacency, containing all finite vertices and at least
one one-line triangle. Two adjacent finite triangles agree at two distinct centers; two adjacent
one-line triangles agree at their finite center. These comparisons propagate one horizontal
translation through the disk, and the normalization removes it. The kernel is zero.
The holomorphic implicit-function theorem now gives one circle pattern on a fixed polydisk
in all δe. The variables X, Y, r are complexified independently, without conjugating parameters.
The solution is real on real parameters and even in each δe.
