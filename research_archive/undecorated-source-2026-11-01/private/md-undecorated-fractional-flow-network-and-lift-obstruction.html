Fractional one-sided comparison flows and the load cost of an entropy lift

This note concerns the actual shared-face comparison problem for a fixed marked simple-sphere source. It does not prove a new full-invariant volume theorem. It isolates two exact facts about the fractional rows that remain even after one has fixed every genuine CROSS, LEFT and RIGHT label.

All ordinary face targets are one; the two protected targets are zero. A local triangle has normalized total load 1/21/2, and its entropy is C(α)=∑i=13f(αi),f(a)=Λ(πa)+Λ(π/2−πa)π,∑iαi=12. This is a comparison flow, not a decomposition of the finite LEFT polynomial. RIGHT interchanges the original middle positions.

The fixed-sector capacity is the maximum of the sum of these perspective entropies and the retained CROSS triangle entropies over the original face equations. This follows from the same compact hypograph separation used in actual_fixed_triangle_boundary_capacity_gap.md. The row perspective is continuous at t=0t=0; the entire domain is compact. Thus branching until every genuine label is fixed does not remove tt. It only removes mixtures between different genuine labels. The latter distinction is essential in actual_shared_radius_certificate_separation.md.

2. The doubled comparison domain is an integral network

These equations have an ordinary capacitated-flow realization. A CROSS triangle is a supply-one node with arcs to its supported face vertices. A selected one-sided row is a zero-supply intermediate node: an arc from cc to this node has capacity one, and its outgoing arcs go to its supported parent-triangle faces. The total demands equal the CROSS supplies: each selected row contributes total baseline two, and each CROSS coupon contributes two supply-one triangle nodes.

The map between this network and the comparison loads is exact. The intermediate incoming flow is tt; its outgoing flows are the zfz_f. Every original incidence remains separate, including repeated face labels if they occur in the allowed source. Negative quantities in (5) are simply supplies, not negative arc capacities.

Every vertex of this bounded network-flow polytope is integral. One elementary proof is to suppose an extreme feasible flow has a fractional arc. At every incident node its integer divergence forces another fractional arc. Following the fractional subgraph produces an undirected cycle. An alternating perturbation around that cycle preserves all balances and stays within the integer capacity bounds for both sufficiently small signs, contradicting extremality. Hence t∈{0,1}t\in\{0,1\} at every vertex.

This fact does not reduce the entropy maximum to those vertices. In z,tz,t coordinates the row objective is tC(z2t), t\,C\!\left(\frac{z}{2t}\right), \tag{6} which is concave. Its Jensen inequality gives a lower bound at an interior convex combination, not an upper bound by the endpoint values. Indeed even an ordinary CROSS triangle has zero entropy at every simplex vertex and positive entropy inside. Total unimodularity is therefore a feasibility statement, not the required volume comparison.

3. Exact stationarity of an interior fractional row

Suppose a feasible comparison maximum has finite common multipliers hfh_f, and a selected LEFT row has 0<t<10<t<1. Stationarity within its supported triangle gives its actual weighted maximizing allocation α\alpha. Differentiation in tt, with α\alpha fixed at that maximizer, gives Q(Lx,Lb,Ly)=12log⁡Lc. Q(L_x,L_b,L_y)=\frac12\log L_c. \tag{7} The calibrated triangle inequality is Q(Lx,Lb,Ly)≥12log⁡(Lx+Lb+Ly), Q(L_x,L_b,L_y)\ge\frac12\log(L_x+L_b+L_y), \tag{8} strict when at least two of the supported input weights are positive. Consequently Lc>Lx+Lb+Ly. L_c>L_x+L_b+L_y. \tag{9} in that nonmonomial case. In particular every supported input has smaller curvature than cc.

For a one-positive-input row, equality in (8) is possible: (7) then says that the sole supported input and cc have equal curvature. Such rows cannot be silently included in the strict ordering argument below. Likewise a radius infimum escaping to the boundary does not supply finite multipliers. Those cases remain separate.

For a row with t=0t=0, the one-sided maximizing derivative only gives Q(Lx,Lb,Ly)≤12log⁡Lc; Q(L_x,L_b,L_y)\le\frac12\log L_c; \tag{10} it does not give equality. The same strict ordering still follows if at least two supported inputs are positive. These statements use the concave comparison maximization, not stationary equations of the original signed source.

4. Completing the deficient row increases entropy but moves face load

Let rr be any probability vector on the supported parent-triangle corners. Complete (3) to two ordinary half-degree triangle rows by putting α̂=tα+1−t2r,β̂=12ec. \widehat\alpha=t\alpha+\frac{1-t}{2}r,\qquad \widehat\beta=\frac12e_c. \tag{11} Concavity and nonnegativity of CC give C(α̂)+C(β̂)≥tC(α). C(\widehat\alpha)+C(\widehat\beta)\ge tC(\alpha). \tag{12} The exact change of original face load is nevertheless q̂−q=1−t2(r−ec). \widehat q-q=\frac{1-t}{2}(r-e_c). \tag{13} No original target may be dropped. In particular choosing the parent middle for rr moves the deficiency along the actual middle edge; choosing either outer moves it along the corresponding actual MAIN edge.

Assume a nonempty collection of rows with t<1t<1 satisfies the strict curvature ordering (9), or its version following (10). Keep every other row, including every CROSS row, unchanged. Then no choices of the rr’s can make the sum of (13) vanish.

To prove this, choose an overloaded face of largest curvature among the selected rows with nonzero deficit. It receives a strictly negative contribution from each row overloaded there. It cannot receive a positive contribution from another selected row: such a row would have a still larger overloaded curvature. Thus the total face defect at this face is negative.

This proof permits several rows overloaded at the same face and needs no pre-existing forest description. It is also unaffected by merging equal-curvature monomial components first, provided every selected nonmonomial row remains strictly descending on that quotient.

Accordingly, acyclicity does not itself complete a fractional perspective flow to a feasible ordinary angle flow. It proves the opposite for this rowwise construction: the retained CROSS rows must also change, and their entropy change must be included.

6. What remains to prove

The universal fixed-sector perspective inequality is still open in this derivation. A successful proof must do at least one of the following while retaining the original source:

  • change the surrounding complete rows and bound their total entropy cost;
  • contract a larger actual block before taking absolute values, retaining the shared face demands;
  • use a quantitative signed phase estimate that exceeds the comparison-flow excess.

The existing anchored and genuine-sector certificate theorems remain valid, and the balanced terminal construction supplies their hypotheses on the decorated diagram. None of the statements here removes the decorations or proves a full invariant estimate on an arbitrary undecorated sphere.

The full invariant still uses its original Cartan character, sine factors, integer Fourier charges and whole first normal. The comparison-flow discussion neither changes those factors nor identifies a comparison tensor with the invariant.
