Two-row telescope audit

Exact two-row telescope and cyclic grading

The finite identities in §4 of md-undecorated-bipyramid-exact-cyclic-transfer.md have the stated signs and normalization. The two endpoints are both necessary. The cyclic trace in §2 is also exact with its stated degree states; no congruence or extra multiplicity is needed. This calculation gives no bound for the full cyclic source.

3. What the cyclic trace grading retains

Use the draft’s notation here: the bipyramid has an even number of equatorial faces, and dd is the quantum degree. For one transfer step, a=tj−1−u,b=u′−v,c=v′,p=d−a−b−c. a=t_{j-1}-u,\quad b=u'-v,\quad c=v',\quad p=d-a-b-c. \tag{11} The final quantity is exactly the south-pole degree of the local homogeneous coefficient. Thus, without any parity restriction on dd, ℓ(u′,v′)=ℓ(u,v)+d−tj−1−p,ℓ(u,v)=u+v. \ell(u',v')=\ell(u,v)+d-t_{j-1}-p, \qquad\ell(u,v)=u+v. \tag{12} At each ordinary target, tj−1=dt_{j-1}=d, this is the stated lowering by pp. Odd rows have p=0p=0. At the unique protected-target row j=1j=1, t0=0t_0=0; nonnegative a=−ua=-u forces u=0u=0, and this odd row has p=0p=0, giving the increase by dd.

On a closed state path, summing (12) forces total south-pole degree dd. This proves the claimed identity tr⁡∏j𝖳j(S)=Sdtr⁡∏j𝖳j(1)\operatorname{tr}\prod_j\mathsf T_j(S)=S^d\operatorname{tr}\prod_j\mathsf T_j(1). Conversely the face monomials uniquely specify the pending states, so this specialization introduces neither a trace multiplicity nor an alias. The exact degree is the integer degree, not a residue modulo the quantum level.

There is no missing grading hypothesis in §2. The limitation is in a subsequent estimate: after cutting the cycle into blocks, both pending degrees and the net south-pole degree must still be matched. An operator norm for an unrestricted block or independently optimized south-pole gauges would discard information that the trace identity itself retains. The aligned specialization in §4 must not be substituted for the opposite-offset cyclic transfer in §2 without a further source identity.

4. Finite exact reproduction

md-undecorated-cancellation-audit-two-row-telescope.py constructs the original bidiagonal Casimir at x=0,y=1x=0,y=1, forms all coefficients of its resolvent numerator by Chebyshev polynomial division, and then takes exactly the rows and columns dictated by the two original face targets. It compares the resulting polynomial, coefficient by coefficient, with (6) or (8) in the rational cyclotomic field. Levels 3–12 give 75 checked polynomial coefficients, including both smallest levels. The script also checks 1300 finite admissible degree states against (12). The JSON output is md-undecorated-cancellation-two-row-telescope-results.json.

These finite identities corroborate the exact derivation; they do not estimate a full bipyramidal source or identify a contributing filling charge.
