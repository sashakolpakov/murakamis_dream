The actual bipyramidal source as a cyclic polynomial transfer

This is a finite source identity, not a volume estimate. It applies to the opposite-offset matching on an even bipyramidal white sphere. It retains one common south-pole variable, every equatorial face demand, the original source polynomials and their spectral parameters. In particular it does not identify this diagram with a circle-operation tree or replace it by a product of scalar Newton edge kernels.

2. A transfer with two pending degrees

Set the original equatorial targets t0=0,tj=d(1≤j<m). t_0=0,\qquad t_j=d\quad(1\le j<m). \tag{5} Use states (u,v)∈{0,…,d}2(u,v)\in\{0,\ldots,d\}^2. Define the polynomial matrix 𝖳j(S)(u,v),(u′,v′)=hj(tj−1−u,u′−v,v′;S), \mathsf T_j(S)_{(u,v),(u',v')} = h_j(t_{j-1}-u,\ u'-v,\ v';S), \tag{6} with a coefficient declared zero if any displayed exponent is negative or outside its actual local support. Then the source in (3)=[Sd]tr⁡(𝖳0(S)𝖳1(S)⋯𝖳m−1(S)). \boxed{\quad \text{the source in (3)} =[S^d]\operatorname{tr} \bigl(\mathsf T_0(S)\mathsf T_1(S)\cdots \mathsf T_{m-1}(S)\bigr). \quad} \tag{7} This matrix has (d+1)2(d+1)^2 states. Its entries are exact local face coefficients. It is generally signed or complex, and is not asserted to be positive or uniformly conditioned.

For the proof, a monomial selection in (4) gives triples (aj,bj,cj)(a_j,b_j,c_j). The target on zj−1z_{j-1} is cj−2+bj−1+aj=tj−1. c_{j-2}+b_{j-1}+a_j=t_{j-1}. \tag{8} Just before row jj, set uj=cj−2+bj−1,vj=cj−1. u_j=c_{j-2}+b_{j-1},\qquad v_j=c_{j-1}. \tag{9} Then (8) fixes aj=tj−1−uja_j=t_{j-1}-u_j, and the next state is (uj+1,vj+1)=(vj+bj,cj). (u_{j+1},v_{j+1})=(v_j+b_j,c_j). \tag{10} These are exactly (6). Every feasible monomial gives one closed state sequence, and conversely every nonzero term in the trace gives one feasible monomial. The states are determined by that monomial, so there is no extra multiplicity. Nonnegative degrees and the targets imply all states lie in the stated range. Finally [Sd][S^d] retains the one original south-pole demand; it cannot be replaced by a product of separate south-pole estimates.

In fact homogeneity makes that last demand automatic once all the equatorial demands are enforced. Every selected local monomial has degree dd, their total degree is mdmd, and the prescribed equatorial degree is (m−1)d(m-1)d. Therefore tr⁡∏j𝖳j(S)=Sdtr⁡∏j𝖳j(1). \operatorname{tr}\prod_j\mathsf T_j(S) =S^d\operatorname{tr}\prod_j\mathsf T_j(1). \tag{7a} Thus the exact source is also the numerical matrix trace at the single common gauge S=1S=1. This uses the one shared homogeneous coefficient, not independent south-pole choices at the even rows.

The same construction works for any fixed genuine CROSS/LEFT/RIGHT assignment by projecting each original hjh_j to its exact path support before forming (6). It also works for the unsplit full-minus-CROSS difference, by subtracting the two complete traces. No projection is called a covariant coupon.

There is an exact grading that may be useful for estimating the transfer. Let ℓ(u,v)=u+v\ell(u,v)=u+v. At an ordinary-target step, homogeneity gives ℓ(u′,v′)=ℓ(u,v)−p, \ell(u',v')=\ell(u,v)-p, \tag{7b} where pp is the local SS-degree. Thus every odd row other than the marked-target step preserves ℓ\ell, and each even row lowers it by its actual south-pole degree. At row j=1j=1, where the previous face is the protected z0z_0, the nonzero conditions force u=0u=0 and ℓ(u′,v′)=d+ℓ(u,v). \ell(u',v')=d+\ell(u,v). \tag{7c} There is precisely one such increase. The total decrease around a closed state path is dd, agreeing with (7a). This grading does not take absolute values and does not alter the local coefficient phases.

3. Why this is not yet a uniform source estimate

The common south-pole demand couples the even rows; (7a) enforces it by total degree. The cyclic trace still couples the pending degrees at the two ends of any cut. Taking separate operator norms of the 𝖳j\mathsf T_j, or choosing independent local south-pole weights before the shared coefficient, removes these constraints.

The known finite filling-functional estimate means that a strict exponential bound on this original shared remainder would pass to the entire original fixed-power filling without an additional normal or Fourier-tail loss. It does not provide that shared bound. A concrete remaining algebraic target is therefore a signed estimate for (7), or for blocks of consecutive matrices with their two boundary states and their SS-degree all retained, compared with the original positive endpoint source and its non-CROSS support gap.

For odd levels that comparator must use the actual regularized full endpoint numerator. Bare evaluation at −2-2 omits its one-sided normal channels. The transfer (6) itself always uses the raw original coupon.

4. A root-safe two-row telescoping identity

The cancellation follows from the original finite recurrence and endpoint currents, not a choice of geometric saddle.
