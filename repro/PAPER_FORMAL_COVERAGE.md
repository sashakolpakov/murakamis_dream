This record maps all 24 current papers to exact compiled results and retained written inputs. It compares the main theorem statements with the saved Lean declarations and their explicit assumptions. The source hashes match the full compilation record. The review concerns those interfaces; it does not reread every local analytic or geometric proof.

Exact declaration names, theorem labels, written source locators and hashes are in [the coverage record](formal_coverage/coverage.json).

## What changed

The recurrence argument now includes the power-series ring map, admissible exponentiation, uniqueness of all-order coefficients, finite denominator clearing, projection to $h=0$, and cancellation of the nonzero meridian divisor. `gauged_classical_identity_from_all_orders` joins these operations. The physical finite equation must still be shown to have the specified expansion; this application premise is not a consequence of a graph edge.

The uniform-profile module retains the moving $A_N(s)$ and $H_N(s)$, constructs their compact positive bounds from uniform nonzero limits, and proves one nonvanishing threshold and a uniform actual logarithmic rate. The summed first coefficient plus $O(N^{-2})$ error and the directed $O(N^{-1})$ profile both supply its error hypothesis. This closes the elementary uniformity gap identified in the baseline report without assuming the desired uniform rate.

## Exact remaining boundary

The remaining operator application is to connect the formal construction to the physical recurrence: constructing the asymptotic coefficients of the specific finite shifted equation, retaining its physical reduced/full gauge, and verifying the formal theorem’s hypotheses. The written $q-1$ normalization and nonzero Laurent symbol, passage from formal identity to the selected holomorphic germ, and the Laurent-prime/irreducible-factor conclusion are also outside the compiled theorem. No operator-existence, full AJ, or full A-polynomial claim follows.

For the uniform profiles, the actual analytic estimates and the literal sine-quotient factor limits remain written. The new theorem proves the subsequent uniform implication, not the saddle or the source. For all volume papers, exact physical normalization, a nonzero full packet, the entire complementary bound and the absolute same-manifold volume calibration remain explicit application inputs. Generic real transfer already handles both parities, fixed signed tuples and common thresholds.

Actual mathematics, conditional assembly and metadata are different: the Lean modules construct operations and prove inequalities/limits; generic assembly theorems use explicit written input estimates; dependency DAGs verify named connections and scope without proving their vertices. No reviewed public statement asserts end-to-end Lean proofs of all 24 papers.
