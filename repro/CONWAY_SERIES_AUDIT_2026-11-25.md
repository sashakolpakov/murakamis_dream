# The Conway paper and its supporting proofs

25 November 2026.

This revision concerns the Conway manuscript and the six papers needed to read its proof. They are parts of the same research project, with shared lemmas. The long reference manuscript is an overlapping account of these results and other families; it is not an additional prerequisite, and it does not supply a second proof of every claim.

The Conway statement fixes a strict ordered tangle construction and a half-twist pattern whose main strands form one knot. Each full-twist integer is then chosen independently beyond a fixed absolute threshold and held fixed while the quantum level tends to infinity. The claimed limit is the volume of that knot's complement. The condition covers a set of natural density one among twist assignments for the fixed construction. It is not a density statement about knots ordered by crossing number.

Thus the analytic and geometric arguments meet at a specific critical action. Source bounds control everything outside the restricted positive sum. The lattice calculation determines that sum and its Fourier transform. The oscillatory theorem proves that its leading contribution survives the whole filling operation. The geometric theorem then identifies the same action, with the same disk framings and primitive slopes, with volume.

## Corrections made in this revision

The introductions now state the problem and the scope before listing intermediate results. Definitions include the ordered tangle operations, strict label inequalities, normalized wire indices, circle colors and deficits, canonical polynomial representatives, derivative data at double roots, and the exact core decomposition. Examples show a strict small construction and a nonzero polynomial class whose spectral values all vanish. The geometry papers explain the faces, gluing maps, peripheral curves and similarity logarithms before using them in their main statements. Their new figures display the packing and endpoint conventions.

Two notation errors affected formulas. In Newton and source bounds, an exposed leaf was written `h_0`, although that symbol also denotes the zero-color polynomial. Leaf occurrences are now distinguished from color subscripts. In source bounds the closing identity explicitly uses `h_{j_0}^{epsilon_0}`; it follows from the turn's adjointness in the full polynomial quotient. The Conway filling calculation also distinguishes the derivative datum from its logarithmic derivative.

Several compressed steps now have their hypotheses in view. The source-bound proof defines its completed comparison expressions before invoking their boundary inequalities. Every unflagged comparison with at least four effective leaves can be rooted between two completed expressions. The three-leaf comparison uses its explicit endpoint calculation instead. The repeated completed-subclass angle induction in Newton was removed: the general type-A boundary theorem and product identities already imply the needed bounds.

The Conway paper writes out the exact shifted Fourier reconstruction, Parseval calculation and coherent remainder array. Its remainder estimate applies to the whole closed source, and its polynomial coefficient cost is retained before taking the exponential rate. The shear calculation keeps the additive constant and primitive equation `U + p V = -2 pi i`. The reduced cut is explained as extraction of the scalar multiplying the identity endomorphism. These changes do not broaden the theorem's hypotheses.

## Written proofs, finite checks and Lean

The rational interval certificates establish the finite inequalities used in the source-bound optimization arguments, together with the enclosure and concavity proofs in the text. The finite algebra programs test the crossing, inverse powers, repeated-root derivatives, Fourier identities and midpoint normalization at their recorded levels. Those examples do not prove a contour deformation or an asymptotic estimate by sampling.

The [Lean coverage map](../formal/coverage.json) records the formalized statements. In particular, [FourierHermite](../formal-real/FourierHermite.lean) proves shifted reconstruction, Parseval and the coefficient estimate; [StationaryElimination](../formal-real/StationaryElimination.lean) proves the block-matrix elimination formula; and [ConwayAssembly](../formal-real/ConwayAssembly.lean) proves the coherent remainder implication using the coefficient bound. The polynomial modules formalize finite reduction and support arguments. The source-specific contour estimates, nonzero filling asymptotic and geometric volume identity have written proofs and remain explicit inputs to the corresponding formal assembly. Compiling those implications does not discharge their analytic or geometric hypotheses.

## What remains open

The finite-exception theorem is not proved. The long-twist threshold must hold in every filling coordinate. Keeping one coordinate short leaves infinitely many assignments outside that theorem, even though their proportion in expanding integer boxes tends to zero. Extending the result after an arbitrary fixed short partial filling still requires a general noncancellation argument with the prescribed closing functional. Neither a change of basis, a positive Gram norm nor the existence of a geometric critical point supplies that lower bound on its own.
