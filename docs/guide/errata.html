Corrections to the frozen reference manuscript

These corrections apply to the 374-page reference PDF, whose SHA-256 is e5b384c8fd103c1e1e7209ac97b3862b71abe227f8dc82e49dc0465bf4ae5437. The PDF and its TeX sources remain frozen while the results are rewritten as separate papers. This file records corrections without silently changing the version that earlier citations refer to.

Appendix D: the derivative of the filling equations

In Section D.4.3, printed page 127 (page 140 of the PDF), the Jacobian of the equations

Vℓ(χ)+rℓUℓ(χ)=−2πirℓ V_\ell(\chi)+r_\ell U_\ell(\chi)=-2\pi i r_\ell

with respect to χ\chi, at χ=r=0\chi=r=0, is

−2πidiag⁡(τℓ), -2\pi i\,\operatorname{diag}(\tau_\ell),

not the printed −4πidiag⁡(τℓ)-4\pi i\,\operatorname{diag}(\tau_\ell).

Here Uℓ=−2πiχℓU_\ell=-2\pi i\chi_\ell, Vℓ=−2∂χℓΦεV_\ell=-2\partial_{\chi_\ell}\Phi_\varepsilon, and τℓ=2icℓ/(1−εℓicℓ)\tau_\ell=2ic_\ell/(1-\varepsilon_\ell ic_\ell), where cℓ=cot⁡(πaℓ)c_\ell=\cot(\pi a_\ell). Direct differentiation of the leaf potential gives

∂χℓ2Φε(0)=−2πcℓ1−εℓicℓ=πiτℓ. \partial_{\chi_\ell}^2\Phi_\varepsilon(0) =\frac{-2\pi c_\ell}{1-\varepsilon_\ell ic_\ell} =\pi i\tau_\ell.

The mixed derivatives between an internal edge variable and a leaf deformation vanish at the complete point. Eliminating the internal stationary variables therefore leaves this diagonal Hessian unchanged. Differentiating V=−2∇ΦεV=-2\nabla\Phi_\varepsilon yields the corrected Jacobian. This also agrees with the complete Hessian in equation (B.41).

The matrix remains invertible because each Im⁡τℓ\operatorname{Im}\tau_\ell is positive. The implicit-function argument and its conclusion about independently sufficiently long signed filling slopes are unchanged. This correction concerns that classical deformation argument; it supplies no estimate for the quantum invariant.

Appendix R: the unchanged matched pair in the explicit core

In Section R.8, printed page 356 (page 369 of the PDF), immediately after equation (R.39), the sentence “The other unchanged region has positive outer weights” describes the wrong support. The outer weights are (2,0)(2,0), and the two middle weights are (1,1)(1,1). This is the pair of triangles T3,Z3T_3,Z_3, whose common edge is v3v0v_3v_0. Equation (R.36) assigns curvatures v3=2v_3=2, v0=0v_0=0, and P=S=1P=S=1.

The required strict inequalities still hold, with quantitative bounds. To see this directly, use the triangle potential QQ defined in equation (R.3), and write

f(t)=Λ(πt)+Λ(π/2−πt)π,g=f(1/4),Λ(s)=−∫0slog⁡(2sin⁡r)dr. f(t)=\frac{\Lambda(\pi t)+\Lambda(\pi/2-\pi t)}{\pi}, \qquad g=f(1/4), \qquad \Lambda(s)=-\int_0^s\log(2\sin r)\,dr.

For weights (2,1,0)(2,1,0), the admissible loads (1/4,1/4,0)(1/4,1/4,0) give

Q(2,1,0)≥2g+14log⁡2. Q(2,1,0)\ge 2g+\tfrac14\log2.

Both triangles have those weights, so their complete rate satisfies X=2Q(2,1,0)≥4g+12log⁡2X=2Q(2,1,0)\ge4g+\tfrac12\log2. Each overloaded middle has weight h=1h=1. Its pure-middle comparison is therefore X−log⁡h=XX-\log h=X, and its mixed comparison satisfies

Q(2,h,0)−12log⁡h≥2g+14log⁡2>350. Q(2,h,0)-\tfrac12\log h \ge 2g+\tfrac14\log2>\frac3{50}.

The last inequality follows already from the stronger comparison 2g−34log⁡2>3/502g-\tfrac34\log2>3/50 in (R.39). Thus this correction preserves the stated uniform core gap and the insertion theorem. It changes neither the matching nor the protected zero weight. The corrected description and its branch bounds appear in Section 9 of the triangular-insertion paper, after equation (9.5) and in the table following (9.10). The insertion audit also records the correction.
