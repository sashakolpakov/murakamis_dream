Research guide

Notation and conventions

The calculations in this guide pass through three kinds of object: a finite quantum expression, a function describing its large-level growth, and a geometric structure on a specified link complement. A symbol does not acquire a geometric meaning merely because it occurs in a critical-point equation. The proof must identify the relevant matrix, path, or gluing map. This chapter gives the conventions needed to read those identifications and to compare the different approaches in the mechanism and geometry chapters.

For a first reading, From a knot diagram to a volume limit defines the topological objects, writes out a finite contraction, and derives the coefficient estimate. This chapter is a reference for the notation used after those definitions.

Letters are reused in the technical archive. In particular, a tangle turn, a crossing matrix, and a shift of a meridian variable are different operations. Local definitions take precedence when a note uses another convention.

The invariant and the order of limits

Fix the diagram, its ordered tangle construction, its integer twist powers, and any finite certificate used by a theorem. Then let the integer quantum level NN tend to infinity. Statements for arbitrary finite trees or spheres allow arbitrarily large fixed diagrams; they do not allow the diagram to grow with NN. A universal sufficient twist bound and a uniform error estimate over all diagrams are different assertions.

Finite sums, colors and derivatives

A color specifies a representation on a physical strand or auxiliary circle. An internal channel is a representation label introduced by a decomposition of tensor products. A head index specifies a basis vector inside a representation. These are different finite indices.

The ordinary auxiliary color is j∈{1,…,N}j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}, and its highest weight is j−1j-1. Complete color means j=Nj=N, with highest weight dd. Its external deficit is h=N−jh=N-j, and the corresponding scaled deficit is χ=h/N\chi=h/N. Some older formulas use nn for this color; their nn must not be confused with a crossing power nin_i.

An internal polynomial or head index r∈{0,…,d}r\in\{0,\ldots,d\} often gives a scaled variable x=r/Nx=r/N. A different formula may use the half-shifted channel variable aj=j+1/2N,cj=2cos⁡(2πaj). a_j=\frac{j+1/2}{N},\qquad c_j=2\cos(2\pi a_j). For the folded spectral coordinate, set δj=12−aj,cj=−2cos⁡(2πδj). \delta_j=\frac12-a_j,\qquad c_j=-2\cos(2\pi\delta_j). On the representative half of the spectrum, the continuous variable δ\delta near zero describes the internal endpoint near −2-2; it is not the external color deficit χ\chi. At odd levels the endpoint δ=0\delta=0 is an actual simple node. At even levels the nearest representative has δ=1/(2N)\delta=1/(2N). Those finite offsets are retained in the interpolation.

The choice r/Nr/N, r/(N−1)r/(N-1), or (r+1/2)/N(r+1/2)/N must remain visible. They have the same interior leading limit in suitable regimes, but different exact endpoints and first corrections. For example, r1+r2≤dr_1+r_2\le d becomes x1+x2≤1−1/Nx_1+x_2\le1-1/N when xi=ri/Nx_i=r_i/N. Replacing it by a closed limiting inequality is an estimate, not an exact source identity.

A normal is a derivative in a specified direction. At a repeated Casimir node cc, a polynomial operator can depend on both p(c)p(c) and p′(c)p'(c); evaluating only p(c)p(c) loses its nilpotent part. This is the reason for Hermite data. A derivative in the generic parameter ζ=qNet\zeta=q_Ne^t, with NN fixed, is another operation. A factor that vanishes at t=0t=0 can have a nonzero derivative there. Neither derivative is the next coefficient of an N→∞N\to\infty expansion.

The physical polynomial compiler records the finite Casimir algebra and its repeated-root data. The homogeneous face source records how its polynomials become original matrix entries. Root-only identities must not be differentiated in a generic parameter without that additional source calculation.

Source polynomials, sectors and the invariant

The source is the full prescribed expression: physical matrices, their index ranges, cups, caps, the marked cut, and every scalar factor. A coupon is one specified operator inserted in a tangle box. A source polynomial is a way of storing the matrix entries of such an operator or the coefficients of a whole contraction.

In the homogeneous face description, a box has ordered faces (x,b,c,y)(x,b,c,y): b,cb,c are its middle faces and x,yx,y its outer faces. Its polynomial He(x,b,c,y)H_e(x,b,c,y) has total degree dd. For a diagram with RR boxes, the product has degree RdRd. With two protected faces u,vu,v, the original marked contraction is expressed by [∏f≠u,vLfd]∏e=1RHe(Lxe,Lbe,Lce,Lye)|Lu=Lv=0. \left[\prod_{f\ne u,v}L_f^d\right]\! \left.\prod_{e=1}^R H_e(L_{x_e},L_{b_e},L_{c_e},L_{y_e}) \right|_{L_u=L_v=0}. The brackets mean coefficient extraction, not substitution of face radii. There are RR ordinary targets, each of degree dd, and two protected targets of degree zero. A shared face has one variable wherever it occurs. Giving separate occurrences unrelated values would change the problem.

For physical coupons this coefficient formula gives the original scalar with its stated exterior normalization. If HeH_e is instead a spectral numerator depending on a variable σe\sigma_e, the extracted result SN(𝝈)S_N(\boldsymbol\sigma) is still a polynomial in those spectral variables. The original trace and filling functional remain to be applied. The general Morse source theorem keeps the turns, mark, primitive face grid, and both level parities in this description.

Several decompositions of the source are used:

Term Exact meaning
Complete-color source The original contraction with every designated auxiliary color equal to NN.
Color core The term retaining the complete-circle part of each exact external-color identity. It can be an entire function of χ\chi.
Color alias A complementary small-color term in that identity. Its exponential smallness is a theorem to be proved, not part of its definition.
CROSS sector The specified central-cut support in a local continuant/cofactor spectral numerator.
LEFT or RIGHT sector One of the complementary one-sided supports in that same numerator.

The capitalized sector names are finite-support labels; CROSS does not mean a physical crossing matrix. A color core and an all-CROSS projection arise from different decompositions and must not be identified by their names. A nonzero all-CROSS period with the correct geometric rate does not by itself prove the full invariant’s rate: the other sectors might contribute or cancel it. The cofactor theorem defines that projection, and the balanced planar theorem explains a sufficient comparison with the remaining sectors.

Tangles and geometric variables

A tangle box has four ordered endpoints. A product stacks compatible boxes in their specified order. A physical quarter-turn bends an incoming strand into an outgoing one and changes the input/output presentation. Its evaluation includes the original duality maps. The cylindrical closure joins lower endpoints to upper endpoints outside the box; the cap closure joins the two upper endpoints and the two lower endpoints respectively. These closures can have different topology. The knot test counts both components formed at final closure and any circles already closed inside a tangle.

The guide usually writes RR for a checked crossing operator, including the interchange of its two tensor factors, and TT for a tangle turn. Some older notes call a turn RR. The shift called TT in a quantum difference equation below is a third operation.

A construction tree records products and turns; it is not a knot projection. A circle-marked junction contains a crossing-circle operator. A bare junction is a maximal consecutive group of products without one. Resolving a high-valence product into binary products introduces internal bookkeeping variables, not new physical quarter-turn edges. For a vertex vv, dvd_v is its graph degree, including the specified boundary attachments. It has no relation to the quantum top index d=N−1d=N-1.

In strict-tree geometry, a physical turn edge has a normalized polynomial label xex_e. When exposed as the ordinary output of a rooted branch, that output angle is 1−xe1-x_e. The set ℰT\mathcal E_T in the longitude formulas consists of the original physical turn edges; hidden binary-resolution edges are excluded. The strict-tree geometric theorem explains this convention and the case xe=1/2x_e=1/2.

A geometric gluing face may be a quadrilateral with four ordered ideal vertices. “Ideal” means that its vertices lie on the sphere at infinity of hyperbolic space. The vertex order specifies the face identification. Some archive notes call such a boundary face a port; elsewhere that word refers to an external representation factor. It is necessary to check which meaning is intended.

Circle packings, shapes and geometric actions

In the face-source geometry, a positive real LfL_f is the curvature, or inverse radius, of a finite white circle. A protected value Lu=0L_u=0 denotes a straight line, not a circle of radius zero. Sending the marked tangency to infinity gives two parallel protected lines. The remaining circles lie in their common strip. Shared positive radii used in a Cauchy estimate are choices of weights; only a separately proved stationary identification makes them the geometric packing.

A complex number zΔz_\Delta attached to an ideal tetrahedron is its ordered cross-ratio shape. Its branch and orientation matter; a coordinate with positive imaginary part gives a positively oriented tetrahedron in the usual vertex convention. The same letter zz can instead be a formal polynomial variable, a spectral variable, or the microscopic meridian coordinate below. These uses are unrelated.

The real factorial rate uses J(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt,0≤x≤1, J(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt,\qquad 0\le x\le1, with the integrable endpoint limits. The Lobachevsky function is Λ(θ)=−∫0θlog⁡|2sin⁡u|du. \Lambda(\theta)=-\int_0^\theta\log|2\sin u|\,du. Thus J(x)=−Λ(πx)/πJ(x)=-\Lambda(\pi x)/\pi on that real interval. Complex continuations require a specified logarithm and path; the absolute value in the real definition of Λ\Lambda is not a holomorphic prescription.

For a positive complete source, ΦD\Phi_D or F0F_0 often denotes its maximal limiting action. Once the original geometry has been proved, its normalization is 2πΦD=Vol⁡(YD), 2\pi\Phi_D=\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D), where YDY_D is the complete augmented exterior. A filled critical action is generally complex. Its real part is converted to the filled volume only after the original primitive peripheral equations and the regulator boundary correction have been checked.

A boundary profile maximizes an open tangle’s action with one output index fixed. It retains that boundary constraint and its original joining terms. It is not an independent volume budget that can be added arbitrarily to a neighboring profile.

Packets, gaps and finite actions

A packet is a local asymptotic formula which includes the critical action, its nonzero amplitude, and a controlled remainder. A typical notation is ℐN=NβaNeNSN(1+O(N−1)). \mathcal I_N =N^\beta a_N e^{NS_N}(1+O(N^{-1})). Here ℐN\mathcal I_N is the specified integral or sum, β\beta is its level exponent, aNa_N is the amplitude, and SNS_N is the finite-level critical value. A nonzero lower asymptotic needs control of aNa_N and all contributions outside the packet. Merely knowing SN→SS_N\to S does not permit replacing eNSNe^{NS_N} by eNSe^{NS} with relative error tending to zero.

A strict exponential gap at rate Φ\Phi means an estimate such as |ℰN|≤NCeN(Φ−γ),γ>0, |\mathcal E_N|\le N^C e^{N(\Phi-\gamma)}, \qquad \gamma>0, uniformly on the parameter set stated in that theorem. A polynomial factor NCN^C does not change the rate. The constants and starting level may depend on the fixed diagram, fixed twists, compact domain, and derivative order. Uniformity through a complex neighborhood is stronger than a bound at physical integer samples.

A Hessian records second derivatives of the relevant phase. The face Cauchy integral and the spectral filling integral use different Hessians and different determinant branches. “Primitive lattice” means the retained integer summation coordinates have no hidden finite-index multiplicity. “First charge” refers to the unit Fourier magnitudes in the exact filling transform; other charges and endpoint currents remain part of the original period until estimated.

Augmented and filled complements; peripheral coordinates

An augmented exterior YDY_D is the complement of the main strands together with the auxiliary crossing circles. Filling an auxiliary cusp attaches a solid torus along the prescribed primitive slope and restores the twists. The resulting knot exterior is S3\K𝒑S^3\setminus K_{\boldsymbol p}. Its volume generally differs from Vol⁡(YD)\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D). The main knot cusp remains unfilled.

On each auxiliary torus, μi\mu_i goes once around its crossing circle and λi\lambda_i is the recorded disk longitude. They form a primitive integral basis. Choose the specified matrix eigenline. Let mim_i and ℓi\ell_i be the corresponding determinant-one eigenvalues divided by their values ±1\pm1 at completeness, so that both normalized eigenvalues equal 11 there. Continue their logarithms from that point and write Ui=2Log⁡mi,Vi=2Log⁡ℓi. U_i=2\operatorname{Log}m_i,\qquad V_i=2\operatorname{Log}\ell_i . These are logarithms of similarity multipliers, equivalently twice the normalized eigenvalue logarithms. For example, if the chosen auxiliary longitude matrix in the Morse collar has eigenvalue Ji=−1J_i=-1 at completeness, then ℓi=−Ji\ell_i=-J_i and Vi=2Log⁡(−Ji)V_i=2\operatorname{Log}(-J_i). At the smooth unit-charge filling saddle the convention is Ui+piVi=−2πi. U_i+p_iV_i=-2\pi i. The equation is an equality of the continued logarithms. Its exponential alone would allow a wrong winding, and at a parabolic longitude could permit a nontrivial filling holonomy. The primitive collar calculation fixes the paths; the metric completion theorem checks the actual log-link periods and the smooth solid torus.

Two useful descriptions of the auxiliary deformation are Ui=−2πiχiin the external-color coordinate,Vi=−4πiδiin the internal spectral coordinate. U_i=-2\pi i\chi_i \quad\text{in the external-color coordinate},\qquad V_i=-4\pi i\delta_i \quad\text{in the internal spectral coordinate}. They belong to the same selected branch only after the stationary equations and the marked geometric adapter identify them. In the angular circle-tree chart one instead writes Vi=2iτiV_i=2i\tau_i. The angle τi\tau_i is therefore not a cusp shape.

For the main cusp, normalize a horospherical Euclidean coordinate so that the positive meridian translates by 11. The preferred longitude follows the knot once with zero linking number; its translation is the cusp shape τK\tau_K. If UK=2log⁡MU_K=2\log M and VK=2log⁡(−L)V_K=2\log(-L) are centered at the complete lift, then dVK/dUK=τKdV_K/dU_K=\tau_K there. The sign −L-L centers the negative-unipotent preferred longitude lift L=−1L=-1.

Under surgery on the auxiliary circles, the image of the augmented main longitude need not remain preferred. If ki=lk⁡(K,Ci)k_i=\operatorname{lk}(K,C_i) is the integer linking number with the iith crossing circle, the stated disk convention gives λfill=λaugμK∑ipiki2. \lambda_{\mathrm{fill}} =\lambda_{\mathrm{aug}}\, \mu_K^{\sum_i p_i k_i^2}. This shear changes the marked basis on the filled cusp. The preferred-longitude proof tracks it independently of the quantum asymptotic.

The main-meridian quantum AA-period

The raw unrolled highest-weight coordinate is x=qNαx=q_N^\alpha. The marked meridian coordinate is M=−qNx. M=-q_Nx. Thus complete weight α=d\alpha=d gives M=1M=1, and quotient color α=j−1\alpha=j-1 gives M=−qNjM=-q_N^j. For the reduced, zero-framed ADO family FN(M)F_N(M), FN(1)=KN(K),FN(−qNj)=Cj(qN). F_N(1)=K_N(K),\qquad F_N(-q_N^j)=C_j(q_N). The ordinary reduced jj-color period is Cj(q)C_j(q). This notation is distinct from a color core also called CNC_N in some notes. The pointing theorem fixes these equalities and the component convention.

The microscopic variable is ss, defined by M=qNs=ez/(2N),z=2πis. M=q_N^s=e^{z/(2N)},\qquad z=2\pi i s. Bounded ss describes an O(1/N)O(1/N) neighborhood of M=1M=1; it is not a fixed meridian neighborhood. The proved strict-tree profile uses |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1, before the first ordinary-color resonances at s=±1s=\pm1. In particular FN(qN)=1F_N(q_N)=1 even when FN(1)F_N(1) has positive volume growth.

Difference operators, classical curves and selected solutions

A quantum AA-operator is a specified finite difference operator P(q,M,T)=∑j=0raj(q,M)Tj,(TF)(M)=F(qM),TM=qMT. P(q,M,T)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}a_j(q,M)T^j,\qquad (TF)(M)=F(qM),\qquad TM=qMT. Here rr is the operator order, fixed independently of NN; it is not the top color index dd. The aja_j are rational functions in the formal quantum and meridian variables. A genuine all-level annihilator comes from the original finite source before its values are evaluated. Choosing a new ratio of two values at each level is not such a construction.

An equation PF=0PF=0 does not select its solution by itself. The pointed period also retains a fixed vector c0(q)c_0(q) of small-color Laurent polynomials and the prescribed companion transport row rN(q)r_N(q). At a root pole its value is KN(K)=CT⁡t=0[rN(qN(1+t))c0(qN(1+t))]. K_N(K)=\operatorname{CT}_{t=0} \bigl[r_N(q_N(1+t))c_0(q_N(1+t))\bigr]. The constant term is taken after multiplying the entire Laurent germs. The parameter tt here is a local evaluation variable, not the geometric deformation M=eπitM=e^{\pi it} used in another note. The pointed operator theorem explains the finite initial data and its volume statement.

To form a classical symbol, first clear the specified rational denominators and remove the common power of q−1q-1, then take the first nonzero specialization and replace the commuting shift variable by LL. The geometric AA-curve records the meridian and preferred-longitude eigenvalues of the selected geometric representations of the knot group. An arbitrary eliminated source equation can contain other components or points excluded from its original chart.

In the geometric convention, MM is the positive main meridian eigenvalue and LL is the preferred-longitude eigenvalue on the same line. The source cut selects the M−1M^{-1} line, so LL is the inverse longitude eigenvalue there. At the complete filled knot, M=1M=1 and L=−1L=-1. On the covered strict-tree germ, Lgeom(M)=−Ma(D)∏e∈ℰTM2−Xe(M)1−M2Xe(M),Xe=e2πixe. L_{\mathrm{geom}}(M) =-M^{a(D)} \prod_{e\in\mathcal E_T} \frac{M^2-X_e(M)}{1-M^2X_e(M)}, \qquad X_e=e^{2\pi ix_e}. The full strict-tree longitude theorem identifies these source variables and paths, including bare product junctions. This replaces the earlier circle-tree-only identification. Its operator corollary, and the directed all-orders theorem, specify when the selected geometric irreducible factor divides the first nonzero classical symbol. Such divisibility does not identify every factor, its multiplicity, or the complete quantum recurrence ideal.

What a torsor records

A torsor is a space with a free and transitive action of a group, without a preferred origin. Choosing one point identifies it with that group, but a formula using the chosen coordinate must record the choice. Several different torsors occur in the archive.

For auxiliary holonomy, a half-longitude eigenvalue satisfies ℓ2=eV\ell^2=e^V, and the logarithm of ℓ\ell has integral-period ambiguity. The actual collar and complete anchor select the sheet and logarithm. A retained half twist changes Uϵ=U0−ϵ2V0,Vϵ=V0. U^\epsilon=U^0-\frac{\epsilon}{2}V^0, \qquad V^\epsilon=V^0. On the selected double cover this is an integral monomial change of peripheral coordinates. It does not authorize a half-integer substitution into the pure-link filling formula.

For a regular quantum difference module at a root of order mm, a chosen scalar frame can satisfy a cyclic norm equation ∏j=0m−1σj(a)=v, \prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\sigma^j(a)=v, where σ\sigma is the prescribed cyclic field automorphism and vv a central eigenvalue. Replacing the frame by ff changes aa to aσ(f)/fa\,\sigma(f)/f. This algebraic norm is not the absolute value of the knot invariant, nor an operator norm. The intrinsic norm-torsor construction states the additional hypotheses needed to compare its selected period with a regulator.

A third local example is the Casimir transport conic: after choosing a spectral sheet and a reference point, crossing powers act multiplicatively on its regular fibers. The endpoint fibers degenerate, and the original excluded divisors and boundary targets remain part of the problem. An abstract norm torus or a local conic does not by itself identify a knot’s geometric longitude or prove a nonzero source asymptotic.

These distinctions explain why the guide keeps the operator, regulator, relative-cycle, and full-source methods separate. Each technical route states which object is constructed and which comparison is still required.
