Research guide

What remains to be proved

The volume limit is now proved for every fixed Conway tangle expression that passes the strict type test, with independently sufficiently long signed twists and a knot-producing endpoint pairing. The results chapter defines the construction and the test. For that class, the passage from the full invariant to the volume of the actual knot complement is complete.

The main questions beyond it are coverage of knot presentations, short twists, and the relation between the quantum recurrence and its selected classical geometric factor. Other approaches to arbitrary plat diagrams remain useful. Their open hypotheses are described below as questions about those broader approaches, not as missing steps in the strict-tree theorem.

The boundary of the current Conway theorem

The strict-tree theorem concerns a specified ordered expression made from turned two-strand twist boxes, stacking, quarter-turns and closure. Its finite test asks whether the normalized state constraints admit strict interior values. It does not prove that every arborescent knot has such a presentation with all twist magnitudes large.

Three estimates close the previously missing mixed-tree case. The positive probe theorem controls a whole internal component while keeping its coefficient targets. The spectral propagation theorem carries losses in every physical color through that component. The geometric comparison identifies the constants with the original boundary action. These are proofs for the full signed source, not just an unsigned upper bound at an isolated critical point.

What remains is to understand presentations outside the strict test and how different tangle presentations of the same knot are related. Equality of two algebraic source contractions does not itself establish a tangle isotopy or preserve the meridian and longitude. The threshold in the proved theorem may depend on the entire fixed tree. It is not uniform over growing trees, and the theorem does not cover arbitrary short twists.

There is also a precise operator question. A genuine all-level recurrence for the ADO invariant and finite initial data selecting its solution are already available. The pointed quantum A-period theorem reconstructs the Kashaev invariant from that data, including the required derivatives at singular steps, and gives its volume limit for the covered knots. The unresolved stronger question is the identification of the selected classical geometric factor and the quantum module carrying its marked logarithmic regulator data. Operator existence and the scalar period theorem should not be listed as open for this class.

Language used in the alternative routes

The remaining sections concern several exact representations of the invariant. A source is a finite polynomial, tensor contraction or sum that equals the specified invariant after its indices are contracted. A coupon is one local operator in that contraction, usually attached to a crossing circle or twist box. A color is an integer representation label in the finite calculation.

A shared-face coefficient is obtained by assigning a variable to each face of a diagram, multiplying the local polynomials, and extracting the prescribed power of every ordinary face variable. The same face has one variable wherever it occurs. Taking separate local maxima would discard this constraint. The two faces at the mark are protected: their variables are set to zero in the appropriate coefficient formula.

A spectral parameter is a variable in the polynomial functional calculus of a local operator. Repeated spectral nodes require values and derivatives; the first derivative data are often called normal terms. A derivative in this spectral variable is different from a derivative in the quantum parameter qq.

An action is the function whose value gives an exponential rate, usually in an expression of the form eNAe^{N A}. A local packet includes that exponential, a nonzero leading coefficient and a uniform error near a selected critical point. A period is the contour or finite Fourier expression used to reconstruct the invariant. Showing that one packet exists does not prove that its period dominates every other term.

The general plat target and its quantifiers

A 2k2k-strand plat is a braid closed by pairing neighboring endpoints at the top and bottom. Fix a diagram DD, its crossing-circle augmentation YDY_D, and the actual meridian-longitude markings. The general plat target asks for a threshold T(D)T(D) such that every knot-producing fixed signed twist tuple with min⁡i|mi|>T(D)\min_i|m_i|>T(D) satisfies

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(KD,𝒎)|=Vol⁡(S3\KD,𝒎). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K_{D,\mathbf m})| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K_{D,\mathbf m}).

The diagram and twist tuple are fixed before NN grows. The threshold is allowed to depend on the diagram, not merely on the number of strands. The geometric filling theorem supplies the geometric part under its positive marked gluing hypothesis. A quantum source estimate is a further requirement.

One finite geometric certificate is the simple-white criterion: the actual white cellulation must be a simple triangulated sphere with the specified matching. It allows separating triangles and arbitrary fixed width. It supplies a labeled convex ideal right-angled polyhedron for the cut diagram. It does not assert that every hyperbolic plat passes the test or that the presentation is minimal.

Several explicit families already satisfy the required source estimates. The one-end layer family works at every fixed width k≥2k\ge2. The bridge-gap extension permits independent fixed insertion depths, and the marked unit-chain operation adds arbitrarily many regions to its specified cores. Their constructions and thresholds are explained in the results chapter. The Fox-coloring argument gives exact bridge number under an additional congruence; the earlier reduced-layer theorem is a separate included family. These are not classifications of all plats at those widths.

The complete augmented source is understood

Before the crossing circles are filled, the signed augmented-source theorem proves the complete-link equality

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|AD,N|=2Vol⁡(QD)=Vol⁡(YD). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|A_{D,N}| =2\operatorname{Vol}(Q_D)=\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D).

Here QDQ_D is the labeled ideal right-angled polyhedron for the cut diagram, and AD,NA_{D,N} is the marked complete-circle source with its framing normalization. Retained half-twists may independently have sign 0,+1,−10,+1,-1. Both level parities and composite levels are included. The theorem does not manufacture the geometric certificate for an arbitrary diagram. The coverage comparison explains prior overlaps and why parallel belts with a white bigon fall outside this certificate.

The algebra behind the result is useful more generally. The homogeneous source theorem assigns to every Casimir-polynomial coupon fef_e a homogeneous polynomial HfeH_{f_e} of degree d=N−1d=N-1 in its four face variables. The exact marked contraction is

The bracket means coefficient extraction, and u,vu,v are the two marked faces. The identity keeps the cup, cap, mark, parity and endpoint factors. The finite face grid and total degree rule out extra whole-level aliases; one does not choose an order for eliminating interacting operators.

For a complete circle, the positive face-polynomial formula uses

Da(x,b,y)=det⁡[bI+x2H−+y2H+],P̃s(x,y)=∑j=0sSsSjSs−jxs−jyj, D_a(x,b,y)=\det\!\left[bI+\frac{x}{2}H_-+\frac{y}{2}H_+\right],\qquad \widetilde P_s(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^s\frac{S_s}{S_jS_{s-j}}x^{s-j}y^j,

where H±H_\pm are the specified positive Hermitian matrices, Sj=∏r=1j2sin⁡(πr/N)S_j=\prod_{r=1}^j2\sin(\pi r/N), and s=d−2as=d-2a. Their coefficients are nonnegative and their combined degree is dd. The marked coefficient theorem gives the entire source as the sum over folded labels aa of these shared coefficients, multiplied by the explicit source scalars, multiplicities and half-twist phases. The normalization includes the factor NRN^R for RR crossing circles. Even-level carries cancel only in the whole marked expression.

The geometric rate follows from a sequence of global results. The transverse rank theorem removes the two framing directions and fixes the common scale. The positive coefficient saddle theorem controls the entire coefficient torus and gives a nonzero Gaussian contribution. The absolute action calculation cones the actual facets and fixes the additive volume constant. The uniform channel-rate theorem controls all folded labels at the same radii.

Near the maximal folded label M=⌊(N−1)/2⌋M=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor, write ai=M−tia_i=M-t_i and s0=N−1−2Ms_0=N-1-2M. The sector ratio has the relative form

FN(𝑴−𝒕)FN(𝑴)=exp⁡[−∑iCi,Nti(ti+s0)N](1+o(1)),Ci,N>0. \frac{F_N(\mathbf M-\mathbf t)}{F_N(\mathbf M)} =\exp\!\left[-\sum_i C_{i,N}\frac{t_i(t_i+s_0)}N\right](1+o(1)), \qquad C_{i,N}>0.

The exact half-twist phases sum to the nonzero factor (πN)R/2∏i(Ci,N−πiϵi)−1/2(\pi N)^{R/2}\prod_i(C_{i,N}-\pi i\epsilon_i)^{-1/2}. Thus the signed conclusion does not follow by discarding phases from the positive proof. The estimates control the intermediate and macroscopic tails as well as this central window.

The positive endpoint and its unique geometric maximum

Another useful comparison source is the positive spectral endpoint. For even NN, the endpoint theorem gives rate

WD=Vol⁡(YD)2π W_D=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)}{2\pi}

for the marked source with PN=−VN(−2)=4(2+ΩN)−1P_N=-V_N(-2)=4(2+\Omega_N)^{-1} at every crossing region. Here ΩN\Omega_N is the Casimir operator and VNV_N its polynomial resolvent numerator. This comparison source is not the filled knot invariant.

The closed half-block estimates and full-kernel identity give a continuous local entropy hh on the entire degree simplex. Every monomial of degree N−1N-1 occurs in the full positive kernel, so the global rate is the maximum of ∑eh(qe)\sum_e h(q_e) subject to the same shared-face demands. If individual cross and one-sided patterns are retained, the finite-level support test detects offsets that the limiting polytope alone can miss.

Several reductions prove that this maximum is geometric. Identity-forest contraction removes pure-middle identity coupons without changing targets. The hybrid Hall reduction keeps cross factors as two triangle rows and one-sided factors as single rows. Boundary regularity recovers positive shared multipliers on the resulting pieces. The representation assembly then constructs a representation of the original augmented-link group with relative volume 2πWD2\pi W_D.

That volume calculation uses the signed cut-ball regulator chain. It does not assume that an open regulator remains unchanged when its reference flag is reset. The previously found fixed-degree convexity counterexample is also not used as a false shortcut. The unique-maximizer theorem identifies the only maximizing flow with the marked complete geometry. Compactness gives a strict gap outside every fixed neighborhood, but not a gap uniform over all diagrams.

Identity insertions have a strictly lower rate or give zero, because the resulting representation kills an essential meridian. The serial-coupon estimate separately shows that a fixed literal chain on one ordered pair changes the positive source by at most a polynomial factor. It does not justify replacing arbitrary separating cuts by serial chains.

Odd levels need the independent spectral derivative. The odd regularization theorem uses

FN={−VN(−2),N even,VN(−2)−N−2∂σVN(−2),N odd. F_N=\begin{cases} -V_N(-2),&N\text{ even},\\ V_N(-2)-N^{-2}\partial_\sigma V_N(-2),&N\text{ odd}. \end{cases}

It has the same local entropy and global rate. If SNS_N is the shared contraction of the numerators, then

|∂𝝈αSN(−2,…,−2)|≤eRα!N2|α|ZD,N;FN. |\partial_{\boldsymbol\sigma}^{\alpha}S_N(-2,\ldots,-2)| \le e^R\alpha!N^{2|\alpha|}Z_{D,N;F_N}.

This derivative is in the spectral parameter, not in qq. Positive path estimates also control shrinking complex disks. The spectral complement theorem carries the strict one-sided gap to a small fixed neighborhood, with explicit cost ∑earcosh⁡(1+re/2)\sum_e\operatorname{arcosh}(1+r_e/2) for disk radii rer_e.

From a local packet to a whole filling period

The full spectral packet theorem gives a nonzero asymptotic for the entire numerator on a fixed complex neighborhood of σe=−2\sigma_e=-2. Writing σe=−2cos⁡(2πδe)\sigma_e=-2\cos(2\pi\delta_e), it has the form

SN(𝝈(𝜹))=(−1)(N−1)RN2ReNAN(𝜹)(2πN)(R−1)/2det⁡HN(𝜹)(1+O(N−1)). S_N(\boldsymbol\sigma(\boldsymbol\delta)) =(-1)^{(N-1)R} \frac{N^{2R}e^{N A_N(\boldsymbol\delta)}} {(2\pi N)^{(R-1)/2}\sqrt{\det H_N(\boldsymbol\delta)}} \bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr).

The finite action ANA_N and normalized Hessian HNH_N come from the one shared-face saddle, with the original parity offsets and mark. The square root is continued from its positive value. Convergence of ANA_N alone would not justify replacing it by its limit inside an exponential; the proof keeps the finite action until the end.

The local inputs are the exact cofactor factorization, ordinary-middle packet, and protected-middle packet. Interpolation rigidity proves exponential accuracy for the actual off-node polynomial, rather than for an arbitrary interpolation of repeated-node values. The global angular estimate, with its separate protected-middle proof, bounds the rest of the coefficient torus.

The marked-action theorem fixes the peripheral normalization. Restoring half-twists gives

A𝝐=A0+πi∑eϵeδe2,Ue=∂δeA𝝐,Ve=−4πiδe.A_{\boldsymbol\epsilon}=A_0+\pi i\sum_e\epsilon_e\delta_e^2, \qquad U_e=\partial_{\delta_e}A_{\boldsymbol\epsilon},\qquad V_e=-4\pi i\delta_e.

Here Ue,VeU_e,V_e are the chosen logarithmic meridian and longitude variables. For crossing exponent ne=ϵe−2pen_e=\epsilon_e-2p_e and Fourier charge ℓe\ell_e, the critical equation is Ue+peVe=−2πiℓeU_e+p_eV_e=-2\pi i\ell_e. At the unit-charge long-filling branch, the boundary correction cancels the Gaussian terms and gives the actual filled volume.

The local charge comparison makes the unit charge strictly largest in a fixed scaled box, with a lower gap proportional to ∑i(ℓi2−1)/ni2\sum_i(\ell_i^2-1)/n_i^2. This compares critical values only. The localized-period theorem additionally moves the original cube to the finite saddle, keeps all joining currents, and proves a nonzero Gaussian coefficient. All 2R2^R unit-charge signs contribute equally. Neither local statement by itself controls remote charges.

The whole cross-period theorem supplies that global control for the central source sector. The complete-radius balance theorem then controls the entire complementary source when its hypothesis holds. Its algebraic sufficient test is

(x+y+b)(x+y+c)≥max⁡(b2,c2), (x+y+b)(x+y+c)\ge\max(b^2,c^2),

using the four curvatures from one common complete packing. The triangle balance lemma proves this test, while the sharper zero-middle 9/49/4 estimate covers the explicit every-width family even when the simpler product test fails. The global one-sided estimate is uniform over the phases of that shared coefficient torus.

For arbitrary certified plats, the unresolved issue is a replacement for balance when it fails. It is not a missing local geometric coefficient. The fixed-root scope note also explains that this route does not require a generic-qq normal theorem.

The classified global upper problem

The global sharp majorant controls every closed degree and real spectral parameter. Let qq record the normalized shared loads, let Hδ(q)H_\delta(q) be its local upper rate, and let 𝒫D\mathcal P_D impose the original face demands. Then

sup𝜹|SN(𝜹)|≤eN(MD+o(1)),MD=maxq∈𝒫D,𝜹∈[0,1/2]R∑eHδe(qe). \sup_{\boldsymbol\delta}|S_N(\boldsymbol\delta)|\le e^{N(M_D+o(1))},\qquad M_D=\max_{q\in\mathcal P_D,\;\boldsymbol\delta\in[0,1/2]^R} \sum_eH_{\delta_e}(q_e).

The source path decomposition names the central part the cross sector and the two one-sided parts the left and right sectors. In normalized degrees, cross means that both middle loads are at most 1/21/2. A seam is their boundary; a non-cross load overloads one middle face. Exceptional cap corners meet both protected faces.

Boundary activation and stationary classification leave only complete cross factors, strict straddling one-sided factors and at most two marked caps at a maximizing configuration. The seam theorem proves Hδ≤hH_\delta\le h on all cross degrees. If aDa_D is the smallest geometric middle deficit, its seam gap is πaD2/2\pi a_D^2/2. The entire seam-neighborhood filling period is exponentially negligible relative to the nonzero cross period for fixed sufficiently long fillings.

Consequently, a competing non-cross maximum at or above the geometric rate ΦD\Phi_D must be macroscopically overloaded. The odd-cycle obstruction prevents a common phase assignment there. It does not give a sufficient numerical loss by itself. The flat-cap example proves that a uniform local exponential angular gap is false. The unresolved question is an absolute bound at ΦD\Phi_D, not merely a positive relative loss from a larger envelope. See the results discussion for the complete qualifications.

Phase estimates, energy bounds and finite covers

A regular source window restricts local starting and stopping rows away from singular endpoints. The four-state transfer identity keeps a before state, an active two-state block and an after state, including the direct diagonal path. The positive active transfer controls an outer phase mismatch. The analytic switching theorem controls a middle mismatch while retaining both boundary terms. The regular-window theorem supplies the strict endpoint losses needed to use that identity.

The mixed cycle criterion permits supported single-protected-face windows and entire cross coupons. Only nonzero incidences have phases. Fractionally packing incompatible cycles yields a shared loss e−κNe^{-\kappa N}. Its scope is summarized with the local extensions in the results chapter. To prove a general invariant bound, the positive majorant rate minus κ\kappa must still be below ΦD\Phi_D, and the decomposition must cover the whole source.

A different route uses one-sided relative energy. For a coefficient aN(q;σ)a_N(q;\sigma) and its positive endpoint reference pN(q)p_N(q), weighted Cauchy bounds the exponential rate of |aN|2/pN|a_N|^2/p_N by a triangle entropy. The folded-angle rearrangement is uniform through both parities and degree boundaries. The protected-outer theorem identifies its sharp local value.

The shared-flow criterion combines this energy gg with endpoint entropy hh and another envelope henvh_{\mathrm{env}}, using min⁡{henv,(h+g)/2}\min\{h_{\mathrm{env}},(h+g)/2\} on the same feasible flow. A strict maximum below ΦD\Phi_D on the non-cross set implies the full filling theorem. The missing statement is the universal shared inequality; no new local asymptotic conjecture is needed for this criterion.

The finite-sector cover theorem is another sufficient condition. It allows finitely many genuine cross/left/right branches, each with one common weight per face and a strict bound below the geometric rate. It extends the anchored criterion. On a fixed seventeen-region marked source, the separation theorem proves that every anchored choice fails by more than 0.0250.025, while forty refined branches pass with gap greater than 0.040.04. This establishes a strict extension of certificate classes, not a classification of unmarked knots under all possible presentations.

The undivided-capacity result explains why a single optimized positive torus can lose exponential accuracy. The fixed-triangle boundary gap settles certain endpoints, not all fractional mixtures. Universal existence of the finite-sector bounds remains open for the general plat route.

The external-color route and its exact cancellations

The external-color method changes the colors of the crossing circles before filling. It must distinguish internal folded-label deficits from external color deficits. The complete complex-core theorem gives a nonzero relative asymptotic on a fixed complex color neighborhood. Its finite action SNS_N and Hessian remain in the formula. The geometric deformation theorem identifies the limiting action by

2πRe⁡S(𝝌)=Vol⁡(ρ𝝌)+12∑iRe⁡UiIm⁡Vi,Ui=−2πiχi. 2\pi\operatorname{Re}S(\boldsymbol\chi) =\operatorname{Vol}(\rho_{\boldsymbol\chi}) +\frac12\sum_i\operatorname{Re}U_i\operatorname{Im}V_i, \qquad U_i=-2\pi i\chi_i.

For real small color parameters χi\chi_i, the boundary correction vanishes and the rate is the representation volume. Complex parameters require the correction. The varying-action transfer permits the filling calculation to keep SNS_N until its limit is taken.

The external reflection identity writes a color N−hN-h circle as a cosine-weighted complete term plus the literal color hh circle. In the window h=zNh=z\sqrt N, the complete term has a Gaussian limit fixed by the primitive cusp shape. At the opposite endpoint it has a full-size alias, canceled exactly by the small-color term. A bound for only the complete term would miss this cancellation.

The source-divisor theorem explains why nonzero leading amplitude at one point is not enough for every long slope tuple. A nonzero analytic germ allows an exceptional divisor; an amplitude that is a unit, or a specified coordinate monomial, gives the stronger uniform conclusion. The established complete core has a unit amplitude. The remaining source terms still need their own gap before that conclusion transfers to the invariant.

The finite local equation is also known. The fixed-fold Fourier period gives a four-component difference equation of bounded shift range with its actual finite polynomial solution and characteristic factors. Exponential fold interpolation controls the complex extension on the original grid. After face extraction, the equation couples neighboring coefficients; this particular local equation has not been reduced to a scalar knot equation by that elimination alone.

The coupled face system preserves the shared coefficients and selected fold relation. At a marked double root, the deformed circle-pattern theorem recovers the geometric radii and eigenline germs from a nonsingular finite system. The primitive collar calculation then fixes the actual disk word and half-twist shear. Its one-side multiplier and the square from a double reflection must not be interchanged.

Small-circle insertions and smoothing

The small-circle operator preserves the polynomial ring after all original paths are summed. Apparent negative powers cancel at the physical endpoints, and total degree excludes extra whole-level aliases. A color hh insertion costs at most Ch−1C^{h-1} in the stated weighted norm.

The simultaneous insertion theorem treats every untwisted subset with connected identity smoothing, even with cyclic dependencies or protected outgoing faces. It does not require a global elimination order; the ordered construction is a special case. For disconnected smoothings, the subset contribution is exactly zero when 2∑i∈J(hi−1)<N−12\sum_{i\in J}(h_i-1)<N-1. The demanded face degree exceeds the available degree. Deleted crossing circles carrying a half-twist are outside that untwisted statement.

A geometric gap can be supplied by the smoothing-capacity condition or the fixed-radius criterion. A capacity is the optimized exponential bound from the positive angle/weight problem. The latter note includes a matched-graph example where its restricted test fails, but there is no proved adapter making it a counterexample for an original flat plat.

The capacity decomposition handles unattained positive-radius infima. Loops are infeasible, separating two-edge cycles split the capacity additively, and nontrivial simple blocks carry actual right-angled polyhedra. Double-triangle blocks have zero contribution. This decomposition does not move the quantum mark or assert a finite quantum factorization.

The representation-volume strictness theorem proves a strict gap when the reconstructed representation kills an essential meridian. The block representation construction extends the argument to degenerate blocks, keeping the original cuts and shaded pairings. It identifies the regulator with the sum of block volumes without claiming that the whole exterior splits geometrically into those blocks.

These ingredients give the untwisted color corridor: for all sufficiently small normalized deficits on any fixed untwisted subset, the whole mixed source differs from its geometric core by an exponentially small relative error. Both parities are included. Its limit is the volume of the selected marked representation. A complete filled knot metric and a bound on the full complementary color region are further requirements.

For signed replacements, the homogeneous source theorem gives a finite shared-radius certificate. Each unchanged circle contributes its two-triangle potential; each replaced signed circle contributes its four-angle potential, while an unsigned replacement has the sharper log⁡max⁡(b,c)\log\max(b,c) bound. Every nonempty reflected subset must have a common face-weight choice whose total rate, after subtracting the demanded face powers, is below the complete rate. These finitely many strict margins absorb the insertion costs on one color cube. Existence of the certificates is not automatic. The single-circle signed theorem gives useful special cases and explicit curvature tests.

The positive Newton obstruction shows that a positive local kernel can have shared capacity above the complete geometric value. Its graph example is not asserted to come from the original flat-plat compiler. The resummed small-circle kernel also has a unique-path coefficient exponentially larger than its crossing reference, even after Newton cancellations. These are obstructions to local norm shortcuts, not counterexamples to a bound after the full signed extraction.

The peripheral volume-gap theorem gives another geometric tool: keeping a fixed peripheral trace square away from the parabolic value forces a uniform volume loss. Applying it to the quantum source still requires a bound for the whole period, including its finite endpoints and signs.

Direct twist transport and the classical operator question

The direct-power identity keeps a signed twist power inside its original box. Two kernels on the same pair compose by one coefficient extraction. This retains repeated-node derivative data and avoids introducing an external-color expansion.

The Casimir conic theorem describes the resulting local characteristic. With outer weights fixed, A=x+yA=x+y and P=xyP=xy, it reads

σ=2+(b+z+A)2P−bz,bj+1+σbj+bj−1+2A=0. \sigma=2+\frac{(b+z+A)^2}{P-bz},\qquad b_{j+1}+\sigma b_j+b_{j-1}+2A=0.

On a regular generic fiber, the cover λ2+σλ+1=0\lambda^2+\sigma\lambda+1=0 turns the transport into multiplication by λn\lambda^n in a norm-one coordinate. At σ=−2\sigma=-2 the transport is additive; at σ=2\sigma=2 the fiber is a nonreduced double line. The excluded divisors and choice of reference point matter. The word torsor in this route records that the multiplicative coordinate has no preferred origin until a reference is chosen.

The physical meridian theorem identifies the local map with the actual crossing move (A,B)↦(A−1BA,A)(A,B)\mapsto(A^{-1}BA,A), preserving BABA, and gives the inverse for a negative crossing. With the cup, cap and mark phases included, every regular layered critical point produces a representation of the filled knot group. This forward construction does not prove that every root of a cleared equation lifts to the regular chart, classify escaping or singular points, or give a nonzero finite-sum coefficient for the geometric branch.

Taking absolute values of all Hermite spectral terms gives an envelope at least as large as the drilled volume. It cannot prove the smaller filled-volume rate. The spectral total-derivative identity therefore keeps the full polynomial numerator, both the ordinary and nilpotent terms at repeated nodes, all nonzero Fourier charges and endpoint currents. Its zero charge vanishes identically.

The Gaussian numerator formula is

(−1)N−1VN(−2cosh⁡w)=LN+2∑h=1N−1cosh⁡((N−h)w)Lh. (-1)^{N-1}V_N(-2\cosh w) =L_N+2\sum_{h=1}^{N-1}\cosh((N-h)w)L_h.

The LhL_h are the physical-color coupons. This is a polynomial identity, not just an equality of sampled values. It gives a positive majorant near the negative endpoint, and a subexponential closing-cap cost near the other endpoint on a shrinking cap for fixed twists.

The even cross/one-sided reduction partitions the positive numerator into paths crossing the middle rows and paths staying on one side. The crossing part lies between NHM/2N H_M/2 and 3NHM/23N H_M/2, coefficientwise, for the maximal complete-channel polynomial HMH_M. The remaining one-sided factors form a rooted forest under the stated diagram hypothesis. They need not vanish, and their half-sized blocks still have level NN. The global cross gap and whole-period theorem control the cross contribution; a general bound for the macroscopic one-sided complement remains the issue beyond the sufficient criteria above.

The earlier matching-edge character relation and continuant operator period remain exact inputs. The retained alternating Newton equations match the primitive-collar trace relation after the specified elimination and rational change of variables. The local action comparison identifies the selected reciprocal logarithmic action with the complete-core action up to πi\pi i. Its continued critical point lies outside the terminating low-color range, so this germ identity does not select a contributing finite-sum cycle.

These local comparisons do not themselves identify the classical geometric factor of the knot’s quantum A-operator. That limitation coexists with the separately proved all-level operator and pointed-period theorem described at the start of this chapter. The source and torsor library retains the different operator, regulator and relative-cycle approaches, including their counterexamples and corrections.

How the alternatives fit together

For strict Conway trees, the full source estimate and marked filling argument already prove the knot-volume limit. For general certified plats, the complete augmented source, positive endpoint, complex geometric packet and entire cross period are established. Balance, finite sector covers and shared-flow energy inequalities give sufficient conditions for the remaining source. Universal validity of those conditions is the outstanding issue in those routes.

The pretzel, belt, signed Conway, Fourier, weighted-Cauchy, cube and other constructions remain in the results chapter and research library. Their narrower hypotheses should be kept when using them. In particular, a local norm obstruction, a projected cube calculation or an equality of classical actions is not a counterexample to the volume conjecture, and it is not by itself a proof of the full invariant limit.
