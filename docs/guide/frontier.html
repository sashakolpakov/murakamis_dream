Research guide

What remains to be proved

The current papers prove volume limits for specified diagram classes under explicit sufficient conditions. The central open problem in this project is to remove the requirement that every surviving filling be long. The question is about the original reduced Kashaev invariant and the volume of the complete knot complement.

The results chapter gives the established hypotheses. The research status records the separate extension project. The manuscripts are frozen at their stated results; exploratory lemmas are not added to their premises.

The established long-filling result

Fix a finite strict Conway construction, its auxiliary crossing circles and a half-twist pattern whose strand connections form one knot. Let rr be the number of auxiliary circles. Write 𝒑=(p1,…,pr)∈ℤr\boldsymbol p=(p_1,\ldots,p_r)\in\mathbb Z^r for the primitive integer filling coefficients and K(𝒑)K(\boldsymbol p) for the resulting knot. All this data is fixed before the quantum level NN grows.

The Conway theorem supplies a threshold PP, depending on the fixed construction, such that every fixed assignment with |pj|>P|p_j|>P for all jj has

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K(𝒑))|=Vol⁡(S3\K(𝒑)). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N} \log|K_N(K(\boldsymbol p))| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K(\boldsymbol p)).

The signs and relative magnitudes of the coefficients are unrestricted. The strictness and knot-component tests remain part of the theorem. The ordered-reduction result extends its use to presentations whose surviving net twists satisfy these conditions. A large written exponent can cancel against another one, so the hypothesis must be checked after that reduction.

For this fixed parameter lattice, long assignments have natural density one. The excluded set is contained in a union of slabs {|pj|≤P}\{|p_j|\le P\}. Each slab is infinite when r≥2r\ge2. This does not give finitely many exceptional assignments, or a density statement for distinct knots ordered by crossing number.

Other papers permit some short powers under different conditions. The circle-tree criterion uses summed or directed degree-and-twist inequalities. Planar weak balance, terminal decorations and certified triangular gluings have their own hypotheses. Those results do not prove the arbitrary-short-filling extension of the Conway theorem.

The desired statement after partial filling

Choose a proper subset S⊊{1,…,r}S\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,r\}, and fix an arbitrary integer tuple (pj)j∈S(p_j)_{j\in S}. These coefficients may be short, zero or have mixed signs. The proposed extension asks for a threshold PSP_S, allowed to depend on the construction and every already fixed coefficient, such that all assignments satisfying

|pj|>PS(j∉S) |p_j|>P_S\qquad(j\notin S)

and producing hyperbolic knots have the same original-invariant volume identity. The partially filled exterior need not satisfy the positivity conditions used at the unfilled augmented point. It must be analyzed with its actual remaining boundary data.

This is stronger than proving another collection of isolated short-word examples. The partial-filling formulation keeps these quantifiers explicit. The proposed statement remains open.

If it were established for every proper subset and every fixed tuple, a finite branching argument would give finitely many exceptional assignments for each original template. At the first stage, an assignment outside the long region has some bounded coordinate. There are finitely many choices of that coordinate and its bounded value. Fix one such choice and apply the proposed theorem to the remaining coordinates. Repeat. There are at most rr stages and finitely many branches at each stage, so only finitely many fully bounded leaves remain. A threshold at a later branch may depend on all earlier choices; no uniform threshold over every partial filling is required.

Assignments and knot types must still be distinguished. Different tuples can represent the same knot. In particular, zero fillings and exact cap reductions can turn an apparently long twist into removable curls. The remaining scalar is then the unresolved core invariant, up to its known phase. Such padding shows why a general partial-filling theorem can be substantially stronger than a collection of long-slope estimates.

A covering shortcut also needs care. If a finite unbranched cover extends over a filled solid torus, a primitive meridian upstairs maps to the primitive meridian downstairs, up to sign. In the pulled-back drilled metric its length is unchanged, while a degree-dd boundary cover multiplies horotorus area by dd. Its normalized slope length therefore becomes ℒ/d\mathcal L/\sqrt d. This does not turn a fixed short filling into a geometrically long one. The current research status explains this restriction; a quantum descent theorem would be additional work.

What a different orthogonality can simplify

Let BB synthesize a physical boundary vector from coefficients: v=Bcv=Bc. In a positive Hermitian pairing,

∥v∥2=c*B*Bc. \|v\|^2=c^*B^*Bc.

The matrix B*BB^*B is the Gram matrix of the spanning vectors. It annihilates directions that represent zero. Replacing it by the identity is valid for orthonormal coordinates, or after proving a suitable comparison for the actual matrix. Squaring unrelated expansion coefficients separately can otherwise count contributions that cancel when the boundary states are assembled.

The conceptual chapter and formal statement guide make this distinction precise. The original closing row remains part of the data. A norm upper bound does not supply a lower bound for that one matrix coefficient.

For the full color-orbit transform, Fourier orthogonality is already exact. Arrange the complete value and ordinary colors as cN=(KN,C1,…,CN−1)c_N=(K_N,C_1,\ldots,C_{N-1}), and use a unitary discrete Fourier transform. If uNu_N is the transform of its first coordinate vector, then

ĉN=KNuN+eN,eN⟂uN,∥ĉN∥2=|KN|2+∥eN∥2. \widehat c_N=K_Nu_N+e_N,\qquad e_N\perp u_N,\qquad \|\widehat c_N\|^2=|K_N|^2+\|e_N\|^2.

The ordinary-color estimate bounds ∥eN∥2\|e_N\|^2 polynomially in NN, with the diagram fixed. The missing geometric growth of the actual norm is not changed by choosing another orthonormal basis. A better basis may nevertheless make that norm accessible.

Internal tangle coordinates raise a different question. Repeated Casimir roots require both values and derivatives. The local Hermite frame retains both directions, and the ordinary-cap tree identity gives a positive normalized pairing for its specified trees. Arbitrary interior crossings and overlapping gluings still require their own compatibility argument. A channel invisible to one closing row can become visible after another crossing; it cannot be discarded early.

A fixed-degree minimum for the original source

The normalized Conway formulation turns exact packet equations into a positive least-squares problem. At each proper vertex, let xvx_v be the full boundary packet, including its repeated-root directions. After the stated normalization, a leaf, turn or product has a prescribed map Φv\Phi_v, and LL is the original normalized closing row. The energy is

EN(x)=∑v proper∥Φv((xc)c child of v)−xv∥2+|Lxtop|2. E_N(x)=\sum_{v\text{ proper}} \|\Phi_v((x_c)_{c\text{ child of }v})-x_v\|^2 +|Lx_{\mathrm{top}}|^2.

The variables lie in a product of unit packet balls. Each product is bilinear, so the energy has polynomial degree at most four. Its degree is fixed as NN grows; its number of coordinates is not. The full Newton packet has dimension NN.

The note proves uniform strong convexity for this specifically normalized problem, an interior minimizer, and the comparison

Btot2eN≤|KN|2≤197196Btot2eN,eN=min⁡EN. B_{\mathrm{tot}}^2e_N\le |K_N|^2 \le\frac{197}{196}B_{\mathrm{tot}}^2e_N, \qquad e_N=\min E_N.

The known budget BtotB_{\mathrm{tot}} contains all local normalization costs. It can contribute to the exponential rate and must be retained. These inequalities include source zeros. Positivity of the energy does not assert that its minimum is positive for every assignment.

The orthogonal elimination chooses one child packet at each binary product and retains those variables as uu. The other variables, denoted zz, enter the residual linearly:

EN(u,z)=∥A(u)z+b(u)∥2. E_N(u,z)=\|A(u)z+b(u)\|^2.

The original normalized maps make the relevant linear solve uniformly nondegenerate and its solution feasible. Thus the inner minimization can be performed exactly:

minzEN(u,z)=b(u)*[I−A(u)(A(u)*A(u))−1A(u)*]b(u). \min_z E_N(u,z) =b(u)^*\left[I-A(u)(A(u)^*A(u))^{-1}A(u)^*\right]b(u).

The bracket is an orthogonal projection. Its Gram factors remain in the formula; they have not been replaced by independent coefficient costs. An equivalent boundary formulation has a Gram matrix between II and (226/225)I(226/225)I. The original scalar observation is retained. Elimination removes linear variables, although the resulting rational or expanded polynomial expression need not still have degree four.

The unresolved step is to evaluate this minimum at the geometric rate after arbitrary fixed partial fillings. By the displayed comparison, this would require two-sided exponential estimates for Btot2eNB_{\mathrm{tot}}^2e_N at rate Vol⁡(S3\K)/π\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K)/\pi. In particular a lower bound is needed. Substituting a trial configuration into a minimization problem gives an upper bound. It does not prove that every admissible configuration has energy at the desired scale.

Strong convexity does provide a useful lower certificate. With the real Euclidean gradient, the same note proves

DN(x):=EN(x)−12∥∇EN(x)∥2≤eN. D_N(x):=E_N(x)-\tfrac12\|\nabla E_N(x)\|^2 \le e_N.

This polynomial has degree at most six. A configuration with DN(x)>0D_N(x)>0 gives the genuine lower bound Btot2DN(x)≤|KN|2B_{\mathrm{tot}}^2D_N(x)\le |K_N|^2. For a geometric argument, one would need configurations whose energies have the desired rate and whose gradient defects are small relative to those energies. An absolute error bound is insufficient when the energy itself is exponentially small. Producing such a controlled positive certificate for every fixed short partial filling is still open.

Geometry and noncancellation are separate inputs

Casson–Rivin optimization concerns the volume on an angle-structure domain. At a positive interior critical point, the permitted variations supply the geometric gluing equations. The actual face identifications, primitive peripheral slopes and absolute volume normalization remain essential. The geometry chapter and marked-filling paper explain those steps for their stated constructions.

For an oscillatory quantum sum, a geometric critical point must also occur with a nonzero coefficient in the original contour or discrete sum. Competing contributions must not cancel it at the same exponential scale. The whole filling theorem provides this conclusion from explicit local and global source hypotheses. Extending those hypotheses across an arbitrary short component remains part of the proposed partial-filling theorem.

The external-color route and its exact cancellations

An external color is an auxiliary representation label used to expand a crossing-circle operation. The full finite formula can contain complementary colors, or aliases, in addition to the selected central expression. A small-color term can cancel an alias of a complete-color term. Estimating the latter alone changes the expression being bounded.

The finite spectral paper retains the derivative channels and the full closing functional. The Conway source estimates control the required physical and Fourier complements for their stated strict constructions. The Casimir-source paper evaluates the central contribution for its broader planar setting. Its conclusion is distinct from an unrestricted bound for every complementary source term. A positive local majorant or one small-color corridor is therefore not a full source theorem.

Direct twist transport and the classical operator question

The alternative operator approach keeps a signed power inside its original tangle and follows the resulting quantum relations. Its local classical spectral variables are not automatically the preferred meridian and longitude of the closed knot. The gluing and closing maps must identify the peripheral curve with its original marking.

The meridian-expansion paper derives a selected classical factor under its stated hypotheses, including a fixed nonzero annihilating operator and the needed source expansions. It does not construct a universal operator from an arbitrary local polynomial. The torsor discussion explains the coordinate and descent questions separately. The present finite-exception target still concerns the original scalar invariant, whether its proof uses Fourier sums, a variational minimum or operator relations.
