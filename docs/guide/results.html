Research guide

Volume equalities: the families and the hypotheses

The results on this page compare a finite quantum invariant with the volume of the knot or link complement that the diagram actually constructs. For a knot KK, the principal conclusion is

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K)|=Vol⁡(S3\K). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K)| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K).

Here KNK_N is the reduced Kashaev invariant in the normalization fixed in the proof. The knot is held fixed while the positive integer level NN grows. The statements below include both even and odd levels unless a smaller scope is explicitly stated.

There are two main ways to specify the proved families. The Conway construction builds a knot from two-strand tangles according to a finite tree. The plat construction starts with a braid and pairs its endpoints. The hypotheses for these constructions are different, so they are described separately. Later sections explain useful conditional theorems and local identities whose conclusions are smaller than a full knot-volume theorem.

One twist threshold for arbitrarily many tangles

There is a stronger statement about the quantifiers for the ordered circle-tree construction described below. A single integer threshold works for every finite tree in that construction. It does not have to be increased when more tangle boxes are added or when their nesting becomes deeper.

Let dvd_v be the number of tree edges incident to vertex vv, and let nvn_v be the signed number of half-twists in its braid. The local theorem assumes maxvdvdv2+nv2≤2−120. \max_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2}\le2^{-120}. The actual strand pairing must give one knot, with no extra closed component inside a tangle. Under these conditions the knot complement is hyperbolic, and the Kashaev volume equality at the top of this page holds through both even and odd levels. The theorem also gives the uniform normalized meridian profile on |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1.

For n≠0n\ne0, the elementary inequality dd2+n2≤12|n| \frac{d}{d^2+n^2}\le\frac1{2|n|} shows that |nv|≥2119|n_v|\ge2^{119} at every vertex suffices. The huge number is an explicit sufficient threshold, not a proposed sharp bound. The assertion of interest is that it is independent of the number of tangles. Each tree and every twist count is still fixed before NN grows. The theorem allows either twist sign and does not require the magnitudes at different vertices to be comparable.

Here is the reason that a local condition is enough. Evaluate a subtree but leave its attaching edge variable uu free. Its exact finite evaluation is a polynomial Mv,N(u)M_{v,N}(u). Only the attaching edge contains uu, so its trigonometric degree is at most N−1N-1, regardless of how many boxes lie below it. The proof shows that Mv,N(0)≠0M_{v,N}(0)\ne0 and controls the ratio Mv,N(u)/Mv,N(0)M_{v,N}(u)/M_{v,N}(0). Factoring out this exact denominator preserves the contribution already obtained from the subtree. At the next vertex the error needs to be smaller only than the change caused by that vertex’s filling. The proof supplies this comparison, including all high indices, repeated-root derivatives, and Fourier boundary contributions.

The triangles abbreviate subtrees of the construction. The angle uu is retained; the trace acts on tt. The figure’s denominator is the exact subtree value whose nonvanishing is proved in the induction.

The critical point produced by this calculation solves the same equations as the positive deformation of the original octahedral pieces. Its primitive filling periods give cone angle 2π2\pi at the endpoint, so the completed metric is smooth on the original knot complement. The volume constant is fixed by the complete augmented link. Thus the geometric identification and the noncancellation estimate are both proved for this class.

The local and summed conditions below are alternative sufficient tests. The summed condition has a substantially better numerical constant; the local condition has no restriction on the total number of vertices. Neither includes every short-twist knot.

An explicit bound that also permits some short twists

There is a numerical criterion for a second, precisely specified construction. Take any finite ordered tree with at least one edge. At each vertex place an integer two-strand braid. Stack the turned child tangles on their prescribed sides of that braid, and use a terminal vertex for the cylindrical closure. The order and turns are part of the diagram. Following all strands must give one knot, without an additional component closed inside a tangle.

To describe its geometry, replace each integer braid by a crossing circle and retain its parity as a half twist. Every junction in this tree has such a circle. Let dvd_v be its number of incident tree edges and nvn_v its signed number of half-twists. The quantitative theorem proves that

∑vdvdv2+nv2≤2−25 \sum_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2}\le2^{-25}

implies hyperbolicity and the Kashaev volume limit for the original knot complement. The whole tree and all integers are fixed before NN tends to infinity. Both parities of NN are included. The same theorem gives the uniform meridian profile on M=eπis/NM=e^{\pi i s/N}, |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1, with the first correction determined by the preferred cusp shape.

This bound has two useful consequences. If the tree has EE edges, then |nv|≥8192E|n_v|\ge8192\sqrt E at every vertex suffices. Short powers are also possible: start with any ordered tree on mm vertices, with any fixed powers, and attach BB terminal circles to each vertex. If the added powers have magnitudes at least BB, the sum is less than 2m/B2m/B. Hence B≥226mB\ge2^{26}m suffices, provided the resulting strand pairing is a knot. These very large constants make the result explicit; they are not proposed as sharp thresholds.

A product junction without a braid can be represented by the identity braid R0R^0. Adding its crossing circle changes the augmented link; filling it on the specified zero-twist slope recovers the original diagram. Its contribution to the criterion is 1/dv1/d_v. This can help at a high-degree junction, but does not give a free way to remove the numerical hypothesis.

The volume identification is proved under the same inequality. The actual augmented exterior has two octahedra per tree edge. Their positive deformations keep the original primitive filling curves; the final cone angle is exactly 2π2\pi, so the filled metric is smooth. The boundary-corrected action equals its volume, with the constant fixed at the complete augmented link. The finite-sum proof then supplies the nonzero contribution and bounds every competing region. Neither of these two arguments is left as a hypothesis.

This theorem does not cover arbitrary short-twist arborescent knots. The following long-net-twist theorem covers other presentations and allows junctions without crossing circles without the displayed degree condition.

Arbitrarily many tangles with sufficiently long net twists

Fix an ordered construction from two-strand twist boxes, quarter-turns and stacking, followed by the cylindrical closure of a product of two boxes. Each original twist box has its own integer nin_i. The number of boxes and the depth of their nesting may be arbitrary, but remain fixed in the theorem.

The net-twist theorem gives a finite reduction of this construction. It partitions the original boxes into disjoint groups B1,…,BsB_1,\ldots,B_s and assigns each surviving box the sum

Wj=∑i∈Bjni. W_j=\sum_{i\in B_j}n_i.

The groups depend on the written construction, before the integers are chosen. Moving a twist box past another tangle can rotate that tangle through a half-turn. The reduction records these rotations. Thus the ordered output may depend on the parities of the integers, although the groups and their sums do not.

The output is either a strict presentation of the kind described in the next section, or the closure of a single two-strand braid. The second possibility gives a torus link or knot and is excluded from the hyperbolic conclusion. In the first case, there is a finite threshold BB depending on the original construction such that

|Wj|>B(1≤j≤s) |W_j|>B\qquad(1\le j\le s)

implies the volume equality above whenever the actual closed link is a knot. The reduction and the type of its output are determined before taking a quantum limit. The twist integers are then fixed, and NN tends to infinity through all integer levels. The threshold is uniform over the finitely many parity choices; the magnitudes of the different sums need not be comparable.

For example, let h(c)h(c) be a quarter-turned box with cc half-twists, and consider

C(Rah(c)Rbh(d)). C\bigl(R^a h(c)R^b h(d)\bigr).

Here RaR^a and RbR^b are integral braid boxes in the same stack. They can be brought together and replaced by Ra+bR^{a+b}. The standard rational box h(c)h(c) is preserved by the half-turn needed during this move. The reduced closing expression has one integral box and the two ordinary boxes h(c),h(d)h(c),h(d), so it passes the strict test. Its size conditions concern |a+b||a+b|, |c||c| and |d||d|. Replacing a,ba,b by a+2m,b−2ma+2m,b-2m leaves the knot and the sum a+ba+b unchanged, even though both written magnitudes can become arbitrarily large. This is why the theorem uses net twists.

The knot condition concerns all strands, including any circles already closed inside an intermediate box. Checking only the pairing of its four exposed endpoints can miss such a component. The proof retains the component count. A one-component closed link has no internal closed circle, because that circle would remain separate from the component formed by closing the remaining arcs.

The reduction is an ambient isotopy of the filled link. It therefore preserves the reduced invariant at every level and identifies the knot exteriors. Applying the strict theorem to the output supplies a complete hyperbolic metric; pulling that metric back to the original exterior gives exactly the volume in the equality. The argument compares the filled knots. Their auxiliary augmented links need not be the same.

This gives a theorem for arbitrary fixed ordered cylindrical compositions with sufficiently long net twists, subject to the knot condition and the elementary exception. It does not prove the conjecture for every short-twist arborescent knot. The following extension handles cap closures and exact cancellations explicitly.

Cap closures and exact cancellations

A cap closure joins the two upper endpoints to each other and the two lower endpoints to each other. Write it as D(E)=C(TE)D(E)=C(TE). Unlike a cylindrical closure, a cap closure of a stack can split along a sphere meeting the link in two points:

D(FG)=D(F)#D(G). D(FG)=D(F)\mathbin{\#}D(G).

The two pieces are joined along the components containing the cut arcs. If the input is a knot, both pieces must be knots. A zero twist can expose this operation: the quarter-turn of the zero braid is a cup and a cap, so C(h(0)Q)=D(Q)C(h(0)Q)=D(Q).

The arbitrary-closure theorem repeatedly makes these cuts, combines twists, and removes zero sums. It retains all components and all labels. Its terminal factors are strict presentations, two-strand torus braid closures, or explicit unknots. Every surviving strict weight is still a sum of a disjoint group of original integers. The groups can now change when a sum vanishes. There are only finitely many possibilities for a fixed expression, so one threshold covers them all.

Suppose the original knot is hyperbolic and every surviving strict weight exceeds this threshold in absolute value. Then there is exactly one strict factor, and all other factors are unknots. To see why, take a meridian in each nontrivial factor. Both a hyperbolic factor and a torus-knot factor contain another commuting element outside the cyclic meridian subgroup. Two such factors would produce two elements which commute with the common meridian but not with each other. In a complete hyperbolic knot group this is impossible: the centralizer of a nontrivial parabolic consists of commuting translations. A sole torus factor is excluded by its nontrivial center.

Removing the unknot summands now gives an actual isotopy to the long strict knot. This proves the volume equality for the original complement, as well as the same pointed quantum A-period and Gram limits. It is a broader test for presentations of already covered knot types; the analytic theorem for the strict factor is unchanged.

An optional sufficient condition is especially simple. For every nonempty set BB of original boxes, require

∑i∈Bni=0or|∑i∈Bni|>BE. \sum_{i\in B}n_i=0 \quad\text{or}\quad \left|\sum_{i\in B}n_i\right|>B_E.

Then every hyperbolic knot from the chosen expression satisfies the volume equality. In particular, for a fixed integer vector aa, take ni=tain_i=t a_i. Every nonzero subset sum has magnitude at least |t||t|, so the conclusion applies to the hyperbolic knots obtained when |t|>BE|t|>B_E. The integer tt is fixed before NN grows. A given ray need not contain hyperbolic knots: the terminal factors determine that topological question.

One can also avoid assuming hyperbolicity in advance. Under the longness condition and the one-component test, the input is hyperbolic exactly when the reduction contains one strict factor and all others are unknots. The elementary factors are explicit: D(Rs)D(R^s) is always an unknot, and the knot C(Rs)C(R^s) is an unknot exactly for s=±1s=\pm1. The proof supplies a finite criterion; it does not supply numerical values for the analytic thresholds.

Every strict Conway composition tree

Begin with two strands carrying nin_i signed half-twists, denoted by RniR^{n_i}. Turn the box through a quarter-turn TT to obtain the basic piece hi(ni)=T(Rni)h_i(n_i)=T(R^{n_i}). The subscript distinguishes the pieces; their integers are independent. Ordered multiplication FGFG stacks two boxes, and C(FG)C(FG) is the prescribed cylindrical closure of their remaining endpoints. The endpoint order, the turns and the closing convention are part of the presentation.

The strict-tree theorem applies to any fixed expression of this form that passes a finite recursive test. The labels in the test describe possible normalized output states of an open box:

Label Interval of strict output states
L (0,1/2)(0,1/2)
H (1/2,1)(1/2,1)
A (0,1)(0,1)

These state variables belong to the proof; they are not crossing numbers. Every basic piece starts with label L. A quarter-turn exchanges L and H and leaves A unchanged. At a stack, LL, LA and AA give A, while HL and HA give H; either input order is allowed. HH has no strict state. At the final closure the pair must be AA, AL, AH or LH, in either order. The compiler proves that these rules are equivalent to strict feasibility of the state constraints, rather than merely necessary tests.

For example, each stack of two basic pieces has label A, and closing two such stacks passes. Closing only h1h2h_1h_2 has root pair LL and fails. A failed test says that this theorem does not apply to that presentation; it is not a counterexample to the volume conjecture.

For every passing presentation τ\tau, there is a finite threshold B(τ)B(\tau). Choose integers with |ni|>B(τ)|n_i|>B(\tau), with either sign and with no restriction on their relative sizes. Follow the strands to check that the resulting endpoint pairing has one component and no internal closed component. Then the resulting knot is hyperbolic and has the volume limit above. The entire presentation and every twist integer are fixed before NN tends to infinity. The threshold may depend on the whole tree.

The proof uses auxiliary circles around the twist regions. Filling these circles inserts the chosen twists. All auxiliary circles are filled in the knot conclusion; the knot cusp remains unfilled. Thus the right side is the knot complement volume, not the complete augmented-link volume and not the volume of a closed three-manifold.

The estimate for a general tree must preserve cancellation across large internal components. The positive probe bound controls such a component without changing its coefficient targets. The spectral propagation theorem carries strict losses in the physical color variables through it. The geometric comparison identifies the exponential constants with the action of the original cells. These estimates restore the full signed source from its nonzero local geometric contribution.

The pointed quantum A-period corollary gives an operator formulation of the same result. A genuine recurrence for the ADO invariant, together with finite initial data, selects the knot’s solution. At singular steps it retains a bounded number of derivatives in the quantum parameter. The resulting signed scalar period is exactly the Kashaev invariant and therefore has the same volume limit. This is a statement about that selected scalar, not about a matrix norm or a sum of absolute values.

The same corollary applies to the net-twist theorem: the normalized presentation constructs the same knot and hence the same selected invariant. No equality of two presentation-dependent recurrence operators is required.

The volume limit while the meridian varies

The reduced ADO invariant is a Laurent polynomial in a meridian parameter, with Kashaev’s invariant as its value at completeness. Write this polynomial as FN(M)F_N(M), in the zero-framing convention with unknot value one. Thus FN(1)=KN(K)F_N(1)=K_N(K). The moving-meridian theorem proves a uniform volume limit and a nonzero profile for every fixed strict Conway tree with independently sufficiently long signed twists.

The strictness test is the one defined above. There is no further restriction on the size, valence or adjacency of its multiplication junctions. To describe the proof, group consecutive multiplications with no quarter-turn between them into one junction. A junction carrying an original crossing-circle operator is a circle junction; one with no such operator is bare. A connected group of bare junctions can have any fixed finite tree shape. A product with many branches is treated as a whole product, with its actual turns and branch order retained.

The general component comparison controls the cancellations in each such group. The quantum calculation uses a finite polynomial basis with indices 0,…,N−10,\ldots,N-1. Moving the meridian changes some multiplication traces. The proof first separates each trace into its complete value and its difference from that value. It estimates the entire difference, rather than taking the absolute value of every local crossing term.

An index is called high when it exceeds ⌊(N−1)/2⌋\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor. With one high index hh, the Fourier estimate bounds the trace difference by a complete tensor with that index reflected to N−1−hN-1-h. The reflected pairing joins the two neighboring products into one larger product in a positive comparison tree. A coupled pressure inequality shows that deleting these turns cannot increase the maximum exponential rate. It retains the correlations between every remaining branch. With several high indices, a separate half-label comparison gives a boundary state of the same positive calculation. Strict Fourier and boundary estimates then make the total correction exponentially smaller than the original leading rate.

This is a tree of quantum operations, not a knot diagram. The comparison does not assert that deleting a turn preserves a knot. The invariant keeps the original deformed edge factors, colors and framing, and the geometric conclusion still concerns the original knot complement.

Earlier proofs covered separated bare junctions, arbitrary trivalent components, and components satisfying a path inequality. Those remain included cases, with their original proofs in the source-bound note, trivalent extension, and path estimate. They are no longer additional conditions on the moving-meridian theorem.

The general theorem also permits larger bare junctions and arbitrary adjacency between them. It uses the actual reduced operation tree, without replacing a physical turn by an artificial edge.

Restore the twists by filling all auxiliary crossing circles. There is a threshold depending on the fixed presentation such that every choice of signed crossing powers nin_i above that threshold in absolute value gives the following conclusion, provided the resulting diagram is a knot. The powers have their prescribed parities, may have either sign, and need not have comparable sizes. Fix them before taking the limit.

Put qN=eπi/Nq_N=e^{\pi i/N} and let M=qNsM=q_N^s. For every fixed R<1R<1,

sup|s|≤R|2πNlog|FN(qNs)|−Vol(S3\K)|→0. \sup_{|s|\le R} \left| \frac{2\pi}{N}\log|F_N(q_N^s)| -\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K) \right|\longrightarrow0.

Both even and odd levels are included. This is uniform at every meridian in the indicated disk, and the invariant is nonzero there for all sufficiently large levels. The disk has radius of order 1/N1/N in the MM-coordinate. The twist threshold does not depend on RR, although the required starting level may.

The main analytic issue is cancellation after the twists are restored. The proof expresses the actual crossing powers by Fourier integrals and compares all Fourier indices at a common critical point. This matters when the twist sizes are very different: a small error controlled for one crossing cannot simply be compared with the total change in volume. The joint comparison proves that the first Fourier term has a nonzero coefficient and that all remaining terms have a smaller exponential rate. The geometric calculation then identifies the critical action with the marked Dehn-filling action of the original augmented complement. The peripheral equation uses the primitive filling curve, with one full angular turn. The resulting solid tori fill the auxiliary crossing-circle cusps; the knot cusp remains complete and unfilled. The volume in the formula is therefore the volume of the actual knot complement.

The first correction records the shape of the knot cusp. A horospherical cross-section is a Euclidean torus. The geometric longitude identity and this asymptotic apply to the same strict-tree class under the stated long-twist hypotheses. Passage through s=±1s=\pm1, the first ordinary-color resonances, remains open for the general strict-tree profile. The selected classical geometric factor is identified for the covered trees of crossing circles; extending that identification to strict presentations with bare junctions remains open. The construction and twists cannot grow with NN, and small surviving twists are not covered.

The intrinsic transfer under tangle reduction gives the same uniform volume limit, nonvanishing and preferred-cusp expansion for the net-twist and arbitrary-closure presentations described earlier. For a cylindrical input, its reduction must give a strict knot with sufficiently long surviving net twists. For either cylindrical or cap closure with zero sums, the input must satisfy the closure theorem: a hyperbolic input with sufficiently long surviving strict weights reduces to one strict knot factor and unknot factors. The reduction is an actual ambient isotopy after those unknot factors are removed.

This transfers the whole Laurent family FN(M)F_N(M) with its transported orientation and zero framing, not just the complete value FN(1)F_N(1). The meridian and preferred zero-linking longitude are transported as well, so the knot volume and τK\tau_K agree. A single threshold covers the finitely many parity and zero-sum outcomes of a fixed input expression. The presentation-dependent scalar AN(s)A_N(s) can be computed on the strict representative; no equality of the two auxiliary augmented complements or their raw scalar ledgers is needed.

An exact calculation for oriented tangles at a varying meridian

There is also a general finite result, independent of any large-twist assumption. The oriented tangle theorem expresses every fixed Conway construction using its actual strand colors, turns, products and primitive closing trace. It retains the dual color on a reversed strand and all repeated-root information. The Newton coordinate changes and their inverses have polynomial bounds in NN when the displacement ss, defined by M=qNsM=q_N^s, stays in a fixed compact set.

The quarter-turn theorem now computes the physical turn itself, with the dual strand colors and the braiding that identifies their two orders retained. In fixed polynomial coordinates it sends the input Newton coefficient with index nn to index N−1−nN-1-n, multiplied by Wn(s)HN(s),Wn(s)=∏r=1n(1−sin⁡2(πs/N)sin⁡2(πr/N)),HN(s)=sin⁡(πs)Nsin⁡(πs/N), \frac{W_n(s)}{H_N(s)},\qquad W_n(s)=\prod_{r=1}^n \left(1-\frac{\sin^2(\pi s/N)}{\sin^2(\pi r/N)}\right), \qquad H_N(s)=\frac{\sin(\pi s)}{N\sin(\pi s/N)}, with one additional scalar 11 or qN±s2/2q_N^{\pm s^2/2}, determined by the strand orientations. The proof includes the repeated channels and the normalization of the actual evaluation and coevaluation maps.

The accompanying multiplication theorem gives every coefficient explicitly. It includes the products with i+j≥Ni+j\ge N, where i,ji,j are Newton basis indices. These products vanish at completeness but generally become nonzero when ss moves. Their signs and interior exponential rates are determined by the formula. Their nonzero first derivatives come entirely from the moving basis: in fixed polynomial coordinates multiplication has zero first derivative at completeness. In those coordinates, multiplication by the Casimir changes by a single matrix entry.

These local identities are inputs to the source-growth theorem just stated. The general component proof keeps the signed whole-junction traces, separates their differences from the complete tensors, and compares the resulting entire trees while retaining the original geometric constants. A turn norm by itself would not supply this whole-sum comparison, and polynomial control of node derivatives does not automatically preserve angular localization. The operator criterion provides another precise sufficient condition: uniform bounds for the reconstruction frame and a lower singular-value bound for a linear system of bounded size. Under those conditions, an already proved complete volume limit extends uniformly to a small microscopic meridian disk. The conditions have not yet been proved for a general Conway family.

Included tree families and the manuscript

The weighted-tree theorem supplies an explicit construction with one independent integer per vertex, arbitrary fixed branching, and chains of vertices. Its literal closure is specified in the theorem. If HH is the number of basic pieces and voctv_{\mathrm{oct}} the volume of a regular ideal octahedron, its complete augmented exterior has volume 2voct(H−1)2v_{\mathrm{oct}}(H-1). The knot volume is the separately calibrated value after filling; it is not replaced by that complete volume.

The manuscript treats an earlier included family. Start with two-piece stacks, then allow turns, stacks of constructed boxes, and the attachment of one basic piece on either side. The technical name “completed box” refers to this recursive class. Close a stack of two such boxes. The paper gives the detailed proof for that class; the strict-tree theorem extends it to all expressions passing the test above.

Isolated closures C(h1⋯hs)C(h_1\cdots h_s) with s≥3s\ge3 and the proved root-star constructions are included in the later results. The isolated two-piece closure C(h1h2)C(h_1h_2) is not added as a limiting case: it fails the strict root test. The prior-source comparison records overlap between root-star formulas, earlier Fourier cancellation, and the all-odd belt calculation. A scalar source identity alone is not a statement that two tangle diagrams are isotopic.

An explicit full-invariant family at every width k≥2k\geq2

A 2k2k-strand plat is a braid whose top and bottom endpoints are paired in the standard way. The one-end layer theorem gives a family with 3k−33k-3 twist regions for each fixed k≥2k\ge2. In standard braid notation, where σj\sigma_j crosses strands jj and j+1j+1, the word is

(∏j=1k−1σ2jαj)(∏j=1k−1σ2j+1βj)(∏j=1k−1σ2jγj). \left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\sigma_{2j}^{\alpha_j}\right) \left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\sigma_{2j+1}^{\beta_j}\right) \left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\sigma_{2j}^{\gamma_j}\right).

Choose the first-layer exponents odd and the other two layers even. The endpoint pairing then gives a knot. For each fixed width and retained half-twist sign pattern, independently sufficiently large fixed full-twist magnitudes give the full Kashaev volume limit. Both level parities are included, and the twist magnitudes need not be comparable.

There is also a subfamily whose minimal bridge number is exactly kk. Require every box exponent to be divisible by three, while retaining the odd/even pattern above. The Fox-coloring certificate gives 3k3^k colorings, whereas a knot with bb bridges has at most 3b3^b. This extra congruence proves the bridge lower bound; it is not needed for the volume theorem.

The geometric estimate is uniform in each fixed width. The circle-packing contact parameters satisfy

at the interior indices 1≤j≤k−21\le j\le k-2. A convex comparison argument and the exact seed (2/3,4/5,2/3)(2/3,4/5,2/3) give the required curvature bounds. The cases k=2,3k=2,3 use their explicit small packings. The zero-middle balance lemma converts the bound 9/49/4 into the source estimate needed for filling. This proves a family at each bridge number, not all knots of that bridge number. The earlier reduced-layer family remains a separate special case.

Independent tangle depth at every initial bridge gap

The simultaneous bridge-gap theorem permits an independent fixed depth mj≥0m_j\ge0 at each first-layer bridge gap. In the theorem’s zero-based slot convention, replace slot 2j−12j-1 by the alternating word

(2j−1,2j)mj(2j−1),1≤j≤k−1. (2j-1,2j)^{m_j}(2j-1),\qquad 1\le j\le k-1.

Taking the new exponents even preserves the original knot parity; other parity choices are allowed when the endpoint permutation gives a knot. The threshold can depend on the entire depth vector. The inserted circles occupy disjoint empty gaps in the original packing. If the bounding curvatures are A≤BA\le B, the chain has Cn=B+nA\sqrt{C_n}=\sqrt B+n\sqrt A. Arbitrarily many twist regions at each fixed width

The marked unit-chain theorem is another extension operation. Its input is a balanced core whose first box is in zero-based slot 1, with no slot 0, and with the first cap marked. Prepending (1,2)m(1,2)^m adds 2m2m boxes with independent even signed exponents. These boxes use the first four strands and preserve the knot parity.

Geometrically, the operation inserts unit disks into an empty region between the marked lines while leaving every old disk fixed. The new crossing regions satisfy balance, and the changed first region has a larger margin. Applied to the preceding core, this gives 3k−3+2m3k-3+2m twist regions for every fixed k≥2k\ge2 and m≥0m\ge0. The threshold may depend on both parameters. This is a closure rule for the stated cores, not a theorem about arbitrary multilayer plats.

A full filling theorem with a finite geometric hypothesis

For more general plats, a useful sufficient test is available from the complete circle packing. The diagram must carry the labeled convex ideal right-angled geometry used by its crossing-disk cut; “ideal” means that the vertices lie at infinity. The simple-white condition in the technical theorem is a finite condition on the diagram’s white-face cellulation.

At a crossing region, denote the four face curvatures by (x,b,c,y)(x,b,c,y) and put A=x+y>0A=x+y>0. Curvature is the reciprocal radius, with the marked line faces assigned zero. The algebraic balance lemma shows that

(A+b)(A+c)≥max⁡{b2,c2} (A+b)(A+c)\ge\max\{b^2,c^2\}

at every region is sufficient for the complete-radius filling theorem. The stronger test |b−c|≤x+y|b-c|\le x+y is often easier. Every region must use the same complete packing; choosing unrelated local radii would not establish the hypothesis.

The sharper form compares the triangle actions Qb(0)+Qc(0)Q_b(0)+Q_c(0) with log⁡b\log b and log⁡c\log c for positive middle curvatures. It is this sharper test, proved by the zero-middle lemma, that covers the explicit every-width family; the simpler product inequality can fail there.

The proof first evaluates the entire central filling sector, called the cross sector in the source decomposition. It then uses the one-sided spectral estimate to bound the complementary left and right sectors below it. All Fourier charges and endpoint terms are retained. Thus every passing fixed diagram has the full volume limit for independently sufficiently long signed twists. Universal validity of the geometric test is not asserted.

A global source upper bound and the classification of its maxima

The remaining general-plat problem can be formulated on a finite-dimensional space of shared face loads. A source here means the exact finite polynomial or tensor expression for the invariant before its indices are summed. Each crossing-region factor supplies total normalized degree one; each ordinary face demands one, while the two marked faces demand zero. These shared constraints define a compact polytope 𝒫D\mathcal P_D for the fixed diagram DD.

The closed-domain majorant assigns a continuous upper exponential rate Hδ(q)H_\delta(q) to a local load qq and real spectral parameter δ\delta. With RR crossing regions, set

MD=maxq∈𝒫D,𝜹∈[0,1/2]R∑eHδe(qe).M_D=\max_{q\in\mathcal P_D,\;\boldsymbol\delta\in[0,1/2]^R} \sum_e H_{\delta_e}(q_e).

The full shared numerator satisfies sup⁡𝜹|SN(𝜹)|≤eN(MD+o(1))\sup_{\boldsymbol\delta}|S_N(\boldsymbol\delta)|\le e^{N(M_D+o(1))}, uniformly through both parities and closed degree boundaries. This is an upper bound, not a matching lower asymptotic.

On a diagram satisfying the geometric certificate, boundary activation and stationary classification restrict the possible maxima. The central, or cross, sector has both middle loads at most 1/21/2. The left and right sectors overload one middle face. The remaining exceptional cap configurations meet the two marked zero faces. Common positive face weights are recovered at a maximum, and there are at most two such caps.

The seam theorem controls the boundary between these sectors. Let ΦD\Phi_D be the complete geometric rate and let aD>0a_D>0 be the smallest deficit of a geometric middle load from 1/21/2. Every seam-only flow has rate at most ΦD−γD\Phi_D-\gamma_D, where γD=πaD2/2\gamma_D=\pi a_D^2/2. A fixed seam neighborhood has the same kind of absolute gap, and its whole filling period is exponentially smaller than the nonzero cross period for sufficiently long fixed fillings.

Thus a competing non-cross maximum of value at least ΦD\Phi_D must have a uniform positive overload. The odd-cycle theorem forces an incompatible phase cycle at such a region. This excludes a fully phase-coherent non-cross maximum, but does not quantify enough cancellation to prove a general volume bound. The flat-cap example shows why a universal local exponential angular loss is false. Neither MD≤ΦDM_D\le\Phi_D nor a general filling theorem follows from this classification alone.

Phase cancellation on regular source windows

A window restricts the starting and ending rows of a local finite transfer. The regular-window theorem proves a strict exponential loss from phase mismatch when the weights are positive and the relevant maxima stay in the interior. The positive two-state transfer, four-state identity, and analytic switching estimate retain the exact boundary terms needed for this conclusion. The direct diagonal path is present in the full source and is excluded only in the specified disjoint windows.

There are separate proofs when one outer face or one middle face is marked and has weight zero. These do not extend to two zero weights or pole-touching maxima. The mixed-window theorem also allows complete cross factors, whose positive endpoint majorant has only logarithmic polynomial cost.

The fractional cycle-packing criterion combines compatible local losses on the shared incidence graph. It gives a loss e−κNe^{-\kappa N}, with κ>0\kappa>0, relative to the specified positive majorant. To obtain a volume theorem one must still show that the majorant’s rate minus κ\kappa is below ΦD\Phi_D, and cover all source windows. A positive loss relative to an oversized upper bound is not by itself an absolute volume bound.

From coefficient Cauchy to a shared volume inequality

The one-sided energy theorem bounds every one-sided coefficient, uniformly over real spectra, degree boundaries and both parities. If pN(q)p_N(q) is the positive endpoint coefficient, its weighted Cauchy estimate controls the rate of |aN(q;σ)|2/pN(q)|a_N(q;\sigma)|^2/p_N(q) by a complete-triangle entropy. The proof uses a folded angle with the same distribution as the original angle and keeps all unused rows and the odd derivative reference.

The shared-flow criterion combines this energy bound gg with the endpoint entropy hh and an earlier envelope henvh_{\mathrm{env}}. It uses hsharp=min⁡{henv,(h+g)/2}h_{\mathrm{sharp}}=\min\{h_{\mathrm{env}},(h+g)/2\} on each common feasible flow. If the maximum of the sum of these bounds over the closed non-cross set is strictly below ΦD\Phi_D, the full long-filling theorem follows. This is a sufficient finite-dimensional condition, not a proved universal inequality. The protected-outer result identifies the sharp local energy there; the unresolved issue is the global comparison with the original shared demands.

A general finite cover of the source sectors

The finite-sector cover theorem proves the full limit whenever finitely many genuine source-sector branches admit strict shared-radius bounds. Each face has one weight wherever it occurs; different branches may use different common weight vectors. This extends the anchored criterion.

The extension is strict for a specified seventeen-region marked plat. An exact rational flow makes every anchored bound exceed the geometric rate by more than 0.0250.025, whereas forty refined branches give a gap greater than 0.040.04 below it. The separation theorem and verifier establish both statements. A five-region example with word (1,3,2,1,3)(1,3,2,1,3) separately fails balance but passes six anchored witnesses. These compare certificates on fixed presentations; they do not classify all alternative presentations of the same knots.

The undivided-capacity theorem explains why optimizing a single positive coefficient torus can lose an exponential factor even when the coefficient itself has the correct rate. The remaining general sector question is whether every relevant non-cross string has a strict optimized shared bound below ΦD\Phi_D. Pure endpoints and the fixed-triangle boundary gap do not settle fractional mixtures.

Complete signed augmented links at arbitrary width

An augmented link retains the auxiliary crossing circles rather than filling them. The signed augmented-source theorem applies to every fixed connected augmented plat with a labeled convex ideal right-angled polyhedron QDQ_D realizing its actual cut and face pairings. Earlier Conway, pretzel, belt and cap results

The arbitrary-width seed construction began with stacks As=h1⋯hsA_s=h_1\cdots h_s, s≥3s\ge3, and then allowed turns and stacks of already constructed boxes. The signed Conway filling theorem proves the volume limit for its specified closures and any subset of independently long auxiliary fillings. Its parity construction supplies knot-producing choices when all auxiliary circles are filled. That earlier theorem excluded the minimal isolated three-piece closure; the later isolated theorem and strict-tree result now include it. This is a change in coverage, not a change in the hypotheses of the older proof.

For the retained-circle diagrams, the positive Conway theorem constructs the complete complement and identifies the entire positive source rate with its volume. The coverage comparison records antecedents. The pure long-filling theorem permits any subset of long auxiliary fillings, but its pure seed diagrams retain internal main components. Full twists cannot join those components; the signed version changes the pairing and requires its own source estimate.

The earlier long-pretzel width theorem and source-to-volume proof cover fixed odd widths at least nine and the specified two-component even widths at least ten. Their particular alias estimate does not cover widths three, five and seven. This is a limitation of that proof, not a statement that every smaller presentation lies outside all later results.

The signed octahedral belt-stack theorem treats every fixed nonempty stack with its prescribed outer crossing power and arbitrary fixed signs. Any subset of auxiliary circles can be filled along independently sufficiently long signed slopes. After all fillings, odd outer power gives knots and even outer power gives two-component links. The marked theta calculation supplies a complete augmented-link equality 4v84v_8, where v8=voctv_8=v_{\mathrm{oct}}, with a separate sixteen-tetrahedron geometric certificate. Its complete statement alone is not a theorem about arbitrary fillings or arbitrary graphs.

General identities with a smaller conclusion

Several approaches begin with the local algebra at a crossing-circle edge. The generating-action theorem, continuant period, and rectangle bound identify a finite operator, its local character relation and the range of certified geometric targets. A local character relation still needs a contributing global period before it gives the volume of a knot.

For arbitrary fixed plat width, the complete compiler, mixed-color compiler, and entire Fourier core retain the finite phases and derivative terms. The matching geometry supplies the complete complement under a positive labeled realization. The general plat target states the further source estimate needed for unrestricted coverage.

Recoupling means changing the order in which local representation factors are paired. The positive scalar formula, marked block identity, and completed-projector formula fix the normalization of those changes and preserve their first normal derivatives. The native-energy bound controls the resulting projectors by explicit magnetic energies, which can themselves grow exponentially. The Fourier energy profile gives boundary rate 5v3/π5v_3/\pi, with v3v_3 the regular ideal tetrahedron volume; the shrinking-fold theorem extends it to every sequence approaching the fold. These are local costs, not a volume estimate for their full contraction.

The shared-cycle formula preserves bounded integer flows and the marked Fourier charge. Its positive local coefficient theorem gives an interior variational rate. The positive Fourier partition yields a Cauchy upper bound with shared constraints, but a sharp geometric rate and matching lower bound for that approach remain open. The radial-frame obstruction explains one failed geometric interpretation.

The single-Racah recoupling formula is an exact local identity. For its supported folded colors, the hyperideal-volume theorem identifies the metric-normalized scalar rate as the extended tetrahedron volume divided by π\pi, uniformly through the stated boundary limits. The normalization matters: the bare factorial sum has a different action. This local volume relation is not asserted to be new.

Finally, the positive Conway variational theorem evaluates the whole positive source as twice the maximum of a concave ideal-tetrahedron volume functional on its exact support polytope. Its cylindrical factors have the geometric interpretation described above. Cap products instead have their exhibited connected-sum decomposition and additive limits; they are not assigned one complete hyperbolic metric.

Cube sectors are kept separate

The cube calculation tests how local geometric contributions behave under sewing. The central odd-level sector and the four-rung sum have the stated geometric rate. In the latter, four rung labels are summed while eight cycle labels remain fixed. The four-rung theorem and projected-chain theorem retain these restrictions.

Removing the cycle restrictions, controlling the full cube invariant and treating its even-level growth remain open in this route. Numerical cube calculations do not establish dominance or cancellation control. The frontier chapter explains how these alternatives relate to the current general questions without treating them as missing steps in the strict-tree theorem.

A classical characteristic selected by the full expansion

The filtered characteristic theorem applies to the proved strict-tree and quantitative circle-tree source classes, including the local criterion above. Given a genuine all-level annihilator, it proves that the full microscopic expansion selects an analytic curve in its classical symbol. The selected factor is reciprocal and has an explicit rational peripheral expression. Its tangent is the preferred cusp shape.

For the circle-tree presentations, the geometric longitude theorem now identifies the entire selected curve with the knot’s preferred meridian–longitude deformation. If nvn_v are the original crossing powers and w(D)w(D) is their oriented writhe, its longitude is L(M)=−M−w(D)−∑vnv∏eM2−Ee(M)1−M2Ee(M). L(M)=-M^{-w(D)-\sum_v n_v} \prod_e\frac{M^2-E_e(M)}{1-M^2E_e(M)}. The edge variables EeE_e solve the stated rational gluing equations on the branch obtained from the original filling path. This branch condition matters: elimination alone retains other representations.

The proof first constructs the nonparabolic representations of the pieces and joins them along their labeled twice-punctured disks. It retains the changes of puncture eigenlines caused by a half twist. Compare the squared preferred-longitude eigenvalue with the square of the displayed edge product. The boundary pairing shows that their ratio has zero derivative when the auxiliary variables vary at fixed MM. The edge equations have no additional algebraic constants over ℂ(M)\mathbb C(M), so this ratio is a rational function of MM. For every M≠0M\ne0, auxiliary parameters can be chosen so that the representation and all edge factors are finite and nonzero. The ratio therefore has no zeros or poles on ℂ*\mathbb C^*, leaving only a constant times an integer power of MM. The marked complete cusp fixes that constant and exponent, and the Seifert-surface calculation fixes the remaining sign.

Combining this result with the full quantum expansion proves that the selected geometric factor divides the first nonzero classical symbol of every genuine all-level annihilator in the covered circle-tree classes. The other factors of the symbol, the full recurrence ideal, and the extension to other strict presentations are separate questions. The existing volume theorems keep their broader stated scopes.
