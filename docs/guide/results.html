Research guide

Equality results and their scope

The project seeks equality for broad classes of hyperbolic knots, with the right side established as the volume of the actual complement. The results below are research proofs recorded and internally reviewed in this repository. They are not claims of external peer review.

Arbitrarily large Conway trees with single-leaf attachments

The current composition theorem starts from hhhh and permits arbitrary finite applications of T(E)T(E), E1E2E_1E_2, hEhE and EhEh. The retained closure C(FG)C(FG) has two completed children. This includes repeated single-leaf additions such as En+1=hT(En)E_{n+1}=hT(E_n). The earlier theorem also includes every isolated C(hs)C(h^s) with s≥3s\ge3. There is no additional individual-leaf condition.

For any allowed loop-free knot half-twist pattern and independently sufficiently long fixed signed full twists, the original invariant satisfies

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K)|=Vol⁡(S3\K).\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K)| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K).

Both level parities and composite levels are included. The diagram and slopes are fixed before the limit, and the threshold may depend on that diagram. Only auxiliary crossing-circle cusps are filled; the knot cusp remains unfilled.

The new estimate treats a single attached leaf and its gate together with the completed child’s output tangent. A 55-cell exact certificate checks 880 rational inequalities on the full slope square and original product domain. Together with the retained large-XX leaf estimate, it gives a margin 1/25001/2500 at every proper attachment. The exact source induction retains all colors, Hermite directions and phases. The geometric induction automatically keeps every leaf in the original packet’s strict high-index range.

The previous uniform two-leaf estimate remains the base of this induction. Its 288 exact inequalities cover the trees that defeated the older leafwise test, including C(A2ℛ(A4))C(A_2\mathcal R(A_4)). The isolated three-leaf case retains its separate marked regrouping and elementary bound. Isolated C(A2)C(A_2) remains excluded.

The theorem concerns the stated finite tangle grammar. Coverage of every arborescent presentation is open. The later general core and marked-mask theorems extend the local analytic and marked filling inputs to every strict knot pattern. The remaining general source requirement is the nonempty-alias estimate. No claim of new knot isotopy classes follows from the enlarged presentation grammar.

Independently sufficiently long fixed signed auxiliary fillings have the original invariant’s volume limit, through all levels, with the knot cusp unfilled.

A global source upper bound and the classification of its maxima

The closed-domain majorant theorem now bounds every coefficient of the original spectral numerator, uniformly over all real spectra, closed degree boundaries, both level parities and sampled zeros. Its continuous sharp functional is denoted by Hδ(q)H_\delta(q). On the original shared flow polytope 𝒫D\mathcal P_D, define

MD=maxq∈𝒫D,𝜹∈[0,1/2]R∑eHδe(qe).M_D=\max_{q\in\mathcal P_D,\;\boldsymbol\delta\in[0,1/2]^R} \sum_e H_{\delta_e}(q_e).

Each port supplies one, each ordinary face demands one, and the two protected faces demand zero. Polynomially many original degree arrays then give the uniform upper bound

sup𝜹|SN(𝜹)|≤eN(MD+o(1)).\sup_{\boldsymbol\delta}|S_N(\boldsymbol\delta)|\le e^{N(M_D+o(1))}.

This is an upper bound for the actual raw shared numerator. It asserts neither equality of rates nor a nonzero lower asymptotic.

On a certified simple-white diagram, the full-fiber activation theorem excludes every ordinary zero incidence and every diagonal port at a global maximum. The stationary classification then gives finite common positive ordinary face weights. Each port is complete strict CROSS at δ=0\delta=0, a strict pole-straddling LEFT/RIGHT branch at 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/2, or one of at most two marked cap corners. A cap has two protected zero incidences, 0<ν<1/20<\nu<1/2, and equal remaining ordinary weights. Protected zeros are retained throughout. For a maximum restricted to the closed non-CROSS union with value at least ΦD\Phi_D, the strict middle margin is now automatic by the seam theorem below.

The CROSS-degree domination and seam theorem proves Hδ(q)≤h(q)H_\delta(q)\le h(q) for every real spectrum whenever both original middle loads are at most 1/21/2. Write qgeomq^{\mathrm{geom}} for the actual complete endpoint allocation and set

aD=mine{12−qe,bgeom,12−qe,cgeom}>0,γD=π2aD2.a_D=\min_e\{\tfrac12-q^{\mathrm{geom}}_{e,b},\tfrac12-q^{\mathrm{geom}}_{e,c}\}>0, \qquad \gamma_D=\frac\pi2 a_D^2.

Every seam-only flow has sharp entropy at most ΦD−γD\Phi_D-\gamma_D. The proof uses strong concavity on the actual latent triangle allocations, projected to the original incidence loads. Compactness then gives a uniform strict overload margin for all high-value non-CROSS candidates and a fixed neighborhood of the seams with an absolute source gap.

For independently sufficiently long fixed signed fillings, the whole seam-neighborhood period, with all original charges and endpoint currents, is exponentially negligible relative to the nonzero actual filled-volume all-CROSS period:

IseamICROSS=O𝒑(e−cDN),cD>0.\frac{I_{\mathrm{seam}}}{I_{\mathrm{CROSS}}}=O_{\mathbf p}(e^{-c_DN}),\qquad c_D>0.

Both level parities are included. This removes the seam remainder; the macroscopic, strictly overloaded non-CROSS contribution remains open.

The elementary odd-cycle theorem further proves that every non-CROSS port at such a maximum forces an odd four-cycle on the actual port/face incidence graph. Thus a phase-coherent maximum must be entirely complete CROSS. This is a qualitative sign obstruction, not a quantitative cancellation estimate.

The remaining general step is to turn these forced mismatches into an absolute bound at the complete-volume rate. The flat-cap counterexample explains one limitation: a locally mismatched alternating cap window can retain a polynomial fraction of its positive envelope. Neither MD≤ΦDM_D\le\Phi_D nor the full general filling-period bound follows from the classification. The previously proved knot families and conditional certificates below remain unchanged.

Phase cancellation on regular source windows

where ENE_N is the original positive window envelope. The positive two-state tail handles the outer relative phase. The four-state source identity and analytic switch theorem handle a mismatched middle phase while retaining both boundary terms. The unrestricted source includes the direct i=ji=j path; the disjoint regular windows considered in this estimate exclude it explicitly.

The supported boundary cases now have their own exact proofs. With one outer face protected, the active transfer becomes scalar and the three supported phases determine alignment. With one middle face protected, the initial row is fixed exactly; only a strict regular stop maximum is needed, and finite row reflection gives the protected-output case. Neither result includes two zero weights or a pole-touching maximum.

From coefficient Cauchy to a shared volume inequality

The new local energy theorem controls every one-sided coefficient of the actual spectral numerator, uniformly over the full real spectral interval, both level parities and all degree boundaries. Relative to the positive endpoint coefficient pNp_N, it gives

1Nlog⁡supσ|aN(q;σ)|2pN(q)≤C(qx,qy,12−qx−qy)+o(1).\frac1N\log\frac{\sup_\sigma|a_N(q;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(q)} \le C(q_x,q_y,\tfrac12-q_x-q_y)+o(1).

The proof combines an exact weighted Cauchy inequality with an explicit rearrangement of the spectral angle. Its folded angle has the same uniform distribution as the original angle, so the weighted integral is bounded by a terminal complete-triangle integral. It keeps all unused rows and the odd derivative reference; it does not assume a coefficient limit from a weighted limit.

The general shared-flow criterion combines this bound with the earlier one-sided envelope. Write hh for the known endpoint coefficient entropy, gg for the triangle energy bound above (and g=hg=h on CROSS), and henvh_{\mathrm{env}} for the earlier envelope, also equal to hh on CROSS. On each original feasible flow set

hsharp=min⁡{henv,(h+g)/2}.h_{\mathrm{sharp}}=\min\{h_{\mathrm{env}},(h+g)/2\}.

If the maximum of ∑ehsharp(qe)\sum_e h_{\mathrm{sharp}}(q_e) over the closed non-CROSS shared-flow set is strictly below ΦD\Phi_D, the full original invariant has the actual filled-volume limit for independently sufficiently long fixed signed twists. This is a proved sufficient reduction with an explicit finite-dimensional hypothesis. The universal entropy inequality is still open. The source’s face loads are retained throughout; the two terms are evaluated on the same flow, not independently maximized.

The protected-outer theorem is sharp: with one outer face zero, the optimized relative-energy rate is exactly C(w,12−w,0)C(w,\tfrac12-w,0), where ww is the outer degree. The general interior bound needs no new local asymptotic conjecture. What remains is the global comparison with the actual hyperbolic volume.

Cap products retain their exhibited connected-sum decomposition, with additive limits; they are not asserted to have one complete hyperbolic metric.

Cube sectors are kept separate

The cube provides a useful test of shared geometric cores and sewing estimates. The central odd-level sector and the four-rung sum have the stated actual geometric rate. The latter sums all four rung labels while keeping eight cycle labels fixed. Projected chains retain analogous restrictions.

Removing these cycle projections, controlling the full invariant, and treating even-level cube growth are unresolved. The four-rung proof and projected-chain theorem preserve those distinctions. The full-cube computations are diagnostics, not a proof of dominance or cancellation control.
