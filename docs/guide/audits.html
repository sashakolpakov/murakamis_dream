Checking the formulas and reproducing the calculations

Following the proof’s dependencies

The manuscript’s final step is an explicit implication. Write the exact invariant as KN=uN(GN+EN)K_N=u_N(G_N+E_N), where uNu_N is the recorded normalization, GN=aNeN𝒜G_N=a_Ne^{N\mathcal A} is the selected contribution, and ENE_N is everything else. To obtain the action as the growth rate, one needs

log⁡|uN|N→0,log⁡|aN|N→0,limsupN→∞log⁡|EN|N<Re⁡𝒜. \frac{\log|u_N|}{N}\longrightarrow0,\qquad \frac{\log|a_N|}{N}\longrightarrow0,\qquad \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log|E_N|}{N} <\operatorname{Re}\mathcal A.

The reverse triangle inequality then prevents cancellation of the leading contribution. A separate geometric argument identifies 2πRe⁡𝒜2\pi\operatorname{Re}\mathcal A with the volume of the intended knot complement. The reproduction chapter gives the complete elementary argument and records which earlier results supply each hypothesis. A critical point, a norm bound, or a representation with the right peripheral equations supplies only part of this chain.

The manuscript audit records the current review. It led to an explicit comparison between the regulator’s fundamental chain and the filled metric in Appendix D, and a more precise complex-contour estimate in Appendix B. It also made the multidegree hypothesis explicit in the standalone tensor norm statement. The review does not certify every theorem in the manuscript; its scope and remaining dependencies are stated in that record.

The later review follows the directed-tree induction and the all-orders recurrence argument separately. In the former, the child values used to normalize a subtree are exact complex numbers, and the bounds retain the whole remainder at both level parities. In the latter, the conclusion requires every finite expansion order on all parameter translates used by the operator. The coefficient substitution then gives a geometric factor of its classical symbol. It does not identify the full A-polynomial. Appendix F now also spells out why its regulator and its completed metric compute the volume of the same oriented cell chain. The geometric-germ argument in Appendix I now works directly on the retained positive augmented triangulation at the filling endpoint. Its primitive slope equations define the projective filled-group germ, with the main meridian as local coordinate. This avoids a separate existence theorem for a positive triangulation of the filled manifold in this part of the proof.

A further reading follows the restricted source estimates separately from the general central-contribution theorem. It found no circular use of the unresolved general remainder bound in the inspected restricted-family proofs. The source coverage record lists those dependencies and their parity restrictions. The geometry record states the assumptions used to identify the filled metric and its deformation germ. These are bounded reading records, not a complete verification of every imported lemma.

Two wording changes make the all-orders argument more precise. Its annihilator has fixed order rPr_P, independent of the quantum level; the separate spectral degree is N−1N-1. Its norm comparison applies to the reduced polynomial of degree less than NN. A higher-degree product is reduced before that comparison is used.

Checking finite implications in Lean

With the pinned Lean toolchain installed, run:

python3 repro/check_proof_logic.py --output repro/results/proof-logic

The formal project proves finite lemmas about the exact parity cutoffs, polynomial and Hermite identities, tensor subtraction, and scalar rate comparisons. It also constructs division by a split monic polynomial on coefficient lists and proves that the remainder’s top coefficient is the iterated divided difference. Repeated roots are retained without division by their differences. The constructed operations are proved linear and independent of trailing zero coefficients. Adding a multiple of the monic modulus leaves the remainder and its top-coefficient functional unchanged. The remainder is also proved unique under its coefficientwise degree bound, and independent of the order of the roots. These statements allow repeated roots and padded coefficient lists. Appendix N now explains this construction before specializing to the spectral polynomial. A further formal theorem characterizes the functional by additivity, vanishing on multiples of the modulus, and its values in low degree. Applying it to the original crossing operator requires proof of each of these properties for that operator. The runner compiles the proofs and separately checks their logical assumptions. It rejects admitted proof steps. These declarations retain their mathematical hypotheses: they do not formally prove the analytic source estimates, the diagram-to-invariant identity, or hyperbolic completion.

For example, the tensor identity subtracts the term that uses evaluation at every coordinate. It retains all terms with one or more derivatives, as required at repeated spectral roots. Proving that the actual source has the claimed degree and vanishes on the specified product grid is a separate part of Appendix N. This distinction is recorded in the formal project’s explanation of every declaration.

The repository contains written proofs, exact finite certificates, numerical checks and recorded outputs. They answer different questions. This chapter explains which calculations support which steps, and how to rerun them without treating a successful sample as a theorem.

An exact symbolic calculation can establish an identity in formal variables. A rigorously bounded interval calculation can establish an inequality over an entire stated parameter domain. A finite counterexample can disprove a proposed identity. By contrast, checking many quantum levels or plotting a growth rate does not prove a uniform limit as N→∞N\to\infty. The scope of each script must be read together with the mathematical argument that uses it.

The current strict Conway tree theorem includes bounds for the full quantum sum, a nonzero selected contribution, the original normalization and the actual marked filling geometry. Its proof is not waiting for a theorem about all knots. The more specialized general-plat calculations catalogued below retain their own hypotheses and limitations.

Running a named group

The research-check runner executes a selected group serially. The group manifest contains every command in its recorded order. Listing and previewing groups do not run a scientific calculation:

python3 repro/run_research_checks.py --list
python3 repro/run_research_checks.py general-plat --dry-run

To run one group, omit the dry-run option. The runner saves a separate combined output log for every command and a JSON summary containing exit codes and durations. It continues after a failed check so the summary records the whole selected group, then returns a nonzero exit code if any command failed. It does not run unselected groups. The individual scientific scripts keep their existing behavior, including any result files they normally write.

By default, logs go in a timestamped directory under the system temporary directory. To retain them in a chosen location, use:

python3 repro/run_research_checks.py pointed-a --log-dir /path/to/check-logs

The runner uses the Python interpreter that launched it. Select another interpreter with the --python option if the chosen group needs a different environment. The manifest replaces the former long command block; it preserves those commands rather than silently adding them to every build.

Where to start for the current theorem

The proof has three kinds of inputs. Exact source identities retain every color, repeated-node derivative and scalar factor. Analytical estimates control the large-NN limit uniformly on the domains used by the proof. Geometric constructions identify the cells, face maps, cusp markings and filling slopes of the actual complement. A finite algebra script can check an identity used in any of these arguments, but it cannot replace the uniform estimate or the topological construction.

The two-leaf block inequality and single-leaf attachment inequality have finite rational certificates. They bound the competing alias expressions over their whole slope domains. Their verification uses exact interval arithmetic, explicit remainder bounds and finitely many vertices of fixed polytopes. Candidate points may help find a certificate; the verifier proves the displayed inequalities without trusting an optimizer.

From the repository root, their principal verifiers are:

python3 repro/run_research_checks.py conway-certificates

The unflagged-region probe estimate applies those scalar inequalities to arbitrary coefficient arrays with the stated bounds. The spectral propagation proof then carries the estimates through turns and products, at the actual geometric action. These are written analytical arguments. They do not infer their conclusions from a longer list of sampled levels.

The general marked geometry follows the original diagram’s face pairings and primitive cusp paths. Its completeness and smooth-filling arguments are also mathematical proofs rather than numerical manifold recognition. The theorem uses the original literal diagram for topology even when a different algebraic expression is useful for an estimate.

Coefficient regions in the general-plat method

In this group of notes, a degree records how much of a face variable occurs in a polynomial coefficient. The labels central, left and right name regions of those degree inequalities; they are not new types of knot crossing. A protected variable is one fixed by the marked coefficient extraction. A seam is a boundary shared by two such degree regions. These definitions explain why a theorem on one region need not control the whole coefficient sum.

Sharp bounds and boundary arguments

The closed-domain upper theorem, full-fiber activation and stationary classification prove the corresponding boundary and stationary statements. Their arguments establish the directional entropy gains, free-spectrum retuning and protected cap exclusions directly. The odd main-strand cycle theorem uses exact source characters and original face demands; the flat-cap limitation is an exact alternating-sum inequality.

The seam theorem concerns the boundary between two degree regions of the original coefficient sum. Its moving cosine-log integral and pointwise tail comparison prove all-spectrum central-degree domination; exact latent-triangle strong concavity gives the explicit gap γD=πaD2/2\gamma_D=\pi a_D^2/2. The existing whole-domain integration-by-parts theorem retains all charge currents and gives seam-period negligibility for fixed long fillings. These conclusions are analytical. For this general-plat route, the remaining macroscopic noncentral part is not controlled by the seam theorem alone.

The four-state and window audits below support the retained finite source factors and indices; they do not establish the analytic majorant, classify its maxima, or prove a global volume comparison. Each linked note states the domain and hypotheses of its proof.
