Research guide

Reproducing and reading the audits

What is being checked

The repository contains written arguments, exact finite algebra audits, numerical diagnostics, and recorded outputs. These are different kinds of evidence.

An exact symbolic identity can verify a stated algebraic step for independent formal variables. A finite-field counterexample can refute a proposed exact identity when the reduction is valid. A successful list of finite levels alone does not prove a uniform asymptotic theorem. Floating-point growth plots need their own error control before they can support a rigorous estimate.

Each technical note specifies its proof and the narrower role of its audit. The archive keeps scripts and result files accessible together.

The global sharp-source and boundary proofs

The closed-domain upper theorem, full-fiber activation and stationary classification have full internal analytical reviews. Their boundary inputs prove the directional entropy gains, free-spectrum retuning and protected cap exclusions directly. The odd MAIN-cycle theorem uses exact source characters and original face demands; the flat-cap limitation is an exact alternating-sum inequality.

The seam theorem also has full internal analytical/source reviews. Its moving cosine-log integral and pointwise tail comparison prove all-spectrum CROSS-degree domination; exact latent-triangle strong concavity gives the explicit gap γD=πaD2/2\gamma_D=\pi a_D^2/2. The existing whole-domain integration-by-parts theorem retains all charge currents and gives seam-period negligibility for fixed long fillings. No numerical audit is used to infer these conclusions, and the macroscopic non-CROSS remainder is still open.

No new numerical asymptotic audit is claimed for this batch. The existing four-state and window audits below support the retained finite source factors and indices; they do not establish the analytic majorant, classify its maxima, or prove a global volume comparison. The written proofs and their internal review scope are recorded in each note. They are not external peer review.

Exact transfers supporting the phase estimate

The new analytic phase argument has two focused finite checks:

python3 experiments/audit_research_original_four_state_transfer.py
python3 experiments/audit_research_regular_spectral_window_phase_exclusion.py

The four-state audit checks 196 recurrence/path comparisons and 85 exact omitted-half scalar identities across ten levels and both parities. The window audit checks 402 exact restricted matrix/path identities for half and full rows, including sampled spectral zeros and zero outer weights. Its equality is sign-adjusted on both sides; the common raw sign is established in the source proof.

These validate finite indexing and retained factors. The original physical-matrix interface is inherited from the previously reviewed numerator formula. The positive transfer, analytic gauge, strict boundary gaps and shared-torus phase exclusion are proved analytically and independently reviewed; these finite checks do not prove their exponential estimates or a global volume bound.

The protected-window and mixed-cycle extensions have full internal analytical reviews. The outer-zero proof specializes the exact transfer; the middle-zero proof retains the actual initial seed and proves its output reflection by finite tilings. The whole-CROSS input uses the already reviewed central factorization and cofactor angular theorem. Fractional cycle packing is a finite combinatorial argument. No new numerical asymptotic audit is claimed for these consequences, and the global majorant comparison remains conditional.

Rebuilding this guide and its figures

The guide is generated from book/chapters/ and the published notes. Its static HTML uses native MathML and local CSS and JavaScript; reading it requires no account or external math service.

python3 tools/build_book.py

python3 tools/render_figures.py

The main README is deliberately short. The long research log, all existing technical note paths, and audit imports are preserved.

The native-energy audit checks the actual generic high-degree adapter, dual measures, residue and completed-projector jets, and exact rank-one norms. The full positive endpoint volume theorem

The half-block audit compares the original numerator coefficients, the positive path recurrence, negative roots, exact extreme coefficients, and the finite Lobachevsky formula. Its finite rate profiles remain diagnostics; the uniform closed-domain limit has a written proof.

The earlier shared-source audit checks 228 original coefficients, 4590 support patterns, 748302 affine-cut conditions, 118 one-level support certificates and 236 eventual-flow tests. Those tests remain relevant to fixed half-label patterns. The full-kernel theorem instead uses every degree-N−1N-1 monomial, continuous entropy on the full simplex, and unrestricted integral flow rounding. A separate transfer audit checks 39599 Laurent coefficient comparisons and the path injection used in the central corridor.

The general endpoint theorem now proves the geometric upper bound as well. Its structural audit constructs actual Morse faces at widths two, three and four, retains edge identities through genuine identity smoothing, and finds digon disks from the dual graph independently of the Hall subsets. It checks 41 loopless smoothings, 918 hybrid patterns, 121004 Hall cuts, 716 tight sets and 816 independent positive-arc max-flow tests. The general Hall classification, source reduction and volume comparison have separate written proofs.

The signed-chain audit checks 270 signed cyclic fans, four repeated-contact fans, 24 actual circle/line tangencies, eight contact triangles and two oriented strip chains. These validate the volume formulas; the original group pairings, degree-one relative chain and compatible cut-disk collapse are analytical arguments. The serial audit compares 3960 original physical entries, including 1290 illegal-total zeros, and 3120 independent coefficient bounds for literal powers of the same ordered-pair coupon.

The odd regularization audit checks the original spectral derivative and the positive comparison used for the odd coupon VN(−2)−N−2VN′(−2)V_N(-2)-N^{-2}V_N'(-2). The resulting whole-source spectral-jet bound is exact at both parities. These are spectral derivatives at the fixed root, not genuine-qq normals. Independent proof/code reviews and fresh redirected replays accompany the new results; finite fixtures do not establish a selected Gaussian cycle, a complementary phase estimate or the general filled-knot asymptotic.

Its finite tests are scoped to those inputs; samples do not prove the uniform asymptotic or select the later spectral Gaussian contour.

The factorization note compares the central formulas with the original unused-row path polynomial and retains both parity endpoints and the odd central-skipping term. Independent fresh replays reproduce those checks.

The protected-middle relative packet, nodal rigidity, global local-angle theorem and whole-source assembly have full independent analytical reviews. The finite Cauchy–Lagrange and original face-torus arguments carry the asymptotic conclusions. The direct-power scope retains all fixed-root spectral derivatives while keeping genuine-qq normals separate. Those input audits do not establish global charge dominance or the general filled-knot limit. The later localized contribution is proved by the analytical argument below.

The localized original filling period

The localized unit-charge theorem has independent full analytical reviews of the original raw marked prefactor, the finite-level action, both determinant branches, and the actual cube translation with every side current retained. Its proof gives a nonzero relative Gaussian coefficient at both level parities. The charge comparison, applied to that same finite action, bounds the other charges in a fixed finite local box.

No new numerical audit is claimed for this analytical consequence. The exact remainder keeps the finitely many selected charges outside the cube and all other charges on their whole original domains; it does not separately sum remote sharp-cube pieces. The proof does not bound this global remainder or establish the general filled-knot volume limit.

Whole-domain bounds and the balanced full invariant

The new large-charge audit checks original operator entries, both signed Gaussian exponents, and paired endpoint currents. The diagonal-sector audit includes removable physical nodes, exact path partitions and sewn polynomial integration-by-parts checks. The CROSS audit checks the start decomposition, rescaled transfer matrices and finite characteristic formulas. All three have independent fresh primary-agent replays agreeing with the recorded results. Their finite checks support exact formulas; the uniform bounds come from the separately reviewed written proofs.

The whole-CROSS period, all-angle LEFT envelope, and complete-radius balance theorem have full analytical internal reviews. The proofs retain entire-domain remote charge sums and endpoint currents, including both level parities. The elementary entropy lemma checks the absolute half-degree normalization and all protected-zero cases. These arguments prove a full invariant theorem under the stated balance hypothesis. Those conditional arguments alone do not prove realization. The uniform family construction below now supplies it, without treating numerical experiments as a proof.

Uniform width and exact bridge number

The family theorem has full internal analytical reviews of the literal incidences, packing reflection, both recurrence branches, endpoints, and convex comparison. That proof covers every k≥2k\geq2; finite packing samples do not justify its coverage. The zero-middle lemma uses an exact rational lower bound independently recomputed. The Fox certificate proves the signed crossing identity, invariance, exact plat count and bridge bound directly. No numerical asymptotic or coverage audit is claimed for these proofs.

The two focused family audits supplement these proofs: the reduced-source audit checks 38 literal widths and the rational comparison seed; the one-end-source audit checks 39 literal widths, the changed linear boundary, and all circle contacts and noncontacts in the exact width-two and width-three packings. Primary-agent fresh replays agree with both recorded outputs. These finite checks do not replace the uniform convex comparison.

Shared-radius certificates beyond complete-packing balance

The anchored theorem has full internal analytical reviews of the common-face Cauchy bounds, both parity partitions, the fixed complex strip and the entire Fourier remainder. Its explicit example uses six stated rational vectors. Arb interval arithmetic evaluates finite Lobachevsky sums with an explicit uniform tail bound; exact algebra checks the original golden-ratio packing. Root and a separate reviewer freshly replayed the audit. The resulting strict inequalities certify this example, not universal coverage of the criterion.

The fixed-triangle capacity lemma is analytical: closed strong concavity gives the explicit squared-distance loss, and finite convex separation proves the shared dual formula even without attained positive multipliers. It does not assert the same comparison for fractional identity mixtures.

A structural extension at arbitrary fixed depth

The unit-chain theorem proves the literal prefix dictionary and an exact extension into the empty marked gap. Its proof preserves every old disk and checks all added ports analytically; depth is arbitrary. The focused audit checks 56 original source charts and 12 exact small seed extensions, including 1,084 circle/line support pairs. Root and an independent reviewer freshly replayed it. These finite checks support the source dictionary and geometry, while the proof establishes the unbounded-depth statement.

Simultaneous insertions at every initial bridge gap

The simultaneous theorem has independent full proof/code reviews and redirected exact replays. Its audit checks 413 nonuniform-depth source charts, seven exact symbolic transport/ratio identities, and 25 joint small packings with 2,333 support checks. The analytical proof establishes the general source induction, disjoint-gap construction, curvature order and all port bounds. The extended diagram retains its own full normalization; no equality of its framing scalar with the core’s scalar is asserted.

Refined sector covers and a rigorous obstruction

The general finite-cover theorem has independent full analytical and scope reviews. Its source partition and entire Fourier-remainder transfer use the original shared face targets and both level parities.

The separation verifier checks that both certificates use exactly the same original seventeen-port source and mark. An 84-variable rational flow rules out one anchored test by more than 0.0250.025; an 83-variable complete flow and forty rational radius vectors certify the refined cover by more than 0.040.04. All face equations are exact, and Arb evaluations include explicit Fourier-series tails. The public scripts contain no numerical optimizer or private diagnostic inputs.

The undivided positive-capacity obstruction is analytical. Its proof establishes coercivity, rules out escaping level-dependent radii, and distinguishes the bound’s exponential loss from the actual coefficient’s correct volume rate. Finite positive-source normalization and the pure-face entropy values were independently reviewed.

Uniform coefficient energy and rearrangement

The protected-outer audit constructs the physical coproduct Casimir and extracts coefficients by the original finite Fourier transform. It verifies both parity reference factors and exact spectral sampling nodes. Symbolic identities and fixed integral checks supplement the analytical uniform boundary proof.

The general one-sided audit separately verifies the nonnegative parameter expansion, signed-parameter Cauchy inequalities, interior original coefficients, and the rearrangement identities. The uniform transfer estimate and compact finite-tilt argument are proved analytically, rather than inferred from sampled large levels. Independent readers checked the full local theorem and the conditional shared-flow consequence; no audit claims the remaining global entropy inequality.
