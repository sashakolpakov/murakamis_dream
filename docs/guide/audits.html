Research guide

Reproducing the arguments and calculations

The papers contain written proofs, exact finite certificates and a partial Lean formalization. Each has a different role. A rational certificate can establish the inequality it encodes. A Lean proof establishes its stated proposition. A numerical sample can expose an incorrect formula, but cannot establish a uniform asymptotic estimate. Building a PDF checks its presentation and references.

Start with the collection-wide audit and the reproduction instructions. The seven-paper Conway audit records the earlier revision of the Conway theorem and its prerequisites. The current subject papers give the detailed statements and proofs; the reference supplies a unified account of overlapping material.

Following the proof’s dependencies

The dependency graph records named mathematical results and the places where later proofs use them. An arrow runs from a prerequisite to its consumer. The detailed view shows the source statement, its direct prerequisites and the reason for each use. Selecting a result gives a reading route in which shared prerequisites occur once.

The current inventory has 154 statement entries and 348 recorded uses, organized into 16 components and 21 subject papers. The statement, component and article graphs are acyclic. These are checks on the recorded dependencies. An omitted premise would still be a mathematical error even if the resulting graph were acyclic.

A separate proof-logic record distinguishes the original diagram class, its knot-component test, its filling hypothesis and the conclusion to which each argument applies. For example, evaluating a nonzero central contribution is a different statement from bounding the rest of the invariant. The latter premise cannot be removed from the final volume argument. Replay that structural check with:

python3 repro/check_manuscript_logic.py --output repro/results/manuscript-and-lean/manuscript-logic

The current record checks 49 declared steps and rejects 23 deliberately invalid substitutions. It detects errors in those recorded implications; it does not turn their written analytic or geometric inputs into formal proofs.

Separating the strict Conway proof

The Conway application uses five companion papers directly and one further geometric prerequisite. The paper guide lists that route. Its seven papers form a fixed, dated edition so that their cited theorem numbers can be used together.

The standalone article and reference include the same Conway proof files. The proof keeps the original coherent color contraction, uses the pure auxiliary source, and restores each full signed crossing power by its exact finite coefficients. The whole Fourier remainder estimate and the nonzero leading coefficient are both needed. A local critical-point calculation alone would supply neither conclusion.

After building the papers, check their actual inputs, compiled statement labels, published PDFs and companion references with:

python3 tools/build_papers.py
python3 tools/build_dependency_graph.py
python3 tools/check_paper_collection.py

The collection record covers all 21 current papers. Its source and PDF hashes describe the edition checked. They identify files; they do not verify their proofs.

Checking the implications in Lean

The formal coverage guide explains the relationship between the written arguments and the retained Lean code. There are 76 selected manuscript statements in 28 modules. For each one, a correspondence record states the symbol dictionary, hypotheses, conclusion and any step that remains written.

The records distinguish four relationships. Fifteen are exact matches with the cited abstract statement. Ten specialize a more general formal theorem. Forty-one verify a specified component of a longer proof. Ten prove a conditional implication whose application hypotheses must be supplied by the written arguments. An exact scalar lemma still needs a written application identifying its sequence with the knot invariant.

Each selected statement has an independent expected-type contract: an anonymous Lean example first spells out the proposition and then proves it using the selected theorem. This catches a mismatch in hypotheses or conclusions that checking only a theorem name would miss. The mathematical interpretation of the symbols is also read against the manuscript; Lean does not translate the prose automatically.

The formalization retains the full coefficient pairing, including its Gram matrix and null directions. It checks the normalized shifted Fourier transform, derivative data at repeated roots, and the finite crossing coefficient bound. The stationary calculation uses an ordinary transpose for a holomorphic Hessian. The scalar asymptotic argument keeps a strict exponential gap for the entire remainder and permits the finite-level action to vary with the level.

The final Conway implication assumes the written physical source identity, contour estimates, nonzero limiting amplitude and marked geometric action-volume comparison. Those are visible hypotheses. The fixed-boundary variational modules likewise require the diagram’s written support and cut calculations before their generic balance results apply.

Run the complete formal replay with:

python3 repro/check_lean.py --output repro/results/manuscript-and-lean/lean

The runner uses the pinned Lean and Mathlib versions, clears only local generated build directories, and compiles the active modules and all 76 contracts. It also inventories all 534 public declarations and checks their axiom dependencies. Admitted proofs and extra axioms are rejected. The formal setup gives the dependency installation instructions for a fresh checkout.

Three compiler controls test substantive distinctions. Removing the factor 1/N1/N from Parseval gives the wrong norm already for a constant array at level two. Replacing the holomorphic mixed-block identity by a conjugate-transpose identity fails over ℂ\mathbb C. Replacing a strict remainder gap by a nonstrict one permits complete cancellation of the leading term. The valid control sources compile; the altered types are rejected. These controls check the intended interfaces, rather than a numerical volume limit.

Exact certificates and finite checks

The registered finite suite collects the checks needed by the current papers. It runs each program in a separate process with assertions enabled and stops on a failure:

python3 repro/check_paper_algebra.py --output repro/results/paper-collection/finite

Its 20 groups include finite spectral and Newton identities, both parity conventions, primitive peripheral coordinates and the rational constants used by the stated volume families. Some groups invoke retained component checks. The saved record identifies the scripts, data and manuscript sources by their hashes.

The planar certificate verifiers use outward bounds and explicit series remainders on a finite domain cover. The written argument explains why those cells cover the required continuous region. In particular, a list of sampled radii would not replace that cover. The Conway attachment certificates are exact rational checks for their stated domains; their analytic interpretation is proved in the source-bound paper.

The small certificate suite can also be run without the optional scientific Python packages:

python3 repro/verify.py --output repro/results

Numerical matrix comparisons, Gaussian samples and selected-level tests have a narrower purpose. They check formulas and conventions. They do not prove analytic continuation, uniform error estimates, survival of a geometric contribution or completion of a hyperbolic metric.

Running a named group

The research runner is separate from the publication suite. It groups the retained exploratory checks so that one need not copy a long sequence of individual commands. Use its help and group listing to select the desired calculation. A research record describes its own scope; it is not another prerequisite for the Conway theorem merely because it has been computed successfully.

Rebuilding this guide and its figures

The guide is built from the retained chapter, figure and note sources:

python3 tools/build_book.py
python3 tools/check_book.py

The second command checks local links, fragments, image descriptions, search coverage and rendered formulas. A typesetting or link check has no mathematical proof content. After editing a native TikZ drawing, regenerate its exports with python3 tools/render_figures.py before rebuilding the guide. See the guide source instructions for the required software.

Obsolete working notes, duplicated reports and failed diagnostic outputs are absent from the maintained collection. Their source snapshot remains in Git history. The current library contains the retained arguments and research questions; it is not a second archive of every intermediate calculation.
