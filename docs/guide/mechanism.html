Research guide

From the original source to a volume limit

Preserve the source before taking a limit

The invariant is a specified contraction of quantum operators, cups, caps, and a marked functional. A formula with the same classical A-curve can still represent a different quantum solution or select a different period. The pointed structure keeps the source, lattice, peripheral data, and marked normal information together.

At a root of unity, paired spectral channels can merge. Terms that vanish at the root can contribute to the required derivative. In the current Racah formulas, this includes a retained [2N]T[2N]T term. Evaluating first and then differentiating a truncated formula loses information.

The torsor status note explains how this viewpoint relates to earlier work in the repository. A classical A-polynomial alone has not supplied the selected period or its absolute geometric normalization.

Exact finite functions at arbitrary bridge width

The complete-color and all-color formulas now apply to every fixed plat. They specify the physical columns, normal terms, inverse measures and phases before any asymptotic estimate.

Their entire core function has the explicit form

CN(χ)=∑𝒋AN(𝒋)∏icos⁡(πmjiχi),mj=N−2j−1.C_N(\chi)=\sum_{\mathbf j} A_N(\mathbf j)\prod_i\cos(\pi m_{j_i}\chi_i),\qquad m_j=N-2j-1.

Here χ\chi is the auxiliary color deficit divided by NN. The coefficients are actual marked channel contractions. This finite function has exact Fourier reconstruction and Parseval identities at both parities, including the odd zero-frequency endpoint. At physical high colors it is the source’s core; the full source also contains the explicitly retained alias terms.

Thus the Fourier proposal has a concrete object to estimate. Parseval controls a norm, while the desired invariant is a particular coherent contraction. A uniform complex asymptotic, physical alias suppression and survival of the geometric contribution still require proof for a general plat.

Exact recoupling exposes a local geometric scalar

Four external objects can be paired in two ways. The actual change of basis is computed before root specialization, with its genuine pivotal turn.

The resulting finite Racah sum admits a positive, face-normalized scalar on the supported folded chamber. Its logarithmic rate is the volume of a specific hyperideal tetrahedron. This closes a local algebra-to-geometry step.

It does not yet bound every entry of the completed physical transport in the required metric. A source normalization can have poles or zeros at the root, and complementary channels carry additional normal currents. The scalar and normal theorem states what is controlled and what remains.

An exact partner-reflection theorem now proves that all 64 complementary color choices have the same normalized root value. It uses the actual reciprocal source coefficients and residues of channel projectors on a regular invariant lattice. The same work gives an exact counterexample to equality of first normal derivatives. Root-value symmetry therefore helps organize the source without removing the normal-transport problem.

The all-partner first-normal theorem now controls every supported reflected branch. A positive finite presentation of the complete generic source, combined with the exact Ward and Fourier identities, gives |KB′(q)|≤CN2(1+log⁡N)WN2|K'_B(q)|\leq C N^2(1+\log N)W_N^2 and a logarithmic relative bound for the coherent tail itself. The earlier two-current obstruction remains valid for those earlier relations alone; the new source presentation supplies the missing information. General higher normal orders and the completed physical transport metric remain open.

Weighted Cauchy needs the right energy

A useful inequality has the form

|KN|2≤eo(N)ZN.|K_N|^2\leq e^{o(N)}Z_N.

Weighted Cauchy can prove such an estimate once the inner product, original source cost, and dual cost are explicit. Choosing a positive form alone does not make those costs small. The repository contains exact counterexamples to proposed unmarked norms and to replacing a full magnetic tensor by its scalar rate.

The whole-support boundary Gram theorem gives polynomial costs on each supported local cell. The next step is to transport this control through the actual global composition. Correlated seam labels must remain shared; maximizing local factors independently can change the answer.

An upper bound is only half of equality

For sources with phases, a matching lower bound requires survival of the geometric contribution. A large positive comparison partition does not exclude cancellation in the original invariant.

For the specified positive Conway expressions, positivity supplies a different route. Exact support is an additive integer semigroup, which approximates a maximizing configuration along every sufficiently large level. Uniform factorial estimates prove the whole-source variational limit. The new geometric argument then proves that the reduced maximum is strict, matches the actual quadrilateral faces, and reconstructs the complete complement of every retained cylindrical factor. The new mixed-color proof supplies the additional filling estimates: exact leaf aliases lie in a square-zero radical, a strict seed-level gap survives arbitrary positive composition, and a finite complex lattice window has a nonzero Gaussian amplitude. This proves the long-full-twist source limit. The separate exact face-map and peripheral construction now identifies its rate with actual filled volume. The unchanged pure grammar still cannot produce knots by those fillings.

Signed half twists change that source and its connectivity. The new supporting-plane argument bounds any number of propagated aliases by adding tangents to the same global action; the linear terms cancel under the actual wire constraints. A separately checked finite complex packet supplies the nonzero contribution. This works for every fixed C(FG)C(FG) with robust children, and the corresponding marked geometry gives the filled knot and link equality stated in the results chapter.
