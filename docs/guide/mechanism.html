Research guide

How the full quantum invariant acquires the knot volume

The main result concerns a particular, already defined knot invariant. For a knot KK and an integer level NN, write KN(K)K_N(K) for its reduced Kashaev invariant, normalized to be 11 on the unknot. The quantum parameter is qN=eπi/Nq_N=e^{\pi i/N}. A diagram computes KNK_N by multiplying crossing matrices, joining their indices along strands, and summing the remaining indices. Cups, caps and the marked cut specify how the strands close. In the technical notes, this entire prescribed expression is called the source. It includes its scalar factors and derivatives, not just the part that seems largest.

The strict Conway tree theorem proves limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K)|=Vol⁡(S3\K) \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K)| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K) for every fixed literal expression satisfying its finite compatibility test, once all independent signed twist weights are sufficiently long and the endpoint pairing gives a knot. A literal expression records the actual order of tangle products and quarter-turns; algebraically equal scalar formulas are not silently treated as isotopic diagrams. The tree and its integer weights stay fixed as NN grows. This is a theorem for the stated class, not a claim that every arborescent knot has such a presentation.

Why retaining the full expression matters

A color is a representation label on a strand or an auxiliary crossing circle. At a root of unity, two spectral labels can have the same eigenvalue. The value at that eigenvalue then fails to determine the operator: its first derivative in the transverse spectral direction can also contribute. The notes call these derivative terms normals. Derivatives in the generic quantum parameter are another kind of normal, so the variable of differentiation must always be specified. Let ζ\zeta denote the generic quantum parameter before specialization. For example, a factor [2N]ζ[2N]_\zeta vanishes when ζ=qN\zeta=q_N but can have a nonzero derivative there. Deleting it before differentiating changes the prescribed expression.

The full invariant is a signed, generally complex sum. A large term or a large positive upper comparison does not prove that the sum has that size. Two requirements must be met together: the geometric contribution has a nonzero coefficient, and everything omitted from its neighborhood is uniformly smaller. A packet in this guide means a local asymptotic formula with its action, nonzero prefactor and controlled error. A whole-source bound means a bound on the entire original quantum sum, including all colors and derivative terms.

These requirements have now been proved for the strict-tree class. The older local methods later in this chapter remain useful for other presentations. Their unresolved steps are not unresolved steps in the strict-tree theorem.

The completed proof for strict trees

The exact tangle recursion supplies the starting point. A physical leaf is a quarter-turned twist operator. Multiplication stacks two tangles in their recorded order, while a turn changes which two boundary strands are regarded as inputs. Expanding these operations in a fixed finite basis gives exact formulas and exact inequalities on the integer indices. After the forced closing index is removed, the remaining indices form a primitive integer lattice: there is no hidden multiplicity from using only a sublattice. This is what permits a local lattice sum to be compared with an integral without losing its normalization.

At complete colors, the factorial weights have a real limiting action: the logarithm of a weight, divided by NN, has that limit. The action is a function of the scaled leaf and product indices, subject to the original compatibility equations. It must be maximized with those equations still imposed. Strictness ensures that the maximum occurs where the relevant cell angles are positive. The geometry chapter explains why the resulting cells reconstruct the actual augmented exterior, rather than merely a collection of tetrahedra of the correct total volume.

The local analysis then permits the auxiliary colors to vary slightly in a complex neighborhood. The general core theorem and the quarter-index theorem retain the exact recurrence, its boundary data and the original phases. They give a nonzero analytic contribution near the geometric critical point. The quarter-index case is needed because some strict diagrams have a limiting scaled leaf index exactly 1/41/4; it is an equal-modulus boundary for two transfer roots, not a collision of those roots. The proof controls their accumulated contributions instead of inferring dominance from their distinctness.

The remaining difficulty is global: a pointwise estimate on each factor can be too large after the factors are joined. The solution uses two compatible estimates for parts of the tree. A product carrying an actual character operator is controlled by the decorated-character theorem. A connected product region without such an operator is closed against an auxiliary positive comparison polynomial. The probe theorem bounds that comparison at the region’s actual geometric boundary action. The comparison polynomial is used only in the inequality; it does not replace a physical coupon in the invariant.

The spectral propagation theorem shows that these estimates survive the next turn and product. Here a spectral norm records both values and first derivatives at repeated spectral nodes. The proof keeps a neighborhood of the unique maximizing indices separate from its complement. In the neighborhood, the actual polynomial factors give angular decay and a local loss when a physical color is far from complete. Outside it, a fixed exponential gap absorbs the polynomial interpolation costs. Induction over the finite tree therefore controls the full expression at the correct action, without assigning the same cost to every turn.

This yields two global facts. Every physical color tuple obeys the required upper bound, and any tuple with at least one color a fixed fraction away from complete has a strict exponential loss. The difference between a full leaf and its chosen local expression is an explicit complementary-color term, called an alias. Expanding these differences turns every unwanted term into an actual full quantum evaluation with at least one small color. The global color bound suppresses them. This is why the argument controls all aliases at once; it does not assume that individual absolute-value estimates preserve the cancellations.

Finally, an exact finite Fourier identity inserts the prescribed integer twists into the original leaf frames. The local packet produces a nonzero saddle contribution. Estimates on the whole Fourier domain, including its boundary terms, make the remaining part exponentially smaller. The marked filling theorem identifies the resulting action with the volume of the actual knot complement. Only auxiliary crossing-circle cusps are filled. The main knot cusp remains complete and unfilled.

The same limit written through a quantum AA-operator

A quantum AA-operator is a finite difference operator in a meridian variable. It is more data than a classical algebraic curve, but an equation alone can still have several solutions. The actual solution is selected here by its original quantum-group definition and finitely many fixed small-color values.

Write x=qNαx=q_N^\alpha for the raw representation coordinate and M=−qNxM=-q_Nx for the marked meridian coordinate. If FN(M)F_N(M) is the reduced ADO family in this coordinate, then FN(1)=KN(K),FN(−qNj)=Cj(qN). F_N(1)=K_N(K),\qquad F_N(-q_N^j)=C_j(q_N). The complete point is M=1M=1; Cj(q)C_j(q) is the ordinary reduced jj-color period. These identities include the original framing and quotient-color conventions.

The finite cut-state proof supplies a genuine level-independent annihilator P(q,M,T)=∑i=0dai(q,M)Ti,TM=qMT. P(q,M,T)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_i(q,M)T^i,\qquad TM=qMT. Here dd is the fixed operator order, the aia_i are rational coefficient functions, and (TF)(M)=F(qM)(TF)(M)=F(qM). The operator is obtained from the finite quantum formula before evaluating the invariant. It is not defined by a ratio of already known values. The finite-pointing theorem then expresses the exact scalar as KN(K)=CT⁡t=0[rN(qN(1+t))c0(qN(1+t))]. K_N(K)=\operatorname{CT}_{t=0} \bigl[r_N(q_N(1+t))\,c_0(q_N(1+t))\bigr]. Here c0c_0 is a fixed vector of small-color Laurent polynomials, rNr_N is the ordered companion-matrix product prescribed by PP, and CT\operatorname{CT} means the constant term of the whole Laurent expansion. Singular intermediate steps are retained. Only a fixed number of derivatives of c0c_0 is required, independent of NN.

The pointed quantum AA-volume corollary proves that this exact signed scalar has the knot-volume limit above. It also gives a positive ratio of coefficients of Gram determinants, equal to a sum of squared color periods, with the corresponding volume limit. The signed scalar is not replaced by a matrix norm or by a sum of absolute values. The full ADO normalization is retained as a rational factor whose whole value at M=1M=1 is iN+3/Ni^{N+3}/N.

The geometric longitude theorem supplies a separate nonparabolic main-meridian construction for the circle trees. It identifies the selected curve with the preferred-longitude deformation. Combined with the full microscopic expansion, it identifies the selected geometric factor of the first nonzero classical symbol. The full quantum-module comparison and extension to other strict presentations remain open; the earlier auxiliary-color deformation alone does not provide those statements.

Other local tools, and their different scope

The rest of this chapter explains methods developed for general augmented plat diagrams. A plat is a braid diagram closed by fixed cups and caps; its bridge width counts the chosen bridges. These results often hold at every fixed width, but some routes below still need a global estimate for unrestricted plats. This limitation concerns that route and class. It does not undo the complete proof just described.

A few recurring names have a concrete role. A channel is an intermediate representation label in a change of basis. A Casimir matrix is the operator whose eigenspaces label those channels. A qq-Hahn polynomial is a finite polynomial appearing in an actual matrix column; its root locations can control cancellation. A resolvent is (zI−Ω)−1(zI-\Omega)^{-1} for such a matrix Ω\Omega. A Ward identity is an exact relation between shifted or differentiated terms of the original sum. These objects are used to keep track of the full contraction, not to introduce another knot invariant.

Several notes also use the word completed for an operator that combines two colliding spectral channels with their value and derivative terms. It does not mean “a proof has been completed.” A fold is the midpoint where the paired channel labels meet. A complex tube is a fixed neighborhood of the real parameter region; an estimate on the integer samples alone is not an estimate throughout that tube.

A finite function of the auxiliary colors

For this subsection, fix one augmented plat diagram. It has bb auxiliary crossing circles, each surrounding two strands in a specified part of the diagram. The auxiliary color nin_i, an integer between 11 and NN, chooses the representation on the iith circle. Complete color means ni=Nn_i=N. Its deficit is hi=N−nih_i=N-n_i. The complete-color formula and all-color formula compute the original marked scalar from the same crossing matrices, cups and caps for every fixed plat width.

A specified function of noninteger colors is needed because a filling formula is a finite Fourier sum in the physical integer colors. To estimate that sum by stationary phase, one needs a specified analytic function near those samples. An arbitrary interpolation would change the saddle. The entire color function constructs the needed finite function directly from the spectral decomposition of the complete-circle operator.

Put MN=⌊(N−1)/2⌋M_N=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor. The distinct spectral channels are numbered j=0,…,MNj=0,\ldots,M_N, with eigenvalues cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N)c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N). Let LN,jL_{N,j} denote the actual component of the complete-circle operator at channel jj, including the derivative-derived term when the node is repeated. For a tuple 𝒋=(j1,…,jb)\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_b), define AN(𝒋)A_N(\mathbf j) by inserting LN,jiL_{N,j_i} at each auxiliary circle and carrying out the original matrix contraction. The fixed half twists, cups, caps, marked trace and external scalar all remain in this coefficient.

Now let χ=(χ1,…,χb)\chi=(\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_b) be a complex vector and set mj=N−2j−1,CN(χ)=∑j1=0MN⋯∑jb=0MNAN(j1,…,jb)∏i=1bcos⁡(πmjiχi). \begin{aligned} m_j&=N-2j-1,\\ C_N(\chi)&= \sum_{j_1=0}^{M_N}\cdots\sum_{j_b=0}^{M_N} A_N(j_1,\ldots,j_b) \prod_{i=1}^{b}\cos\!\bigl(\pi m_{j_i}\chi_i\bigr). \end{aligned} Every index and coefficient is therefore fixed by the original diagram. The function is entire because it is a finite sum of cosines. It is even in each variable. When NN is odd, the final frequency is zero and its cosine is exactly 11; that endpoint is included.

At physical high colors, substitute χi=hi/N\chi_i=h_i/N. The value of CNC_N is exactly the term obtained by keeping the complete-circle part of each color identity. This term is called the core. The same exact color identity also has a complementary small-color term, called an alias. Expanding the product gives CN(h/N)C_N(h/N) plus one explicit term for every nonempty subset of alias choices. This is an identity of full operators, including their repeated-node derivatives. It does not say the aliases are small. At complete colors hi=0h_i=0, they vanish, so CN(0)C_N(0) is the full complete-color scalar.

Finite Fourier orthogonality recovers every coefficient AN(𝒋)A_N(\mathbf j) from the values of CNC_N on the grid hi/Nh_i/N, 0≤hi<N0\le h_i<N. Parseval’s identity then controls a weighted sum of |AN(𝒋)|2|A_N(\mathbf j)|^2. It does not control cancellation in their signed sum. For unrestricted plats, this finite formula alone does not prove the required complex asymptotic or suppress the alias terms. For the strict Conway class, the later whole-sum argument described above supplies precisely those additional estimates.

The finite polynomials select a local complex action

After its forced zero is removed, every actual physical q-Hahn polynomial has simple negative real roots. The proof uses an exact sine recurrence, interlacing and sine coefficient operators; the numerical root checks only test the implementation.

Consequently its logarithm, real on the positive axis, is holomorphic on the slit plane. Real factorial estimates first identify its strictly concave positive-axis variational limit.
