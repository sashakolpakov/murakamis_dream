From the original source to a volume limit

Preserve the source before taking a limit

The invariant is a specified contraction of quantum operators, cups, caps, and a marked functional. A formula with the same classical A-curve can still represent a different quantum solution or select a different period. The pointed structure keeps the source, lattice, peripheral data, and marked normal information together.

At a root of unity, paired spectral channels can merge. Terms that vanish at the root can contribute to the required derivative. In the current Racah formulas, this includes a retained [2N]T[2N]T term. Evaluating first and then differentiating a truncated formula loses information.

The torsor status note explains how this viewpoint relates to earlier work in the repository. A classical A-polynomial alone has not supplied the selected period or its absolute geometric normalization.

Exact finite functions at arbitrary bridge width

This finite function has exact Fourier reconstruction and Parseval identities at both parities, including the odd zero-frequency endpoint. Thus the Fourier proposal has a concrete object to estimate. Parseval controls a norm, while the desired invariant is a particular coherent contraction. A uniform complex asymptotic, physical alias suppression and survival of the geometric contribution still require proof for a general plat.

An upper bound is only half of equality

For sources with phases, a matching lower bound requires survival of the geometric contribution. A large positive comparison partition does not exclude cancellation in the original invariant.

The local energy geometry makes this distinction explicit. The highest-weight block at the fold has the volume action of a variable ideal octahedron. Away from the fold, its actual measure forces a signed boundary term. Even at the real energy maximum, the marked complex phase need not be stationary. A global argument must use the original incidence and peripheral conditions to address those phases.

For the specified positive Conway expressions, positivity supplies a different route. Exact support is an additive integer semigroup, which approximates a maximizing configuration along every sufficiently large level. Uniform factorial estimates prove the whole-source variational limit. The new geometric argument then proves that the reduced maximum is strict, matches the actual quadrilateral faces, and reconstructs the complete complement of every retained cylindrical factor. The new mixed-color proof supplies the additional filling estimates: exact leaf aliases lie in a square-zero radical, a strict seed-level gap survives arbitrary positive composition, and a finite complex lattice window has a nonzero Gaussian amplitude. This proves the long-full-twist source limit. The separate exact face-map and peripheral construction now identifies its rate with actual filled volume. The unchanged pure grammar still cannot produce knots by those fillings.

Signed half twists change that source and its connectivity. The new supporting-plane argument bounds any number of propagated aliases by adding tangents to the same global action; the linear terms cancel under the actual wire constraints. A separately checked finite complex packet supplies the nonzero contribution. This works for every fixed C(FG)C(FG) with robust children, and the corresponding marked geometry gives the filled knot and link equality stated in the results chapter.
