Research guide

A guide to the volume reference

Read the reference PDF · Source and build instructions · Papers organized by subject

The reference begins with the volume theorem for strict Conway compositions, proved through the pure auxiliary source. It includes the same proof files as the 17-page Conway paper. The rest of the volume reference retains independent circle-tree and planar constructions with their own hypotheses.

The companion manuscripts are from the same project. The Conway paper uses five directly and one further geometric prerequisite, as explained in the collection guide. They reorganize the shared mathematical material; the long reference also contains results outside this seven-paper route.

The signed local calculation used for the separate recurrence problem is proved in the quarter-index paper. Standalone meridian, longitude and recurrence papers remain available separately. The guide’s other methods chapters keep the history of alternative approaches; this chapter describes the current volume proofs.

Readers new to the invariant can begin with the first-reading chapter. The results chapter gives the complete family-by-family scope.

The question and the fixed data

At level NN, let KN(K)K_N(K) be the reduced Kashaev invariant of a knot KK, normalized to be one on the unknot. For a hyperbolic knot, the volume conjecture asks whether

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K)|=Vol⁡(S3\K). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K)| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K).

The volume is that of the complete knot complement. In every theorem described here, the finite diagram, its strand connections, disk framings and integer powers are fixed before NN grows. The limit includes all sufficiently large integer levels, not just primes or one parity. The auxiliary crossing circles are filled; the main knot cusp remains complete.

A crossing circle bounds a disk pierced by two strands. Filling its primitive slope μℓ+pℓλℓ\mu_\ell+p_\ell\lambda_\ell inserts the recorded twists, where μℓ\mu_\ell is its meridian and λℓ\lambda_\ell its disk longitude. With retained half twist ϵℓ∈{0,±1}\epsilon_\ell\in\{0,\pm1\}, the crossing power is

nℓ=ϵℓ−2pℓ. n_\ell=\epsilon_\ell-2p_\ell.

The factor two distinguishes full twists from individual crossings. The strand connections, including any component closed inside a box, must give one knot.

The strict construction

A two-strand tangle is drawn in a box with four ordered endpoints. Stacking joins the adjacent endpoints without changing their order. A quarter-turn carries the entire tangle and its crossing disks. Cylindrical closure joins bottom-left to top-left and bottom-right to top-right outside the last box.

The elementary leaf is the clockwise quarter-turn of an augmented crossing-circle box; the turn carries its disk and endpoint labels. Every occurrence has its own crossing circle. The strictness test assigns a real label to every exposed output:

Operation Constraint
Elementary leaf 0<a<1/20<a<1/2
Quarter-turn x↦1−xx\mapsto1-x
Product with inputs x,yx,y and output zz max⁡(x,y)<z<x+y<1\max(x,y)<z<x+y<1
Final closing pair The two outputs sum to one; its forced product is omitted

An expression is strict when these finitely many constraints have a solution. An elementary closing child is allowed. A leaf at the stationary value 1/41/4 is allowed as well; the proof does not require separated roots of a signed leaf recurrence.

For example, C((h1h2)(h3h4))C((h_1h_2)(h_3h_4)) is strict when every leaf has label 3/103/10 and the two product outputs have label 1/21/2. Then 3/10<1/2<3/5<13/10<1/2<3/5<1. Retained crossing parities (0,1,1,1)(0,1,1,1) give a knot: tracing the elementary endpoint pairings through the stacks and the outside arcs gives one component and no internally closed component.

For each fixed strict expression with at least two leaves and an admissible parity pattern, there is a threshold P0P_0. Every fixed tuple with |pℓ|>P0|p_\ell|>P_0 at every auxiliary circle has the volume limit. Signs and ratios are unrestricted. The tuple does not grow with NN.

The assignments covered by this condition have natural density one in ℤb\mathbb Z^b, where bb is the fixed number of circles. Among integer tuples in [−R,R]b[-R,R]^b, at most

b(2⌊P0⌋+1)(2R+1)b−1 b(2\lfloor P_0\rfloor+1)(2R+1)^{b-1}

have a bounded coordinate. Their proportion tends to zero, but these excluded slabs are still infinite. Different assignments may describe the same knot. The theorem is not a density claim for distinct knots ordered by crossing number or a result for every short filling.

Five steps in the pure-source proof

The finite source is the evaluation of the augmented diagram as a function of its auxiliary colors. A color is an integer representation label; a circle is a component of the diagram. Summing colors and filling a circle are different operations.

The pure source uses no retained half twists on the auxiliary circles. Its core Newton coefficients are positive. The complementary-color terms, called aliases, remain part of the physical invariant and need separate estimates. The proof proceeds as follows.

  1. Keep the exact integer sum. The finite Newton calculus gives product, turn and closing coefficients, including derivatives at repeated spectral roots. Turns substitute r↦N−1−rr\mapsto N-1-r; the closing constraint has coefficient 11 or −1-1. Solving these equations leaves a lattice of cell volume one, with every bounded integer offset retained.

  2. Interpolate the positive pure core exactly. The canonical factorial GNG_N has precisely the finite sine-factorial values. Its complex expansion applies at the unique strict positive maximum. One lattice contour lemma gives the nonzero local Gaussian coefficient. This argument also works at a quarter-index leaf; no signed recurrence estimate is used.

  3. Pay for the physical and Fourier complements. The source-bound theorem controls the full physical remainder and every macroscopic color deficit. A second use of the lattice lemma evaluates the color Fourier sum. A separate contour estimate bounds the rest of the Fourier torus. A local Taylor expansion alone would not supply that global estimate.

  4. Perform each full crossing power. Fourier–Hermite inversion expands every positive or negative integer power, including odd powers, in ordinary colored circles. The whole filling-sum theorem retains all Fourier charges and endpoint currents and proves that their leading coefficient is nonzero.

  5. Identify the same action geometrically. The marked classical shear restores the retained half twist and the original integer filling slope. The geometric theorem proves the metric on the specified filled exterior, its primitive cusp completion and the absolute action–volume equality.

The Conway source guide lists each numbered companion import. The proof requires all five steps. A positive auxiliary sum alone does not control a signed closing.

What orthogonality does, and what it does not do

The reference opens with explicit linear-algebra propositions used by the proof. They explain where exact simplification is available.

Transport the coefficient pairing

Let B:ℂm→VB:\mathbb C^m\to V send coefficients cc to a vector BcBc in a finite-dimensional inner-product space VV. Its Gram matrix is G=B*BG=B^*B, with *^* the adjoint. Then

∥Bc∥2=c*Gc,ker⁡G=ker⁡B. \|Bc\|^2=c^*Gc,\qquad \ker G=\ker B.

Thus the form becomes positive definite on the quotient by coefficients representing zero. Under a coordinate change c=Sdc=Sd, it becomes S*GSS^*GS. It can be replaced by the identity only when those coordinates are orthonormal.

A closing row ℓ:V→ℂ\ell:V\to\mathbb C is additional data. The estimate |ℓ(Bc)|≤∥ℓ∥c*Gc|\ell(Bc)|\le\|\ell\|\sqrt{c^*Gc} is an upper bound. A large nonzero vector can still lie in ker⁡ℓ\ker\ell. The volume proof must therefore estimate the specified closing, not replace it by a norm.

A linear corridor is a sequence of prescribed linear maps. Replacing it by their matrix product is exact. A product with one fixed input can be part of such a corridor; a product with two independently summed inputs cannot be treated as one prescribed map.

Use all shifted Fourier characters

For a real shift α\alpha, set wk=e2πi(k+α)/Nw_k=e^{2\pi i(k+\alpha)/N}, 0≤k<N0\le k<N. The coefficients

Ha=1N∑k=0N−1fkwk−a,0≤a<N, H_a=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f_kw_k^{-a}, \qquad 0\le a<N,

satisfy

fk=∑a=0N−1Hawka,∑a|Ha|2=1N∑k|fk|2. f_k=\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}H_aw_k^a,\qquad \sum_a|H_a|^2=\frac1N\sum_k|f_k|^2.

The shift contributes only unit phases to the complete character basis. The filling calculation uses α=1/2\alpha=1/2, so wkN=−1w_k^N=-1.

The repeated spectral roots require normal data as well as values. For the crossing power nn, the value data hkh_k have modulus one and the normal data are dk=n(k−(N−1)/2)hkd_k=n(k-(N-1)/2)h_k. Parseval controls both vectors. Their exact coefficient reconstruction gives

∑j|βj|≤2N(1+|n|12), \sum_j|\beta_j| \le2\sqrt N\left(1+\frac{|n|}{\sqrt{12}}\right),

where the βj\beta_j are the coefficients of the full power in the ordinary-circle basis. Discarding the derivatives would reconstruct a different element of the nonsemisimple algebra. The estimate is a polynomial filling cost; it does not prove a lower bound.

For the full closing, let j∈{0,…,N−1}rj\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^r range over every color tuple and let DN(j)D_N(j) be the original source error. The exact identity and its subsequent bound are

EN=∑j(∏ℓ=1rβN,ℓ,jℓ)DN(j),|EN|≤(∏ℓ=1r∑k|βN,ℓ,k|)BNif |DN(j)|≤BN for every j. E_N=\sum_j\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^r\beta_{N,\ell,j_\ell}\right)D_N(j), \qquad |E_N|\le \left(\prod_{\ell=1}^r\sum_k|\beta_{N,\ell,k}|\right)B_N \quad\text{if }|D_N(j)|\le B_N\text{ for every }j.

The sum remains coherent until the triangle inequality is applied. For fixed powers and a fixed number of circles, the coefficient product grows only polynomially. The Lean module ConwayAssembly derives this whole-error estimate from the proved Fourier–Hermite bound.

Eliminate stationary variables with their Hessian

Let F(y,t)F(y,t) be a holomorphic phase. Suppose Fy(y0,0)=0F_y(y_0,0)=0 and Fyy(y0,0)F_{yy}(y_0,0) is invertible. There is then a local stationary solution y*(t)y_*(t). For Φ(t)=F(y*(t),t)\Phi(t)=F(y_*(t),t),

Φtt=Ftt−FtyFyy−1Fyt. \Phi_{tt}=F_{tt}-F_{ty}F_{yy}^{-1}F_{yt}.

This is stationary elimination, not an exact contraction of the finite sum. The displayed matrix is its Schur complement. In the pure source, color evenness makes the mixed block vanish at zero. The critical value Φ(0)\Phi(0) must also be retained: derivatives alone do not determine the additive constant needed for volume.

The final complex implication

The reference proves the final logarithmic step directly. Suppose the exact invariant has the form

KN=uN(NρaNeNSN+EN),|uN|=1, K_N=u_N\left(N^\rho a_Ne^{NS_N}+E_N\right),\qquad |u_N|=1,

with fixed real ρ\rho, complex sequences aN→a≠0a_N\to a\ne0 and SN→SS_N\to S, and

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡|EN|<ℜS. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|E_N|<\Re S.

Here ENE_N is the entire remainder, not one selected tail. The leading term has rate ℜS\Re S, so the remainder divided by it tends exponentially to zero. The reverse triangle inequality makes KNK_N eventually nonzero and gives the ordinary limit N−1log⁡|KN|→ℜSN^{-1}\log|K_N|\to\Re S. If the marked geometric proof identifies 2πℜS2\pi\Re S with the knot-complement volume, the desired volume limit follows.

A factor 1+O(N−1)1+O(N^{-1}) belongs to the leading amplitude aNa_N; it need not have a smaller exponential rate. Also SN→SS_N\to S permits the logarithmic conclusion without asserting eNSN/eNS→1e^{NS_N}/e^{NS}\to1.

The accompanying cost lemma is zero-safe. If nonnegative budgets and costs satisfy BNB_N of upper rate at most bb, CNC_N of upper rate at most zero, and |EN|≤CNBN|E_N|\le C_NB_N, then the remainder has upper rate at most bb. Upper rate means limsup⁡N−1log⁡(⋅)\limsup N^{-1}\log(\cdot), with log⁡0=−∞\log0=-\infty. In particular a nonnegative polynomial cost does not change that upper rate. No division by a possibly zero budget is needed.

Other volume families retain their own hypotheses

The ordered circle-tree construction has a crossing circle at every vertex. An edge is a prescribed quarter-turn attachment, and the cyclic order and strand frame are retained. For vertex degree dvd_v and signed crossing power nvn_v, the summed criterion is

∑vdvdv2+nv2≤2−25. \sum_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2}\le2^{-25}.

A separate directed criterion has the simple sufficient consequence dv≥228d_v\ge2^{28} or |nv|≥227|n_v|\ge2^{27} at each vertex. The degree alternative permits fixed short or zero powers. The graph remains fixed, and the component test must give one knot. The circle-tree paper proves the two analytic routes separately. Its geometric companion constructs the metric under Lv=dv2+nv2≥630L_v=\sqrt{d_v^2+n_v^2}\ge630; that geometric criterion alone does not establish a quantum asymptotic.

The local ratio test max⁡vdv/(dv2+nv2)≤2−120\max_v d_v/(d_v^2+n_v^2)\le2^{-120} is now a direct corollary of the directed theorem. A short numerical implication supplies its degree-or-twist alternative, so no separate local quantum induction remains. The summed proof still gives its explicit first microscopic meridian correction. The directed and local statements give the centered ratio 1+O(N−1)1+O(N^{-1}), without claiming that sharper correction.

For planar constructions, begin with a finite simple triangulated sphere and a perfect matching of its dual triangles. Each matched pair determines one crossing-circle region. There are several different sufficient hypotheses:

Construction What supplies the full remainder bound
Terminal decoration Explicit finite insertions give strict shared packing inequalities.
An unchanged matched sphere Weak balance, including the stated small-defect extension.
Protected triangular sums Certificates for all locally allowed noncentral strings in each input.
Ordinary triangular insertion Certified pieces and small positive boundary ratios at every chosen witness.

The terminal decoration preserves its chosen strand pairing but can change the knot type. Its theorem fills all original and added circles sufficiently long. The ordinary insertion also has a fixed-boundary geometric minimization and retains the old coefficient demands. None of these statements asserts a general remainder bound for every unchanged sphere.

The results page gives the conditions and diagrams. The reference keeps their complete proofs in the corresponding chapters and appendices, rather than importing the strict Conway conclusion into a different diagram class.

The supporting-tangent certificates are exact finite parts of specified inequality proofs. Their rational points, domain cover and outward bounds establish those inequalities; numerical optimization is not part of verification. The figure is available as a vector PDF and editable TikZ source.

Two useful conclusions that are not full volume theorems

The uniform filling appendix starts with a polynomial SNS_N of separate degree at most N−1N-1 in rr spectral variables. For fixed crossing powers nen_e, its filled contraction satisfies

|ZN|≤∏e=1r(1+|ne|/2)∥SN∥[−2,2]r. |Z_N|\le\prod_{e=1}^r(1+|n_e|/2) \,\|S_N\|_{[-2,2]^r}.

The constant is independent of NN, and the functional retains the derivatives at repeated roots. A strict estimate for the shared norm remains a separate premise of any volume application.

The matched-sphere geometric-contribution appendix evaluates one specified central contribution of the original invariant. It proves a nonzero asymptotic at the actual filled volume rate. Without a complementary-source bound it does not prove that the whole invariant has that rate. The restricted planar theorems above supply the missing bound under their own conditions.

Reading and reproducing the argument

For the strict result, read the conceptual chapter, then the shared Conway proof. Its main export is Theorem 𝚌𝚊:𝚖𝚊𝚒𝚗\texttt{ca:main}.
