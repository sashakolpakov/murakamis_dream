Murakami’s Dream

Quantum invariants and actual hyperbolic volume

The question driving this project is whether the original quantum invariant of a hyperbolic knot has exponential growth determined by the volume of its complement:

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K)|=Vol⁡(S3\K).\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K)|=\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K).

We pursue this through the pointed quantum A-structure, exact finite source formulas, positive energies, and hyperbolic angle optimization. The original marked source and peripheral factors stay part of the problem.

The current target is a theorem about composing Conway algebraic, or arborescent, tangles: fix any admissible finite tangle tree, then put independently sufficiently many signed twists into its designated boxes. The diagram and twists stay fixed as NN grows. Bridge number is not the organizing parameter. The right side is the knot complement volume; Dehn filling removes auxiliary crossing-circle cusps and leaves the knot cusp unfilled.

The full-invariant theorem now permits repeated single-leaf attachments as well as completed products and quarter-turns. The current theorem starts from hhhh and allows T(E)T(E), E1E2E_1E_2, hEhE and EhEh, closing with two completed children. Any finite number and depth of these operations is allowed. Constructible knot parity patterns and independently sufficiently long fixed signed twists give the knot complement volume, through both level parities and composite levels. The earlier isolated seed closures of width at least three remain included; isolated two-leaf closure remains excluded.

The new estimate combines the attached leaf, its original multiplication gate, and the completed child’s output tangent. Joint concavity and exact rational inequalities prove a uniform alias loss that survives further attachments. The original complex packet and primitive peripheral action then give the full invariant limit. The tangle formulation distinguishes this class from the remaining problem: arbitrary root-leaf closures, turned bare leaves, and coverage of all arborescent presentations. The later general core theorem covers every strict knot pattern, and marked filling geometry now covers every strict tree. The remaining general source requirement is the nonempty-alias estimate. A root-star companion theorem proves the full limit for C(h0T(h1⋯hs))C(h_0T(h_1\cdots h_s)), s≥2s\ge2, including mixed pure/odd leaves. Its overlap with the earlier belt source is recorded explicitly; no new knot classification is claimed.

The earlier plat constructions, complete-packing certificates, weighted Cauchy estimates, Fourier methods and torsor calculations remain in the equality results and the frontier. They provide other certified families and approaches to the general problem. The current research theorem does not establish the conjecture for all arborescent or all hyperbolic knots, and its internal reviews are not external peer review.

Read the argument in order

The Conway composition manuscript gives the current theorem as a detailed paper, with preliminaries, proofs, illustrations and a reproducibility suite. A Sphinx edition provides a second documentation format.

The research library organizes the work by question: equality results, quantum sources, geometry, torsors, Fourier methods, the cube, and the general frontier. Each route gives a main argument, its scope, and supporting notes. The figure gallery introduces the main objects visually.

Start with the equality results for the exact knot and link families and their quantifiers. Then read the mechanism: what the source formulas do, where weighted Cauchy enters, and why a local scalar estimate does not finish the problem. The geometry chapter explains what certifies the volume of the actual complement.

The current frontier identifies the remaining general steps. Notation and reproducing audits are reference chapters. The searchable archive contains the full technical notes, scripts, and recorded results; it is not a second reading list to complete before understanding the project.
