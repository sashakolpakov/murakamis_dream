Research papers

Finite quantum invariants and hyperbolic volume

The manuscripts are organized by the mathematical results they establish and the results they use. The dependency graph records those relations before assigning paper boundaries. A finite spectral theorem should be citable independently of an asymptotic theorem; a geometric filling construction should state precisely the information needed to compare its volume with a quantum action.

The 374-page reference account contains 93 pages of main text, 266 pages of appendices, and 15 pages of front matter and references. The appendices collect several subjects: finite quantum algebra, geometric constructions, asymptotic estimates, sufficient conditions for different knot families, and meridian and longitude results. Those subjects accumulated in one file; the length does not show that one theorem requires 374 pages. The account remains available while this division is prepared. The working limit of 30 pages per paper is an editorial constraint, not the principle for grouping results.

Explore the dependency graph. Each statement lists the results it uses, the reason for each use, and the exact place where the statement or proof can be found. Arrows run from prerequisites to their consumers. The overview groups related results; the detailed view retains the individual statements.

The components are finer than the eventual papers. They are not a proposal for one paper per box. For example, finite spectral algebra and exact diagram evaluation now form one article, with the global contraction and normalization calculations included. The graph keeps them as separate components because the abstract norm theorem does not use that contraction argument.

The current source-asymptotics proof defines its finite action directly from the exact cofactor polynomial. The earlier fold interpolation remains in the reference account and guide as an alternative construction; the direct proof does not require both routes. This is one concrete way the dependency analysis shortens the presentation without removing a needed argument.

Three other distinctions determine the division. The classical filling construction does not need a nonzero quantum asymptotic; their comparison comes later, through the same marked critical equations. A nonzero geometric contribution does not bound the rest of the invariant. Finally, weak balance, protected triangular gluing, ordinary triangular insertion and the three-braid comparison provide different sufficient estimates for that remainder. Protected gluing does not require weak balance. The ordinary-insertion estimate has a separate proof; the construction of a common core for several inserted diagrams also uses protected gluing.

The current drafts

The seventeen papers total 262 pages, with individual lengths from 11 to 28 pages. They provide complete accounts of the results described below, with numbered companion imports. This is a partial extraction of the reference manuscript, rather than a replacement for all its theorem families.

Finite spectral evaluation and uniform filling bounds. 28 pages. TeX source · PDF

This paper asks how a fixed twisting operation acts on a polynomial of degree less than the root-of-unity level. It defines the functional at simple and repeated spectral roots, proves its norm bound, and explains why the canonical polynomial representative matters. It then proves the exact coefficient formula for a marked planar diagram constructed from a matched triangulated sphere. The proof includes the face variables, the integer support, the shifted grids at even levels, the cups and caps, and the single reduced cut. A finite Fourier and matrix calculation compares the result with the reduced Kashaev invariant, including the framing factor. These results supply the finite formulas used by later papers. They do not estimate the large-level invariant or identify an exponential rate with volume.

Newton coefficients and positive sources for Conway tangles. 18 pages. TeX source · PDF

This paper supplies the additional finite calculus for composing turned two-strand tangles. It proves the Newton product, physical quarter-turn, closure and colored-circle formulas in the full Casimir quotient, retaining the derivatives at repeated spectral roots and the retained half-twist factors. It uses the finite paper’s operator and reduced Kashaev normalization. For the stated class of fixed Conway compositions, it then proves the positive complete auxiliary-color source’s variational limit, strict interior maximum and stationary boundary bounds at both level parities. The positive source is not the signed filled invariant. The analytic estimates for that invariant remain separate. The next two papers supply the moving-meridian formulas and the complete geometric interpretation, respectively.

Exact Newton calculus for tangles with a moving meridian. 15 pages. TeX source · PDF

The parameter of the main strand now varies while the quantum level stays fixed. The paper starts with the physical representation matrices and computes the partial trace before identifying its polynomial form. It proves all six oriented quarter-turn formulas and keeps the derivatives needed when spectral roots coincide, including at the additional collisions away from the complete meridian. The closing scalar and crossing phases are retained. The resulting tree formula sums every index and gives the same contraction regardless of the order in which vertices are eliminated. These are exact finite identities; they do not show which part of the sum dominates as the level grows. The comparison with Kashaev’s original normalization imports the finite spectral paper at levels at least three; the algebraic identities hold from level two.

Pyramids, prisms, and complete geometry of Conway closures. 13 pages. TeX source · PDF

The positive-source maximum in the Newton paper determines ideal hyperbolic cells. This paper proves that the stationary equations match their entire ordered quadrilateral faces, and that successive attachments stay convex. It then identifies the assembled boundary with the original projection cut, checks the face maps and edge returns, and constructs compatible horospheres at every cusp. The metric is therefore on the specified augmented link complement. Its volume is twice the sum of the cell volumes, or 2π2\pi times the source action. The original meridian and disk longitude form a primitive basis, also for the retained half-twist partners. The theorem uses the completed-root grammar of the Newton paper. A corollary identifies the positive complete source’s rate with this volume; neither result asserts a signed filling asymptotic.

Complementary color bounds for Conway tangle sources. 16 pages. TeX source · PDF

The exact colored-circle formula has a core and a complementary term. This paper bounds the sum of every contribution containing a complementary term, uniformly over the integer colors and retained half twists. It also bounds the whole source when one color stays a fixed fraction of the level away from complete color. A finite recurrence preserves the signed cancellation inside each core. Fixed matrix blocks bound both recurrence roots, including their collisions; rational supporting planes then give two local strict comparisons that propagate through the completed-root construction. The theorem is an upper bound before filling. It supplies neither a nonzero filled contribution nor a volume identity.

Marked deformations and long fillings of Conway closures. 12 pages. TeX source · PDF

Starting with the complete Conway metric, this paper defines a classical cell potential and continues the full face maps and group relations. The original meridian and disk longitude give the primitive logarithmic filling equations. One neighborhood works for independently long signed slopes, without restrictions on their ratios. The metric completion keeps the main cusps complete and adds smooth solid tori along the original auxiliary slopes. The complete volume fixes the action’s constant, and a comparison of the same fundamental chain gives the absolute filled volume. Its proof does not use a quantum asymptotic. The reference errata explain the corrected factor in the filling Jacobian.

Holomorphic source asymptotics and volume limits for Conway closures. 16 pages. TeX source · PDF

This paper completes the analytic argument for the completed-root Conway construction. It first proves a canonical factorial interpolation that can also be cited without any knot hypothesis. The actual terminal condition of the signed leaf recurrence selects its holomorphic solution; an exponentially accurate interpolation then permits the shared wire sum to be moved to a complex contour. A second finite sum gives an even Fourier polynomial with a real local action and a strict bound away from its maximum. Expanding the full physical crossing powers makes the oscillatory filling theorem applicable to this polynomial. The entire invariant has a nonzero leading coefficient through both level parities. The final comparison fixes the quantum action’s additive constant and identifies it with the independently constructed filled metric volume. The knot diagram and independently long signed powers are fixed before the level tends to infinity. The broader strict-tree syntax is separate.

Uniform spectral bounds for local Casimir sources. 14 pages. TeX source · PDF

This paper proves the estimates needed before applying Cauchy’s coefficient formula to a collection of tangles. It defines the local polynomial by an explicit banded matrix and partitions its paths into three degree regions. Theorem 1.1 bounds all three regions over the entire real spectral interval and for arbitrary phases of the face variables. The proof includes the signed recurrence, both unused spectral products, the endpoint optimization, both level parities and face variables fixed exactly at zero. It imports the operator identification from the finite spectral paper. It does not assume a geometric saddle or a volume identity.

Oscillatory filling sums with independently long twists. 11 pages. TeX source · PDF

This paper addresses cancellation in the complete Fourier filling sum. Theorem 1.1 assumes a nonzero holomorphic expansion near the complete point and a strict bound for the shared source away from it. It then proves a nonzero asymptotic for the entire sum, including every nonzero integer Fourier charge and the original endpoints. The twist integers are fixed, independently signed and sufficiently large; their ratios are unrestricted. The proof estimates the infinite tail before localizing the remaining finitely many integrals. An elementary polynomial example verifies both hypotheses explicitly. For a knot source, construction of those hypotheses and identification of the critical action with volume remain separate theorems.

Holomorphic asymptotics of central shared face coefficients. 12 pages. TeX source · PDF

Theorem 1.1 supplies the two source estimates used by the filling-sum paper. Given positive stationary face weights, it proves a nonzero holomorphic Gaussian expansion for the actual central coefficient and a strict spectral bound away from its complete point. The proof treats the entire shared face torus, including the regions outside the Gaussian neighborhood. It retains both level parities, the original coefficient targets and the odd skipped-row term. A tetrahedral example illustrates why repeated face labels denote one variable and why the Gaussian measure has no extra integer-lattice factor. Section 7 states the exact normalized input to the oscillatory theorem. Bounding the other degree sectors of the invariant remains the task of an application theorem.

Circle patterns and primitive fillings of augmented links. 18 pages. TeX source · PDF

This is the classical geometric foundation. Starting from the marked matched sphere, the paper constructs its complete augmented exterior and one common deformed circle pattern. It calculates the peripheral paths through each original crossing disk, including retained half twists. The critical equations then specify a primitive filling slope. The proof constructs the smooth complete metric and compares its volume with the absolute critical action using the same fundamental chain. Theorem 1.1 holds for independently sufficiently long signed fillings; it does not assume a quantum asymptotic. Two drawings explain the single disk pairing and the logarithmic torus periods. The final corollary states the exact action comparison used by the quantum applications.

Positive octahedra and primitive fillings for trees of crossing circles. 15 pages. TeX source · PDF

For an ordered tree with an original crossing circle at every vertex, this paper builds the actual two-octahedron block for each edge and retains its ordered disk pages, half twists and primitive curves. With dvd_v the vertex degree and nvn_v the signed crossing power, the condition dv2+nv2≥630\sqrt{d_v^2+n_v^2}\ge630 at every vertex gives a positive cone path ending at a smooth complete filling, together with an absolute action-volume identity and quantitative core-length and volume-loss bounds. A more flexible criterion uses different radii at the vertices. The theorem allows zero power at a sufficiently high-degree vertex. Its only specialized companion import is the geometric foundation’s local peripheral-volume differential, not that paper’s long-filling existence theorem. It supplies geometry for subsequent asymptotic applications; it does not select a nonzero contribution to a quantum invariant.

Degree and twist estimates for circle-tree invariants. 15 pages. TeX source · PDF

For the literal ordered circle tree, this paper proves the full invariant’s volume limit when the sum of dv/(dv2+nv2)d_v/(d_v^2+n_v^2) is at most 2−252^{-25}. It permits zero crossing power at a sufficiently high-degree vertex. The proof retains the first derivatives at repeated spectral roots when bounding discarded Newton indices. A complex edge-sum calculation gives the local action and amplitude. A joint Fourier argument controls all charges and endpoint terms, keeping other variables on real integrals or their physical grids while estimating each infinite tail. The first charge has a nonzero coefficient at both parities. The positive-octahedra companion identifies its action with the original knot-complement volume. A second theorem gives the first centered microscopic meridian correction, including its physical framing. Local and directed degree criteria, and the all-orders longitude statements, are separate results.

Shared face estimates and triangular gluing for planar link diagrams. 15 pages. TeX source · PDF

The first criterion bounds every noncentral sector using one weight for all occurrences of each face. At the complete packing, non-strict balance suffices: convexity gives a linear local gain under a common change of weights, while stationarity bounds the total action cost quadratically. The second theorem joins two marked triangular faces and transports a certificate for every locally allowed sector string. It counts the shared boundary target once. A fully specified seventeen-region example and rational certificates show that this gluing theorem applies beyond weak balance. The full volume conclusion imports the five numbered foundation results; the source estimates and their transport are proved here.

Balanced circle packings from terminal tangle decorations. 13 pages. TeX source · PDF

Starting from a simple marked matched sphere, the paper gives an explicit finite number of terminal insertions on both sides of each original crossing circle. It proves their literal endpoint connections, constructs the packing extension while fixing all old circles, and solves the curvature recurrence. The resulting inequalities establish strict balance at every original and added region. Even stacks of odd terminal tangles preserve a chosen knot pairing, while generally changing the knot type. The strict-balance criterion in the planar paper then gives the full reduced Kashaev volume limit after independently sufficiently long fixed signed powers at all regions. The exact source, central asymptotic and primitive volume inputs are named foundation results; the insertion and all-length balance arguments are proved here.

Triangular insertion and volume limits for augmented diagrams. 17 pages. TeX source · PDF

This paper permits insertion at an ordinary triangular face, where all three boundary weights are positive. Its geometric theorem minimizes the disk action with those weights fixed and proves that the result is the removed triangle’s potential plus a constant. It proves existence, uniqueness and the joint limit when two boundary weights vanish. The coefficient estimate then distinguishes a wholly central inserted piece from every other sector string, keeping each old face target once. Explicit circle chains supply small boundary ratios for any fixed finite list of certified inputs. The proof includes the exact recurrence and all-length inequalities; interval arithmetic checks the finitely many constants and certificate inequalities used in the example.

Volume limits for long alternating-generator three-braids. 14 pages. TeX source · PDF

This application concerns the ordinary closure of

σ2n0σ1n1⋯σ2nm−2σ1nm−1,m≥6 even. \sigma_2^{n_0}\sigma_1^{n_1}\cdots \sigma_2^{n_{m-2}}\sigma_1^{n_{m-1}},\qquad m\ge6\text{ even}.

For each fixed number of factors, the signed powers are independently sufficiently large in magnitude, and their permutation must give a knot. The generators alternate; the crossing signs need not alternate. The braid axis is not a component. The paper develops the diagram, its circle packing and the estimates that control the entire remainder. It imports three explicitly stated results: the exact source identity, the uniform local coefficient estimates, and the nonzero geometric filling contribution with its absolute volume calibration. Those dependencies are substantial. The finite input and local upper bounds are proved in the first two papers above. The filling-sum paper proves the analytic implication once its local expansion and global gap are supplied. The source-asymptotics paper constructs that expansion and gap. The circle-pattern paper supplies the marked geometric volume comparison. The application now cites numbered statements in these companion papers, and checks their common normalization and hypotheses.

What still needs its own paper

The division should follow results rather than cut the reference text into equal lengths. The following groups have different mathematical purposes:

Subject Result to explain and prove Dependencies that must remain visible
General strict Conway trees Extend the completed-root argument to the broader syntax, including bare junctions Exact normal form, boundary profiles, signed component bounds, quarter-index expansions and marked geometry
Local and directed circle-tree criteria Prove the separate subtree estimates that remove the summed-condition restriction Exact moving-meridian tree calculus, conditional source bounds, outgoing estimates and classical completion
Geometric longitude and recurrences All-order meridian expansions force the selected geometric factor to divide a classical symbol The moving-meridian source, full expansion, normalization and preferred geometric longitude

The frozen reference manuscript’s first theorem uses its completed-root grammar. The research notes extend this to more general strict ordered trees, with extra normal-form, boundary-profile and spectral-propagation arguments. The dependency graph now records those extra steps separately. It does not treat the narrower manuscript chapters as proofs of the broader extension. The extension still needs its own paper and detailed review of those additional inputs.

These remaining subjects still need their own complete accounts. Each candidate needs a precise theorem, complete proofs of its own claims, defined notation, and an explicit list of imported theorems.

For a citation between papers, the exporting paper must state a numbered theorem with its normalization, degree restrictions, level parity and uniformity assumptions. The importing paper must name that theorem and check those hypotheses. A reference to a folder of calculations is not a substitute. The seventeen drafts now include the completed-root Conway volume theorem and the summed circle-tree criterion, together with their separate finite, analytic and geometric foundations. The broader strict-tree extension, the local and directed circle-tree estimates, and the all-orders meridian and longitude results still require their own extraction and proof review. The dependency graph keeps those obligations visible.

The illustrated guide retains the broader approaches and unsuccessful comparisons. The reference manuscript remains available for the existing full account. Exploratory research is separate from this editing work.

Building and reproducing the papers

With TeX Live or MacTeX, latexmk, Python and PyMuPDF installed, run from the repository root:

python3 tools/build_papers.py

Use a directory name to build just one paper, for example python3 tools/build_papers.py filling-bounds. The command checks the page limit, unresolved references and overfull boxes, then copies the PDFs and their source-hash records to docs/papers/. Add --no-publish to keep the outputs in each paper’s build/ directory. These are typesetting checks, not mathematical verification.

Rebuild the dependency graph with:

python3 tools/build_dependency_graph.py

Its source is papers/dependencies.json. The command verifies the named source locations, checks both statement and component graphs for cycles, and refuses a recorded completed result that imports an open result. It generates the interactive graph, vector overview and source-hash record in docs/papers/. These checks validate the declared dependency record, not the proofs at its nodes.

Replay the papers’ finite identities and rational certificate constants with one command:

python3 repro/check_paper_algebra.py --output repro/results/paper-algebra

The runner requires SymPy, python-flint and NumPy. It saves a separate record and log for each registered check group, and stops on failure. The planar certificate groups retain their supporting interval records in separate subdirectories. The new Newton group compares physical matrix turns, traces, circle coefficients and crossing normals with the stated formulas over exact finite fields. The terminal group checks symbolic curvature identities, oriented insertions and literal pairing graphs; its finite count examples supplement the written construction for every length. The circle-tree group checks rational seam and peripheral identities and the displayed numerical bounds. The constant verifier uses outward rational bounds with explicit series remainders. The argument for every even number of braid factors is written in the three-braid paper; it is not inferred from computations for finitely many braid lengths. The finite checks complement the written proofs of the analytic estimates and geometric constructions.

The local-bound and filling-sum checks can also be run separately:

python3 papers/local-coefficients/check.py --output repro/results/local-coefficients.json
python3 papers/oscillatory-fillings/check.py --output repro/results/oscillatory-fillings.json

These require SymPy. They check finite polynomial identities and algebraic normalizations, with their exact scope recorded in the output. They do not replace the written uniform estimates, contour arguments or asymptotic proofs.
