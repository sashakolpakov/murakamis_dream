Research papers

Finite quantum invariants and hyperbolic volume

The manuscripts are organized by the mathematical results they establish and the results they use. The dependency graph records those relations before assigning paper boundaries. A finite spectral theorem should be citable independently of an asymptotic theorem; a geometric filling construction should state precisely the information needed to compare its volume with a quantum action.

The 374-page reference account contains 93 pages of main text, 266 pages of appendices, and 15 pages of front matter and references. The appendices collect several subjects: finite quantum algebra, geometric constructions, asymptotic estimates, sufficient conditions for different knot families, and meridian and longitude results. Those subjects accumulated in one file; the length does not show that one theorem requires 374 pages. The account remains available while this division is prepared. The working limit of 30 pages per paper is an editorial constraint, not the principle for grouping results.

Explore the dependency graph. Each statement lists the results it uses, the reason for each use, and the exact place where the statement or proof can be found. Arrows run from prerequisites to their consumers. The overview groups related results; the detailed view retains the individual statements.

The components are finer than the eventual papers. They are not a proposal for one paper per box. For example, finite spectral algebra and exact diagram evaluation now form one article, with the global contraction and normalization calculations included. The graph keeps them as separate components because the abstract norm theorem does not use that contraction argument.

Three other distinctions determine the division. The classical filling construction does not need a nonzero quantum asymptotic; their comparison comes later, through the same marked critical equations. A nonzero geometric contribution does not bound the rest of the invariant. Finally, weak balance, protected triangular gluing, ordinary triangular insertion and the three-braid comparison provide different sufficient estimates for that remainder. Protected gluing does not require weak balance, and ordinary insertion does not require the protected-gluing theorem.

The current drafts

Finite spectral evaluation and uniform filling bounds. TeX source · PDF

This paper asks how a fixed twisting operation acts on a polynomial of degree less than the root-of-unity level. It defines the functional at simple and repeated spectral roots, proves its norm bound, and explains why the canonical polynomial representative matters. It then proves the exact coefficient formula for a marked planar diagram constructed from a matched triangulated sphere. The proof includes the face variables, the integer support, the shifted grids at even levels, the cups and caps, and the single reduced cut. A finite Fourier and matrix calculation compares the result with the reduced Kashaev invariant, including the framing factor. These results supply the finite formulas used by later papers. They do not estimate the large-level invariant or identify an exponential rate with volume.

Uniform spectral bounds for local Casimir sources. TeX source · PDF

This paper proves the estimates needed before applying Cauchy’s coefficient formula to a collection of tangles. It defines the local polynomial by an explicit banded matrix and partitions its paths into three degree regions. Theorem 1.1 bounds all three regions over the entire real spectral interval and for arbitrary phases of the face variables. The proof includes the signed recurrence, both unused spectral products, the endpoint optimization, both level parities and face variables fixed exactly at zero. It imports the finite operator identification from the preceding paper. It does not assume a geometric saddle or a volume identity.

Oscillatory filling sums with independently long twists. TeX source · PDF

This paper addresses cancellation in the complete Fourier filling sum. Theorem 1.1 assumes a nonzero holomorphic expansion near the complete point and a strict bound for the shared source away from it. It then proves a nonzero asymptotic for the entire sum, including every nonzero integer Fourier charge and the original endpoints. The twist integers are fixed, independently signed and sufficiently large; their ratios are unrestricted. The proof estimates the infinite tail before localizing the remaining finitely many integrals. An elementary polynomial example verifies both hypotheses explicitly. For a knot source, construction of those hypotheses and identification of the critical action with volume remain separate theorems.

Volume limits for long alternating-generator three-braids. TeX source · PDF

This application concerns the ordinary closure of

σ2n0σ1n1⋯σ2nm−2σ1nm−1,m≥6 even. \sigma_2^{n_0}\sigma_1^{n_1}\cdots \sigma_2^{n_{m-2}}\sigma_1^{n_{m-1}},\qquad m\ge6\text{ even}.

For each fixed number of factors, the signed powers are independently sufficiently large in magnitude, and their permutation must give a knot. The generators alternate; the crossing signs need not alternate. The braid axis is not a component. The paper develops the diagram, its circle packing and the estimates that control the entire remainder. It imports three explicitly stated results: the exact source identity, the uniform local coefficient estimates, and the nonzero geometric filling contribution with its absolute volume calibration. Those dependencies are substantial. The finite input and local upper bounds are proved in the first two papers above. The filling-sum paper proves the analytic implication once its local expansion and global gap are supplied. The construction of that expansion and gap, and the marked geometric volume comparison, still have their complete accounts in the reference manuscript and its cited technical notes.

What still needs its own paper

The division should follow results rather than cut the reference text into equal lengths. The following groups have different mathematical purposes:

Subject Result to explain and prove Dependencies that must remain visible
Holomorphic source asymptotics A nonzero local expansion for the shared coefficient and a strict spectral gap away from it Exact local formulas, the common face lattice and estimates outside the local chart
Marked hyperbolic fillings The classical critical action is the volume of the smoothly filled knot complement The complete packing, deformed circle pattern, primitive peripheral curves and absolute volume normalization
Sufficient conditions on planar compositions Weak balance, decoration and joining operations control the full remainder The source, analytic and geometric results above
Ordered tangle trees Explicit degree and twist conditions give the volume limit The recursive finite evaluation and its geometric construction
Geometric longitude and recurrences All-order meridian expansions force the selected geometric factor to divide a classical symbol The full expansion, its normalization and the geometric longitude

This is a proposed division, not a claim that five more finished papers already exist. The filling-sum theorem now has its own paper, but its source hypotheses and the classical geometry still need separate accounts. A short reference chapter that cites proofs elsewhere would not supply those accounts by itself. Each candidate needs a precise theorem, complete proofs of its own claims, defined notation, and an explicit list of imported theorems.

For a citation between papers, the exporting paper must state a numbered theorem with its normalization, degree restrictions, level parity and uniformity assumptions. The importing paper must name that theorem and check those hypotheses. A reference to a folder of calculations is not a substitute. At present the four drafts above are written; several of their shared foundations still have their complete accounts in the reference manuscript and technical notes. The dependency graph makes that remaining editorial work visible.

The illustrated guide retains the broader approaches and unsuccessful comparisons. The reference manuscript remains available for the existing full account. Exploratory research is separate from this editing work.

Building and reproducing the papers

With TeX Live or MacTeX, latexmk, Python and PyMuPDF installed, run from the repository root:

python3 tools/build_papers.py

Use a directory name to build just one paper, for example python3 tools/build_papers.py filling-bounds. The command checks the page limit, unresolved references and overfull boxes, then copies the PDFs and their source-hash records to docs/papers/. Add --no-publish to keep the outputs in each paper’s build/ directory. These are typesetting checks, not mathematical verification.

Rebuild the dependency graph with:

python3 tools/build_dependency_graph.py

Its source is papers/dependencies.json. The command verifies the named source locations, checks both statement and component graphs for cycles, and refuses a recorded completed result that imports an open result. It generates the interactive graph, vector overview and source-hash record in docs/papers/. These checks validate the declared dependency record, not the proofs at its nodes.

The rational constants in the three-braid argument have a separate command:

python3 repro/check_cyclic_source_constants.py --output repro/results/cyclic-source-constants.json

It uses outward rational bounds with explicit series remainders. The argument for every even number of factors is written in the paper; it is not inferred from computations for finitely many braid lengths.

The two analytic papers each have one additional reproduction command:

python3 papers/local-coefficients/check.py --output repro/results/local-coefficients.json
python3 papers/oscillatory-fillings/check.py --output repro/results/oscillatory-fillings.json

These require SymPy. They check finite polynomial identities and algebraic normalizations, with their exact scope recorded in the output. They do not replace the written uniform estimates, contour arguments or asymptotic proofs.
