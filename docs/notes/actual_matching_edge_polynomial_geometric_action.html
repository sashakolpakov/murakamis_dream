The selected scalar polynomial generates the actual recoupled transport

30 October 2026. At the complete original channel, the canonical leading action of the actual target Casimir polynomial has exactly the derivative required by the ACTUAL inverse cyclic four-port turn. Its additive scalar constant is fixed by a positive finite-source anchor. This is a classical marked-transport comparison with an actual scalar asymptotic, not an asymptotic theorem for the polynomial operator acting in the surrounding contraction.

The exact finite operator and all its original generic normals remain those in the geometric target polynomial theorem. The actual selected scalar branch comes from the root-location and slit-domain theorem, not from choosing a solution of a formal characteristic equation.

1. Follow the actual inverse turn

Use the original trace-minus-two incoming meridians

A=-[[1,1],[0,1]], B=-[[1,0],[-4,1]],
P=BA, H=P+I, C=I+bH,
Aout=C A C^-1, Bout=C B C^-1.

The proved physical bend has tuple (Aout,Bout,B^-1,A^-1). Its target pairs the middle objects. To write the target as an ordinary two-head operator one applies the INVERSE of the original pivotal turn R. Its based tuple, up to one common conjugacy, is

(Bout,B^-1,A^-1,Aout),
(Ain_target,Bin_target)=(Aout^-1,A),
(Aout_target,Bout_target)=(Bout,B^-1).             (1)

Here is a direct basepath check of the cyclic order. If L_i are the four original local lower Borel factors, their total product is I for the invariant tensor. Rotating their local order from (1,2,3,4) to (2,3,4,1) rotates their four based meridians and conjugates all of them by L_1. Thus it has precisely the common-conjugacy effect just stated. The finite pivotal clock in R is still present in the operator identity; it gives no extra leading magnetic-momentum factor, since its ratio under one physical index shift is q^-2 -> 1. Its actual constant phase is NOT removed from the finite source.

The target ordered product is

T=B^-1 Bout=A Aout^-1,
Tr T=2+16b^2.                                    (2)

Set alpha=8b^2-4b+1. Direct calculation gives the genuine target conjugator

D=T-alpha I,
D A D^-1=B^-1,
D Aout^-1 D^-1=Bout,
[D,T]=0, det D=-64b^4.                           (3)

In particular (3) retains WHICH ordered meridian is sent to which; it is stronger than matching the target trace alone.

2. The physical rectangle determines its eigenratio

For the certified complete geometry the actual primitive marking gives b=-i/(2c), independently of the retained half-twist sign. Suppose first 0<c<=1, and write

c=sech eta, eta>=0,
Tr T=-2cosh(2eta),
J=-exp(-2eta)=exp(2pi i xi),
xi=1/2+i eta/pi.                                 (4)

This specifies the target eigenflag. On the two T-eigenlines the conjugator D has eigenvalues J-alpha and J^-1-alpha. Its selected eigenratio is therefore

R_target=(J-alpha)/(J^-1-alpha)
   =(1+i sinh eta)/(1-i sinh eta)
   =exp(2i atan(sinh eta)).                      (5)

The opposite eigenflag inverts (5). Both (3) and the chosen expression in (5) extend through eta=0. In the normalization that gives eigenvalue one on the other flag, the parabolic limit is

D_normalized=I+(i/2)(T+I) at eta=0.              (6)

This coefficient is a target transport parameter; it is not an independently introduced crossing-circle cusp shape. In particular there is no reason to assign it the orientation inequality of an original auxiliary cusp.

3. The actual scalar source fixes the generating derivative

Let a_N/N->1/2 in the original folded range. The root-location theorem proves local uniform convergence of the actual canonical logarithms

N^-1 Log p_(a_N)(z) -> Psi_(1/2)(z)
             on C minus [2,infinity).

Define the scalar action along the actual target trace by

F(eta)=Psi_(1/2)(-2cosh(2eta)).                   (7)

For eta real and nonnegative, the positive finite-product proof gives

F(eta)=max_(0<=v<=1/2)
   integral_0^v log[cos^2(pi s)
           *(cosh^2 eta-sin^2(pi s))/sin^4(pi s)] ds.

Its unique maximum is v=atan(cosh eta)/pi, the actual rectangle parameter. Differentiation at the maximum gives an elementary exact formula. In c=sech eta coordinates,

dF/dc=-2/c * integral_0^(atan(1/c)/pi)
                                  ds/(1-c^2 sin^2(pi s))
      =-2 arccos c/(pi c sqrt(1-c^2)).

Using dc/deta=-c tanh eta proves

F'(eta)=2 atan(sinh eta)/pi.                    (8)

The limit at eta=0 is zero. Its additive constant is fixed by the ACTUAL positive source evaluation at z=-2:

F(0)=4 integral_0^(1/4) log cot(pi s) ds
    =4 Catalan/pi=v8/pi.                        (9)

Thus no arbitrary period constant or logarithm winding was selected:

F(eta)=v8/pi+(2/pi) integral_0^eta atan(sinh r) dr. (10)

These formulas hold holomorphically on |Im eta|<pi/2, with the branch continued from eta=0. Indeed -2cosh(2eta) avoids [2,infinity) on that strip. The analytic arctangent in (8) is its continuation from zero; its derivative is 1/cosh eta, with no pole inside the strip. The identity theorem extends the real calculation. One must not substitute a pointwise principal inverse trigonometric value after leaving the real axis.

Because xi=1/2+i eta/pi, equation (8) is precisely

partial_xi Psi_(1/2)(J+J^-1)
                   =-2i atan(sinh eta),
exp[-partial_xi Psi_(1/2)(J+J^-1)]=R_target.       (11)

The sign in (11) is the actual spectral-multiplier sign. Let Theta be the original fully normalized algebraic two-column generating phase. We use it here as the already proved classical character relation, not as a new asymptotic for a column at the complex target color. Its algebraic critical-character map says exp(partial_xi Theta)=R_target. Multiplication by the scalar source polynomial adds Psi_(1/2)(J+J^-1) to that phase. Target-channel stationarity is therefore

exp(partial_xi[Theta+Psi])=1,

exactly (11). It is NOT R_target=exp(partial_xi Psi). This calculation uses the original inverse turn (1), the physical ordered matrices (3), and the canonical scalar branch (7).

4. General input color gives the actual four-parabolic character relation

The same comparison holds for EVERY fixed real INPUT parameter 0<alpha<=1/2, not only the complete input alpha=1/2. Keep it distinct from the TARGET parameter xi=1/2+i eta/pi. Put

lambda_input=2cos(2pi alpha), s=sin(pi alpha),
z=J+J^-1=-2cosh(2eta),
F_alpha(eta)=Psi_alpha(z).

Here Psi_alpha is again the actual selected scalar limit proved by the finite polynomial root theorem. For eta>0 its positive variational formula has unique maximum satisfying

sin^2(pi v_*)=s^2 cosh^2 eta/(s^2+cosh^2 eta).

Differentiate its original integral at that maximum. With t=tan(pi v), the integral of 1/(cosh^2 eta-sin^2(pi v)) is elementary. It gives

partial_eta F_alpha
  =(2/pi) atan[s sinh eta/sqrt(1+(1-s^2)sinh^2 eta)]
  =(2/pi) asin(s tanh eta).                       (12)

Both inverse functions in (12) are continued from zero; for positive real eta their values lie in (0,pi alpha). For example the equivalence follows by squaring the tangent and using 1+sinh^2 eta=cosh^2 eta. Thus the selected scalar multiplier gives the eigenratio

R_alpha=exp[-partial_xi Psi_alpha(J+J^-1)]
       =exp[2i asin(s tanh eta)],
R_alpha+R_alpha^-1
    =2-(2-lambda_input)(z+2)/(z-2).              (13)

This is precisely the ACTUAL four-parabolic trace equation in the inverse-turn convention (1), as can be proved without importing a character-variety formula. Diagonalize the target product T=diag(J,J^-1) and write its second incoming meridian as

B_t=[[b11,b12],[b21,b22]],
Tr B_t=Tr(B_t^-1 T)=-2, det B_t=1.

The two trace equations give

b11=-2J/(J+1), b22=-2/(J+1),
b12 b21=-(J-1)^2/(J+1)^2.                       (14)

For a target centralizer with eigenratio R, take D=diag(R,1). The original input product is, BY THE ACTUAL INVERSE TURN, (D B_t D^-1)^-1 B_t. Therefore

lambda_input=Tr(D B_t^-1 D^-1 B_t)
   =2-[(J-1)^2/(J+1)^2](2-R-R^-1).             (15)

Since (J-1)^2/(J+1)^2=(z-2)/(z+2), equations (13) and (15) are identical. This proves the generating relation for general input trace, not just the number of its roots. At alpha=1/2 the explicit physical collar computation (3)–(5) fixes WHICH of the two inverse eigenratio branches is the marked one. Continuing that branch in alpha gives (13); no new arbitrary sign is selected at each input value.

The expressions extend on |Im eta|<pi/2 using the same scalar slit branch. The parabolic target eta=0 has the finite normalized limit

D_normalized=I+(i s/2)(T+I).

Its source trace is 2+16(i s/2)^2=lambda_input, as it must be. This is a classical parabolic limit, not a substitute for an actual generic-q normal. For the analytic rectangle parameter c>1 one may take eta=i arccos(1/c) inside the same strip; the continued marking agrees with the real physical target in the preceding theorem. This statement does not assert that any finite real-color contour can be moved there without poles or without additional contributions.

The additive value of each general-input scalar action is already fixed by its actual positive finite-source limit at any z<-2, followed by the slit-domain continuation. No additive period is inferred from (13) alone. Equation (9) is the explicit complete-input anchor.

5. What has and has not been compared

The target trace, the ordered target conjugator, its selected eigenratio, and the entire scalar generating derivative now agree with the actual recoupled geometry. The scalar action has its finite-source anchor (9). The original generic identity remains D_a^-1 p_a(Omega) before every normal extraction; at root D_a=1, and its fixed scalar generic jets have the already proved polynomial relative bound on compact real targets below -2. No generic normal is replaced by a root derivative.

Equation (11) is a comparison at the actual classical critical point. The algebraic ordinary-column character map is valid on its regular complex channel chart, but the existing ordinary-column ASYMPTOTIC theorem keeps that channel real and discrete. This note does not extend that theorem to (4). Nor does scalar convergence in (7) establish the action of the degree-O(N) polynomial p_a(Omega) on the complementary selected boundary state. Establishing that action, its actual phase, and its contribution to the original finite source remains necessary.

In particular (9) is a scalar anchor, not a new proof that an assembled marked critical action equals the complement volume. Any such assembly must also retain the pivotal clock, original row and half-twist factors, native measures, shared wires, and the full physical normal already specified by the exact source identities.
