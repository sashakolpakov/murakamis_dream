2. Four literal states

Equations (2) are the polynomial transfer (BrRrRr−1Zr)=Tr(Br−1Rr−1Rr−2Zr−1),Tr=(bαr0001βrAγrP001001βrAγrPcαr). \begin{pmatrix}B_r\\R_r\\R_{r-1}\\Z_r\end{pmatrix} =T_r\begin{pmatrix}B_{r-1}\\R_{r-1}\\R_{r-2}\\Z_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}, \qquad T_r=\begin{pmatrix} b\alpha_r&0&0&0\\ 1&\beta_rA&\gamma_rP&0\\ 0&1&0&0\\ 1&\beta_rA&\gamma_rP&c\alpha_r \end{pmatrix}. \tag{4} The initial vector is (1,0,0,0)T(1,0,0,0)^T. The direct before-to-after entry 11 accounts for starting and ending at the same row. There is no denominator at the initial rows or a sampled spectral zero.

The diagonal blocks are the before scalar bαrb\alpha_r, the original active companion Cr=(βrAγrP10), C_r=\begin{pmatrix}\beta_rA&\gamma_rP\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, and the after scalar cαrc\alpha_r. Reversing the block order makes (4) upper triangular. On a strict compact row and spectral chart its active eigenvalues have limits Λ±(t)=2s[As±s2(x−y)2+4xycos⁡2(πδ)],s=sin⁡(πt). \Lambda_\pm(t)=2s\left[As\pm \sqrt{s^2(x-y)^2+4xy\cos^2(\pi\delta)}\right], \qquad s=\sin(\pi t). \tag{5} For positive x,yx,y, 0<t<1/20<t<1/2, and 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/2, the positive root is separated from the other root. The latter can vanish at the pole and must not be inverted. On the post-pole side α(t)<0\alpha(t)<0, the after scalar is negative for positive cc, so it is separated from Λ+\Lambda_+ even where their moduli coincide. The phase change c↦−cc\mapsto-c can restore resonance.

This identifies a possible input to the analytic switching theorem. It alone does not prove suppression where a genuine eigenvalue crossing remains, and separation from the secondary eigenvalue must also be checked.
