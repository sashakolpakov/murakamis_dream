The meridian profile and volume for Conway knots with long twists

1 November 2026.

This proof concerns the actual reduced ADO invariant of a filled knot, with its zero framing and its prescribed meridian coordinate. The meridian moves by an amount of order 1/N1/N, while the quantum parameter remains at the exact 2N2N-th root of unity. For the class of presentations described below, the invariant has a nonzero asymptotic on every fixed microscopic disk before the first ordinary-color resonance. Its logarithmic growth is the volume of the resulting knot complement.

The proof keeps the whole finite quantum sum. It obtains a nonzero leading term by treating the Fourier variables for all filled circles together. The geometric input is the original strict augmented geometry, its marked deformation and its smooth long fillings. The resulting volume is the volume of the knot complement after the auxiliary crossing circles have been filled; the knot cusp itself remains unfilled.

1. The presentations and the theorem

Start with one fixed literal closed Conway construction in ordered products, physical quarter-turns and the original crossing-circle leaves. Its endpoint orders, knot pattern and local disk framings are fixed. Assume its positive wire domain has a strict state: original leaf parameters lie in (0,1/2)(0,1/2), and each retained multiplication gate satisfies max⁡(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \max(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \tag{1} Only the forced final flat gate is deleted, with its exact complementary-index relation retained. The original geometric construction then gives a complete augmented exterior with a unique strict positive source optimizer. We retain the original knot/component qualifications; in particular the strict two-leaf knot case includes a pure leaf, rather than the two-odd-leaf link pattern.

There is a useful graph description of the additional condition in this theorem. Collapse consecutive multiplications with no intervening turn into a product junction. 8. What this theorem does and does not extend

The earlier complete-value volume theorem covers every strict literal Conway tree under its stated knot and long-filling hypotheses. The present theorem gives more information—an actual complex microscopic main-meridian profile and a uniform volume rate—but for the narrower class specified by the bare-component condition (2). It does not replace the broader complete-value theorem or reduce its scope.

The disk restriction is essential. The present proof treats |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1; it does not cross the first ordinary-color resonances s=±1s=\pm1, where the reduced ordinary-color anchor and the full modified dimension have different behavior. The removable same-color Casimir collisions within this disk are included by the full physical source. A zero-free microscopic disk is also not a fixed open disk in the MM-plane.

No theorem for arbitrary adjacent bare components failing (2) is obtained. No filling integer is allowed to grow with NN. No conclusion is drawn for the ordinary-color-subtracted regular remainder through its resonance divisors. Finally, the existence of an exact meridian annihilator and the pointed complete period is separate from identifying a selected classical main-meridian AA-factor. The scalar profile and volume theorem here do not make that identification.
