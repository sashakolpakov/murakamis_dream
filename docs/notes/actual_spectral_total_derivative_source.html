An exact spectral total derivative for the original root source

, 31 October 2026. This is an exact fixed-root identity for the original pair Casimir, retaining its simple and repeated spectral nodes. No contour deformation or uniform exponential estimate is asserted.

Put N>=3,d=N-1,q=exp(pi i/N). Let chi(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2, and let Omega be the ACTUAL pair Casimir from the original source theorem. Define its polynomial resolvent numerator

V(z)=chi(z)(z-Omega)^(-1).

This is the unique degree-(N-1) matrix polynomial supplied by polynomial division and chi(Omega)=0. It is not the determinant adjugate of the full N^2-dimensional matrix. At a paired c=2cos(phi), its resolvent is

(z-Omega)^(-1)=P/(z-c)+Q/(z-c)^2+regular.

Write h(z)=chi(z)/(z-c)^2 locally. The Chebyshev ODE gives

h(c)=N^2/(4-c^2), h'(c)/h(c)=c/(4-c^2).

Consequently Q=V(c)/h(c) and P=V’(c)/h(c)-h’(c)V(c)/h(c)^2. With Qhat(z)=(4-z2)V(z)/N2 this is

Q=Qhat(c), P=Qhat'(c)+c Qhat(c)/(4-c^2).

For any function f holomorphic at the spectral nodes, therefore,

f(c)P+f'(c)Q
  =[(4-c^2)(fV)'(c)-c f(c)V(c)]/N^2
  =-d_phi[2sin(phi) f(2cos(phi))V(2cos(phi))/N^2].    (1)

When N is odd, the simple node is c=-2, chi’(-2)=N^2, and the right side of (1) at phi=pi is 2f(-2)P_*. Thus the original simple term has exactly one half of that derivative. Each paired node occurs twice in the full odd-angle grid and the derivatives are equal there. The exact original operator is accordingly

f(Omega)=-1/2 sum_(k=0)^(N-1) B_f'(phi_k),
B_f(phi)=2sin(phi) f(2cos(phi))V(2cos(phi))/N^2,
phi_k=(2k+1)pi/N.                                   (2)

No cyclic physical head is introduced: the cyclic variable in (2) is the spectral parameter, with the original projector multiplicities.

For R^n use its exact full-Hermite Gaussian

f(2cos(phi))=q^(n d^2/4)
   exp[-n(phi-pi)^2/(2hbar)], hbar=2pi i/N,
0<=phi<=2pi.

On this interval B_f has equal zero endpoint values and equal first derivatives. Its second derivatives can differ at the endpoints, reflecting the K-cut at z=2. Thus its periodic extension is C^1, but endpoint currents must be retained in further integration by parts. In particular the zero Fourier integral of -B_f’ is exactly zero.

With Fourier convention hat B(j)=(2pi)(-1)int_0(2pi) B(phi)e^(-ijphi)dphi, the finite midpoint formula gives the convergent fixed-N identity

f(Omega)=-(iN^2/2)sum_(ell in Z) ell (-1)^ell hat B(Nell). (3)

The ell=0 term is zero. For each fixed N,n the sum is absolutely convergent: periodic B is C^1 with piecewise smooth higher derivatives, so hat B(j)=O(j^-3). Constants here may depend exponentially on N; this observation gives no uniform global bound. Formula (3) is an alternative exact source period, closely related to the existing Fourier-Hermite Gaussian formulas. It does not erase their endpoint currents or determine which nonzero charge contributes after sewing.

Why complete-channel samples do not control the derivative

At a paired node, L_N(c)=0 and L_N’(c)=(-1)(N-1)N/(2sin2(phi)). Hence its complete sector equals

L_N'(c)Q=(-1)^(N-1)2V(c)/N.

The complete positive packet therefore controls V(c), whereas V’(c) also carries the independent semisimple projector P. The simplest illustration is a diagonal coefficient on the face boundary x=y=0: Omega is diagonal there, so at a paired eigenvalue c its entry V(z)=chi(z)/(z-c) vanishes at z=c, but V’(c)=chi’’(c)/2 is nonzero. At even N every spectral node is paired, so all corresponding complete diagonal samples vanish while the semisimple derivatives reconstruct the identity. This example concerns the ACTUAL local polynomial specialization, not a freely chosen off-grid interpolation.

Thus the proved exponential interpolation of complete packet values cannot simply be differentiated to estimate (2) or (3). A bound on the original polynomial resolvent numerator between nodes, including its semisimple derivative and the retained endpoint charges, is an additional input. No claim that such a bound is impossible is made.

Exact link to the retained physical colors

Polynomial division of the Chebyshev identity gives, in the SAME original Casimir quotient,

V(z)=U_(N-1)(Omega/2)
       +2sum_(h=1)^(N-1)T_(N-h)(z/2)U_(h-1)(Omega/2)
    =(-1)^d L_N
       +2sum_(h=1)^(N-1)(-1)^(h-1)T_(N-h)(z/2)L_h.   (4)

One proof starts from

[T_N(x)-T_N(y)]/(x-y)=U_(N-1)(y)
  +2sum_(h=1)^(N-1)T_(N-h)(x)U_(h-1)(y),

then substitutes x=z/2,y=Omega/2 and T_N(Omega/2)=-1. The scalar polynomial identity also follows immediately by induction in N using the two Chebyshev recurrences. It was checked exactly for N=2,…,14 before recording it here.

The homogeneous source and every actual exterior functional preserve (4) by linearity. Thus controlling the original off-node numerator means controlling a SPECIFIED coherent combination of all physical colors, with their signs. It is not determined by the complete packet alone. On the other hand a bound on this coherent combination could be sharper than bounds on its separate colors, so (4) is not a no-go statement for a phase-preserving resolvent method.

Validation and scope

The focused audit uses the original coproduct Casimir and independently built signed R matrices. It checks all monomials through degree N-1 and powers n=+/-1,…,+/-5 at N=3,…,8, including both parity conventions. The divided-Chebyshev identity is also checked symbolically at N=2,…,14. Results record finite identities; they assert no uniform Fourier constants.

The theorem is at a fixed root of unity. It does not establish a uniform Fourier estimate or select a contributing cycle.
