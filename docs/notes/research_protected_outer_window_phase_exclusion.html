Regular-window phase exclusion with one protected outer face

31 October 2026. research local theorem. This is the exact scalar-active extension of the regular-window phase theorem. It treats y=0y=0 and x,b,c≠0x,b,c\ne0, with their moduli uniformly positive. It does not cover a zero middle weight or two zero outer weights. Its conclusion is relative to the original positive window envelope, not a comparison with hyperbolic volume.

4. Scalar separation and both boundary terms

If (8) fails by a fixed amount, at least one supported middle phase mismatches ϑx\vartheta_x by a fixed amount. On its window, ara_r and the relevant middle scalar have strictly positive moduli bounded above and below. For two such moduli u,v≥m*>0u,v\ge m_*>0, |ueiϕ−veiψ|≥2m*|sinϕ−ψ2|. |ue^{i\phi}-ve^{i\psi}| \ge2m_*\left|\sin\frac{\phi-\psi}{2}\right|. \tag{12} The exact coefficients are analytic in the scaled row variable, with αh(t)=4cos⁡2(π(t+h/2))−4sin⁡2(πδ),a(t)=4xsin⁡2(πt),h=N−1. \alpha_h(t)=4\cos^2(\pi(t+h/2))-4\sin^2(\pi\delta), \qquad a(t)=4x\sin^2(\pi t),\qquad h=N^{-1}. The scalar gap (12) therefore extends to a fixed complex neighborhood of the selected window, uniformly for large NN. This is the full Sylvester hypothesis in the analytic switching theorem. No secondary eigenvalue, positive two-state gauge or outer-relative-phase estimate is involved.

Apply that theorem to the mismatched matrix in (10). Keep both pure endpoint terms and the residual switch sum. The residual coupling is O(e−cN)O(e^{-cN}); it remains multiplied by the original scalar products. Here those active products concatenate exactly: Kv,w(x,0)Ku,v(x,0)=Ku,w(x,0). K_{v,w}(x,0)K_{u,v}(x,0)=K_{u,w}(x,0). \tag{13} Their absolute values are exactly the corresponding positive products.

To compare with ENE_N, first factor out all common unused products outside the two windows. These may include sampled zeros in the full-row case, and the omitted-half product may also vanish. If a common factor vanishes, both sides of (9) vanish. Otherwise the same estimate applies to the remaining variable products, with no bound on an inverse common factor. The variable α\alpha’s stay away from zero, while β\beta stays positive on the active compact interval. Their logarithmic Riemann sums give uniform rate differences equal to the differences of (7), with O(log⁡N)O(\log N) errors.

Each gauge endpoint term places the relevant start or stop at a window boundary, up to one mesh step. Strict endpoint maximality and separation of the two endpoint actions give a fixed exponential loss relative to the positive interior window sum. Both endpoint terms are retained. For the residual, (13) reassembles the active products exactly; the one-row factors and their reciprocals on the selected windows are uniformly bounded. Each insertion is therefore bounded by its e−cNe^{-cN} residual factor times a constant multiple of an original positive switch summand, up to one mesh shift. Summing polynomially many locations costs only a polynomial factor. Absorb it in a smaller exponential constant and restore all common factors. This proves (9).

5. Protected targets and the shared phase graph

In the original marked coefficient the protected face has target zero. All polynomial exponents are nonnegative, so specializing that variable to zero is exactly the required constant-term restriction. No phase or Cauchy integration variable is supplied for that face.

Consequently these ports may be combined with the four-positive ports in the cycle-packing criterion. Use just the three supported incidences (x,b,c)(x,b,c), with labels (0,sμ/π,sν/π)(0,s_\mu/\pi,s_\nu/\pi). The local loss and the unconditional positive-envelope bound have exactly the forms needed there. The circular telescoping, fractional packing and original shared-target Cauchy arguments are unchanged.

The case x=0x=0, y,b,c≠0y,b,c\ne0, follows by exchanging the two outer variables in the literal recurrence. Neither case permits a forced zero middle, two zero outers, a boundary maximum or a window touching a pole. No universal packing capacity, envelope margin or filled-volume theorem is inferred from this extension.

The finite dictionary is an algebraic specialization of the already four-state and restricted-window identities, whose existing audits include a zero outer weight. The exponential estimate above is analytical; no additional finite-level test is claimed as its proof.
