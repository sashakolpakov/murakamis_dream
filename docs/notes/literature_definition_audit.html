Literature definition, independent audit, and equivalence question

Current scope, 25 October 2026:

The target is Kashaev’s original invariant. The AK definitions audited below are comparison objects. The subsequent direct crossing audit and ADO pointing calculation now specify the original enhanced state and its literature all-level annihilator ideal, with independent matrix, pairing and normalization proofs. No AJ or all-level holonomicity theorem is assumed merely from accepting that ideal’s definition.

  • Use a quantum object defined in the existing literature, with its prescribed state, operator conventions, normalization and parameter regime.
  • Do not assume the correctness of the published proofs. Reconstruct and check the identities and analytic arguments actually needed.
  • Preserve the earlier custom constructions as comparison candidates. They become results about the chosen literature object only after an equivalence has been established; a common classical A-curve or volume rate is insufficient.
  • Retain peripheral variables and allowed boundary factors before specializing to the complete ideal point. A numerical multiplier in a specialized 2–3 comparison is not by itself an obstruction to the appropriately normalized move identity.

1. An existing definition to audit

Andersen–Malusa, A Geometric Quantisation view on the AJ-conjecture for the Teichmueller TQFT, arXiv:1711.11522v3 (30 April 2024), Section 5, Definition 5, printed page 21:

https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.11522v3

The object is the specified Andersen–Kashaev state J^(b,N)_(M,K), and its annihilating left ideal in the q-difference algebra. The paper singles out a primitive operator of minimal shift degree when the relevant ideal is nonzero. The state and its quantum operators are described in Sections 2.1–2.3 and 5. This is not an instruction to choose a convenient solution of a naively ordered classical A-polynomial.

For general knots the same source treats existence/nontriviality and the classical-symbol identification as conjectural. Those are proof obligations here, not consequences of accepting the definition. The purported statements for 4_1 and 5_2 likewise remain to be independently checked.

The source and original PDF for the underlying construction are Andersen–Kashaev, A TQFT from quantum Teichmueller theory: https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.6295 . Fix a source version before transferring formulas.

2. Necessary checks

  1. Write the exact published state integral, quantum dilogarithm, Gaussian, Haar/spectral measure, contour and peripheral multiplication/shift operators.
  2. Verify the local functional equations and quantum commutators directly.
  3. Verify noncommutative elimination using explicit certificates.
  4. Check the passage from integrand equations to state equations: contour changes, residues, endpoint terms and parameter domains must be accounted for.
  5. Establish nonzero state, minimality and the classical limit with all removable or nonremovable factors recorded.
  6. For volume, establish the appropriate asymptotic for that exact normalized state. Existing claims about contour deformation, saddle selection and uniform error estimates are not assumed.

The earlier computations in quantum_a_volume.md do not discharge these checks for the literature state.

3. How to test equivalence with the custom candidate

Match variables first: in the classical symbol the literature Q corresponds to m^2, so its q-commutation parameter must be compared with the square of the parameter for a shift acting on m itself. Include orientations, Fourier or Gaussian conjugations and peripheral/framing changes explicitly.

Then compare operators at generic q and generic peripheral variables, allowing the units or boundary factors permitted in the stated construction. For a second-order equation with coefficients a_0(X),a_1(X),a_2(X), EX=qXE,

I(X)=a_0(qX)a_2(X)/(a_1(qX)a_1(X))

is invariant under multiplying the equation by a scalar function and under a scalar gauge f(X)=g(X)u(X). It is consequently a useful test for the simplest possible equivalence, including right multiplication by scalar units. It does not test equivalence by a general matrix gauge or change of polarization.

Finally relate the states themselves. An operator equivalence alone does not identify a selected integration cycle, a particular solution, or an exponentially significant normalization.

4. Peripheral factors and the factor-eight observation

The local angle-factorial test evaluated two specialized amplitudes, 1/27 and 1/216 at N=6. Its ratio 8 establishes only failure of strict equality of those bare amplitudes.

A factor such as (1+M^a) is a unit near M=1 and specializes to 2. Products of such factors can produce 8. This illustrates why the numerical observation cannot decide the correct move law; it does not identify its missing factor.

For each move, the calculation should retain its boundary variables and determine whether the multiplier is an allowed unit, a change of boundary trivialization, a projective scalar, a quantum-dilogarithmic factor, or a genuine anomaly. Its behavior under composition of moves must then be checked.

Factors vanishing at the ideal point require separate limit analysis. Generic localization permits division by them, while the analytic germ at M=1 may not. That distinction is already visible in right factors involving Q-1 in the literature operator comparison.

5. Current proof status

The literature definitions are starting data. No equivalence between the custom cone-normalized states and the literature state is established. No failure of topological invariance modulo allowed boundary factors has been proved by the factor-eight test. Neither publication nor matching numerical volume asymptotics is being used as a substitute for an audited proof.

An initial exact algebraic check is reproducible with python3 experiments/compare_literature_quantum_a.py. It compares the custom figure-eight operator with the explicit candidate q P’_q obtained from equation (13) of arXiv v3. Their scalar-gauge invariants differ for generic q, although their classical invariants agree. Thus a mere scalar normalization in the same peripheral coordinates cannot identify these two operators. This does not exclude a more general intertwiner or change of polarization, and it does not assume that the candidate is the minimal annihilator of the literature state.

The same calculation finds a discrepancy among the formulas as printed in arXiv v3: right-multiplying q P’_q by Q-1 agrees with equation (5) in the constant and quadratic shift coefficients, but its linear shift coefficient has an additional factor qQ-1. Inserting that factor into the printed equation (5) makes the asserted right-factor identity exact. This was checked against the original v3 TeX source, not inferred from PDF text extraction. The classical prefactor stated in Theorem 17 also needs reconciliation with the explicit specialization in the proof of Theorem 19. These may be typographical errors; they do not by themselves disprove the underlying construction. The journal version and any corrections must be checked before attributing the discrepancies to the final published article.

6. Subsequent independent proofs and exact comparisons

The full derivations are in literature_state_volume.md and custom_literature_equivalence.md.

  1. The Faddeev defining integral gives a uniform complex O(b^2) logarithmic remainder on every smaller strip |Im z|<=pi-delta, for all Re z. The proof explicitly handles the upper indentation and normalization constant.

  2. On a contour through the regular ideal saddle, the exact literature figure-eight state satisfies

    J_b(0)=3^(-1/4) exp(-Vol(4_1)/(2 pi b^2))(1+o(1)).

    The contour has a fixed strip gap; the manuscript’s compact-fold pole accumulation is not being passed through.

  3. The v3 Ore certificate for equation (13) is exactly correct. It was expanded in the noncommutative four-generator algebra, separately from the printed equation (5) discrepancy above. The integrand equations were recovered from the defining integral, and the finite contour shifts justified near the semiclassical parameter. A local uniform saddle expansion and the nonsquare classical discriminant rule out a rational first-order annihilator. Thus the explicit second-order operator is minimal for this level-one state on its specified analytic branch. No general-level-N theorem is inferred.

  4. The same remainder estimate and a negative definite real Hessian prove a volume law for the published FAMED state integral (4.5), given positive complete shapes and the finite face, edge and peripheral matrix certificates. This does not prove a quantum annihilator exists for every member of that class, nor verify blanket knot-family or census membership claims.

  5. Our compact Psi_q is an exact factor of Phi_b after its second, dual compact product is included. Functional equations and the left-end normalization independently prove this equality in the convergent complex-b regime.

  6. The two figure-eight companion systems are inequivalent even under rational two-by-two gauges in the same peripheral coordinates. The proof uses finite pole propagation and formal degrees at zero and infinity; a scalar invariant alone would not suffice. Changes of polarization and nonrational transforms remain separate questions.

The exact generic-q factors are retained throughout. In particular the minimal operator’s classical specialization contains (Q^2-1) times the geometric quadratic; those factors cannot be canceled at Q=1 without a limit analysis. The bare factor-eight observation about the different positive factorial model continues to have only the limited interpretation in Section 4 above.

There is a further scalar-normalization discrepancy to reconcile in the displayed change of variables preceding equation (4.5) of the FAMED preprint: the stated Jacobian is p_1 but the displayed fiber prefactor is p_1 rather than 1/p_1. The broad volume proof explicitly fixes the printed integral; the difference is a recorded nonzero constant, not an assumed exact equality of differently normalized states. This discrepancy was also checked in the original v2 TeX source, lines 3277–3292. The N=1 dilogarithm convention was separately checked from the finite-product definition (122) of Andersen–Kashaev, arXiv:1409.1208.

7. General existence and the explicit twist families

general_annihilator_and_volume.md now proves a nonzero annihilator exists for the rescaled geometric FAMED integrals in the Andersen–Malusà b-shift algebra. Exact finite products give a common denominator with linear degree growth. A dimension count eliminates the internal multiplication variables. If the resulting operator vanishes when the internal shifts are set to 1, exponential moments extract its first nonzero Taylor coefficient. A finite restriction-of-scalars argument returns to the prescribed peripheral field. The operator of least degree then exists by skew-polynomial division.

The same exact state has a locally uniform, nonzero geometric saddle asymptotic. Consequently the classical symbol contains the geometric peripheral component with explicitly recorded PSL/SL lift, inverse and factor-of-two conventions. No claim that all other classical components have been removed is made.

There are two finite-b conventions that must not be identified: the natural Andersen–Malusà algebra uses q_b=exp(2pi i b^2) and a spectral shift -i b; FAMED Section 4.4 uses q_F=exp(4pi i hbar) and scaled shift +4pi i hbar. The existence theorem here is for the former action on the rescaled published integral. It does not establish the latter’s conditional AJ hypothesis. Moreover the source’s displayed WKB substitution for its positive shift gives exp(-H_l) from its stated potential derivative, so its inverse-longitude convention requires reconciliation before use.

For 4_1 the exact conversion of the two literature state normalizations is

J_F(hbar,2pi sqrt(hbar)t)
   = zeta_b/(2pi sqrt(hbar)) exp(pi(b+b^(-1))t) J_AM,b(t).

This boundary factor changes the classical longitude root’s sign. It is retained at generic peripheral value, and only then evaluated at t=0. The factor is exponentially significant at a fixed scaled boundary value.

twist_family_audit.md reconstructs the two explicit infinite face families, their face Gaussian, the edge/face matrix identity, and positive complete geometric angle maxima by formulas valid for every fixed twist parameter. It includes a multiplier/horosphere proof that an interior angle maximum produces a complete metric. The exact finite checks rebuild edge incidences and the matrices from the face pairings for p=0,…,8 in both parities. They support the all-p formulas; they are not used as a substitute for them.

A further generic-boundary discrepancy was found in arXiv:1903.09480v5: the even-family theorem prints x(x-y_U-y_W), while the derivation at TeX lines 8791–8802 gives x(y_U-y_W-x). The direct face-matrix substitution agrees with the latter. Both expressions agree at x=0. The exact state fixed for this audit is the later FAMED integral, and the final journal version of the older twist paper has not been checked for the discrepancy.

The subsequent twist_topology_and_peripherals.md reconstructs the knot-diagram identification and independently identifies the peripheral curves by constant-size normal-curve certificates. Its meridian separates the marked-edge germs before drilling; its primitive longitude is an integral boundary in the complement, and its angular branch correction is zero. Exact diagnostics and optional Regina recognition check eighteen examples against those uniform formulas.

Global exact state transformations and the extension to every alternating knot remain unfinished. The active goal is not marked complete on the basis of the state-integral criterion or the twist-family theorem for specified literature realizations.

8. Independent positive local move and a fiber-normalization equivalence

faddeev_pentagon_audit.md derives the Faddeev Fourier formula from both shift equations, fixes its constant using Fourier inversion and a Fresnel asymptotic, and obtains the generic scalar Ramanujan integral from two shift identities and its residue at zero. Pole cancellation in the contour shifts is explicit. These give the bounded operator pentagon by an integrable spectral regularization.

For the charged operators defined in AK (18), the resulting exact identity is

T_12(a_4,c_4)T_13(a_2,c_2)T_23(a_0,c_0)
   =exp(pi i c_b^2 P/3)T_23(a_1,c_1)T_12(a_3,c_3),
P=2(c_0+a_2+c_4)-1/2,

under the full positive charge relations recorded in that note. It holds with all face boundary variables unspecialized. The charged proof is a contour translation of the scalar uncharged kernel identity; it does not treat the separately unbounded exponential charge factors as bounded. The phase and all boundary-linear terms are also checked symbolically.

The same note identifies the exact factor J_coarea=J_printed/p_1^2 for the FAMED fiber Jacobian convention. It is a nonzero constant normalization, so these two states have the same annihilator and volume rate. This does not silently replace the printed state: its factor is recorded throughout.

The local identity can be contracted with a convergent exterior kernel or Schwartz boundary states. An explicit invertible face matrix also makes the full positive tensor contraction absolutely convergent; this supplies the gluing justification for embedded replacements of the audited operator pentagon pattern when both full face matrices are invertible. General gluing, movement outside the positive chamber, the level/framing convention, and the exact polarized fiber-state covariance still need their own checks. No generic multiplier for the different positive factorial comparison model is inferred from it.

9. Exact peripheral phase and state recovery

peripheral_phase_and_state.md upgrades the magnitude relationship to the complex identity

Z_AK(X,alpha)=sigma_X exp(pi i N/12+i K_X(alpha)/hbar)
     integral_(R+i mu) (J_printed(hbar,x)/p_1^2)
                               exp(lambda x/(4pi hbar)) dx,
sigma_X=sign(det(script-A)p_1).

It derives K_X from the charged kernel and defining inversion identity, including the imaginary lift in the boundary coordinate. Choosing the published AK level ell=-4K_X/pi removes exactly that angle phase. The fiber state is then uniquely recovered by analytic continuation and Fourier–Laplace injectivity with the proved exponential tail bounds.

On balanced angles K_X is affine apart from the universal term -lambda mu/(8pi). Compatible positive moves cancel this quadratic term; the remaining scalar transform law is eta exp(i(d_0+d_1 lambda)/hbar). A zero-slope certificate identifies the same state up to its recorded scalar. A nonzero slope requires an explicit meridian translation and its contour check. The slope is not silently set to zero.

The generic mixed-sign phase identity is symbolic. Eighteen twist angle spaces were checked in their entirety, and pointwise quantum checks using the independently derived real-b remainder agree within 9e-15. The raw phase varies even at fixed peripheral angles in all eighteen examples. Actual move sequences, other vertex-order patterns, and the all-alternating geometric extension remain unfinished.

10. A calibrated connecting move for the figure-eight state

tetrahedral_symmetry_and_figure_eight.md derives all three adjacent vertex transposition identities from the audited Fourier formula. The published Gaussian triangle kernels supply an exact S_3 action: their cyclic product has cube scalar exp(3pi i/12-pi i/4)=1. Gaussian regularization justifies their cancellation in the proper contractions used here.

The note reconstructs the actual 3–2 bipyramid in the p=0 even twist triangulation. Its canonical signs are all negative, so the adjoint pentagon is required. It transports the full positive angle space and the preferred peripheral rows, which reverse both meridian and longitude. The affine phase certificate has slope zero and constant 11pi/12.

An explicit orientation-preserving automorphism of the resulting two-tetrahedron triangulation also reverses both peripheral curves and has zero phase difference. Transform uniqueness proves its selected state is even. Thus, for the stated face orders and printed normalizations,

J_3(hbar,x)=-exp[-pi i/12+11pi i/(12hbar)]J_2(hbar,x).

The corresponding minimal order-two operator is exactly the same; its reflection multiplier is only -q(-6)Q(-6), and no nonmonomial peripheral factor has been removed. The exact face, angle, phase, automorphism and operator checks are reproducible. Independent two-dimensional versus one-dimensional integration agrees within 1.2e-12 in four checked cases. General connecting sequences and the all-alternating extension remain open.

The further argument in Section 6.1 of peripheral_phase_and_state.md removes the affine phase slope for any certified positive path with compatible complete geometric endpoints. At lambda=0 the full saddle has x(y*)=0, so hbar times the logarithmic longitude derivative of its normalized transform tends to zero. Comparing the exact affine transform laws forces d_1=0. Consequently selected-state annihilator ideals and volume limits are invariant along these certified proper positive paths; no meridian translation is discarded by convention. Path existence beyond the audited components and the all-alternating geometric certificates are still required.

11. An independent edge theorem and a certified non-twist example

face_edge_stokes_identity.md proves the edge rows A_ed=B_ed G for any globally branched oriented face pairing with invertible script-A. The proof constructs a face assignment from arbitrary edge values and cancels an oriented face sum. It also proves rank B_ed=N-1 for one-cusped triangulations. This sharpens the earlier finite-certificate treatment; it neither assumes nor proves that the preferred-longitude row follows automatically.

The folded-layer construction in the appendix of Guéritaud–Futer, arXiv:math/0406242, gives explicit face permutations from an R/L word. Its combinatorics have been reconstructed in experiments/layered_two_bridge.py. The general geometricity proof in that paper has not been adopted as an unaudited hypothesis.

For the particular word RLRL, six_three_and_two_bridge_audit.md supplies an independent rational Rouché certificate, polynomial gluing identities, rational logarithmic-argument bounds, and a preferred-longitude cellular boundary certificate. These prove positive complete geometric and peripheral data for the specified 6_3 state and put it under our analytic volume and annihilator theorems. Optional combinatorial isomorphism checks calibrate the standard knot marking; they are kept separate from the exact geometric and preferred-longitude calculations. No claim that all canonical two-bridge triangulations are proper is used: exhaustive branching searches find singular face matrices for every compatible ordering of several such triangulations, beginning with 6_2.

12. Evaluating singular face contractions before extracting the state

singular_faces_and_six_two.md evaluates the same charged kernels using independent Fourier integration. It assumes rank D=N and a surjective kernel-to-frequency matrix C; neither assumption is replaced by a presumed TQFT existence theorem. The resulting normalization is a product of three explicit Jacobians, and the residual integral is absolutely convergent. The Stokes identity extends to its allowed shape subspace. Complete horosphere decorations then identify the geometric critical point and give a volume theorem for the full reduced tensor under the displayed hypotheses.

For 6_2 a separate exact certificate produces a three-variable selected meridian fiber, the complete positive algebraic shapes and logarithmic branches, the preferred-longitude cellular relation, and its entire angle-dependent tensor-to-fiber phase. Its existence and volume law follow without inverting the singular face matrix. The boundary factorization is the type used in AK v2 Conjecture 1(1); it is not the undefined specialization of the proper FAMED formula. Minimal annihilator existence uses the same Andersen–Malusà convention as before. The full intrinsic AK conjecture, including arbitrary triangulation identifications and H-triangulation limits, has not been proved by this calculation.

13. The Fourier rank conditions are automatic for branched knot exteriors

automatic_face_regularity.md identifies the exact local linear obstruction with a cellular ideal edge cocycle. It then proves that the obstruction vanishes from H^1(M,boundary M)=0 for a knot exterior, using the cohomology sequence and the boundary meridian generator. This proves rank D=N and surjectivity of C uniformly, independently of the convergence assertion in AK’s Theorem 7. The completed independent full-tensor volume theorem now applies to every globally branched complete positive geometric ideal triangulation of a knot exterior in S^3. The general selected boundary state and triangulation identifications remain separate obligations.

14. Preferred-longitude cochains and the meridian shear

Two further primary sources were located and read in this continuation:

  • Kambampati, arXiv:2610.04103v1, submitted 2 October 2026, supplies a cochain construction for the preferred longitude, including singular face matrices.
  • Wong, arXiv:2512.23198v1, submitted 29 December 2025, defines generalized FAMED and additional longitude–meridian conditions. Its analytic theorems are not premises of the new positive-geometry argument.

preferred_longitude_cochains.md reconstructs the first source’s local face potentials, derives its auxiliary potentials from endpoint sums, and proves closure and peripheral period matching on the dual spine. The independent local audit checks 60 potential/arc identities. A separate integer endpoint formula, checked in all 1152 ordered arc-pair cases, proves the peripheral symplectic normalization with the full factor two. The resulting rank and preferred-longitude relations are proved using these cancellations and knot-exterior cohomology, rather than adopted from the source’s theorem.

general_boundary_state_and_framing.md then proves the selected-state volume law whenever the longitude increases the B rank. It permits a rational meridian shear, records its exact Gaussian correction and all scalar phases, and proves volume preservation by a positive-Phi saddle argument. Its exact operator action is Q -> q^beta Q E^(2beta), E -> E, with no specialization. The additional zero-shear expectation in Wong’s Definition 1.5 is not used. The other affine-plane case, flat tetrahedra, arbitrary triangulation identifications and H-triangulation limits remain outside that theorem.

15. Inverse Fourier recovery when longitude moves the shape plane

fourier_boundary_state.md now treats that other boundary rank, using the same charged tensor and the same AK level definition. Its Fourier and Gaussian operators are those in the A=R specialization of Section 6 of Andersen–Kashaev, arXiv:1409.1208v1. The particular inverse transform, measure and angle-dependent quadratic level are specified and independently derived. No new tetrahedral function or unselected recurrence solution is substituted.

The inverse Fourier integral is absolutely convergent when the longitude moves the shape plane. Its extra critical equation is the meridian equation; its real Hessian is negative definite at the complete geometric point. The full small-peripheral factorization includes an explicit real level polynomial. In the ordinary-fiber case it recovers the preceding fiber/Gaussian state exactly. Thus the combined volume theorem and the independent annihilator argument apply to every globally branched complete positive geometric ideal knot triangulation. Neither extra condition (2) nor the zero-shear expectation in Wong’s Definition 1.5 is needed for this tensor-derived realization. Literal equality to a different printed complex-state formula still requires matching its specified normalization.

An exact six-tetrahedron 5_2 certificate realizes the additional rank case. Its algebraic shapes, logarithmic branches, preferred-longitude cellular relation and Fourier constants are independent checks. Optional Regina calibration verifies the three legal combinatorial 2–3 moves and the initial marked meridian; this does not assume a quantum state identity along them. General global triangulation identification, flat tetrahedra, all-alternating geometric realizations and H-triangulation limits remain open. This does not claim the full intrinsic AK conjecture.

16. Structural period torsors instead of additional knot cases

The current comparison is structural_literature_delta.md. It explicitly marks standard local definitions, independent reconstructions and the missing global assertion. In particular the cyclic dilogarithm and its 6j interpretation are existing constructions; renaming them is not a new proof of the conjecture.

heisenberg_period_torsor.md derives the cyclic norm equation and its determinant root as a finite Heisenberg torsor. It evaluates every finite face contraction by character orthogonality, including composite levels at which the support is not a free module. Its coisotropic quotient constructs a boundary torsor on each nondegenerate gluing chart.

pointed_steinberg_recoupling.md identifies the local Faddeev difference module with the pointed Steinberg vacuum at rational b^2. It derives two generic quantum left-ideal identities, an explicit q-factorial kernel and its determinant unit, and proves the basis-free pentagon by coassociativity. No local integral identity is needed for that algebraic coherence theorem. Published matrix conventions and their global periods still have to be matched.

rational_quantum_dilogarithm_torsor.md independently reconstructs the existing exact local rational-parameter formula, retaining its Heisenberg period and branches. Finally bloch_lift_and_boundary_torsor.md proves the rational boundary wedge identity and records the Bloch period whose regulator is the volume exactly. The target is a global pointed period carrying that lift and its regulator comparison. Neither Hilbert 90 for an unpointed module nor fixed-level rigidity alone supplies the remaining comparison across quantum levels.

The later pointed_period_descent.md proves the explicit Racah closure and its signed shared-face normalization. affine_frame_period_transport.md and projective_frame_period_torsor.md give full projective-frame covariance of those local tensors on a compatible root cover, using explicit norm-one intertwiners and a determinant rigidity argument anchored at the identity frame. Their exact audits retain the scalar before normalization; the inversion diagnostic gives 1084/925. The unit vacuum is generally transported to a nonconstant vector, so local pointed recoupling does not by itself select the global literature period.

The comparison also now includes Baseilhac–Benedetti’s existing matrix dilogarithms and analytic character-cover families, Theorem 1.1 and Section 4.4. The latter already describes the classical period as a parallel Chern–Simons section. Their global theorems have not been adopted as premises; their published normalizations, weights and selected periods must be compared explicitly to ours.

equivariant_roots_and_phase_descent.md now constructs the root multipliers around the knot from the peripheral root characters, normalizes triangle transports multiplicatively and treats the remaining scalar pentagon/equivariance discrepancy in relative group cohomology. The residual phase is evaluated on the relative fundamental class; it is not removed by field Hilbert 90.

boundary_regulator_period.md independently derives the classical boundary connection and invariant norm, including the centering term for affine logarithmic edge constraints. It has the ordered-coordinate form of Baseilhac–Benedetti Section 4.4, equations (56)–(57), while recording that their longitude/meridian order is reversed from ours; exchanging that order dualizes the line. It also retains a possible flat unitary phase twist. Its exact regulator norm does not prove the remaining quantum comparison for the selected literature state.

17. The rational state as an exact relative period

rational_period_pushforward.md gives the exact change of variables from the prescribed rational Gaussian–Faddeev class, with the source contour, Jacobian, quantum half phases and Q^n factors retained. It proves multidimensional cubical folding and a unimodular Koszul reduction, and derives the local gluing residue and its Hessian. Garoufalidis–Kashaev 1411.6062, Section 2.1, is the one-dimensional methodological comparison; its global theorem is not used in higher dimensions.

The source-pole criterion requires positive factors and polar coorientation; independent argument regulators can be used only with the corresponding holomorphic period germ. The Faddeev-pair diagnostic has H=-1 and a nonzero period, so open gluing solutions alone need not retain the selected period at a special parameter. Its generic moving gluing residue has a regular value at the removed polar divisor, verified independently using the defining Phi integrals. The cycle and that specialization data remain part of the comparison object.
