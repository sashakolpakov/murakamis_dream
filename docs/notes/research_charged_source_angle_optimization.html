Angle optimization for the complete charged bridge source

Private written deduction, 14 November 2026. The object throughout is the original closed, marked braid scalar. No numerical calculation, test, or change to a frozen manuscript is used.

The exact bridge formula has a useful consequence before any saddle is chosen. Including its clock prefactors turns the absolute weight of every term into reciprocal sine factorials on a transportation polytope. Its limiting exponent is a concave sum of ideal-tetrahedron volumes. The phase-sensitive block estimate then gives a finite-dimensional variational upper bound for the original scalar, with every clock and closing factor retained. This upper bound is not yet identified with the volume of the knot complement, and it supplies no lower bound for the coherent clock sum.

1. The original events and their endpoint charges

Use the complete signed braid and the exact conventions of the charged bridge formula. There are BB overbridge events and bb closing events. Put k=B+bk=B+b, the number of anchor segments, and d=N−1d=N-1. Every event has total polynomial degree dd, and the original coefficient requires degree dd at every anchor. Distinct incidences at the same anchor remain distinct.

An overbridge with m≥1m\ge1 encounters has m+1m+1 ordered binary blocks, with lengths L0=d−h1,Lj=hj−hj+1(1≤j<m),Lm=hm.(1) L_0=d-h_1,\qquad L_j=h_j-h_{j+1}\ (1\le j<m),\qquad L_m=h_m. \qquad\text{(1)} Their sum is dd. A closing event has one block of length dd. Let a block e:u→ve:u\to v give exponent ne,0n_{e,0} to its tail anchor and exponent ne,1n_{e,1} to its head anchor. Thus ne,0,ne,1≥0,Le=ne,0+ne,1.(2) n_{e,0},n_{e,1}\ge0,\qquad L_e=n_{e,0}+n_{e,1}. \qquad\text{(2)} The two incidences are kept separately even when u=vu=v.

Define 𝒫\mathcal P by nonnegative real incidence coordinates xe,ϵx_{e,\epsilon} satisfying ∑e in event v∑ϵ=01xe,ϵ=1,∑(e,ϵ) assigned to anchor axe,ϵ=1.(3) \sum_{e\text{ in event }v}\sum_{\epsilon=0}^1x_{e,\epsilon}=1, \qquad \sum_{(e,\epsilon)\text{ assigned to anchor }a} x_{e,\epsilon}=1. \qquad\text{(3)} This is a compact bipartite transportation polytope, with event vertices on one side and anchor vertices on the other. Its underlying incidence graph is fixed by the original word and the chosen exact overbridge grouping.

Proposition 1. The original clock assignments together with one chosen monomial from each block, whose product has the prescribed anchor degrees, are in bijection with d𝒫∩ℤincidencesd\mathcal P\cap\mathbb Z^{\mathrm{incidences}}.

Indeed (2) recovers every block length. For an overbridge the inverse of (1) is hj=∑ℓ=jmLℓ.(4) h_j=\sum_{\ell=j}^{m}L_\ell . \qquad\text{(4)} Nonnegative lengths summing to dd give precisely the original monotone clocks. The second equations in (3) are the original anchor coefficient conditions. Conversely a chosen block monomial gives these incidences and clocks uniquely. The exact block coefficients have already summed their internal choices; there is no missing binomial multiplicity in this bijection.

3. Concavity of the actual absolute exponent

Define J(t)=∫0tlog⁡(2sin⁡πs)ds,𝒱(x)=−∑e,ϵJ(xe,ϵ).(10) J(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi s)\,ds,\qquad \mathcal V(x)=-\sum_{e,\epsilon}J(x_{e,\epsilon}). \qquad\text{(10)} The continuous values at zero and one are included. A closing row has two entries summing to one, so its contribution to (10) is zero.

For nonnegative y1,…,ysy_1,\ldots,y_s with sum one, set Aj=∑i≤jyiA_j=\sum_{i\le j}y_i. Reflection J(1−t)=−J(t)J(1-t)=-J(t) gives the exact identity −∑i=1sJ(yi)=∑j=2s−1{−J(Aj−1)−J(yj)−J(1−Aj)}.(11) -\sum_{i=1}^sJ(y_i) =\sum_{j=2}^{s-1} \{-J(A_{j-1})-J(y_j)-J(1-A_j)\}. \qquad\text{(11)} Each summand is the volume divided by π\pi of an ideal tetrahedron with angles πAj−1,πyj,π(1−Aj)\pi A_{j-1},\pi y_j,\pi(1-A_j). The sum telescopes, so there is no added volume constant. This identity extends to the closed simplex by continuity.

The tetrahedral angle-volume function is strictly concave on its open angle simplex. For the normalized tetrahedral volume −J(a)−J(b)−J(c)-J(a)-J(b)-J(c), the negative Hessian in the first two normalized angles is π\pi times (cot⁡(πa)+cot⁡(πc)cot⁡(πc)cot⁡(πc)cot⁡(πb)+cot⁡(πc)),a+b+c=1.(12) \begin{pmatrix} \cot(\pi a)+\cot(\pi c)&\cot(\pi c)\\ \cot(\pi c)&\cot(\pi b)+\cot(\pi c) \end{pmatrix},\qquad a+b+c=1. \qquad\text{(12)} The diagonal entries of the displayed cotangent matrix are positive by the sine addition formula, and that matrix has determinant one. The fan maps in (11) jointly detect every nonzero tangent variation of a row with s≥3s\ge3. Thus its row entropy is strictly concave in the open simplex. A two-entry row has identically zero entropy. Summing proves concavity of 𝒱\mathcal V on 𝒫\mathcal P, with strict concavity wherever every nonzero allowed variation changes a positive row with at least three entries. Forced zero faces and directions supported only on two-entry rows must be retained; no general interior-maximizer assertion is needed.

Define the positive sum without its external scalar by ZN+=∑n∈d𝒫∩ℤincidences∏e,ϵSne,ϵ−1. Z_N^+=\sum_{n\in d\mathcal P\cap\mathbb Z^{\mathrm{incidences}}} \prod_{e,\epsilon}S_{n_{e,\epsilon}}^{-1}. Then limN→∞1Nlog⁡ZN+=maxx∈𝒫𝒱(x).(13) \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log Z_N^+ =\max_{x\in\mathcal P}\mathcal V(x). \qquad\text{(13)} If the polytope is empty the sum is zero; otherwise the following proof also establishes that its required integer points exist. Uniformly for 0≤j<N0\le j<N, log⁡Sj=NJ(j/N)+O(log⁡(N+1))\log S_j=NJ(j/N)+O(\log(N+1)). This follows by subtracting the two endpoint logarithms from log⁡(2sin⁡πt)\log(2\sin\pi t) and applying elementary integral comparison to the remaining smooth function and to each endpoint logarithm. There are polynomially many incidence assignments. This proves the upper bound in (13).

To prove the lower bound, round dxdx for a maximizing real flow while retaining its row and column sums. Confine each coordinate to its floor/ceiling interval. A nonintegral supported subgraph has no vertex of degree one, since all required sums are integral. It therefore has an even cycle. Alternately increase and decrease its entries until one becomes integral, keeping every sum and every floor/ceiling interval. Repeat. The resulting integer flow differs coordinatewise from dxdx by at most one. Continuity of (10), or its endpoint modulus O(δ|log⁡δ|)O(\delta|\log\delta|), proves the matching exponential lower bound for this positive sum. This is a lower bound for ZN+Z_N^+, not for the coherent scalar (9).

4. Fixed-clock capacity equals constrained angle optimization

Let λe=xe,0+xe,1\lambda_e=x_{e,0}+x_{e,1}, and let ℒ\mathcal L be the projection of 𝒫\mathcal P onto these block lengths. Each overbridge row has length sum one; every closing length is one. For fixed λ∈ℒ\lambda\in\mathcal L, put vλ(t)=J(λ)−J(t)−J(λ−t),0≤t≤λ. v_\lambda(t)=J(\lambda)-J(t)-J(\lambda-t),\qquad 0\le t\le\lambda. The original anchor constraints are ∑e:v→ate−∑e:a→vte=1−∑e:a→vλe.(14) \sum_{e:v\to a}t_e-\sum_{e:a\to v}t_e =1-\sum_{e:a\to v}\lambda_e. \qquad\text{(14)} The endpoint cases λ=0,1\lambda=0,1 remain included.

For positive radius ratios rr, define Iλ,r(θ)=12π∫−πλπλlog⁡|1+rei(θ+s)|ds.(15) I_{\lambda,r}(\theta)=\frac1{2\pi} \int_{-\pi\lambda}^{\pi\lambda} \log|1+r e^{i(\theta+s)}|\,ds. \qquad\text{(15)} The block theorem proves Iλ,ey(0)=max0≤t≤λ{vλ(t)+yt}.(16) I_{\lambda,e^y}(0) =\max_{0\le t\le\lambda}\{v_\lambda(t)+yt\}. \qquad\text{(16)} Write sa=log⁡ras_a=\log r_a for anchor log-radii. The exact dual identity for the closed feasible polytope is maxx∈𝒫,xe,0+xe,1=λe𝒱(x)=−∑eJ(λe)+infs{∑e:u→v[λesu+Iλe,esv−su(0)]−∑asa}.(17) \begin{split} \max_{x\in\mathcal P,\,x_{e,0}+x_{e,1}=\lambda_e}\mathcal V(x) ={}&-\sum_eJ(\lambda_e)\\ &+\inf_s\left\{\sum_{e:u\to v} [\lambda_es_u+I_{\lambda_e,e^{s_v-s_u}}(0)] -\sum_a s_a\right\}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(17)} Closing lengths contribute J(1)=0J(1)=0. In particular the prefactor in the first line of (17) is exactly the limiting magnitude of the original clock factors; it is not omitted.

The inequality from left to right follows immediately from (16) and (14). Equality holds also on tight faces, without claiming a finite minimizing radius there. To see it, let DtD t be the incidence imbalance in (14), and let F(t)=∑evλe(te)F(t)=\sum_ev_{\lambda_e}(t_e). The set {(Dt,z):0≤t≤λ,z≤F(t)}\{(Dt,z):0\le t\le\lambda,\ z\le F(t)\} is closed and convex. Separate it from (b,z0)(b,z_0), where bb is the feasible target in (14) and z0>max⁡Dt=bF(t)z_0>\max_{Dt=b}F(t). The coefficient of zz in a strict separating functional must be positive: it cannot be negative because the set extends downward, and cannot be zero because it contains a point with first coordinate bb. Normalize that coefficient to one. The remaining coefficients give an affine multiplier whose unconstrained maximum is less than z0z_0. Letting z0z_0 decrease to the constrained maximum proves the reverse inequality. Formula (16) evaluates that maximum as the radial expression in (17). This argument handles loops, forced endpoints, and degenerate lengths.

Thus the positive radial capacity, including the original clock prefactor, has precisely the same limiting variational problem as the incidence angles. These are two representations of the same positive comparison, not independent optimizations whose values may be combined arbitrarily.

5. A phase-sensitive upper bound for the complete original scalar

The original block gains have limits we(λ)=exp⁡(iωe(λ))w_e(\lambda)=\exp(i\omega_e(\lambda)) as the normalized clocks approach λ\lambda. These follow from the explicit integer-linear exponents in the charged bridge formula, including the signs from x=−q−1x=-q^{-1}. One may use their circle values; no globally selected argument is required. Finite pivot shifts and the −1-1 in d=N−1d=N-1 remain in (9). Their limiting shifts are obtained from those exact formulas, not reset independently.

For any fixed positive anchor radii define 𝒜(λ,r)=−∑eJ(λe)+∑e:u→vλelog⁡ru−∑alog⁡ra+maxϑ∈𝕋k∑e:u→vIλe,rv/ru(ωe(λ)+ϑv−ϑu).(18) \begin{split} \mathcal A(\lambda,r)={}&-\sum_eJ(\lambda_e) +\sum_{e:u\to v}\lambda_e\log r_u-\sum_a\log r_a\\ &+\max_{\vartheta\in\mathbb T^k} \sum_{e:u\to v}I_{\lambda_e,r_v/r_u} (\omega_e(\lambda)+\vartheta_v-\vartheta_u). \end{split} \qquad\text{(18)} The expression is invariant under a common scaling of all radii. If cχ=limsup⁡NN−1log⁡|χN|c_\chi=\limsup_NN^{-1}\log|\chi_N| is finite, then limsupN1Nlog⁡|KN|≤cχ+supλ∈ℒinfra>0𝒜(λ,r).(19) \boxed{\displaystyle \limsup_N\frac1N\log|K_N| \le c_\chi+\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal L} \inf_{r_a>0}\mathcal A(\lambda,r).} \qquad\text{(19)} In the original framing conventions the external factor is retained explicitly; its known subexponential size makes cχ=0c_\chi=0.

To prove (19), apply the original Cauchy coefficient integral at any fixed radii in each clock sector. The uniform midpoint estimate in the block phase-loss theorem gives log⁡|BL,N(reiθ)|≤NIL/N,r(θ)+O(log⁡(N+1))\log|B_{L,N}(r e^{i\theta})|\le NI_{L/N,r}(\theta)+O(\log(N+1)), including every zero. The factorial estimate and (6) supply the first term of (18). There are only polynomially many clocks for the fixed word. Along a subsequence giving the largest nonzero sector rate, its feasible normalized lengths have a convergent subsequence λN→λ∈ℒ\lambda_N\to\lambda\in\mathcal L. For every fixed choice of radii, the arc integrals are jointly continuous in their length and center, uniformly on the center circle. This also holds at a logarithmic zero, by integrability and translation continuity of the logarithmic kernel. The sector rate is consequently at most 𝒜(λ,r)\mathcal A(\lambda,r) for every fixed rr. Taking the infimum now proves (19). Neither a radius chosen uniformly for all clock sectors nor an interchange of supremum and infimum is used. If all sectors vanish the bound is immediate.

The maximum in (18) equals the centered positive arc sum minus the exact graph arc-loss energy. Consequently the infimum of (18) over radii is bounded by the positive capacity rate in (17), and can be strictly smaller. The quantitative cycle estimate in the block theorem gives an explicit loss when the actual gains have incompatible phases on a regular cycle. Boundary lengths and diverging optimal radii are included in (19); they do not automatically give a uniform positive loss.

6. Which geometric equations have been obtained

There is a direct local volume interpretation on a smaller real chamber. Order one overbridge row by its original blocks, taking the tail incidence and then the head incidence of each block. If every angle in that row satisfies 0<xi<1/20<x_i<1/2, put equatorial vertices on the unit circle with successive arguments differing by 2πxi2\pi x_i, and use apices 0,∞0,\infty in the upper-half-space boundary. The convex ideal bipyramid is subdivided by its axial edge into tetrahedra of shapes e2πixie^{2\pi i x_i}. Its volume is ∑iD(e2πixi)=−2π∑iJ(xi).(20) \sum_i D(e^{2\pi i x_i}) =-2\pi\sum_iJ(x_i). \qquad\text{(20)} The gaps less than π\pi put the origin inside the equatorial polygon, so these tetrahedra have disjoint interiors and positive orientations. The row equation gives angle 2π2\pi around the internal axial edge. The outer equatorial edge in position ii has dihedral angle 2πxi2\pi x_i; the polar edge at the intervening equatorial vertex has dihedral angle π(1−xi−1−xi).(21) \pi(1-x_{i-1}-x_i). \qquad\text{(21)} This follows by adding the two neighboring tetrahedral angles π/2−πxi−1\pi/2-\pi x_{i-1} and π/2−πxi\pi/2-\pi x_i.

The anchor equations in (3) are therefore the correct numerical equatorial angle sums if those incidences are realized by face pairings. The charged coefficient metadata alone do not specify or verify all such face pairings for the original knot exterior. In particular a class CC of glued polar edges must satisfy ∑(v,i)∈C(1−xv,i−1−xv,i)=2,(22) \sum_{(v,i)\in C}(1-x_{v,i-1}-x_{v,i})=2, \qquad\text{(22)} and the remaining shape and peripheral equations must be retained. No assertion that (22) follows from (3) is made here. At a block’s internal pair of incidences the angle (21) depends only on its length. Between blocks it depends on their separate endpoint charges. Consequently the full clock-dependent phase (8), not only fixed-clock graph holonomy, must enter a proposed gluing comparison.

Outside the chamber of (20), the algebraic entropy identity (11) and all variational results remain valid. The axial subdivision need no longer be a positive geometric subdivision. None of these positive comparisons changes the phase or declares the original cycle to pass through the absolute optimizer. The two-crossing positive-chart obstruction is avoided in the exact bridge identity, but is not resolved by assigning independent positive shapes to the original crossings. A geometric identification of (18), and a nonzero contribution to (9), remain separate tasks.
