A polynomial bound for one full microscopic endpoint under a unit turn

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The original signed unit-leaf operator sends one full microscopic endpoint kernel to a polynomially bounded ordinary representative on the entire real spectral interval. The proof uses its exact forced Askey–Wilson equation and retains the complete first spectral normals. It does not bound that operator on arbitrary polynomials or on products of endpoint kernels.

1. Statement and original normalization

Put N≥2,D=N−1,θ=π/N,q=eiθ,η=(−1)D,κ=qD2/2, N\ge2,\quad D=N-1,\quad \theta=\pi/N,\quad q=e^{i\theta},\quad \eta=(-1)^D,\quad \kappa=q^{D^2/2}, sj=2sin⁡(jθ),Sj=∏ℓ=1jsℓ,cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)θ), s_j=2\sin(j\theta),\quad S_j=\prod_{\ell=1}^j s_\ell,\quad c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\theta), ej(Z)=Sj−2∏a<j(ca−Z),PN(Z)=2TN(Z/2)+2,KN(w,Z)=∑j=0Dej(w)ej(Z).(1) e_j(Z)=S_j^{-2}\prod_{a<j}(c_a-Z),\quad P_N(Z)=2T_N(Z/2)+2,\quad K_N(w,Z)=\sum_{j=0}^D e_j(w)e_j(Z). \qquad\text{(1)} The algebra is 𝒜N=ℂ[Z]/(PN)\mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[Z]/(P_N), with its original Frobenius functional, physical turn, and crossing: ϕN(eiej)=𝟏i+j=D,Tej=eD−j,Uσ=κσTR−σT,σ=±1.(2) \phi_N(e_i e_j)=\mathbf1_{i+j=D},\qquad Te_j=e_{D-j},\qquad U_\sigma=\kappa^\sigma T R^{-\sigma}T,\quad \sigma=\pm1. \qquad\text{(2)} Products in this algebra are represented by their ordinary remainders of degree at most DD, denoted can\operatorname{can}. This convention is essential at an ordinary free endpoint.

For A<∞A<\infty, define wN(α)=2cosh⁡(α/N),FN,σ,α(Z)=(Uσ(Z)KN)(wN(α),Z),|α|≤A.(3) w_N(\alpha)=2\cosh(\alpha/N),\qquad F_{N,\sigma,\alpha}(Z) =(U_\sigma^{(Z)}K_N)(w_N(\alpha),Z),\qquad |\alpha|\le A. \qquad\text{(3)} Here α\alpha may be complex.

Theorem 1. There are finite constants CA,pAC_A,p_A, independent of N,σ,αN,\sigma,\alpha, such that supZ∈[−2,2]|FN,σ,α(Z)|≤CANpA.(4) \boxed{\quad \sup_{Z\in[-2,2]}|F_{N,\sigma,\alpha}(Z)|\le C_A N^{p_A}. \quad} \qquad\text{(4)} For every fixed pair of nonnegative integers a,ba,b, the same conclusion holds for ∂αa∂ZbF\partial_\alpha^a\partial_Z^bF, with a possibly larger constant and power. In particular every physical value and every first spectral normal is retained, at both parities.

The powers in (4) are deliberately not optimized. They may depend on the fixed parameter bound AA. No assertion concerns a parameter bound growing with NN.

3. The exact forced equation

Let CC denote multiplication by ZZ and put J=TCTJ=TCT. Because crossing multiplication commutes with CC, JUσ=UσJ.(15) J U_\sigma=U_\sigma J. \qquad\text{(15)} The exact ordinary reproducer TKN(w,⋅)=PN(Z)−PN(w)ηN2(Z−w) TK_N(w,\cdot) =\frac{P_N(Z)-P_N(w)}{\eta N^2(Z-w)} implies in the full algebra (w−J)KN(w,⋅)=ηPN(w)eD/N2. For the index variable rr, put c(r)=2cos⁡((2r+1)θ),A(r)=sr+14s2r+1s2r+2,C(r)=sr4s2rs2r+1.(17) c(r)=2\cos((2r+1)\theta),\qquad A(r)=\frac{s_{r+1}^4}{s_{2r+1}s_{2r+2}},\qquad C(r)=\frac{s_r^4}{s_{2r}s_{2r+1}}. \qquad\text{(17)} The removable values are C(0)=C′(0)=0C(0)=C'(0)=0. Set JN=⌊(N−1)/2⌋J_N=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor. Only rows 0≤j<JN0\le j<J_N will be used. They are all regular. At even levels JNJ_N is the last double root; at odd levels it is the last distinct root, which is simple. We never use the singular next row at even levels.

4. Polynomial propagation through the entire value grid

For all regular positive rows, qj=(sin⁡(jθ)sin⁡((j+1)θ))3cos⁡((j+1)θ)cos⁡(jθ)∈(0,1),q0=0.(21) q_j= \left(\frac{\sin(j\theta)}{\sin((j+1)\theta)}\right)^3 \frac{\cos((j+1)\theta)}{\cos(j\theta)} \in(0,1),\qquad q_0=0. \qquad\text{(21)} Both ratios are in (0,1)(0,1), since (j+1)θ<π/2(j+1)\theta<\pi/2. Also A(j)≥16π2(j+1)2N2.(22) A(j)\ge\frac{16}{\pi^2}\frac{(j+1)^2}{N^2}. \qquad\text{(22)} Indeed sj+1≥4(j+1)/Ns_{j+1}\ge4(j+1)/N, whereas s2j+1s2j+2≤16π2(j+1)2/N2s_{2j+1}s_{2j+2}\le16\pi^2(j+1)^2/N^2. For j≥1j\ge1, balance the difference rather than diagonalizing the transfer: Xj=(hj(j+1)(hj−hj−1)). X_j=\binom{h_j}{(j+1)(h_j-h_{j-1})}. 5. The original spectral normals

Use the index derivative yj=c′(j)F′(cj),c′(j)=−4θsin⁡((2j+1)θ).(29) y_j=c'(j)F'(c_j),\qquad c'(j)=-4\theta\sin((2j+1)\theta). \qquad\text{(29)} Differentiate the ordinary identity (20), with q,d,fq,d,f regarded as their functions of rr. All coefficient derivatives in (30) have a uniform polynomial bound in NN. For clarity, a coarse bound sufficient here is |qj′|+|dj′|+|fj′|≤CAN10.(31) |q_j'|+|d_j'|+|f_j'|\le C_A N^{10}. \qquad\text{(31)} For j≥1j\ge1, every nonzero sine in (17) is at least 2/N2/N in absolute value. The anchor y0=c′(0)F′(c0)y_0=c'(0)F'(c_0) is polynomial by (14). To propagate only the required double-root normals, use (30) through the row preceding the last double root. Balancing (yj,(j+1)(yj−yj−1))(y_j,(j+1)(y_j-y_{j-1})) gives exactly the same matrices MjM_j as in (25). The added vector is (gj,(j+2)gj)(g_j,(j+2)g_j), which is polynomial by (28),(31). Forward variation of constants and (27) now give maxdouble cj|yj|≤CANpA(1).(33) \max_{\text{double }c_j}|y_j| \le C_A N^{p_A^{(1)}}. \qquad\text{(33)} There is no inverse fundamental-matrix estimate in this argument. At the odd simple node its value was already included in (28); no normal there is needed or supplied by this argument. The lone N=2N=2 normal is the initial anchor itself.

Insert these values and normals into (7). This proves the real interval bound (4). Markov supplies every fixed ordinary spectral derivative. Finally FF is entire in α\alpha; repeating (4) on |α|≤A+1|\alpha|\le A+1 and applying Cauchy’s formula supplies every fixed α\alpha-derivative, jointly with the spectral derivatives. This proves Theorem 1.

6. Exact source consequence and limits

For a leaf with original power σ=±1\sigma=\pm1 and precisely the one full endpoint factor in (3), its original contraction is ϕN(Z)[Rσ(Z)KN(wN(α),Z)KN(Z,X)]=T(RσKwN(α))(X)=R−σ(X)FN,σ,α(X)in 𝒜N.(35) \phi_N^{(Z)} [R^\sigma(Z)K_N(w_N(\alpha),Z)K_N(Z,X)] =T(R^\sigma K_{w_N(\alpha)})(X) =R^{-\sigma}(X)F_{N,\sigma,\alpha}(X) \quad\text{in }\mathcal A_N. \qquad\text{(35)} At a traced neighbor this changes its power by −σ-\sigma and inserts the polynomially bounded full coupon FF. All its other factors and joint normal operations remain. At a free neighbor the product on the right must first be taken in its canonical ordinary representative. The actual Gaussian physical jets and (7) show that this representative is polynomially bounded too.

The argument establishes one such turn, including all physical jets. It does not identify FF with a product of microscopic endpoint kernels, and gives no closure theorem for repeated turns after branching. In particular it does not assert a polynomial norm for UσU_\sigma on every polynomial with polynomial real norm. A product input has a different forced equation; it is not covered merely because each factor satisfies (16). No scalar lower bound, filled-action estimate, or whole-current gap follows from (4).

The archived exact unit-turn note already gives the full triangular matrix, finite hypergeometric sums, and adapter (13). The archived all-color normal theorem supplies the original difference equation and the nontrivial endpoint normal bound (11). The added result here is the polynomial full-grid propagation for the exact forced problem, and its single-factor leaf consequence.
