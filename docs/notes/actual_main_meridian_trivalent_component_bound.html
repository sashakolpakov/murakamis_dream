Controlling meridian deformations in trivalent Conway regions

This special-case argument is retained in full. The general component theorem and filled-meridian theorem now permit every fixed strict bare component, without an additional valence or path condition.

1 November 2026.

A Conway construction joins two-string tangles by ordered products and physical quarter-turns. Consecutive products with no intervening turn can be collected into a single product junction. A junction is bare if it contains no original crossing-circle operator. A connected bare region can have many such junctions. This note proves a uniform source estimate when each junction in that region is trivalent, meaning that exactly three branches meet there, including branches attached to the rest of the construction.

The valence is measured in the reduced tree of actual operations. A junction with four or more branches cannot be made eligible by drawing it as several binary products: the new edges would have no physical quarter-turn. That distinction is used in the proof. The number of trivalent junctions is otherwise unrestricted, provided the whole construction is fixed before the level tends to infinity.

Moving the main meridian changes the polynomial relation used by the quantum calculation. A product that was zero in the complete quotient can acquire an additional term. We will call these additional trace terms; in a finite expansion one chooses either the complete term or an additional term at each junction. Such a choice is a sector. The theorem controls every sector with at least one additional term by the same original complete geometric action, with a strict loss. It retains all labels on internal edges. In particular an internal maximizing label is allowed to equal or exceed one half.

6. Consequence for the finite source and the remaining scope

At a fixed finite trivalent component there are finitely many choices between complete and additional terms and polynomially many integer states. The paired edge factors and all prescribed typed scalars are bounded on |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1. Uniform closed coefficient rates and (18) therefore bound the entire absolute sum of all nonempty additional-term sectors by eN(F0−δ+o(1)). e^{N(F_0-\delta+o(1))}. \tag{19} No cancellation between additional terms is used for this estimate. In sectors with no additional term, the bounded paired weights are compared termwise with the original positive complete contraction. A high original boundary input or high output probe has the complete-component strict gap. This is the complete coefficient-probe theorem; it permits low rates g+cg+c and high rates cc. Thus the component has its original full norm budget, and its clean part retains all complete-supported internal labels while keeping its boundary inputs and output low. The remainder has a strict spectral deficit. Inherited full-cone deficits pass by affine homogeneity in the constants, as in the grouped-error argument, Section 6. The global trivalent extension proves the remaining source-to-window and filling interfaces.

Near the original strict maximizing state, every cubic has total normalized degree less than 3/23/2. Consequently its raw polynomial trace has degree less than 2N−12N-1 on a fixed window, and the moving trace equals the complete trace exactly there. The internal labels in this regular neighborhood may cross half, but stay in a compact subinterval of (0,1)(0,1), where the canonical paired-edge interpolation is regular. Circle-incident labels remain in the original regular low interval. These are the local inputs for the grouped-window argument; no high cutoff is imposed on bare–bare edges.

The theorem established here is a component comparison for arbitrary-size trivalent bare components in the actual typed tree. It is independent of the earlier many-boundary condition. It can coexist with singleton higher-valence components and other already controlled components in the mutual source induction. The global theorem supplies the additional conditioned window, joint filling contour and marked scalar arguments required for the volume and meridian-profile conclusions. Those conclusions do not follow from the component norm bound alone.

Higher-valence bare components are not settled by this proof. Their binary resolutions have unturned complementary-label edges, on which adjacent moving vertices carrying an additional term can occur. A raw low higher-valence product can also have an additional trace term without a high exterior incidence. The nonadjacency and unique-low-arm accounting above must not be applied to such artificial resolutions.
