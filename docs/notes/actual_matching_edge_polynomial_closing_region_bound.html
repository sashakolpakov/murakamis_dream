Genuine scalar normals have vanishing exponential cost near the closing point

30 October 2026. The original Casimir polynomial and every fixed genuine moving-q derivative have an absolute upper bound of vanishing exponential rate on an ENTIRE small complex disk about z=2. This is larger than the shrinking zero-free disk of the endpoint theorem. Zeros may occur in the present fixed disk; no relative lower bound or canonical logarithm is asserted there.

Use N>=3, d=N-1, q=exp(pi i/N), zeta=q exp(epsilon), Q=zeta^2 and 0<=a<=floor(d/2). All polynomials and face factors are the original generic ones, before extracting any source normal.

1. All spectral denominators cancel in the finite Sears expression

In the exact generic endpoint formula put

z=Z+Z^-1,
A=zeta^-1 Z^-1 Q^-d,
B=zeta^-1 Z Q^(-d-a).

The two spectral-dependent series denominators there are EXACTLY

C=zeta Z Q^(d+1-a)=A^-1 Q^(1-a),
zeta Z Q^(-d-a-1)=B.

For the kth term, finite product reversal gives

(A;Q)_a/(C;Q)_k
   =(-1)^k A^k Q^[k(2a-k-1)/2](A;Q)_(a-k),
(B;Q)_a/(B;Q)_k=(BQ^k;Q)_(a-k).

Consequently the exact source polynomial is the finite sum

p_a(zeta,Z+Z^-1)=sum_(k=0)^a T_k(zeta,Z),

T_k=(-1)^k A^k Q^[k(2a-k+1)/2]
   *(Q^-a;Q)_k^2 (zeta Z;Q)_k^2
   *(A;Q)_(a-k)(BQ^k;Q)_(a-k)
   /[(Q;Q)_k^2(Q^-d;Q)_a(Q^(-d-a-1);Q)_a].      (1)

The exponent of Q is an integer. Every denominator in (1) depends ONLY on Q and is a root unit in this actual range. Thus (1) is valid even when an uncanceled spectral denominator in the preceding Sears presentation vanishes. Each T_k is a Laurent polynomial in Z of degrees between -a and a, with regular generic-q coefficients. The full sum is the already proved polynomial in z; this assertion does not require any symmetry of an individual summand.

At zeta=q,Z=1 the exact endpoint convolution says

T_k(q,1)=b_k^2 b_(a-k)^2>0,
b_n=product_(r=1)^n sin((r-1/2)pi/N)/sin(r pi/N),
sum_k T_k(q,1)=p_a(q,2),
1/(4a+1)<=p_a(q,2)<=1.                         (2)

2. A uniform absolute bound on a fixed small disk

There are absolute constants c,C,r0>0 such that for 0<r<=r0,

|p_a(q exp(epsilon),z)|
   <=p_a(q,2) exp[C N sqrt(r) log(2+1/r)+C log N],
|epsilon|<=c N^-3, |z-2|<=r.                   (3)

In particular the upper exponential rate in (3) tends to zero as r decreases to zero, uniformly in the original row and both parities.

Here is the estimate from the actual finite terms. Choose a solution of Z+Z^-1=z with |Z-1|<=C sqrt(r), and take r0 small enough that |Z-1|<1/2. No holomorphic choice of this root over the disk is made. The identity (1) is pointwise valid for either solution.

The denominators, Q-only numerator factors and q monomials in (1) have relative logarithmic change O(N^2(1+log N)|epsilon|). Their factors have nonzero root exponent distances, and the harmonic bound is the same one proved for the endpoint unit products. A factor with Z dependence in its NUMERATOR instead satisfies

|1-zeta^m Z^e|/|1-q^m|
    <=1+C N (|Z-1|+N|epsilon|)/dist(m,2NZ),

where e=+1 or -1 and |m|<=4N. At the base point these distances are the nonzero odd distances 1,3,… in consecutive intervals. They occur with uniformly bounded multiplicity. The separate factor Z^-k costs at most exp(CN|Z-1|).

For v>=0 the elementary integral comparison gives

sum_(j=1)^N log(1+Nv/j)
  <=N[log(1+v)+v log(1+1/v)]
  <=C N v log(2+1/v),                         (4)

with value zero at v=0. The first inequality uses the decreasing integrand log(1+Nv/x) on (0,N]. Apply (4) with v=C(|Z-1|+N|epsilon|). The extra N|epsilon|<=cN^-2 costs at most O(log N); the same is true of the Q-only changes. We obtain a bound for EACH term, uniform in k,

|T_k(q exp(epsilon),Z)|
  <=T_k(q,1)
     exp[C N sqrt(r) log(2+1/r)+C log N].        (5)

Summing (5) uses the exact positive endpoint weights in (2). There is no replacement by a separately maximized summand, and no additional number-of-terms cost is required. This proves (3), including points where numerator factors vanish. Lower bounds away from z=2 are not part of this argument.

3. Genuine q jets and fixed spectral derivatives

For each fixed nonnegative integer m, Cauchy on the SAME generic epsilon disk proves

sup_(|z-2|<=r) |partial_epsilon^m p_a(q exp(epsilon),z)|_(epsilon=0)
   <=C_m N^(3m) p_a(q,2)
         exp[C N sqrt(r) log(2+1/r)+C log N].   (6)

This differentiates (1), an identity of the original generic source. It does not differentiate a root-only positive replacement.

For any additionally fixed spectral order s, applying Cauchy on a disk of radius r inside |z-2|<=2r gives

sup_(|z-2|<=r) |partial_epsilon^m partial_z^s p_a|_(epsilon=0)
   <=C_(m,s) r^-s N^(3m) p_a(q,2)
         exp[C N sqrt(r) log(2+1/r)+C log N],   (7)

after changing C and restricting r<=r0/2. For fixed r, the factor r^-s has zero N-exponential rate. The tiny-endpoint theorem remains available if a chosen r itself shrinks so quickly that (7) is not the useful form of the bound.

The original face scalar D_a and its inverse remain uniformly bounded on this epsilon disk by their canceled root-unit product estimate. Thus (3),(6),(7) also apply to the actual normalized scalar D_a^-1 p_a, with adjusted constants and the corresponding genuine product derivatives.

4. The bound is for a closing region, not for a contributing cycle

The estimates allow the scalar factor and all fixed required normals to be bounded throughout a small closing region, rather than just at the point 2 or on a fixed compact of its limiting slit plane. They make no claim that this region is zero-free: the scalar roots are above 2 and can enter a fixed disk as N grows.

The actual resolvent still has its own nearby spectral poles. Any bound on a closing part of the ORIGINAL contour must combine (6) or (7) with its actual resolvent estimate and retain the permitted contour and all residues. The present theorem by itself neither selects that contour nor proves geometric saddle dominance or a nonzero source contribution.
