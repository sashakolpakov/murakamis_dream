Real-meridian transport and one form for every meridian jet

Written research proof, 23 November 2026. The original complete-head Hermitian form extends to the actual root meridian family on positive real meridians. The full original mixed-color crossing, including its unrolled Cartan diagonal, preserves it. Holomorphic polarization then gives all meridian derivative identities at once. A finite packet of meridian jets has an explicit nondegenerate Hermitian form preserved by the truncated original word.

The forms are indefinite. The result removes repeated meridian-jet adjoint calculations; it does not bound a marked closing below, reduce the physical dimension to a fixed number, or concern derivatives of the quantum parameter away from its root.

1. The actual root meridian family

Fix N≥2,d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,Fh=(Q;Q)h.(1) N\geq2,\quad d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2, \quad F_h=(Q;Q)_h . \qquad\text{(1)} The quantum parameter stays fixed throughout. On each original balanced head use the physical basis |a⟩|a\rangle, 0≤a≤d0\leq a\leq d, and x=−q−1M,α(M)=d+NπiLog⁡M,qα(M)=x,Kx|a⟩=xQ−a|a⟩,𝗁α|a⟩=(α−2a)|a⟩,f|a⟩=(1−Qa+1)|a+1⟩(a<d),f|d⟩=0,ex|a⟩=(x−1−QxQ−a)|a−1⟩(a>0),ex|0⟩=0.(2) \begin{gathered} x=-q^{-1}M,\qquad \alpha(M)=d+\frac{N}{\pi i}\operatorname{Log} M,\qquad q^{\alpha(M)}=x,\\ K_x|a\rangle=xQ^{-a}|a\rangle,\qquad \mathsf h_\alpha|a\rangle=(\alpha-2a)|a\rangle,\\ f|a\rangle=(1-Q^{a+1})|a+1\rangle\quad(a<d),\qquad f|d\rangle=0,\\ e_x|a\rangle=(x^{-1}-QxQ^{-a})|a-1\rangle\quad(a>0), \qquad e_x|0\rangle=0 . \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(2)} These are the original matrices of [A,E]; the zero endpoint in exe_x is part of the module. It is not replaced by a cyclic arrow. The branch Log⁡M\operatorname{Log} M is real for M>0M>0, continued from that branch when a complex meridian germ is used. In particular α(1)=d\alpha(1)=d.

Define the same one-head physical form at every meridian: Z|a⟩=Qa|a⟩,Rd|a⟩=|d−a⟩,HN=q−1Z−1Rd.(3) Z|a\rangle=Q^a|a\rangle,\qquad R_d|a\rangle=|d-a\rangle, \qquad H_N=q^{-1}Z^{-1}R_d . \qquad\text{(3)} Thus HN|a⟩=qQa|d−a⟩H_N|a\rangle=qQ^a|d-a\rangle, HN†=HNH_N^\dagger=H_N, and HN2=IH_N^2=I. Write A♯=HNA†HNA^\sharp=H_NA^\dagger H_N on one head, and use the corresponding tensor form on several heads.

For positive real MM, x‾=Qx\bar x=Qx and α‾=2d−α\bar\alpha=2d-\alpha. Direct substitution in (2) proves Kx♯=Kx,ex♯=ex,f♯=Q−1f,𝗁α♯=−𝗁α.(4) K_x^\sharp=K_x,\qquad e_x^\sharp=e_x,\qquad f^\sharp=Q^{-1}f,\qquad \mathsf h_\alpha^\sharp=-\mathsf h_\alpha . \qquad\text{(4)} For example, on input a>0a>0, the coefficient of HNex†HNH_Ne_x^\dagger H_N is Q[Q−1x−1−xQ−a]=x−1−QxQ−aQ[Q^{-1}x^{-1}-xQ^{-a}]=x^{-1}-QxQ^{-a}. On input a=0a=0, the actual top zero of ex†e_x^\dagger gives zero. Similarly the coefficient for HNf†HNH_Nf^\dagger H_N is Q−1(1−Qa+1)Q^{-1}(1-Q^{a+1}), including its top zero. For 𝗁α\mathsf h_\alpha, reflection of aa gives α‾−2(d−a)=−α+2a\bar\alpha-2(d-a)=-\alpha+2a.

These identities are compatible with the actual coproducts Δe=e1K2+e2,Δf=f1+K1−1f2,ΔK=K1K2,Δ𝗁=𝗁1+𝗁2.(5) \Delta e=e_1K_2+e_2,\qquad \Delta f=f_1+K_1^{-1}f_2,\qquad \Delta K=K_1K_2,\qquad \Delta\mathsf h=\mathsf h_1+\mathsf h_2 . \qquad\text{(5)} For instance (Δe)♯=Δe(\Delta e)^\sharp=\Delta e and (Δf)♯=Q−1Δf(\Delta f)^\sharp=Q^{-1}\Delta f. Distinct tensor factors commute in these calculations. No positive real form is asserted: the nonzero nilpotent exe_x is self-adjoint for an indefinite form.

2. Full original crossings, with their unrolled diagonal

Substitution converts the original universal coefficient in [C, (5)] exactly into that of PN(ex1f2)P_N(e_{x_1}f_2); it introduces no additional scalar. Its checked version is Ř(M1,M2)=𝖯R0(M1,M2)\check R(M_1,M_2)=\mathsf P R_0(M_1,M_2), where 𝖯\mathsf P is the literal flip from input colors (M1,M2)(M_1,M_2) to output colors (M2,M1)(M_2,M_1). In particular the highest entry κ=qα1α2/2\kappa=q^{\alpha_1\alpha_2/2} has not been removed. Away from completeness this separate scalar need not have modulus one.

The nilpotent argument Y=ex1f2Y=e_{x_1}f_2 satisfies YN=0Y^N=0. From (4), Y♯=Q−1YY^\sharp=Q^{-1}Y. The elementary factorial conjugation and finite Euler inverse are Fh¯=(−1)hQ−h(h+1)/2Fh,PN(Y)−1=∑h=0dYh/Fh.(7) \overline{F_h}=(-1)^hQ^{-h(h+1)/2}F_h,\qquad P_N(Y)^{-1}=\sum_{h=0}^dY^h/F_h . \qquad\text{(7)} The inverse follows coefficientwise below degree NN from the finite QQ-binomial identity; larger powers vanish. Hence PN(Y)♯=∑h=0dYh/Fh=PN(Y)−1.(8) P_N(Y)^\sharp=\sum_{h=0}^dY^h/F_h=P_N(Y)^{-1}. \qquad\text{(8)} No factor FNF_N occurs. For the original full Cartan factor, (4) gives (𝗁α1𝗁α2)♯=𝗁α1𝗁α2(\mathsf h_{\alpha_1}\mathsf h_{\alpha_2})^\sharp =\mathsf h_{\alpha_1}\mathsf h_{\alpha_2}. Conjugating the coefficient πi/(2N)\pi i/(2N) in (6) therefore proves D12♯=D12−1,R0♯=PN(Y)−1D12−1=R0−1.(9) D_{12}^\sharp=D_{12}^{-1},\qquad R_0^\sharp=P_N(Y)^{-1}D_{12}^{-1}=R_0^{-1}. \qquad\text{(9)} This uses the full unrolled diagonal, not an absolute-value estimate on κ\kappa. Since the flip is an isometry between the two identical tensor forms, the checked crossing satisfies Ř(M1,M2)†(HN⊗HN)Ř(M1,M2)=HN⊗HN(M1,M2>0).(10) \boxed{\quad \check R(M_1,M_2)^\dagger(H_N\otimes H_N) \check R(M_1,M_2)=H_N\otimes H_N \quad(M_1,M_2>0).\quad} \qquad\text{(10)} Its genuinely typed negative crossing is the corresponding inverse. When a negative crossing starts with unequal colors (M1,M2)(M_1,M_2), that inverse is taken from the positive map with input colors (M2,M1)(M_2,M_1). No exchange of these source and target orders is implicit.

For a signed colored word 𝒲ω(𝑴)\mathcal W_\omega(\boldsymbol M) of width bb, label meridians on the initial strands and carry them through its actual crossing permutation. Put ℋb=HN⊗b\mathscr H_b=H_N^{\otimes b}. Successive applications of (10), including overlapping adjacent pairs, give 𝒲ω(𝑴)†ℋb𝒲ω(𝑴)=ℋb,𝑴∈(0,∞)b.(11) \mathcal W_\omega(\boldsymbol M)^\dagger\mathscr H_b \mathcal W_\omega(\boldsymbol M)=\mathscr H_b, \qquad \boldsymbol M\in(0,\infty)^b . \qquad\text{(11)} Every original crossing scalar is included. The number of crossings, their signs and both level parities cause no exception.

3. Polarization and all derivative Wronskians

Choose a positive real meridian tuple 𝑴0\boldsymbol M^0, and use the logarithmic coordinates Mi=Mi0etiM_i=M_i^0e^{t_i} on a small conjugation-invariant polydisc. Let W(t)=𝒲ω(𝑴0et)W(t)=\mathcal W_\omega(\boldsymbol M^0e^t). It is the actual holomorphic matrix germ with its original unrolled branches. Define the holomorphic reflected transpose W♮(t)=W(t‾)¯𝖳.(12) W^\natural(t)=\overline{W(\bar t)}^{\mathsf T}. \qquad\text{(12)} Equation (11) holds on its real slice. The identity theorem, applied one coordinate at a time to that real open box, therefore yields W♮(t)ℋbW(t)=ℋb.(13) \boxed{\qquad W^\natural(t)\mathscr H_bW(t)=\mathscr H_b. \qquad} \qquad\text{(13)} This is an identity of full meridian germs. Reflection conjugates the continued logarithmic branches; unrelated windings are not identified.

For a multi-index α\alpha, put Di=Mi∂MiD_i=M_i\partial_{M_i} and Dα=∏iDiαiD^\alpha=\prod_iD_i^{\alpha_i}. Differentiating (13) at the positive real tuple gives every mixed meridian derivative identity in one formula: ∑β≤α(αβ)(Dβ𝒲ω(𝑴0))†ℋbDα−β𝒲ω(𝑴0)={ℋb,α=0,0,α≠0.(14) \boxed{\quad \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta} (D^\beta\mathcal W_\omega(\boldsymbol M^0))^\dagger \mathscr H_b D^{\alpha-\beta}\mathcal W_\omega(\boldsymbol M^0) =\begin{cases}\mathscr H_b,&\alpha=0,\\0,&\alpha\ne0. \end{cases}\quad} \qquad\text{(14)} Equivalently one may replace every Dγ𝒲ω(𝑴0)D^\gamma\mathcal W_\omega(\boldsymbol M^0) by ∂tγW(0)\partial_t^\gamma W(0). They are exact at every finite order, not asymptotic estimates.

The same germ identity also retains actual two-fiber transports. Where the specified continued logarithmic lifts are defined, put Tδ(t)=W(t+δ)W(t)−1T_\delta(t)=W(t+\delta)W(t)^{-1}. Substituting (13) at the two arguments, in the displayed order, gives Tδ‾(t‾)†ℋbTδ(t)=ℋb.(14a) T_{\bar\delta}(\bar t)^\dagger\mathscr H_bT_\delta(t) =\mathscr H_b . \qquad\text{(14a)} For a meridian shift M↦qjMM\mapsto q^jM, δ=jπi/N\delta=j\pi i/N, so the paired shift is −δ-\delta. It does not say that one such imaginary-shift matrix is an isometry by itself, or identify a closed quantum-A companion with this full matrix.

4. An explicit nondegenerate form on finite meridian jets

Fix nonnegative truncation orders r1,…,rbr_1,\ldots,r_b and set A𝒓=ℂ[t1,…,tb]/(t1r1+1,…,tbrb+1).(15) A_{\boldsymbol r}=\mathbb C[t_1,\ldots,t_b]/ (t_1^{r_1+1},\ldots,t_b^{r_b+1}). \qquad\text{(15)} Its involution conjugates coefficients and fixes the formal variables. For physical jet packets u,v∈A𝒓⊗ℂNbu,v\in A_{\boldsymbol r}\otimes\mathbb C^{N^b}, define [u,v]𝒓=[t1r1⋯tbrb]u(t)*ℋbv(t).(16) [u,v]_{\boldsymbol r} =[t_1^{r_1}\cdots t_b^{r_b}] u(t)^*\mathscr H_bv(t). \qquad\text{(16)} Here the star on a formal vector means coefficientwise conjugate transpose with the same formal variables, not substitution of t‾\bar t. The matrix of (16) is explicitly Jr1jet⊗⋯⊗Jrbjet⊗ℋb,(Jrjet)ij=δi+j,r,0≤i,j≤r.(17) J_{r_1}^{\mathrm{ jet}}\otimes\cdots\otimes J_{r_b}^{\mathrm{ jet}} \otimes\mathscr H_b, \qquad (J_r^{\mathrm{ jet}})_{ij}=\delta_{i+j,r},\quad0\leq i,j\leq r. \qquad\text{(17)} Thus it is Hermitian and nondegenerate. Every chosen derivative coefficient and every physical head coordinate remains present; no additional radical quotient is taken. Its matrix squares to the identity, so all its singular values are one. This is a statement about this indefinite coefficient pairing, not a positive norm estimate or a condition-number bound for a word.

Let W𝒓W_{\boldsymbol r} be multiplication by the Taylor polynomial of W(t)W(t) in this jet algebra. Equation (13) implies its exact truncated coefficient identity, and hence [W𝒓u,W𝒓v]𝒓=[u,v]𝒓.(18) [W_{\boldsymbol r}u,W_{\boldsymbol r}v]_{\boldsymbol r} =[u,v]_{\boldsymbol r}. \qquad\text{(18)} Indeed substitute W(t)u(t),W(t)v(t)W(t)u(t),W(t)v(t) into (16), use (13), and extract the top coefficient. Terms killed by (15) cannot affect it. The jet map is invertible since its constant coefficient is invertible. For one parameter and first jets its matrix is (W00W1W0)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}W_0&0\\W_1&W_0\end{smallmatrix}\right), and its form is (0ℋbℋb0)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\mathscr H_b\\\mathscr H_b&0\end{smallmatrix}\right). Thus (18) simultaneously contains the zeroth- and first-order identities.

5. Original boundary observations and scope

The adjoint used here is not an unrecorded change of Hopf dual or of head order. The original antipodes remain 𝒮(K)=K−1\mathscr S(K)=K^{-1}, 𝒮(e)=−eK−1\mathscr S(e)=-eK^{-1}, 𝒮(f)=−Kf\mathscr S(f)=-Kf, with their original left/right placements. For example, a genuine linear dual reverses the weights and has α∨=2d−α,x∨=Q−1x−1,M∨=M−1.(19) \alpha^\vee=2d-\alpha,\qquad x^\vee=Q^{-1}x^{-1},\qquad M^\vee=M^{-1}. \qquad\text{(19)} This follows from the reversed spectrum of −𝗁α-\mathsf h_\alpha. It is different from the anti-linear reflection used in (12). No linear dual color is silently replaced by the same meridian.

For any prescribed original meridian-dependent source p(t)p(t) and row Λ(t)\Lambda(t), its scalar and all its retained jets are still the coefficients of Λ(t)W(t)p(t)\Lambda(t)W(t)p(t). The original marked cap, ordinary turns, pivots, modified dimension and framing/peripheral lines are kept in these boundary data. Equations (13)–(18) do not replace Λ\Lambda by the adjoint of pp. At the complete point the ordinary cup is isotropic for the tensor form, whereas the original marked cup/cap pairing is one; directly use [A, (18)] and ∑a=0dQa=0\sum_{a=0}^dQ^a=0 for the former. These are distinct observations. No original closing or pivot is declared to be a metric isometry.

This gives one exact orthogonality calculation for all meridian jet orders and arbitrary signed overlapping words. It avoids constructing and checking a separate derivative pairing at each order. The jet dimension is still Nb∏i(ri+1)N^b\prod_i(r_i+1). Neither fixed jet order nor the fixed degree of an auxiliary variational formulation makes the physical boundary dimension independent of NN. There is no positive jet norm, general marked scalar lower bound, geometric principal-symbol nonvanishing theorem, or estimate for derivative order growing with NN here. In particular the parameter qq was never varied: moving-qq normal jets are not meridian jets, and [E, Section 7] explicitly warns against identifying them.
