The actual meridian cut and the geometric cyclic point source

Research note, 26 October 2026. The original two-factor pivotal cut has two normalized branch weights that are consecutive meridian translates. Their ideal, their normalization defect, and the cokernel of the regularized cut recover the same length-one point module as the geometric cyclic lattice. A specified formal gauge places the geometric branch collision on exactly these actual cut walls.

This proves a local comparison of the source and cut presentations as modules over the quantum–meridian base, with their residues and full normalization line recorded. It does not identify the actual global affine difference extension, its selected knot section, or its regulator norm across levels.

1. Two consecutive shift factors come from the actual cut

Keep the original conventions

q=q_N=exp(pi*i/N), x=-M/q, Y=M^2, U=M^(2N),
sigma(M)=qM, rho=q-q^(-1), c=q+q^(-1).

Use the branch-marked comparison of the actual Casimir pair with the geometric node in Section 7 of the geometric rigidity note. Write d=L_+-L_-, d(u)=kappa*u+O(u^2), u=Log M, kappa!=0, and

B=R[xi]/(xi(xi-d)), R=C[[u]].

The branch xi=d is marked + and xi=0 is marked -. If f belongs to B, denote its branch values by f_+,f_-. Set

p=q^(-1)M^2-qM^(-2),
r=qM^2-q^(-1)M^(-2).

There is an exact meridian identity, before any specialization:

r=sigma(p).                                    (1.1)

Let Phi_N be the actual scalar pivotal cut on this pair, including the full ADO modified dimension. The earlier exact cut formulas give the branch expression

Phi_N(f)=Gamma_N [r f_+ + p f_-]/d,             (1.2)

where the complete common frame is

Gamma_N=(-1)^N d_N(M) d/
                [(M-M^(-1))(M^N+M^(-N))]
       =-zeta_N U d/[(U-1)(U+1)],
zeta_N=q^(N(1-N)/2).                           (1.3)

This retains both peripheral factors and the original phase. It is an identity of whole meridian germs; its zeros and poles are not separately evaluated. At completeness it has value

Gamma_N(1)=-zeta_N*kappa/(4N).                 (1.4)

For example, to derive (1.2) directly, let

gamma_N=(-1)^N d/
                [(M-M^(-1))(M^N+M^(-N))].

The density coefficients from that note obey

theta_-=theta_0=-gamma_N*p,
theta_+=theta_0+theta_1*d=gamma_N*r.

The residue functional on B is (f_+-f_-)/d. Substituting these two density values and multiplying by the actual d_N proves (1.2). This is a consequence of the actual projector cuts, not a chosen geometric trace.

2. The common algebraic cut germ has a point defect

For the local two-parameter comparison put

S=C[[u,h]], q=exp(h), M=exp(u),
sigma(u)=u+h, sigma(h)=h.

Keep Gamma_N as the full normalization line in (1.3). After factoring this recorded line, the cut coefficients have a common algebraic germ independent of N. This does not assert an off-root deformation of the entire N-dimensional root representation.

The two wall equations have expansions

p=4u-2h+O((u,h)^2),
r=4u+2h+O((u,h)^2).

Consequently

(p,r)=(u,h)=:m,
det partial(p,r)/partial(u,h)|_0=16.           (2.1)

This equality follows from the invertible linear coefficient matrix and formal inversion. Their walls are exactly the balanced half-step meridian walls on the chosen local cover:

p=0 <=> M^2=q,
r=0 <=> M^2=q^(-1).                           (2.2)

For example p=q(-1)M(-2)(M2-q)(M2+q); the other factor is a unit near q=M=1. The first wall equation pulls back to the second under sigma. Their product is the exact expression

pr=(M^2-M^(-2))^2-(q-q^(-1))^2.               (2.3)

Thus their intersection is a reduced point, with ordered residue

Res_0 du dh/[p sigma(p)]=1/16.                (2.4)

At a fixed actual root q_N!=1, this is not a second source at M=1: p(1)=-rho and r(1)=rho are both nonzero. The point in (2.1) records their joint quantum–meridian degeneration.

3. The cut pairing and its regularized dual retain this point

Extend the geometric node to S and retain d=d(u). The normalized density element is

theta_hat=-p+b xi,
b=(p+r)/d=c(M^2-M^(-2))/d.                    (3.1)

Its branch values are -p and r. Since d=kappa*u+O(u^2), b is a unit and b(0,0)=8/kappa. The full density is Gamma_N theta_hat; factoring its line has not changed the period.

For the residue functional Res_B(a+b_1 xi)=b_1, the normalized trace-pairing matrix in the basis 1,xi is

H_cut=[ b    r  ],
      [ r   d r ].

It has determinant pr. Elementary simultaneous row and column operations with the unit b reduce it to

diag(b,pr/b).

Therefore the pairing’s cokernel is the nodal divisor module

coker H_cut = S/(p r).                        (3.2)

Its normalization has the exact sequence

0 -> S/(pr) -> S/(p) direct_sum S/(r)
                 -> S/(p,r) -> 0,             (3.3)

where the last map is the difference of the two reductions at their intersection. Injectivity uses (p) intersect (r)=(pr), since p,r are regular local coordinates. Its cokernel is exactly the length-one point module S/m. Thus normalization of the cut degeneracy divisor loses a specified point datum.

There is also a direct presentation from the cut itself. On the normalization V=S^2, multiplying (1.2) by d gives the regular row

ell_hat:V -> S, (f_+,f_-) -> r f_+ + p f_-.

Its image is m and its single primitive relation is (p,-r). Hence

0 -> S --(p,-r)--> S^2 --(r,p)--> S
                                 -> S/m -> 0. (3.4)

For exactness, if r a+p b=0, coprimality gives a=p t and b=-r t. This is the complete proof of the relation. The full regularized cut is Gamma_N ell_hat, with Gamma_N still recorded. The factor d used for regularization has a simple meridian zero; replacing it by another such factor requires keeping their unit ratio. This does not evaluate the original cut by substitution.

4. Calibrating the geometric cyclic generator to the actual wall

Let a,b_geo be any two geometric formal shift ratios in S^times, with common complete value L_c and classical difference

a(0,u)-b_geo(0,u)=kappa*u+O(u^2), kappa!=0.

Here a and b_geo are the two ratios of the nonzero geometric normal blocks, with their existing leading marked actions. We now give an exact formal unit gauge whose branch collision is the actual wall p=0.

On the specified square-root meridian cover define

R_0(h)=[b_geo/a]_(M=q^(1/2)),
A(h)=(R_0(h)-1)/(q^3-q),
g(M,h)=1+(M^2-q)A(h).                         (4.1)

R_0(0)=1, and q^3-q has a simple h zero, so A belongs to C[[h]] and g is a unit with g(0,0)=1. The formula uses division by a known first-order zero with divisible numerator, not a punctured formal germ. In particular

g|_(M=q^(1/2))=1,
sigma(g)|_(M=q^(1/2))=R_0.

Change the first branch ratio by the exact unit gauge

a_star=a sigma(g)/g, b_star=b_geo.             (4.2)

It follows identically that a_star-b_star vanishes on p=0. Its classical specialization remains a(0,u)-b_geo(0,u), because sigma is identity at h=0. Therefore

Delta_star=a_star-b_star=v p,
v in S^times, v(0,0)=kappa/4.                (4.3)

This proves the factorization: the vanishing gives divisibility by the smooth local equation p, and the derivative ratio gives the displayed nonzero unit. No higher recursion is needed. The construction is the unique affine function of M^2 with the two prescribed values in (4.1). It preserves the leading marked regulator action. It chooses a model geometric cyclic generator; it does not identify the original selected knot generator.

For the split module with ratios a_star,b_star, keep e=(1,1) and f=(1,0). The complete all-shifts cyclic lattice is exactly

Lambda_star=sum_(j in Z) S T^j e
           =S e direct_sum (p,r)S f.         (4.4)

Indeed Te and T^2e generate component differences Delta_star and a_star sigma(Delta_star)+Delta_star sigma(b_star). Their ideal is (p,r)=m. Every positive or negative shifted difference vanishes at (0,0); invertibility of both ratios covers negative shifts. This proves both inclusions in (4.4).

5. A local source and cut presentation now agree

Equation (4.4) has generators e,p f,r f and one relation

r(p f)-p(r f)=0.

Dualizing this presentation gives

Ext^1_S(Lambda_star,S)=S/(p,r)=S/m.           (5.1)

Explicitly the dual relation is the row (0,r,-p); its cokernel is S/(p,r). After reversing the sign of its last generator and discarding the zero first column, it is the regularized actual cut row (r,p) in (3.4). This is an explicit comparison of the two source presentations, not only an equality of their lengths. The normalization quotient in (3.3) gives the same point module.

The calibrated geometric ordered residue also has the required value:

Res_0 du dh/[Delta_star sigma(Delta_star)]
               =(4/kappa)^2/16=1/kappa^2.     (5.2)

The two density branch values have an opposite ordered sign, since they are -p,r; their normalized ordered residue is -1/16. This sign is retained under comparison rather than absorbed into the phase zeta_N or a longitude sign. The full physical frame Gamma_N has the exact complete value (1.4) and remains separate.

Thus the actual two-factor cut has now specified the same local point ideal, presentation, and calibrated residue as a geometric cyclic model whose collision has been placed on its meridian wall. This strengthens a fixed-N nodal-order isomorphism. It identifies how a source appears when q and M degenerate together and why an ordinary normalization or ordinary dual alone can forget it.

The following assertions have not been established by this local comparison:

  • The actual global meridian source has this difference-module presentation, including its induced transport on the source.
  • The actual selected knot section is the calibrated model generator.
  • Sewing the other crossings preserves this class and pairs it nontrivially with the geometric block.
  • The resulting selected root period has the regulator norm across levels.

In particular (5.1) does not imply vanishing of the global affine extension obstruction in Section 8 of the normal-period note. That requires an intertwining map with the actual source arrows, not just the presentation over S. The positive triangulation supplies the geometric ratios needed in Section 4; the actual projector cuts supply the walls in Sections 1–3.

6. Coverage and independent exact checks

Modified traces have existing coverage in Beliakova–Blanchet–Gainutdinov, 1801.00321. The literature quantum-A definition remains the recurrence definition covered by Garoufalidis–Sun, 0802.4074. Only their abstracts were consulted for scope in this step; no proof or recurrence coefficient from either is used. No novelty claim is made for modified-dimension numerators, normal crossings, cyclic-vector changes, or the elementary presentation duality.

The audit verifies the universal shift identity, all wall factorizations, the two-coordinate Jacobian, the complete trace-pairing reduction, and the primitive cut relation with exact rational functions. Four rational formal ratio pairs check (4.1)–(4.3), including nonzero subprincipal terms and both complete longitude signs.

Twelve generic root cases at N=2,3,4,5,6,9 construct the original tensor E,F,K and actual highest-vector projectors, then contract them with the original pivotal matrix. Their two projector cuts agree with (1.2), including the complete common peripheral frame. Six complete cases independently contract the confluent Casimir projector and nilpotent. The level checks include both parities and even and odd composite levels. They validate the exact coefficient derivation, not the global selected-period assertions. Results.
