Repeated endpoint unit turns retain the terminal action

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

One unit turn of a full microscopic endpoint kernel has polynomial real norm. Repeating that operation does not preserve this bound: two turns of the same sign already have the positive exponential rate of the original two-crossing terminal message. This follows from an exact forced equation and a polynomial norm bound for its original difference operator. No new knot calculation is involved.

1. Exact repeated-turn forcing

Use the original full algebra 𝒜N=ℂ[Z]/(PN),PN(Z)=2TN(Z/2)+2,D=N−1,θ=π/N,η=(−1)D,κ=eiπD2/(2N).(1) \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[Z]/(P_N),\quad P_N(Z)=2T_N(Z/2)+2,\quad D=N-1,\quad \theta=\pi/N,\quad \eta=(-1)^D,\quad \kappa=e^{i\pi D^2/(2N)}. \qquad\text{(1)} Write q=eiθq=e^{i\theta}, Sj=∏h=1j2sin⁡(hθ)S_j=\prod_{h=1}^j2\sin(h\theta), S0=1S_0=1, cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)θ)c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\theta), and ej(Z)=Sj−2∏a<j(ca−Z)e_j(Z)=S_j^{-2}\prod_{a<j}(c_a-Z). The Newton basis, original crossing and turn satisfy KN(w,Z)=∑j=0Dej(w)ej(Z),Tej=eD−j,ϕN(eiej)=𝟏i+j=D.(2) K_N(w,Z)=\sum_{j=0}^D e_j(w)e_j(Z),\quad Te_j=e_{D-j},\quad \phi_N(e_i e_j)=\mathbf1_{i+j=D}. \qquad\text{(2)} All classes below have their canonical ordinary representatives of degree at most DD. Set Vn=κnTR−nT,n∈ℤ,Gn,N=T(R−n),Fn,N,α=VnKN(wN(α),⋅),wN(α)=2cosh⁡(α/N).(3) V_n=\kappa^n T R^{-n}T,\quad n\in\mathbb Z,\qquad G_{n,N}=T(R^{-n}),\quad F_{n,N,\alpha}=V_nK_N(w_N(\alpha),\cdot),\quad w_N(\alpha)=2\cosh(\alpha/N). \qquad\text{(3)} Thus Vσ=UσV_\sigma=U_\sigma for σ=±1\sigma=\pm1, and VnVℓ=Vn+ℓ.(4) V_nV_\ell=V_{n+\ell}. \qquad\text{(4)} For example F2σ=Uσ2KwN(α)F_{2\sigma}=U_\sigma^2K_{w_N(\alpha)}.

2. A real norm bound for the original difference operator

Write ∥p∥ℝ=sup⁡[−2,2]|p|\|p\|_{\mathbb R}=\sup_{[-2,2]}|p|. The original off-grid difference formula, with t=(2r+1)θt=(2r+1)\theta, is (ℒp)(2cos⁡t)=c0f(t)+4sin⁡4((t+θ)/2)sin⁡tsin⁡(t+θ)[f(t)−f(t+2θ)] (\mathcal Lp)(2\cos t) =c_0 f(t)+ \frac{4\sin^4((t+\theta)/2)}{\sin t\sin(t+\theta)} [f(t)-f(t+2\theta)] +4sin⁡4((t−θ)/2)sin⁡tsin⁡(t−θ)[f(t)−f(t−2θ)],f(t)=p(2cos⁡t),c0=2cos⁡θ.(7) \hspace{3.8cm}+ \frac{4\sin^4((t-\theta)/2)}{\sin t\sin(t-\theta)} [f(t)-f(t-2\theta)], \qquad f(t)=p(2\cos t),\quad c_0=2\cos\theta. \qquad\text{(7)} All apparent singularities in the combined expression are removable for degree at most DD.

3. A polynomial upper comparison for a fixed number of turns

Fix n∈ℤn\in\mathbb Z and A<∞A<\infty. The endpoint-side adapter extends exactly to this fixed power: Fn,N,α(Z)=κn[can⁡w(R(w)−nKN(w,Z))]w=wN(α).(15) F_{n,N,\alpha}(Z) =\kappa^n[\operatorname{can}_w (R(w)^{-n}K_N(w,Z))]_{w=w_N(\alpha)}. \qquad\text{(15)} Indeed Frobenius self-adjointness of multiplication gives the same transposed Newton matrix as for a single turn. The original physical values of R−nR^{-n} have modulus one and their angular derivatives are bounded by |n|N/2|n|N/2. The full-Hermite Fourier reconstruction and the endpoint normal QN=∂wKN(c0,⋅)Q_N=\partial_wK_N(c_0,\cdot), with ∥QN∥ℝ≤N4/4\|Q_N\|_{\mathbb R}\le N^4/4, therefore give polynomial initial value and first normal for (15), uniformly on |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A.

Apply the single-turn proof’s forced propagation to (6), retaining Gn,NG_{n,N} in its forcing. In its notation the regular physical rows have 0≤qj<1,Aj≥16(j+1)2/(π2N2),|(c0−wN(α))/Aj|≤CA/(j+1)2.(16) 0\le q_j<1,\qquad A_j\ge16(j+1)^2/(\pi^2N^2),\qquad |(c_0-w_N(\alpha))/A_j|\le C_A/(j+1)^2. \qquad\text{(16)} The balanced forward matrices have norm at most 1+CA/(j+1)1+C_A/(j+1), so all their products are polynomial in NN. All coefficient derivatives have polynomial bounds. The differentiated equation has the same homogeneous matrices, so its forcing and its propagated physical normals have a polynomial factor times 1+∥Gn,N∥ℝ1+\|G_{n,N}\|_{\mathbb R}. Stable full-Hermite reconstruction then yields ∥Fn,N,α∥ℝ≤CA,nNpA,n(1+∥Gn,N∥ℝ).(18) \|F_{n,N,\alpha}\|_{\mathbb R} \le C_{A,n}N^{p_{A,n}} (1+\|G_{n,N}\|_{\mathbb R}). \qquad\text{(18)} The regular-row endpoints and the odd simple node are exactly those of the single-turn proof; no coalescing next row is introduced.

The harmless 11 can be absorbed. In fact ϕN(Gn,N)=R−n(c0)=κ−n(19) \phi_N(G_{n,N})=R^{-n}(c_0)=\kappa^{-n} \qquad\text{(19)} has modulus one. The claim holds uniformly for real microscopic parameters in a fixed compact interval. The lower comparison is not extended through the complex zeros in (20).

4. Two same-sign turns already have positive rate

The original two-crossing terminal theorem, in the same normalization, proves T(R2)(−2)=e−iπ/42eN(2G/π+iπ/8)(1+O(N−1)),(22) T(R^2)(-2)=\frac{e^{-i\pi/4}}2 e^{N(2G/\pi+i\pi/8)}(1+O(N^{-1})), \qquad\text{(22)} where G=∑j≥0(−1)j/(2j+1)2>0G=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j/(2j+1)^2>0 is Catalan’s constant, and write M∞=4G/πM_\infty=4G/\pi. The negative-power polynomial has conjugate coefficients. The theorem also bounds the entire real interval by CNaeN2G/πC N^a e^{N\,2G/\pi}. Consequently limN→∞N−1log⁡∥T(R±2)∥ℝ=2G/π.(23) \lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1} \log\|T(R^{\pm2})\|_{\mathbb R}=2G/\pi. \qquad\text{(23)} This is through all levels, with neither parity omitted. Combining with (21) gives limN→∞N−1log⁡∥Uσ2KN(wN(α),⋅)∥ℝ=2G/π=M∞/2,σ=±1.(24) \boxed{\quad \lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1} \log\|U_\sigma^2K_N(w_N(\alpha),\cdot)\|_{\mathbb R} =2G/\pi=M_\infty/2,\qquad \sigma=\pm1. \quad} \qquad\text{(24)} The convergence is uniform for real |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A. In particular, for all sufficiently large NN, ∥Uσ2KN(wN(α),⋅)∥ℝ≥cAN−5eN2G/π.(25) \|U_\sigma^2K_N(w_N(\alpha),\cdot)\|_{\mathbb R} \ge c_A N^{-5}e^{N\,2G/\pi}. \qquad\text{(25)} The input and its first unit-turn image both have polynomial real norm. Their second same-sign turn has positive exponential norm. For the input this also follows directly from (18) at n=0n=0: G0,N=eD=ηPN/[N2(Z−c0)]G_{0,N}=e_D=\eta P_N/[N^2(Z-c_0)] has real norm at most one, by ∥PN′∥ℝ≤N2\|P_N'\|_{\mathbb R}\le N^2. Hence the single-endpoint polynomial theorem cannot be iterated as a polynomial-norm closure rule, even without multiplying different endpoint factors.

5. Scope and source readback

The norm in (24) is an ordinary real-interval polynomial norm. Further physical traces can cancel its large values; no lower bound for an arbitrary closed graph follows. The conclusion instead identifies the actual terminal action which a repeated source reduction must retain. It neither refutes an action-scale estimate nor proves one for arbitrary short components. Complex parameters where PN(wN(α))=0P_N(w_N(\alpha))=0 are explicitly excluded from the lower-rate conclusion.
