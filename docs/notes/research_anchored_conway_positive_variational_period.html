A compact positive variational formula for Conway knot sources

21 November 2026. Research note; previous finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

A finite Conway construction has an explicit tensor compiler with output (1,KN)(1,K_N), where KNK_N is its original reduced Kashaev invariant. The constant coordinate is kept orthogonal to the physical boundary space. It supplies an anchor even when KN=0K_N=0, without introducing ordinary main colors.

This note gives a compact quartic minimization whose value QNQ_N satisfies 1+|KN|2≤QN≤(1+1/N)(1+|KN|2).(1) 1+|K_N|^2\le Q_N\le(1+1/N)(1+|K_N|^2). \qquad\text{(1)} For a fixed expression with mm tensor vertices, the minimization has 2N(m−1)2N(m-1) real variables, and its degree is at most four, independently of mm and NN. The objective is an explicit positive sum of squared local transport residuals. The theorem is quantitative weighted Cauchy–Schwarz; its proof does not require the reference-coordinate or matrix interpretations. Those interpretations give an exact boundary inner product and four positive local norm factors below. Joint convexity and a hyperbolic-volume limit are not proved for the quartic problem.

Fix N≥2N\ge2, and put d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,κ=qd2/2,cj=q2j+1+q−2j−1, d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad \kappa=q^{d^2/2},\quad c_j=q^{2j+1}+q^{-2j-1}, s0=1,sr=∏u=1r2sin⁡(πu/N). s_0=1,\qquad s_r=\prod_{u=1}^r2\sin(\pi u/N). Use the full actual pair algebra and its normalized Newton basis HN=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2),br=∏j=0r−1(cj−z)sr2,0≤r≤d.(2) H_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2),\qquad b_r=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(c_j-z)}{s_r^2}, \quad 0\le r\le d. \qquad\text{(2)} Here TNT_N is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. We identify HNH_N with its NN-dimensional coefficient space in (2), with the Euclidean Hermitian norm. The quotient retains the value and first derivative at every double root; no normal direction is discarded.

The actual turn and multiplication are Jbr=bd−r,M(f,g)=fg(mod⁡2TN(z/2)+2). Jb_r=b_{d-r},\qquad M(f,g)=fg\pmod{2T_N(z/2)+2}. All other coefficients are zero, including products with i+j≥Ni+j\ge N. The physical signed crossing coupons are R±1=κ±1∑r=0d(−q)±rbr.(4) R^{\pm1}=\kappa^{\pm1}\sum_{r=0}^d(-q)^{\pm r}b_r. \qquad\text{(4)} These are the full repeated-root identities of the Newton source theorem and Conway compiler.

Take a fixed literal tangle expression made from these signed crossings, identity coupons, products and quarter-turns. Its ordered, oriented closure is assumed to be one knot, with the original reduced marking and zero-framing convention. Let fE∈HNf_E\in H_N be its actual open boundary packet. Write its exact original closing row as ℓN\ell_N, so that KN=ℓNfE.(5) K_N=\ell_N f_E. \qquad\text{(5)} For the two normalized Newton closures, this is the appropriate row e0𝖳e_0^{\mathsf T} or ed𝖳e_d^{\mathsf T}, with the known framing and normalization line retained in it. In particular log⁡+∥ℓN∥=O(log⁡N)\log^+\|\ell_N\|=O(\log N) for a fixed diagram, where log⁡+x=max⁡(0,log⁡x)\log^+x=\max(0,\log x).

2. An orthogonal constant reference

Give every internal edge the space ĤN=ℂe⋆⊕HN, \widehat H_N=\mathbb C e_\star\oplus H_N, with its orthogonal direct-sum inner product. The symbol e⋆e_\star denotes a separate reference coordinate. It is not the physical algebraic unit b0b_0: indeed Jb0=bdJb_0=b_d.

Compile the expression using the following actual linear or multilinear maps: f̂=(1,f)at a leaf,Ĵ(a,u)=(a,Ju),(6) \widehat f=(1,f)\quad\text{at a leaf},\qquad \widehat J(a,u)=(a,Ju), \qquad\text{(6)} M̂((a,u),(b,v))=(ab,M(u,v)),ℓ̂N(a,u)=(a,ℓNu)∈ℂ2.(7) \widehat M((a,u),(b,v))=(ab,M(u,v)),\qquad \widehat\ell_N(a,u)=(a,\ell_Nu)\in\mathbb C^2. \qquad\text{(7)} The mixed reference/physical input sectors of M̂\widehat M are zero. Thus induction through the tree gives open packet (1,fE)(1,f_E) and final output YN=(1,KN),∥YN∥2=1+|KN|2.(8) Y_N=(1,K_N),\qquad \|Y_N\|^2=1+|K_N|^2. \qquad\text{(8)} Every coefficient of these gates is prescribed before the closed invariant is evaluated. The quantum invariant in the second coordinate has not been changed.

Let the compiler tree have m≥2m\ge2 vertices, including its closing map. Its root has depth zero. For an edge ee, let rer_e be the depth of its child vertex. Let Tv,NT_{v,N} be the coefficient tensor at vertex vv. Its squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the sum of the squared absolute values of all its coefficients. Put BN=∏v∥Tv,N∥HS,tN=BN23mN/2.(9) B_N=\prod_v\|T_{v,N}\|_{\mathrm{ HS}},\qquad t_N=B_N^2\sqrt{3mN/2}. \qquad\text{(9)} Each tensor has a reference coefficient equal to one, so BN≥1B_N\ge1. 5. A compact quartic minimum

Write s=m−1s=m-1, the number of proper vertices, or equivalently edges. Attach a variable wv∈HNw_v\in H_N to each proper vertex’s outgoing edge. Let v0v_0 denote the root’s unique child. The residual at a proper vertex is ℛv(𝒘)={fv−wv,v is a leaf with prescribed packet fv,Jwc−wv,v is a turn with child c,M(wL,wR)−wv,v is a product with children L,R.(16) \mathcal R_v(\boldsymbol w)= \begin{cases} f_v-w_v,&v\text{ is a leaf with prescribed packet }f_v,\\ Jw_c-w_v,&v\text{ is a turn with child }c,\\ M(w_L,w_R)-w_v,&v\text{ is a product with children }L,R. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(16)} Every residual is known explicitly from the original operations. Put cv=BN2rv−1,𝒜N(𝒘)=∑v≠rootcv2∥ℛv(𝒘)∥2,(17) c_v=B_N^{2r_v-1},\qquad \mathcal A_N(\boldsymbol w)=\sum_{v\ne\mathrm{root}} c_v^2\|\mathcal R_v(\boldsymbol w)\|^2, \qquad\text{(17)} 𝒥N(𝒘)=1+|ℓNwv0|2+Ns𝒜N(𝒘),QN=(1+1/N)min∥wv∥2≤BN2𝒥N(𝒘).(18) \mathcal J_N(\boldsymbol w) =1+|\ell_Nw_{v_0}|^2+Ns\mathcal A_N(\boldsymbol w), \qquad \boxed{\quad Q_N=(1+1/N) \min_{\|w_v\|^2\le B_N^2}\mathcal J_N(\boldsymbol w).\quad} \qquad\text{(18)} This is a positive real polynomial problem on 2N(m−1)2N(m-1) real variables. Its degree is at most four: a product residual is bilinear in its two child variables before its squared norm is taken. Its coefficients and ball radii are algebraic at each level. The nonempty compact domain makes the minimum exist. The factor tNt_N from the inner-product construction is not used in (16)–(18).

Theorem. The quartic minimum (18) satisfies (1) for every N≥2N\ge2.

Proof. Let fvf_v be the true open packet of the proper subtree rooted at vv. Repeated Cauchy–Schwarz in its anchored compiler gives ∥(1,fv)∥≤BN\|(1,f_v)\|\le B_N; every omitted vertex norm is at least one. In particular ∥fv∥≤BN\|f_v\|\le B_N. At every product vertex present, ∥M∥op≤BN\|M\|_{\mathrm{ op}}\le B_N; also ∥ℓN∥≤BN\|\ell_N\|\le B_N and ∥J∥op=1\|J\|_{\mathrm{ op}}=1. These bounds are all that the proof uses from the compiler. They can instead be checked directly for any chosen larger bound BN≥1B_N\ge1.

For every allowed collection 𝒘\boldsymbol w, define dv=∥fv−wv∥d_v=\|f_v-w_v\|. At a leaf, dv=∥ℛv∥d_v=\|\mathcal R_v\|. At a turn or product, dv≤∥ℛv∥+BN2∑c child of vdc. d_v\le\|\mathcal R_v\|+B_N^2\sum_{c\text{ child of }v}d_c. For a product, this follows from the exact bilinear identity M(fL,fR)−M(wL,wR)=M(fL−wL,fR)+M(wL,fR−wR) M(f_L,f_R)-M(w_L,w_R) =M(f_L-w_L,f_R)+M(w_L,f_R-w_R) and the bounds ∥fR∥,∥wL∥≤BN\|f_R\|,\|w_L\|\le B_N. Following the unique path from each vertex to the root and using ∥ℓN∥≤BN\|\ell_N\|\le B_N therefore gives |KN−ℓNwv0|≤∑v≠rootBN2rv−1∥ℛv∥≤s𝒜N.(19) |K_N-\ell_Nw_{v_0}| \le\sum_{v\ne\mathrm{root}}B_N^{2r_v-1}\|\mathcal R_v\| \le\sqrt{s\mathcal A_N}. \qquad\text{(19)} The weighted Cauchy inequality (a+b)2≤(1+1/N)(a2+Nb2)(a+b)^2\le(1+1/N)(a^2+Nb^2) for nonnegative a,ba,b now proves 1+|KN|2≤(1+1/N)𝒥N(𝒘)1+|K_N|^2\le(1+1/N)\mathcal J_N(\boldsymbol w). For the reverse bound choose wv=fvw_v=f_v. This is allowed, every residual vanishes, and 𝒥N=1+|KN|2\mathcal J_N=1+|K_N|^2. This proves (1). No step divides by a closed invariant. ▫\square

If two such compilers give the same original KNK_N, then |log⁡QN−log⁡QN′|≤log⁡(1+1/N)≤1/N.(20) |\log Q_N-\log Q_N'|\le\log(1+1/N)\le1/N. \qquad\text{(20)} Both logarithms lie in the same interval of that length. This includes replacing a fixed proper subtree by its full open boundary packet and retaining its original normalization. The raw polynomial objectives need not agree pointwise. A different valid packet bound BNB_N also changes the logarithmic minimum by at most the same amount.

6. The product of local norm factors

The four factors (12)–(15) give a second, less economical minimum. Let ΦN(𝒘)\Phi_N(\boldsymbol w) be their product and define PN=min∥we∥2≤BN2ΦN(𝒘). P_N=\min_{\|w_e\|^2\le B_N^2}\Phi_N(\boldsymbol w). It has the same number of variables as (18), but degree at most 4m4m. It satisfies the exact bounds 1+|KN|2≤PN≤e2/N(1+|KN|2).(21) 1+|K_N|^2\le P_N\le e^{2/N}(1+|K_N|^2). \qquad\text{(21)} For the lower bound, gauges preserve the original output YNY_N, and leaf-by-leaf Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its squared norm by ΦN\Phi_N. For the upper bound, use we=few_e=f_e. All transport residuals vanish. Every original local norm is at most BNB_N, and ∥Mfe∥HS≤∥M∥HS∥fe∥≤BN2\|M_{f_e}\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}\le\|M\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}\|f_e\|\le B_N^2. A leaf factor is one, a turn factor is at most 1+BN2/tN21+B_N^2/t_N^2, and a product factor is at most 1+3BN4/tN21+3B_N^4/t_N^2, because r≥1r\ge1 and tN≥1t_N\ge1. The product lower bound has a polynomial sum-of-squares certificate. Telescoping gives a real polynomial sum-of-squares expression for ΦN(𝒘)−1−|KN|2\Phi_N(\boldsymbol w)-1-|K_N|^2. The entries are polynomials in we,w¯ew_e,\overline w_e with tNt_N fixed. This certifies the finite lower inequality (21); it supplies no geometric lower bound.

7. Growth of the source constants

The elementary identities sd=Ns_d=N and sasd−a=Ns_as_{d-a}=N imply N2−d≤sa≤2dN2^{-d}\le s_a\le2^d. Formula (3) consequently gives the coarse bound μijr≤27d\mu_{ijr}\le2^{7d}, and hence ∥M∥HS≤N3/227d\|M\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}\le N^{3/2}2^{7d}. The crossing leaves in (4) have norm N\sqrt N; the turn is unitary. Together with the known closing row this proves, for each fixed literal construction, log⁡BN=O(N),log⁡tN=O(N).(23) \log B_N=O(N),\qquad\log t_N=O(N). \qquad\text{(23)} Fixed signed powers can first be expanded into signed crossing leaves. The constants may depend on those fixed powers and on the construction.

8. The remaining geometric statement

For any V>0V>0, (1) gives limN→∞πNlog⁡QN=V⇔limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN|=V.(24) \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac\pi N\log Q_N=V \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}N\log|K_N|=V. \qquad\text{(24)} Indeed log⁡(1+|KN|2)\log(1+|K_N|^2) differs from max⁡(0,2log⁡|KN|)\max(0,2\log|K_N|) by at most log⁡2\log2, including zeros with log⁡0=−∞\log0=-\infty. A strictly positive limiting rate removes that maximum and implies eventual nonvanishing.

This construction uses the complete physical packet and an orthogonal constant reference. It requires no estimate for ordinary colors, and no identification of an auxiliary-circle Fourier norm with a main-color norm. All physical normal directions and the original closing row remain. The translations form the additive group of HNH_N, since S(u)S(v)=S(u+v)S(u)S(v)=S(u+v); their square-zero off-diagonal block supplies the elementary boundary centering used in the proof.

To prove the proposed volume theorem one must still compare the leading value of this actual positive polynomial problem with hyperbolic volume. For the finite-exception program, that comparison is needed after every required fixed short partial assignment and sufficiently long remaining fillings which give hyperbolic knots. Fixed short subtrees can be contracted exactly before applying the construction. No geometric comparison, regulator identification, or new volume-equality class is inferred from (24) alone.
