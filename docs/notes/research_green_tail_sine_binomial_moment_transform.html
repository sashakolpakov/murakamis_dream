Exact sine-binomial coefficients and a positive finite-moment law for the original Green tail

31 October 2026. analytical research theorem. This starts from the original nodal tail T_(a,M) in the maximal-Green block transport. It provides an exact positive-moment representation of that signed kernel. It does not bound the signed pairing by an endpoint source or by Phi_D. Every moment and polynomial truncation is explicit.

1. Original node, tail, and normalization

Let N>=3, theta=pi/N, q=exp(i theta), M=floor(N/2)-1, and choose 0<=a<=M. Put

m=M−a,h=a+1,sr=2sin⁡(rθ),dr=aa−ar=sr+a+1sr−a,ar=2+2cos⁡((2r+1)θ). m=M-a,\qquad h=a+1,\qquad s_r=2\sin(r\theta), \qquad d_r=a_a-a_r=s_{r+a+1}s_{r-a}, \qquad a_r=2+2\cos((2r+1)\theta).

The original positive nodal tail is precisely

Ta,M(x,c,y)=∑r=0mcm−rKa,a+r(x,y;−aa)∏i=r+1msis2a+i+1. T_{a,M}(x,c,y)= \sum_{r=0}^m c^{m-r}K_{a,a+r}(x,y;-a_a) \prod_{i=r+1}^m s_i s_{2a+i+1}. \tag{1}

In particular its actual Green occurrence is T_(a,M)(x,-c,y). Nothing changes the original level, unused suffix, node a, or parent pairing [c^M].

Define, on the finite ranges used below,

Ba(l)=∏i=1lsa+isi,Ba(0)=1,Ar=∏i=1rsa+is2a+i+1,A0=1,Da,m=∏i=1msis2a+i+1. \begin{aligned} B_a(l)&=\prod_{i=1}^l\frac{s_{a+i}}{s_i},&B_a(0)&=1,\\ A_r&=\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{s_{a+i}}{s_{2a+i+1}},&A_0&=1,\\ D_{a,m}&=\prod_{i=1}^m s_i s_{2a+i+1}. \end{aligned} \tag{2}

All these factors are strictly positive for 0<=r,l<=2m. Indeed the largest denominator index in A_(2m) is N-1 at even N and N-2 at odd N. The largest numerator index in B_a(2m) is also strictly less than N. Empty products are one.

2. The exact nodal continuant coefficients

Normalize the original interval continuant by

Qr(x,y)=Ka,a+r(x,y;−aa)∏i=1rsa+i2. Q_r(x,y)= \frac{K_{a,a+r}(x,y;-a_a)}{\prod_{i=1}^r s_{a+i}^2}.

The actual nodal recurrence is

Q0=1,Q1=x+y,Qr=(x+y)Qr−1−γrxyQr−2,γr=sr−1s2a+rsa+rsa+r−1,r≥2. \begin{gathered} Q_0=1,\qquad Q_1=x+y,\qquad Q_r=(x+y)Q_{r-1}-\gamma_rxyQ_{r-2},\\ \gamma_r=\frac{s_{r-1}s_{2a+r}}{s_{a+r}s_{a+r-1}}, \qquad r\ge2. \end{gathered} \tag{3}

The gamma identity is the original cofactor identity, not a positivity assumption. The following explicit formula holds:

Qr(x,y)=∑l=0rBa(l)Ba(r−l)Ba(r)xlyr−l,0≤r≤m. Q_r(x,y)=\sum_{l=0}^r \frac{B_a(l)B_a(r-l)}{B_a(r)}x^l y^{r-l}, \qquad 0\le r\le m. \tag{4}

Proof. The endpoint coefficients are one. For an interior l, substitute B_a(n)/B_a(n-1)=s_(a+n)/s_n into the coefficient recurrence (3). After multiplication by the common denominator, the required identity is exactly

srsa+lsa+r−l=sa+r[sr−lsa+l+slsa+r−l]−s2a+rslsr−l. s_r s_{a+l}s_{a+r-l} =s_{a+r}\bigl[s_{r-l}s_{a+l}+s_l s_{a+r-l}\bigr] -s_{2a+r}s_l s_{r-l}. \tag{5}

Expanding each sine into exponentials, or using the product-to-sum identity twice, proves (5). This proves (4) inductively from the literal original recurrence. No identification with an external orthogonality measure or quantum turn is needed.

Insert (4) into (1). The scalar factors telescope:

∏i=1rsa+i2Ba(r)∏i=r+1msis2a+i+1=Da,mAr. \frac{\prod_{i=1}^r s_{a+i}^2}{B_a(r)} \prod_{i=r+1}^m s_i s_{2a+i+1} =D_{a,m}A_r.

Consequently the FULL original tail is

Ta,M(x,c,y)Da,m=∑l,k≥0l+k≤mAl+kBa(l)Ba(k)xlykcm−l−k. \frac{T_{a,M}(x,c,y)}{D_{a,m}} =\sum_{\substack{l,k\ge0\\l+k\le m}} A_{l+k}B_a(l)B_a(k)x^l y^k c^{m-l-k}. \tag{6}

The original negative child character is therefore retained by c->-c; it is not absorbed into an unknown normalization.

3. Exact unit-circle alphabet, on the required finite coefficient range

Let

Wa(x,y)={xqa−2r:0≤r≤a}⊎{yqa−2r:0≤r≤a}. W_a(x,y)= \{xq^{a-2r}:0\le r\le a\} \uplus \{yq^{a-2r}:0\le r\le a\}. \tag{7}

as a MULTISET, so repeated values are retained. For any finite multiset W, define H_r(W) by

∏w∈W(1−wt)−1=∑r≥0Hr(W)tr. \prod_{w\in W}(1-wt)^{-1} =\sum_{r\ge0}H_r(W)t^r.

This is a formal rational identity, with no convergence claim. The finite sine-binomial identity is

∏r=0a(1−qa−2rt)−1=∑l≥0bltl,bl=Ba(l)(0≤l≤2m). \prod_{r=0}^a(1-q^{a-2r}t)^{-1} =\sum_{l\ge0}b_l t^l, \qquad b_l=B_a(l)\quad(0\le l\le2m). \tag{8}

For clarity, the sine quotient is asserted ONLY before the denominator reaches a sine zero. The rational series defines b_l at all higher degrees; a literal sine quotient need not do so.

One elementary proof of (8) is induction on a using the actual geometric progression of the variables: separating q^a gives b_l(a)=qa b_(l-1)(a)+q(-l)b_l^(a-1). The product sine expression in (2) satisfies this relation by the finite geometric-series identity. Equivalently, multiplying the rational series by its denominator verifies its coefficients up to degree 2m, where every sine division is legitimate.

Thus on the required range,

Hr(Wa(x,y))=∑l=0rBa(l)Ba(r−l)xlyr−l. H_r(W_a(x,y)) =\sum_{l=0}^r B_a(l)B_a(r-l)x^l y^{r-l}. \tag{9}

The unit-circle phases in (7) are exact original-level phases. Their existence alone does not identify a positive inner product on the shared face coefficient or justify a physical turn.

4. A positive beta-product law with the exact moments

There exists a positive random variable X_(N,a) such that

𝔼XN,ar=Ar,0≤r≤2m. \mathbb E X_{N,a}^{\,r}=A_r,\qquad 0\le r\le2m. \tag{10}

Its construction retains all original sine factors. Use h=a+1 and take mutually independent variables

B0∼Beta⁡(h,h),Vk∼Beta⁡(kN+h,h),Uk∼Beta⁡(kN−2h+1,h),k≥1. B_0\sim\operatorname{Beta}(h,h),\qquad V_k\sim\operatorname{Beta}(kN+h,h),\qquad U_k\sim\operatorname{Beta}(kN-2h+1,h),\qquad k\ge1. \tag{11}

All shape parameters are positive. Define the partial products

XK=B0∏k=1KVkUk. X_K=B_0\prod_{k=1}^K\frac{V_k}{U_k}.

For real s in the open interval

−h<s<N−2h+1. -h<s<N-2h+1. \tag{12}

the beta integral gives

𝔼B0s=Γ(h+s)Γ(2h)Γ(h)Γ(2h+s),𝔼(Vk/Uk)s=Γ(kN+h+s)Γ(kN+2h)Γ(kN+h)Γ(kN+2h+s)⋅Γ(kN−2h+1−s)Γ(kN−h+1)Γ(kN−2h+1)Γ(kN−h+1−s). \begin{aligned} \mathbb E B_0^s &=\frac{\Gamma(h+s)\Gamma(2h)} {\Gamma(h)\Gamma(2h+s)},\\ \mathbb E(V_k/U_k)^s &=\frac{\Gamma(kN+h+s)\Gamma(kN+2h)} {\Gamma(kN+h)\Gamma(kN+2h+s)}\\ &\quad\cdot \frac{\Gamma(kN-2h+1-s)\Gamma(kN-h+1)} {\Gamma(kN-2h+1)\Gamma(kN-h+1-s)}. \end{aligned} \tag{13}

At an integer r, Euler’s sine product yields

Ar=(h)r(2h)r∏k≥1∏i=0r−1kN−h−ikN−2h−ikN+h+ikN+2h+i. A_r=\frac{(h)_r}{(2h)_r} \prod_{k\ge1}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1} \frac{kN-h-i}{kN-2h-i} \frac{kN+h+i}{kN+2h+i}. \tag{14}

Here (h)_r denotes the rising factorial. This is precisely the moment of X_K in the limit, with the two factors in (13). No square is present here; this is a different law from the earlier protected-cap moment.

For completeness, convergence is not merely formal. For fixed N,a and s in a compact subinterval of (12), the logarithm of the k-th factor in (13) is O_s((kN)^(-2)); its first two derivatives in s obey the same bound. The two potential terms of order 1/(kN) cancel. Hence

∑k|𝔼log(Vk/Uk)|<∞,∑kVar⁡(log⁡(Vk/Uk))<∞. \sum_k\left|\mathbb E\log(V_k/U_k)\right|<\infty, \qquad \sum_k\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\log(V_k/U_k)\bigr)<\infty.

The centered log series converges in L2, and its means converge absolutely. Thus X_K converges in probability to a positive finite X_(N,a). For any integer 0<=r<=2m choose epsilon>0 with r+epsilon<N-2h+1. Formula (13) and its convergent product give sup_K E X_K^(r+epsilon)<infinity. Uniform integrability then gives E X_(N,a)^r=lim_K E X_K^r, proving (10).

The same argument gives the EXACT Mellin transform for every real s in (12), not only the integers:

MN,a(s):=𝔼XN,as=𝔼B0s∏k≥1𝔼(Vk/Uk)s. M_{N,a}(s):=\mathbb E X_{N,a}^{\,s} =\mathbb E B_0^s\prod_{k\ge1}\mathbb E(V_k/U_k)^s. \tag{14a}

with the factors in (13). For s!=0 choose p>1 sufficiently close to 1 that ps is still in (12). The convergent gamma product at ps bounds E|(X_K)s|p uniformly. This proves uniform integrability also for negative s, since X_K converges in probability to a strictly positive X. The value at s=0 is one.

Gamma recursion followed by Euler’s sine product gives the exact successive-Mellin ratio

MN,a(s+1)MN,a(s)=sin⁡((h+s)θ)sin⁡((2h+s)θ). \frac{M_{N,a}(s+1)}{M_{N,a}(s)} =\frac{\sin((h+s)\theta)}{\sin((2h+s)\theta)}. \tag{14b}

whenever both s and s+1 lie in (12). In this domain both sine arguments lie strictly between 0 and pi. This is a consequence of the explicit Mellin product (14a), not an assertion that a shift equation alone determines a measure.

There is a stronger FINITE simplification because h=a+1 is an integer. Finite gamma recursion in each factor of (13), followed by Euler’s sine product, gives

MN,a(s)=∏j=h2h−1sin⁡(jθ)sin⁡((j+s)θ). M_{N,a}(s)= \prod_{j=h}^{2h-1} \frac{\sin(j\theta)}{\sin((j+s)\theta)}. \tag{14c}

for every real s in (12). Explicitly, the base gamma ratio becomes product_j j/(j+s), and the k-th pair becomes product_j [(kN+j)(kN-j)]/[(kN+j+s)(kN-j-s)]. These are exactly the Euler factors in (14c). For integer r the same identity is also immediate by telescoping the two length-r sine products.

In particular an alternative exact realization needs only h independent variables: for h<=j<=2h-1 let Y_j have density

sin⁡(πj/N)πyj/N−11+ydy,y>0,XN,a=∏j=h2h−1Yj1/N. \frac{\sin(\pi j/N)}{\pi}\, \frac{y^{j/N-1}}{1+y}\,dy,\qquad y>0, \qquad X_{N,a}=\prod_{j=h}^{2h-1}Y_j^{1/N}. \tag{14d}

Every j/N lies strictly between zero and one. The Euler beta integral proves that the density is normalized and that E Y_j^(s/N)=sin(j theta)/sin((j+s)theta) whenever -j<s<N-j. Intersecting these intervals gives exactly (12). Thus (14d) proves (14c) and all finite moments directly. It has the same law as the earlier construction, since their log-variable moment-generating functions agree on an interval about zero. Either realization may be used in the finite identities below.

The available upper threshold is EXACTLY

N−2h+1={2m+1,N even,2m+2,N odd. N-2h+1= \begin{cases} 2m+1,&N\text{ even},\\ 2m+2,&N\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \tag{15}

Thus every moment needed for the degree-m kernel, even its squared finite polynomial, is justified. No moment beyond this domain is asserted or used.

5. Exact signed kernel and original boundary pairing

Combining (6), (9), and (10) gives the finite identity

Ta,M(x,c,y)=Da,m𝔼Hm({c}⊎XN,aWa(x,y)). T_{a,M}(x,c,y) =D_{a,m}\,\mathbb E H_m\bigl(\{c\}\uplus X_{N,a}W_a(x,y)\bigr). \tag{16}

Here H_m is the complete homogeneous polynomial of degree m, not an untruncated rational function. The original signed tail is

Ta,M(x,−c,y)=Da,m𝔼Hm({−c}⊎XN,aWa(x,y)). T_{a,M}(x,-c,y) =D_{a,m}\,\mathbb E H_m\bigl(\{-c\}\uplus X_{N,a}W_a(x,y)\bigr). \tag{17}

Let the actual CROSS spectator product retain its original shared variables and write

PN(c,Y)=∑sps(Y)cs,P≤M(c,Y)=∑s=0Mps(Y)cs. P_N(c,Y)=\sum_s p_s(Y)c^s,\qquad P_{\le M}(c,Y)=\sum_{s=0}^M p_s(Y)c^s.

Then EXACTLY

[cM]Ta,M(x,−c,y)PN(c,Y)=Da,m(−1)m𝔼∑r=0mHr(−XN,aWa(x,y))pa+r(Y). [c^M]T_{a,M}(x,-c,y)P_N(c,Y) =D_{a,m}(-1)^m\,\mathbb E \sum_{r=0}^m H_r\bigl(-X_{N,a}W_a(x,y)\bigr)p_{a+r}(Y). \tag{18}

The actual factor R_a(x,b,y;-a_a) is to be multiplied afterward, as in the original Q_(a,N). Equation (18) uses precisely the original face demand M in the reduced block, hence the original d demand before the already proved lower-block factorization. The only spectator coefficients used are a<=s<=M. Nothing here permits changing a, shifting a neighboring demand, or assigning independent radii to shared faces.

Every random power in (16)-(18) is at most m. The sum is finite, so extraction and expectation may be interchanged. For a nontrivial cap component the existing independent signed path-dilation averages may be applied coefficientwise first; the same total-degree truncation remains mandatory.

The formal generating representation behind (16) is useful but must remain a FINITE coefficient statement. The integral of product_(w in W)(1-Xwt)^(-1) is not automatically a holomorphic function near t=0, and may have actual poles for real t. For example if a is even, W contains x and y themselves; X has unbounded support, so positive real t can meet a denominator zero. No untruncated rational integral, missing moment, or absolute contraction is inferred from (16).

6. Exact limitation of copying the protected-cap tail argument

Unlike the earlier cap law, this law does not have a uniformly bounded last available even moment. Put S_j=product_(r=1)^j s_r. The elementary reflection S_(N-1-r)=N/S_r gives

A2m={S2h−1ShSh−1,N even,s1S2h−1Sh+1Sh−1,N odd. A_{2m}= \begin{cases} \dfrac{S_{2h-1}}{S_hS_{h-1}},&N\text{ even},\\[6pt] \dfrac{s_1S_{2h-1}}{S_{h+1}S_{h-1}},&N\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \tag{19}

These are exact, including m=0. If h/N->alpha in (0,.5), uniform sine-product Riemann sums give

limN→∞1Nlog⁡A2m=∫02αlog⁡(2sin⁡(πt))dt−2∫0αlog⁡(2sin⁡(πt))dt=2∫0αlog⁡(2cos⁡(πt))dt>0. \begin{aligned} \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log A_{2m} &=\int_0^{2\alpha}\log(2\sin(\pi t))\,dt -2\int_0^\alpha\log(2\sin(\pi t))\,dt\\ &=2\int_0^\alpha\log(2\cos(\pi t))\,dt>0. \end{aligned} \tag{20}

The final strict inequality is elementary: log(2cos(pi t)) decreases, crosses zero once at 1/3, and its integral from 0 to 1/2 is zero. Its partial integral is therefore strictly positive at every interior alpha.

Thus the bounded-last-moment estimate used by the protected-cap shared-star proof cannot simply be imported at a macroscopic Green node. This is an exact obstruction to that proposed proof step, not a counterexample to the signed pairing estimate. The signed expression (18), including its original source coefficients, can still be much smaller than any positive moment or norm estimate.

Provenance and scope

The original K, T, and lower-block factors are those of the original maximal-Green block transport sections 4–6. Equation (3) is its original normalized nodal recurrence. The positive-product construction is derived explicitly here rather than identified with the previous cap metric.

The result is an exact finite algebraic and moment identity. It supplies neither a polynomial bound for the remaining operator nor a shared capacity or filling estimate.
