A nonzero initializer from the actual charge normal

Research proof, 23 November 2026.

The first charge normal of the original analytic Hermite coupon gives an explicit nonzero asymptotic at zero charge. This is a derivative of the actual finite source, obtained directly from its physical nodes. Its exponential rate is the complete-edge rate, independent of the fixed signed crossing powers. It is an inserted observation, not the original unit coupon or a lower bound for the original knot invariant.

1. The original finite source and the assertion

Fix an integer N≥2N\ge2, put d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and

η=(−1)d,PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,ϕN(f)=ηN2[zd]rem⁡PNf.(1) \eta=(-1)^d,\qquad P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\qquad \phi_N(f)=\eta N^2[z^d]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}f. \qquad\text{(1)}

Here TNT_N is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. Use the full algebra AN=ℂ[z]/PNA_N=\mathbb C[z]/P_N, including its repeated-root normals, and the original Newton polynomials and edge kernel of [S]:

Sa=∏j=1a2sin⁡(πj/N),S0=1,ea(z)=Sa−2∏j<a(2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N)−z), S_a=\prod_{j=1}^a2\sin(\pi j/N),\quad S_0=1,\quad e_a(z)=S_a^{-2}\prod_{j<a}(2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N)-z), KN(t,u)=∑a=0dea(−2cos⁡t)ea(−2cos⁡u).(2) K_N(t,u)=\sum_{a=0}^{d}e_a(-2\cos t)e_a(-2\cos u). \qquad\text{(2)}

Let 𝒞N,χ\mathcal C_{N,\chi} be the actual entire Hermite family of [D, Section 9]. At a double root cτ=−2cos⁡τc_\tau=-2\cos\tau, 0<τ<π0<\tau<\pi, its complete data are

𝒞N,χ(cτ)=cos⁡(N(1−χ)τ),𝒞N,χ′(cτ)=−N(1−χ)sin⁡(N(1−χ)τ)2sin⁡τ.(3) \mathcal C_{N,\chi}(c_\tau)=\cos(N(1-\chi)\tau),\qquad \mathcal C_{N,\chi}'(c_\tau) =-\frac{N(1-\chi)\sin(N(1-\chi)\tau)}{2\sin\tau}. \qquad\text{(3)}

At the odd-level simple root −2-2, its value is one. Define

𝔡N=∂χ𝒞N,χ|0.(4) \mathfrak d_N=\left.\partial_\chi \mathcal C_{N,\chi}\right|_0. \qquad\text{(4)}

This notation does not denote a modified quantum dimension.

Let Γ=(V,E)\Gamma=(V,E) be a fixed finite undirected graph carrying exactly the same-index kernels (2). Repeated edges and loops are allowed. Choose a nonempty set I⊆VI\subseteq V of selected vertices, none isolated. Put m=|I|m=|I|, e=|E|e=|E|, and let dv≥1d_v\ge1 be the original degree of v∈Iv\in I, counting a loop twice. Every unselected boundary angle is fixed at zero. Fix integers nvn_v, of either sign or zero, before taking N→∞N\to\infty.

Use the original crossing packet RN=qd2/2∑a=0d(−q)aeaR_N=q^{d^2/2}\sum_{a=0}^d(-q)^a e_a, with the displayed exponential meaning of its fractional power, and retain all its original phases. Its nodal value, in the normalization of [S, Sections 3 and 7], is

RN(ct)n=CN,neinNt2/(4π),CN,n=exp⁡(πin(N−1)24N).(5) R_N(c_t)^{n}=C_{N,n}e^{inNt^2/(4\pi)},\qquad C_{N,n}=\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi i n(N-1)^2}{4N}\right). \qquad\text{(5)}

Products and negative powers are taken in the full original algebra. Define the actual normal source

ZNnor=(∏v∈IϕN(v))[∏v∈IRN(zv)nv𝔡N(zv)∏vw∈EKN(tv,tw)],zv=−2cos⁡tv.(6) Z_N^{\mathrm{ nor}}= \left(\prod_{v\in I}\phi_N^{(v)}\right) \left[\prod_{v\in I}R_N(z_v)^{n_v}\mathfrak d_N(z_v) \prod_{vw\in E}K_N(t_v,t_w)\right], \qquad z_v=-2\cos t_v. \qquad\text{(6)}

For a loop the factor is KN(tv,tv)K_N(t_v,t_v). No outside polynomial is reduced, and no semisimple replacement precedes (6).

2. The charge derivative retains exactly the nodal values

At each double root, cos⁡(Nτ)=η\cos(N\tau)=\eta and sin⁡(Nτ)=0\sin(N\tau)=0. Differentiating the actual data (3), rather than a lattice asymptotic, gives

𝔡N(cτ)=0,𝔡N′(cτ)=ηN2τ2sin⁡τ,PN″(cτ)=N22sin⁡2τ.(8) \mathfrak d_N(c_\tau)=0,\qquad \mathfrak d_N'(c_\tau)=\frac{\eta N^2\tau}{2\sin\tau},\qquad P_N''(c_\tau)=\frac{N^2}{2\sin^2\tau}. \qquad\text{(8)}

The normal’s value at the odd simple root is zero. Since PNP_N is monic of degree NN, the top coefficient of a remainder equals the sum of the finite residues of f/PNf/P_N. For any polynomial EE, put f=𝔡NEf=\mathfrak d_NE. The residue at a double root is 2𝔡N′(c)E(c)/PN″(c)2\mathfrak d_N'(c)E(c)/P_N''(c). Consequently the exact full-algebra identity is

ϕN(𝔡NE)=2N2∑τ∈𝒯Nτsin⁡τE(cτ),𝒯N={π(N−1−2l)N:0≤l<⌊N/2⌋}.(9) \phi_N(\mathfrak d_NE) =2N^2\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T_N}\tau\sin\tau\,E(c_\tau), \quad \mathcal T_N=\left\{\frac{\pi(N-1-2l)}N: 0\le l<\lfloor N/2\rfloor\right\}. \qquad\text{(9)}

Thus 𝔡N2=0\mathfrak d_N^2=0 in ANA_N, while ϕN(𝔡Nf¯f)≥0\phi_N(\mathfrak d_N\overline f f)\ge0; conjugation fixes the real variable zz. These are consequences of the original first normals. They do not discard those normals from (6).

Enumerate the distinct double nodes as cl=2cos⁡((2l+1)π/N)c_l=2\cos((2l+1)\pi/N), 0≤l<r=⌊N/2⌋0\le l<r=\lfloor N/2\rfloor. They are strictly decreasing. The literal factors in (2) show

ea(cl)>0(a≤l),ea(cl)=0(a>l),KN(cl,cj)=∑a≤min⁡(l,j)ea(cl)ea(cj)>0.(10) e_a(c_l)>0\ (a\le l),\qquad e_a(c_l)=0\ (a>l),\qquad K_N(c_l,c_j)=\sum_{a\le\min(l,j)}e_a(c_l)e_a(c_j)>0. \qquad\text{(10)}

Here the notation KN(cl,cj)K_N(c_l,c_j) means evaluation in its spectral arguments. In particular every row a≥ra\ge r vanishes at the retained nodes. At odd levels this also kills a=(N−1)/2a=(N-1)/2, whose only other nodal value lies at the simple root. This does not assert that its double-root normals vanish. Formula (9) now converts (6) exactly to a finite nodal sum with weights 2N2tsin⁡t2N^2t\sin t, kernels (10), and the phases (5).

3. A global nodal tail bound

For a physical node ct=−2cos⁡tc_t=-2\cos t, a nonzero Newton value obeys

0≤ea(ct)ea(−2)=∏j<a(1−1−cos⁡t1+cos⁡((2j+1)π/N))≤e−a(1−cos⁡t)/2.(11) 0\le\frac{e_a(c_t)}{e_a(-2)} =\prod_{j<a}\left(1- \frac{1-\cos t}{1+\cos((2j+1)\pi/N)}\right) \le e^{-a(1-\cos t)/2}. \qquad\text{(11)}

If a numerator is zero the same inequality remains true. A zero denominator can occur only beyond the surviving rows at odd levels; those values already vanish. At a complete boundary t=0t=0, the ratio is one wherever defined.

For completeness, the required complete-index estimates are elementary. Set

b(x)=2∫0xlog⁡(cot⁡πs)ds.(12) b(x)=2\int_0^x\log(\cot\pi s)\,ds. \qquad\text{(12)}

On compact subintervals of 0<x<1/20<x<1/2, midpoint summation in the cosine numerator and Stirling’s formula in the sine denominator give

ea(−2)=eNb(a/N)2Nsin⁡(πa/N)(1+O(N−1)).(13) e_a(-2)=\frac{e^{Nb(a/N)}}{2N\sin(\pi a/N)}(1+O(N^{-1})). \qquad\text{(13)}

One obtains its uniform logarithmic version down to a=0a=0 by separating log⁡(2sin⁡πs)=log⁡s+log⁡(2sin⁡πs/s)\log(2\sin\pi s)=\log s+\log(2\sin\pi s/s). The latter summand is smooth at zero and the former is handled by Stirling. The cosine summand is smooth on every such lower interval. The function bb increases to its unique maximum at 1/41/4, then decreases; 2b(1/4)=M∞2b(1/4)=M_\infty.

Fix, for example, ϵ=1/10\epsilon=1/10. Equations (12)–(13) imply ∑a<ϵNea(−2)2≤CNAeN(M∞−δ)\sum_{a<\epsilon N}e_a(-2)^2\le CN^A e^{N(M_\infty-\delta)} for some δ>0\delta>0. Also [S, (18)] gives KN(0,0)≤CNAeNM∞K_N(0,0)\le CN^A e^{NM_\infty}. Applying (11) to the remaining indices, and using (1−cos⁡t)+(1−cos⁡u)≤4(1-\cos t)+(1-\cos u)\le4, proves for all physical or complete endpoints

0≤KN(t,u)≤CNAeN{M∞−c[(1−cos⁡t)+(1−cos⁡u)]}(14) 0\le K_N(t,u)\le CN^A e^{N\{M_\infty-c[(1-\cos t)+(1-\cos u)]\}} \qquad\text{(14)}

with some fixed c>0c>0. Thus the whole nodal sum outside any fixed small angle box is exponentially smaller than eNeM∞e^{NeM_\infty}. Each selected angle receives its original degree dv≥1d_v\ge1. The weights in (9) are polynomial in NN, and the nodal crossing values have modulus one. This also controls every loop and repeated edge, without dropping a physical row or endpoint current.

4. The half-Gaussian coefficient in both parities

The full local kernel theorem imported in [S, (18)] has its original separately even phase and amplitude. In particular, near zero,

KN(t,u)=N−3/2a(t,u)eNψ(t,u)(1+O(N−1)),a(0,0)=14,ψ=M∞−t2+u24π+O((t2+u2)2).(15) K_N(t,u)=N^{-3/2}a(t,u)e^{N\psi(t,u)}(1+O(N^{-1})), \quad a(0,0)=\tfrac14,\quad \psi=M_\infty-\frac{t^2+u^2}{4\pi}+O((t^2+u^2)^2). \qquad\text{(15)}

The amplitude calibration can also be checked directly from (13). The phase 2b2b has second derivative −8π-8\pi at 1/41/4, so real Laplace summation of ea(−2)2e_a(-2)^2 gives KN(0,0)∼14N−3/2eNM∞K_N(0,0)\sim\tfrac14N^{-3/2}e^{NM_\infty}. The exact consecutive-product ratio makes the low sequence unimodal, so its omitted shoulders have a strict gap. At odd levels the high values are zero. At even levels, putting b=d−a<N/2b=d-a<N/2, reflection gives ea(−2)=[N2eb(−2)cos⁡2(π(b+1/2)/N)]−1≤1e_a(-2)=[N^2e_b(-2)\cos^2(\pi(b+1/2)/N)]^{-1}\le1 for a≥N/2a\ge N/2. Thus no additional leading coefficient is hidden in that calibration.

Put xv=Ntvx_v=\sqrt N\,t_v in the exact nodal sum. The crossing phase and all incident quadratic terms give exp⁡[−(dv−inv)xv2/(4π)]\exp[-(d_v-in_v)x_v^2/(4\pi)] at vertex vv. Furthermore tsin⁡t=t2(1+O(t2))t\sin t=t^2(1+O(t^2)).

The positive grid has spacing 2π/N2\pi/N. For even NN it is the positive midpoint grid; for odd NN it is the positive integer grid, with its zero point absent and of zero weight. Every factor is separately even. Reflection therefore converts either grid to half a full Gaussian lattice. The full-lattice rectangle or midpoint rule has error O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) at spacing 2π/N2\pi/\sqrt N: apply the cellwise second-derivative remainder to a Gaussian times a fixed polynomial.

Taylor’s formula in (15), followed by Gaussian moment bounds and (14), gives the same relative error for the actual kernel product. For instance first restrict to |xv|≤N1/12|x_v|\le N^{1/12}; the error is bounded by N−1N^{-1} times a fixed polynomial in the xvx_v, against a decaying Gaussian. The remaining scaled angles have exponentially small Gaussian tails by (14). These arguments apply to complex Gaussians because ℜ(dv−inv)=dv>0\Re(d_v-in_v)=d_v>0.

Each vertex’s coefficient is consequently

2N2N2πN−3/2∫0∞x2e−(dv−inv)x2/(4π)dx=2πN3/2(dv−inv)−3/2.(16) 2N^2\frac{N}{2\pi}N^{-3/2} \int_0^\infty x^2e^{-(d_v-in_v)x^2/(4\pi)}\,dx =2\pi N^{3/2}(d_v-in_v)^{-3/2}. \qquad\text{(16)}

Multiplying (16), the edge factors 4−eN−3e/24^{-e}N^{-3e/2}, and the exact phase εN\varepsilon_N proves (7).

5. Original-source meaning and continuation limits

If ZN(𝝌)Z_N(\boldsymbol\chi) denotes (6) with each 𝔡N\mathfrak d_N replaced by its actual coupon, finite Hermite interpolation gives the exact identity

ZNnor=(∏v∈I∂χv)ZN(𝝌)|𝟎.(17) Z_N^{\mathrm{ nor}}= \left.\left(\prod_{v\in I}\partial_{\chi_v}\right) Z_N(\boldsymbol\chi)\right|_{\boldsymbol0}. \qquad\text{(17)}

No lattice-only charge asymptotic was differentiated. The uniform zero-face theorem [Z] instead divides a specified principal Fourier interpolation; it expressly distinguishes that interpolation from the whole off-lattice coupon source. Formula (7) evaluates the actual normal observation (17), with arbitrary fixed signed powers.

However 𝒞N,0=η1\mathcal C_{N,0}=\eta\,1 and 𝒞N,1=1\mathcal C_{N,1}=1. Thus the full source is not divisible by the charges. Its all-zero value remains ηm\eta^m times the uninserted source. Subtraction or antisymmetrization would change the observation; in particular 𝒞N,1−𝒞N,−1=0\mathcal C_{N,1}-\mathcal C_{N,-1}=0. The positive form in (9) also does not define a quotient preserved by Newton reversal Jea=ed−aJe_a=e_{d-a}, the original turn: its null space contains ede_d, while Jed=e0Je_d=e_0 is not null. For example, a reversed edge ∑aea(cl)ed−a(cj)\sum_a e_a(c_l)e_{d-a}(c_j) vanishes at every pair of double nodes: nonzero factors would require both a<ra<r and d−a<rd-a<r, which is impossible. Such a retained edge is outside theorem (7).

Accordingly (7) supplies a nonzero inserted initializer at the complete edge rate eM∞eM_\infty. For a general graph this has not been identified with a manifold volume. It gives no source-preserving continuation to the unit coupon, no control of transported cuts or relative-cycle coefficients, and no filled-volume or original-knot lower bound.
