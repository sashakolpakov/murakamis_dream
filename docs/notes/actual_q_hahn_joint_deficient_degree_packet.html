A joint complex packet in the deficient geometric chamber

30 October 2026. The actual monic q-Hahn numerator has an N-independent joint complex-degree and magnetic corridor around the real deficient frame. Both its selected root amplitude and its genuine generic-q numerator derivatives are controlled there. The proof uses the original finite determinant, an explicitly bounded low block, and a positive tail matrix.

This does not continue the full exceptional operator’s generic boundary factors or select a nonzero global period. The original forbidden integer-degree slices remain identically zero.

1. The finite determinant and the joint domain

Fix

0<sigma_*<kappa<alpha_0<1/2.

Put theta=pi/N, a/N=alpha in [alpha_0,1/2], with the actual integer channel 0<=a<=floor((N-1)/2). The degree t=N sigma may be complex. Use the original root monic numerator R_a(z;c) from the joint source theorem, with

c=-cos(pi(sigma+1/N)), C=-c,
d_n=1/[cos(n theta)cos((n+1)theta)],
b_n=sin(n theta)/[cos(n theta)
          sqrt(sin((2n-1)theta)sin((2n+1)theta))].        (1)

Its exact recurrence is

R_0=1, R_1=z-C d_0,
R_(n+1)=(z-C d_n)R_n
               -b_n^2[C^2-cos^2(n theta)]R_(n-1).       (2)

No physical integer-degree assumption is used in (2): it is the already proved polynomial identity for the literal finite Newton numerator, for every complex degree.

For each fixed sufficiently small angle vartheta>0 there are epsilon>0 and R>1, independent of N and a, such that R_a has no zeros on the PRODUCT domain

|sigma-sigma_*|<epsilon,
Omega_R={z: |z|>R,
               vartheta<Arg z<2pi-vartheta}.             (3)

Here Arg is the logarithm cut on the positive real axis. Both epsilon and R may depend on vartheta; R can be chosen O(1/sin vartheta), while epsilon can be chosen proportional to vartheta. All statements below concern sufficiently large N and fixed compact subsets of (3), uniformly in the allowed discrete channels.

2. A positive tail plus a bounded error

Choose m=floor(kappa N), so 0<m<a for large N. In the determinant matrix with characteristic polynomial (2), take indices 0,…,m-1 as a low block. Its diagonal entries are C d_n; take its upper entries to be 1 and its lower entries to be b_n2(C2-cos^2(n theta)). These entries and their C derivatives are uniformly bounded. Indeed n/N<=kappa+o(1)<1/2, and

b_n cos^2(n theta)
   =sin(2n theta)/[2 sqrt(sin((2n-1)theta)
                              sin((2n+1)theta))]<=1.     (4)

The last inequality holds throughout the used range, for N>=5: sin(2n theta)>=sin(2theta), and sin((2n-1)theta)sin((2n+1)theta) =sin^2(2n theta)-sin^2(theta). Thus the low block J_L has operator norm at most a constant L, independent of N.

On the tail m,…,a-1 choose the analytic square root sqrt(C2-cos2(n theta)) positive at C=cos(pi sigma_), and take the symmetric adjacent entry b_n times this square root. All these branches exist on a fixed C disk: kappa>sigma_ separates every radicand uniformly from zero. Let D_T be the principal tail submatrix of the positive matrix whose diagonal is d_n and whose adjacent entries are b_n. Its positivity is already proved by the exact principal minors in the joint source determinant theorem. The tail matrix is EXACTLY

J_T(C)=C D_T+E_T(C),
(E_T)_(n-1,n)=(E_T)_(n,n-1)
   =-b_n cos^2(n theta)
              /[C+sqrt(C^2-cos^2(n theta))],              (5)

with zero diagonal. Formula (4) and the strict cutoff show

||E_T(C)||+||partial_C E_T(C)||<=E_0.                     (6)

The same conclusion for every fixed C derivative follows on a slightly smaller disk. No lower eigenvalue bound for D_T, and no uniform bound on its largest eigenvalue, is required.

Keep the interface upper entry equal to 1 and the lower entry gamma_m=b_m2(C2-cos^2(m theta)), which is uniformly bounded. The resulting tridiagonal determinant is exactly R_a by (2). This bounded nonsymmetric low block avoids choosing square roots at the moving integer-degree zero of a recurrence coefficient.

If C stays in a sufficiently small disk about cos(pi sigma_*), and z is in (3), then

dist(z,C[0,infinity))>=c |z| sin(vartheta).                (7)

The inverse of zI-C D_T therefore has norm at most the reciprocal of the right side. Choosing R sufficiently large, (6) gives the same bound, up to a factor two, for (zI-J_T)^-1. The low inverse has norm at most (|z|-L)^-1. Its Schur complement changes the tail by the rank-one matrix

gamma_m [(zI-J_L)^-1]_(m-1,m-1) e_0 e_0^T.              (8)

Its norm is O(1/|z|). A second Neumann estimate proves that the FULL determinant is nonzero and that its displayed tridiagonal matrix M has

||M^-1||<=C_vartheta/|z|.                               (9)

This proves (3) using the actual finite determinant, including both parities and the folded endpoint.

3. Joint logarithmic bounds and the source-selected amplitude

Fix a negative reference z_=-R_ with R_>R. The product domain (3) is simply connected. Define the logarithm of R_a(z)/R_a(z_) to be zero at z_*, for every sigma. Then

|partial_z Log R_a|+|partial_sigma Log R_a|<=C_K N         (10)

on each fixed compact K, with analogous normalized higher derivative bounds on smaller compacts.

For completeness the large D_T causes no loss in (10). The Schur tail is S=zI-C D_T-E_T-gamma e_0e_0^T, with gamma the bounded expression in (8), and

C S^-1 D_T=z S^-1-I-S^-1 E_T
                           -gamma S^-1 e_0e_0^T.         (11)

Thus its trace against a C derivative is O(N) by (6),(9). The low-block derivative is bounded; gamma and its derivatives are bounded by the low resolvent formula. This proves (10).

There are functions S_(sigma,alpha)(z), A_(sigma,alpha)(z), holomorphic jointly in sigma and z on (3), with S(z_)=0, A(z_)=1 and A nowhere zero, such that

R_a(z;c)/R_a(z_*;c)
   =A_(sigma,alpha)(z) exp[N S_(sigma,alpha)(z)]
                                         (1+O(N^-1)).    (12)

The error and its fixed derivatives are uniform on compacts. The phase and amplitude depend smoothly on the real channel parameter alpha, which always remains the ACTUAL a/N.

The anchor is selected by an exact source identity, not by a choice of an algebraic root. For every complex sigma put

t_hi=N(1-sigma)-2,
u_hi=t_hi/2+iN log(R_*)/(2pi),
beta_a=s_(2a)/s_a^2.

The reflection R_a(z;-c)=(-1)^a R_a(-z;c) and the original ordinary normalization give exactly

R_a(-R_*;c)=(-1)^a beta_a^-1
   product_(r=0)^(a-1)2sin(pi(t_hi-r)/N)
   *q^[-(N-1)u_hi-(a^2-(N-1)a)/2]
   *fhat_a(t_hi,u_hi).                                 (13)

This is in the already proved JOINT high-degree physical packet: Re(t_hi/N)>1/2, its real magnetic coordinate is centered, and the complete-channel margins follow from alpha_0>sigma_. Its two wire coordinates remain strictly inside their domains for a small fixed complex sigma disk. The offset -2, the actual half-integer center when present, and every phase in (13) remain literal. All sine factors have a fixed distance from their zeros. Consequently (13) supplies a nonzero relative absolute anchor, jointly in sigma, by that ordinary packet and an elementary finite-product Euler–Maclaurin estimate. It also does so on an open interval of negative z around z_.

The bounds (10) give a normal family for the normalized logarithms. Every limit agrees on that negative interval with the anchor’s source-selected leading phase. The identity theorem fixes it on Omega_R, for each complex sigma; compactness makes convergence uniform jointly in sigma and the real parameter alpha. There is no interpolation from integer degree samples in this argument.

Here are explicit transport equations proving the relative error. Put D=z partial_z, kappa_q=2pi i,

U=exp(pi i sigma)/z, T=exp(2pi i sigma),
J=exp(2pi i alpha).

The original finite numerator satisfies the exact root equation

(QU-1)(U-T)R(z/Q)+(QT-U)(1-U)R(Qz)
  +[2U+2QTU-(1+Q)U^2-T(Q^-a+Q^(a+1))]R(z)=0.           (14)

Set A0=(U-1)(U-T), C0=2U(1+T-U)-T(J+J^-1) and

H(p,z)=A0(e^(kappa_q p)+e^(-kappa_q p))+C0.

The selected p=D S satisfies H=0. Its discriminant is

C0^2-4A0^2=-16T^2 sin^2(pi alpha) W/z^2,
W=1-2cos(pi sigma)z+cos^2(pi alpha)z^2.                   (15)

All roots of W remain in the excluded small positive sector. Indeed write C_s=cos(pi sigma), b=cos(pi alpha), and use the analytic sqrt(C_s2-b2) positive at the real center. The roots are 1/(C_s+sqrt(C_s2-b2)) and (C_s+sqrt(C_s2-b2))/b^2, with the second omitted at b=0. The gap alpha_0>sigma_* gives uniform branches and a uniform argument bound, even as b tends to zero. Shrink epsilon if necessary. Also A0 has zeros only near the unit circle, outside Omega_R. Hence H_p is nonzero throughout the domain.

The exact first transport term is

H1=kappa_q[U(U-T)e^(-kappa_q p)
         +T(1-U)e^(kappa_q p)+2TU-U^2-TJ],
rho=-(H1+(D p)H_(p,p)/2)/H_p.                            (16)

Larger-compact Cauchy bounds, the exact shifts in (14), and the proved actual leading convergence first give p_N-p=O(N^-1), where p_N=D(N^-1 Log R_a). A second substitution then gives p_N=p+rho/N+O(N^-2), uniformly jointly in sigma and z. Define D Log A=rho and A(z_)=1 and integrate along compact paths from z_. This proves (12), including fixed derivatives. It is the actual finite-shift bootstrap, not a formal WKB choice.

4. Genuine generic-q numerator normals

Now use the original generic recurrence with B=Q^-N, not the root-only recurrence (2). Hold t=N sigma and a fixed while q=q0 exp(epsilon_q) varies. A marked magnetic lift u is also held fixed, with |u|<=C N and z(q)=q^(t-2u). Assume its root parameters lie in a compact subset of (3). Then

|partial_(epsilon_q)^r R_a(q;t,z(q))|_0
       <=C_r N^(10r) |R_a(q0;t,z(q0))|.                  (17)

The same estimate applies to the original unnormalized Newton numerator after retaining its exact K_a and monomial factors. The bounded marked lift is necessary: u->u+NL leaves the root coordinate unchanged but changes its genuine q derivatives.

To prove (17), first conjugate the root matrix above by the diagonal unitary with entries (-1)^n, so its offdiagonal signs are positive in the following convention. Its inverse bound (9) is unchanged. In the low block and at the interface freeze the upper entries at 1. In the tail freeze each upper entry at its ROOT complex value b_n sqrt(C2-cos2(n theta)); it is bounded away from zero. Set every generic lower entry to minus the actual generic recurrence coefficient beta_n divided by that frozen upper entry. The diagonals use the actual generic alpha_n. This determinant is exactly the original generic R_a.

The previously proved generic recurrence estimates, with t/N in a fixed complex compact, give |alpha_n’|<=C N^5 and |beta_n’|<=C N^7 on the required small generic disk. The same bounds follow directly by differentiating their finite rational formulas: the only small denominators are the original spectral gaps, while q^t and its marked derivatives have bounded scaled parameters. Thus the matrix changes by at most C N^7 |epsilon_q|; z(q) changes by O(N|epsilon_q|). By (9), on |epsilon_q|<=c N^-10 the determinant ratio and its inverse are bounded, since its dimension is at most N. Cauchy’s formula gives (17). The original K_a and monomials are controlled on this same disk by their finite root-unit products. No derivative of a root-only reflection is used.

5. The actual geometric chart and the remaining period

The real-deficient-frame theorem has sigma_*=1/6. Its two determinant arguments have |z| asymptotic to a positive constant times X^2 and imaginary parts of opposite sign, each comparable to X, with X arbitrarily large. Keep the midpoint in a compact subinterval of its allowed range. Choose vartheta proportional to 1/X and then X sufficiently large. The proof above needs only R=O(1/sin vartheta)=O(X), so BOTH actual arguments lie strictly in (3). A small fixed complex neighborhood of this chosen geometric frame, or a fixed compact family around it, is therefore covered. One fixed vartheta does not cover the entire unbounded X-family, whose arguments approach the cut.

The negative-real anchor in (13) is exactly the reflected source branch calibrated in that framing theorem. At the folded limit its magnetic characteristic is

Y=(z-i sqrt(1-2cos(pi/6)z))^2
                      /[1-2cos(pi/6)z+z^2],             (18)

with the square root continued from positive values at negative z through the upper or lower half-plane respectively. The two values agree with the actual input/output geometric momenta, including the output’s C^-1 disk shift. This comparison uses the proved anchor and corridor, not arbitrary root selection.

The entire completed root kernel is a residual sine product times two such R polynomials. In this deficient corridor the residual sine arguments, after removing their recorded integer sign, lie in a strict zero-free strip because alpha>Re sigma by a fixed margin. Thus its exact product and (12),(13) give a nonzero joint ROOT packet, including complex degree, in a neighborhood of the actual geometric frame. At a maximal folded channel the residual product remains empty or one factor as specified by the exact source, so both level parities are kept.

The normal estimate (17) is for the original q-Hahn numerators. The full exceptional generic operator still has its unspecialized boundary factors and requires its own lift; it cannot be obtained by varying q in the canceled root kernel. In particular, an actual large-partner column cannot be replaced by the small-channel numerator in (17) solely because their root values coincide; their genuine generic derivatives are different. Most importantly, the complete fixed forbidden integer-separation physical slice is identically zero. Its magnetic deformation alone must retain that zero. Joint degree analysis now has a local analytic input, but no degree contour, nonzero global coefficient, complementary source gap, or general volume equality has been proved here.
