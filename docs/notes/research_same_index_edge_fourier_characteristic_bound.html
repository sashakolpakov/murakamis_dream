A Fourier characteristic bound for the actual same-index edge source

Private written proof, 23 November 2026.

The actual same-index edge kernels satisfy a homogeneous, fixed-degree quantum-torus equation on their entire Laurent polynomials. An exact Gaussian analysis frame turns that equation into a quantitative bound: outside its real characteristic set, the normalized Fourier energy is at most C/(Nδ2)C/(N\delta^2). The estimate also holds before vertex gluing for arbitrary coupled channel coefficients on any fixed graph.

All Laurent coefficients and endpoint channels are retained. The norm here is the Fourier coefficient norm in separate incidence activities. It is not identified with a marked cap norm or a closed knot invariant.

1. The literal edge family and its equation

Fix N≥2,d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,Sk=∏j=1k2sin⁡(πj/N),S0=1.(1) N\ge2,\quad d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\qquad S_k=\prod_{j=1}^k2\sin(\pi j/N),\quad S_0=1. \qquad\text{(1)} The actual common Newton basis in [F] is ek(X)=Sk−2∏j=0k−1(2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N)−X)e_k(X)=S_k^{-2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N)-X). Consequently its complete angular Laurent representative is exactly Bk(z):=ek(−z−z−1)=q−k2(−qz;Q)k(−q/z;Q)kSk2,0≤k≤d.(2) B_k(z):=e_k(-z-z^{-1}) =\frac{q^{-k^2}(-qz;Q)_k(-q/z;Q)_k}{S_k^2}, \qquad 0\le k\le d. \qquad\text{(2)} Indeed each paired factor on the right is q−(2j+1)(1+q2j+1z)(1+q2j+1/z)=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N)+z+z−1q^{-(2j+1)}(1+q^{2j+1}z)(1+q^{2j+1}/z) =2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N)+z+z^{-1}. No denominator in (2) vanishes.

Write Tzf(z)=f(Qz)T_zf(z)=f(Qz), and put az=1+qz,bz=1+q−1/z,Az=azTz−bz,Dz=bzTz−az.(3) a_z=1+qz,\quad b_z=1+q^{-1}/z,\qquad A_z=a_zT_z-b_z,\quad D_z=b_zT_z-a_z. \qquad\text{(3)} The two shifted products in (2) give the cleared identity (az+Qkbz)Bk(Qz)=(bz+Qkaz)Bk(z),AzBk=−QkDzBk.(4) (a_z+Q^kb_z)B_k(Qz)=(b_z+Q^ka_z)B_k(z), \qquad A_zB_k=-Q^kD_zB_k. \qquad\text{(4)} For example, before clearing, their quotient is (1+qQkz)bz/[az(1+q−1Qk/z)](1+qQ^kz)b_z/[a_z(1+q^{-1}Q^k/z)]. The denominator times Bk(Qz)B_k(Qz) is cleared in (4), so the proof includes its zeros, k=0k=0, k=dk=d, and both level parities.

For arbitrary complex numbers μ0,…,μd\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_d, define KN(z,y)=∑k=0dμkBk(z)Bk(y),PN=AzDy−DzAy.(5) K_N(z,y)=\sum_{k=0}^d\mu_k B_k(z)B_k(y),\qquad P_N=A_zD_y-D_zA_y. \qquad\text{(5)} The two terms in PN[Bk(z)Bk(y)]P_N[B_k(z)B_k(y)] agree by (4). Thus

PNKN=0(6) P_NK_N=0 \qquad\text{(6)} as an identity of full Laurent polynomials. It holds for arbitrary activity-independent insertion weights, and with any channel cutoff implemented by zero coefficients. No channel shift or partial summation has produced an endpoint current.

The physical coefficients in [F] include γe(s)HN(s)/Wk(s)\gamma_e(s)H_N(s)/W_k(s), with their prescribed orientation scalars. They are independent of both angular activities and hence are examples of (5), at every parameter where they are defined. The same assertion applies to finite Laurent coefficients of those weights: it does not require their positivity or a bound on their size.

There is also a coupled version. For a fixed graph with E≥1E\ge1 edges, give every edge two separate incidence variables ze,yez_e,y_e, and allow any complex coefficient array: FN(𝒛,𝒚)=∑𝒌∈{0,…,d}Eμ𝒌,N∏e=1EBke(ze)Bke(ye).(7) F_N(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)= \sum_{\mathbf k\in\{0,\ldots,d\}^E} \mu_{\mathbf k,N}\prod_{e=1}^E B_{k_e}(z_e)B_{k_e}(y_e). \qquad\text{(7)} The coefficients may couple every channel and depend on the level and all nonangular source parameters. With Pe,NP_{e,N} acting only on its own incidence pair, (4) proves Pe,NFN=0P_{e,N}F_N=0 for every edge. No independence of the edge coefficients is assumed. Every individual Laurent exponent in (7) lies between −d-d and dd.

2. An exact full-lattice Gaussian frame

For a dimension n≥1n\ge1, identify Laurent Fourier coefficients with f∈ℓ2(ℤn)f\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z^n). Fix σ>0\sigma>0, let

cN,n=(N/(πσ))n/4,(ℱNf)(θ,ξ)=cN,n∑m∈ℤne−|m−Nξ|2/(2σN)eim⋅θfm,dν=dθ(2π)ndξ,θ∈𝕋n,ξ∈ℝn.(8) \begin{split} c_{N,n}&=(N/(\pi\sigma))^{n/4},\\ (\mathcal F_Nf)(\theta,\xi) &=c_{N,n}\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^n} e^{-|m-N\xi|^2/(2\sigma N)}e^{im\cdot\theta}f_m,\\ d\nu&=\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^n}\,d\xi, \quad\theta\in\mathbb T^n,\quad\xi\in\mathbb R^n. \end{split} \qquad\text{(8)} First define this on finitely supported vectors. Integration in θ\theta removes cross terms, and cN,n2∫ℝne−|m−Nξ|2/(σN)dξ=1.∫|ℱNf|2dν=∥f∥22.(9) c_{N,n}^2\int_{\mathbb R^n} e^{-|m-N\xi|^2/(\sigma N)}d\xi=1. \qquad \int|\mathcal F_Nf|^2d\nu=\|f\|_2^2. \qquad\text{(9)} This extends ℱN\mathcal F_N isometrically to the full lattice. The centers range over all of ℝn\mathbb R^n; they are not cut off at a finite Fourier support boundary. The frame principle is the same elementary resolution already used in [G], with a full lattice here rather than a finite Newton interval.

Let ZjZ_j mean multiplication by the jj-th Laurent variable, so (Zjf)m=fm−ej(Z_jf)_m=f_{m-e_j}, and let (Tjf)m=Qmjfm(T_jf)_m=Q^{m_j}f_m. Both are unitary on the full lattice. For integer vectors a,ba,b, set za=eia⋅θz^a=e^{ia\cdot\theta}, Yb=e2πib⋅ξY^b=e^{2\pi ib\cdot\xi}. Their normalized overlap is e−|a|2/(4σN)e^{-|a|^2/(4\sigma N)}. For the second, angular integration leaves |Qb⋅m−e2πib⋅ξ|2|Q^{b\cdot m}-e^{2\pi ib\cdot\xi}|^2 against the squared window. For a normal-ordered, fixed-degree quantum-torus polynomial PN=∑a,bcab,NZaTbP_N=\sum_{a,b}c_{ab,N}Z^aT^b, define pN(θ,ξ)=∑a,bcab,NzaYbp_N(\theta,\xi)=\sum_{a,b}c_{ab,N}z^aY^b. Assume its finite exponent set and coefficient bounds are uniform. Applying (10) first to ZaTbfZ^aT^bf, then to TbfT^bf, and summing gives ∥ℱN(PNf)−pNℱNf∥L2≤CP,σN−1/2∥f∥2.(11) \|\mathcal F_N(P_Nf)-p_N\mathcal F_Nf\|_{L^2} \le C_{P,\sigma}N^{-1/2}\|f\|_2. \qquad\text{(11)} All noncommutative ordering phases stay in the coefficients cab,Nc_{ab,N}. The proof uses full shifted output vectors, without a finite compression.

3. The actual real characteristic bound

For (5), its exact left-ordered expression and symbol are c1,N=azby−bzay,c2,N=bzby−azay,PN=c1,N(TzTy−1)+c2,N(Tz−Ty),pN=c1,N(z,y)(YzYy−1)+c2,N(z,y)(Yz−Yy).(12) \begin{gathered} c_{1,N}=a_zb_y-b_za_y,\qquad c_{2,N}=b_zb_y-a_za_y,\\ P_N=c_{1,N}(T_zT_y-1)+c_{2,N}(T_z-T_y),\\ p_N=c_{1,N}(z,y)(Y_zY_y-1)+c_{2,N}(z,y)(Y_z-Y_y). \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(12)} On the frame phase space, |z|=|y|=|Yz|=|Yy|=1|z|=|y|=|Y_z|=|Y_y|=1. The coefficient bounds and exponent set are absolute, uniformly for every N≥2N\ge2. As q→1q\to1, the symbol differs by O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) uniformly from p0=(1+z)(1+y)zy{(z−y)(YzYy−1)+(1−zy)(Yz−Yy)}.(13) p_0= \frac{(1+z)(1+y)}{zy} \{(z-y)(Y_zY_y-1)+(1-zy)(Y_z-Y_y)\}. \qquad\text{(13)} The prefactor in (13) is retained. In particular its zeros at z=−1z=-1 or y=−1y=-1 are part of this characteristic set. No division by that factor is used.

Apply (11) to the exact null vector (6), and use (9) to replace pNp_N by p0p_0. This proves, without a source hypothesis, ∫|p0|2|ℱNKN|2dν≤CσN∥KN∥22,∫{|p0|≥δ}|ℱNKN|2dν≤CσNδ2∥KN∥22(δ>0).(14) \int|p_0|^2|\mathcal F_NK_N|^2d\nu \le\frac{C_\sigma}{N}\|K_N\|_2^2, \quad \int_{\{|p_0|\ge\delta\}}|\mathcal F_NK_N|^2d\nu \le\frac{C_\sigma}{N\delta^2}\|K_N\|_2^2 \quad(\delta>0). \qquad\text{(14)} Here n=2n=2. The norm is exactly the full Laurent coefficient norm. The constant is independent of all coefficients μk\mu_k. If the kernel is zero, the same inequalities hold trivially.

For (7), use n=2En=2E, and let pe,0p_{e,0} be (13) on its edge coordinates. Tensoring the frame calculations, or applying (10) in those two coordinates only, gives ∫∑e=1E|pe,0|2|ℱNFN|2dν≤CσEN∥FN∥22.(15) \int\sum_{e=1}^E|p_{e,0}|^2|\mathcal F_NF_N|^2d\nu \le\frac{C_\sigma E}{N}\|F_N\|_2^2. \qquad\text{(15)} In particular the mass on {max⁡e|pe,0|≥δ}\{\max_e|p_{e,0}|\ge\delta\}, outside the common δ\delta-characteristic neighborhood, is at most CσE/(Nδ2)C_\sigma E/(N\delta^2) times the full mass. This holds for arbitrary entangled channel weights, not only products of independent edge kernels.

4. Normalized angular jets retain their forcing

Let 𝒟j=zj∂zj\mathscr D_j=z_j\partial_{z_j}, including every incidence variable, and let 𝒜\mathcal A be a fixed finite downward-closed set of multiindices containing zero. Define the actual jet vectors uα=N−|α|𝒟αFN,α∈𝒜.(16) u_\alpha=N^{-|\alpha|}\mathscr D^\alpha F_N, \qquad\alpha\in\mathcal A. \qquad\text{(16)} Since each Fourier exponent of (7) is in [−d,d][-d,d], ∥uα∥2≤∥FN∥2\|u_\alpha\|_2\le\|F_N\|_2. These derivatives do not automatically satisfy the homogeneous equation. The exact product rule, because every 𝒟j\mathscr D_j commutes with the shifts, is Pe,Nuα=−∑β<α(αβ)N−(|α|−|β|)(𝒟α−βPe,N)uβ.(17) P_{e,N}u_\alpha= -\sum_{\beta<\alpha}\binom\alpha\beta N^{-(|\alpha|-|\beta|)} (\mathscr D^{\alpha-\beta}P_{e,N})u_\beta. \qquad\text{(17)} Here β<α\beta<\alpha means componentwise inequality and inequality somewhere, and 𝒟γP\mathscr D^\gamma P differentiates its coefficients only. Every resulting fixed-degree operator has uniformly bounded norm. Thus the right side of (17) is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) as an operator on the fixed direct sum of predecessor jets. Applying (11) to each jet, including that residual, proves ∫∑e|pe,0|2∑α∈𝒜|ℱNuα|2dν≤Cσ,𝒜EN∑α∈𝒜∥uα∥22.(18) \begin{split} \int\sum_e|p_{e,0}|^2 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}|\mathcal F_Nu_\alpha|^2d\nu &\le\frac{C_{\sigma,\mathcal A}E}{N} \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}\|u_\alpha\|_2^2. \end{split} \qquad\text{(18)} Its off-characteristic version has the additional factor δ−2\delta^{-2}. All jet coordinates and their commutator forcing remain. This is not an assertion about moving-quantum-parameter derivatives or an automatic conversion of angular derivatives to Casimir normals at parabolic angles.

5. What remains at the original gluing

The fixed graph and arbitrary coefficient array in (7) give a broad pre-gluing source class, including physical insertions and retained low or high channel sums. The actual vertex operations in [F] still identify incidence activities, multiply by their prescribed crossing polynomials, reduce in their proper relations, and apply the original marked functionals. Those operations, with all their scalar factors, remain the original closing map ΛN\Lambda_N; none is replaced by the positive Fourier norm above.

In particular (15) does not commute a positive phase-space cutoff through ΛN\Lambda_N, nor bound its effect on the complementary normal channels. No ordinary-color Plancherel energy is identified with this norm. The real variables in (8) describe a unitary torus phase space, not a complex hyperbolic critical chart. The estimates are polynomial concentration bounds, not exponential complementary gaps, a selected geometric cycle coefficient, or a scalar lower bound. They impose the explicit relation (13) on the full normalized source energy before those remaining operations.
