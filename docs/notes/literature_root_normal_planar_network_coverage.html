Primary-source coverage of the root-normal planar-network period

29 October 2026. This is a definitions and theorem-scope comparison only. It neither imports nor endorses the papers’ proofs. No identification of our period with a named invariant is asserted from terminology or matching growth.

The source object being compared is the exact period in the original NC Morse-graph reduction:

$$ q_*=e^{\pi i/N},\quad \zeta(t)=q_*e^t,\qquad Z_\Gamma={C_\Gamma\over\nu_N} \left.(S_\Gamma\Xi_\Gamma)'\right|_{t=0}. $$

Here ΞΓ\Xi_\Gamma uses the specified ordinary canonical ordered 3j tensors; SΓS_\Gamma is the explicit product of source coefficients. The labels satisfy 0≤je≤(N−1)/20\le j_e\le(N-1)/2. When every vertex is supported, ∑e∋vje≥N−1\sum_{e\ni v}j_e\ge N-1, the source factors are nonzero at the root and ΞΓ(0)=0\Xi_\Gamma(0)=0. Thus this case retains a fixed first normal, even when that normal itself vanishes. The distinct marked-white boundary case is retained in the cited source theorem.

Closest root-normal definition

Costantino–Guéritaud–van der Veen, On the volume conjecture for polyhedra, arXiv:1403.2347, §1.2.2 (Evaluation), Conjecture 1.3 and Theorem 1.4. Their ev⁡N(f)\operatorname{ev}_N(f) extracts the first nonzero Laurent coefficient about A*=eπi/(2N)A_*=e^{\pi i/(2N)}; for their unitary spin network, whose square is meromorphic, they extend this by a square root, up to sign. The polyhedral volume statement uses (π/N)log⁡|ev⁡N⟨Γ,cN⟩U|(\pi/N)\log|\operatorname{ev}_N \langle\Gamma,c_N\rangle^U|. Theorem 1.4 establishes the stated limit for the tetrahedron-derived local-move family shown there. The paper explicitly notes that this family excludes the cube; its general-graph statement remains a conjecture, with an additional conditional result. Primary paper.

This is the closest prescription found, but it does not fix the order to one. For a regular scalar ff with a simple zero, our coordinate convention gives ∂tf=(A*/2)ev⁡N(f)\partial_t f=(A_*/2)\operatorname{ev}_N(f), since ζ=A2\zeta=A^2. At a higher-order zero our first normal is zero and their extracted coefficient is not. The canonical/unitary vertex factors, their root orders, and CΓSΓ/νNC_\Gamma S_\Gamma/\nu_N have not been equated.

The earlier Costantino, 6j-symbols, hyperbolic structures and the Volume Conjecture, Geometry & Topology 11 (2007), 1831–1854, likewise explicitly studies the lowest Laurent term of quantum 6j-symbols, and gives results for fundamental shadow links in connected sums of S2×S1S^2\times S^1. This is additional coverage of the leading-term prescription, not an identification of every fixed-first-normal planar network. Primary paper.

Named neighboring invariants

Yokota invariant, in Belletti, An upper bound conjecture for the Yokota invariant, Definitions 2.2, 2.5, 2.7; Theorem 4.9 Odd rr, q=e2πi/rq=e^{2\pi i/r}, even integer colors at most r−2r-2, with triangle sum at most 2r−42r-4. For a trivalent graph, Yr=|⟨Γ,col⟩U|2Y_r=|\langle\Gamma,\mathrm{col}\rangle^U|^2, with unitary theta evaluation 1; higher valence uses a weighted desingularization sum. No fixed root derivative occurs in this definition. Its upper-bound theorem covers graphs generated from the tetrahedron by the two specified vertex/face moves; the geometric supremum is the volume of the right-angled rectification. Under the naive identification r=Nr=N, integer colors 2je2j_e in our supported sector have sum at least 2N−22N-2, outside that admissibility cutoff. Root, color and normalization changes therefore require an explicit comparison. Primary paper.
Renormalized Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant, Geer–Patureau-Mirand–Turaev, Modified quantum dimensions and re-normalized link invariants, §3, Theorem 3 For closed colored ribbon graphs admitting an allowed simple cut color VV, the definition is F′(L)=dJ(V)⟨TV⟩F'(L)=d_J(V)\langle T_V\rangle, where dJ(V)=d0S′(V,J)/S′(J,V)d_J(V)=d_0S'(V,J)/S'(J,V) and d0≠0d_0\ne0 is a chosen scale. This is a cut/modified-dimension prescription, including root-of-unity examples. The theorem is not a definition by a fixed first derivative of an arbitrary ordinary integral spin network. Equality would need the cut category, vertex lifts and scale matched. Primary paper.
Logarithmic knot invariant, Jun Murakami, From colored Jones invariants to logarithmic invariants, introductory Theorem 1 At ξ=eπi/N\xi=e^{\pi i/N}, the named invariant obeys γs(N)=ξ2N∂q[(q−q−1)(Vs+V2N−s)]q=ξ\gamma_s^{(N)}=\frac{\xi}{2N}\partial_q[(q-q^{-1})(V_s+V_{2N-s})]_{q=\xi}, 1≤s≤N1\le s\le N. The paper identifies γN(N)\gamma_N^{(N)} with its Kashaev normalization. This is a genuine root-derivative formula, but for knots and this prescribed combination of colored Jones polynomials, rather than our arbitrary canonical 3j network with its source factors. Primary paper.
Colored-Alexander/SL(2,C) graph invariant, Costantino–Murakami, On SL(2,C) quantum 6j-symbols and their relation to hyperbolic volume The graph invariant uses nonintegral quantum-group representations and extends a colored Alexander invariant. Its tetrahedral volume results do not, by definition alone, cover the ordinary integral-label first-normal network above. Primary paper.

The word “renormalized” is also used for a separate operation in Costantino, Integrality of Kauffman brackets of trivalent graphs: multiplying the generic bracket by explicit quantum-factorial factors to obtain an integral Laurent polynomial. That is not itself a root-normal prescription. Primary journal definition.

Augmented graphs and right-angled link complements

Van der Veen, The volume conjecture for augmented knotted trivalent graphs, Definition 2, Definition 7 and Theorem 2. For a fixed KTG move sequence 𝒮\mathcal S, the theorem supplies a threshold n(𝒮)n(\mathcal S): each unzip must receive at least that many augmentation rings. At A=eπi/(2N)A=e^{\pi i/(2N)}, the normalized invariant is ϕNθNrsixjNt+1\phi_N^{\theta}N^r\mathrm{sixj}_N^{t+1} for odd NN, and zero for even NN, where tt counts triangle moves, rr rings, and $\mathrm{sixj}_N=\sum_{k=0}^{(N-1)/2}[{(N-1)/2\atop k}]^4$. Its outside has volume (2t+2)v8(2t+2)v_8. Normalization divides by the bracket of the ss-fold unlink for split components, not all link components. Thus this is substantial augmented-graph/odd-NN coverage, with an exact central quartic factor. It does not state coverage of every singly augmented NC Morse graph at both parities. A literal graph mapping and the prescribed normalization are still needed. Primary paper.

Ibarra–McQuire–Purcell, Augmented links, shadow links, and the TV volume conjecture: a geometric perspective, Theorems 2.12 and 4.15. The paper identifies octahedral fully augmented link complements with fundamental shadow-link complements, and states the Turaev–Viro volume limit for octahedral fully augmented links with no half-twists, at q=e2πi/rq=e^{2\pi i/r}, odd rr. For cc crossing circles the volume is 2(c−1)v82(c-1)v_8. This covers a related geometric class and a different quantum invariant; it does not define our derivative period. Primary journal paper.

Coverage conclusion

Among the checked definitions, leading Laurent evaluation, renormalized RT, logarithmic knot invariant, and Yokota invariant are different prescriptions. None of the checked statements directly names or covers the entire weighted fixed-first-normal period above for every original NC Morse graph. This is not a claim that no such identification exists elsewhere.

Any claimed coverage must specify the variable and normal coordinate, root order, canonical versus unitary vertex factors, color lattice, original single marked cut and framing factors, graph construction, and parity. No proof technique from these sources is used in this comparison.
