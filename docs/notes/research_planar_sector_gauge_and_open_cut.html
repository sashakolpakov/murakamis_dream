Sector charges and the complete open planar array

Private written proof, 17 November 2026. We prove the charge-change formula for the full two-ended planar source, including both end colors. The input is Kashaev’s explicit planar weight [K, (2.7),(2.8),(3.6)]. Neither its claimed invariance nor its asserted freedom in the sector charges is a proof premise.

1. Corners, end colors, and the source

Let GG be a connected finite plane graph with four half-edges at each crossing and two vertices of degree one. Both degree-one vertices meet the unbounded face. Loops, multiple edges and repeated occurrences of a region at a crossing are allowed. Each crossing specifies its overpassing pair of opposite half-edges. A corner is an occurrence of the sector between consecutive half-edges at a crossing. Thus four corners are retained even if the same face occupies two sectors.

Fix N≥2N\ge2 and a primitive NN-th root qq. Every color and charge below belongs to ℤ/Nℤ\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z. In counterclockwise order at a crossing, label the edge colors i,j,k,li,j,k,l, with j,lj,l on the overpassing strand, and let a,b,c,da,b,c,d label the following corners: (i,j),(j,k),(k,l),(l,i). (i,j),\quad(j,k),\quad(k,l),\quad(l,i). Use the specified finite weight R(i,j,k,l∣a,b,c,d)=qa−k−jW(j−i−a,i−l−d,l−k−c,k−j−b),(1) R(i,j,k,l\mid a,b,c,d) =q^{a-k-j}W(j-i-a,\ i-l-d,\ l-k-c,\ k-j-b), \qquad\text{(1)} where WW is exactly [K, (2.7)], including its finite products, integer-residue representatives and two theta factors. It is enough here that its four arguments in (1) are retained exactly.

A sector assignment is admissible if the four charges at every crossing sum to one, the charges on every bounded face sum to one, and every corner of the unbounded face has charge zero. Face sums count corner occurrences. For two fixed end-edge colors u,vu,v, put Fa(u,v)=∑internaledge colors∏crossings tRt∏all edges eqje.(2) F_a(u,v)=\sum_{\substack{\text{internal}\\\text{edge colors}}} \prod_{\text{crossings }t}R_t \prod_{\text{all edges }e}q^{j_e}. \qquad\text{(2)} The two end edges also occur in the last product. This is the original fixed-end source when u=v=0u=v=0. When four distinct regions occur at every crossing, its charge conventions agree literally with [K, (3.1)–(3.6)]. With repeated regions, (1),(2) specify the occurrence-level convention explicitly, rather than identifying different corner charges by the name of their face.

2. A planar potential lemma

Suppose corner values δc\delta_c sum to zero at each crossing and around each bounded face, and vanish at every unbounded-face corner. There are edge values xex_e such that at each corner from a half-edge ee to its next counterclockwise half-edge e′e', δc=xe′−xe.(3) \delta_c=x_{e'}-x_e. \qquad\text{(3)} They can be chosen zero on both end edges.

To prove this integrally, replace each crossing by a small circle. Its four incident half-edges meet that circle at four separate vertices; connect consecutive vertices by the four circle arcs. Keep the original graph edges between these circles and the two degree-one vertices. Give an original edge increment zero and an oriented circle arc increment δc\delta_c, with sign reversed on the oppositely oriented arc. This is a connected plane graph. Its bounded faces are the small crossing disks and the original bounded regions, with the same corner occurrences. The assumed sums say that the increment around every bounded-face boundary is zero.

The oriented boundaries of the bounded faces generate the integral cycle group of a connected plane graph. One direct proof takes any simple cycle and sums the faces inside it: interior edge occurrences cancel and its boundary remains. Split any closed edge walk at its repeated vertices into simple cycles, canceling immediate backtracks. Bridges contribute nothing. This proves the assertion for loops and repeated face boundaries as well. It uses only integer sums, so it remains valid after reduction modulo any NN.

The increment therefore integrates along paths to a vertex potential, independent of the chosen path. Its value is the same at both ends of each original edge, giving xex_e, and its circle steps give (3). The outer boundary walk visits both degree-one vertices. Every increment along that walk is zero: original edges have zero increment and outer corners have δc=0\delta_c=0. The two end values are equal. Subtract their common value to make both zero. Connectivity also shows that this normalized potential is unique.

3. Exact charge change, with its phase

Let aa and a′a' be admissible assignments. Their corner difference δ=a′−a\delta=a'-a satisfies the potential lemma. Choose its normalized edge values xex_e, and replace every edge color by je′=je+xej'_e=j_e+x_e. The end colors are unchanged. At one crossing, equation (3) says a′−a=xj−xi,b′−b=xk−xj,c′−c=xl−xk,d′−d=xi−xl. a'-a=x_j-x_i,\quad b'-b=x_k-x_j,\quad c'-c=x_l-x_k,\quad d'-d=x_i-x_l . All four arguments of WW in (1) are unchanged, as elements of ℤ/Nℤ\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z. Consequently their integer residues and theta supports are unchanged as well. The explicit prefactor gives R(i+xi,j+xj,k+xk,l+xl∣a′,b′,c′,d′)=q−xi−xkR(i,j,k,l∣a,b,c,d).(4) R(i+x_i,j+x_j,k+x_k,l+x_l\mid a',b',c',d') =q^{-x_i-x_k}R(i,j,k,l\mid a,b,c,d). \qquad\text{(4)} In particular this comparison loses no zero entry or endpoint residue.

The color change is a bijection of the finite internal summation set. Each edge factor in (2) supplies qxeq^{x_e}. Multiplying (4) proves the identity of the entire open arrays Fa′(u,v)=qΩ(x)Fa(u,v),Ω(x)=∑exe−∑crossings t(xfirst under-half-edge+xsecond under-half-edge).(5) \boxed{F_{a'}(u,v)=q^{\Omega(x)}F_a(u,v),\qquad \Omega(x)=\sum_e x_e- \sum_{\text{crossings }t} (x_{\text{first under-half-edge}}+ x_{\text{second under-half-edge}}).} \qquad\text{(5)} The second sum counts half-edge occurrences; a loop can contribute twice. This is an explicit unit independent of u,vu,v and of the summation colors. No primality, oddness or division modulo NN is required.

Thus an admissible sector assignment may be changed with both end colors fixed, while retaining the single root phase in (5). The conclusion concerns the whole open array, so it also retains a specified closing functional once that functional is applied to the same end coordinates. It does not identify a different cap functional, prove scalarity of the open array, establish a geometric contribution, or supply a large-level lower bound.
