A positive variational formula for an actual source on a tree

20 November 2026. Research note; the public manuscripts remain frozen.

A scalar contraction on a tree is the infimum of a product of positive local norms, after allowing full matrix changes of basis on its internal edges. The same statement holds for the norm of a vector-valued output. There is no replacement of the original closing functional in this formula. That functional is one of the tensors being optimized.

The infimum is generally approached by increasingly singular changes of basis. Existence of the formula therefore gives neither a geometric minimizer nor a volume asymptotic. A second result gives a useful way to certify near optimality: on a fixed tree, matching the full reduced density matrices across every edge approximately forces the contraction norm to be close to the product of the local norms. The estimate is independent of the dimensions of the edge spaces. Matching only their diagonal entries is insufficient.

These facts apply to the complete Conway compiler. They also apply directly to the actual all-main-color energy, by treating multiplication of the original physical braid operators as a tree of linear maps. The latter application retains the main-color cap projector and does not identify that energy with an auxiliary-color or unfilled covariance norm.

1. Tensors, pairings, and the permitted gauges

Let 𝒯\mathcal T be a finite connected tree, with vertex set VV and internal edge set EE. At each edge ee, fix a finite-dimensional complex coordinate space HeH_e, with its Euclidean Hermitian norm. Choose one endpoint as its positive end. A tensor at that end has an HeH_e factor; the tensor at the other end has an He*H_e^* factor. The edge contraction is the complex bilinear evaluation pairing.

One distinguished vertex may also have an uncontracted output in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space FF. All other tensor factors are contracted along the tree. Denote the resulting output by Y=Contr⁡(Tv:v∈V)∈F.(1) Y=\operatorname{Contr}(T_v:v\in V)\in F. \qquad\text{(1)} The scalar case is F=ℂF=\mathbb C. Each local tensor has its ordinary coefficient Hilbert–Schmidt norm, denoted by ∥Tv∥\|T_v\|. The dual coordinate factors have the corresponding Euclidean norm.

An edge gauge is an invertible matrix ge∈GL(He)g_e\in\mathrm{GL}(H_e). It acts by geg_e at the positive end and by ge−𝖳g_e^{-\mathsf T} at the negative end. These two actions preserve the bilinear evaluation. Consequently the gauged tensors Tv(g)T_v(g) have exactly the same output YY, with its complex phases. Define 𝒞(T)=infge∈GL(He)∏v∈V∥Tv(g)∥.(2) \mathcal C(T)=\inf_{g_e\in\mathrm{GL}(H_e)} \prod_{v\in V}\|T_v(g)\|. \qquad\text{(2)} Every matrix and norm in this definition is specified before the output is evaluated. No factor contains a division by YY.

The original source must be included among the tensors. For example, a linear closing row is a one-leg tensor at the root. Leaving it out and optimizing an open network is a different problem. A retained external scalar can be put into that root tensor or kept as its known absolute multiplier.

2. The exact variational identity

Theorem 1. For every network in Section 1, including a zero output, 𝒞(T)=∥Y∥.(3) \boxed{\mathcal C(T)=\|Y\|.} \qquad\text{(3)} There is no dimension factor in (3).

Proof. Contracting a leaf vector into its neighbor has operator norm at most the norm of that vector. Repeated Cauchy–Schwarz along the tree therefore gives ∥Y∥≤∏v∥Tv(g)∥(4) \|Y\|\le\prod_v\|T_v(g)\| \qquad\text{(4)} for every gauge. This proves one inequality in (3). The bilinear functional associated with a vector has the same Euclidean norm as that vector; complex conjugation in its Riesz vector changes no norm. In particular no norm of an unnormalized Bell vector is inserted in (4).

For the reverse inequality, use induction on the number of vertices. For one vertex, its tensor is precisely the output, and the assertion is immediate. Otherwise choose a leaf different from the output vertex. If its vector is zero, every product in (2) and the output are zero. Suppose its vector is nonzero. By interchanging the two edge conventions if necessary, regard it as a vector a∈Hea\in H_e. Choose an invertible edge gauge sending aa to the first unit vector e0e_0. Write the transformed neighboring tensor as ∑iei*⊗Ai. \sum_i e_i^*\otimes A_i. The remaining factors in AiA_i can include the output space FF. The reduced tree obtained by contracting the leaf has neighboring tensor A0A_0, and its output is still YY.

Apply the further gauge Dt=diag⁡(1,t,…,t)D_t=\operatorname{diag}(1,t,\ldots,t) at the leaf end of this edge, with t→+∞t\to+\infty. The leaf remains e0e_0, of norm one; its neighbor becomes e0*⊗A0+t−1∑i>0ei*⊗Ai. e_0^*\otimes A_0+t^{-1}\sum_{i>0}e_i^*\otimes A_i. Its norm tends to ∥A0∥\|A_0\|. Fix any gauges on the other edges before taking this limit. Their actions commute with this edge action, so the limiting product is exactly the corresponding product for the reduced tree. Taking its infimum proves 𝒞(T)≤𝒞(Treduced)\mathcal C(T)\le\mathcal C(T_{\mathrm{ reduced}}). Induction and (4) give (3). If A0=0A_0=0, the same limit gives zero; thus this argument also covers a zero output without a continuity assumption on the infimum. ▫\square

This proof is an exact contraction argument, rather than an assertion that a metric chosen from the unknown answer exists. It also explains why unbounded condition numbers are permitted in (2). The diagonal matrices DtD_t used in the proof become singular in the limit.

The tree hypothesis concerns the contraction graph used in this theorem. A physical diagram with cycles may be compiled into a tree by bundling all live indices into a larger matrix index. The resulting gauge acts on that whole bundled space. It is not then a product of gauges on the original individual physical wires.

3. Positive matrices and geodesic convexity

Full complex gauges can be replaced by positive Hermitian matrices. Indeed write ge=UePeg_e=U_eP_e, where UeU_e is unitary and Pe>0P_e>0. At the two ends, the actions are UePe,U¯ePe−𝖳. U_eP_e,\qquad \overline U_eP_e^{-\mathsf T}. The two unitary factors do not change their local tensor norms. Removing all of them shows that positive gauges have the same infimum.

It is convenient to use He=Pe2=ge*ge>0H_e=P_e^2=g_e^*g_e>0 as variable. The squared local norm is ∥Tv∥H2=⟨Tv,(⨂e positive at vHe)⊗(⨂e negative at vH¯e−1)⊗IFTv⟩.(5) \|T_v\|_H^2= \left\langle T_v, \left(\bigotimes_{e\text{ positive at }v}H_e\right) \otimes \left(\bigotimes_{e\text{ negative at }v}\overline H_e^{-1}\right) \otimes I_F\,T_v\right\rangle. \qquad\text{(5)} The IFI_F factor is present only at the output vertex. Assume for this paragraph that each local tensor is nonzero. The objective is L(H)=∑vlog⁡∥Tv∥H.(6) L(H)=\sum_v\log\|T_v\|_H. \qquad\text{(6)}

Proposition 2. The function (6) is convex along simultaneous positive-matrix geodesics He(t)=He(0)1/2exp⁡(tXe)He(0)1/2,Xe=Xe*.(7) H_e(t)=H_e(0)^{1/2}\exp(tX_e)H_e(0)^{1/2}, \qquad X_e=X_e^*. \qquad\text{(7)}

Proof. In (5), pull the fixed factors He(0)1/2H_e(0)^{1/2}, or their conjugate inverses, into the tensor. Diagonalize each Hermitian XeX_e. On a negative end the corresponding generator is −X¯e-\overline X_e. The remaining tensor factors act on different legs and commute. Thus the squared norm at a vertex has the form ∑αcαetℓα,cα≥0, \sum_\alpha c_\alpha e^{t\ell_\alpha}, \qquad c_\alpha\ge0, with real ℓα\ell_\alpha. The second derivative of its logarithm is the variance of these real numbers for their normalized positive weights. It is nonnegative. Sum the resulting convex functions and divide by two. ▫\square

Convexity does not assert attainment. Even when Y≠0Y\ne0, the minimizing matrices can escape to infinity in the positive cone. Restricting (2) to diagonal gauges, prescribed tensor-product gauges, or a bounded condition number generally gives only an upper bound on ∥Y∥\|Y\|.

4. A dimension-independent certificate from full edge marginals

There is a local condition which certifies that a specified set of gauges is already close to the infimum. It uses full reduced density matrices, including their off-diagonal entries.

Fix gauges and suppose all gauged local tensors are nonzero. Normalize them to unit vectors τv=Tv(g)/∥Tv(g)∥\tau_v=T_v(g)/\|T_v(g)\|. For an internal edge, let ρv,e\rho_{v,e} be the reduced density matrix of τv\tau_v on that coordinate factor; trace over all its other factors, including FF when appropriate. Each density matrix is positive with trace one. Because the contraction is bilinear, the two endpoint matrices are compared with a transpose: δe=∥ρv,e−ρw,e𝖳∥1,δ=maxeδe.(8) \delta_e=\|\rho_{v,e}-\rho_{w,e}^{\mathsf T}\|_1, \qquad \delta=\max_e\delta_e. \qquad\text{(8)} Here ∥⋅∥1\|\cdot\|_1 is the trace norm. Transposition leaves this norm unchanged, so (8) is independent of the chosen endpoint order.

Theorem 3. Let m=|V|≥2m=|V|\ge2, put S=∑e∈EδeS=\sum_{e\in E}\delta_e, and write [a]+=max⁡(a,0)[a]_+=\max(a,0). Then ∥Y∥∏v∥Tv(g)∥≥[1−4mS−5mS]+.(9) \boxed{\quad \frac{\|Y\|}{\prod_v\|T_v(g)\|} \ge[1-4m\sqrt S-5mS]_+. \quad} \qquad\text{(9)} The estimate has no dependence on any edge dimension.

Proof. Work first with the normalized local tensors. For a density matrix put L(ρ)=1−tr⁡(ρ2)L(\rho)=1-\operatorname{tr}(\rho^2). Two elementary properties control the errors without a dependence on tree depth. For a bipartite density matrix, L(ρAB)≤L(ρA)+L(ρB).(10) L(\rho_{AB})\le L(\rho_A)+L(\rho_B). \qquad\text{(10)} Indeed the commuting swaps FA,FBF_A,F_B on two copies satisfy (I−FA)(I−FB)≥0(I-F_A)(I-F_B)\ge0. Taking its trace against ρAB⊗ρAB\rho_{AB}\otimes\rho_{AB} gives (10). Also |L(ρ)−L(σ)|≤∥ρ−σ∥1∥ρ+σ∥op≤2∥ρ−σ∥1.(11) |L(\rho)-L(\sigma)| \le\|\rho-\sigma\|_1\|\rho+\sigma\|_{\mathrm{ op}} \le2\|\rho-\sigma\|_1. \qquad\text{(11)}

Orient the tree toward its output vertex. A nonroot vertex tensor is pure on its parent leg and all its child legs together. Their two reduced density matrices have the same nonzero eigenvalues, by the singular-value decomposition. Its parent-leg linear entropy is therefore at most the sum of its child-leg linear entropies, by repeated use of (10). At a leaf that entropy is zero. Across each edge, (11) costs at most 2δe2\delta_e. Induction from the leaves shows that the linear entropy on every half-edge is at most 2S2S. The output space at the root causes no change: the root is not used for a parent-leg induction.

Choose a top unit eigenvector av,ea_{v,e} of each half-edge density matrix, and let ηv,e=1−λmax(ρv,e)\eta_{v,e}=1-\lambda_{\max}(\rho_{v,e}). Since tr⁡ρ2≤λmax(ρ)\operatorname{tr}\rho^2\le\lambda_{\max}(\rho), ηv,e≤2S\eta_{v,e}\le2S. The tensor product of these rank-one projectors at a vertex has probability pv≥1−∑e∋vηv,e≥1−2deg⁡(v)S.(12) p_v\ge1-\sum_{e\ni v}\eta_{v,e} \ge1-2\deg(v)S. \qquad\text{(12)} The projectors act on different legs and commute, so this is the elementary union bound for their complementary projections.

Suppose temporarily that S<1/(16m2)S<1/(16m^2), so every pvp_v is positive. Normalize the projected vertex tensor, and call the result φv\varphi_v. At a nonroot it is a product of its chosen half-edge vectors, with the scalar phase fixed by the projection of τv\tau_v. At the root it is their product tensored with a unit output vector in FF, again with that phase retained. Consequently ∥τv−φv∥2=2−2pv≤2(1−pv)≤4deg⁡(v)S.(13) \|\tau_v-\varphi_v\|^2=2-2\sqrt{p_v} \le2(1-p_v)\le4\deg(v)S. \qquad\text{(13)}

The vectors at the ends of an edge are nearly paired. Write ρ=ρv,e\rho=\rho_{v,e}, σ=ρw,e𝖳\sigma=\rho_{w,e}^{\mathsf T}, and let P,QP,Q be their top rank-one eigenprojections. A trace-zero Hermitian matrix tested against a projection has absolute trace at most half its trace norm; its positive and negative parts have equal trace. Hence 1−ηv,e−δe/2≤tr⁡(Pσ)≤tr⁡(PQ)+ηw,e. 1-\eta_{v,e}-\delta_e/2\le\operatorname{tr}(P\sigma) \le\operatorname{tr}(PQ)+\eta_{w,e}. The last inequality splits σ\sigma into its top eigenspace and its positive remainder of trace ηw,e\eta_{w,e}. The bilinear edge overlap te=|av,e𝖳aw,e|t_e=|a_{v,e}^{\mathsf T}a_{w,e}| therefore satisfies te2≥1−92S.(14) t_e^2\ge1-\tfrac92 S. \qquad\text{(14)} The product network (φv)(\varphi_v) has output norm ∏ete\prod_e t_e, at least 1−92(m−1)S1-\tfrac92(m-1)S: use 1−a≥1−a\sqrt{1-a}\ge1-a and multiply the resulting bounds. The tree Cauchy estimate (4) and a multilinear telescoping sum give ∥Contr⁡(τv)∥≥1−92(m−1)S−∑v∥τv−φv∥≥1−92(m−1)S−22m(m−1)S.(15) \begin{split} \|\operatorname{Contr}(\tau_v)\| &\ge1-\tfrac92(m-1)S-\sum_v\|\tau_v-\varphi_v\|\\ &\ge1-\tfrac92(m-1)S-2\sqrt{2m(m-1)S}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(15)} Here ∑vdeg⁡(v)=2(m−1)\sum_v\deg(v)=2(m-1) and Cauchy–Schwarz were used. This proves the weaker lower bound (9) when S<1/(16m2)S<1/(16m^2). If S≥1/(16m2)S\ge1/(16m^2), its bracket is nonpositive, so (9) follows from nonnegativity of a norm. Restoring the local norms proves the assertion. ▫\square

In particular, for a fixed tree and a sequence of levels, the condition δN→0\delta_N\to0 implies ∥YN∥∏v∥Tv,N(gN)∥→1.(16) \frac{\|Y_N\|}{\prod_v\|T_{v,N}(g_N)\|}\longrightarrow1. \qquad\text{(16)} This criterion involves only local tensors, their prescribed gauges, and their reduced density matrices. It does not assume a lower bound for the unknown output. It also handles growing trees: since S≤(m−1)δS\le(m-1)\delta, the condition δ=o(m−3)\delta=o(m^{-3}) suffices for the ratio in (16) to tend to one. No optimal numerical constants or dependence on mm are claimed.

The same matrices are the full gauge derivative. Vary one edge gauge by ge(s)=esXgeg_e(s)=e^{sX}g_e, with X=X*X=X^*. Direct differentiation gives dds|s=0∑vlog⁡∥Tv(g(s))∥=tr⁡X(ρv,e−ρw,e𝖳).(17) \left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\sum_v\log\|T_v(g(s))\| =\operatorname{tr}X (\rho_{v,e}-\rho_{w,e}^{\mathsf T}). \qquad\text{(17)} Exact full matrix balance is therefore the stationary condition. On a tree it saturates (4). A pure leaf marginal then forces its neighbor to factor across that edge; removing it propagates this factorization. Thus a finite exactly balanced gauge is a restrictive condition. The limits used in Theorem 1 need not provide such a finite gauge.

Diagonal or classical moment balance is weaker. On a single edge, the unit vectors (1,1)/2(1,1)/\sqrt2 and (1,−1)/2(1,-1)/\sqrt2 have the same diagonal probabilities but zero bilinear pairing. Their full density matrices, compared as in (8), differ. Theorem 3 cannot be invoked after discarding that difference.

5. The actual Conway source and the actual main-color energy

The complete Conway compiler in Orthogonal boundary packets under Conway composition uses the full Newton boundary space, the actual multiplication tensor, the actual turn, arbitrary full leaf packets, and the original closing row. Its expression graph, with that row included, is a tree of the kind above. Theorem 1 therefore gives an exact positive variational formula for its original scalar. All repeated-root derivative channels and the original leaf phases remain in the tensors.

There is also a direct application to the whole main-color energy, without using a complete-color Conway space for ordinary main colors. Fix a braid on bb strands and use the original NN-dimensional physical modules at each of its NN main-color orbit points. Set AN=⨁j=1NEnd⁡(Vj,N⊗b),dim⁡AN=N2b+1.(18) A_N=\bigoplus_{j=1}^N\operatorname{End}(V_{j,N}^{\otimes b}), \qquad \dim A_N=N^{2b+1}. \qquad\text{(18)} Equip this space with its coordinate Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Each signed crossing of the fixed word supplies one known vector in ANA_N. Componentwise matrix multiplication supplies a fixed bilinear tensor. Compile the word as any finite multiplication tree. Its output is the tuple of the original physical braid operators.

At the root use the explicit linear map which takes the prescribed (0,0)(0,0) partial pivotal trace in each color, with its zero-framing factor. Thus the uncontracted output is exactly YN=(c1,…,cN).(19) Y_N=(c_1,\ldots,c_N). \qquad\text{(19)} The map is given by the original pivot matrices and selected cut entry; it is not defined from the evaluated numbers cjc_j. The main-color cap-projection theorem proves, with precisely these original conventions, ∥YN∥2=EN=|KN|2+RN,0≤RN≤(N−1)(N/2)2b−2.(20) \|Y_N\|^2=E_N=|K_N|^2+R_N, \qquad 0\le R_N\le(N-1)(N/2)^{2b-2}. \qquad\text{(20)} In particular all the cap coherence in that theorem remains inside the root tensor in the present construction. It has not been replaced by an independent product window or an identity projector.

For this concrete tree, Theorem 1 says πNlog⁡EN=infHe>02πN∑vlog⁡∥Tv,N∥H.(21) \boxed{\quad \frac{\pi}{N}\log E_N =\inf_{H_e>0}\frac{2\pi}{N} \sum_v\log\|T_{v,N}\|_H. \quad} \qquad\text{(21)} The left side is finite: the ordinary trivial main color is one. The internal gauges in (21) are full matrices on the bundled space (18). The claim does not make the original physical crossing diagram acyclic, or make these gauges tensor products of single-wire metrics.

The diagonal physical-gauge hierarchy in the weighted plat-energy note is a useful restricted upper-bound problem. The equality here uses the full edge matrix gauges of a compiled tree. Restricting those gauges again would require a separate argument for equality.

6. A concrete geometric certificate and the remaining theorem

Suppose the original knot is hyperbolic and its complement volume is V>0V>0. A sufficient certificate for its volume identity is now:

  1. Specify positive full edge metrics for the actual tree in (18)–(19), independently of the unknown output.
  2. Prove that their full marginal defects (8) tend to zero.
  3. Prove that their local norm objective tends to the geometric value: 2πN∑vlog⁡∥Tv,N∥HN→V. \frac{2\pi}{N}\sum_v\log\|T_{v,N}\|_{H_N}\longrightarrow V.

Equivalently, if FN=∏v∥Tv,N∥HN=exp⁡(NV/(2π)+o(N))F_N=\prod_v\|T_{v,N}\|_{H_N} =\exp(NV/(2\pi)+o(N)) and SN=∑eδe,N→0S_N=\sum_e\delta_{e,N}\to0 for this fixed tree, Theorem 3 gives (16). These statements imply πN−1log⁡EN→V\pi N^{-1}\log E_N\to V. The polynomial remainder in (20) then gives the original Kashaev volume identity. Eventual nonvanishing is a consequence, rather than a premise.

This replaces a global closing-angle estimate by a local full-matrix balance certificate. It does not show that the classical angle equations already provide that certificate. Their scalar or diagonal balance must still be connected to the full matrices in (8), including their phases. The local Gaussian covariance of actual crossing kernels is one possible input to such a construction; no such global construction is proved here.

Nor may an infimum and a large-level limit simply be interchanged in (21). Candidate geometric metrics give upper bounds on the infimum. Theorem 3 supplies a lower comparison only when their full defects are controlled. Unknown escaping metric sequences are otherwise still allowed.

For the finite-exception problem, the certificate would have to survive every required fixed short partial filling. Its constants can depend on that fixed partial assignment; the filling coefficients remain fixed before NN tends to infinity. One must prove the certificate for all sufficiently long remaining assignments that give hyperbolic knots, and repeat it on the finitely many short-coordinate branches. Theorem 1 is valid on every such branch, but does not supply its geometric metrics. Exact reductions which leave a fixed hyperbolic core in an infinite assignment fiber still require a volume proof for that core. The variational reformulation does not remove that obligation.
