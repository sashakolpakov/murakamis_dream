Analytic Hermite coupons on the original positive coefficient circle

Private written proof, 17 November 2026.

The fixed-degree restriction on inserted coupons can be removed for functions holomorphic on the entire coefficient disk. Their full Hermite interpolants have degree as large as N−1N-1, but their original trace is governed by the value of the analytic function at the actual coefficient saddle, with an explicit analytic-norm error. Slowly varying exponential coupons have a relative estimate and remain in this class under any fixed number of ordinary products.

A function given only on a slit or near the saddle is different: its poles, cut banks, and endpoint currents remain in an exact formula below. In particular this theorem does not discard the original crossing’s cut at C≥2C\ge2, and does not prove closure under the physical turn.

1. Actual product, coefficient circle, and full Hermite interpolation

Use the original conventions N≥3,D=N−1,J=⌊D/2⌋,η=(−1)D,ca=2cos⁡((2a+1)π/N), N\ge3,\quad D=N-1,\quad J=\lfloor D/2\rfloor,\quad \eta=(-1)^D,\quad c_a=2\cos((2a+1)\pi/N), PN(C)=2TN(C/2)+2,ϕN(f)=ηN2[CD]rem⁡PNf,Fj(C)=KN(cj,C).(1) P_N(C)=2T_N(C/2)+2,\qquad \phi_N(f)=\eta N^2[C^D]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}f,\qquad F_j(C)=K_N(c_j,C). \qquad\text{(1)} Fix m≥3m\ge3 and ε>0\varepsilon>0. Consider all physical tuples 0≤ji≤J0\le j_i\le J with s=∑iji,k=s−D≥εN,fN(C)=∏i=1mFji(C),ΓN=ϕN(fN)>0.(2) s=\sum_i j_i,\qquad k=s-D\ge\varepsilon N,\qquad f_N(C)=\prod_{i=1}^mF_{j_i}(C),\qquad \Gamma_N=\phi_N(f_N)>0. \qquad\text{(2)} Every trace below includes canonical reduction and all repeated-root normals, even when the ordinary degree of fNf_N exceeds DD.

The positive coefficient source supplies positive Jacobi eigenvalues rj,b>0r_{j,b}>0, da=c0−ca≥0d_a=c_0-c_a\ge0, and 𝔞N=∏i,brji,b−1,HN(x)=∏i,b(1+rji,bx)∏a=0N−1(1−dax),ΓN=N2𝔞N[xk]HN.(3) \mathfrak a_N=\prod_{i,b}r_{j_i,b}^{-1},\qquad H_N(x)= \frac{\prod_{i,b}(1+r_{j_i,b}x)} {\prod_{a=0}^{N-1}(1-d_ax)},\qquad \Gamma_N=N^2\mathfrak a_N[x^k]H_N . \qquad\text{(3)} Let ρN\rho_N be its unique mean-kk saddle. Uniformly in (2), for sufficiently large NN, 0<a≤ρN≤b<1/4,cN≤VN≤CN,(4) 0<a\le\rho_N\le b<1/4,\qquad cN\le V_N\le CN, \qquad\text{(4)} where VNV_N is the exact tilted variance. Define ζN=c0−ρN−1,CN(θ)=c0−ρN−1e−iθ,−π≤θ≤π.(5) \zeta_N=c_0-\rho_N^{-1},\qquad C_N(\theta)=c_0-\rho_N^{-1}e^{-i\theta}, \quad-\pi\le\theta\le\pi . \qquad\text{(5)} The circle in (5) contains every physical root strictly inside, since da<4<ρN−1d_a<4<\rho_N^{-1}. All these circles and their interiors lie in one fixed disk centered at 22.

Choose a closed disk Ω0={|C−2|≤R0}\Omega_0=\{|C-2|\le R_0\} with R0>1/a+2R_0>1/a+2, and a larger open disk Ω1\Omega_1 with a fixed positive buffer around Ω0\Omega_0. Every coefficient circle has a fixed positive distance from ∂Ω0\partial\Omega_0; the Cauchy estimates below use this interior margin and norms on Ω0\Omega_0, not an uncontrolled norm on Ω1\Omega_1. For a function aNa_N holomorphic on Ω1\Omega_1, let qNq_N be its unique polynomial of degree at most DD matching the following data: qN(c)=aN(c),qN′(c)=aN′(c)(c a double physical root),(6) q_N(c)=a_N(c),\quad q_N'(c)=a_N'(c) \quad(c\text{ a double physical root}), \qquad\text{(6)} and only the value at the odd simple root. This is ordinary confluent interpolation with exactly NN conditions. Equivalently, (qN−aN)/PN(q_N-a_N)/P_N is holomorphic throughout Ω1\Omega_1. The polynomial qNq_N is an actual coupon in the original algebra, with its original spectral normals.

2. Exact source integral and the degree-free estimate

Let SρNS_{\rho_N} be the exact sum of independent Bernoulli and geometric variables in the coefficient source, and put χN(θ)=e−ikθHN(ρNeiθ)HN(ρN),pN=ℙ(SρN=k).(7) \chi_N(\theta)= e^{-ik\theta}\frac{H_N(\rho_Ne^{i\theta})}{H_N(\rho_N)}, \qquad p_N=\mathbb P(S_{\rho_N}=k). \qquad\text{(7)} The same source proves, uniformly, |χN(θ)|≤e−cNsin⁡2(θ/2),pN=(2πVN)−1/2(1+O(N−1)),(8) |\chi_N(\theta)|\le e^{-cN\sin^2(\theta/2)},\qquad p_N=(2\pi V_N)^{-1/2}(1+O(N^{-1})), \qquad\text{(8)} ℙ(SρN=k+h)/pN=1+Oh(N−1)(h fixed).(9) \mathbb P(S_{\rho_N}=k+h)/p_N=1+O_h(N^{-1}) \quad(h\text{ fixed}). \qquad\text{(9)} These are statements about this actual finite product, not a probability model replacing the original source.

Theorem 1. For the full Hermite coupon (6), ϕN(qNfN)ΓN=12πpN∫−ππχN(θ)aN(CN(θ))dθ=aN(ζN)+O(N−1MN),(10) \boxed{\quad \frac{\phi_N(q_Nf_N)}{\Gamma_N} =\frac1{2\pi p_N}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \chi_N(\theta)a_N(C_N(\theta))\,d\theta =a_N(\zeta_N)+O(N^{-1}M_N), \quad} \qquad\text{(10)} where MN=sup⁡Ω0|aN|M_N=\sup_{\Omega_0}|a_N|. The constant is uniform in (2) and aNa_N; no bound on the degree or on the polynomial coefficient norm of qNq_N is imposed.

Proof of the exact formula. On the positively oriented spectral circle |C−c0|=ρN−1|C-c_0|=\rho_N^{-1}, the original trace is ϕN(qNfN)=ηN22πi∮qN(C)fN(C)PN(C)dC.(11) \phi_N(q_Nf_N)=\frac{\eta N^2}{2\pi i} \oint \frac{q_N(C)f_N(C)}{P_N(C)}\,dC. \qquad\text{(11)} The residues are precisely the full physical trace. Replacing qNq_N by aNa_N changes the integrand by a holomorphic function: the quotient in (6) times the ordinary polynomial fNf_N. Its contour integral is exactly zero.

Set z=c0−Cz=c_0-C, then x=1/zx=1/z. The spectral circle is positive in zz and negative in xx; this reversal and the sign PN(c0−z)=(−1)N∏a(z−da)P_N(c_0-z)=(-1)^N\prod_a(z-d_a), together with η=(−1)N−1\eta=(-1)^{N-1}, give ϕN(qNfN)=N2𝔞N2πi∮|x|=ρN+HN(x)aN(c0−x−1)x−k−1dx.(12) \phi_N(q_Nf_N)= \frac{N^2\mathfrak a_N}{2\pi i} \oint_{|x|=\rho_N}^{+} H_N(x)a_N(c_0-x^{-1})x^{-k-1}\,dx . \qquad\text{(12)} Only values on this circle are used; no analytic extension of aN(c0−x−1)a_N(c_0-x^{-1}) to x=0x=0 is asserted. Divide by the exact uninserted coefficient (3) to obtain (10).

Proof of the error. A useful consequence of (8)–(9) is 12πpN∫−ππχN(θ)sin⁡θdθ=O(N−1),1pN∫−ππ|χN(θ)|θ2dθ=O(N−1).(13) \frac1{2\pi p_N}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\chi_N(\theta) \sin\theta\,d\theta=O(N^{-1}), \qquad \frac1{p_N}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} |\chi_N(\theta)|\theta^2\,d\theta=O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(13)} The first identity is the difference of the probabilities at k−1k-1 and k+1k+1, divided by 2ipN2ip_N. The second follows from the Gaussian bound (8) and pN≍N−1/2p_N\asymp N^{-1/2}. On a fixed small arc put AN(θ)=aN(CN(θ))A_N(\theta)=a_N(C_N(\theta)). Cauchy’s estimate on the circle’s fixed buffer inside Ω0\Omega_0 gives |AN′|+|AN″|≤CMN|A_N'|+|A_N''|\le CM_N, so AN(θ)=AN(0)+AN′(0)sin⁡θ+O(MNθ2).(14) A_N(\theta)=A_N(0)+A_N'(0)\sin\theta+O(M_N\theta^2). \qquad\text{(14)} Outside this arc, (8) is exponentially small, and |AN|≤MN|A_N|\le M_N. Equations (13)–(14) prove (10). The polynomial quotient was canceled exactly before any absolute estimate. ▫\square

For later use, the same proof gives a local version which does not require a holomorphic function on the whole disk. Suppose AN(θ)A_N(\theta) is bounded and piecewise defined on the outer circle, has one C2C^2 branch on |θ|≤δ|\theta|\le\delta for a fixed 0<δ<π0<\delta<\pi, and let Mout=sup[−π,π]|AN|,M1=|AN′(0)|,M2=sup|θ|≤δ|AN″(θ)|. M_{\mathrm{ out}}=\sup_{[-\pi,\pi]}|A_N|,\quad M_1=|A_N'(0)|,\quad M_2=\sup_{|\theta|\le\delta}|A_N''(\theta)|. Then |12πpN∫χNAN−AN(0)|≤Cδ{M1+M2N+Ne−cδNMout}.(15) \left| \frac1{2\pi p_N}\int\chi_N A_N-A_N(0) \right| \le C_\delta\left\{ \frac{M_1+M_2}{N} +\sqrt N\,e^{-c_\delta N}M_{\mathrm{ out}}\right\}. \qquad\text{(15)} Subtract AN(0)+AN′(0)sin⁡θA_N(0)+A_N'(0)\sin\theta to prove this. The tail containing M1M_1 is absorbed into CM1/NCM_1/N. Formula (15) estimates only the outer integral; the exact source currents for a slit function are retained in section 4.

3. Slowly varying actual coupons and ordinary composition

Theorem 2. Suppose bNb_N is holomorphic on Ω1\Omega_1 and LN=1+supΩ0|bN|=o(N),aN=ebN.(16) L_N=1+\sup_{\Omega_0}|b_N|=o(\sqrt N),\qquad a_N=e^{b_N}. \qquad\text{(16)} For its full Hermite coupon qNq_N, uniformly in (2), ϕN(qNfN)ebN(ζN)ΓN=1+O(LN2/N).(17) \boxed{\quad \frac{\phi_N(q_Nf_N)} {e^{b_N(\zeta_N)}\Gamma_N} =1+O(L_N^2/N). \quad} \qquad\text{(17)} In particular the original observed trace is nonzero for large NN. It has the same exponential rate as ΓN\Gamma_N, since |bN(ζN)|=o(N)|b_N(\zeta_N)|=o(N).

Proof. Fixed-buffer Cauchy estimates give, on a small fixed angular arc, BN(θ):=bN(CN(θ))−bN(ζN),|BN(θ)|≤CLN|θ|,|BN″(θ)|≤CLN. B_N(\theta):=b_N(C_N(\theta))-b_N(\zeta_N),\quad |B_N(\theta)|\le CL_N|\theta|,\quad |B_N''(\theta)|\le CL_N . Taylor’s formula for the exponential implies |eBN(θ)−1−BN′(0)sinθ|≤C(LN+LN2)θ2eCLN|θ|.(18) \left|e^{B_N(\theta)}-1-B_N'(0)\sin\theta\right| \le C(L_N+L_N^2)\theta^2 e^{CL_N|\theta|}. \qquad\text{(18)} Its normalized integral against (8) is O(LN2/N)O(L_N^2/N). Indeed completing the square in −cNθ2+CLN|θ|-cN\theta^2+CL_N|\theta| gives the bound 1pN∫θ2e−cNθ2+CLN|θ|dθ≤CN(1+LN2/N)eCLN2/N=O(N−1).(19) \frac1{p_N}\int\theta^2 e^{-cN\theta^2+CL_N|\theta|} \,d\theta \le \frac{C}{N}(1+L_N^2/N)e^{CL_N^2/N}=O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(19)} The linear term in (18) is O(LN/N)O(L_N/N) by (13). On the complementary arc, the normalized integral is at most CNe−cN+2LNC\sqrt N e^{-cN+2L_N}, which is negligible. Apply the exact formula (10) to obtain (17). ▫\square

The hypothesis is on an analytic representative, not on the coefficients of its degree-DD interpolant. For example bN=λNbb_N=\lambda_N b, with bb fixed holomorphic and λN=o(N)\lambda_N=o(\sqrt N), is allowed.

This class has an exact ordinary composition law. If qN,1,…,qN,rq_{N,1},\ldots,q_{N,r} interpolate ebN,1,…,ebN,re^{b_{N,1}},\ldots, e^{b_{N,r}}, then their canonical product interpolates e∑νbN,νe^{\sum_\nu b_{N,\nu}}, including every mixed product-rule term at the repeated roots. For fixed rr, condition (16) is preserved. Therefore (17) applies to this canonical product, with its actual leading coefficient exp⁡(∑νbN,ν(ζN))\exp(\sum_\nu b_{N,\nu}(\zeta_N)). No physical turn or additional unspecified closing map occurs in this composition assertion.

For three rows on a strict macroscopic compact, the common characteristic theorem further identifies ζN=2−4/L(𝒋/N)+O(N−1).(20) \zeta_N=2-4/L(\mathbf j/N)+O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(20)} Apply its exact observed-CC asymptotic and the coefficient source’s observed-CC formula to the same trace to prove (20). For bounded analytic coupons this replacement changes (10) by O(MN/N)O(M_N/N); under (16) it changes the relative leading factor in (17) by 1+O(LN/N)1+O(L_N/N). It supplies no global continuation of the selected branch.

4. Poles, cuts, and the canonical remainder

There is an exact version retaining every obstruction to the disk hypothesis. Let aNa_N be a selected holomorphic branch near all physical roots, with its full jets defining qNq_N as in (6). Suppose it continues to the interior of the coefficient circle after removing specified poles and cuts. Cut banks and endpoint arcs are oriented as inner boundaries of that punctured domain, so that the domain lies on their left. Assume these contours avoid the physical roots and their limiting integrals exist; otherwise keep the finite excision contours themselves.

Write ℬN\mathscr B_N for the sum of the oriented inner cut-bank and endpoint integrals, and let the indicated residues be the isolated nonphysical poles. Then, exactly, ϕN(qNfN)=ηN22πi∮outeraNfNPNdC+𝒞N,(21) \boxed{\quad \phi_N(q_Nf_N)= \frac{\eta N^2}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{ outer}} \frac{a_N f_N}{P_N}\,dC+\mathscr C_N, \quad} \qquad\text{(21)} 𝒞N=ηN22πiℬN−ηN2∑nonphysicalpolesRes⁡aNfNPN.(22) \mathscr C_N= \frac{\eta N^2}{2\pi i}\mathscr B_N -\eta N^2 \sum_{\mathrm{ nonphysical\ poles}} \operatorname{Res}\frac{a_Nf_N}{P_N}. \qquad\text{(22)} This is the residue theorem on the punctured disk: its physical residues equal those of the full Hermite coupon. No external coefficient or first normal has been substituted.

For a real slit [u,v][u,v] directed toward the outer boundary, the two banks contribute ∫uv(aN,+−aN,−)(C)fN(C)PN(C)dC(23) \int_u^v \frac{(a_{N,+}-a_{N,-})(C)f_N(C)}{P_N(C)}\,dC \qquad\text{(23)} with this orientation. Endpoint arcs remain unless their limits are proved to vanish. There is no branch reset on the outer circle; its successive arcs use the prescribed continued branch.

On a cut-free saddle arc, (15) gives the exact insertion criterion ϕN(qNfN)ΓN=aN(ζN)+Oδ(M1+M2N+Ne−cδNMout)+𝒞NΓN.(24) \frac{\phi_N(q_Nf_N)}{\Gamma_N} =a_N(\zeta_N) +O_\delta\!\left(\frac{M_1+M_2}{N} +\sqrt N e^{-c_\delta N}M_{\mathrm{ out}}\right) +\frac{\mathscr C_N}{\Gamma_N}. \qquad\text{(24)} In the disk-holomorphic case the last term is exactly zero, which is the substantive simplification in Theorems 1–2. For a general slit representative it is an original source term, not an interpolation error. A nonzero leading observation follows only if the displayed errors and the actual current are small relative to aN(ζN)a_N(\zeta_N).

The original Gaussian representative of a fixed crossing has a spectral cut at C≥2C\ge2. Its canonical polynomial is entire, but replacing that polynomial on the circle by one selected Gaussian branch falls under (21)–(24), not the disk theorem. No bound on its cut current, outer-circle growth, or subsequent physical turn is supplied here.
