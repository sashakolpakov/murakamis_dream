Coverage check: normalized Racah factorials and hyperideal volume

29 October 2026. Coverage only: primary-source definitions and theorem statements were checked. Their proofs are not inputs to our independent factorial/normal theorem or local geometry theorem. The local 6j–hyperideal-volume relationship is established literature; no novelty is claimed for recovering it here.

Our convention is zeta=exp(pi i/N), [u]=(zetau-zeta-u)/(zeta-zeta^-1), integer graph spins j<=floor((N-1)/2), and four face sums>=N-1. W_N is the positive magnitude of the regularized face-normalized scalar, with [N] removed before evaluation. Its exact Casimir angles are pi-(2j+1)pi/N. The uniform result includes closed-chamber sequences and O(log N) error for log W_N; its fixed normal jets are derivatives in zeta=q exp(t), not merely coefficients in a large-N expansion.

Closest prior statements

Costantino (2007). Definition3.2 gives the strict hyperbolic-type conditions 1<face sum<2 and triangle deficits between0 and1. Theorem3.9 gives a simple pole and the volume growth of the regularized [n]6j coefficient. His [u] uses q^(u/2), so q_paper=zeta^2 makes the quantum integers IDENTICAL to ours. The D-block volume is twice the tetrahedron volume, matching our pi/N normalization. This already covers the interior leading rate, without an odd-level restriction; parity/composite levels alone are not a new distinction. Definitions3.1–3.2 and Theorem3.9, published article.

Chen–Murakami (2023; preprint2017). Theorems1–2 give the volume limit when a tetrahedron has an ideal or ultra-ideal vertex and a refined Gram-determinant prefactor for specified rational-angle sequences. They take odd r, xi_r=exp(2pi i/r), q=xi_r^2 and [u]=(q(u/2)-q(-u/2))/(q(1/2)-q(-1/2)). Their scaling is spin/r=(pi-angle)/(4pi). Substituting r=2N would match our quantum integer but violates their odd-r hypothesis. Their explicit halfshift is also prior art. Theorems1–2 and definition in§2, journal DOI.

Belletti–Detcherry–Kalfagianni–Yang. Theorem1.2 gives the sharp, tuple-uniform v8+O(log r/r) upper capacity for r-admissible6j symbols; AppendixTheoremA.1 states the ideal/hyperideal volume limit under explicit face/deficit conditions. Their root is exp(2pi i/r), r odd, with doubled integer colors. This is substantial overlapping coverage, but a universal v8 bound is not the same statement as our label-dependent two-sided closed-chamber volume estimate. Setting r=2N introduces the [N] poles that their odd-root evaluation avoids. Theorem1.2,§2 and AppendixA.

Belletti–Yang (2025). Theorem1.4 covers generalized hyperbolic volume growth under a negative diagonal-cofactor hypothesis. Theorem1.5 states a refined asymptotic along sequences with fixed sides of the color wall and a limiting tetrahedron without ideal vertices. The root remains exp(2pi i/r), r odd. Their§2 records the earlier ideal/hyperideal cases as well. These results preclude presenting the local volume mechanism as new. Theorems1.4–1.5 and§2.2, arXiv version.

Costantino–Murakami (2013). Their graph face model and6j symbols use non-integral quantum-group representations and relate them to truncated-tetrahedron volume. This is relevant background, not an identified equality with our ordinary highest-weight Hermite source. Publisher abstract and primary paper.

What this bounded check establishes

The verified interior local volume claim is covered, most directly by Costantino after the explicit root substitution. Our exact all-chamber O(log N) estimate, scalar generic-normal/inverse-jet bounds and retained [2N] partner current have more specific technical statements; this bounded statement check neither proves they are absent elsewhere nor supports a novelty claim. A refined large-level asymptotic is not automatically a theorem about fixed-root generic-q jets, and a lowest-nonzero Laurent coefficient is not an arbitrary prescribed source normal.

None of these local statements supplies our literal marked-source normalization, a positive completed recoupling Gram, or general graph sewing/peripheral constraints. Turaev–Viro conclusions are not silently substituted for original Kashaev invariants. The full-source problem remains separate.
