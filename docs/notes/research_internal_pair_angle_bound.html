The two selected angles of a positive internal pair

Research proof, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

Consider two consecutive selected vertices, with one complete outside endpoint attached to each of them. This note proves that both selected angles have real part less than π/2\pi/2, throughout the charge cube, when both crossing powers are integers at least three. The proof uses the coupled period equations. Separate height bounds would not imply this conclusion.

The result concerns geometry. It does not assert an asymptotic for a complete-end kernel at a complex selected angle, or a nonzero coefficient in the finite two-vertex source.

1. The family and the first possible boundary point

Let the powers be n,p∈ℤ≥3n,p\in\mathbb Z_{\ge3}, and let the charges belong to [0,1]2[0,1]^2. The fractional-charge theorem gives the continuous positive geometric family. At an active vertex write its angle as t=a−iH,0<a<π,0<H<23/20.(1) t=a-iH,\qquad 0<a<\pi,\quad 0<H<23/20. \qquad\text{(1)} At a zero-charge or complete endpoint the angle is zero. The bounds we need from the maximum-phase theorem are cosh⁡H<7/4,a<9π4nχ.(2) \cosh H<7/4,\qquad a<\frac{9\pi}{4n}\chi . \qquad\text{(2)} The original return from this vertex to a neighbor of angle uu is r(t,u)=asinh⁡(tan⁡(t/2)cos⁡(u/2))=k+ig.(3) r(t,u)=\operatorname{asinh}\bigl(\tan(t/2)\cos(u/2)\bigr)=k+ig. \qquad\text{(3)} Its branch has k>0k>0 and −π/2<g<π/2-\pi/2<g<\pi/2 when the first vertex is active. There are two incidences, one to a complete endpoint. Their returns satisfy k0+k1=nH2,g0+g1=na2−πχ.(4) k_0+k_1=\frac{nH}{2},\qquad g_0+g_1=\frac{na}{2}-\pi\chi . \qquad\text{(4)}

Theorem 1. Throughout this charge cube, both selected angles satisfy 0≤ℜt<π/2.(5) 0\le \Re t<\pi/2. \qquad\text{(5)}

It suffices to exclude a first point with a=π/2a=\pi/2. Indeed, join the complete point to any prescribed charge by the straight charge segment. Along this segment the geometric solution is continuous. At a first such point the other selected angle still has real part at most π/2\pi/2. A vertex of power at least five cannot be the first one, by (2).

At the proposed first point put z=tan⁡(t/2)=sech⁡H−itanh⁡H=e−iω,ω=arctan⁡(sinh⁡H).(6) z=\tan(t/2)=\operatorname{sech}H-i\tanh H=e^{-i\omega}, \qquad \omega=\arctan(\sinh H). \qquad\text{(6)} Let C=cos⁡(u/2)C=\cos(u/2) for the other selected endpoint. Its real angle is at most π/2\pi/2, and its height is less than 23/2023/20. Therefore ℜC2≥12,0≤arg⁡C<arctan⁡(23/40).(7) \Re C^2\ge\frac12,\qquad 0\le\arg C<\arctan(23/40). \qquad\text{(7)} The first inequality follows from ℜC2=(1+cos⁡(ℜu)cosh⁡(−ℑu))/2\Re C^2=(1+\cos(\Re u)\cosh(-\Im u))/2. The complete-end return r0=asinh⁡zr_0=\operatorname{asinh}z has the exact components k0=asinh⁡sech⁡H,g0=−arctan⁡(2sinh⁡(H/2)).(8) k_0=\operatorname{asinh}\sqrt{\operatorname{sech}H}, \qquad g_0=-\arctan\bigl(\sqrt2\sinh(H/2)\bigr). \qquad\text{(8)} These formulas follow by squaring sinh⁡(k0+ig0)=z\sinh(k_0+ig_0)=z, with the positive real and negative imaginary branches. 4. Scope and reproducibility

The argument uses the original two incidences at each selected vertex, including its complete outside end. It does not apply to an arbitrary interior vertex of a longer selected path. It also uses the integer alternatives n=3n=3, n=4n=4, and n≥5n\ge5; no assertion for every noninteger power between three and five is made.

The bound places the two selected angles in a smaller regular region for subsequent contour work. It does not by itself prove ℜψ(t,0)>−ℑt\Re\psi(t,0)>-\Im t, nor does it control the endpoint and alias terms in a finite-source contour.
