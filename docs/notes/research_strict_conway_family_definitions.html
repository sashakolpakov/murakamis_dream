What is fixed in a strict Conway filling family?

18 November 2026. Expository research note. This note spells out the definitions and quantifiers in the frozen statements. It proves no new volume identity and changes none of those statements.

The phrase “fix an admissible strict Conway tangle construction, its crossing circles, and a half-twist pattern giving one knot” means: choose one finite recipe for assembling two-strand boxes; keep a separate label and crossing disk at every elementary box; check a finite strictness test; and choose the local even/odd strand connections so that the closed main strands form one circle. The integers subsequently varied add full twists inside those same disks. They do not change the recipe or its component pairing.

1. Ordered four-endpoint tangles

A box is a three-ball containing a tangle whose four boundary endpoints are fixed and ordered. In the rectangular drawing they are 0=bottom left,1=bottom right,2=top right,3=top left. 0=\text{bottom left},\qquad 1=\text{bottom right},\qquad 2=\text{top right},\qquad 3=\text{top left}. The box may also contain closed components. Those components must be retained when boxes are assembled. Boundary order matters: an abstract isomorphism of tangles that changes the attaching points is not the specified operation.

The literal operations, in the conventions of [G, §1], are as follows.

  • Product FGFG. Place FF below GG, join F2F_2 to G1G_1 and F3F_3 to G0G_0, and keep the exposed tuple (F0,F1,G2,G3)(F_0,F_1,G_2,G_3). This is an ordered stack.
  • Turn T(F)T(F). Rotate the entire box clockwise through a quarter turn, carrying its strands, disks, framings and marks. The new ordered tuple is (F1,F2,F3,F0)(F_1,F_2,F_3,F_0).
  • Cylindrical closure C(F)C(F). Join endpoints 00 to 33 and 11 to 22 by the prescribed empty outside arcs.

Products can be reparenthesized by rescaling their stack collars. Their factors cannot simply be exchanged. Likewise, the physical half-turn T2T^2 must not be erased merely because the corresponding operator on a particular finite source algebra squares to the identity. The distinctions between physical moves and algebraic source identities are explicit in [C, §4]. In particular, there is no rule T(FG)=T(F)T(G)T(FG)=T(F)T(G).

Write RnR^n for the standard two-strand braid with n∈ℤn\in\mathbb Z signed half twists; R0R^0 is the two through strands. The elementary filled leaf is hi(ni)=T(Rni).(1) h_i(n_i)=T(R^{n_i}). \qquad\text{(1)} An expression is a finite rooted construction tree built from these leaves, products and turns, and closed as C(FG)C(FG). There are H≥2H\ge2 separately labeled leaf occurrences. Each occurs once and has its own parameter nin_i. Two leaves with the same numerical twist are still two different boxes.

This is a presentation of a Conway algebraic, or arborescent, tangle. It is not a claim that every presentation of every arborescent knot is strict, or that every hyperbolic knot has this presentation.

2. Crossing circles and the retained half twists

Before filling a leaf, keep an auxiliary unknot cic_i surrounding its two strands. It bounds the original crossing disk, punctured twice by the main strands. The disks lie in the separate leaf boxes and retain their original local framings. Turns move these disks along with their boxes.

At leaf ii, retain ϵi∈{0,1,−1} \epsilon_i\in\{0,1,-1\} half twists: none, one positive, or one negative. Remove the other full twists but keep cic_i. After stacking and closing, this gives an augmented link with HH labeled auxiliary components and its main components. Denote its exterior by YτϵY_\tau^\epsilon.

On the boundary torus of cic_i, μi\mu_i is the meridian of that auxiliary component. The disk longitude λi\lambda_i is its push-off in the original crossing-disk framing. They are a primitive ordered integral basis. They are not the meridian and preferred longitude of the final main knot.

Use the orientations of [G, §1] and [M, §§6–7]. Filling the slope μi+piλi,pi∈ℤ,(2) \mu_i+p_i\lambda_i,\qquad p_i\in\mathbb Z, \qquad\text{(2)} removes the crossing circle and inserts pip_i full disk turns with the convention ni=ϵi−2pi.(3) n_i=\epsilon_i-2p_i. \qquad\text{(3)} Thus one positive unit of this surgery coordinate subtracts two positive braid half twists. Equation (3) fixes the sign convention; one must not replace it by ϵi+2pi\epsilon_i+2p_i while retaining the same oriented peripheral basis. At pi=0p_i=0, meridional filling restores just the retained ϵi\epsilon_i crossing.

The surgery is performed in the original disk neighborhood. Its ambient manifold remains S3S^3. The main cusp is not filled. If only some crossing circles are filled, the others remain actual components of the link exterior.

Both +1+1 and −1-1 give odd endpoint pairing, but their signed half-twist markings are retained. A comparison of those two parents also changes the description of the primitive meridian; it is not an instruction to forget the sign.

3. Strictness: a finite real-variable test

Strictness is a condition on the construction tree before choosing the large integer twists. Its variables are normalized source-state or angle variables, not the integers nin_i, the surgery coefficients pip_i, or the quantum level NN.

Assign a real output variable to every open subexpression. Require: 0<ai<12at every elementary leaf,y=1−xacross a turn,max⁡(x,y)<z<x+y<1at every retained product.(4) \begin{aligned} &0<a_i<\tfrac12 &&\text{at every elementary leaf},\\ &y=1-x &&\text{across a turn},\\ &\max(x,y)<z<x+y<1 &&\text{at every retained product}. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(4)} At the final cylindrical root C(FG)C(FG), remove its forced last product and require only that the two outputs be bb and 1−b1-b. Do not impose the strict product inequality on that removed gate. The construction is strict when these conditions have a solution.

The exact compiler in [C, §2] replaces this feasibility problem by four possible open-output sets: L=(0,12),H=(12,1),A=(0,1),⌀. L=(0,\tfrac12),\qquad H=(\tfrac12,1),\qquad A=(0,1),\qquad \varnothing. Here HH as a type label is unrelated to the number HH of leaves. A leaf has type LL. A turn exchanges L,HL,H, preserves AA, and preserves emptiness. The product table is

Lower / upper type LL HH AA
LL AA HH AA
HH HH ⌀\varnothing HH
AA AA HH AA

An empty child makes its ancestors empty. The table is symmetric as a feasibility statement; this does not make the physical product commutative. At closure the permitted pairs are AA,AL,AH,LH(5) AA,\quad AL,\quad AH,\quad LH \qquad\text{(5)} and their reversals. This is exactly the condition that the two open output sets contain complementary points.

For example, two LL inputs can produce any z∈(0,1)z\in(0,1): choose equal inputs aa with z/2<a<min⁡(z,1/2)z/2<a<\min(z,1/2). Two HH inputs cannot satisfy x+y<1x+y<1. These illustrate why the type test concerns genuine open intervals, rather than nonnegative boundary states.

The geometric meaning is substantial. For a strict construction, the frozen marked-geometry theorem constructs the complete augmented-link metric from the original ideal cells. The strict state places the variational problem in its interior; the proved optimizer is unique and nondegenerate. The original disks and primitive cusp markings survive this construction [G, §1; M, §§1–7].

Strictness alone does not say that an arbitrary short filling is hyperbolic or satisfies the volume identity. Conversely, failure of the strict test for one presentation is not a counterexample to that identity, nor an assertion that its finite invariant vanishes.

4. The separate knot test, and a checked example

Ignore over/under information just for the purpose of following components. The three possible pairings of four terminals are I=(03)(12),U=(01)(23),X=(02)(13). I=(03)(12),\qquad U=(01)(23),\qquad X=(02)(13). An even leaf T(R2k)T(R^{2k}) has pairing UU; an odd leaf T(R2k+1)T(R^{2k+1}) has pairing XX. A turn exchanges I,UI,U and fixes XX. Products use the actual attachment convention of §1:

Lower / upper pairing II UU XX
II II UU XX
UU UU UU, plus an internal circle UU
XX XX UU II

Every internal circle is recorded and kept. At the end, cylindrical closure adds the pairing II: an output UU or XX gives one main component, whereas an output II gives two. An internal circle can never reconnect to an endpoint later. This is the full knot test, as used in [M, §7] and [V, §1].

Here is a small actual admissible construction: τ=C((h1h2)(h3h4)),ϵ=(0,1,1,1).(6) \tau=C\bigl((h_1h_2)(h_3h_4)\bigr),\qquad \epsilon=(0,1,1,1). \qquad\text{(6)} Both retained products have type LL→ALL\to A; the closing pair is AAAA. More explicitly, take every ai=3/10a_i=3/10 and both retained outputs 1/21/2. Then 3/10<1/2<3/5<13/10<1/2<3/5<1, and the two closing outputs are complementary. This is a feasible strict state, not a claim about the maximizing state.

For the component check, the lower child has pairing U⋅X=UU\cdot X=U; the upper child has X⋅X=IX\cdot X=I; their product has U⋅I=UU\cdot I=U. No U⋅UU\cdot U product occurs, so no internal circle is created. Closing UU by II traces the single cycle 0→1→2→3→0. 0\longrightarrow1\longrightarrow2 \longrightarrow3\longrightarrow0. Thus (6) gives one main knot for every integer tuple (n1,n2,n3,n4)=(−2p1,1−2p2,1−2p3,1−2p4). (n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4) =(-2p_1,1-2p_2,1-2p_3,1-2p_4). Hyperbolicity and the volume conclusion are guaranteed by the cited theorem only in its stated long regime. Changing the example to four odd leaves gives I⋅I=II\cdot I=I, hence two main components. Strictness and the knot test really are different tests.

5. What “admissible” adds

In this discussion, “admissible” is shorthand for a specified bundle of data and checked hypotheses; it is not a further universal topological adjective. The bundle consists of:

  1. A fixed finite literal expression with H≥2H\ge2, the ordered attachments of §1, and distinct labeled leaf occurrences.
  2. The original crossing circles, their disjoint leaf neighborhoods, crossing disks, peripheral bases, and signed half-twist markings.
  3. A successful strictness test (4), equivalently the recursive table.
  4. A half-twist pattern whose complete component traversal gives one main component, including the internal-circle check.

For the scalar invariant one also fixes its single reduced cut and framing convention. The frozen statement uses unknot value one and the specified correction from blackboard to zero framing [V, §1]. These normalization data do not add a new topological restriction on the knot.

There is no extra requirement that both closing children be completed boxes: the current strict theorem allows bare or turned elementary closing children. The older completed-root grammar remains the scope of a separate signed local expansion, as [V, §1] explicitly explains. The general strict theorem uses the pure source and imposes no strict-quarter leaf bound or signed-packet prerequisite. Nor does admissibility mean “already known to satisfy the desired volume identity”; the combinatorial tests precede that theorem.

6. The order of quantifiers in the frozen result

Fix the data in §5. The frozen theorem [V, Theorem 1.1] supplies a finite threshold P0=P0(τ,ϵ)P_0=P_0(\tau,\epsilon) such that, for every fixed integer tuple 𝒑\mathbf p satisfying |pi|>P0for all i,(7) |p_i|>P_0\quad\text{for all }i, \qquad\text{(7)} the resulting knot is hyperbolic and limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(Kϵ,𝒑)|=Vol⁡(S3\Kϵ,𝒑).(8) \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K_{\epsilon,\mathbf p})| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K_{\epsilon,\mathbf p}). \qquad\text{(8)} The limit runs through all sufficiently large integer levels, not only a prime or odd subsequence. The precise source theorem also gives a nonzero leading asymptotic, with its framing phases retained.

The logical order is fix the construction and pattern;choose a long fixed tuple;N→∞.(9) \boxed{\ \text{fix the construction and pattern}\ ;\ \text{choose a long fixed tuple}\ ;\ N\longrightarrow\infty.\ } \qquad\text{(9)} “Independently long” means no constraint on relative signs or ratios of the coordinates. It does not allow 𝒑\mathbf p or the construction tree to grow with NN. A maximum over the finitely many retained patterns gives a single threshold for one fixed construction if desired; no threshold uniform over all constructions is asserted.

Since ni=ϵi−2pin_i=\epsilon_i-2p_i, the same condition can be stated using large absolute physical crossing powers, with a correspondingly adjusted bound and the fixed parity pattern.

The geometric theorem also permits filling a subset of crossing circles by long slopes while leaving the others unfilled [G, §1]. That is a theorem near the complete augmented parent. It is not the assertion that one may first perform arbitrary short fillings and then invoke the same theorem on the new exterior.

7. The stronger arbitrary-partial-filling target

Fix a proper subset I⊊{1,…,H}I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,H\} and any fixed integers aia_i, i∈Ii\in I, with no longness condition. Fill those circles first and keep the entire resulting source and its remaining boundary data. The target is a bound B(τ,ϵ,I,𝒂) B(\tau,\epsilon,I,\mathbf a) such that (8) holds for every fixed completion with pi=ai(i∈I),|pj|>B(τ,ϵ,I,𝒂)(j∉I),(10) p_i=a_i\ (i\in I),\qquad |p_j|>B(\tau,\epsilon,I,\mathbf a) \ (j\notin I), \qquad\text{(10)} whenever the final filled knot is hyperbolic. The threshold may depend on the already fixed short fillings. The statement must remain applicable after further partial fillings; when no free coordinate remains, the iteration stops.

The intermediate exterior need not itself be hyperbolic. Discarding such an intermediate would change the target unless its relevant hyperbolic descendants were separately controlled [P, §§3–4, 8].

Condition (7) misses infinitely many assignments when H≥2H\ge2: one coordinate can stay short while another becomes arbitrarily large. Those are untreated assignments, not counterexamples. Distinct assignments can also represent the same knot. Thus “all coordinates long,” “finitely many exceptional assignments for one template,” and “finitely many exceptional knot types” are different assertions.

Recent general local source results concerning amplitudes, critical schemes, transverse normal forms or exact current identities do not change these quantifiers by themselves. To prove (10), they must be assembled into a theorem for the entire actual partially filled source, retaining its closing functional and boundary terms and proving the geometric contribution is nonzero. This note makes no such additional assembly claim.
