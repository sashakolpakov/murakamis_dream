The original Green pairing as a finite algebraic period and its exact Mellin cost

31 October 2026. analytical research theorem. Unpublished. The kernel below is the original nodal Green tail with its original face extraction. Its contour form is an exact reformulation, not a new quantum invariant. The final volume comparison remains unproved.

1. The finite source data

Use the exact sine-binomial and finite-moment theorem. Put

M=⌊N/2⌋−1,0≤a≤M,m=M−a,h=a+1,q=eπi/N,sj=2sin⁡(πj/N),Sj=∏r=1jsr. M=\lfloor N/2\rfloor-1,\quad 0\le a\le M,\quad m=M-a,\quad h=a+1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad s_j=2\sin(\pi j/N),\quad S_j=\prod_{r=1}^j s_r.

Let W be the MULTISET of 2a+2 values

W=Wa(x,y)={xqa−2r,yqa−2r:0≤r≤a}. W=W_a(x,y)=\{xq^{a-2r},yq^{a-2r}:0\le r\le a\}. \tag{1}

Keep all repeated nodes. Write H_r for the complete homogeneous polynomial on a multiset. The original tail identity is

Ta,M(x,c,y)=Da,m𝔼Hm({c}∪XW),Da,m=∏i=1msis2a+i+1. T_{a,M}(x,c,y)=D_{a,m}\,\mathbb E H_m(\{c\}\cup XW), \qquad D_{a,m}=\prod_{i=1}^m s_i s_{2a+i+1}. \tag{2}

Here X=XN,a>0X=X_{N,a}>0 is the independently constructed beta-product variable. Its actual Mellin transform MN,a(t)=𝔼XtM_{N,a}(t)=\mathbb E X^t exists on

−h<t<N−2h+1,MN,a(t+1)MN,a(t)=sin⁡(π(h+t)/N)sin⁡(π(2h+t)/N) -h<t<N-2h+1, \qquad \frac{M_{N,a}(t+1)}{M_{N,a}(t)} =\frac{\sin(\pi(h+t)/N)}{\sin(\pi(2h+t)/N)} \tag{3}

whenever both moments in the ratio exist. For nonnegative integer r this is exactly the retained sine product A_r. In particular all integer moments from -a to m exist. No moments beyond the actual domain in (3) will be used.

In fact the integer h makes this transform FINITE:

MN,a(t)=∏j=h2h−1sin⁡(πj/N)sin⁡(π(j+t)/N). M_{N,a}(t)=\prod_{j=h}^{2h-1} \frac{\sin(\pi j/N)}{\sin(\pi(j+t)/N)}. \tag{3a}

One concrete choice is X=∏j=h2h−1Yj1/NX=\prod_{j=h}^{2h-1}Y_j^{1/N}, with independent positive variables of density

sin⁡(πj/N)πyj/N−11+ydy,y>0. \frac{\sin(\pi j/N)}\pi\, \frac{y^{j/N-1}}{1+y}\,dy,\qquad y>0. \tag{3b}

The Euler beta integral gives each factor of (3a), with exactly the moment interval in (3). This agrees with the upstream infinite-product law: for integer h its gamma factors telescope to j/(j+t) and, for each k>=1, (kN+j)(kN-j)/((kN+j+t)(kN-j-t)); Euler’s sine product then gives (3a). Thus no moment-interpolation uniqueness is assumed.

Let P(c,Y)=sum_s p_s(Y)c^s be the actual CROSS spectator product, with its shared boundary variables still identified, and define P_<=M by truncating ONLY its c degree at M. The original nodal pairing is

Qa,N(Y)=Ra(x,b,y;ua)[cM]Ta,M(x,−c,y)P(c,Y). Q_{a,N}(Y)=R_a(x,b,y;u_a) [c^M]T_{a,M}(x,-c,y)P(c,Y). \tag{4}

Thus its untruncated original parent demand remains the one established in the maximal-Green component theorem. No extra sum or independent face radius is introduced.

2. An exact confluent divided difference

For nodes zeta_1,…,zeta_n, define their confluent divided difference of a polynomial F by

[ζ1,…,ζn]F=12πi∮F(t)∏j=1n(t−ζj)dt, [\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_n]F =\frac1{2\pi i}\oint \frac{F(t)}{\prod_{j=1}^n(t-\zeta_j)}\,dt, \tag{5}

with a positive contour enclosing every node. This definition remains valid at collisions, including zero nodes. Expanding at infinity proves

[ζ1,…,ζn]td={0,d<n−1,Hd−n+1(ζ1,…,ζn),d≥n−1. [\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_n]t^d =\begin{cases}0,&d<n-1,\\H_{d-n+1}(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_n),&d\ge n-1. \end{cases} \tag{6}

The signed version of (2), followed by the original coefficient extraction, therefore gives exactly

Qa,N=(−1)mRa(x,b,y;ua)Da,m𝔼[−XW](ta+1P≤M(t,Y)). Q_{a,N} =(-1)^m R_a(x,b,y;u_a)D_{a,m}\, \mathbb E\,[ -XW ]\bigl(t^{a+1}P_{\le M}(t,Y)\bigr). \tag{7}

Indeed n=2a+2, so the monomial p_s t^(a+1+s) gives H_(s-a)(-XW); only a<=s<=M survive. This recovers exactly the signed finite sum from the upstream kernel theorem. Every surviving power of X is between 0 and m.

3. A fixed contour, retaining all scalar factors

There is a useful stronger form of (7). Define the finite diagonal Mellin operator on the truncated polynomial by

(ℳN,aP)(w,Y)=∑s=0MMN,a(s−a)ps(Y)ws. (\mathscr M_{N,a}P)(w,Y) =\sum_{s=0}^M M_{N,a}(s-a)\,p_s(Y)w^s. \tag{8}

Choose a fixed circle |w|=R>max(|x|,|y|). For each X>0 take the contour t=Xw in (5). Homogeneity gives

𝔼[−XW](ta+1P≤M(t,Y))=12πi∮|w|=Rwa+1(ℳN,aP)(w,Y)∏v∈W(w+v)dw. \mathbb E\,[ -XW ]\bigl(t^{a+1}P_{\le M}(t,Y)\bigr) =\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|w|=R} \frac{w^{a+1}(\mathscr M_{N,a}P)(w,Y)} {\prod_{v\in W}(w+v)}\,dw. \tag{9}

The exchange of expectation and this FIXED contour is justified, not formal: before expectation its numerator is X^(-a)P_<=M(Xw,Y). Every absolute random power is X^(s-a), with -a<=s-a<=m strictly inside (3), and the fixed denominator is bounded away from zero on the circle. There are finitely many terms. This also explains why the untruncated spectator polynomial cannot simply be inserted.

The exact original cofactor adapter is R_a=S_(2a)D_a(x,b,y), where D_a is the physical half-triangle determinant. Reflection S_(N-1-r)=N/S_r proves the FULL scalar simplification

RaDa,m=γN,aDa(x,b,y),γN,a={N/s2a+1,N even,N/(s2a+1sm+1),N odd. R_aD_{a,m}=\gamma_{N,a}D_a(x,b,y),\qquad \gamma_{N,a}= \begin{cases} N/s_{2a+1},&N\text{ even},\\ N/(s_{2a+1}s_{m+1}),&N\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \tag{10}

To check it, D_(a,m)=S_m S_(a+M+1)/S_(2a+1). In the even case S_m S_(a+M+1)=N; in the odd case it is N/s_(m+1). These identities include m=0. In particular gamma is nonzero and bounded above and below by powers of N, uniformly in a. It is retained explicitly.

Combining (7), (9), and (10) is the exact original-source period:

Qa,N(Y)=(−1)mγN,aDa(x,b,y)2πi∮|w|=Rwa+1(ℳN,aP)(w,Y)∏r=0a(w+xqa−2r)(w+yqa−2r)dw. Q_{a,N}(Y)= \frac{(-1)^m\gamma_{N,a}D_a(x,b,y)}{2\pi i} \oint_{|w|=R} \frac{w^{a+1}(\mathscr M_{N,a}P)(w,Y)} {\prod_{r=0}^a(w+xq^{a-2r})(w+yq^{a-2r})}\,dw. \tag{11}

All integer Mellin entries in (8) are finite products of sine ratios by (3). Thus (11) is a finite algebraic functional over the original cyclotomic coefficient field. The positive probability law gives one representation and useful estimates for that functional; it does not change its definition. Repeated poles in (11) mean confluent residues, not factors to be divided out or discarded.

The diagonal operator also has an exact finite q-differential description. On its actual coefficient range 0<=s<=M,

MN,a(s−a)=S2a+1SsSaSs+a+1. M_{N,a}(s-a)=\frac{S_{2a+1}S_s}{S_aS_{s+a+1}}. \tag{11a}

Define the symmetric sine derivative on polynomials by

∂qf(w)=f(qw)−f(q−1w)iw,∂qwn=snwn−1. \partial_q f(w)=\frac{f(qw)-f(q^{-1}w)}{iw},\qquad \partial_q w^n=s_n w^{n-1}.

For the actual numerator F(w)=wa+1(ℳN,aP)(w)F(w)=w^{a+1}(\mathscr M_{N,a}P)(w),

∂qa+1F(w)=S2a+1SaP≤M(w). \partial_q^{a+1}F(w)=\frac{S_{2a+1}}{S_a}P_{\le M}(w). \tag{11b}

Every sine division here has index at most M+a+1<=2M+1<N. Thus this is a literal root-safe polynomial identity. Moreover F is the unique solution divisible by w^(a+1) of degree at most M+a+1: the displayed diagonal derivative has no zero coefficient on that space. No root-of-unity resonance is silently inverted. This supplies a finite operator characterization of the same numerator in (11); it asserts no generic-q AJ equation or new topological invariance theorem.

There is a precise affine freedom here. All solutions of (11b) of degree at most M+h form a torsor for the additive space of polynomials of degree below h. The full-node contour in (11) annihilates this space: its denominator has degree 2h, and a numerator of degree below h has no residue at infinity. The original period is therefore independent of those q-integration constants. This statement concerns the stated full-node cycle; it need not hold for a contour enclosing only a selected subset of poles.

This freedom can remove one whole q-string without losing a residue. Put

Px(w)=∏r=0a(w+xqa−2r),F(w)=Px(w)Bx(w)+Ax(w),deg⁡Ax<h. P_x(w)=\prod_{r=0}^a(w+xq^{a-2r}),\qquad F(w)=P_x(w)B_x(w)+A_x(w),\quad \deg A_x<h.

This is ordinary division by a MONIC polynomial; it is valid even at zero or colliding face arguments. The remainder does not contribute to the full period. Also ∂qhAx=0\partial_q^h A_x=0. Thus B_x is exactly the unique polynomial of degree at most M solving

∂qh(Px(w)Bx(w))=S2a+1SaP≤M(w). \partial_q^h\bigl(P_x(w)B_x(w)\bigr) =\frac{S_{2a+1}}{S_a}P_{\le M}(w). \tag{11c}

Uniqueness follows from the same degree bound: a difference would make P_x times a polynomial lie in the degree-below-h kernel, hence vanish. Finally the symmetric q-string divided-difference identity is

[zqa,zqa−2,…,zq−a]B=∂qaB(z)Sa,deg⁡B≤M. [zq^a,zq^{a-2},\ldots,zq^{-a}]B =\frac{\partial_q^aB(z)}{S_a},\qquad \deg B\le M.

Check it on w^s: both sides vanish for s<a, and for s>=a the finite sine-binomial identity gives S_s z^(s-a)/(S_a S_(s-a)). All required indices are below N. The exact same original nodal pairing is therefore

Qa,N=(−1)mγN,aDa(x,b,y)Sa(∂qaBx)(−y). Q_{a,N}= \frac{(-1)^m\gamma_{N,a}D_a(x,b,y)}{S_a} (\partial_q^a B_x)(-y). \tag{11d}

Confluent nodes cause no exception: monic division and the polynomial q-string identity include them. In particular x=y retains the repeated-pole information of the original contour. The additional factor 1/S_a in (11d) is explicit and can be exponential; it has not been declared harmless. The order h grows with the quantum node, so (11c) is not a fixed-order generic-q quantum A-polynomial assertion.

For a genuine cap tree, first replace the spectator coefficients by their proved finite signed path-dilation averages. Its total component degree is truncated at M before that average. The independent Green variable X then gives the same (7)-(11) coefficientwise, retaining the original outside factor U_0^(number of cap edges). All moments used by each independent variable stay in its proved range.

4. The exact interior Mellin growth

Consider ANY sequence of the original indices for which h/N -> alpha with 0<alpha<1/2. Put Y_N=log X_(N,a). For

−α<s<1−2α,κα(s)=∫0slog⁡sin⁡(π(α+t))sin⁡(π(2α+t))dt, -\alpha<s<1-2\alpha, \qquad \kappa_\alpha(s)=\int_0^s \log\frac{\sin(\pi(\alpha+t))}{\sin(\pi(2\alpha+t))}\,dt, \tag{12}

the integral is oriented when s<0. Then

1Nlog⁡𝔼eNsYN→κα(s). \frac1N\log\mathbb E e^{NsY_N}\longrightarrow\kappa_\alpha(s). \tag{13}

Convergence is uniform on compact subsets of the admissible (alpha,s) region. This covers both parities without replacing N.

Proof at integer t=Ns uses (3) and M(0)=1: log M(t) is the sum of the retained log sine ratios, oriented backward for negative t. On a compact subregion the two sine arguments stay uniformly away from their zeros. The Riemann sums therefore give (12) uniformly. For arbitrary real t between adjacent integers, convexity of log M(t) places it below the joining chord and above the extrapolated preceding chord. The successive integer differences are uniformly bounded on these compact sets, again by (3). Interpolating changes the logarithm by O(1), which proves (13). No derivative of an unproved asymptotic is used.

There is also a direct proof from the finite product (3a), valid for real s without interpolation. Its normalized logarithm is the Riemann sum of log(sin(pi t)/sin(pi(t+s))) over h/N<=t<2h/N. The limit is the integral over alpha<=t<=2alpha. It equals (12): both vanish at s=0, and their s derivatives are the first expression in (14). All arguments remain in a fixed compact subset of (0,1) on the stated parameter compacts, so the convergence is uniform.

The limit is strictly convex:

κα′(s)=log⁡sin⁡(π(α+s))sin⁡(π(2α+s)),κα″(s)=πsin⁡(πα)sin⁡(π(α+s))sin⁡(π(2α+s))>0. \begin{aligned} \kappa_\alpha'(s)&= \log\frac{\sin(\pi(\alpha+s))}{\sin(\pi(2\alpha+s))},\\ \kappa_\alpha''(s)&= \frac{\pi\sin(\pi\alpha)} {\sin(\pi(\alpha+s))\sin(\pi(2\alpha+s))}>0. \end{aligned}\tag{14}

Its first derivative ranges from -infinity to +infinity on the open interval in (12). The typical positive value is exp(kappa_alpha’(0))=1/(2cos(pi alpha)).

5. A proved large-deviation cost, not a bounded-moment shortcut

Define for y real

Iα(y)=sup−α<s<1−2α{sy−κα(s)}. I_\alpha(y)=\sup_{-\alpha<s<1-2\alpha} \{sy-\kappa_\alpha(s)\}. \tag{15}

The laws of Y_N satisfy the large-deviation bounds with speed N and this rate: the logarithmic probability of any open set is at least minus the infimum of I there, and that of any closed set is at most minus its infimum. The rate is good, finite and strictly convex, with its unique zero at log(1/(2cos(pi alpha))).

Here is a direct proof sufficient for the stated claim. Fixed positive and negative s in (12), together with (13) and Markov’s inequality, give exponential tightness. For a compact set, apply the same bound with a near-optimal s at each point and take a finite interval cover; letting interval widths decrease proves the closed-set upper bound. For the lower bound at y choose the unique s with kappa_alpha’(s)=y and tilt the probability law by exp(NsY_N)/M(Ns). Under this law, small positive and negative tilts, (13), and strict differentiability give exponentially small probabilities for Y_N outside (y-epsilon, y+epsilon). Undoing the tilt on that interval gives lower rate -I_alpha(y)-|s|epsilon. Let epsilon decrease to zero and then take the best y in the open set. Exponential tightness extends the upper bound from compact to closed sets. Finally I grows at least linearly at both infinities by choosing one fixed tilt of each sign, so its level sets are compact. These arguments establish the asserted bounds directly rather than importing a large-deviation theorem.

The optimizing tilt has an explicit form. For xi=e^y>0 put

ϑ=Arg⁡(1−ξe−πiα)∈(0,π(1−α)),sξ=ϑ/π−α. \vartheta=\operatorname{Arg}(1-\xi e^{-\pi i\alpha}) \in(0,\pi(1-\alpha)),\qquad s_\xi=\vartheta/\pi-\alpha. \tag{16}

Indeed xi=sin(pi(alpha+s))/sin(pi(2alpha+s)) is equivalent to tan vartheta=xi sin(pi alpha)/(1-xi cos(pi alpha)), with precisely the displayed continuous argument branch. Equations (15)-(16) therefore evaluate the cost without a free saddle choice.

At the upper endpoint, the continuous value of (12) is

κα(1−2α)=2∫0αlog⁡(2cos⁡(πt))dt>0. \kappa_\alpha(1-2\alpha) =2\int_0^\alpha\log(2\cos(\pi t))\,dt>0. \tag{17}

This agrees with the exact last-even-moment computation in the upstream theorem. It is the exponential cost missed by copying the bounded-last-moment cap argument. The large-deviation statement does not make that cost disappear.

6. Uniform multiplier rates at every node and the exact Plancherel norm

The interior probability limit has an all-node algebraic counterpart. Define the continuous function

ℓ(t)=∫0tlog⁡(2sin⁡(πu))du,0≤t≤1, \ell(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin(\pi u))\,du,\qquad 0\le t\le1,

with its integrable endpoint values, and put

K(α,β)=ℓ(2α)+ℓ(β)−ℓ(α)−ℓ(α+β),0≤α,β≤12. K(\alpha,\beta)=\ell(2\alpha)+\ell(\beta) -\ell(\alpha)-\ell(\alpha+\beta), \qquad 0\le\alpha,\beta\le\tfrac12. \tag{18}

Uniformly over ALL original integers 0≤a,s≤M0\le a,s\le M and both parities,

1Nlog⁡MN,a(s−a)=K(a/N,s/N)+O(log⁡N/N). \frac1N\log M_{N,a}(s-a) =K(a/N,s/N)+O(\log N/N). \tag{19}

To prove it, split the log-sine sum at its midpoint, where its monotonicity changes. Comparison of each monotone part with its integral gives, uniformly for 0≤r≤N−10\le r\le N-1,

log⁡Sr=Nℓ(r/N)+O(log⁡N). \log S_r=N\ell(r/N)+O(\log N).

The first and last intervals contribute O(log⁡N)O(\log N) by integrating log⁡t\log t and log⁡(1−t)\log(1-t); away from them the monotone-sum error is bounded by the endpoint oscillation, also O(log⁡N)O(\log N). Apply this to the four products in (11a). Replacing (2a+1)/N(2a+1)/N and (s+a+1)/N(s+a+1)/N by 2a/N2a/N and (s+a)/N(s+a)/N changes NℓN\ell by at most O(log⁡N)O(\log N), including at the endpoints. This proves (19). In the interior, K(α,β)=κα(β−α)K(\alpha,\beta)=\kappa_\alpha(\beta-\alpha), either directly by changing the integral limits or by comparing the derivative in β\beta and the value at β=α\beta=\alpha. Equation (19) remains valid when α\alpha approaches zero or 1/21/2; it does not claim exponential tightness of the probability laws at those endpoints.

7. What this resolves and what it does not

The isolated Green/CROSS pairing is now one explicit cyclotomic rational period with a finite diagonal sine-ratio operator, and the positive representation of that operator has an explicit rate function. This retains the actual quantum jets, parent demand, odd scalar, and all confluent poles. Its derivation makes no appeal to equality with a different invariant or a generic A-polynomial.

An absolute estimate on (11) is still required. Its denominator has O(N) factors and its input polynomial has degree O(N); a contour deformation, a residue expansion, or a Cauchy inequality can therefore carry exponential costs. The proved moment rate quantifies one such cost but does not bound the whole signed integrand by Phi_D. Nor does the positive law justify replacing the signed integral by its Laplace maximum. The original peripheral action is retained by the prior whole-source assembly, not newly identified by (11).

The interior probability-rate proof assumes alpha stays away from zero and one half. Section 6 gives uniform algebraic multiplier rates at ALL endpoint indices; it does not assert a good probability large-deviation principle there. No general prescribed-diagram volume theorem is claimed here.

Scope

The q-boundary formula retains its integration constants, monic division, colliding strings and explicit Sa−1S_a^{-1} factor. Section 6 uses the exact surviving-degree normalization and both parity/endpoint limits. No numerical asymptotic scan substitutes for those estimates.

The block-contraction theorem still governs any use of this kernel in the complete source. A mask that cuts an internal signed sum need not preserve the period. The current critical path records the remaining absolute comparison and the distinction between selected diagrams and arbitrary prescribed long-twist diagrams.
