Independent audit of the whole Ward atom and its two-nome completion

27 October 2026. Detailed examination of the cyclotomic-divisor and two-nome construction, especially Sections 2, 3, 5 and 6. The fixed-level sectorial nature of the assertions is necessary and is stated in that note. This audit does not promote its finite root formula to an ordinary meromorphic parameter germ, a whole selected-knot source, or a volume theorem.

1. Fixed-level sectorial expansions and the normal-slope norm

For a fixed primitive n-th root, split the absolutely convergent logarithm sum into m divisible by n and its complement. In a closed negative-real epsilon sector the nonresonant denominators have a uniform positive bound: for m times absolute epsilon small this is the finite root separation, and for it large the radial modulus is bounded below. Their differences from the root value are bounded by a constant times m times absolute epsilon. For the resonant terms the bound

|11−eu+1u−12|≤C|u| \left|\frac1{1-e^u}+\frac1u-\frac12\right|\leq C|u|

holds on that sector, using its Taylor expansion near zero and radial bounds outside a disk. Geometric decay in z then sums the errors. This proves the displayed O(epsilon) estimate uniformly on |z| at most r less than one, with n fixed. Its constant may depend on n; it does not give an estimate for growing n.

Putting U=exp(t n epsilon), each resonant term of the log ratio has limit -t z^(kn)/k. The nonresonant difference is O(epsilon). Uniformity for compact t follows after absorbing exp(C m absolute epsilon) into a slightly larger z disk. Thus the exceptional value is exactly (1-zn)t with its z=0 mark. At t=1 the actual identity F(Q,zQ^n)/F(Q,z)=(z;Q)_n proves its norm interpretation without an interchange of two limits. The rational-slope telescope uses its different shifted arguments; it is not an off-exceptional identity claiming that the first ratio raised to a power is that product.

2. The root completion constants, including the source half-power

Write c=2 pi i, H=c/N+epsilon, X=exp(Nx), V=Log(1-X). Direct inversion, not an asymptotic approximation, gives

H̃=4π2/H+cN,1H̃=1N2ϵ+1cN,w=Xexp⁡(γH̃),γ=1−x/c. \widetilde H=4\pi^2/H+cN,\quad \frac1{\widetilde H}=\frac1{N^2\epsilon}+\frac1{cN},\quad w=X\exp(\gamma\widetilde H),\quad \gamma=1-x/c.

The first-nome constant after its polar term is log D_N minus V/2. The dual n=1 atom has constant -Li_2(X)/(cN)+(gamma-1/2)V. Subtracting these two constants cancels their entire simple polar asymptotic term and gives

ΨN=DNexp⁡(Li⁡2(X)/(cN))(1−X)x/c−1. \Psi_N=D_N\exp(\operatorname{Li}_2(X)/(cN))(1-X)^{x/c-1}.

With nu=(1-X)^(1/N), g_D=D_N/nu^((N-1)/2) and S=Li_2(X)+(Nx)V this is precisely

ΨN=gDexp⁡(S/(cN))ν−(N+1)/2. \Psi_N=g_D\exp(S/(cN))\nu^{-(N+1)/2}.

Both half-power factors are required: their combined exponent is -N on nu, producing the factor (1-X) inverse in the preceding formula. The first shift x to x+c/N changes S by cV and changes g_D by (1-z)/nu, which combine to the original Ward multiplier 1-z. The second shift x to x+c gives the multiplier 1-X. The original central norm is 1-X. The stated conclusion is the radial/sectorial fixed-N value; the calculation alone does not prove holomorphic extension around a whole root neighborhood.

3. Exact accumulating zeros are a decisive parameter obstruction

For fixed real x less than zero, put H_k=(x-c)/k. Its real part is negative, so both infinite products in the actual defining ratio converge. Since 4 pi squared plus 2 pi i x equals c(x-c), one has w(H_k)=exp(ck)=1. Only the numerator factor 1-w vanishes: every remaining numerator factor is 1-Qtilde^j with modulus of Qtilde less than one, and the denominator has modulus of z and Q less than one. The derivative of w at H_k is nonzero. These are therefore exact simple zeros, accumulating at H=0 inside its product domain.

A nonzero horizontal solution of a meromorphic rank-one connection at zero is nonzero at every sufficiently small nonzero ordinary point, by uniqueness of its scalar differential equation. Such a solution cannot have these zeros. A finite algebraic ramification and a fixed nonzero Puiseux meromorphic gauge do not remove them. The uncompleted atom already has accumulating nonzero cyclotomic exponential singularities. These two explicit facts forbid an automatic ordinary meromorphic-line interpretation of either atom. They do not prohibit cancellations in a fully specified word/source.

4. The physical-root leading estimate has the stated sign

For g(t)=t Log(1-z exp(ct)), elementary trapezoid summation gives sum_(j=1)^(N-1) g(j/N)=N integral_0^1 g-(g(0)+g(1))/2+O(1/N). Termwise integration on |z| less than one gives integral g=-Li_2(z)/c. Thus log D_N=-Li_2(z)/H_N-Log(1-z)/2+O(1/N), uniformly on a fixed marked compact z chart. The remaining logarithm in the exact completion formula is exponentially small there because X=z^N. The note’s physical-root estimate follows with its negative one-half logarithmic term. It does not supply a selected geometric knot period or a uniform estimate across the accumulating divisors.
