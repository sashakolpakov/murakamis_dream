An exact odd central Clebsch anchor for the trivalent source

29 October 2026. At the odd-level simple Steinberg channel, the actual trivalent black source has one genuine three-channel invariant tensor and one explicit finite scalar. Its highest and lowest Clebsch columns, middle column, normalization and positive complete-circle factor are computed below. Its coherent scalar is then evaluated by OWN finite binomial identities as a positive fourth-power sine-binomial sum, with a nonzero all-odd asymptotic. This supplies a central anchor for the new ordered branching packet. No even-level confluence estimate, uniform complex packet, global source survival or geometric maximum is claimed.

1. The actual simple channel and its full Clebsch columns

Let N=2m+1>=3, d=2m, q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, x=qd=-q(-1), F_r=(Q;Q)_r. Use the original balanced Ecal,F,K and their ordered coproducts. Define a physical pair vector of degree m by

h=sum_(a=0)^m h_a |a,m-a>,
h_a=q^(a^2) [m choose a]_Q
   =q^(ma) beta(m,a),
beta(m,a)=|[m choose a]_Q|.                          (1)

The last equality uses the principal root: the Gaussian binomial has phase q1, and all its sine factors have positive sign. Its highest-vector equation follows from the literal ratio

h_(a+1)/h_a
  =-x^(-1) Q^a (1-Q^(m-a))/(1-Q^(a+1)).            (2)

Indeed Ecal_pair h=0 and its highest K is x, so its Casimir is -2. Put

W_r=F_pair^r h/F_r, 0<=r<=d,
W|r>=W_r.                                         (3)

The same original commutator used for a physical highest-weight-N module gives

F_pair W_r=(1-Q^(r+1)) W_(r+1),
Ecal_pair W_r=(Q^(-r)-1) W_(r-1),
K_pair W_r=x Q^(-r) W_r.                           (4)

Endpoints are zero. All interior coefficients in (4) are nonzero, so W is an actual injective Hopf intertwiner V_N -> V_N tensor V_N. There is no generic semisimple replacement of the root module here.

Every entry has the explicit finite Clebsch form. If b=m+r-a and 0<=a,b<=d, then

W_r(a,b)=F_a F_b F_m
   *sum_s q^(s^2) x^[-r+a-s] Q^[s(r-a+s)]
    /[F_s^2 F_(m-s)^2 F_(a-s) F_(r-a+s)],          (5)

where max(0,a-r)<=s<=min(m,a). If a or b is outside the physical basis, the entry is zero. All denominator indices in this finite range are less than N. To prove (5), write F_pair=A+B with A=F_1, B=K_1^(-1)F_2, so AB=Q^(-1)BA. Expand

(A+B)^r=sum_k [r choose k]_Q A^k B^(r-k),

and apply its k=a-s term to the s-th summand of (1). Dividing by F_r leaves precisely the six displayed factorials. Thus (5) is an own finite binomial calculation, with its full root phases.

2. Exact source normalizations, including both ends

Two useful cancellations are

W_d(m+t,d-t)=h_t, 0<=t<=m,
W_m(0,d)=q^(-m^2).                                 (6)

For the first, the lowest-vector equation in (4) shows that its successive ratio is the ratio in (1); only its first scalar needs checking. Substitute r=d,a=m in (5), then use

F_t F_(d-t)=N(-1)^t Q^[t(t+1)/2].                  (7)

Its first scalar becomes

(-1)^m q^[-m(3m+1)]
              sum_(s=0)^m Q^(s^2) [m choose s]_Q^2.

Finite q-Vandermonde makes the sum [2m choose m]_Q=N/F_m^2. For completeness, extract the coefficient of z^m in (z;Q)_m(zQ^m;Q)m=(z;Q)(2m) using the finite binomial expansion, then set a=m-k in its convolution. Its residual exponent is a^2, which proves exactly this finite Vandermonde identity. Since F_m2=N(-1)m q2, the total scalar is q^(-2mN)=1. This proves the first equation(6). In the second equation, only s=0 survives in (5); the value is x(-m)N/F_m2=q(-m2).

Let f_a(z) be the original middle-degree Casimir continuants normalized by f_0=1. The node -2 is SIMPLE at odd N. Consequently

W_m(a,d-a)=q^(-m^2) f_a(-2).                       (8)

No derivative node is treated as a second simple value. The simple node is being used only at the odd central anchor.

The exact positive/reflection normalization is

W^dagger P W=I_N,
(L_N P)_Steinberg=N W W^dagger.                    (9)

Here the subscript selects the actual -2 simple block on the full physical pair, in every degree m,…,3m. To check the first column of (9), use (1), conjugate the Gaussian phase, and apply terminating q-Chu with parameters(Q(-m),Q(-m);Q), argument Q. It gives

h^dagger P h
  =q^(m^2) sum_a Q^[a(a+1)] [m choose a]_Q^2
  =q^(m^2)(-1)^m Q^[-m^2-m(m+1)/2]=1.             (10)

The last exponent uses q^N=-1. The elementary finite q-Chu proof is given in the source quadratic transform; it is a polynomial binomial expansion, not an imported summation theorem. Adjoint covariance Ecal_pair^dagger P=-P F_pair now gives the norm of each descendant: its ratio is |1-Q(r+1)|2 before division by F_(r+1)/F_r. Thus every W_r has P-norm one, and different r have disjoint physical degrees. The actual simple spectral projector is W W^dagger P, proving the second part of (9). Equivalently sqrt(N) W is a valid full-degree CD factor for this channel; replacing first-entry normalization by it is a KNOWN unit phase change within each magnetic row, not an arbitrary frame removal.

3. The entire central black vertex is one genuine 3j tensor

Use the actual nested six-head J=p_16 p_23 p_45 and define the unscaled Hopf row pi=W^dagger P. Its central black packet is

U^0=(pi tensor pi tensor pi) J.                    (11)

The row pi is a Hopf map by (4),(9) and the original adjoint covariance. Hence (11) is a genuine invariant of V_N tensor V_N tensor V_N. This invariant space is ONE dimensional. Bending its third leg by the original duality identifies it with Hom(V_N,V_N tensor V_N). Every such map has image in the -2 Casimir kernel. That actual simple kernel is exactly the single W(V_N) above, with dimension N; Schur’s elementary E/F/K argument therefore gives dimension one.

Define its canonical nonzero tensor

Theta=(W tensor I) p,
Theta_(a,b,c)=N x^(-c) W_(d-c)(a,b).               (12)

In particular Theta_(d,m,0)=N q(m2), by (6). Therefore the WHOLE central black vertex is

U^0=N^2 q^(-m^2) A_N Theta,
A_N=sum_(k=0)^m x^(k-d)
                     conjugate(h_k^2 W_m(m+k,m-k)). (13)

To obtain its scalar, evaluate (11) at the magnetic indices(d,m,0). The only remaining physical colors are the N-term source contraction of the branching kernel, with h_(m-t) from the lowest and highest columns. Reindex k=m-t and use (6). It gives U0_(d,m,0)=N3 A_N, which with (12) proves (13). In the positive CD factor sqrt(N) W, the vertex is N^(3/2) times U^0, including the original N^3 of J.

Formula(5) makes A_N a fully explicit terminating factorial sum. The next section proves its positive collapse, retaining all phases; that positivity is not a premise of the source reduction above.

The ordinary and marked white packets remain the distinct actual kernels of the branching theorem, with each f_alpha replaced by the specified sqrt(N) W_r. In particular the marked vertex is NOT declared to be a zero-current 3j covector: its last arc has the exact P-removal and whole-source degree qualification already proved there. Equation(13) concerns the invariant BLACK vertex only. Thus this central reduction preserves the source distinction that prevented the false trivalent norm-square replacement.

4. A finite coherent quartic transform

The scalar of the ACTUAL vertex has the exact evaluation

A_N=q^[m(2-m)] sum_(k=0)^m beta(m,k)^4.             (14)

In particular it is nonzero at EVERY odd N, including composite levels. Here is a finite proof, rather than an absolute-value bound.

Let f=(f_a(-2))_(a=0)^d and use the actual symmetric anti-Pascal recoupling J on the complete N-channel source module. Root factorial reflection(7) rewrites its entries as

J_(i,j)=x D_i D_j [i+j choose i]_(Q^(-1)),
D_i=(-q^(-1))^i, i+j<=d; zero otherwise.            (15)

It is important that this is the full invertible recoupling, not just the simple node. It obeys

conjugate(J) P J=P.                               (16)

This follows by finite q-binomial inversion. More explicitly, the (i,j) entry of the left side has a=j,…,d-i. If i+j>d it is zero. If n=d-i-j=0 its sole term is the squared modulus of an antidiagonal entry, hence one. If n>0, put a=j+t and use(7) on its factorials. After factoring the nonzero t-independent terms, the sum is

sum_(t=0)^n (-1)^t Q^[t(t-1)/2+(1-n)t]
                    [n choose t]_Q
   =(Q^(1-n);Q)_n=0.

This proves(16) with the actual phase, without assuming a positive or unitary ordinary pivotal trace.

The other actual pair Casimir is the real upper bidiagonal matrix B with diagonal c_i=q(2i+1)+q(-2i-1) and upper entry b_i=Qi+Q(-i)-2. The established source relation J A J(-1)=BT gives y=J f explicitly:

y_i=0, i<m;
y_(m+k)=beta(m,k)^2, 0<=k<=m.                     (17)

For clarity, this needs only its first-order eigenvector recurrence. Before i=m the diagonal c_i+2 is nonzero. After i=m, -b_i/(c_i+2)=sin2(pi(m+1-k)/N)/sin2(pi k/N), i=m+k. The final entry is y_d=f_0=1. This proves(17), including its absolute normalization, directly from the two actual Casimir matrices.

By(1),(8),(13), A_N=q(-3m2) f^dagger J f. Since y is REAL, (16) gives conjugate(f)=P J P y. Put z=P y. It has support0,…,m and z_i=beta(m,i)^2. Therefore

f^dagger J f=z^T J z.                             (18)

All entries i,j<=m in this quadratic form have i+j<=d, so the zero-truncation in(15) no longer intervenes. Finite Vandermonde, by the same product-coefficient proof as in Section2, gives

[i+j choose i]_t
   =sum_(k=0)^min(i,j) t^(k^2)[i choose k]_t[j choose k]_t.

Consequently (15),(18) become

z^T J z=x sum_(k=0)^m Q^(-k^2) C_k^2,
C_k=sum_(i=k)^m beta(m,i)^2 D_i [i choose k]_(Q^(-1)). (19)

The SINGLE remaining transform is exactly evaluable:

C_k=(-1)^m q^[k^2-m(m+1)] beta(m,k)^2.             (20)

To prove it, put n=m-k and convert every Gaussian factor in(19) to base Q. Factoring its k-th term gives

C_k=(-1)^k q^(-k) Q^[k^2-mk] [m choose k]_Q^2
  *sum_(r=0)^n (Q^(-n);Q)_r^2 Q^(-kr)
                          /[F_r(Q^(k+1);Q)_r].    (21)

Here Q^(m+1)=-q is precisely the half-root relation that cancels the otherwise alternating summation argument. Apply the SECOND terminating q-Chu identity with b=Q^(-n), c=Q^(k+1):

sum_(r=0)^n (Q^(-n);Q)_r(b;Q)_r
                   (c Q^n/b)^r/[F_r(c;Q)_r]
   =(c/b;Q)_n/(c;Q)_n.                            (22)

Its argument is cQ(2n)=Q(-k), exactly as in(21). This is another own finite identity: take the already proved q-Chu of Section2 at base Q^(-1), parameters b(-1),c(-1), and use (a;Q(-1))_r=(-a)r Q[-r(r-1)/2](a(-1);Q)_r. That substitution gives(22) term by term and on its right side. All its denominators here have indices<=m<N. At the root its right side is

(Q^(m+1);Q)_n/(Q^(k+1);Q)_n
    =(-1)^n Q^[-mn+n(n-1)/2].

Substituting this into(21) and using [m choose k]_Q2=Q[k(m-k)] beta(m,k)^2 gives(20).

Now EVERY k-dependent phase cancels in(19):

f^dagger J f=x q^[-2m(m+1)] sum_k beta(m,k)^4
            =q^(-2m^2) sum_k beta(m,k)^4.

Multiplying by q(-3m2) gives q(-5m2) times that positive sum. Finally q(-5m2)=q3, since their exponent difference is -2mN. This proves(14) with the actual root lift and full source.

5. Nonzero local scalar asymptotic, with its exact normalization

Let G(t)=log(2sin(pi t)), J(t)=integral_0^t G(u)du and j0=-8J(1/4)=v8/pi. Here v8=4 times Catalan’s integral is only a named numerical constant; no link-volume identification is assumed. Then the finite positive scalar in(14) obeys

sum_k beta(m,k)^4
   =(1/4) N^(-3/2) exp(N j0)(1+O(N^(-1))),
A_N=(q^[m(2-m)]/4) N^(-3/2) exp(N j0)(1+O(N^(-1))). (23)

An elementary sine-factorial Laplace proof keeps the affine shift m=(N-1)/2. On compact0<t<1, Euler summation after separating the logarithmic endpoint gives

s_a=sqrt(2N sin(pi a/N)) exp[N J(a/N)](1+O(N^(-1))).

The same endpoint separation gives the uniform logarithmic error O(log N) for all0<=a<N, sufficient for a global large-deviation gap. Since s_m^2=N EXACTLY, beta(m,k)4=N2/(s_k^4 s_(m-k)^4). Its limiting exponent -4J(t)-4J(1/2-t) has a UNIQUE maximum at1/4: its derivative is4log cot(pi t), positive before1/4 and negative afterward, and its second derivative there is -8pi.

Write k=m/2+sqrt(N)u, retaining its actual integer or half-integer coset. The compact factorial formula gives the symmetric local density

beta(m,k)^4
   =(1/2)N^(-2) exp[N j0-4pi u^2]
      *[1+O((1+u^4)/N)]                          (24)

on a growing Gaussian window; outside a smaller fixed macroscopic window there is the strict exponent gap just proved. The odd terms cancel because k and m-k occur symmetrically. The shifted Gaussian Riemann sum has mesh N^(-1/2), integral1/2 and relative errorO(1/N), by the integrable second-derivative trapezoidal estimate. Multiplying the factor1/2 in(24) by this integral gives exactly1/4 in(23). This proves both the all-odd nonvanishing and its scalar rate.

The corresponding highest black-source coefficient in the CD frame sqrt(N)W is N^(9/2)A_N, hence has leading magnitude (N^3/4) exp(Nj0). The ENTIRE black tensor still includes Theta in(13). No claim about its full positive norm, the marked white tensor, even confluent channels or a sewn graph’s maximum follows by maximizing this one coefficient.
