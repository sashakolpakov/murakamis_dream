The weighted centralizer current is not a matrix gauge derivation

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The actual weighted Ward current has the required classical centralizer field. It is nevertheless not an inner derivation of the finite tensor matrix algebra. A local matrix-algebra splitting therefore does not turn this existing current into a change of basis. This statement concerns the specified lift; it does not rule out a different lift with additional terms.

The sole complete proof source is W, the actual weighted Ward lift, pinned below. All finite calculations needed for this conclusion are given here.

1. The current and its part vanishing on the identity

Use N≥2N\ge2, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, Q=q2Q=q^2, ρ=q−q−1\rho=q-q^{-1}, and h=2πi/Nh=2\pi i/N. On b≥1b\ge1 actual heads retain W’s ordered coproducts 𝖪=K1Kt,𝖤=ℰ1Kt+ℰt,𝖥=F1+K1−1Ft.(1) \mathsf K=K_1K_{\mathrm{ t}},\qquad \mathsf E=\mathcal E_1K_{\mathrm{ t}}+\mathcal E_{\mathrm{ t}}, \qquad \mathsf F=F_1+K_1^{-1}F_{\mathrm{ t}}. \qquad\text{(1)} The subscript t{\mathrm{ t}} denotes the remaining tensor heads. For b=1b=1, set Kt=1K_{\mathrm{ t}}=1 and ℰt=Ft=0\mathcal E_{\mathrm{ t}}=F_{\mathrm{ t}}=0.

In W’s exact order the coefficients needed here are AE=K1F1𝖪−1,AF=ℰ1.(2) A_E=K_1F_1\mathsf K^{-1},\qquad A_F=\mathcal E_1 . \qquad\text{(2)} Keep its entire third coefficient AKA_K; its explicit value will not enter the test. Write 𝒟(B)=h−1∑ι=E,F,K[Xι,AιB],(XE,XF,XK)=(𝖤,𝖥,𝖪).(3) \mathcal D(B)=h^{-1}\sum_{\iota=E,F,K}[X_\iota,A_\iota B], \qquad (X_E,X_F,X_K)=(\mathsf E,\mathsf F,\mathsf K). \qquad\text{(3)} Removing its multiplication term gives δ(B):=𝒟(B)−𝒟(1)B=h−1∑ιAι[Xι,B].(4) \delta(B):=\mathcal D(B)-\mathcal D(1)B =h^{-1}\sum_\iota A_\iota[X_\iota,B]. \qquad\text{(4)} Thus δ(1)=0\delta(1)=0, as any derivation would require. Its exact product defect, however, is δ(BC)−δ(B)C−Bδ(C)=h−1∑ι[Aι,B][Xι,C].(5) \delta(BC)-\delta(B)C-B\delta(C) =h^{-1}\sum_\iota[A_\iota,B][X_\iota,C]. \qquad\text{(5)} This follows by expanding the two commutators in (4). The defect may not be dropped merely because the principal commuting symbols have a vector-field interpretation.

2. A diagonal test at the original root head

Evaluate (4) on 𝖪\mathsf K. The weight relations give [𝖤,𝖪]=(1−Q)𝖤𝖪,[𝖥,𝖪]=(1−Q−1)𝖥𝖪. [\mathsf E,\mathsf K]=(1-Q)\mathsf E\mathsf K,\qquad [\mathsf F,\mathsf K]=(1-Q^{-1})\mathsf F\mathsf K. Since 𝖪−1𝖤𝖪=Q−1𝖤\mathsf K^{-1}\mathsf E\mathsf K=Q^{-1}\mathsf E, substitution in (2)–(4) yields δ(𝖪)=1−Q−1h(ℰ1𝖥𝖪−K1F1𝖤).(6) \delta(\mathsf K)=\frac{1-Q^{-1}}h \bigl(\mathcal E_1\mathsf F\mathsf K -K_1F_1\mathsf E\bigr). \qquad\text{(6)} Expanding (1), commuting only different tensor heads, and using [ℰ1,F1]=ρ(K1−K1−1)[\mathcal E_1,F_1]=\rho(K_1-K_1^{-1}), gives δ(𝖪)=1−Q−1h[ρ(K12−1)Kt+ℰ1FtKt−K1F1ℰt].(7) \delta(\mathsf K)=\frac{1-Q^{-1}}h \left[ \rho(K_1^2-1)K_{\mathrm{ t}} +\mathcal E_1F_{\mathrm{ t}}K_{\mathrm{ t}} -K_1F_1\mathcal E_{\mathrm{ t}} \right]. \qquad\text{(7)} All these are identities of the actual finite matrices. In particular their zero endpoint arrows remain unchanged.

In the original tensor weight basis the last two terms in (7) have zero diagonal: they change the first-head index. At complete color M=1M=1, K1wa=−q−1Q−awa, K_1w_a=-q^{-1}Q^{-a}w_a, so diag⁡δ(𝖪)=(1−Q−1)ρh(K12−1)Kt≠0.(8) \operatorname{diag}\delta(\mathsf K) =\frac{(1-Q^{-1})\rho}h (K_1^2-1)K_{\mathrm{ t}} \ne0 . \qquad\text{(8)} Indeed on the first-head index a=0a=0, K12=Q−1≠1K_1^2=Q^{-1}\ne1, while KtK_{\mathrm{ t}} is invertible. This proves (8) for every N≥2N\ge2 and every width b≥1b\ge1. Here diag\operatorname{diag} means taking matrix diagonal entries in this specified basis, not an invariant trace.

For any finite matrix HH, the diagonal of [H,𝖪][H,\mathsf K] is zero in the same basis. Consequently δ\delta cannot have the form δ(B)=[H,B].(9) \delta(B)=[H,B]. \qquad\text{(9)} It is not any derivation of the full matrix algebra either. For completeness, every complex-linear derivation DD of Mat⁡n(ℂ)\operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb C) is inner: in matrix units set H=∑iD(Ei1)E1iH=\sum_i D(E_{i1})E_{1i}. Applying the product rule to EklEi1E_{kl}E_{i1}, then multiplying by E1iE_{1i} and summing, gives [H,Ekl]=D(Ekl)[H,E_{kl}]=D(E_{kl}). The matrix units span the algebra, proving the assertion.

3. Consequence for a framing descent

W proves that (3), with its full multiplication term and marked cut/pivot boundary, quantizes the principal field s∂rs\partial_r on the stated geometric chart. That sourced identity remains valid. Equations (5)–(8) show precisely why one cannot reinterpret it as an inner splitting change of the finite matrix algebra.

A proposed replacement by such a change must supply a different finite derivation and compare its entire current, including the difference from (3), with the original source. Subtracting 𝒟(1)B\mathcal D(1)B alone does not suffice. No relative framing period, contour coefficient or volume-conjecture lower bound follows from this calculation.
