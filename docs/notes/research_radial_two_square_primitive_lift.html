Two-square radial limits and a regular lifted primitive system

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

For a proper selected subset of an original circle tree, finite two-square degenerations along a positive radial approach have a restricted form. At a charge strictly between zero and one they are internal matching edges, their endpoint real angles are zero, and both endpoints have another strictly positive original edge. The original period equations are nonsingular at every such limit after retaining a vanishing edge square root as an independent variable. This includes the square-root fold, not only the regular imaginary square-root values.

The classification uses integer original powers, a common radial charge, complete outside angles, and a supplied positive unit-charge solution. The resulting local implicit-function theorem permits independent nearby complex charges, but it does not assert this classification for limits of arbitrary charge paths. Nor does it complete a cone metric at a two-square corner or select a quantum cycle.

Call an edge two-square if both of its square factors are corners at the admitted limit. Denote the set of these edges by TT.

The fold is regular in this lifted system even though qq cannot be a holomorphic function of two unconstrained endpoint angles there. The projection of the charge-parametrized solution to the angle coordinates need not be locally invertible. 6. Consequences and limits of the statement

For the supplied positive radial approach, every finite corner alternative now has a regular local system of original primitive equations: ordinary angle coordinates suffice when no qeq_e vanishes, and the finitely many matching square roots give the lifted coordinates in Theorem 5 otherwise. Close terms of the approaching sequence lie on that unique local branch by the implicit-function theorem.

This is local analytic control of the specified limit. It does not show that the positive radial set is connected, that a continued branch still consists of positive original cells, or that a completed cone metric exists at a two-square limit. In particular the previously proved uniform-end and marked-metric continuation theorems have regular single-square hypotheses; those theorems are not invoked at the two-square edges here. A metric attachment, volume calibration there, and transport of the original relative quantum cycle require additional arguments.

The classification is not asserted for arbitrary independent charge approaches or arbitrary noninteger powers. Independent charge variation occurs only after the common-charge integral limit has been classified and its lifted Jacobian proved invertible. No new class of short fillings satisfying the original volume identity is claimed.
