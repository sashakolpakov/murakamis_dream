A single-leaf alias gap that composes with an arbitrary completed child

31 October 2026. source-envelope theorem. The proof retains the actual seven-factorial multiplication gate and the completed child’s output tangent. It uses no child coefficient lower asymptotic or replacement coupon. The finite source and lattice adapter and geometric proper-port theorem are separate inputs. The applicable closed class has two completed root children and proper completed ports 0<X,Y<10<X,Y<1. Root-leaf closures that reach the quarter-index equal-modulus dominance boundary remain separate.

1. Exact parameter domain at a single-leaf attachment

Attach the original leaf hh to a completed child EE by the original product. The selected common XX is the cotangent of the leaf coordinate aa. Write the child port as (B,X,Y)(B,X,Y), its output as (Bp,X,Yp)(B_p,X,Y_p), and the corresponding output angles as b,cb,c. Original product stationarity gives

a=arccot⁡Xπ,b=arccot⁡Bπ,c=arccot⁡Bpπ,Y2=1+B2−X2,S=X+B>0,Bp=X−1S,Yp=YS,μ=PE′(b)=2log⁡(X/Y),λ=Pparent′(c)=2log⁡(X/Yp). \begin{aligned} a&=\frac{\operatorname{arccot}X}{\pi},& b&=\frac{\operatorname{arccot}B}{\pi},& c&=\frac{\operatorname{arccot}B_p}{\pi},\\ Y^2&=1+B^2-X^2,&S&=X+B>0,\\ B_p&=X-\frac1S,&Y_p&=\frac YS,\\ \mu&=P_E'(b)=2\log(X/Y),& \lambda&=P_{\mathrm{parent}}'(c)=2\log(X/Y_p). \end{aligned}\tag{1}

Here arccot\operatorname{arccot} takes values in (0,π)(0,\pi). Assume both child and parent are proper: 0<X<10<X<1 and 0<Y,Yp<10<Y,Y_p<1. Then Y<1Y<1 gives |B|<X|B|<X, while Yp<1Y_p<1 is equivalent to

B>L(X):=12X−X. B>L(X):=\frac1{2X}-X.

Since B<XB<X, this forces X>1/2X>1/2. Every newly attached leaf in this class therefore remains in the strict source interval

14<a<arctan⁡(2)π. \frac14<a<\frac{\arctan(2)}\pi.\tag{2}

The only region not covered by the earlier individual-leaf certificate is 1/2<X<2/31/2<X<2/3. On its closed enlargement 1/2≤X≤2/31/2\le X\le2/3,

0<L(X)≤B≤X,12X≤Y≤1. 0<L(X)\le B\le X,\qquad \frac1{2X}\le Y\le1.

The exact reflection B′=1/(X+B)−XB'=1/(X+B)-X maps [L(X),X][L(X),X] onto itself, interchanges its endpoints, and satisfies Yp2=1+(B′)2−X2Y_p^2=1+(B')^2-X^2. Consequently, setting p=eμp=e^\mu and q=eλq=e^\lambda,

p,q∈[X2,4X4]⊂[1/4,1],μ,λ∈[−2log⁡2,0]. p,q\in[X^2,4X^4]\subset[1/4,1],\qquad \mu,\lambda\in[-2\log2,0].\tag{3}

This compact slope domain follows from actual proper ports; it is not an extra assumption on the source.

2. A universal dual inequality with a linear child

Retain the original complete, core and alias actions:

Define

P(μ,λ)=maxD,x≤1/2[g(x)+m(x,y,z)+μy−λz],Q(μ,λ)=maxD[k(x)+m(x,y,z)+μy−λz]. \begin{aligned} P(\mu,\lambda) &=\max_{D,\,x\le1/2} [g(x)+m(x,y,z)+\mu y-\lambda z],\\ Q(\mu,\lambda) &=\max_D[k(x)+m(x,y,z)+\mu y-\lambda z]. \end{aligned}\tag{4}

Only the new leaf has the core restriction x≤1/2x\le1/2 in PP. The completed child coordinate yy retains its full interval [0,1][0,1]: it is bounded by its own tangent, not identified with another bare leaf.

There is an exact calibration on the whole square (3). Set

Here 1/2≤X<11/2\le X<1. Indeed X≥1/2X\ge1/2 is equivalent to p+q−pq≥1/4p+q-\sqrt{pq}\ge1/4, which follows from (p−q)2+pq≥1/4(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2+\sqrt{pq}\ge1/4. Since g′(a)=2log⁡Xg'(a)=2\log X, global concavity proves

Square certificate. On the full square in (3),

Q(μ,λ)≤P(μ,λ)−11000. Q(\mu,\lambda)\le P(\mu,\lambda)-\frac1{1000}. \tag{8}

The exact verifier and rational data partition the entire square into 55 dyadic rectangles. On each rectangle, a fixed feasible rational pure point gives a lower bound for PP. 3. The actual new-leaf inequality and inherited gap

Let C=PE(b)C=P_E(b) be the child’s selected complete value. Suppose its core and aggregate alias arrays satisfy, uniformly in all physical colors and all output indices rr,

|CE,r|≤exp⁡(N[C+μ(r/N−b)+eN]),|AE,r|≤exp⁡(N[C+μ(r/N−b)−γE+eN]),eN→0,γE>0. \begin{aligned} |C_{E,r}|&\le\exp\!\left(N[C+\mu(r/N-b)+e_N]\right),\\ |A_{E,r}|&\le\exp\!\left(N[C+\mu(r/N-b)-\gamma_E+e_N]\right),\\ e_N&\longrightarrow0,\qquad\gamma_E>0. \end{aligned}\tag{9}

This is an output-tangent upper bound. It does not say that the same weights minimize a separate constrained problem at every rr.

For an alias at the new leaf, adding the child tangent gives its constant C−μbC-\mu b plus k(x)+m+μyk(x)+m+\mu y. Equations (7)–(8) prove

k(x)+m(x,y,z)+C+μ(y−b)≤Pparent(c)+λ(z−c)−11000,Pparent(c)=g(a)+C+m(a,b,c),1/2<X≤2/3. \begin{aligned} k(x)+m(x,y,z)+C+\mu(y-b) &\le P_{\mathrm{parent}}(c)+\lambda(z-c)-\frac1{1000},\\ P_{\mathrm{parent}}(c)&=g(a)+C+m(a,b,c), \qquad 1/2<X\le2/3. \end{aligned}\tag{10}

This is the original parent action and slope, with its actual boundary coordinate. For X≥2/3X\ge2/3, the earlier exact algebraic leaf certificate gives

k(x)≤g(a)+g′(a)(x−a)−12500. k(x)\le g(a)+g'(a)(x-a)-\frac1{2500}.

The original gate and child tangents cancel the x,yx,y terms by (6). Thus the same block inequality holds for every proper attachment with uniform margin

δleaf=12500. \delta_{\mathrm{leaf}}=\frac1{2500}.\tag{11}

For a new core leaf, g̃\widetilde g is bounded by its complete tangent because a>1/4a>1/4. The same calculation gives the parent tangent with zero loss.

The exact finite parent split is

Cparent=Ch⋆CE,Aparent=Ah⋆CE+Ch⋆AE+Ah⋆AE. \begin{aligned} C_{\mathrm{parent}}&=C_h\star C_E,\\ A_{\mathrm{parent}}&=A_h\star C_E+C_h\star A_E+A_h\star A_E. \end{aligned}\tag{12}

Here ⋆\star is the original Newton kernel, including i+j≤d=N−1i+j\le d=N-1 and all normal directions. The three terms lose, respectively, δleaf\delta_{\mathrm{leaf}}, γE\gamma_E, and their sum. Polynomially many kernel indices and the uniform O(log⁡N)O(\log N) factorial errors can be absorbed into a new error tending to zero. Hence (9) propagates with

γparent=min⁡(γE,δleaf)>0. \gamma_{\mathrm{parent}} =\min(\gamma_E,\delta_{\mathrm{leaf}})>0.\tag{13}

The order EhEh gives the same statement by the exact commutative product; no coupon is moved through a turn.

Turns and completed/completed products preserve the tangent hypotheses. The actual turn reverses rr to d−rd-r, changes the slope from μ\mu to −μ-\mu, and has finite affine error −μ/N-\mu/N. A product of two completed children uses their two tangent bounds and the actual gate tangent; internal derivatives cancel, and a contaminated sector retains at least its smaller child margin. This proves a finite-tree induction, including hT(E)hT(E) when T(E)T(E) is a proper completed child. The closing demand and all turns still use d/N=1−1/Nd/N=1-1/N, so their total finite error is O(1)O(1) in the exponent for a fixed tree.

The two-leaf block lemma supplies the A2A_2 base. Larger original seeds may use their earlier leaf proof or the proved composition where proper. Every nonempty alias sector in a proper completed/completed closure therefore has a positive global gap. The all-core concave majorant and actual far-color recurrence give the physical-color complement. 4. A stronger response bound on actual proper ports

The square certificate does not depend on this section. The following separate argument shows that, for 1/2≤X≤2/31/2\le X\le2/3, the dual gap is at least its value at μ=λ=−log⁡4\mu=\lambda=-\log4. A four-vertex certificate then gives the stronger margin 3/503/50 on this smaller parameter set.

Fix p,q>0p,q>0 and optimize m+μy−λzm+\mu y-\lambda z over y,zy,z at a fixed 0<x<10<x<1. Each fiber has strict points. Every saturated gate constraint has a nonvertex-flat original tetrahedron, so motion toward a strict point has positive infinite inward derivative; the linear terms remain finite. The gate maximum is strict. Its Hessian in y,zy,z is negative definite: the first angle triple controls yy, and with yy fixed the second controls zz. The fiber maximum is unique and analytic in xx, and its partial Legendre profile is concave.

Write

U=cot⁡(πx),V=cot⁡(πy),W=cot⁡(πz),A=U−W>0,r=q/p,c0=q(p+q+1)p. U=\cot(\pi x),\quad V=\cot(\pi y),\quad W=\cot(\pi z),\qquad A=U-W>0,\quad r=q/p,\quad c_0=\frac{q(p+q+1)}p.

5. Scope and the root-leaf frontier

This is an upper-envelope induction at a selected proper completed-port configuration of the original source. The source and geometry companions supply such configurations for their stated grammar. No claim is made that every algebraic tangle has the required strict states or local packet.

In particular C(hT(As))C(hT(A_s)) is a different boundary regime. Its seed has X=1X=1, B=Y=1/sB=Y=1/s, so every inner leaf label equals 1/41/4, whereas the outer leaf has cotangent 1/s1/s. Its pure retained cells can remain strict. The inherited signed source packet meets an equal-modulus dominance boundary there, not an eigenvalue collision or a forced-flat geometric cell. Equations (11)–(13) do not remove that separate packet obligation.
