Independent audit of the ordinary-color identification

28 October 2026. This checks the two representation comparisons needed before applying the positive path trace in the regular-period note. Neither comparison assumes a published colored-Jones or ADO comparison proof. They are exact finite-matrix statements, including the Cartan scalar and zero framing. The positive path trace itself is a separate algebraic step.

The conclusion is that, for a one-component zero-framed closure of a fixed b-strand braid and every 1 <= j < N,

F_N^M(-q^j) = F_N^Y(Q^j) = C_j(q),
C_j(q) = theta_j^(-w) tau(P rho(cable(word)) P)/[j],
q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2, theta_j=q^((j^2-1)/2).         (1)

The coordinate superscripts in (1) are essential. In root_orbit_pointing.md, the reduced family is a function of M=-q x=-q^(alpha+1); here it is denoted F_N^M(M). In regularized_kashaev_period.md, the symbol F_N denotes its even descent to Y=M^2; here that function is denoted F_N^Y(Y). Thus

F_N^M(M)=F_N^Y(M^2),
alpha=j-1 <=> x=q^(j-1), M=-q^j, Y=Q^j.

In particular one must NOT insert -q^j into the Y-coordinate function. The complete point is M=1, Y=1. All these are reduced periods; the full modified-dimension factor can have poles at the ordinary orbit points and is not asserted polynomially bounded there.

For an independent check that this descent is legitimate, use the actual stripped Laurent crossing formulas. Under x -> -x, a transfer of order h contributes (-1)^(u+v+h) for either crossing sign, where u,v are its inputs. Modulo two this is (-1)^(c-u), with c its first checked output: c=v+h for a positive crossing and c=v-h for a negative one. Conservation of the integer pair sum makes c-u the negative change of the sum of index times strand position. Its total is zero on every diagonal cut state. The remaining pivot/framing sign is (-1)^((N-1)(b-1-w))=1 for a knot. Hence the reduced Laurent family is even in x, equivalently in M. Since it is Laurent polynomial, it descends uniquely to a Laurent polynomial in Y=M^2. This proof uses the original reduced framing; the unreduced/full state has the separate sign line and pole factors described in the pointing notes.

Here tau is the algebraic fundamental pivotal closure trace, P is the product of the actual Jones–Wenzl idempotents p_(j-1), and every crossing in rho is the full fundamental matrix, with its common Cartan scalar. The usual value of q^(1/2) is exp(pi*i/(2N)). The source is the original reduced cut; the modified-dimension line is not divided out in a new way.

1. The invariant quotient is genuinely the ordinary color

At alpha=j-1 the original N-dimensional unrolled color V has

H v_a=(j-1-2a)v_a,
K v_a=q^(j-1-2a)v_a,
E v_a=[a][j-a]v_(a-1), F v_a=v_(a+1).

All missing vectors are zero. The subspace

U=span(v_j,...,v_(N-1))

is invariant: its only possible downward escape is E v_j, whose coefficient is zero. H,K,F preserve it as well. The quotient V/U has basis v_0,…,v_(j-1), with exactly the ordinary j-dimensional highest-weight matrices. At j=1 this quotient is the trivial color.

The actual uncheck R is

R=q^(H tensor H/2) sum_(h=0)^(N-1) c_h E^h tensor F^h,
c_h=q^(h(h-1)/2)(q-q^(-1))^h/[h]!.                  (2)

Every summand preserves U on each input strand separately, as does the Cartan factor. The inverse has the same preservation property, either from its finite inverse formula or from invertibility of its induced diagonal blocks. Its induced quotient is the defining ordinary R: all terms h >= j vanish there. The checked R exchanges the two typed strands; it does not exchange the status of a strand with an unrelated one. The quotient maps consequently satisfy, for both crossing signs,

(pi tensor pi) B_N = B_j (pi tensor pi).             (3)

The pivot and twist preserve U. There is no singular factorial in this calculation: h < N in (2), and h < j on the quotient.

2. Why quotienting only the open strand quotients the entire knot

This is the essential one-component trace lemma. Choose a basis adapted to U and V/U on every physical strand. Along each underlying oriented strand segment, attach status 1 to a basis vector in U and status 0 to a quotient-basis vector. For every nonzero crossing coefficient, status can change from 0 to 1, but cannot change from 1 to 0. This is exactly preservation of U by each uncheck factor, with the strand types followed through the check permutation. The diagonal pivot preserves status, and a closing partial trace identifies the status at the two ends of each traced strand.

To calculate the induced open cut on V/U, its two open ends both have status 0. If the closed braid is a knot, there is one underlying component, and it passes through those ends. Following that entire component from its open input to its open output, a status-1 segment could never return to the final status 0. Therefore every segment of every contributing state has status 0. The contributing summands are precisely those obtained by replacing every color by V/U and taking the same cut trace there. This proves the all-strands color comparison; Schur’s lemma alone would not prove it.

This argument is termwise and works for arbitrary positive/negative words and any number of strands. It does not require a splitting V=U direct-sum W into modules. The original reduced cut on V is scalar even at this reducible color: it commutes with K and F, K has N distinct eigenvalues, and every internal arrow of F has coefficient one. Thus its scalar is the same scalar found on the nonzero quotient.

The component assumption cannot be omitted. For the identity two-strand braid, a separate closed N-color component has pivotal trace zero, whereas replacing it by the trivial quotient gives trace one. Our audit checks this failure as well as the knot identities. No analogous unqualified statement for links is used.

3. Pivotal, framing and half-root scalars

Write m=j-1 and epsilon_j=(-1)^m. On the quotient,

K^(1-N)=epsilon_j K,
theta_ADO=epsilon_j theta_j,
theta_j=q^(m(m+2)/2).                              (4)

Indeed KN=q(Nm) I=epsilon_j I, since the other weight term is q^(-2Na)=1. Also

theta_ADO/theta_j=q^(-Nm)=epsilon_j

from the defining ADO twist q((alpha2+2(1-N)alpha)/2). For a cut of a b-strand knot with writhe w, the b-1 traced pivots and the framing correction therefore produce the total factor

epsilon_j^(b-1-w)=1.                               (5)

The braid permutation is a b-cycle, so w and b-1 have the same parity. This proves the first equality in (1) without an unspecified phase.

The same bookkeeping can be performed in the stripped matrices used by the exact audits. The common positive Cartan scalar is q(m2/2), and its inverse occurs at a negative crossing. After removing it, the ordinary residual twist is

theta_j/q^(m^2/2)=q^m=x.

The ADO residual twist is x^(1-N)=epsilon_j x. Both conventions give (5). In particular no half-root factor is silently rounded to an integer q power. The proof covers even N and both parities of j.

4. The fundamental cable is the literal colored R

This comparison is needed to use a positive fundamental path trace for the actual j-color; calling a cable a color is not a proof. Work first over rational functions of q^(1/2), and retain the coproducts

Delta E=E tensor K+1 tensor E,
Delta F=F tensor 1+K^(-1) tensor F.

Formula (2), now as a locally finite operator on highest-weight modules, has the directly verifiable identities

(Delta tensor 1)R=R_13 R_23,
(1 tensor Delta)R=R_13 R_12.                      (6)

For detail, put Q=q^2 and h=r+s. The finite q-binomial expansion gives

(Delta E)^h = sum_(r+s=h) {h choose r}_Q
                          E_1^r E_2^s K_2^r,
(Delta F)^h = sum_(r+s=h) {h choose r}_Q
                          F_2^r K_2^(-s) F_3^s,
c_h {h choose r}_Q = Q^(rs)c_r c_s.                (7)

For the first identity of (6), move the R_23 Cartan factor left through E_1^r F_3^r in R_13. It supplies K_2^r; moving it past E_2^s supplies Q^(rs). This is exactly the first expansion in (7). For the second identity, moving the R_12 Cartan factor through the R_13 nilpotent term supplies K_2^(-s), and ordering it with F_2^r again supplies Q^(rs). The second expansion in (7) follows. These coefficient identities prove (6), including the Cartan scalar; they do not assume a tensor-category comparison theorem. The q-binomial identities themselves follow by induction from AB=QBA.

Let V_f be the fundamental two-dimensional module. In V_f^(tensor m) put

J_m(v_r)=(Delta^(m-1)F)^r(v_0 tensor ... tensor v_0),
                 0 <= r <= m.                    (8)

The coproduct relations give

E J_m(v_r)=[r][m-r+1]J_m(v_(r-1)),
F J_m(v_r)=J_m(v_(r+1)),
K J_m(v_r)=q^(m-2r)J_m(v_r).

The endpoint at r=m+1 is zero. There is a nonzero vector at every weight for m < N, so J_m is an injective intertwiner at the roots under consideration. Repeated use of (6), followed by naturality under this explicitly verified intertwiner, proves

B_fused (J_m tensor J_m)=(J_m tensor J_m) B_j,        (9)

where B_fused is the ordinary m-by-m crossing cable with its m^2 fundamental crossings. The inverse identity follows by invertibility. Equivalently (9) follows by expanding the two sides with (7). The highest-highest entry on each side is q(m2/2), so no additional central scalar is available or required.

On fundamental 01,10 the operator

U=q I-q^(1/2)B_f

has block [[q,-1],[-1,q^(-1)]] and is zero on 00,11. The kernel of all adjacent U_i in a fixed weight has dimension one: each adjacent exchange fixes the q-ratio between the two coefficients, and exchanges connect all binary words of that weight. It is the vector in (8). Thus the common kernel has dimension m+1 and is exactly im J_m. The Jones–Wenzl recursion used in the regular-period note has image this common kernel: its image is annihilated by the U_i, and it acts as identity on every common-kernel vector, by induction. Consequently its actual tensor image is precisely the j-color.

All specializations in this paragraph are regular for m <= N-2. The recursion uses only [1],…,[m], which are nonzero, and the ordinary R uses only [h]!, h <= m. No projector on a color of zero quantum dimension is used in the bound. The constant-color j=1 case is the empty tensor power and is immediate.

5. The trace identification and bound

The fundamental coproduct pivot is K^(tensor m), and (8) identifies its restriction with the ordinary pivot K_j. For any operator preserving the idempotent image, finite matrix trace on that image equals Tr(K^(tensor m) p_m A). Applying this to every block of (9) gives the second equality in (1). The framing scalar theta_j is exactly the one in (4). It also follows directly from the positive crossing’s pivotal partial trace: its highest-input entry has just the zero-transfer, zero-closed-index summand q(m2/2)q^m=theta_j.

The fundamental trace obeys

tau_(l+1)(A tensor I)=(q+q^(-1))tau_l(A),
tau_(l+1)((A tensor I)U_l)=tau_l(A).

The second equation is the literal partial pivotal trace of the displayed 2-by-2 block of U. These recursions identify it with the path trace whose local positivity is audited separately. At the principal root q=exp(pi*i/N), that trace is positive, the fundamental braid is unitary, and p_m is an orthogonal projection there. Its trace is [j], and the b-fold projection has trace [j]^b. Therefore

|F_N^M(-q^j)|=|F_N^Y(Q^j)|=|C_j(q)| <= [j]^(b-1)
   <= sin(pi/N)^(1-b) <= (N/2)^(b-1).              (10)

This applies to every j<N uniformly, with the original one-component source and framing. At j=N the quantum dimension vanishes, so the division in (1) and the positive-color argument do not apply.

6. Independent exact audit

The new audit constructs the defining full colored R independently, builds the fundamental coproduct highest-weight embedding (8), constructs the literal crossing cable, and tests both signs of (9). It also assembles the tensor-basis Jones–Wenzl projector, checks its image, generator intertwining and quantum trace, and restores the full Cartan/twist factors. The quotient tests use a separate original N-dimensional matrix construction and compare all colors j<N through complete knot cuts, including four-strand words and non-highest open inputs. A separate closed-component failure is checked explicitly.

Fresh result: PASS, seven quotient levels N=2,…,8, 228 whole-knot quotient-cut comparisons, and 22 literal signed-cable families through N=9, including boundary colors j=N-1 and both level parities. An additional 28 exact cut comparisons at generic x and -x, away from the integer-color orbit, independently check the even M descent used to pass to the Y-coordinate. No full-state pole is evaluated. Saved results.

The checks supplement the all-level arguments above. The result is a verification of the ordinary-color polynomial bound’s input, not a volume estimate for the Kashaev color or a claim of reflection positivity of its modified source.
