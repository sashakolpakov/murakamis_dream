The low-index complete-end kernel at even levels

Research proof, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The last physical color at an even level is close to the complete color, but they are different. The full kernel cannot simply be evaluated at the last physical row and called the complete-end kernel. This note proves the needed comparison for its low-index part. The comparison is relative, so it preserves the nonzero coefficient.

The proof uses an exact Chebyshev interpolation formula. Its alternating weights have sum one. The actual row recurrence controls the variation of the values against those weights; no positivity of the final quantum trace is assumed.

1. The original low-index kernel

Let N=2mN=2m, J=m−1J=m-1, and θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N. Use the original Newton polynomials and physical colors ek(z)=∏a=0k−1(ca−z)Sk2,ca=2cos⁡((2a+1)θ),Sk=∏a=1k2sin⁡(aθ).(1) e_k(z)=\frac{\prod_{a=0}^{k-1}(c_a-z)}{S_k^2},\qquad c_a=2\cos((2a+1)\theta),\qquad S_k=\prod_{a=1}^k2\sin(a\theta). \qquad\text{(1)} Define LN(w)=∑k=0m−1ek(−2)ek(w),Fj,N(w)=KN(cj,w).(2) L_N(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}e_k(-2)e_k(w),\qquad F_{j,N}(w)=K_N(c_j,w). \qquad\text{(2)} Thus LNL_N is the original complete-end kernel with its radical Newton indices k≥mk\ge m removed. At every physical color those removed polynomials vanish. In particular Fj,N(w)=∑k=0m−1ek(cj)ek(w)(0≤j≤J).(3) F_{j,N}(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}e_k(c_j)e_k(w) \quad(0\le j\le J). \qquad\text{(3)} Fix a compact set of free colors 𝒲γ,R={w:ℜw≤2−γ,|w|≤R},γ>0,(4) \mathcal W_{\gamma,R} =\{w:\Re w\le2-\gamma,\ |w|\le R\}, \qquad \gamma>0, \qquad\text{(4)} and allow constants to depend on this set. All asymptotic statements below are uniform on (4) as the even level tends to infinity.

Theorem 1. The original low-index kernel satisfies LN(w)FJ,N(w)=1+O(N−1/2).(5) \boxed{\qquad \frac{L_N(w)}{F_{J,N}(w)}=1+O(N^{-1/2}). \qquad} \qquad\text{(5)} The same estimate holds after every fixed number of derivatives in ww. In particular, LNL_N is nonzero on (4) for all sufficiently large even levels.

2. Exact alternating interpolation

The mm distinct physical colors are the zeros of Tm(z/2)T_m(z/2). Lagrange interpolation of the polynomial of degree at most m−1m-1 in the first variable in (3) therefore gives LN(w)=∑h=0m−1(−1)hah,NFJ−h,N(w),ah,N=1mcot⁡((h+12)θ)>0.(6) L_N(w)=\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}(-1)^h a_{h,N} F_{J-h,N}(w),\qquad a_{h,N}=\frac1m\cot\bigl((h+\tfrac12)\theta\bigr)>0. \qquad\text{(6)} For completeness, put φj=(2j+1)θ\varphi_j=(2j+1)\theta. The Lagrange coefficient at z=−2z=-2 is Tm(−1)(−2−cj)Tm′(cj/2)/2=(−1)m+j+1mtan⁡(φj/2).(7) \frac{T_m(-1)}{(-2-c_j)T_m'(c_j/2)/2} =\frac{(-1)^{m+j+1}}m\tan(\varphi_j/2). \qquad\text{(7)} Here Tm′(cos⁡φj)=m(−1)j/sin⁡φjT_m'(\cos\varphi_j)=m(-1)^j/\sin\varphi_j. Substitution of j=J−hj=J-h proves (6). Applying interpolation to the constant polynomial also proves the exact identity ∑h=0m−1(−1)hah,N=1.(8) \sum_{h=0}^{m-1}(-1)^h a_{h,N}=1. \qquad\text{(8)} The weights decrease with hh, and elementary differentiation of the cotangent gives constants independent of NN such that ah,N≤Ch+1,0<ah,N−ah+1,N≤C(h+1)2.(9) a_{h,N}\le\frac C{h+1},\qquad 0<a_{h,N}-a_{h+1,N}\le\frac C{(h+1)^2}. \qquad\text{(9)} The second estimate is needed only when h<m−1h<m-1. For example, use sin⁡x≥2x/π\sin x\ge2x/\pi on [0,π/2][0,\pi/2] in the derivative m−1θcsc⁡2((h+1/2)θ)m^{-1}\theta\csc^2((h+1/2)\theta). Also am−1,N=O(N−2)a_{m-1,N}=O(N^{-2}).

3. The actual rows vary slowly near the last row

The endpoint Riccati theorem applies to the original normalized solution, not to an independently chosen approximate solution. Write ρr=Fr,N/Fr−1,N,ur=ρr−1,Qr=cos⁡(rθ)sin⁡3(rθ),r0=⌊N/4⌋.(10) \rho_r=F_{r,N}/F_{r-1,N},\qquad u_r=\rho_r-1,\qquad Q_r=\frac{\cos(r\theta)}{\sin^3(r\theta)},\qquad r_0=\lfloor N/4\rfloor. \qquad\text{(10)} For all sufficiently large NN, it gives, on the entire tail, ℜur≥cQr,|ur|≤CQr,r0≤r≤J,Qr≍(N/2−r)/N.(11) \Re u_r\ge c Q_r,\qquad |u_r|\le C Q_r, \qquad r_0\le r\le J, \qquad Q_r\asymp (N/2-r)/N. \qquad\text{(11)} The upper bound throughout this tail follows explicitly from that proof. At distance at least N\sqrt N from the endpoint, its ratio error is O((N/2−r)−1)O((N/2-r)^{-1}), which is O((N/2−r)/N)O((N/2-r)/N) there; its limiting root differs from one by O(Qr)O(Q_r). In the final square-root layer the proof gives |ur|≤CQr|u_r|\le C Q_r directly. The lower bound is its propagated positive-real barrier. At every earlier row one still has ℜρr>1\Re\rho_r>1.

Set Bh=FJ−h,N/FJ,N,B0=1.(12) B_h=F_{J-h,N}/F_{J,N},\qquad B_0=1. \qquad\text{(12)} All ratios are defined by the zero-free physical solution. Equations (11) imply, for 0≤h≤J−r00\le h\le J-r_0, |Bh|≤Cexp⁡(−ch2/N),|Bh−1|≤Cmin⁡{h(h+1)/N,1},|Bh+1−Bh|≤C(h+1)Nexp⁡(−ch2/N).(13) \begin{split} |B_h|&\le C\exp(-c h^2/N),\\ |B_h-1|&\le C\min\{h(h+1)/N,1\},\\ |B_{h+1}-B_h|&\le \frac{C(h+1)}N\exp(-c h^2/N). \end{split} \qquad\text{(13)} The final line is used when J−h≥r0J-h\ge r_0. Indeed Bh+1=Bh/ρJ−h,|ρr|≥1+cQr.(14) B_{h+1}=B_h/\rho_{J-h},\qquad |\rho_r|\ge1+cQ_r. \qquad\text{(14)} Taking the product in (14), and using log⁡(1+cQr)≥c′Qr\log(1+cQ_r)\ge c'Q_r on this bounded tail, proves the first estimate in (13). Summing the elementary difference bound |Bh+1−Bh|≤|uJ−h||B_{h+1}-B_h|\le |u_{J-h}|, and using |Bh|≤1|B_h|\le1, proves the second. Retaining the first estimate in the difference bound proves the third. After this tail, monotonicity of the moduli |Fj,N||F_{j,N}| gives |Bh|≤Ce−cN,|Bh+1−Bh|≤Ce−cN(h>J−r0).(15) |B_h|\le C e^{-cN},\qquad |B_{h+1}-B_h|\le C e^{-cN} \quad(h>J-r_0). \qquad\text{(15)}

4. The alternating sum preserves its coefficient

Divide (6) by FJ,NF_{J,N}, and subtract (8). The error is exactly ∑h=0m−1(−1)hdh,dh=ah,N(Bh−1).(16) \sum_{h=0}^{m-1}(-1)^h d_h, \qquad d_h=a_{h,N}(B_h-1). \qquad\text{(16)} Partial sums of (−1)h(-1)^h have modulus at most one. Summation by parts therefore bounds (16) by |dm−1|+∑h=0m−2(ah,N−ah+1,N)|Bh−1|+∑h=0m−2ah+1,N|Bh+1−Bh|.(17) |d_{m-1}|+ \sum_{h=0}^{m-2}(a_{h,N}-a_{h+1,N})|B_h-1| +\sum_{h=0}^{m-2}a_{h+1,N}|B_{h+1}-B_h|. \qquad\text{(17)} The endpoint term is O(N−2)O(N^{-2}). By (9), (13), and the bound |Bh−1|≤2|B_h-1|\le2, the first sum is at most a constant times ∑h≤N1(h+1)2h(h+1)N+∑h>N1(h+1)2=O(N−1/2).(18) \sum_{h\le\sqrt N}\frac1{(h+1)^2}\frac{h(h+1)}N +\sum_{h>\sqrt N}\frac1{(h+1)^2} =O(N^{-1/2}). \qquad\text{(18)} The second sum, through the tail, is bounded by CN∑h≥0e−ch2/N=O(N−1/2).(19) \frac CN\sum_{h\ge0}e^{-c h^2/N} =O(N^{-1/2}). \qquad\text{(19)} Its remaining terms are exponentially small by (15). This proves (5). Apply the argument on a slightly larger compact subset of ℜw<2\Re w<2; Cauchy’s formula then proves the derivative estimates.

The cancellation in (16) has been estimated relative to the actual last row. This is the point that an absolute bound for the interpolation weights would miss.

5. Consequence for the geometric exponential rate

Put Ψ(w)=∫01/2Log⁡λ(x,w)dx,λ(x,w)=exp⁡(2asinh(2−w2cotπx)).(20) \Psi(w)=\int_0^{1/2}\operatorname{Log}\lambda(x,w)\,dx, \qquad \lambda(x,w)=\exp\left(2\operatorname{asinh} \left(\frac{\sqrt{2-w}}2\cot\pi x\right)\right). \qquad\text{(20)} The branches and normalization are those of the endpoint theorem. In particular Ψ(−2cos⁡t)=ψ(t,0)\Psi(-2\cos t)=\psi(t,0) on the original branch. Since J/N=1/2−1/NJ/N=1/2-1/N, the omitted part of the integral contributes only O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) to its exponent. Combining Theorem 1 with the endpoint theorem gives LN(w)=exp⁡{NΨ(w)+ẼN(w)},|∂wℓẼN(w)|≤Cℓlog⁡N(21) L_N(w)=\exp\{N\Psi(w)+\widetilde E_N(w)\},\qquad |\partial_w^\ell\widetilde E_N(w)|\le C_\ell\log N \qquad\text{(21)} for every fixed ℓ\ell. The logarithm of the quotient in (5) is the branch near zero, so it adds only O(N−1/2)O(N^{-1/2}), with the same derivative bounds, to the logarithm of the physical row.

At odd levels the low-index part at a complete end equals the full kernel, because every radical polynomial vanishes at the simple physical node −2-2. The odd-level conclusion of the endpoint theorem and (21) thus give a nonzero complete-end low-index factor at both parities. In a source calculation the removed radical terms must still be retained and bounded after their original traces. No estimate of the full even-level off-grid kernel is inferred.

This qualification is necessary. The exact radical asymptotic gives the full kernel different odd- and even-level exponential rates at w=(1−33i)/2w=(1-3\sqrt3\,i)/2. At that color the low sector still has the rate proved here. The additional even-level sector is larger before the original traces are taken.

6. Reproducing the finite identities

The accompanying checker constructs the original Newton polynomials, their last physical rows and their low complete-end sums separately. It checks the interpolation weights and the full finite identity (6). The reported relative errors in (5) are diagnostics; the proof of the uniform estimate is (10)–(19).
