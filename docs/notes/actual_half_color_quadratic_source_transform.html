A finite half-color quadratic transform for the original source

29 October 2026. This proves the exact simple-channel coefficient of the original figure-eight four-plat in the complete Casimir frame. The finite proof uses one terminating q-Chu identity, proved below by finite binomial expansion. It retains all root phases, common Cartan factors and the actual original cup/cap opening.

The resulting coefficient is a positive sine-binomial square sum times its known phase. It tests the pointed energy of the complete channel Gram, not the value of the invariant after its final cap.

1. A reusable odd-root quadratic transform

Let N=2m+1>=3, let Q have exact order N, put F_r=(Q;Q)_r and a=Q^(-m)=Q^(m+1). All factorials below have index at most 2m and are nonzero. Define

S_m=sum_(k=0)^m Q^[-k(m-k)] [m choose k]_Q^2.

Then EXACTLY

sum_(t=0)^m Q^t (a;Q)_t^3/F_t^2
   =(-1)^m Q^[-m(3m+1)/2] S_m.                 (1)

This is a finite 3-parameter-to-2-parameter quadratic source transform. It is valid at composite odd levels as well as prime ones, with no square-root choice or root-zero denominator.

Proof. Expand the THIRD Pochhammer by the finite binomial theorem, put t=k+j, n=m-k, and retain the other two factors. The left side becomes

sum_(k=0)^m (-a)^k Q^[k(k-1)/2+k](a;Q)_k^2/F_k^2
   *sum_(j=0)^n Q^j (aQ^k;Q)_j^2
                                  /[F_j(Q^(k+1);Q)_j].

Apply terminating q-Chu to the inner sum with terminating length n, b=Q^-n=aQ^k, c=Q^(k+1). It is

Q^(-n^2)(Q^(m+1);Q)_n/(Q^(k+1);Q)_n
   =Q^(-n^2)(a;Q)_n F_k/F_m.                   (2)

Here the ODD ROOT relation Q^(m+1)=a is essential. Substitute

(a;Q)_r=(-1)^r Q^[-mr+r(r-1)/2]F_m/F_(m-r).

Each k-th term is now (-1)^m Q^[-m(3m+1)/2-k(m-k)] [m choose k]_Q^2. This proves (1), including every sign and exponent.

2. Elementary finite q-Chu and Durfee identities

The finite identity used in (2), whenever its denominators are units, is

sum_(j=0)^n (Q^-n;Q)_j(b;Q)_j Q^j
                              /[F_j(c;Q)_j]
   =b^n(c/b;Q)_n/(c;Q)_n.                     (3)

No published summation proof is a premise. Here is a direct finite proof. First the binomial theorem gives

sum_(s=0)^n (Q^-n;Q)_s Q^s/F_s
                           *(cQ^s;Q)_(n-s)
   =(-c)^n Q^[n(n-1)/2].                      (4)

To check (4), expand the last Pochhammer. Its c^r coefficient contains (Q^(1-(n-r));Q)_(n-r), zero unless r=n. Only that last coefficient survives, giving the right side.

Multiply (3) by (c;Q)_n, expand (b;Q)_j, and extract b^r. Set j=r+s and apply (4) with length n-r and parameter cQ^r. The result is

(-c)^(n-r) Q^[(n-r)(n-r-1)/2][n choose r]_Q,

the b^r coefficient of b^n(c/b;Q)_n. Thus (3) is a rational identity before specialization and holds at the stated roots.

Its useful finite Durfee consequence is

sum_(r=0)^t Q^(r^2)/(F_r^2 F_(t-r))=1/F_t^2.   (5)

Indeed take n=t, b=Q^-t, c=0 in (3), multiply by Q(t2), and reverse its summation index. This proves (5) by the same finite algebra, without an infinite-product interpretation.

3. The actual complete-channel coefficient

Now use the principal q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2, d=2m, x=q^d=Q^m, nu_m=(-1)^m q^[m(m+1)]. Let R_1,R_2 be the two original generators on the complete source multiplicity module, including their common Cartan kappa. The TRUE four-plat action order is [2,-1,2,2], hence

W=R_2^2 R_1^(-1) R_2.

Let V be the complete physical Casimir/Hermite frame, J the actual anti-Pascal recoupling, and p the original cup. Define the COMPLETE right-channel output

z=V^(-1)J Wp.

At odd N the node c=-2 is simple, with normalized eigenvector f_0=1, crossing value nu_m and bilinear norm sigma=x^d nu_m. Its exact coefficient is

z_simple=N Q^[m(m+1)] F_m S_m.                  (6)

At the principal root S_m=sum_(r=0)^m |[m choose r]_Q|^2>0. Thus (6) is not an expanded path or a guessed saddle surviving selection; it is the ENTIRE actual channel after all its sums.

4. Source reduction to the quadratic transform

The actual J is symmetric: its anti-Pascal formula and F_jF_(d-j)=N(-1)^jQ^[j(j+1)/2] give J_(i,j)=J_(j,i). Put y=Jf, h=R_2p, v=R_1^(-1)h. The lower bidiagonal equation for y, with endpoint y_d=f_0=1, gives exactly

y_i=0 for i<m,
y_(m+r)=Q^[-r(m-r)][m choose r]_Q^2.             (7)

The successive ratio is the square of |1-Q^(m-r)|/|1-Q^(r+1)|; its complex factorial form in (7) keeps the phase identity at arbitrary primitive Q.

The FIRST original R_2 on the cup highest lift has a forced final third-head color zero. In the literal positive crossing the transfer must equal that second input color. Its coefficient simplifies to kappa F_(d-i), using x^2=Q^-1. Therefore

h_i=kappa p_i F_(d-i), p_i=Nx^(i-d).            (8)

The literal original inverse crossing then gives

v_i=x^(i-d)(-1)^i Q^[i(i+1)/2-d] F_(d-i)/F_i
                     *sum_(b=i)^d Q^b F_b^3/F_(b-i).     (9)

For example (9) follows directly by the finite inverse-R transfer sum and the SAME reflection identity; the common kappa in (8) cancels the prescribed inverse kappa. No external normalization has been changed.

Multiply (9) by (7), put i=m+r, and interchange b=m+t and r. Using (5) gives

y^T v=C sum_(t=0)^m Q^(m+t) F_(m+t)^3/F_t^2,
C=(-1)^m x^(-m) Q^[m(m+1)/2+1].                 (10)

This intermediate reduction is a finite identity with the original source; in particular no extension beyond the true finite ranges has been made. Since F_(m+t)=F_m(a;Q)_t, (1) evaluates (10):

y^T v=Q^[-2m^2+m+1]F_m^3 S_m
       =F_m^3S_m=N nu_m F_m S_m.               (11)

The remaining exponent is (1-m)(2m+1), a full root period. The last equality is the full reflection identity F_m^2=N nu_m.

Finally the simple inverse channel row is f^T/sigma. Moving the two terminal R_2 factors through J and selecting that simple channel gives

z_simple=(kappa^2 nu_m^2/sigma)y^T v
         =nu_m y^T v.

Here kappa^2=x^d for the actual odd-level full Cartan. Substituting (11) proves (6), with all source and common-line phases retained.

5. Scope for the proposed positive energy

For the balanced complete-channel Gram G=(V^(-1)J)^*(V^(-1)J)/N, the original pointed energy is E_N=N^(-1)||z||_2^2. Equation (6) implies EXACTLY

E_N>=N^2 S_m^2,                                 (12)

because |F_m|^2=N. The asymptotic consequence is developed in the companion energy audit; it concerns THIS particular positive Gram. It does not contradict its exact Cauchy inequality, and it does not rule out a different source-preserving coherent terminal Gram that contracts more of the actual tensor before squaring.
