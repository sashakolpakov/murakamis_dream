Uniform face, angle and quantum-state certificates for the twist families

Research note, 25 October 2026. This note reconstructs the geometric and finite matrix arguments for the explicit face families used in the literature. The quantum asymptotic and annihilator arguments are in literature_state_volume.md and general_annihilator_and_volume.md.

Source conventions are Ben Aribi–Guéritaud–Piguet-Nakazawa, Geometric triangulations and the Teichmüller TQFT volume conjecture for twist knots, arXiv:1903.09480v5, especially Sections 4, 5 and 8; and the later FAMED definition and integral (4.5) in arXiv:2410.10776v2.

We do not use their geometricity or asymptotic theorem as a black box. The face pairings, the geometry of their angle maximum, and the Gaussian/edge identity below are explicit. The subsequent topological and peripheral audit reconstructs their identification with the twist diagrams and independently certifies the meridian and preferred longitude. Global quantum move identities remain separate work; this qualification remains part of the scope of the final triangulation-independent knot theorem. All semiclassical limits are for each fixed p. The constants and strip gaps may depend on p; no simultaneous p->infinity, b->0 estimate is asserted.

1. The two ordered face families

Let p>=0. There are p+3 tetrahedra T_1,…,T_p,U,V,W and 2p+6 faces

e_1,...,e_(p+1), f_1,...,f_p, v,r,s,g,u.

Set f_0=e_1. A tuple lists faces opposite the ordered vertices 0,1,2,3. Pair equal face labels by the order-preserving map on their three vertices.

Tetrahedron Odd family Even family
T_j (e_j,e_(j+1),f_j,f_(j-1)) same
U (r,v,s,g) same
V (g,s,f_p,u) (s,g,f_p,u)
W (u,r,v,e_(p+1)) (u,v,r,e_(p+1))

The signs are (+,…,+,-,-,-) in the odd family and (+,…,+,+,-,-) in the even family. The literature identifies these with X_(2p+3) and X_(2p+2), respectively. The p=0 even member is the three-tetrahedron subdivision of the figure-eight decomposition; the p=0 odd member is the three-tetrahedron 5_2 example.

An edge angle a is attached to 01 and 23, b to 02 and 13, and c to 03 and 12. Following the face pairings gives the chain weights

c_(j-1)+2b_j+c_(j+1),   1<=j<p,   c_0=0,

and the following end weights for p>=1:

s: 2a_U+b_V+c_V+a_W+b_W,
0: 2a_1+c_1+2a_2+...+2a_p+a_V+c_W.

Odd family:

p:   c_(p-1)+2b_p+b_U+b_V+a_W,
p+1: c_p+b_U+2c_U+a_V+c_V+b_W+c_W.

Even family:

p:   c_(p-1)+2b_p+b_U+2c_U+a_V+b_V+a_W+c_W,
p+1: c_p+b_U+c_V+b_W.

Each weight is 2pi. Their sum is twice the sum of all tetrahedral angles, so one edge equation is redundant. The chains above can be checked directly by crossing each face with the two endpoints of the local edge. Every one of the 6(p+3) edge occurrences appears once in this list. The same local crossing rule gives the p=0 matrices recorded in Section 5 below.

The executable face construction in experiments/twist_family_certificates.py does the edge identifications from the vertex maps, rather than using the listed weights as an input. It also checks the oriented face gluings, one ideal vertex, and the Euler characteristic zero of its link in finite examples. For the uniform geometric argument, a connected torus link is the topological input supplied by this ordered face family; the edge cycles and orientation can also be followed inductively along the chain.

2. A positive angle structure for every p

Angles in the following formulas have their usual radian units. For p>=1 set

epsilon=pi/[12(p+1)^2].

For j<p take

(a_j,b_j,c_j)=(epsilon, pi-epsilon(j^2+1), epsilon j^2).

In the odd family take

(a_p,b_p,c_p)
   =(pi/2-epsilon(p^2+2p-1)/2,
     pi/2-epsilon(p-1)^2/2, epsilon p^2),
(A,B,C)=(pi/2+epsilon p^2/2, pi/3, pi/6-epsilon p^2/2),
U=(A,B,C), V=(A,B,C), W=(B,C,A).

In the even family take

(a_p,b_p,c_p)
   =(3pi/4-epsilon(p^2+2p-1)/2,
     pi/4-epsilon(p-1)^2/2, epsilon p^2),
(A,B,C)=(pi/4+epsilon p^2/2,
         2pi/3-epsilon p^2/3, pi/12-epsilon p^2/6),
U=(A,B,C), V=(A,C,B), W=(C,B,A).

All entries are strictly between 0 and pi. Substitution in the preceding edge weights verifies every balancing equation. For p=0, use (A,B,C)= (pi/2,pi/3,pi/6) in the odd family and (pi/6,2pi/3,pi/6) in the even family. The explicit edge-incidence matrices below verify these cases directly.

3. The volume maximum is interior: a reconstructed proof

Let Lambda(theta)=-integral_0^theta log(2sin t)dt. The volume of a positively oriented ideal tetrahedron with angles summing to pi is

V(a,b,c)=Lambda(a)+Lambda(b)+Lambda(c).

It is continuous on the closed angle simplex, positive in its interior and zero on its boundary. Its restricted Hessian, in a,b with c=pi-a-b, is

-[[cot a+cot c, cot c],[cot c,cot b+cot c]].

The diagonal pivots are positive before the minus sign and the determinant is 1, so this Hessian is negative definite. Thus the sum over tetrahedra is strictly concave on the positive angle polytope. It has a maximum on its compact closure, and the explicit positive structure makes that maximum strictly positive.

3.1 The only possible flat tetrahedra at a maximum are taut

A triple (0,b,pi-b) with 0<b<pi gains volume with derivative +infinity along a segment to any positive angle structure: Lambda’(t)=-log(2sin t), and the new positive zero angle supplies -a log epsilon. A taut triple (0,0,pi) has a finite, nonnegative one-sided derivative along that segment; its epsilon log epsilon terms cancel. All other tetrahedra have finite derivatives or the same positive divergence. Hence a maximum cannot contain a flat non-taut tetrahedron. Any zero angle at a maximum belongs to a permutation of (0,0,pi).

3.2 Symmetry reduction preserves a global maximum

In the odd family align the end triples as

U, V, (c_W,a_W,b_W).

In the even family align them as

U, (a_V,c_V,b_V), (c_W,b_W,a_W).

In either case the s equation says 2a_U=a_V+c_W. Replace the three aligned triples by their arithmetic average. Its first coordinate equals a_U. The other two coordinates preserve their respective sums in the endpoint constraints. Thus the averaged triples still satisfy all the edge equations. Concavity and invariance of ideal volume under angle permutation show that their total volume has not decreased. There is therefore a symmetric global maximum with aligned triples all equal to (A,B,C).

For p>=1 the reduced odd endpoint constraints are

c_(p-1)+2b_p+3B=2pi,     c_p+A+3C=pi,

and the reduced even endpoint constraints are

c_(p-1)+2b_p+A+3C=pi,    c_p+3B=2pi.

In both cases the earlier chain equations remain, and c_0=0. These reductions follow by substitution in the full edge weights; the redundant 0 equation then holds automatically.

3.3 Odd family

At a symmetric maximum, c_p=pi would imply A=C=0, B=pi, contradicting the first endpoint constraint. If c_p=0, the convex nonnegative sequence (0,c_1,…,c_p) is identically zero, because

c_(j-1)-2c_j+c_(j+1)=2a_j>=0,   j<p.

Then b_p is 0 or pi by Section 3.1. For b_p=0 the endpoint equations give U=(0,2pi/3,pi/3), forbidden by that section. For b_p=pi all tetrahedra are taut and the total volume is zero. Both possibilities contradict maximality. Thus 0<c_p<pi and T_p is positive.

None of A,B,C can be pi: the endpoint equations exclude B=pi and C=pi, and A=pi would force c_p=0. Hence U,V,W are positive by Section 3.1. The chain equations first give b_j>0 by forward induction, starting from b_1=pi-c_2/2>=pi/2. Descending induction then gives b_j<pi: the first step uses c_p>0, and each following step uses positivity of the next tetrahedron. By Section 3.1 every chain tetrahedron is positive. For p=1 the chain inductions are empty and the endpoint argument is unchanged.

For p=0 the reduced condition is A+3C=pi. Its interior contains positive triples. Its boundary has either a flat non-taut triple or zero total volume, so its maximum is interior as well.

3.4 Even family

The second endpoint equation gives B=(2pi-c_p)/3 in [pi/3,2pi/3]. Thus U is positive by Section 3.1. The chain forward induction gives b_j>0 for j<p.

If b_(p-1)=pi, the last chain equation forces c_p=0. The first endpoint then forces b_p=0, and the two endpoint equations give U=(0,2pi/3,pi/3), a contradiction. Descending induction yields b_j<pi for all j<p, so these tetrahedra are positive. Convexity of (0,c_1,…,c_p) then gives c_p>0. For p=1 the same c_p=0 contradiction follows directly from the endpoints. Thus the only remaining possible flat tetrahedron is

(a_p,b_p,c_p)=(0,0,pi).

Suppose this occurs. Write r=c_(p-1), or r=0 if p=1. The endpoint equations give

U=((pi+r)/2, pi/3, pi/6-r/2),       0<=r<pi/3.

For p>=2 perturb the last two chain triples and U by the following column increments, multiplied by small t>0:

T_(p-1): (-1,+1,0),
T_p:     (+2,0,-2),
U:       (-1,+2/3,+1/3),

and propagate the same aligned U change to V,W. These preserve every angle and edge equation. For p=1 omit the first increment. T_p stays flat and contributes zero volume on the path; all other affected tetrahedra remain positive. Direct differentiation gives

exp(-V'(0+))
   = [sin b_(p-1)/sin a_(p-1)]
        sin^2 B sin C / sin^3 A,                       (3.1)

with the bracket absent for p=1. The chain equations and convexity give a_(p-1) in [pi/6,pi/2], so the bracket is at most 2. Also

sin^2 B sin C/sin^3 A
   = (3/4) sin(pi/6-r/2)/cos^3(r/2) <=3/8.

For the inequality, its logarithmic derivative in t=r/2 is -cot(pi/6-t)+3tan t<=0 on [0,pi/6). Therefore (3.1) is at most 3/4<1, or 3/8<1 for p=1. This gives V’(0+)>0, contradicting the maximum. The maximum is consequently positive in every tetrahedron.

For p=0 the symmetric constraints reduce to B=2pi/3, A+C=pi/3. The strictly concave symmetric volume is maximized at A=C=pi/6, an interior point.

3.5 Why an interior maximum gives a complete hyperbolic metric

Here is the needed direction of the usual Casson–Rivin argument, reconstructed from its multipliers. At an interior maximum on the full angle polytope, there are a multiplier lambda_T for each tetrahedron and mu_e for each global edge such that for opposite local edges e,e’ carrying angle theta,

-log(2sin theta)=lambda_T+mu_e+mu_e'.                    (3.2)

This is ordinary linear algebra: the derivative is orthogonal to the kernel of the affine constraint matrix. Independence of all constraint rows is not required.

Realize the unique positive ideal tetrahedron with each prescribed angle triple. At its ideal vertex v scale the Euclidean cusp triangle by

exp(lambda_T+sum_(w!=v) mu_(vw)).

Its side opposite the corner along vw then has length

exp(mu_(va)+mu_(vb)-mu_(ab))/2,

where v,a,b are the vertices of the face containing that side. This follows from (3.2) and the Euclidean sine rule. The expression depends only on the three global edges of that face. Hence it matches on paired faces.

All cusp triangles therefore glue isometrically. Around a global edge their angles sum to 2pi, so the cusp surface has no cone singularities. The face isometries preserve these horosphere cuts; the possible dilation in the edge holonomy is consequently 1, while its rotation is 2pi. The tetrahedra glue to a nonsingular hyperbolic metric. The compact torus cusp section is flat and gives a complete cusp end. This proves a complete finite-volume metric, whose volume is the sum of the ideal tetrahedral volumes.

This argument supplies positive complete geometric shapes without relying on a numerical maximizer or on the source’s geometricity theorem.

4. The face Gaussian for every p, from elementary elimination

Use the current FAMED spectral variables t_j,t_U,t_V,t_W, and the equations script-A f+script-B t=0, with script-B’s lower block the tetrahedral sign matrix. The chain face equations give

f_j=e_1-sum_(k<=j)t_k,
e_(j+1)=e_j+f_j.

The end equations force f_p=t_U in both parities, hence

e_1=t_U+sum_k t_k,
f_j=t_U+sum_(k>j)t_k,
e_j=j t_U+sum_k min(j,k)t_k.

Odd end solution:

u=t_U-t_V, s=t_V-t_U, g=t_V-2t_U,
v=(p+1)t_U+sum_k k t_k+t_W,
r=(p+2)t_U+sum_k k t_k-t_V+t_W.

Even end solution:

u=s=t_U-t_V, g=2t_U-t_V,
r=(p+1)t_U+sum_k k t_k+t_W,
v=(p+2)t_U+sum_k k t_k-t_V+t_W.

The homogeneous equations have only the zero solution, so script-A is invertible for every p, including p=0. The same elimination uses only unit pivots, so its determinant has absolute value 1.

Taking the symmetric part of the selected x_0 face variables gives Q_f:

(Q_f)_ij=min(i,j),   (Q_f)_(iU)=i,
(Q_f)_(iV)=(Q_f)_(iW)=0,    1<=i,j<=p,

and the U,V,W block

odd:  [[p+2,-3/2,1],[-3/2,1,-1/2],[1,-1/2,0]],
even: [[p+1, 1/2,1],[ 1/2,-1,-1/2],[1,-1/2,0]].

Thus G=-2Q_f+diag((epsilon+1)/2) is explicit. In the chain block G_ij=-2min(i,j)+delta_ij and G_(iU)=-2i. Its end blocks are

odd:  [[-2p-4,3,-2],[3,-2,1],[-2,1,0]],
even: [[-2p-1,-1,-2],[-1,2,1],[-2,1,0]].

5. Edge and peripheral matrices, with no assumed inverse formula

At a positive tetrahedron the angles a,b,c correspond to z,z’‘,z’; at a negative one they correspond to z,z’,z’‘. Convert the actual edge incidences accordingly to G_0,G’,G’‘, and set A_edge=G_0-G’, B_edge=G’‘-G’. Direct substitution of the displayed G in each chain and end row gives

A_edge=B_edge G.

The preferred-longitude rows used in the source, independently identified by normal curves and integral boundary certificates in the topological companion note, are

odd:  a_l=(...,2,-2,2), b_l=(...,0,2,2),
even: a_l=(...,-2,4,2), b_l=(...,0,2,0).

They too satisfy a_l=b_l G. Their branch constant is nu_l=2pi. For p>=1, take edge rows s,0,1,…,p-1,p+1 and this longitude row. In B they read

s: -v_W,
0: -v_1+v_W,
k: 2v_k-v_(k-1)-v_(k+1), 1<=k<p, v_0=0,
p+1: -v_p+v_U+v_V                 (odd),
      -v_p+v_U+v_V-v_W            (even),
l: 2v_V+2v_W                      (odd),
   2v_V                            (even).

Their homogeneous kernel is zero, starting with v_W=v_1=0, then the chain, and finally V,U. Thus B is invertible; unit elimination gives |det B|=2. The corresponding A satisfies B^(-1)A=G. Solving Bv=e_last gives

2 B^(-1)e_last=(0,...,0,-1,+1,0).                       (5.1)

The affine boundary coordinate is therefore x=y_V-y_U. At the complete maximum the aligned U,V triples give exactly the same positive shape, z_U=z_V, so x=0 and gamma=0. No unknown peripheral constant is needed.

For p=0, explicit choices of the two edge rows and the longitude row give

A_odd=[[-1,0,1],[-1,1,-1],[2,-2,2]],
B_odd=[[-1,-1,1],[1,1,0],[0,2,2]],
A_even=[[0,0,-1],[-2,1,1],[-2,4,2]],
B_even=[[1,1,-1],[-1,-1,2],[0,2,0]].

They have |det B|=2, the same identity B^(-1)A=G, and the same boundary vector (-1,+1,0). All p>=0 are thus covered by explicit matrix certificates.

For later exact normalization comparisons, the vector c/pi=B^(-1)(nu/pi)-G1 has chain entries i(2p-i+1), V entry -1, W entry 1, and U entry p^2+p+2 in the odd family or p^2+p+4 in the even family. Here each edge branch constant is 2-sum_j G’_ej, in units of pi.

6. Comparison with the older published twist-state formulas

The FAMED state has all Phi factors in the numerator. Transform to the older spectral variables y’_j by

z_j=-y'_j (chain), z_W=y'_W,
odd: z_U=y'_U,  z_V=y'_U+x_0,
even: z_U=-y'_U, z_V=x_0-y'_U,

where z here denotes spectral integration coordinates, not complex tetrahedron shapes, and x=2pi sqrt(hbar)x_0. Inverting the chain factors, and the U factor in the even case, uses the exact identity

Phi_b(-y')=zeta_b exp(pi i y'^2)/Phi_b(y'),
zeta_b=exp(i pi(b^2+b^(-2))/12).

Consequently the exact FAMED integral equals

C_X zeta_b^r/(2pi sqrt(hbar)) times J_derived(hbar,x_0),

where r=p for odd parity and r=p+1 for even parity, and C_X is the explicit orientation constant of the chosen published fiber convention. Its absolute value is 1 in these unimodular face families. No exponential factor of nonunit modulus has been dropped at the complete point.

The reduced Gaussian on y’_1,…,y’_p,y’_U,y’_W has chain block min(i,j), chain-U entries -i in the odd case and +i in the even case, and U,W block

odd:  [[p,1/2],[1/2,0]],
even: [[p+1,-1/2],[-1/2,0]].

The extra quadratic boundary term is

odd:  x_0(x_0-y'_U-y'_W),
even: x_0(y'_U-y'_W-x_0).                              (6.1)

The linear vector, divided by pi, has chain entries -i(2p-i+1), W entry 1, U entry p^2+p+1 in the odd case and -(p^2+p+3) in the even case, and the extra boundary term is -pi x_0/sqrt(hbar). These formulas follow by ordinary quadratic substitution using Sections 4–5.

A version-specific discrepancy. In arXiv:1903.09480v5, the even theorem statement and its concluding state formula print the odd boundary quadratic x_0(x_0-y’_U-y’_W). The preceding derivation explicitly computes the even expression in (6.1). Our direct face-matrix calculation agrees with that derivation. The difference depends on the internal variable y’_U, so it is not an overall scalar peripheral factor. Both expressions agree at x_0=0, which is why this discrepancy does not change their complete-point volume limit. The exact generic-boundary definition used here is the later FAMED integral (4.5), retaining its own published convention. The final journal version has not been checked for this discrepancy.

7. What follows, and what remains to audit

For every member of these explicit ordered face families, Sections 2–5 provide the positive complete geometric and finite matrix certificates required by the independent state-integral theorem. Thus its published FAMED state has the hyperbolic volume limit. In the Andersen–Malusà b-shift algebra acting on its explicitly rescaled spectral variable, it also has a nonzero annihilator and a primitive minimal operator, by the dimension-count and moment proof. The classical symbol contains the geometric peripheral curve with the stated lift and orientation conventions.

At the complete boundary value, the older displayed twist states have the same limit because their exact ratio to the derived state is a unit-modulus phase times a power of sqrt(hbar). Therefore no arbitrary recurrence boundary condition or colored Jones sequence has been used.

The subsequent topological note reconstructs the diagram-to-face-family construction and independently identifies the peripheral curves. It gives a three-arc marked-edge-germ certificate for the actual meridian, a ten-arc auxiliary curve, and an integral boundary certificate for the preferred longitude. Exact angle-constraint identities show that the longitude branch correction is zero in both parities. Thus these results apply to the stated literature realizations of the named twist knots. The exact global state transformations needed to identify arbitrary triangulations and the broader alternating-knot extension remain open.

Run python3 experiments/twist_family_certificates.py for exact diagnostics on p=0,…,8 in both parities. They recompute edge classes, link counts, face matrices, positive angle equations, and the FAMED and peripheral matrix identities from the face tuples. The proofs above, rather than the finite sample, establish the displayed algebraic and angle statements for all p.
