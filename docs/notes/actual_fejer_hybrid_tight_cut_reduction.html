Hybrid Fejer flow: all proper tight sets come from permitted digon cuts

31 October 2026. This is a finite incidence theorem for the ACTUAL loopless white triangular cellulation obtained after source-preserving identity smoothing. The closed local entropy and original shared source provide its local functions and literal incidence conventions. It does not replace a matching/matching Green cut by two independent entropy maxima.

1. Literal hybrid network

Let T be a loopless triangular cellulation of the sphere, with R+2 white vertices and 2R triangular cells. Multiple edges remain distinct. The dual cubic graph has its original perfect matching: each port is the pair of triangles adjacent to its matching white edge. Keep two distinct protected white vertices u,v joined by an actual edge. Every other white vertex has target1; both protected targets are0.

Some ports are tagged CROSS, others GREEN. A CROSS port retains its TWO triangle nodes, each of supply1/2, incident to that triangle’s ordinary vertices. A GREEN port has ONE node of supply1 and its FOUR original incidences x,b,c,y: shared outer triangle corners x,y are aggregated once. Distinct incidences among these four remain parallel arcs if their global face labels coincide. This is the hybrid limiting flow used to perturb a fixed cross/strict-one-sided entropy point. The full supply is R, equal to the ordinary targets.

For a set S of ordinary vertices let C_0(S) be the total supply of the complete triangle network touching S, and C_H(S) that of the hybrid network. Directly from the original pair of half-supplies,

C_H(S)=C_0(S)+(1/2)g(S),                        (1)

where g(S) counts the GREEN ports for which EXACTLY ONE of the two triangles touches S. In particular merging a pair never lowers a Hall capacity. The finite flow criterion is C_H(S)>=|S| for every S.

2. Complete Hall inequality and the equality case

Take a nonempty proper S among the R ordinary vertices, and put U=V(T), n=|U|. The induced embedded graph G=T[U] is loopless and contains the retained protected edge uv. Let p be the number of original triangular cells wholly supported on U. Each is still an actual face of G; a triangular face of G enclosing removed vertices does NOT count in p. Every other original triangle touches S, so

C_0(S)=(2R-p)/2,
C_0(S)=|S| iff p=2n-4.                         (2)

Here is a face count which also handles disconnected induced graphs and isolated vertices. Move one point of S to infinity. Every original triangle supported on U is bounded in this planar drawing. For a connected component G_i with n_i>=2 vertices, write f_i for its bounded faces, e_i for its edges and p_i for its bounded original triangles. All face boundary walks have length at least2. Euler and edge counting give

e_i=n_i+f_i-1,
2e_i>=3p_i+2(f_i-p_i)+2,
p_i<=2n_i-4.                                  (3)

The final2 is the outer boundary walk. An isolated vertex contributes no triangle. If there are c>=1 nontrivial components and a isolated vertices, summing gives

p<=2(n-a)-4c<=2n-4.                            (4)

Thus the complete and hybrid Hall inequalities hold. For S empty or all ordinary vertices they are the trivial zero or total balance; every triangle has an ordinary corner because it has three distinct vertices and only u,v are protected.

If S is proper and tight, p=2n-4. Equations (3)–(4) force a=0,c=1: G is connected. Every face not one of the p original triangles has boundary walk EXACTLY two edges, including the outer face. Since proper S implies n>=3, such a two-edge face cannot be one bridge traversed twice: its next boundary step would continue around the nontrivial remainder at one of its endpoints. Equivalently a bridge bounding a complete two-step face walk would make the entire connected graph a single edge. Consequently these faces are genuine digons bounded by two DISTINCT parallel original edges.

Every removed S vertex belongs to the interior of one of these digon faces. Conversely each such non-original face contains removed vertices; the original cellulation is triangular. Therefore S is precisely the union of the interior vertex sets of these pairwise interior-disjoint actual digon disks. Each disk is mark-free in its interior. This classification uses the induced graph at S, not an assumed preselected digon decomposition tree.

3. Hybrid tightness merges only neighboring holes with the same endpoints

Suppose C_H(S)=|S|. Then (1) and (4) force both C_0(S)=|S| and g(S)=0. Consider the digon disks from section2. Across an edge on one such disk’s boundary, the inside triangle touches S. If this edge is MATCHING and its port is GREEN, the other triangle must touch S too, or that port would contribute to g(S). That other triangle belongs to a second digon disk on the other side of the SAME boundary edge. The two digons therefore have the same ordered pair of boundary vertices. Merge them across that green edge. Their union is again a digon disk bounded by the two remaining parallel edges.

For clarity, all these mergers can be done at once. The adjacency graph of the S-digon disks across green matching boundary edges has degree at most2. Every connected component has a fixed endpoint pair. It cannot be a cycle for a proper S: a full cyclic family of parallel edge sectors fills the sphere except those two vertices, so the connected induced graph G would have only those vertices, contrary to n>=3. Its components are paths; each merges to one actual digon. This also proves that an intervening matching edge is removed only from the description of the separating boundary, not from the source or from its Green pair.

After merging, every surviving matching boundary edge belongs to a CROSS port. A MAIN boundary edge never splits a paired triangle node. The two boundary edges of each resulting digon have the same type: the disk contains 2v triangular cells for v interior vertices, and the parity count of their one matching incidence per dual vertex forces an even number of matching edges to cross its two-edge boundary. Thus the permitted cuts are exactly

MAIN / MAIN; or MATCHING / MATCHING with BOTH ports CROSS. (5)

Each resulting disk is individually hybrid-tight. Its 2v triangles exhaust the demand v of its interior vertices; no green node crosses its boundary. The original tight set is a union of their interiors. In particular a general GREEN matching/matching cut has NOT been declared tight, split, or assigned additive entropy.

The full set S of ordinary vertices is only the redundant total balance. Its possible U={u,v} one-edge or full-cycle exception does not force any additional ordinary incidence to vanish.

4. Forced zeros and a positive relative-interior witness

For any feasible hybrid flow, a tight S forces every node touching S to send its entire supply into S. Thus each of its arcs to an ordinary vertex outside S is zero. Conversely an ordinary incidence arc is forced zero ONLY this way. One direct proof avoids a nondegeneracy assumption on the balance matrix. To force a small positive amount epsilon on an arc n->f, reduce that row supply and the target at f by epsilon. For any vertex set S containing f, both capacity and demand decrease equally. For S not containing f but touched by n, only capacity decreases. Such a constraint obstructs every positive epsilon exactly when S was tight. The finitely many other inequalities have positive slack, so a sufficiently small epsilon preserves them. The ordinary Hall criterion then supplies the remaining flow.

Hence every non-forced ordinary arc is positive in some feasible flow. Averaging finitely many such flows gives one witness positive on ALL non-forced ordinary arcs and zero on the forced ones. Redundant face balances cause no difficulty. Equations (2)–(5) show that all forced zeros have the precise source-permitted digon origin above.

Inside a MAIN/MAIN tight disk, whole ports remain inside and all incidences at its boundary white faces are zero. At a permitted MATCHING/MATCHING cut, its two inside CROSS triangles each have their two boundary corners zero and their interior middle corner1/2. These are constant, zero-entropy triangles. Only CROSS pairs are cut, so their additive C+C data may be retained/repaired across the boundary; no difference E=C_input-C_output is separated into independent pieces. The original outside and inside middle degrees are not reset by this network statement. Geometry and actual face-pairing assembly remain separate obligations.

These cuts may be iterated as an exact HYBRID flow decomposition. Close the two sides by identifying their two boundary edges. The inside has new protected pair a,b and the outside retains its old pair. A MAIN cut leaves whole port nodes on their original sides. At a permitted matching cut, retain each of the four original CROSS triangle nodes on its own side and pair the two nodes on a closed side only as the formal C+C description. This creates no new physical coupon. The inside boundary loads are zero by tightness, while every outside boundary demand is unchanged. Thus the original hybrid feasible polytope is exactly the Cartesian product of those two piece polytopes, and its entropy is their sum: every GREEN E factor stays whole and every cut factor was additive C+C. The closure remains loopless, both marked vertices are adjacent, and every proper cut strictly reduces the number of triangles on each side. Iteration terminates. On each resulting piece there is no proper tight set, so section4 gives positivity on every ordinary allowed arc; the marked zero-dimensional triangle rows retain their fixed values.

5. Consequence for the boundary-regularity argument

At a full-kernel maximum, tag terminal seams CROSS and keep the remaining strict non-diagonal half ports GREEN. The full continuous-h theorem permits this limiting description without claiming eventual feasibility of a newly fixed finite label pattern. The cross variables are their actual two triangle allocations. A small mixture with the relative-interior witness of section4 remains cross-admissible; each green point stays in its own strict half chamber because its original interval length and upper-endpoint margin are positive.

The proved corner expansions then give a nonnegative epsilon log(1/epsilon) coefficient at every factor. Any zero ordinary degree or cross-triangle corner that is NOT structurally forced by (5) is activated and yields a strict positive gain. Thus such a maximum has no free zeros beyond those tight digon cuts. This assertion keeps diagonal ports excluded; their negative epsilon log(1/epsilon) term is not an allowed remainder.

To turn this into a volume bound one must use the actual permitted cut pieces and their inherited marked flags when constructing finite positive multipliers and the global relative chain. The present proof is the complete finite support/witness classification, not an assertion that independent Green capacities factor across arbitrary digons.

Scope

The induced-face Euler argument includes disconnected induced graphs, digon faces and the proper-subset exception. The iterative cut uses variational C+C bookkeeping; it is not a finite-coupon replacement.

Geometry additionally target-read the final iterative paragraph and confirmed its Cartesian flow product, unchanged outside targets, formal matching parity and termination. This record is scoped to the finite hybrid theorem and its regularity perturbation.

This finite combinatorial proof has no separate numerical audit. It does not itself identify a group representation or a relative fundamental class, and it does not infer a phase-sensitive original filling asymptotic. The geometric piece assembly and whole-source comparison require their separate proofs.
