A forced Rogers connection for the original multiplication tensor

21 November 2026. Research note. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The original centered multiplication tensor satisfies three exact inhomogeneous quantum difference equations. A finite telescoping identity proves their forcing term, including the initialized normal which would be lost by evaluating the hypergeometric sum only at the root. No infinite Rogers sum is substituted.

On the full finite boundary coefficient space, a known cyclic dilogarithm product removes the homogeneous part of the connection. The remaining additive equations are compatible and have an explicit finite Fourier solution. One universal scalar fixes their constant. That scalar also has a direct finite factorial initializer.

These identities preserve the complete tensor and hence its original assembled source. They do not prove noncancellation after arbitrary short fillings.

3. The finite coefficient space and its homogeneous unit

The connection will now be solved on the full boundary coefficient space ℛN=ℂ[x,y,z]/(xN,yN,zN).(18) \mathscr R_N= \mathbb C[x,y,z]/(x^N,y^N,z^N). \qquad\text{(18)} Each element has a unique representative of degree below NN in each variable. Thus it records all N3N^3 entries of the original tensor. This quotient concerns the auxiliary generating powers, not a semisimple quotient of the physical Casimir module. In particular all original repeated-root directions are retained.

The norm defect of the homogeneous connection is explicit: ∏h=0N−1Txhax−∏h=0N−1Txhbx=ηxNDN(y)DN(z).(19) \prod_{h=0}^{N-1}T_x^ha_x-\prod_{h=0}^{N-1}T_x^hb_x =\eta x^ND_N(y)D_N(z). \qquad\text{(19)} For example the two products are (1−xNyN)(1−xNzN)(1-x^Ny^N)(1-x^Nz^N) and (1−ηxN)(1−ηxNyNzN)(1-\eta x^N)(1-\eta x^Ny^Nz^N). Consequently the cyclic product of bx/axb_x/a_x is one in (18), though the off-quotient norm defect is nonzero.

For any nilpotent monomial uu with uN=0u^N=0, define 𝖾N(u)=exp⁡(∑n=1N−1unn(1−Qn)).(20) \mathsf e_N(u)= \exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \frac{u^n}{n(1-Q^n)}\right). \qquad\text{(20)} The exponential is a finite operation in the nilpotent ideal. Its constant is one and 𝖾N(Qu)/𝖾N(u)=1−u\mathsf e_N(Qu)/\mathsf e_N(u)=1-u, by the finite logarithm of 1−u1-u. Define the known unit H=𝖾N(−qx)𝖾N(−qy)𝖾N(−qz)𝖾N(−qxyz)𝖾N(Qxy)𝖾N(Qxz)𝖾N(Qyz).(21) \boxed{\qquad H= \frac{\mathsf e_N(-qx)\mathsf e_N(-qy) \mathsf e_N(-qz)\mathsf e_N(-qxyz)} {\mathsf e_N(Qxy)\mathsf e_N(Qxz)\mathsf e_N(Qyz)} . \qquad} \qquad\text{(21)} Every numerator and denominator has constant one. Using (20) gives exactly aiTiH=biH(i=x,y,z).(22) a_iT_iH=b_iH\quad(i=x,y,z). \qquad\text{(22)} This is the finite cyclic-dilogarithm version of the homogeneous Rogers product, derived here by a finite logarithm.

5. One scalar anchor and its finite initializer

Theorem 2. Let aN=𝒞N(0,0,0)=N−3/2ϕ(R3)a_N=\mathcal C_N(0,0,0) =N^{-3/2}\phi(R^3). In ℛN\mathscr R_N, 𝒞N=aNH+βNHA.(30) \boxed{\qquad \mathcal C_N=a_NH+\beta_NHA . \qquad} \qquad\text{(30)} More generally, the complete set of solutions of the three forced equations (5) in this finite coefficient space is aH+βNHAaH+\beta_NHA, with one arbitrary constant a∈ℂa\in\mathbb C.

In fact DN=1D_N=1 in this quotient, so 𝒞̂N=𝒞N\widehat{\mathcal C}_N=\mathcal C_N. Equation (24), the defining equations of AA, and the common-invariant observation after (28) prove the assertion. The physical constant is exactly aNa_N, since H(0)=1H(0)=1 and A(0)=0A(0)=0. No knot-dependent scalar is used to define the tensor.

The universal anchor itself has the following finite initializer. The forcing cannot be absorbed into a scalar normalization of HH. For example (5) gives the universal first-coefficient identity (Q−1)C100−qC000=βN≠0.(36) (Q-1)C_{100}-qC_{000}=\beta_N\ne0 . \qquad\text{(36)} A pure homogeneous product would give zero on its right. This is an exact source obstruction, not an estimate based on a small selection of knots or levels.

6. Exact use in the original Conway assembly

Equation (30) gives every original tensor entry by taking the unique representative of degree below NN in each variable. Alternatively, choose any nonnegative-power Taylor sections for its right side agreeing with that representative modulo (xN,yN,zN)(x^N,y^N,z^N). Use coefficient-conjugate sections for the conjugate vertices.

The complete generating-section theorem proves the required whole-source rule: first reverse the finite indices along a Conway tree so every internal contraction is anti-diagonal. With the original leaves, turns and both actual closing arrays retained, the scalar is the coefficient [∏eteN−1]∏vVv.(37) [\,\prod_e t_e^{N-1}\,]\prod_v V_v . \qquad\text{(37)} Every vertex section has nonnegative powers on every incident edge. Replacing a vertex by (30) changes it by a sum of terms containing an incident teNt_e^N. Such a term cannot contribute to (37). Telescoping the replacements over the finitely many vertices therefore preserves the original scalar exactly.

This order is essential. Reverse the finite tensors before choosing their Taylor extensions. A reversed untruncated series or a ratio of edge variables may introduce negative powers and does not satisfy the rule. No physical meridian power or Casimir normal is declared invisible. The final coefficient functional has not been replaced by a norm, and no physical source direction is discarded.

The result reduces the universal tensor to a finite cyclic product, a completely specified additive potential, and the universal initializer (32). It does not estimate the coherent coefficient (37) at a geometric contour. In particular the small prefactor βN\beta_N, the Fourier bound (29), or the one-constant description does not supply a lower bound after arbitrary short fillings. Contributions from the homogeneous and forced terms may still cancel in the assembled source.
