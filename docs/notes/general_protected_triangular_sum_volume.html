Full source certificates are closed under protected triangular sums

This note proves a constructive closure theorem for full source certificates. The inputs are actual marked simple-sphere diagrams whose phase-uniform local envelopes admit an unabridged finite shared-radius certificate. The output is their literal protected triangular sum. Its full source has a strict complementary gap at both level parities, and its independently sufficiently long fixed signed fillings satisfy the volume limit for the actual new knot.

The result is stronger than invariance of one cap response. It does not claim that arbitrary triangular blocks preserve a source bound. In particular the certified R54 block does not satisfy the input certificate hypothesis. If the inputs are weak-balanced, the output is itself weak-balanced; this part is a constructive closure corollary within that existing theorem, not a new analytic condition.

Closure theorem. Any fixed finite sequence of the protected triangular sums defined below preserves an unabridged certificate. For every half-twist pattern passing the assembled diagram’s original knot test, all independently sufficiently long fixed signed primitive fillings then satisfy the full reduced-invariant volume limit (22). The required threshold may depend on the entire fixed assembled diagram and pattern. Section 7 gives arbitrary finite sizes for which the resulting marked complete packing fails weak balance, using an existing seventeen-region finite-cover certificate.

6. Arbitrarily many blocks and weak balance as an input

Take any finite number k≥2k\ge2 of certified marked inputs. Repeatedly perform the operation of section 2 at the retained common marked edge uvuv, identifying the chosen ordinary third vertex of the next block with the ordinary third vertex on the selected incident triangle of the current sphere. The previous ordinary coordinates are retained; only the two chosen third vertices are identified. At each step use fresh interior vertices.

After every step the sphere is simple and uvuv is still nonmatching, so it has two incident triangles available for the next step. Thus the construction has no upper bound on kk. 8. Scope and related constructions

Classical volume additivity for belted sums is part of the background to constructions of augmented links. For the geometric, combinatorial, and diagrammatic classification of such decompositions, see Morgan, Ransom, Spyropoulos, Trapp, and Ziegler, Belted sum decompositions of fully augmented links. Van der Veen’s Proof of the volume conjecture for Whitehead chains establishes the volume conjecture for that specific infinite family. No identification of the present ordered protected operation with those marked constructions is used here. In particular classical volume additivity itself is not claimed as a new phenomenon. The statement proved here is closure of the original phase-uniform source certificates, with every shared target retained, and its independent-long-filling consequence for the recorded diagrams.

An arbitrary block need not satisfy (5): the fifty-four-region example has a genuine fixed-sector envelope capacity exceeding its complete action by more than 1/51/5. The present proof does not assert an exact scalar factorization of finite spectral kernels. It proves an inequality for every genuine sector with its full real spectrum and all face phases, and therefore retains the normal data required by the finite filling functional.

The certificate hypothesis cannot be weakened here to an arbitrary closed-source cancellation estimate, or to a cap estimate with other spectra fixed. Such estimates need not provide witnesses for (16). Likewise globally omitted strings require an independent support transfer argument. The theorem changes diagrams by an explicit operation; it is not a claim that the original input knot type is preserved.
