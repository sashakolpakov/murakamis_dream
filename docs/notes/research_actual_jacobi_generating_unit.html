The actual Jacobi generating polynomial on a uniform Casimir disk

18 November 2026. This is a fixed-root analytic estimate for the literal continuant columns of [H]. It proves a zero-free generating disk, an explicit phase and nonzero amplitude, and the actual Casimir-derivative partner on that disk. The finite last-row forcing is retained throughout. There is no interpolation of the integer occupation or of the channel.

The parameter (T+1)/N(T+1)/N stays in a compact subset of (0,1)(0,1). In particular this proof does not apply to T=N−1T=N-1. It is not a meridian normal theorem, an estimate for the inverse Clebsch measure, or a claim about a closed-source coefficient.

6. Genuine Casimir partners and precise scope

The proof was performed on larger complex ρ,λ\rho,\lambda domains. Cauchy estimates applied to the holomorphic relative error in (6) therefore prove its fixed derivative bounds. In particular ∂λLTLT=N∂λϕ+∂λlog⁡𝒜+O(N−1),(28) \frac{\partial_\lambda L_T}{L_T} =N\partial_\lambda\phi+ \partial_\lambda\log{\mathcal{ A}}+O(N^{-1}), \qquad\text{(28)} and the equivalent normalized transport statement is e−Nϕ(∂λLT−NϕλLT)=𝒜λ+O(N−1).(29) e^{-N\phi} (\partial_\lambda L_T-N\phi_\lambda L_T) ={\mathcal{ A}}_\lambda+O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(29)} Here σN,ρ\sigma_N,\rho are held fixed. Higher fixed Casimir derivatives follow by the same argument. At any double node of [H], this estimates the actual partner ∑x−afa′(λ)ρa\sum x^{-a}f_a'(\lambda)\rho^a, including the inhomogeneous row (10). It does not assert that the partner is nonzero at every point; for example it vanishes at ρ=0\rho=0.

There is no branch coalescence in this selected germ at a special finite value of λ\lambda, including λ=−2\lambda=-2. The relevant nondegeneracy is c−1≠0c-1\ne0, uniform only because σN\sigma_N stays away from 0,10,1. At T=dT=d, c=1c=1, the uniform lower bound for the degree-one multiplier fails. That case is outside the theorem. These are fixed-root Casimir derivatives, not qq-derivatives, meridian derivatives, or derivatives of an interpolated integer channel.

The theorem estimates the actual magnetic generating polynomial. Applying it to a binomial dual contour still requires that contour’s normalization and saddle estimate. It supplies neither a bound for the full inverse Hermite frame nor a geometric closing-cycle coefficient.
