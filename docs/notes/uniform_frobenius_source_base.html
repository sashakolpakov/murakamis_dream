A uniform Frobenius base for the source and its marked period

Research note, 26 October 2026. This places the actual scalar source coefficients at all levels on one difference base, retaining the Frobenius coordinate and root phases. A standard multiplicative-to- additive group model resolves the quantum–meridian point. The normalized two-channel source algebra extends there. A second marked chart evaluates the original central-pole normalization as a whole.

The resulting Frobenius period is explicit and regular in h^2. This is the universal root-center period, not the knot’s selected Bloch period. The global source comparison and volume conjecture remain unproved.

1. Keep the Frobenius coordinate independent before restricting a root

Use the fixed literature parameter q_N=exp(pi*i/N) and meridian M. Introduce independent coordinates q,M,w and the finite phase cover

zeta^4=1, U=w^2,
sigma(q)=q, sigma(M)=qM, sigma(w)=-w,
sigma(zeta)=zeta.                              (1.1)

The actual root graph is

Gamma_N: q=q_N, w=M^N, zeta=i^(1-N).           (1.2)

It is sigma-stable because q_N^N=-1. The phases are exactly

zeta_N=q_N^(N(1-N)/2),
epsilon_N=(-1)^N=-zeta_N^2,
eta_N=i^(N+3)=zeta_N^3.

No phase is reset independently at a level. The coefficient field on each phase component is C(q,M,w), with constant field

C(q,U).                                      (1.3)

For a proof, sigma^2 fixes w and scales M by q^2. A rational invariant in M cannot have a finite nonzero pole, since its infinite q^2 orbit would also be poles. It is therefore Laurent in M, and invariance forces all nonzero M exponents to vanish. The remaining sigma invariance is w->-w, giving C(q,w^2).

Adjoining the shift T gives the exact relations

TM=qMT, Tw=-wT.

Under (1.2), the second is the ordinary root identity T MN=q_NN M^N T. Thus this base extends the literature coefficient algebra and recovers it at each specified root. It does not impose monodromy one on a quantum-A module. Here w is a half-Frobenius meridian coordinate; it is not the geometric longitude or the norm-torsor spectral variable.

The original normalization functions are now fixed rational functions:

rho=q-q^(-1), f=M-M^(-1),
d=zeta^3 f/(w-w^(-1)),
C_cut=-zeta U/[(U-1)(U+1)],
epsilon=-zeta^2,
delta=rho(M^2-M^(-2)), nu=epsilon rho^2,
A=epsilon rho(M+M^(-1))
                *(qM^2-q^(-1)M^(-2))/(w+w^(-1)). (1.4)

They restrict to the actual full ADO dimension, cut line and adjacent-source overlap. Direct substitution gives

sigma(d)/d=-f(qM)/f(M), sigma(C_cut)=C_cut.

The central divisor U=1 and the half-root sign of w are retained.

2. Root graphs give a genuine algebraic rigidity test

On each fixed phase component, the union of Gamma_N for infinitely many N in its residue class modulo four is Zariski dense.

Indeed clear denominators of a proposed identity and write

P(q,M,w)=sum_(a,b) f_(a,b)(q)M^a w^b.

Let D_M be the span of its finitely many M exponents. If N>D_M, the exponents a+Nb for distinct pairs (a,b) are distinct. Restriction to Gamma_N is therefore zero only if every f_(a,b)(q_N) is zero. Infinitely many distinct q_N force these fixed polynomials to vanish. This proves the density assertion, including complex coefficients and every phase component.

There is a finite-degree version over a number field K. After clearing denominators let D_q be the q degree and D_M the M exponent span. If

N>D_M, phi(2N)>[K:Q] D_q,                    (2.1)

then an exact whole-graph identity at that one level proves the generic identity on that phase component. The algebraic degree of q_N over K is at least phi(2N)/[K:Q], so each polynomial f_(a,b) has degree too small to vanish at q_N unless it is zero. For K=Q(i), the sufficient bound is phi(2N)>2D_q. The graph must lie in the domain of the cleared comparison.

The degree hypotheses are essential: w-M^N vanishes on a single graph, and a cyclotomic polynomial vanishes at its q_N. Neither satisfies the relevant strict bound. Checking only M=1,w=1, or a few unspecified meridian samples, is not a whole-graph certificate. The actual knot period is not assumed to have this fixed rational presentation or these degree bounds. The test concerns source coefficients and proposed algebraic comparison maps once their bounds have been established.

3. The deformation group is an existing definition

The standard group scheme

G^(lambda)=Spec R[t,1/(1+lambda t)]

is recalled in Mezard–Romagny–Tossici, Section 1.2. We use this definition with lambda=rho and M=1+rho*t. Its group law and inverse follow directly by multiplying M:

t*tprime=t+tprime+rho*t*tprime,
t^(-1)=-t/(1+rho*t).                          (3.1)

For rho invertible it is G_m, while its rho=0 fiber is G_a. This is existing group-scheme theory. Only the abstract and this definition were consulted; no isogeny, extension, or classification proof from that source is assumed here.

Symmetric source coordinates near M=1 are

s=(M-M^(-1))/rho, c=M+M^(-1),
c^2-rho^2 s^2=4, M=(c+rho*s)/2.              (3.2)

This is the selected c=2 chart of the same deformation, since s=t(2+rhot)/(1+rhot). Its group law is

(s,c)*(sprime,cprime)
  =((s cprime+c sprime)/2,
                   (c cprime+rho^2 s sprime)/2). (3.3)

Multiplication preserves the displayed norm; associativity follows either by expansion or by the M multiplication law. On the exceptional identity fiber rho=0,c=2 it becomes addition of s. This resolves the ideal (rho,M-1) in the meridian chart.

The original meridian step is multiplication by (1,q+q^(-1)):

sigma(s)=((q+q^(-1))s+c)/2,
sigma(c)=(rho^2 s+(q+q^(-1))c)/2.             (3.4)

It preserves c2-rho2s^2 and has determinant one. Meridian inversion is (s,c,w)->(-s,c,w^(-1)); direct multiplication gives the relation inversionsigmainversion=sigma^(-1). At the exceptional identity fiber the action is exactly

sigma(s)=s+1, sigma(w)=-w.                   (3.5)

Charge is the ordinary multiplication map of this group. For the cubic source it is (M,w)->(M3,w3), equivalently s->s(c^2-1), c->c^3-3c. It intertwines the original meridian step with the third source step, retaining the charge from the actual projective source construction.

4. The normalized adjacent source extends over the exceptional fiber

Let p=q(-1)M2-qM^(-2), r=sigma(p), and k=q+q^(-1). On (3.2),

p/rho=(k s c-(c^2-2))/2=:p_hat,
r/rho=(k s c+(c^2-2))/2=:r_hat.               (4.1)

Their difference is c^2-2, a unit near c=2. Thus the old point ideal (p,r) pulls back to the principal ideal (rho); its proper transform has no residual point. This resolves the nonfree rank-one factor (u,h) in the earlier cyclic source lattice. The exceptional factor rho remains a specified line and divisor; principalizing the ideal does not discard that source datum.

Normalize the source numerators by their recorded factor nu:

d_hat=delta/nu=epsilon*s*c,
a_hat=A/nu=c*r_hat/(w+w^(-1)).                (4.2)

Here epsilon^2=1 on the phase cover. These are regular on the chosen chart with w+w^(-1) invertible. The two coefficient matrices from the actual source become

t_1_hat=[d_hat a_hat;0 0],
t_2_hat=[0 0;a_hat d_hat].

On the exceptional identity fiber,

d_hat=2 epsilon*s,
a_hat=2(2s+1)/(w+w^(-1)).                    (4.3)

At s=0,w=1 their matrix-algebra generators have nonzero units: a_hat=1 and a_hat2-d_hat2=1. Thus their multiplicity algebra and local scalar comparison torsor extend over the joint quantum–meridian degeneration. This resolves the shrinking fixed-level unit in the preceding normal-form note. It is the two-channel coefficient algebra that extends; the N-dimensional color factor and full knot section have not been algebraized. The original numerator matrices are exactly nu*t_i_hat on every root graph. The original projectors are t_i_hat/d_hat, so this normalization retains their full germs and defines no new state.

The full affine factor has the uniform expression

(1+w^(-2))*a_hat/d_hat
               =epsilon*w^(-1)*r_hat/s.      (4.4)

Its exceptional value is epsilon*w^(-1)(2s+1)/s. The pole at s=0 and the central cap at w^2=1 stay in the relative model; normalizing the numerators has not discarded either of them. The earlier obstruction to a common lattice for both unscaled projectors is consequently still present.

5. One marked Frobenius period realizes every prescribed root

Put h=Log q and retain the original Tate mark tau=pi*i, so N h_N=tau. On the identity chart let

u(h,s)=arsinh(s sinh h), M=exp(u),
log w_per=(tau/h)arsinh(s sinh h).             (5.1)

This is the selected solution of M-M^(-1)=rho*s with M=1 at s=0. The quotient in (5.1) is regular at h=0, since u is odd in h. More precisely

log w_per=tau*s+tau*(s-s^3)h^2/6+O(h^4).

It is analytic in h^2 locally for bounded s. At h=h_N it is exactly N Log M, so w_per=M^N as a whole germ. Translation by q gives u->u+h, hence w_per->exp(tau)w_per=-w_per. Thus this one period section respects the original meridian action and recovers (1.2), rather than choosing unrelated sections at different levels.

At the exceptional fiber it is the horizontal period

dlog w_per=tau ds, w_per(0)=1,
w_per=exp(tau*s).                             (5.2)

This is an algebraic line with connection and a marked horizontal period, not a rational function of s. A regular algebraic group homomorphism G_a->G_m is constant: its defining function is an invertible polynomial, hence constant, and its identity value is one. Thus the nonzero Tate marking cannot be replaced by an algebraic character on the exceptional additive fiber. Retaining the period comparison is necessary even though its coefficient group and connection are algebraic.

All normalized scalar source formulas in (4.1)–(4.3), and the source step (3.4), are even in h after this period section is substituted on a fixed phase component. Indeed c=2sqrt(1+s^2 sinh^2 h), k=2cosh h, and w_per are all even in h. This parity does not assert parity or regularity of the knot’s geometric exponential exp(S/h).

6. The pole normalization becomes a marked regular evaluation

The point s=0,w=1 alone does not evaluate d/rho: its expression is zeta3*s/(w-w(-1)). Retain the second chart

w=1+s*ell, ell invertible.                    (6.1)

Then the whole normalized dimension and cap are regular:

d/rho=zeta^3*w/[ell(w+1)],
s*C_cut=-zeta*w^2/[ell(w+1)(w^2+1)].          (6.2)

Their complete values are zeta^3/(2ell) and -zeta/(4ell). The actual graph fixes the slope, not only the point:

ell_N=(dw/ds)|_(s=0)=N rho_N/2.              (6.3)

Combining (6.2)–(6.3) recovers exactly

d_N(1)=zeta_N^3/N.

On the uniform period section this slope is

ell(h)=tau*sinh h/h,
d(h,0)=zeta^3*h/tau.                         (6.4)

Thus the central normalization is a regular evaluation on a marked chart, including its transverse root-graph direction. No separate zero or pole was evaluated. At the exceptional fiber ell=tau; the period marking fixes that value.

The meridian translates this marked chart to the other cap charts. Its orbit is not a single invariant point; those arrows remain in the lattice/source groupoid. This construction does not make all adjacent projector arrows integral on one lattice.

7. Scope and verification

This supplies a uniform difference base for the actual source coefficients, its standard group deformation, a regular normalized two-channel algebra, and one marked Frobenius period with exact root restrictions. It also gives an algebraic rigidity test for bounded comparisons. These replace levelwise coefficient choices by specified functions and period data.

The horizontal section above is the universal Frobenius period. It is not the knot’s marked Bloch regulator. An actual global source/section comparison with the positive triangulation still has to be constructed, with all matrix and lattice transitions. The root-graph density theorem does not apply to an unknown comparison without its fixed presentation and degree bounds. The source coefficient algebra does not determine the scalar coefficient of the geometric exponential in the selected period.

The subsequent whole-period reconstruction extends this root-only base by an independent v=q^N and t=1/N. Its action is sigma(w)=vw, with commuting derivatives D_q=q partial_q+t^(-1)v partial_v and D_M=M partial_M+t^(-1)w partial_w. The slice v=-1 recovers the base here but is not preserved by D_q. Thus quantum-normal jets require this extension before specialization. Bounded mixed jets then reconstruct any proposed fixed algebraic comparison, including one on a marked etale shape cover. This does not algebraize the full knot state or construct the missing map.

The fixed central normalization theorem controls a comparison factor once its fixed finite algebraic presentation is proved. It does not supply that global comparison. The volume conjecture remains unproved.

The subsequent common Frobenius/regulator period space now puts this Frobenius mark and the geometric regulator action over one explicit logarithmic Heisenberg connection. Its additional Tate period J recovers all four root phases; unlike a frozen phase sheet it carries the q-direction connection. The full complete cap is -i J h/(pi*i), and the geometric exponential retains its volume term. Adjoining the rational Frobenius coordinate preserves an existing nonsplit source class, by a Laurent-coefficient retraction. Thus the common period space does not by itself supply the missing actual source comparison.

The audit verifies the group law, Weyl and meridian matrices, normalized source and cap identities, and period-section translation through h^6. Four phase sheets verify bounded-degree exponent separation and cyclotomic factor degrees. Fourteen root levels verify the original phases and marked complete normalization, with 28 generic scalar root-graph specializations. These are source-base checks, not quantum-volume experiments.
