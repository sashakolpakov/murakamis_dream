Unipotent transport of the actual q-Hahn normal near the fold

Written proof, 24 November 2026.

The actual q-Hahn source has an exact constant column at magnetic input zero. This supplies a value/difference form of its channel recurrence. After a real rescaling of the difference, the nonresonant transfers are close to the identity in operator norm. Their accumulated cost is quadratic in the scaled channel separation.

All columns and normal derivatives below retain the original generic highest-weight N−1N-1 source. The result is a relative upper bound for its paired normal, not a nonzero leading amplitude or a closed-source lower bound.

Apply Cauchy’s formula to the full finite columns (3). They are holomorphic throughout this disk: factorial indices are less than NN, and (15) keeps all TT divisors separated. The singular monic factors have already canceled in that finite source. The derivatives therefore cost only the additional factor 𝜚N−1=OK(N8)\varrho_N^{-1}=O_K(N^8). Finally 2N|Δj|=4Nsin⁡(πs/N)≥8s≥82N|\Delta_j|=4N\sin(\pi s/N)\ge8s\ge8. Together with the corresponding small-column derivative bound, this proves (6), with a larger compact-dependent AKA_K.

The previous source-normalized estimate [F] bounded each regular transfer by a fixed constant. The exact source anchor (11), the explicit root formula (19), and the weighted difference frame instead retain its near-unipotent form. This is why the scaled cost improves from linear to quadratic.

The closest flux rescaling in [V] concerns a different source: a positive anchored four-coherent scalar on its physical half-grid. It proves a terminal fold value and does not estimate this generic q-Hahn derivative. Here no positivity of the column or of a flux is assumed, and both generic partner derivatives remain in (4).

The theorem retains actual integer channels and both level parities. It does not provide a complex-channel interpolation, a nonzero normal amplitude, a bound for the complete contracted coupon, or a contributing original geometric cycle. In particular polynomial cost on the Gaussian channel scale does not prove that the needed short-filled geometric configuration lies there, or that its original cap projection is nonzero. No knot-volume lower bound or finite-exception theorem is claimed.
