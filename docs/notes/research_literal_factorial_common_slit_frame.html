A common slit chart for the actual factorial crossing source

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

Every finite collection of literal crossing factorial clocks can be put off the unit circle by an arbitrarily small complex change of a regular selected-flag frame. No factor clock in the required list is constant on the full framing orbit. This follows from an irreducible prefix-minor calculation, and does not require faithfulness of the representation. Integral wire-logarithm repair then puts all the normalized factorial lengths in one common slit domain.

For complete geometric holonomy, the change can be made arbitrarily close to the regular midpoint frame. An exact complex-binomial cut selects its cut position in the principal stationary scheme, while retaining the original unmarked scalar. Its exponential total-variation cost can be made arbitrarily small, and is recorded rather than omitted. This is a chart and source-presentation theorem. It does not deform the finite state cycle to this point or estimate a full coherent kernel.

1. Actual clocks and the statement

Keep a fixed finite braid whose closure is one knot, its actual signed crossings, pivotal closures, framing and factorial basis. Use the classical correspondence of M and the weighted principal source of S. At a positive crossing, with input clocks A,BA,B and transfer CC, the output clocks are BC,A/CBC,A/C. At a negative crossing they are B/C,ACB/C,AC. The complete color is M=1M=1, with classical x=−1x=-1.

The five leading factorial lengths at a positive crossing are (b+n,a;b,n,a−n)/N,(1) (b+n,\ a;\ b,\ n,\ a-n)/N, \qquad\text{(1)} and at a negative crossing they are (a+n,b;a,n,b−n)/N.(2) (a+n,\ b;\ a,\ n,\ b-n)/N. \qquad\text{(2)} The semicolon separates numerator from denominator factors. Their exponentials are respectively (BC,A;B,C,A/C),(AC,B;A,C,B/C).(3) (BC,A;\ B,C,A/C),\qquad (AC,B;\ A,C,B/C). \qquad\text{(3)} Thus each is a wire occupation clock or a transfer clock. These are the actual five-factor lists, not independent octahedral coordinates. For example M’s positive finite product is Fb+nFbFn(Qx2/A;Q)n. \frac{F_{b+n}}{F_bF_n}(Qx^2/A;Q)_n. At the physical color Qx2=1Qx^2=1, reversing its last product gives (Q−a;Q)n=(−1)nQ−an+n(n−1)/2FaFa−n,(4) (Q^{-a};Q)_n =(-1)^nQ^{-an+n(n-1)/2}\frac{F_a}{F_{a-n}}, \qquad\text{(4)} which proves (1) without deleting its monomial factor. The negative formula is the same reversal with a,ba,b interchanged. All Cartan, sign and pivotal factors remain. At nearby color, write α=−2log⁡M/h\alpha=-2\log M/h, h=log⁡Qh=\log Q; the reversed endpoints are a+α,a−n+αa+\alpha,a-n+\alpha, or b+α,b−n+αb+\alpha,b-n+\alpha. Their leading clocks are A/M2,A/(CM2)A/M^2,A/(CM^2), or B/M2,B/(CM2)B/M^2,B/(CM^2). Fixed Weyl offsets remain in the finite factors.

Let 𝒟=ℂ\((−∞,0]∪[1,∞)).(5) \mathcal D=\mathbb C\setminus \bigl((-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty)\bigr). \qquad\text{(5)} Fix any selected-flag representation with noncentral strand meridians, and any nonempty regular chart of its finite actual braid correspondence. The theorem is that, in every complex neighborhood in that chart, there is a frame and a compatible set of wire logarithms for which every length in (1)–(2) lies in 𝒟\mathcal D. Each length in fact has nonzero imaginary part. The lengths lie in compact subsets of 𝒟\mathcal D on a sufficiently small common neighborhood.

For a complete hyperbolic knot, G supplies the required regular midpoint chart. In that case the cut logarithm can be retained near πi\pi i, and the source cost in Section 5 can be made smaller than exp⁡(εN)\exp(\varepsilon N) for every prescribed ε>0\varepsilon>0. The chart theorem for noncentral representations below uses no faithfulness or cusp-lattice assumption; G is used only to start arbitrarily close to that particular geometric midpoint.

2. Every nonscalar prefix has an irreducible minor

Normalize the selected flag to the vector w=(1,0)tw=(1,0)^t. Write αt=(1,t),vt,s=(1−ts,s)t,F(t,s)=(vt,s,w−vt,s),s≠0.(6) \alpha_t=(1,t),\qquad v_{t,s}=(1-ts,s)^t,\qquad F(t,s)=(v_{t,s},w-v_{t,s}),\qquad s\ne0. \qquad\text{(6)} Then αt(vt,s)=1\alpha_t(v_{t,s})=1, and αt\alpha_t is the first row of F−1F^{-1}. These are the original framing coordinates F(r,s)F(r,s) with r=1−tsr=1-ts. In particular this is the full two-dimensional selected-flag frame chart, not a fixed-ss slice.

For a determinant-one matrix P=(abcd)P=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}, put wP(t,s)=αtPvt,s=a+ct+s[b+(d−a)t−ct2].(7) w_P(t,s)=\alpha_tPv_{t,s} =a+ct+s\bigl[b+(d-a)t-ct^2\bigr]. \qquad\text{(7)} The map P↦wPP\mapsto w_P is linear and injective: its coefficients recover a,c,b,d−aa,c,b,d-a.

If PP is nonscalar, wPw_P is an irreducible nonconstant polynomial in ℂ[t,s]\mathbb C[t,s]. Indeed set A(t)=a+ctA(t)=a+ct, B(t)=b+(d−a)t−ct2B(t)=b+(d-a)t-ct^2. If c≠0c\ne0, B(−a/c)=−1/c,(8) B(-a/c)=-1/c, \qquad\text{(8)} by ad−bc=1ad-bc=1. Thus A,BA,B are relatively prime. If c=0c=0, then a≠0a\ne0, so AA is a unit, and BB can vanish identically only when PP is scalar. In both cases the primitive polynomial A+sBA+sB has degree one in ss over ℂ(t)\mathbb C(t) and is irreducible there; clearing polynomial contents proves irreducibility over ℂ[t,s]\mathbb C[t,s]. For scalar P=±IP=\pm I, instead wP=±1w_P=\pm1. Finally, wP=λwQ⇒P=λQ,λ=±1(9) w_P=\lambda w_Q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P=\lambda Q,\quad\lambda=\pm1 \qquad\text{(9)} for determinant-one matrices. Hence distinct projective nonscalar matrices give nonassociate irreducible prefix polynomials.

These polynomial statements are made before restricting to s≠0s\ne0 or to the regular braid chart. An identity on any of those nonempty complex opens would be the same rational-function identity, so localizing the chart does not invalidate the argument.

3. No required occupation or transfer clock is constant

At any braid stage let H1,…,HbH_1,\ldots,H_b be M’s unframed strand matrices, and Pj=Hj⋯H1P_j=H_j\cdots H_1, P0=IP_0=I. They are determinant-one matrices; HjH_j is minus the corresponding actual meridian and is noncentral. M’s exact Gauss inverse gives Kj=wPjwPj−1,Aj=xwPj−1wPj.(10) K_j=\frac{w_{P_j}}{w_{P_{j-1}}},\qquad A_j=x\,\frac{w_{P_{j-1}}}{w_{P_j}}. \qquad\text{(10)} If AjA_j were constant, injectivity in (9) would give Pj=±Pj−1P_j=\pm P_{j-1}, and therefore Hj=±IH_j=\pm I, a contradiction. This includes input and output wire clocks at every stage.

Consider an adjacent crossing at positions j,j+1j,j+1, and abbreviate the four prefix matrices by P0,P1,P2,P1′P_0,P_1,P_2,P'_1. Here P0P_0 means the prefix immediately before these positions, not necessarily the identity. The prefix P2P_2 is preserved by M’s actual Hurwitz correspondence. The positive transfer formula is C=wP0wP2wP1wP1′,(11) C=\frac{w_{P_0}w_{P_2}}{w_{P_1}w_{P'_1}}, \qquad\text{(11)} and for the inverse crossing it is the reciprocal of (11). For example Kj′=Kj+1/CK'_j=K_{j+1}/C in the positive map and Kj′=Kj+1CK'_j=K_{j+1}C in the inverse map, which proves both formulas directly from (10).

Suppose (11) were a nonzero constant. If P1P_1 is nonscalar, its irreducible polynomial wP1w_{P_1} must divide wP0wP2w_{P_0}w_{P_2}. Equations (7)–(9) then force P1=±P0P_1=\pm P_0 or P1=±P2P_1=\pm P_2. Both are impossible: the intervening strand matrix is a noncentral meridian. If P1P_1 is scalar, both P0P_0 and P2P_2 are nonscalar for the same reason. The left side of the cleared identity has two irreducible factors, counted with multiplicity, whereas the right side has at most one. This is again impossible. Thus every transfer clock is nonconstant.

It follows that every function in (3) is nonconstant on the full selected-flag frame orbit. Multiplying a function by a fixed nonzero color constant or taking its inverse preserves this conclusion. Consequently there are no identically unit-modulus functions in the required factor-clock list. This assertion is about these actual clocks; it does not assert multiplicative independence of them or of arbitrary monomials in wire clocks.

On a sufficiently small connected complex neighborhood in the regular chart all these finitely many clocks are holomorphic and nonzero. For a nonconstant such function the set |C|=1|C|=1 has empty interior: otherwise restriction to complex lines and the one-variable maximum principle would make it constant on an open set, hence everywhere. It is closed in the neighborhood. The complement of the finite union of these sets is therefore open and dense. Choose a frame there. This proves simultaneous off-unit modulus, arbitrarily close to any initially supplied regular frame.

4. Exact wire-logarithm compatibility

There is no assumption that independently chosen logarithms already obey additive state conservation. Here is an integral repair. View the oriented knot diagram as the directed crossing graph: edges are wire arcs and each crossing has two incoming and two outgoing arcs. Let LL be its integer incidence matrix, with incoming coefficient +1+1 and outgoing coefficient −1-1. Its multiplicative conservation law is the actual one AaAb=AcAdA_aA_b=A_cA_d.

Choose any logarithms ζe\zeta_e of the wire clocks. Then Lζ=2πik,k∈ℤV,∑vkv=0.(12) L\zeta=2\pi i\,k,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z^V, \qquad \sum_v k_v=0. \qquad\text{(12)} The graph is connected because the closure is one knot. Its incidence matrix has integer image exactly the vectors of total sum zero. For a direct proof, choose a spanning tree and remove its leaves successively. At a leaf prescribe the unique incident tree-edge integer to cancel the current integer at that leaf, then transfer the remaining integer to its neighbor. The last vertex has integer zero by the total-sum condition. This constructs m∈ℤEm\in\mathbb Z^E with Lm=−kLm=-k. Hence ζ̃=ζ+2πim,Lζ̃=0(13) \widetilde\zeta=\zeta+2\pi i\,m,\qquad L\widetilde\zeta=0 \qquad\text{(13)} without changing any multiplicative clock.

The repair can preserve the chosen cut logarithm. The oriented one-component knot traverses the crossing graph as an Euler circuit, so the cut edge is not an undirected bridge. Choose the spanning tree without that edge. The same leaf elimination then has mcut=0m_{\mathrm{ cut}}=0. Loops of the graph cause no difficulty: their incidence columns are zero, and they are not used in the tree.

Define a positive transfer logarithm by ν=ζ̃c−ζ̃b\nu=\widetilde\zeta_c-\widetilde\zeta_b, so conservation gives also ν=ζ̃a−ζ̃d\nu=\widetilde\zeta_a-\widetilde\zeta_d. For a negative crossing use ν=ζ̃b−ζ̃c=ζ̃d−ζ̃a\nu=\widetilde\zeta_b-\widetilde\zeta_c =\widetilde\zeta_d-\widetilde\zeta_a. These are exactly the additive relations behind (1)–(2), and their exponentials are the prescribed transfers. Every normalized factor length is now its appropriate logarithm divided by 2πi2\pi i. For every such clock ZZ, ℑ(log⁡Z2πi)=−log⁡|Z|2π≠0.(14) \Im\left(\frac{\log Z}{2\pi i}\right) =-\frac{\log|Z|}{2\pi}\ne0. \qquad\text{(14)} This is independent of the integer logarithm lift. All required lengths therefore belong to 𝒟\mathcal D simultaneously, with the exact additive conservation and transfer relations retained. Finite integer shifts of the lifts need not be individually small.

At the chosen point there are finitely many lengths. Shrinking the common neighborhood keeps each in a compact subset of 𝒟\mathcal D. All fixed O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) Weyl offsets remain in slightly larger compact subsets for sufficiently large NN. Nearby color endpoints from Section 1 are included by continuity. This is local uniformity for a fixed source/frame, not a denominator bound uniform over a degenerating family of diagrams.

6. Scope and complete sources

The theorem supplies a common scalar factorial slit chart and an exact source presentation having its geometric point as a regular principal critical point. It does not prove the scalar factorial amplitude theorem itself, a uniform full coherent-kernel phase, an integration contour reaching this point, an rr-centralizer period, or a nonzero geometric coefficient. The construction also does not replace actual support inequalities by analytic clocks.
