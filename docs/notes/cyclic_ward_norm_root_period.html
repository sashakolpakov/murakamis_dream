The cyclic Ward norm-root transfer carries the peripheral period

This gives an explicit group transfer, first-normal affine character, and marked relative logarithmic period on the literal cyclic Ward completion. It goes beyond equality of determinant characters. The transfer is a quotient of a unit-group dilatation, not an isomorphism between the NN-dimensional unit group and the one-dimensional geometric centralizer.

It constructs the full peripheral period $\Log(M^2)/(M-M^{-1})$. It does not construct the internal knot Gaussian pushforward, identify a selected Bloch period, or prove volume growth.

1. The actual finite algebra and its retained normal

Fix N≥2N\ge2, QQ primitive of order NN, and a characteristic-zero coefficient field kk containing QQ. Work near M=1M=1, with R=k[[M−1]],κ=M−M−1,κN=MN−M−N,uN=κN/κ,εN=(−1)N+1. R=k[[M-1]],\quad \kappa=M-M^{-1},\quad \kappa_N=M^N-M^{-N},\quad u_N=\kappa_N/\kappa,\quad \varepsilon_N=(-1)^{N+1}. The quotient uNu_N is a Laurent polynomial and a unit in RR, with uN(1)=Nu_N(1)=N. The actual cyclic Ward completion gives A=R[e]/(eN−εNκN),σ(e)=Qe. A=R[e]/(e^N-\varepsilon_N\kappa_N),\qquad \sigma(e)=Qe. \tag{1.1} The specified matrix realizes multiplication by ee, with its single missing-edge completion retained. Write 𝖭=Norm⁡A/R\mathsf N=\operatorname{Norm}_{A/R} and 𝖳𝗋=Tr⁡A/R\mathsf{Tr}=\operatorname{Tr}_{A/R}. The exact norm is 𝖭(1+te)=1+κNtN. \mathsf N(1+te)=1+\kappa_Nt^N. \tag{1.2} These are the actual algebra and sign of the cyclic Ward norm calculation.

The ideal (e,κ)(e,\kappa) is topologically nilpotent, so logarithms of units congruent to 11 modulo this ideal are well defined in the completed order. No inverse of κ\kappa is taken in AA.

2. A divided-norm unit group and a genuine centralizer transfer

Consider the unit dilatation with coordinates u=(1+κa0)+a1e+⋯+aN−1eN−1. u=(1+\kappa a_0)+a_1e+\cdots+a_{N-1}e^{N-1}. \tag{2.1} Coefficients may lie in a complete RR-algebra. Such uu are units, because their reduction is a principal nilpotent unit. They are closed under multiplication and inversion. Moreover 𝖭(u)≡1(mod⁡κ)\mathsf N(u)\equiv1\pmod\kappa.

Retain the norm-root cover ν(u)N=𝖭(u), \nu(u)^N=\mathsf N(u), and mark its identity sheet by ν(u)≡1(mod⁡κ)\nu(u)\equiv1\pmod\kappa. Since NN is invertible, Hensel recursion gives a unique such root in the completed identity chart. The other μN\mu_N sheets remain separate on the generic cover.

Define B(u)=ν(u)−1κ. \boxed{B(u)=\frac{\nu(u)-1}{\kappa}.} \tag{2.2} This is regular on the unit dilatation. Its fully algebraic divided equation is 𝖭(u)−1κ=∑j=1N(Nj)κj−1B(u)j. \frac{\mathsf N(u)-1}{\kappa} =\sum_{j=1}^{N}\binom Nj\kappa^{j-1}B(u)^j. \tag{2.3} At κ=0\kappa=0 the derivative in BB is NN, so (2.3) also characterizes the completed identity-sheet map without formally dividing a zero equation by zero.

Let the regular meridian centralizer be JM={CM(b):1+κb≠0},CM(b)=(1+κbb01),b*b′=b+b′+κbb′. J_M=\{C_M(b):1+\kappa b\ne0\},\quad C_M(b)=\begin{pmatrix}1+\kappa b&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \quad b*b'=b+b'+\kappa bb'. Then 𝒯N:u↦CM(B(u)) \boxed{\mathcal T_N:u\longmapsto C_M(B(u))} \tag{2.4} is an actual group homomorphism. Indeed uniqueness of the marked root gives ν(uv)=ν(u)ν(v)\nu(uv)=\nu(u)\nu(v), and (2.2) gives the displayed centralizer law. It has the scalar section CM(b)↦(1+κb)1A. C_M(b)\longmapsto (1+\kappa b)\,1_A. Its kernel is the marked norm-one unit group. Its generic dimension is N−1N-1; no group isomorphism with JMJ_M is claimed.

The source of (2.4) must retain the first normal coordinate a0a_0. It is not merely the principal units of k[e]/eNk[e]/e^N.

3. The explicit first-normal affine class

For the special fiber of the dilatation, multiplication has a0″=a0+a0′+εNN∑i+j=Naiaj′, a_0''=a_0+a_0' +\varepsilon_N N\sum_{i+j=N}a_i a_j', \tag{3.1} while the nonconstant coefficients multiply by the ordinary truncated rule modulo eNe^N. Formula (3.1) is the constant wrap term eN=εNκuNe^N=\varepsilon_N\kappa u_N, divided by κ\kappa. It is a specified central affine cocycle.

Let p(z)=1+∑j=1N−1ajzj. p(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}a_jz^j. The transfer (2.2) on this special fiber is the explicit additive character B0(u)=a0+εNN[zN]log⁡p(z). \boxed{B_0(u)=a_0+\varepsilon_N N[z^N]\log p(z).} \tag{3.2} The coefficient is a finite polynomial: truncate the ordinary logarithm at degree NN.

To prove (3.2), use log⁡𝖭(u)=𝖳𝗋log⁡u,𝖳𝗋(ej)=0(N∤j),𝖳𝗋(eN)=NεNκN. \log\mathsf N(u)=\mathsf{Tr}\log u,\quad \mathsf{Tr}(e^j)=0\ (N\nmid j),\quad \mathsf{Tr}(e^N)=N\varepsilon_N\kappa_N. To first order in κ\kappa, only the central term κa0\kappa a_0 and the degree-NN logarithm coefficient can contribute. Thus log⁡ν(u)=κa0+εNκN[zN]log⁡p(z)+O(κ2), \log\nu(u)=\kappa a_0+ \varepsilon_N\kappa_N[z^N]\log p(z)+O(\kappa^2), which proves the formula since uN(1)=Nu_N(1)=N.

For the literal missing-edge path α(t)=1+te \alpha(t)=1+te it gives B0(α(t))=tNB_0(\alpha(t))=t^N. This follows also from (1.2). The normal term is indispensable: all the units 1+κc1+\kappa c reduce to 11 in k[e]/eNk[e]/e^N, but their transfer is the arbitrary value cc. Consequently no map on the bare nilpotent special fiber can recover (2.4). The first-normal extension removes this precise ambiguity.

4. The marked path maps to the actual meridian

For α(t)=1+te\alpha(t)=1+te, the transfer is B(t)=(1+κNtN)1/N−1κ, \boxed{B(t)= \frac{(1+\kappa_Nt^N)^{1/N}-1}{\kappa},} \tag{4.1} with the identity root retained. It satisfies B(0)=0,B(t=M)=M. B(0)=0,\qquad B(t=M)=M. \tag{4.2} Indeed 1+κNMN=M2N1+\kappa_NM^N=M^{2N}, whose marked root near M=1M=1 is M2M^2; then (M2−1)/κ=M(M^2-1)/\kappa=M. The endpoint CM(M)C_M(M) is the original marked meridian in projective normalization. Thus this is an explicit transport of the relative path, not just its determinant value.

The endpoint lift t=Mt=M uses the retained meridian marking. Other lifts t=ζMt=\zeta M, ζ∈μN\zeta\in\mu_N, have the same norm-quotient endpoint but need not have the same norm-one factorial gauge. They remain separate source lifts. The finite norm character alone does not select an internal knot cycle.

The μN\mu_N marking matters. A root ζM2\zeta M^2, ζ≠1\zeta\ne1, would give B=(ζM2−1)/κB=(\zeta M^2-1)/\kappa, with a pole at completion. Such a sheet is not the regular identity chart. It has not been identified with the identity sheet or silently divided out.

There is also an exact power-map diagram. The geometric adapter is pN:JM→JMN,pN(b)=(1+κb)N−1κN. p_N:J_M\longrightarrow J_{M^N},\qquad p_N(b)=\frac{(1+\kappa b)^N-1}{\kappa_N}. \tag{4.3} The whole uNu_N is a unit near completion. By construction, pN(B(u))=𝖭(u)−1κN,pN(B(t))=tN. \boxed{p_N(B(u))=\frac{\mathsf N(u)-1}{\kappa_N},\qquad p_N(B(t))=t^N.} \tag{4.4} The right side is regular before special evaluation. For (4.4) on a general unit, the norm-root cover is part of the input.

5. Exact invariant forms and the relative peripheral period

The invariant centralizer form at fixed MM is ωM=db1+κb. \omega_M=\frac{db}{1+\kappa b}. Pullback along (4.1) gives the exact identity 𝒯N*ωM=1Nκdtlog⁡𝖭(1+te)=uNtN−11+κNtNdt. \boxed{\mathcal T_N^*\omega_M =\frac1{N\kappa}\,d_t\log\mathsf N(1+te) =\frac{u_Nt^{N-1}}{1+\kappa_Nt^N}\,dt.} \tag{5.1} This is regular at κ=0\kappa=0, where it is NtN−1dtNt^{N-1}dt. On the special fiber the transfer is b=tNb=t^N, with its ramified μN\mu_N cover retained; one does not replace its differential by dtdt.

Use the marked relative path t=0t=0 to t=Mt=M, with logarithm zero at the identity. Formal integration in RR, or continuation on the same marked logarithmic chart, gives $$ \boxed{\int_0^M\mathcal T_N^*\omega_M =\frac{\Log(M^{2N})}{N\kappa} =\frac{\Log(M^2)}{\kappa}=L(M).} \tag{5.2} $$ Thus the cyclic Ward block carries the full required peripheral relative period, not merely its complete value or determinant.

For the raw target JMNJ_{M^N}, the invariant form pulls back as NtN−11+κNtNdt. \frac{N t^{N-1}}{1+\kappa_Nt^N}\,dt. Its marked period is L(MN)L(M^N). Comparing with (5.1) yields pN*ωMN=NuNωM,L(MN)=NuN(M)L(M). \boxed{p_N^*\omega_{M^N}=\frac N{u_N}\omega_M,\qquad L(M^N)=\frac N{u_N(M)}L(M).} \tag{5.3} The whole peripheral factor is retained before evaluating the common zero. In particular L(1)=1L(1)=1. The phase ηN=iN+3\eta_N=i^{N+3} of the actual modified dimension dN=ηN/uNd_N=\eta_N/u_N remains its separately marked line; the norm-root μN\mu_N sheet and this phase are different data.

The scalar logarithmic connection is also explicit. Its horizontal period Z=κLZ=\kappa L has dZ=2dlog⁡M,Z(1)=0, dZ=2\,d\log M,\qquad Z(1)=0, or, in the divided frame, dL=−Ldlog⁡κ+2dlog⁡Mκ. dL=-L\,d\log\kappa+\frac{2\,d\log M}{\kappa}. \tag{5.4} The selected regular solution is L=1−κ2/24+O(κ4)L=1-\kappa^2/24+O(\kappa^4). This is the peripheral logarithmic period; it is not the knot Bloch regulator or its undetermined constant.

6. The actual factorial affine source normalizes to this torsor

The literal finite polynomial is F(t,e)=∑j=0N−1(−te)j(Q;Q)j. F(t,e)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-te)^j}{(Q;Q)_j}. Its exact affine equation from the actual missing-edge completion is σF−(1+te)F=−κNtNN. \sigma F-(1+te)F=-\frac{\kappa_Nt^N}{N}. \tag{6.1} The norm-root ν=(1+κNtN)1/N\nu=(1+\kappa_Nt^N)^{1/N} produces the norm-one transport coefficient a(t,e)=1+teν. a(t,e)=\frac{1+te}{\nu}. It has 𝖭(a)=1\mathsf N(a)=1. This normalization is forced for a homogeneous Hilbert–90 gauge: the unnormalized 1+te1+te has norm 1+κNtN1+\kappa_Nt^N, so it cannot be a cyclic coboundary generically.

There is an explicit unique norm-one gauge lifting the actual nilpotent FF. Define H(t,e)=∑j=0N−1tjej∏r=1j(νQr−1),g=𝖭(H)−1/NH, H(t,e)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \frac{t^j e^j}{\prod_{r=1}^j(\nu Q^r-1)},\qquad g=\mathsf N(H)^{-1/N}H, \tag{6.2} with the scalar root marked by value one at κ=0\kappa=0. Every denominator in (6.2) is a unit on that chart. Coefficient comparison gives νσH=(1+te)H. \nu\sigma H=(1+te)H. For the constant coefficient use ∏r=0N−1(νQr−1)=εNκNtN; \prod_{r=0}^{N-1}(\nu Q^r-1) =\varepsilon_N\kappa_N t^N; the other coefficients follow from their displayed recursion. Consequently σg=ag,𝖭(g)=1,g|κ=0=F|κ=0. \boxed{\sigma g=a g,\qquad \mathsf N(g)=1,\qquad g|_{\kappa=0}=F|_{\kappa=0}.} \tag{6.3}

For uniqueness, two gauges have σ\sigma-invariant ratio, which is scalar because 1,e,…,eN−11,e,\ldots,e^{N-1} are eigenvectors with distinct eigenvalues 1,Q,…,QN−11,Q,\ldots,Q^{N-1}. Its norm is its NN-th power. The identity marking forces that ratio to be one. No disconnected constant phase is reset.

The first-normal affine class is explicit: σF−(1+te)Fκ|κ=0=−tN=−B0(1+te). \left.\frac{\sigma F-(1+te)F}{\kappa}\right|_{\kappa=0} =-t^N=-B_0(1+te). \tag{6.4} Thus the additive character of the unit dilatation is precisely the negative of the divided actual source at this order.

One can also write the entire first-normal correction without an existence assertion. Let ϵ=κN\epsilon=\kappa_N, and use g=F+ϵh+O(ϵ2)g=F+\epsilon h+O(\epsilon^2) in the free basis. Put F0=Fmod⁡ϵF_0=F\bmod\epsilon. Then (σ−1)(h/F0)=−tNN(1−(σF0)−1). (\sigma-1)(h/F_0) =-\frac{t^N}{N}\left(1-(\sigma F_0)^{-1}\right). \tag{6.5} The right side has zero constant coefficient in k[e]/eNk[e]/e^N. Its nonconstant coefficient of eje^j is divided by Qj−1Q^j-1 to obtain h/F0h/F_0. Its constant is fixed by the norm-one mark: [e0](h/F0)=−εN[zN]log⁡(∑j=0N−1(−tz)j(Q;Q)j). [e^0](h/F_0) =-\varepsilon_N[z^N]\log \left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-tz)^j}{(Q;Q)_j}\right). \tag{6.6} Formula (6.6) follows from (3.2), since the central normal coefficient is N[e0]hN[e^0]h when ϵ=uNκ\epsilon=u_N\kappa. It retains the actual affine source, including the mixed algebra normal term eN=εNϵe^N=\varepsilon_N\epsilon.

The relative tt-connection of this norm-one gauge satisfies (σ−1)dtlog⁡g=dtlog⁡(1+te)−dtlog⁡ν=(e1+te−κNtN−11+κNtN)dt. (\sigma-1)d_t\log g =d_t\log(1+te)-d_t\log\nu =\left(\frac{e}{1+te} -\frac{\kappa_Nt^{N-1}}{1+\kappa_Nt^N}\right)dt. \tag{6.7} Its trace is zero. The removed scalar component is exactly the norm-root connection used in (5.1).

7. An exact limitation for the full external connection

On A[κ−1]A[\kappa^{-1}], the multiplicative Kummer connection is ∇e=eNdlog⁡(εNκN). \nabla e=\frac eN\,d\log(\varepsilon_N\kappa_N). It is compatible with eN=εNκNe^N=\varepsilon_N\kappa_N, but is logarithmic at the nilpotent special fiber. There is no regular MM-derivation of the order AA extending d/dMd/dM. Indeed such a derivation would give NeN−1D(e)=(εNκN)′. Ne^{N-1}D(e)=(\varepsilon_N\kappa_N)'. Reducing at M=1M=1, the left side belongs to the nilpotent ideal (eN−1)(e^{N-1}) in k[e]/eNk[e]/e^N; the right side is the nonzero constant 2NεN2N\varepsilon_N. This is impossible.

This no-go concerns a derivation compatible with multiplication; it does not rule out arbitrary regular connections on the underlying free module. The norm-root quotient has the regular connection dlog⁡ν=1Ndlog⁡(1+κNtN), d\log\nu=\frac1N d\log(1+\kappa_Nt^N), and its divided relative form (5.1) extends. Thus the quotient and its first-normal source can carry the peripheral period even though a regular multiplicative full-block connection cannot.

8. Scope for the knot problem

The rank-one missing edge, its factorial affine source, the norm-root torsor, the first-normal additive character, and the complete peripheral period now occur in one explicit transport diagram. Neither a group isomorphism nor a bare special-fiber identification was assumed.

The relative path is the lift of the already specified meridian section. The norm-root quotient forgets a norm-one kernel of dimension N−1N-1; its peripheral period does not determine the kernel period or the knot state which couples to it.

This is not yet a map from the actual knot’s internal finite contraction to its geometric Gaussian block. In particular it does not identify its selected global source with the marked Bloch regulator or prove that the geometric contribution survives. Those assertions remain outside the norm-root peripheral theorem.
