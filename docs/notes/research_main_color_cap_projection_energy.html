The main-color Fourier energy retains a coherent cap projection

20 November 2026. Research note; public manuscripts remain frozen.

The main-color Plancherel norm has an exact realization by positive projectors on the original physical braid. Its cap projector is still coherent: the input and output indices of each traced head are paired in a maximally entangled vector. This note gives the actual identity, characterizes its allowed lower-bound test vectors, and computes what happens when a local output window is inserted. It proves no geometric lower bound or additional volume-conjecture case.

1. The original main-color orbit

Fix a zero-framed knot presented as the closure of a braid on bb strands. Its closure has one component. At level N≥2N\ge2, put q=eπi/N,Q=q2,αj=j−1,xj=qj−1,Mj=−qj,1≤j≤N. q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad \alpha_j=j-1,\quad x_j=q^{j-1},\quad M_j=-q^j, \qquad 1\le j\le N. At every jj, use the original NN-dimensional unrolled color VαjV_{\alpha_j}, including its reducible specializations. On its fixed basis eae_a, 0≤a<N0\le a<N, Kjea=xjQ−aea. K_je_a=x_jQ^{-a}e_a. Let WjW_j be the original checked braid operator on Vαj⊗bV_{\alpha_j}^{\otimes b}, including all common Cartan factors and its scalar zero-framing correction. Cut the first strand. The original reduced source is the (0,0)(0,0) entry of its partial pivotal trace: cj=[Tr⁡env{Wj(I⊗gj)}]00,gj=(Kj1−N)⊗(b−1).(1) c_j=\left[\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ env}} \{W_j(I\otimes g_j)\}\right]_{00},\qquad g_j=(K_j^{1-N})^{\otimes(b-1)}. \qquad\text{(1)} This is the reduced tangle scalar with unknot value one, as specified in the ADO source note, Section 1. It does not include the modified-dimension factor, whose ordinary-color specializations can have poles. The selected cut entry retains the reduced marked normalization; the pivotal trace closes only the other strands. In particular cN=KN,cj=Cj(q)(1≤j<N).(2) c_N=K_N,\qquad c_j=C_j(q)\quad(1\le j<N). \qquad\text{(2)}

Here (2) is an actual source comparison, not a renaming of labels. For j<Nj<N, the span of ej,…,eN−1e_j,\ldots,e_{N-1} is invariant under E,F,K,HE,F,K,H. Both finite signed crossings and the pivot preserve it on each strand. Its quotient is the ordinary jj-dimensional highest-weight color. In a partial trace of a one-component braid, a path contributing to the quotient cut cannot enter that invariant subspace and later leave it. Every contributing strand path therefore stays in the quotient. The pivotal and framing convention difference is (−1)(j−1)(b−1−w)=1(-1)^{(j-1)(b-1-w)}=1, since the writhe ww of a braid with cyclic closure permutation satisfies w≡b−1(mod⁡2)w\equiv b-1\pmod2. This proves the ordinary-color assertion in (2); the complete-color assertion is the retained original Kashaev normalization.

The ordinary-color Plancherel proof also proves EN:=∑j=1N|cj|2=|KN|2+RN,0≤RN≤(N−1)(N/2)2b−2.(3) E_N:=\sum_{j=1}^N|c_j|^2=|K_N|^2+R_N,\qquad 0\le R_N\le (N-1)(N/2)^{2b-2}. \qquad\text{(3)} Briefly, in its positive path realization the ordinary color projector is orthogonal and has trace [j]=sin⁡(πj/N)/sin⁡(π/N)[j]=\sin(\pi j/N)/\sin(\pi/N); the cabled braid is unitary. The unreduced colored trace is therefore bounded by [j]b[j]^b. Dividing by its nonzero dimension [j][j] gives |Cj|≤[j]b−1≤(N/2)b−1|C_j|\le[j]^{b-1}\le(N/2)^{b-1}. The argument applies to the whole range j<Nj<N. At j=Nj=N, the dimension is zero and that positive quotient erases the complete sector; we do not use it there.

2. A common physical cap projector

Write the traced environment as ℂD\mathbb C^D, where D=Nb−1D=N^{b-1}, with multi-indices I=(i2,…,ib)I=(i_2,\ldots,i_b). Its pivot has the exact form (gj)I=γj(g*)I,γj=xj(1−N)(b−1),(g*)I=Q−∑s=2bis.(4) (g_j)_I=\gamma_j(g_*)_I,\qquad \gamma_j=x_j^{(1-N)(b-1)},\qquad (g_*)_I=Q^{-\sum_{s=2}^b i_s}. \qquad\text{(4)} Indeed Q(N−1)a=Q−aQ^{(N-1)a}=Q^{-a}. Both γj\gamma_j and every entry of g*g_* have modulus one. Move only the recorded scalar phase into W̃j=γjWj\widetilde W_j=\gamma_jW_j; no source factor is dropped.

For an operator AA, fix the vectorization convention vec⁡A=∑a,a′,I,JA(a,I),(a′,J)ea⊗ea′⊗eI⊗eJ.(5) \operatorname{vec}A =\sum_{a,a',I,J}A_{(a,I),(a',J)} e_a\otimes e_{a'}\otimes e_I\otimes e_J. \qquad\text{(5)} The two first factors are the open output and input; the final factors are the environment output and input. All carry their usual positive coordinate inner products, so ∥vec⁡A∥=∥A∥HS\|\operatorname{vec}A\|=\|A\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}, the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Define unit vectors h=1D∑I(g*)I¯eI⊗eI,r=e0⊗e0⊗h.(6) h=\frac1{\sqrt D}\sum_I\overline{(g_*)_I}\,e_I\otimes e_I, \qquad r=e_0\otimes e_0\otimes h. \qquad\text{(6)} The vector hh is a product of the specified pivotal cap-pair vectors over the b−1b-1 traced heads. Its Schmidt rank across the whole environment output/input split is DD.

Add one main-color register ℂN\mathbb C^N, with basis |j⟩|j\rangle, and set 𝗐=∑j=1N|j⟩⊗vec⁡W̃j,Π=Icolor⊗|r⟩⟨r|.(7) \mathsf w=\sum_{j=1}^N|j\rangle\otimes \operatorname{vec}\widetilde W_j, \qquad \Pi=I_{\mathrm{ color}}\otimes|r\rangle\langle r|. \qquad\text{(7)} This is a direct sum of different actual main-color braid operators, not a braid with independently chosen colors on its different strands.

Proposition 1. The rank-NN positive orthogonal projector in (7) satisfies DΠ𝗐=∑j=1Ncj|j⟩⊗r,EN=D∥Π𝗐∥2.(8) \sqrt D\,\Pi\mathsf w =\sum_{j=1}^Nc_j|j\rangle\otimes r, \qquad E_N=D\|\Pi\mathsf w\|^2. \qquad\text{(8)}

Proof. Equations (1), (4)–(6) give ⟨r,vec⁡W̃j⟩=D−1/2∑I(g*)I(W̃j)(0,I),(0,I)=D−1/2cj. \langle r,\operatorname{vec}\widetilde W_j\rangle =D^{-1/2}\sum_I(g_*)_I(\widetilde W_j)_{(0,I),(0,I)} =D^{-1/2}c_j. Project onto rr in each orthogonal color block and sum their squared norms. This proves both identities. ▫\square

The unitary Fourier matrix ℱkj=N−1/2Q−kj\mathcal F_{kj}=N^{-1/2}Q^{-kj}, 0≤k<N0\le k<N, acts only on the main-color register and commutes with Π\Pi. Applying it to (8) gives exactly the main-color Plancherel norm. It does not remove the cap projector. Opening it instead gives the upper bound EN≤D∑j∥Wj∥HS2E_N\le D\sum_j\|W_j\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}^2. The single-color vectorized closure identity underlying (8) is already proved in the weighted plat-energy note, Section 4; (4) supplies the common projector for the whole actual orbit.

3. Which lower-bound tests preserve this source?

Every vector in ran⁡Π\operatorname{ran}\Pi is uniquely ψ=∑jaj|j⟩⊗r\psi=\sum_j a_j|j\rangle\otimes r. Cauchy gives EN≥|∑ja¯jcj|2∑j|aj|2(a≠0).(9) E_N\ge\frac{|\sum_j\overline a_jc_j|^2}{\sum_j|a_j|^2} \quad(a\ne0). \qquad\text{(9)} For an arbitrary trial vector vv, the valid test is Πv\Pi v: EN≥D|⟨Πv,𝗐⟩|2∥Πv∥2(Πv≠0).(10) E_N\ge D\frac{|\langle\Pi v,\mathsf w\rangle|^2} {\|\Pi v\|^2} \quad(\Pi v\ne0). \qquad\text{(10)} Replacing Πv\Pi v by vv in the numerator has no justification. Moreover, when ∑j|aj|2=1\sum_j|a_j|^2=1, (3) gives the exact separation |∑ja¯jcj−a¯NKN|≤1−|aN|2RN.(11) \left|\sum_j\overline a_jc_j-\overline a_NK_N\right| \le\sqrt{1-|a_N|^2}\,\sqrt{R_N}. \qquad\text{(11)} Thus a test avoiding the complete main color has only polynomial size. A successful exponential lower bound from (9) is possible, but it must retain and estimate the original complete-color contribution.

4. Exact effect of localizing an environment output

Let AA be any operator on the environment output factor alone; extend it by identities on the other factors and call the extension Â\widehat A. Maximal entanglement in (6) gives ΠÂΠ=Tr⁡ADΠ.(12) \boxed{\qquad \Pi\widehat A\Pi=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}A}{D}\Pi. \qquad} \qquad\text{(12)} To verify this, expand ⟨h,(A⊗I)h⟩\langle h,(A\otimes I)h\rangle. The input inner product sets the two indices equal, the pivot phases cancel, and the result is D−1∑IAIID^{-1}\sum_IA_{II}. The remaining factors in (7) are unchanged. In particular a rank-ss orthogonal output-window projector gives exactly (s/D)Π(s/D)\Pi. Sandwiching such a window between the actual source projectors therefore rescales the same unknown source norm; it does not extract an independently localized contribution.

There is a second useful support restriction. If 0≤Q0≤Π0\le Q_0\le\Pi, then ran⁡Q0⊆ran⁡Π\operatorname{ran}Q_0\subseteq\operatorname{ran}\Pi. For z∈ker⁡Πz\in\ker\Pi, positivity gives 0≤⟨z,Q0z⟩≤00\le\langle z,Q_0z\rangle\le0, hence Q01/2z=0Q_0^{1/2}z=0; taking orthogonal complements proves the claim. When D>1D>1, this range contains no nonzero vector whose environment output/input part is a single product vector: every nonzero color block has the full-Schmidt-rank environment vector hh. Consequently an independently product-localized positive projector cannot be placed below Π\Pi in operator order.

These statements hold for all input tensors. They do not prove that a source-compatible entangled geometric test is impossible, or that such a test has small overlap with the actual 𝗐\mathsf w. They specify what must be constructed before positivity can give the desired lower bound without changing the original source.

Finally, this is the orbit of the knot’s main meridian: it changes every main-strand crossing representation and its pivot. Auxiliary crossing-circle colors instead change selected inserted circle coupons while the main representation stays fixed. Their Fourier or Hermite norms cannot be substituted for (8). Likewise a complete-main-color Conway Gram does not become an all-main-meridian compiler from (8). All ordinary and complete physical blocks in (7) must remain as stated.
