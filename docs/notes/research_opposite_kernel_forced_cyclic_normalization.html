The opposite kernel’s finite Gauss row and forced cyclic normalization

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The actual opposite-polarization operator contains a finite balanced hypergeometric polynomial. Its clock contiguity has an explicit extreme-tail source. Going once around the finite clock solves the resulting forced recurrence. On the actual tensor module its assembled denominator is a uniformly bounded analytic unit on a scaled meridian collar. No individual clock denominator or endpoint source is removed.

This identifies the literal normalization, including its meridian normals. The scalar polynomial away from the finite spectrum has an additional norm row, displayed below. Substituting a geometric scalar argument into the finite central relation would lose that row. No coherent phase, amplitude estimate, geometric occurrence, or vanishing relative-window defect is inferred.

The two complete directs are J and R, pinned at the end. All finite summations, contiguities, and cyclic estimates used here are proved below.

5. Actual kernel and relative-window consequences

All matrices in (6), (9), and (31) are on J’s original finite spaces. Applying its compact kernel KB(𝒘,𝒛)=v(𝒘)Bc(𝒛)K_B(\mathbf w,\mathbf z)=v(\mathbf w)B c(\mathbf z) therefore gives exact finite coherent identities and exact convolutions on the original compact resolutions. Every cap ∏i(1+wizi)d\prod_i(1+w_i z_i)^d, Cartan factor, factorial dual, and meridian derivative remains. This does not replace an operator kernel by the scalar evaluation in (13).

There is also an explicit relative row. For any two actual partial-resolution matrices P𝒞,P𝒟P_{\mathcal C},P_{\mathcal D} from J, define Δj=P𝒞𝒜j−𝒜jP𝒟\Delta_j=P_{\mathcal C}\mathscr A_j-\mathscr A_jP_{\mathcal D}. Thus the window comparison has both a coefficient commutator and an explicit extreme-tail source. J’s boundary transgression applies to the two partial resolutions, with their complementary chains retained. No term in (34) is zero merely because the full trace is sewn.

The finite formulas are holomorphic or meromorphic in the common meridian before taking their prescribed continuations. They may consequently be differentiated with all displayed normal terms. They are not ordinary quantum-parameter-normal identities obtained by differentiating the root equations.

For clarity, the following distinction is essential. Equation (30) used the actual operator relation 𝒲N=0\mathcal W_N=0. At a geometric scalar argument (K,Ω)(K,\Omega), even when Ω=z+z−1\Omega=z+z^{-1} comes from a loxodromic representation, the polynomial 𝒲N\mathcal W_N in (15) need not vanish. Equations (14), (18), and (29) retain its complete contribution. Nor does the finite-node evaluation (20) define a preferred complex interpolation of its integer label ll. The canonical scalar continuation is the original polynomial (9).

The result is an actual factorization of the opposite kernel, its explicit finite Gauss polynomial, and a forced cyclic normalization with a controlled assembled meridian denominator. The high-degree endpoint kernel and the relative row (34) remain to be estimated in a proposed geometric chart. No nonzero local amplitude, source-sector coefficient, or geometric relative-cycle transport follows from these identities.
