The pair color lies beyond the half-charge observation contour

Research proof, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The actual angle at either vertex of a positive complete-ended internal pair lies strictly below and to the right of the corresponding half-charge leaf saddle. Its half-charge action is strictly larger. Both statements follow from the original logarithmic periods and elementary bounds for a single analytic derivative. They do not assume a nonzero coefficient for the filled pair.

This supplies the geometric comparison needed to evaluate the proved half-charge Cauchy observation at an actual pair color. The exact Cauchy residue and interpolation remainder must still be kept when making that source substitution.

For each fixed pair the right side of (14) is a fixed positive number. By continuity, a smaller positive gap persists on a fixed complex neighborhood of its selected angle. No uniform positive gap as the powers tend to infinity is asserted.

The position statement is also exactly the one needed by the Cauchy observation contour. Its angular rectangle has real ends tending to ±π/2\pm\pi/2 and imaginary heights ±H0\pm H_0. The two preimages t,−tt,-t of the pair color −2cos⁡t-2\cos t lie strictly outside that rectangle, because H>H0H>H_0. They remain separated from it on a fixed neighborhood. There are no further preimages in that angular period. Thus an exact residue formula for evaluation at this color uses its exterior case, not the case where the evaluation pole is enclosed and cancels the direct term.

These statements compare a specified original pair angle with a specified original-source observation action. They do not identify the coefficient of the full signed pair contraction. In particular the complementary-time observations, the shared wire, and all ordinary boundary normals must still be retained in that contraction.

Indeed Theorem 3 places the color outside the exact observation rectangle, so the exterior residue formula applies. Thus a nonzero local source is proved, including its ordinary normal directions. The remaining whole signed pair contraction still requires the rest of its contour to be controlled.
