The braid cut, the literal empty cap, and global nine-cell sewing

Private written proof, 17 November 2026. Starting with a supplied braid presentation of a knot, we assemble the literal charged tetrahedral sources into a closed triangulated pair. Its normalized source is the original fixed-zero planar source times a specified scalar of modulus one on injective real decorations. All vertex measures, wire clocks, sector phases, and the actual empty closing ball remain in the formula. No state-invariance theorem or printed nine-cell comparison is used.

2. A global integral sector assignment

There is an explicit level-independent 0/1 admissible assignment. Checkerboard-color the regions of the closed shadow. Its black and white Tait graphs are dual, have cc edges, and their vertex counts sum to c+2c+2. The cut edge is adjacent to one black and one white root region. Choose a spanning tree TT of the black graph. Its complementary dual edges form a spanning tree T*T^* of the white graph: a dual cycle would separate the primal tree, and the edge count is exactly the number of dual vertices minus one.

For a crossing whose black edge is in TT, give charge 1 to the black corner farther from the black root along TT. Otherwise give charge 1 to the white corner farther from the white root in T*T^*. Give all other corners zero. Each crossing has exactly one 1. Each nonroot region receives exactly one, at its parent tree edge, and both roots receive zero. After cutting, those roots form the outer region. A loop is never chosen as a tree edge; the dual of a primal bridge is not in the complementary dual tree. Thus the construction also covers repeated regions and multiple edges. The selected child endpoint of a tree edge is a particular corner occurrence, not a possibly repeated region name.

This proves the hypotheses used in Section 1 without solving a level-dependent linear system. We use this χ\chi below.

3. The actual three-ball and one global vertex order

The closed shadow has a dual quadrilateral cellulation of S2S^2, with cc cells, 2c2c sides, and c+2c+2 region vertices. Cut its side dual to the chosen knot edge. The result is a disk QQ with 2c−12c-1 paired interior sides and two free boundary sides. It has cc interior region vertices and the two root vertices on its boundary. This remains an incidence description when cell boundaries repeat. The knot edge separates two distinct checkerboard regions, so the cut is along an embedded side, not a loop in the dual surface.

Suspend QQ between poles 𝖯,𝖲\mathsf P,\mathsf S. Each region vertex sweeps an arc from 𝖯\mathsf P to 𝖲\mathsf S, designated as one edge; its midpoint is not an additional vertex of the chosen cell structure. 6. Exact closed comparison and its scope

Let ZlitZ_{\mathrm{ lit}} be the literal closed source with its factor N−#verticesN^{-\#\mathrm{vertices}}, as in [K,(4.19)]. Equations (2),(13),(18) give Zlit=N−2Kopen∑i,jqiOχ(i,j)=N−1KopenOχ(0,0).(19) Z_{\mathrm{ lit}} =N^{-2}K_{\mathrm{ open}}\sum_{i,j}q^iO_\chi(i,j) =N^{-1}K_{\mathrm{ open}}O_\chi(0,0). \qquad\text{(19)} The primary’s closed normalization is N2ZlitN^2Z_{\mathrm{ lit}}. Consequently the full exact formula is N2Zlit=(∏v=1cN2CN(𝒔v)qϕv)Oχ(0,0).(20) \boxed{\quad N^2 Z_{\mathrm{ lit}} =\left(\prod_{v=1}^c N^2 C_N(\mathbf s_v)q^{\phi_v}\right) O_\chi(0,0). \quad} \qquad\text{(20)} Every phase in (20) is specified by (4) of [C] and (10) above. For distinct real decorations the multiplier has modulus exactly 1. Together with (8), this also gives the normalized enhanced period with its recorded root phase, rather than an unidentified scalar. The equality holds even at a level where that period vanishes.

The construction uses a supplied one-component braid presentation; it makes no separate claim about transporting the sector adapter through every arbitrary Morse presentation. Repeated face occurrences are retained explicitly. If the primary notation identifies sectors merely by the pair (v,f)(v,f), its literal applicability is restricted to diagrams without such repetitions; our finite source specifies the corner extension throughout, with the same proved local tensors.

The proof assigns the actual link, charges, total vertex order, injective decorations, and fixed cut. It is not an invariance theorem for all triangulations, a positive geometric development, an off-root meridian family, or a transport of a selected integration cycle. In particular it supplies no nonvanishing or volume lower bound.
