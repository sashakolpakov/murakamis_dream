A global source-error bound from the original Conway closing

Research proof, 23 November 2026.

The highest product’s endpoint identity controls every lower quadratic error, giving a global exponentially small remainder coefficient without local polar decompositions. The computed polar radius of [P] remains at least as strong; the improvement is over its earlier inexpensive global radius.

1. The original tree and its coherent correction

Fix N≥2N\ge2, put D=N−1D=N-1, and use HN=ℂ[X]/(2TN(X/2)+2)H_N=\mathbb C[X]/(2T_N(X/2)+2) with Newton basis e0,…,eDe_0,\ldots,e_D.

The coefficient Hermitian norm makes these basis vectors orthonormal. All first normals at repeated roots are retained. The original turn, Frobenius row and marked evaluation row satisfy

Jej=eD−j,ϕ(f)=fD,λ0(f)=f0,ϕ(uv)=u𝖳Jv,λ0(uv)=u0v0,λ0J=ϕ,ϕJ=λ0.(1) Je_j=e_{D-j},\quad \phi(f)=f_D,\quad \lambda_0(f)=f_0, \quad \phi(uv)=u^{\mathsf T}Jv,\quad \lambda_0(uv)=u_0v_0,\quad \lambda_0J=\phi,\quad\phi J=\lambda_0. \qquad\text{(1)}

Here fjf_j denotes a Newton coefficient, and transpose has no complex conjugation. These are the full original algebra identities in [P,C].

Take a fixed literal Conway tree with distinct child occurrences. Its proper gates are prescribed leaves, turns JJ, and products M(u,w)=uwM(u,w)=uw.

Use the original budgets of [C], with σ=1/16\sigma=1/16:

bv=16max⁡(1,∥Tv∥HS),Φv=M/bvat a product,Φv=J/bvat a turn.(2) b_v=16\max(1,\|T_v\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}),\qquad \Phi_v=M/b_v\ \text{at a product},\qquad \Phi_v=J/b_v\ \text{at a turn}. \qquad\text{(2)}

Here TvT_v is the corresponding unnormalized physical tensor. The original closing is L=χλε/bρL=\chi\lambda_\varepsilon/b_\rho, with ε∈{0,D}\varepsilon\in\{0,D\} and λD=ϕ\lambda_D=\phi.

The scalar χ\chi, its closing budget bρb_\rho, every other budget, and their product BtotB_{\mathrm{ tot}} are retained. In particular ∥L∥=|χ|/bρ≤σ\|L\|=|\chi|/b_\rho\le\sigma.

Let aa be the true normalized forward field and xx any feasible trial field, meaning ∥xv∥≤1\|x_v\|\le1 at every proper vertex. Define

e=a−x,r=Φ(x)−x,A=DΦ(x),c=(0,…,L on the top packet,…,0), e=a-x,\quad r=\Phi(x)-x,\quad A=D\Phi(x),\quad c=(0,\ldots,L\text{ on the top packet},\ldots,0), d=c(I−A)−1,z=Lxtop,k=KN/Btot,k̂=z+dr.(3) d=c(I-A)^{-1},\qquad z=Lx_{\mathrm{ top}},\qquad k=K_N/B_{\mathrm{ tot}},\qquad \widehat k=z+dr. \qquad\text{(3)}

The inverse is a finite triangular sum. If Qv(h)=Φv(hL,hR)Q_v(h)=\Phi_v(h_L,h_R) at products and is zero elsewhere, [R] proves the exact identities

(I−A)e=r+Q(e),k−k̂=dQ(e).(4) (I-A)e=r+Q(e),\qquad k-\widehat k=dQ(e). \qquad\text{(4)}

Because the tree has one parent per packet, its backward rows obey

dtop=L,du=dvDuΦv(x)(u a child of v).(5) d_{\mathrm{ top}}=L,\qquad d_u=d_vD_u\Phi_v(x)\quad(u\text{ a child of }v). \qquad\text{(5)}

2. The highest product fixes the global coefficient

Suppose first that the tree contains a product, and let v*v_* be the first product encountered going downward from the closing. Every proper gate above it is a turn. Let 𝒰\mathcal U be that turn corridor and put

η=χbρ∏u∈𝒰bu,ζ=|η|bv*.(6) \eta=\frac{\chi}{b_\rho\prod_{u\in\mathcal U}b_u},\qquad \zeta=\frac{|\eta|}{b_{v_*}}. \qquad\text{(6)}

Empty products equal one. By (1) and (5), dv*=ηλνd_{v_*}=\eta\lambda_\nu, with ν∈{0,D}\nu\in\{0,D\} determined by the corridor parity. No turn has been moved through a product. Thus the contracted bilinear matrix at v*v_* is exactly

Bv*={(η/bv*)J,ν=D,(η/bv*)e0e0𝖳,ν=0,∥Bv*∥=ζ.(7) B_{v_*}=\begin{cases} (\eta/b_{v_*})J,&\nu=D,\\ (\eta/b_{v_*})e_0e_0^{\mathsf T},&\nu=0, \end{cases} \qquad \|B_{v_*}\|=\zeta. \qquad\text{(7)}

Here u𝖳Bvw=dvΦv(u,w)u^{\mathsf T}B_vw=d_v\Phi_v(u,w). For either child uu of v*v_*, its sibling packet xsibx_{\mathrm{ sib}} gives, by (1),

∥du∥≤ζ∥xsib∥≤ζ.(8) \|d_u\|\le\zeta\|x_{\mathrm{ sib}}\|\le\zeta. \qquad\text{(8)}

This uses the endpoint contraction. Each lower derivative block has norm at most σ\sigma. At a product this follows from ∥DuΦv(x)∥≤∥Φv∥bil∥xsib∥≤σ\|D_u\Phi_v(x)\|\le\|\Phi_v\|_{\mathrm{ bil}}\|x_{\mathrm{ sib}}\| \le\sigma; at a turn it follows from (2). Consequently a proper descendant vv at distance j≥1j\ge1 below v*v_* satisfies

∥dv∥≤ζσj−1,∥Bv∥≤σ∥dv∥≤ζσjif v is a product.(9) \|d_v\|\le\zeta\sigma^{j-1},\qquad \|B_v\|\le\sigma\|d_v\|\le\zeta\sigma^j \quad\text{if }v\text{ is a product}. \qquad\text{(9)}

All product matrices have norm at most ζ\zeta, with equality at the highest one. Thus the block polar majorant 𝒮x\mathcal S_x of [P,(10a)] satisfies, uniformly over feasible xx,

∥𝒮x∥=ζ.(10) \|\mathcal S_x\|=\zeta. \qquad\text{(10)}

3. The whole remainder and an inexpensive source disk

For any full variation field hh, distinct product sibling pairs are disjoint. Equations (7)–(9) and 2ab≤a2+b22ab\le a^2+b^2 give

|dQ(h)|≤∑vproduct∥Bv∥∥hL∥∥hR∥≤ζ2∥h∥2.(11) |dQ(h)|\le\sum_{v\ {\mathrm{ product}}}\|B_v\|\|h_L\|\|h_R\| \le\frac\zeta2\|h\|^2. \qquad\text{(11)}

The true and trial fields lie in the original product of unit balls. On their joining segment, the derivative of Φ\Phi has norm at most 2σ≤1/8\sqrt2\sigma\le1/8: a row has at most two child blocks and every child occurs once. Integrating that derivative, or using [R]’s residual estimate, proves ∥e∥≤(8/7)∥r∥\|e\|\le(8/7)\|r\|. Combining this with (4),(11) yields

|k−k̂|≤Rend(x),Rend(x)=32ζ49∥r∥2, |k-\widehat k|\le R_{\mathrm{ end}}(x),\qquad R_{\mathrm{ end}}(x)=\frac{32\zeta}{49}\|r\|^2, Btot(|k̂|−Rend)+≤|KN|≤Btot(|k̂|+Rend).(12) B_{\mathrm{ tot}}(|\widehat k|-R_{\mathrm{ end}})_+ \le |K_N|\le B_{\mathrm{ tot}}(|\widehat k|+R_{\mathrm{ end}}). \qquad\text{(12)}

Here (t)+=max⁡(t,0)(t)_+=\max(t,0). With no product, Q=0Q=0, k=k̂k=\widehat k exactly, and the radius is zero.

Write HM=max⁡(1,∥M∥HS)H_M=\max(1,\|M\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}). Since all corridor budgets are at least one and bv*=16HMb_{v_*}=16H_M,

ζ≤1256HM,Rend≤∥r∥2392HM≤∥r∥2392<∥r∥2343(r≠0).(13) \zeta\le\frac1{256H_M},\qquad R_{\mathrm{ end}}\le\frac{\|r\|^2}{392H_M} \le\frac{\|r\|^2}{392}<\frac{\|r\|^2}{343} \quad(r\ne0). \qquad\text{(13)}

Thus (12) improves the earlier inexpensive global coefficient uniformly. It does not improve the computed polar radius RpolR_{\mathrm{ pol}} of [P]. Indeed its scalar majorants obey Δv=∥rv∥+σ∑cΔc\Delta_v=\|r_v\|+\sigma\sum_c\Delta_c, hence ∥Δ∥≤(8/7)∥r∥\|\Delta\|\le(8/7)\|r\| by the same one-parent norm bound. Since [P]’s tvc≤∥Bv∥Δct_{vc}\le\sqrt{\|B_v\|}\Delta_c,

Rpol≤ζ2∥Δ∥2≤Rend.(14) R_{\mathrm{ pol}}\le\frac\zeta2\|\Delta\|^2\le R_{\mathrm{ end}}. \qquad\text{(14)}

One may use (12) without forming any polar matrix, or keep the stronger computed polar disk. The coherent center and every budget are identical.

4. Exponential saving and scope

For j=⌊N/4⌋j=\lfloor N/4\rfloor, [P,(20)] proves from the actual sine-factorial coefficient that

∥M∥HS≥∥M∥bil≥s2j2sj4=exp⁡{(2GCat/π)N+O(log⁡N)},sj=∏a=1j2sin⁡(πa/N).(15) \|M\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}\ge\|M\|_{\mathrm{ bil}}\ge \frac{s_{2j}^{\,2}}{s_j^{\,4}} =\exp\{(2G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/\pi)N+O(\log N)\}, \qquad s_j=\prod_{a=1}^j2\sin(\pi a/N). \qquad\text{(15)}

Here GCat=∫0π/4log⁡(cot⁡t)dtG_{\mathrm{ Cat}}=\int_0^{\pi/4}\log(\cot t)dt. Consequently ζ≤exp⁡{−(2GCat/π)N+O(log⁡N)}\zeta\le\exp\{-(2G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/\pi)N+O(\log N)\}. The saving is global for the stated literal compiler, at every parity and composite level; (15) is its large-level quantitative consequence. Restoring BtotB_{\mathrm{ tot}} retains all lower product budgets. No claim that every multiplication cost disappears is made.

For a sequence of feasible trials, suppose |k̂N|=exp⁡(−αN+o(N))|\widehat k_N|=\exp(-\alpha N+o(N)), ∥rN∥≤exp⁡(−βN+o(N))\|r_N\|\le\exp(-\beta N+o(N)), and ζN≤exp⁡(−γN+o(N))\zeta_N\le\exp(-\gamma N+o(N)). Then (12) is negligible relative to its coherent center whenever 2β+γ>α2\beta+\gamma>\alpha. With the older constant coefficient, the corresponding sufficient condition was 2β>α2\beta>\alpha. A bounded residual permits β=0\beta=0; the hypotheses still require the stated actual center estimate.

The pure-turn corridor and the two original endpoint closings are essential hypotheses. Fixed short proper subtrees may be inserted as their full prescribed leaves. General precomposed unary maps and shared descendants are outside this literal compiler; parameter derivatives of the closing need their own transport. All physical Hermite normals and original phases remain present. No actual center or positive difference has been assigned a geometric rate, and no finite-exception theorem follows.
