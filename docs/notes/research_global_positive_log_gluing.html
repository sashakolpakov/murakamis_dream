Global injectivity of positive marked logarithmic gluing

Research proof, 22 November 2026.

Positive ideal shapes give a global uniqueness statement for a fixed logarithmic gluing system. The proof compares two configurations in the product of shape strips; their joining segment need not satisfy any gluing equation. It also proves that the inverse is holomorphic on the realized image. No existence or source-cycle assertion is included.

1. The positive shape coordinates and the real constraint matrix

Let 𝒮={x∈ℂ:0<Im⁡x<π},z=ex,v(x)=−Log⁡(1−ex).(1) \mathcal S=\{x\in\mathbb C:0<\operatorname{Im}x<\pi\}, \qquad z=e^x, \qquad v(x)=-\operatorname{Log}(1-e^x). \qquad\text{(1)} The logarithm in (1) is principal. Since zz is in the upper half-plane, 1−z1-z is in the lower half-plane, so this defines one holomorphic function on the entire convex strip. In particular, 0<Im⁡v(x)<π,v′(x)=ex1−ex,Im⁡v′(x)=Im⁡(ex)|1−ex|2>0.(2) 0<\operatorname{Im}v(x)<\pi,\qquad v'(x)=\frac{e^x}{1-e^x},\qquad \operatorname{Im}v'(x) =\frac{\operatorname{Im}(e^x)}{|1-e^x|^2}>0. \qquad\text{(2)} For t≥1t\ge1, apply vv separately to the coordinates of x∈𝒮tx\in\mathcal S^t. Let A,BA,B be real tt-by-tt matrices satisfying R=(AB),rank⁡R=t,AB𝖳=BA𝖳.(3) R=(A\ B),\qquad \operatorname{rank}R=t, \qquad AB^{\mathsf T}=BA^{\mathsf T}. \qquad\text{(3)} Define the holomorphic logarithmic constraint map FR(x)=Ax+Bv(x).(4) F_R(x)=Ax+Bv(x). \qquad\text{(4)} Neither block in (3) is required to be invertible.

Use the complex bilinear alternating form Ω((a,b),(c,d))=a𝖳d−b𝖳con ℂ2t.(5) \Omega((a,b),(c,d))=a^{\mathsf T}d-b^{\mathsf T}c \quad\text{on }\mathbb C^{2t}. \qquad\text{(5)} The real kernel of RR is Lagrangian for this form. Indeed it is the image of the rank-tt matrix (−B𝖳A𝖳)\binom{-B^{\mathsf T}}{A^{\mathsf T}}: multiplication by RR gives zero, and the pairing of two of its columns is zero by (3). Its complexification is therefore isotropic and is closed under complex conjugation.

2. A strict comparison for any two positive configurations

For x,x′∈𝒮tx,x'\in\mathcal S^t, write Δx=x−x′\Delta x=x-x' and Δv=v(x)−v(x′)\Delta v=v(x)-v(x'). 3. Actual edge rows and arbitrary primitive filling directions

Consider an actual oriented ideal triangulation with tt tetrahedra and cc torus ideal-vertex links, with positively ordered shapes. Retain every face identification, edge occurrence and marked primitive peripheral path. In the convention (1), the incidence argument in [M] gives t−ct-c independent real edge rows. Their peripheral symplectic quotient has dimension 2c2c; different cusps pair to zero, and each cusp’s primitive meridian and longitude have nonzero intersection pairing.

Append one row γi=aiμi+biλi,(ai,bi)∈ℝ2\{(0,0)},(11) \gamma_i=a_i\mu_i+b_i\lambda_i, \qquad (a_i,b_i)\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}, \qquad\text{(11)} at each cusp. These rows are independent modulo the edge rows and pair to zero. The complete tt-row system therefore satisfies (3), as proved locally in [M]. This rank and isotropy argument is incidence-theoretic and does not depend on the shape values. For integral coprime (ai,bi)(a_i,b_i), (11) is the original primitive path; real coefficients give the corresponding real polarization.

Fix the actual affine logarithmic target, including all edge windings, peripheral signs, and constants introduced by the quad convention. The equations are then precisely FR(x)=q.(12) F_R(x)=q. \qquad\text{(12)} The theorem gives at most one positive assignment satisfying this fixed target, across all positive components, and a nonsingular Jacobian at every such assignment. No completeness hypothesis is needed for this uniqueness and transversality step.

In particular one may keep a meridian row at every unfilled cusp and choose μi+piλi\mu_i+p_i\lambda_i at every designated filled cusp, with arbitrary fixed real pip_i. Mixed signs and pi=0p_i=0 cause no exception. For an actual integral filling the similarity-log equation has its prescribed primitive target, for example Ui+piVi=−2πi.(13) U_i+p_iV_i=-2\pi\mathrm i. \qquad\text{(13)} The logs in (13) are the actual continued similarity logs, with their original primitive basis. They are not uncentered SL eigenlogs or equations only modulo 2πi2\pi\mathrm i.

Deleting dependent edge rows does not lose constraints: their linear relations and constants must agree in the full marked system. If they did not agree, that target would have no solution. Quad changes likewise transform both the rows and their affine constants. The theorem does not compare different logarithmic lifts of one exponentiated equation.

One useful consequence concerns continuation along the same fixed primitive slopes, with the right sides of (13) varying continuously. No two positive solutions at a fixed target can collide, and no Jacobian can become singular at an interior positive point. A continuation remaining in a compact subset of 𝒮t\mathcal S^t extends past a finite endpoint by compactness and (9). This does not prove that such compact containment or continuation exists.

4. Algebraic multipliers at a realized target

Suppose now that A,BA,B have integer entries and that all γi=eqi\gamma_i=e^{q_i} are nonzero algebraic numbers. Exponentiating (12) gives the square rational system Gi(z):=∏jzjAij(1−zj)−Bij=γi,1≤i≤t.(14) G_i(z):=\prod_j z_j^{A_{ij}}(1-z_j)^{-B_{ij}} =\gamma_i,\quad 1\le i\le t. \qquad\text{(14)} Work on ∏jzj(1−zj)≠0\prod_jz_j(1-z_j)\ne0. At a realized positive solution, DzG=diag⁡(γi)JR(x)diag⁡(zj−1)(15) D_zG=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_i)J_R(x) \operatorname{diag}(z_j^{-1}) \qquad\text{(15)} is invertible. Thus this is an isolated nonsingular algebraic solution and all its shapes are algebraic. Here is also a direct field argument. Over a number field containing the γi\gamma_i, differentiate (14) in the finitely generated field generated by the zjz_j. Equation (15) forces every dzjdz_j to vanish. In characteristic zero the dimension of these differentials is the transcendence degree, so that field is a finite algebraic extension.

Other positive solutions of (14) can have different logarithmic targets q+2πikq+2\pi\mathrm i k. They are not identified by this corollary. Global uniqueness is the fixed-log statement (12).

5. Relation to the earlier results and the remaining boundary

[M] proves the all-cusp marked incidence and local polarized Jacobian theorem. [E] proves global comparison, fixed-slope uniqueness and positive-region elimination for its explicit original eight-tetrahedron edge blocks. The argument (6)–(10) gives a global comparison directly in the product of ideal-shape strips, for any real Lagrangian constraint matrix. It does not require the original wire or peripheral image to be convex. Polarized rigidity and geometric variational principles are established subjects; no historical novelty claim is made here.

Uniqueness does not supply a positive point. Already for t=1t=1, A=1,B=−1A=1,B=-1, the target q=−log⁡8q=-\log8 would imply z(1−z)=1/8z(1-z)=1/8, whose two roots are real. There is no positive solution. Nor is the realized image asserted convex or the map to ℂt\mathbb C^t asserted proper. A hyperbolic filling need not be represented by a positive assignment on this same fixed triangulation and log lift.

The result does not construct a cone path, prove primitive metric completion from multiplier equations, identify a quantum source critical scheme, or select the coefficient of its original cycle. The original closing, normal data, and noncancellation estimates remain separate. No finite-exception or volume-limit theorem follows.
