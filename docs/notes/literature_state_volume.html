An independent audit of literature state integrals and their volume limits

Research note, 25 October 2026. No novelty claim is made. The definitions below come from the cited literature. The estimates, contour arguments and algebraic checks needed here are supplied separately; a published theorem is not being used as its own proof.

There are three different conclusions:

  • For the figure-eight knot, the specified Andersen–Kashaev state has the volume limit, and the explicit second-order literature operator is its minimal annihilator over the rational q-difference field, with the qualifications about analytic continuation below.
  • A multivariate argument proves the volume limit for the explicit literature integral (4.5) of Ben Aribi–Wong whenever the stated finite matrix and positive geometric certificates are supplied. This includes the certified geometric FAMED class. The subsequent general_annihilator_and_volume.md proves existence of a minimal operator for these rescaled integrals in the Andersen–Malusà b-shift algebra, without an efficient coefficient formula.
  • Neither argument proves a theorem for every alternating knot, nor establishes all the global topological identities of Teichmüller TQFT.

1. Fixed sources and conventions

  1. Andersen–Kashaev, A TQFT from quantum Teichmüller theory, arXiv:1109.6295v2, Definition 15, equation (38), and Appendix A, equations (42)–(48).
  2. Andersen–Malusà, A Geometric Quantisation view on the AJ-conjecture for the Teichmüller TQFT, arXiv:1711.11522v3, Definition 5 and Section 5.1. The algebraic certificate was checked against this version’s TeX source. Corrections to other printed formulas in that version are discussed in literature_definition_audit.md.
  3. Ben Aribi–Wong, The Andersen–Kashaev volume conjecture for FAMED geometric triangulations, arXiv:2410.10776v2, Definitions 1.1 and 2.7–2.10 and equation (4.5). Their asymptotic theorem is not assumed below.
  4. Andersen–Kashaev, Complex Quantum Chern–Simons, arXiv:1409.1208, equation (122), fixes the finite-product convention for the level-N dilogarithm.

Take b>0, q=exp(2 pi i b^2), and

hbar = (b+b^(-1))^(-2) = b^2/(1+b^2)^2.

The level-one boundary operators are

Q f(x)=exp(-2 pi b x) f(x),     E f(x)=f(x-i b),
E Q=q Q E.

Thus classical Q is m^2. All factors in Q are retained until after taking operator identities. The complete point is Q=1 in this convention; it is not an ideal point of the algebraic curve at infinity in the Culler–Shalen sense.

2. Quantum dilogarithm from its defining integral

Start with the literature definition

log Phi_b(Z) = integral_(R+i0)
    exp(-2 i Z w)/(4 sinh(b w) sinh(w/b) w) dw.             (2.1)

The contour passes above zero. It can equally be taken on a sufficiently small positive horizontal line. The exponential of this integral is holomorphic and nonzero in |Im Z|<(b+b^(-1))/2. This assertion follows from locally uniform convergence on a displaced contour, not from an asserted zero/pole formula.

2.1 A uniform complex remainder on an entire horizontal strip

For every delta>0 there is C_delta such that, for 0<b<=1 and |Im z|<=pi-delta,

log Phi_b(z/(2 pi b))
  = Li_2(-exp z)/(2 pi i b^2) + R_b(z),
sup_z |R_b(z)| <= C_delta b^2.                            (2.2)

The logarithm is the one prescribed by (2.1), and the dilogarithm branch is analytic on |Im z|<pi and tends to zero as Re z tends to minus infinity. The supremum includes every real part of z.

Proof. Substitute w=b t into (2.1). Subtract the leading kernel to obtain

I(z)=1/4 integral_(R+i0) exp(-i z t/pi)/(t^2 sinh t) dt,
K_b(t)=1/(4 t sinh t) [1/sinh(b^2 t)-1/(b^2 t)].

First evaluate I without assuming a quantum-dilogarithm asymptotic. Put

J(z)=integral_(R+i0) exp(-i z t/pi)/sinh t dt.

A rectangle translated upward by i pi gives

(1+exp z) J(z)=-2 pi i exp z.

Indeed the integrand changes by -exp z on translation, and the enclosed pole at i pi has residue -exp z. The vertical edges tend to zero for |Im z|<pi. Consequently

I''(z)=-J(z)/(4 pi^2)
      = i exp z/(2 pi(1+exp z)).

This is the second derivative of Li_2(-exp z)/(2 pi i). Both I and I’ tend to zero as Re z tends to minus infinity: move the t contour to Im t=a, 0<a<pi, and use its exponentially decaying kernel. This fixes both integration constants. The finite-part integrals at zero are thereby fixed by the upper contour, rather than by an unspecified regularization.

On the positive real axis K_b is real, and its odd continuation has residue

c_b^rem=-b^2/24

at zero. Write c=c_b^rem in the following formulas; this is not the usual quantum-dilogarithm constant i(b+b^(-1))/2. Taylor expansion, uniformly for 0<b<=1, gives

K_b(t)=c/t+O(b^2 t)                    (0<t<=1).

For t>=1 use |1/sinh v-1/v|<=C v for v>0, obtaining

|K_b(t)|<=C b^2 exp(-t).

Define H_b(t)=K_b(t)-c exp(-t)/t. Then

integral_0^infinity |H_b(t)| exp((1-delta/pi)t) dt
    <= C_delta b^2.

The clockwise upper indentation at zero contributes -i pi c. Pairing the positive and negative halves of the real contour therefore yields the exact, ordinary convergent formula

R_b(z)=-2 i [ integral_0^infinity H_b(t) sin(z t/pi) dt
                     + c arctan(z/pi)] - i pi c.          (2.3)

Here integral exp(-t) sin(z t/pi)/t dt=arctan(z/pi), with the branch analytic on |Im z|<pi. This arctangent is bounded on each smaller closed strip, including as Re z tends to either infinity. The preceding weighted L1 bound controls the remaining integral uniformly on that strip. This proves (2.2). QED.

Two normalization checks follow directly:

R_b(0)=i pi b^2/24,
R_b(z)+R_b(-z)=i pi b^2/12.                              (2.4)

Together with Li_2(-ez)+Li_2(-e(-z))=-pi2/6-z2/2, they give the exact inversion identity, initially on the common strip,

Phi_b(Z) Phi_b(-Z)
   = exp(i pi Z^2+i pi(b^2+b^(-2))/12).                  (2.5)

The scalar in (2.5) has modulus one for b>0. It has not been silently discarded in an operator or state comparison.

2.2 Functional equation independently recovered

Subtract the logarithms (2.1) at Z-i b/2 and Z+i b/2. The factor sinh(bw) cancels, leaving

-1/2 integral_(R+i0) exp(-2 i b Z t)/(t sinh t) dt.

Its derivative in Z is 2 pi b exp(2 pi b Z)/(1+exp(2 pi b Z)), by the same rectangle evaluation of J. Both it and log(1+exp(2 pi b Z)) vanish at the left end of the strip. Hence

Phi_b(Z-i b/2)
   =(1+exp(2 pi b Z)) Phi_b(Z+i b/2).                    (2.6)

The second functional equation follows by b<->b^(-1) in (2.1). Thus neither the functional equation nor the normalization in (2.2) is imported from an unchecked asymptotic theorem.

3. The exact literature figure-eight state

Equation (38) of Andersen–Kashaev defines

chi_b(x)=integral_(R-i0) Phi_b(x-y)/Phi_b(y)
                            exp(2 pi i x(2y-x)) dy.      (3.1)

In the level-one convention used for the Andersen–Malusà boundary operators,

J_b(x)=exp(4 pi i c_b x) chi_b(x),
c_b=i(b+b^(-1))/2.                                     (3.2)

At x=0 the two states agree. Our volume theorem refers to this specified state, rather than an arbitrarily chosen solution of a difference equation.

The level identification can be checked directly from the definition, not just its functional equations: the finite product (122) in Complex Quantum Chern–Simons has j=0,…,N-1 and argument

x/sqrt(N)+(1-1/N)c_b-i b^(-1)j/N-i b{(j+n)/N}.

At N=1 and n=0 it is the single factor Phi_b(x). The change n->-n in Andersen–Malusà is trivial at N=1, the Haar measure becomes ordinary Lebesgue measure, and the Gaussian is exp(pi i y^2). Their Gaussian ratio equals exp(2 pi i x(2y-x)). The algebra below also derives the operator directly from (3.1)–(3.2), so it does not need an assumed general-N identification or a general-N proof.

Theorem 3.1. As b tends to zero through positive real values,

J_b(0)=chi_b(0)
  = 3^(-1/4) exp(-Vol(S^3\4_1)/(2 pi b^2)) (1+o(1)).    (3.3)

In particular

lim -2 pi b^2 log|J_b(0)|
  = lim -2 pi hbar log|J_b(0)|
  = Vol(S^3\4_1)=2 D(exp(i pi/3)).                       (3.4)

Here D is the Bloch–Wigner dilogarithm. The geometric identification is the two-regular-ideal-tetrahedron decomposition of the complete figure-eight complement; its six tetrahedral edge angles around each edge sum to 2 pi.

Proof. Set u=2 pi b y and eta=2 pi/3. Move the contour to

u=t-i eta,  -infinity<t<infinity.

Both u and -u remain in the fixed strip |Im u|<pi, where the defining logarithms are holomorphic and nonzero. Equation (2.2) gives

chi_b(0)=1/(2 pi b) integral_R
   exp(F(t)/(2 pi b^2))
   exp(R_b(-u)-R_b(u)) dt,
F(t)=[Li_2(-exp(-u))-Li_2(-exp u)]/i.                    (3.5)

The last exponential is 1+O(b^2), uniformly for all t. The classical action S(u)=i F(t) satisfies

S'(u)=log(1+exp(-u))+log(1+exp u),
S'(-i eta)=0,
S''(-i eta)=-i sqrt(3).

On the stated line, the sum of logarithms is the continuous logarithm of 2+2 cosh(t-i eta). For t>0 that number has negative imaginary part, since its imaginary part is -sqrt(3) sinh t. For t<0 its imaginary part is positive. It follows that Re F has its unique global maximum at t=0, with

F(0)=-2 D(exp(i pi/3))=-V,
F''(0)=-sqrt(3).

The real derivative tends to -eta at the positive end and +eta at the negative end. Thus Re F(t)<=-V-c|t| outside a compact interval. This gives absolute convergence and uniform control of the noncompact tails.

For completeness, (R-i0) in (3.1) can first be any small negative horizontal displacement. Between two strictly negative scaled heights in (-pi,0), the same tail computation is uniform; the vertical ends of the deformation vanish. This proves contour independence and then its prescribed lower boundary value. No poles are crossed in moving to the chosen line.

On a small interval around zero, expand the holomorphic F to second order. Its quadratic term is real and negative. Outside that interval the strict maximum and linear tails give an exponentially smaller contribution. Scaling t=b v and dominated convergence gives

integral exp(F(t)/(2 pi b^2)) dt
   = exp(-V/(2 pi b^2)) (2 pi b)/3^(1/4) (1+o(1)).

The uniform O(b^2) quantum amplitude cannot change or cancel this nonzero leading Gaussian term. Multiplying by 1/(2 pi b) proves (3.3). QED.

This is a fixed ideal geometric saddle, separated from the quantum poles by a fixed gap in scaled coordinates. It is not the shrinking compact tetrahedron fold in Section 11 of the user’s manuscript. No uniform passage through that fold has been used.

4. Exact quantum A-operator and minimality for the figure-eight state

Let I(x,y) be the integrand of (3.1), without the outside factor in (3.2). Put Q_1=exp(-2 pi b x), Q_2=exp(-2 pi b y), and let E_1,E_2 shift x,y downward by i b. From (2.6) and the elementary Gaussian shift,

E_1 I=q Q_1^2 Q_2^(-2)(1+q^(-1/2)Q_1^(-1)Q_2) I,
E_2 I=Q_1^(-2)(1+q^(1/2)Q_1^(-1)Q_2)^(-1)
                     (1+q^(-1/2)Q_2^(-1))^(-1) I.        (4.1)

This independently verifies the local input to the literature elimination. Clearing denominators in the proper order gives exactly g_1,g_2 in the v3 source. The explicit certificate

q^(9/2) Q_1^2 P=q a_1 g_1-a_2 g_2

was independently expanded in the four-generator Ore algebra. The executable certificate, including every coefficient, is experiments/audit_ore_certificate.py; its residual is identically zero.

This check alone does not justify integrating shifts. To do that, first take b sufficiently small, x in a small neighborhood of zero in the scaled variable X=2 pi b x, and the contour used in Section 3. All the finitely many shifts in P lie in a fixed smaller holomorphic strip. The action at X=0 has strictly decaying linear tails, and these tails persist uniformly for small complex X and small contour translations. Therefore vertical boundary terms vanish. Integrating E_2^j I gives the same integral as I; E_1 can be brought outside the integral. This proves

P_q(E,Q,1) chi_b=0.

The shift of the outside factor in (3.2) is q times that factor. Consequently the polynomial q P_q(q^(-1)E,Q,1) annihilates J_b. In normal order it is

Ahat_q=a_2(Q) E^2+a_1(Q) E+a_0(Q),
a_2=q^2 Q^2(q Q^2-1),
a_1=-(q^2 Q^2-1)
      [q^4 Q^4-q^3 Q^3-q(q^2+1)Q^2-q Q+1],
a_0=q^2 Q^2(q^3 Q^2-1).                                 (4.2)

The identity holds on the just-proved nonempty parameter domain. It extends by analytic continuation wherever the same literature state and operators are continued. This last wording is deliberate: a newly chosen contour after crossing poles is not automatically the same continued state.

4.1 Minimality is a separate argument

We now prove that the state cannot have a rational first-order annihilator. This avoids inferring minimality from the elimination output.

In scaled variables X=2 pi b x, u=2 pi b y the classical exponent numerator for chi_b is

S(X,u)=Li_2(-exp(X-u))-Li_2(-exp u)-X(2u-X).               (4.3)

The saddle at (X,u)=(0,-2 pi i/3) is nondegenerate. The implicit function theorem gives a holomorphic saddle u(X) for small complex X. The gap at the maximum, the linear tails, and the fixed strip gap in Section 3 persist in a small complex neighborhood of X=0. Deform the contour a small amount to pass through u(X). The same Gaussian argument, uniformly on compact subsets of this neighborhood, proves a nonzero leading asymptotic and therefore

J_b(x-i b)/J_b(x) -> ell(Q),    Q=exp(-X),                (4.4)
ell=exp(-partial_X S(X,u(X))).

The prefactor exp(4 pi i c_b x) in (3.2) contributes q to this ratio and hence does not change (4.4). The derivative of the saddle itself does not enter, because partial_u S=0.

Put Y=exp(-u(X)). Exponentiating the critical equation and evaluating the derivative in (4.4) gives

Q Y^2+(Q^2+Q-1)Y+Q^2=0,
ell=(Q^2+QY)/Y^2.

Eliminating Y, for Q^2!=1 and then by analytic continuation, gives

Q^2 ell^2-F(Q) ell+Q^2=0,
F(Q)=Q^4-Q^3-2Q^2-Q+1.                                 (4.5)

Its discriminant is

F(Q)^2-4Q^4
  =(Q-1)^2(Q+1)^2(Q^2-3Q+1)(Q^2+Q+1),                  (4.6)

which is not a square in C(Q). The branch ell(Q) cannot be a rational function, even though it is holomorphic locally at the complete point.

Suppose (r_1(Q,q)E+r_0(Q,q))J_b=0 with rational coefficients. Clear denominators and remove their common vanishing power at q^(1/2)=1 (or q=1 when coefficients are rational in q). At least one specialized coefficient at q=1 is nonzero. Taking (4.4) would give

r_1(Q,1) ell(Q)+r_0(Q,1)=0,

contradicting (4.6). A nonzero order-zero annihilator is excluded by (3.3). The coefficients in (4.2) have no common nonunit factor at generic q. Thus (4.2), up to the declared Laurent units and a choice of sign, is the primitive minimal-degree operator of Definition 5 for this state. This argument is over the rational q-difference coefficient field; it does not assert new Hilbert-space domain or all-level-N results.

4.2 The full classical peripheral factor

The exact specialization of (4.2) is

Ahat_1=(Q^2-1)[Q^2 E^2-F(Q)E+Q^2].                      (4.7)

The primitive generic-q coefficients acquire a common factor only in the classical limit. Dividing by Q^2-1 and then setting Q=1 is not the same operation as specializing the full operator at Q=1. In particular this is a concrete example of why the user’s warning about peripheral factors matters.

The independently established volume statement is (3.4) for the state of this minimal operator, with its normalization fixed by (3.1)–(3.2). It does not assign a volume rate to every solution of (4.2).

5. A broad state-integral theorem, with explicit certificates

We now use the existing integral (4.5) of Ben Aribi–Wong, rather than choosing a new positive integrand. The theorem below provides sufficient data that can be checked without accepting their asymptotic proof.

Let X be an ordered ideal triangulation with N tetrahedra. To avoid confusing the two pairs of matrices, write script-A, script-B for face matrices and A,B for the N-by-N Neumann–Zagier matrices. Supply the following certificates:

  1. script-A is invertible and the face construction gives a real symmetric Gaussian matrix Q_f. Let

    H=Q_f-diag((epsilon_j+1)/4),    G=-2H.

  2. B is invertible and the finite identity G=B^(-1)A holds. This is the matrix identity in the literature FAMED definition.

  3. Positive complete shapes z_j in the upper half plane, with compatible logarithms, satisfy

    A Log z+B Log z''=i nu,      nu in pi Z^N,

    and the remaining edge and meridian completeness equations. The tetrahedra are the actual geometric triangulation, so sum D(z_j)=Vol(M).

  4. For the boundary coordinate of the literature integral, set

    p=2 B^(-1)e_N.

    Supply a rational constant gamma for which

    p^T(-Log z+i pi 1)=i pi gamma.                        (5.1)

    It can be checked directly at the geometric solution, or obtained from the symplectic peripheral certificate described below. Choose p_1!=0 by relabeling.

In this notation the literature integral at its complete boundary value is

J_X(hbar,0)=C_X (2 pi sqrt(hbar))^(-N)
   integral exp((i y^T H y+c^T y)/(2 pi hbar))
             product_j Phi_b(y_j/(2 pi sqrt(hbar)))
             dy_2 ... dy_N,
c=B^(-1)nu-G pi 1,
y_1=(i pi gamma-sum_(j>=2) p_j y_j)/p_1.                (5.2)

Use the exact nonzero constant C_X of the chosen literature convention and the horizontal contour through y*=-Log z+i pi 1. Its imaginary coordinates all lie in (0,pi). Thus the source’s angle structure can be chosen to be the actual complete geometric angle structure.

Normalization detail. Equation (4.5) as printed has C_X=p_1/det(script-A). The displayed preceding change of variables x=p^T y-i pi gamma has Jacobian p_1, so directly disintegrating the full Lebesgue measure instead gives 1/(p_1 det(script-A)), with signs depending on orientation conventions. This constant discrepancy needs reconciliation in an exact state comparison. The subsequent faddeev_pentagon_audit.md, Section 7.1, makes the conversion explicit: J_coarea=J_printed/p_1^2, with orientations fixed consistently. This is also independent of which coordinate is eliminated when the coarea fiber form is used. For the complex identity with positive Lebesgue disintegration, the orientation sign is sign(det(script-A)p_1). The full tensor phase and its exact state-recovery formula are derived in peripheral_phase_and_state.md. It is present also in the v2 TeX source, lines 3277–3292; it is not a PDF extraction artifact. The theorem uses the printed integral as its fixed definition; either explicit nonzero constant has the same volume limit. No boundary-dependent or exponentially significant factor is hidden in that observation.

Theorem 5.1. For the exact integral (5.2), with these certificates,

lim_(b->0+) -2 pi hbar log|J_X(hbar,0)|=Vol(M).           (5.3)

The integral is nonzero for sufficiently small b. The conclusion is not conditioned on a presumed asymptotic formula or absence of cancellations.

5.1 Finite identities and geometric saddle

The kinematical reduction requires no stationary phase: integrating delta(script-A f+script-B t) in the face variables f gives

K_X(t)=|det(script-A)|^(-1) exp(2 pi i t^T Q_f t).

The determinant follows from an ordinary invertible linear change of variables. Formula (2.5) converts negative and positive tetrahedral factors to the common product of Phi_b used in (5.2), with the explicit unit-modulus constants retained when reconstructing the whole partition function. The face identity G=B^(-1)A is a supplied finite matrix identity, not a consequence of calling a triangulation geometric. The subsequent face_edge_stokes_identity.md proves its edge rows for every globally branched triangulation with an invertible face matrix; the specified peripheral row and its completion remain separate requirements. The subsequent peripheral-phase note explicitly reconstructs those constants and the complete angle-dependent phase, rather than using the magnitude formula as a state identity.

Condition (5.1) can be certified using rational matrices C,D with

A D^T-B C^T=Id,
B^(-1)A symmetric,
2 e_N^T(C Log z+D Log z'')=H_m+i pi nu_m.

Indeed B^(-T)=D G-C, so at the complete gluing solution

p^T y*=H_m+i pi nu_m
        -2 i e_N^T D B^(-1)nu
        +2 i pi e_N^T B^(-T)1.

Here H_m=0 by completeness and the other coefficients give gamma explicitly. This reconstructs the step needed from the symplectic peripheral data. The existence of particular C,D can be checked as part of the certificate; their geometric interpretation is not inferred merely from invertibility of B.

The classical phase of (5.2) is

F_0(y)=i y^T H y+c^T y-i sum_j Li_2(-exp y_j).

Its gradient is

grad F_0=-i G y+c+i Log(1+exp y).

At y=-Log z+i pi 1, Log(1+exp y)=Log z’’. Hence

grad F_0(y*)=i B^(-1)(A Log z+B Log z'')+B^(-1)nu=0.     (5.4)

This is a full critical point before restricting to the boundary fiber.

For y=h+i d, direct use of the definition of the Bloch–Wigner function gives

Re F_0(y)=h dot Re(grad F_0(y))+sum_j D(-exp y_j).

At y, -exp y_j=1/z_j, and D(1/z)=-D(z). Therefore

Re F_0(y*)=-sum_j D(z_j)=-Vol(M).                        (5.5)

Only elementary identities of the dilogarithm and the checked gluing equations enter this critical-value calculation.

5.2 A uniform bound, the hbar/b^2 distinction, and no cancellation

It would be incorrect to replace sqrt(hbar) by b inside Phi_b without accounting for the error. Put s=b^2 and define the exact classical part

F_s(y)=i y^T H y+c^T y
         -i/(1+s)^2 sum_j Li_2(-exp((1+s)y_j)).          (5.6)

Since hbar/b2=(1+s)(-2), (2.2) gives the exact integrand representation

exp(F_s(y)/(2 pi hbar)) a_b(y),
a_b(y)=exp(sum_j R_b((1+s)y_j)).                         (5.7)

On any horizontal fiber with all (1+s)Im y_j in a fixed compact subset of (0,pi), a_b=1+O(s) uniformly over all its real directions.

Parametrize the boundary fiber by y=y*+T u, u in C^(N-1), where T is the real, full-column-rank matrix implementing (5.2). On a horizontal contour its real Hessian is

Hess_u Re F_s
   = -T^T diag( Im[exp((1+s)y_j)/(1+exp((1+s)y_j))] ) T. (5.8)

Every diagonal entry inside the bracket is strictly positive, because 0<(1+s)Im y_j<pi. Hence (5.8) is negative definite at every finite real point. At s=0, the complex Hessian restricted to the fiber is invertible: its Hermitian real part is negative definite. The holomorphic implicit function theorem therefore gives a critical point u_s=O(s) of the restricted F_s. The contour can be translated a small complex amount to u_s+R^(N-1); all imaginary coordinates keep a fixed strip gap.

Here are the global bounds needed for this translation and for Gaussian asymptotics. On the translated contour, Re F_s has zero gradient at its critical point and is strictly concave everywhere. On the unit ball its negative Hessian has a uniform positive lower bound for sufficiently small s. Along any outward ray concavity makes the radial derivative nonincreasing. Consequently there is c>0, independent of small s, such that

Re F_s(y_s+T v)
   <= Re F_s(y_s)-c min(|v|^2,|v|),  v in R^(N-1).      (5.9)

The same linear tail bound, with adjusted constants, holds during the small contour translation. The quantum amplitude is uniformly bounded by (5.7). Thus all boundary faces at infinity vanish in the contour deformation, and the noncompact tails are uniformly negligible in stationary phase.

The Gaussian matrix has negative definite real part. Its Gaussian integral is nonzero, with the square root of its determinant fixed by continuation from real negative definite matrices. Equations (5.7)–(5.9) therefore give

J_X(hbar,0)
  = C_X/(2 pi sqrt(hbar))
      exp(F_s(y_s)/(2 pi hbar))
      /sqrt(det(-Hess_u F_s(y_s))) (1+o(1)).             (5.10)

In particular a cancellation between unrelated saddles has not been assumed away: the contour has a unique strict maximum, its local Gaussian is nonzero, and the rest is exponentially smaller. Since F_s(y_s)->F_0(y*) and hbar tends to zero, (5.5) and (5.10) prove (5.3). QED.

The N=1 case, if applicable, is a zero-dimensional fiber and follows directly from (5.7); the determinant and Gaussian integration are then omitted.

5.3 What this proves about a broad knot class

For every knot exterior with a supplied positive geometric FAMED triangulation and compatible peripheral certificates, the published integral (4.5) has the volume law (5.3), by the above argument. The theorem does not depend on a claimed census computation or a claimed proof that a family has these certificates. Such membership claims need their own verification.

The subsequent twist-family note supplies uniform face, angle and geometric certificates for the explicit literature families. Its topological companion reconstructs the diagrammatic identification with the named twist knots and independently identifies the meridian and preferred longitude, including their branches. General two-bridge and alternating families are not yet covered by those formulas. Menasco’s checkerboard polyhedra provide combinatorics; their existence alone does not supply the positive shape solution and matrix identity in items 1–4. A claim that they are actual all-right-angled cells of the complete metric requires additional hypotheses.

Theorem 5.1 is a theorem about the specified literature state integral. The subsequent note general_annihilator_and_volume.md supplies a finite dimension-count and exponential-moment proof of annihilator existence in the Andersen–Malusà b-shift algebra for these rescaled integrals, as well as their classical geometric component. The canonical knot-state identification and the different finite-b operators of the FAMED paper remain separate questions. twist_family_audit.md supplies uniform geometric and matrix formulas for the two explicit twist face families; the topological companion identifies those families and their peripheral rows. Invariance of the whole state under all triangulation changes, including the correct boundary factors, also requires its own exact identities.
