One positive coefficient for any fixed number of physical rows

Private written proof, 17 November 2026.

The original trace of a product of physical value rows has an exact one-variable coefficient representation. Its generating function is a product of linear factors and reciprocal linear factors, all with positive coefficients. Exponential tilting therefore turns the trace into the probability that a sum of Bernoulli and geometric random variables equals a prescribed integer.

This representation gives a uniform nonzero leading coefficient for any fixed number of rows, including rows at the midpoint. It also evaluates every fixed-degree polynomial inserted into that trace. It does not estimate a crossing power or an inserted boundary derivative: their degrees or coefficients depend on the level.

3. Uniform control up to the midpoint

Fix m≥3m\ge3 and ε>0\varepsilon>0. 4. A uniform leading coefficient

5. Fixed-degree observations in the original trace

Let q(C)q(C) be any fixed polynomial. Formula (6) gives exactly ϕN(q(C)∏iFji(C))Γ𝒋(m)=[xk]{H(x)q(c0−x−1)}[xk]H(x).(20) \frac{\phi_N(q(C)\prod_i F_{j_i}(C))} {\Gamma_{\mathbf j}^{(m)}} =\frac{[x^k]\{H(x)q(c_0-x^{-1})\}}{[x^k]H(x)} . \qquad\text{(20)} The numerator uses only finitely many adjacent coefficients of HH. Their ratios are given by (16). Thus ϕN(q(C)∏iFji(C))Γ𝒋(m)=q(c0−ρ−1)+Oq(N−1).(21) \frac{\phi_N(q(C)\prod_i F_{j_i}(C))} {\Gamma_{\mathbf j}^{(m)}} =q(c_0-\rho^{-1})+O_q(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(21)} This holds uniformly on (11); no derivative at a repeated root has been dropped in taking the trace. It also holds uniformly for polynomials of bounded degree with bounded coefficients. If |q(c0−ρ−1)|≥δ>0|q(c_0-\rho^{-1})|\ge\delta>0, the observed trace has a nonzero leading coefficient and the same exponential rate as Γ𝒋(m)\Gamma_{\mathbf j}^{(m)}.

The qualifier about bounded degree is essential. The original crossing RpR^p, even for fixed pp, is represented in the finite algebra by a polynomial whose degree grows with NN. The Fourier cancellations in that trace are not covered by (16) or (21). Likewise replacing a physical value row by its independent boundary derivative changes the positive factorization used in (3).
