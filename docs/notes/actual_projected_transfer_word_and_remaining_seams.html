The projected transfer word, its degree repair, and its remaining source seams

28 October 2026. The two projected outer-collar theorems are genuine original-operator contour identities. They can be inserted simultaneously in a fixed signed word. A contractive degree average makes the entire off-grid word preserve the original integer degree, without changing its integral or its collar estimates.

This does not yet reduce the sharp bound to the known regular critical regulator theorem. The retained physical interpolation still has Fourier interval currents and fails the other crossing Ward equations pointwise. This is a limitation of THIS phase representation, not an impossibility of tame linear projections onto the intertwiner space.

1. Exact full word and a cost-free degree average

Use Rhat_sigma(z) of the lower and upper collar theorems. It acts on the ORIGINAL orthonormal two-head space H_N tensor H_N. Every physical input index remains an integer in 0,…,d, d=N-1. The full original Cartan factor kappa is included. Write D|a,b>=(a+b)|a,b> and

E_D(A)=sum_T P_T A P_T
      =integral_0^(2pi) exp(itD) A exp(-itD) dt/(2pi),
B_sigma(z)=E_D(Rhat_sigma(z)).                    (1)

This is a completely positive contraction in operator norm and a contraction in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. It is a fixed linear map of the matrix entries, so holomorphy, removable zeros, and the collar bounds survive unchanged. The integer transfer slices are also graded, hence E_D fixes EACH original integer slice in this case.

Consequently, for any of the enlarged finite-height rectangles Gamma_i,

R_sigma=integral_(Gamma_i) pi cot(pi z) B_sigma(z) dz/(2pi i).

For a fixed braid word the SAME original scalar is exactly

K_N=chi_(beta,N) integral_(product Gamma_i)
         Phi_N(B_m(z_m)...B_1(z_1))
                  product_i [pi cot(pi z_i) dz_i/(2pi i)]. (2)

Here B_i is embedded on its actual neighboring heads, all spectators are identities, and Phi_N is the original marked pivotal closure or cup/cap functional. All integrals are compact and all head spaces finite, so their ordered composition and Fubini in (2) are exact.

The product is now degree preserving POINTWISE in every transfer z_i. The original marked cap may therefore be restricted to its forced degree-d fiber after the ordinary caps, exactly as for the original whole word. This removes a real potential source error; the degree leak of the unsymmetrized interpolant is not unavoidable.

For any fixed number of factors the added individual collars have exp(o(N)) operator cost, uniformly over their physical inputs. This is not an estimate on an unrestricted exterior word, which may itself have an exponential norm or scalar period.

2. What the repaired physical kernel actually is

Put

s_c(nu)=[exp(2pi i nu)-1]/[2pi i(nu-c)],
sinc(pi w)=sin(pi w)/(pi w).

The removable values are retained. If T=a+b, the positive kernel (1) is

B_+(z)|a,b>=r(a,b;z)
   sum_(c=max(0,T-d))^(min(d,T))
      sinc^2(pi(b+z-c)) |c,T-c>.                 (3)

Indeed s_(T-c)(a-z)=exp(-2pi iz)s_c(b+z), and the two phase factors cancel in their product, leaving the ENTIRE square in (3). For complex z it is not the modulus square. For real z its coefficients are nonnegative before multiplying by the original scalar r.

The exact Fourier representation is

sinc^2(pi w)=integral_(-1)^1 (1-|t|) exp(2pi iwt)dt. (4)

Thus (2) retains physical interval data even after repairing degree. Splitting (4) into its two analytic intervals leaves endpoints t=+/-1 and a common interface t=0. The weight vanishes at the outer endpoints only to first order; its derivative has a jump at zero. These are not automatically exponentially negligible. Integrating explicitly gives

sinc^2(pi w)=[2-exp(2pi iw)-exp(-2pi iw)]/(4pi^2 w^2), (5)

with the complete removable value at w=0. Splitting the numerator without its joint cancellation would introduce a false double pole.

At an INTERIOR stationary point of (4) the missing output relation b+z=c is restored. At an interval endpoint or interface it is not an available stationarity equation. Such a restricted critical scheme must not simply be substituted into the previously proved FULL original critical ideal.

The existing uniform vector bound exp(2pi|Im nu|) is tame at the FIXED transfer height of the collar theorems. At a macroscopic complex transfer z=N zeta, however, (5) can itself have exponent 2pi N|Im zeta|. This is a leading source phase, not a half-density which may be discarded. For example for w=N xi with Im xi>0 and xi bounded away from zero,

sinc^2(pi N xi)
      =-exp(-2pi iNxi)/(4pi^2N^2xi^2)(1+o(1)).    (6)

The analogous opposite exponential occurs when Im xi<0. These are the original Fourier endpoint sectors. They may subsequently cancel in the complete source, but the outer-collar estimate does not prove that cancellation or identify their coupled stationary support.

A quantitative whole-word bound for the COMPLETE projection

There is a stronger estimate than bounding the individual sinc entries. It controls the complete interval/interface aggregate, with no N-dependent conditioning. Embed the original finite head space isometrically in L^2([0,2pi],dtheta/(2pi)). If S is the head swap and D_r(z) is diagonal on the ORIGINAL finite input basis with entries r(a,b;z), then literally

Rhat_+(z)=P_N^(tensor 2) M_z S D_r(z),
(M_z f)(theta1,theta2)
            =exp(iz(theta1-theta2)) f(theta1,theta2). (6a)

The output coefficients of (6a) are exactly the two sinc vectors, so no boundary source has been changed. Multiplication has norm exp(2pi|Im z|), the swap is isometric and P_N is orthogonal. Thus

||B_+(z)||op <= ||Rhat_+(z)||op
          <=exp(2pi|Im z|) max_(a,b physical)|r(a,b;z)|. (6b)

The same holds for the literal negative antiunitary mirror. In particular REAL-transfer projection is contractive: it adds NO N factor and no exponential rate to the scalar diagonal coefficient envelope. This is not an estimate on a new norm or a changed source.

There is also an exact sharp absolute-column version. For nu=u+iv,

sum_(c in Z) |sinc^2(pi(nu-c))|
                   =sinh(2pi|v|)/(2pi|v|),        (6c)

with value 1 at v=0. Indeed the original Fourier coefficients satisfy |s_c(nu)|^2=exp(-2pi v)|sinc^2(pi(nu-c))|. Plancherel for exp(i nu theta) gives sum |s_c|^2=(1-exp(-4pi v))/(4pi v), which proves (6c). Restricting to the finite allowed c in (3) only decreases the sum. Thus (3)’s physical output column has l1 norm at most |r| times the right side of (6c). At real transfer the weights form a subprobability distribution.

For any original b-head one-cut source, or fixed-width marked plat, its actual functional norm is polynomial in N at complete color. Tensoring the local operators with spectators changes none of (6b). Consequently the COMPLETE interval sources in any fixed word satisfy the genuine bound

|Phi_N(B_m(z_m)...B_1(z_1))|
  <=poly_beta(N) exp(2pi sum_i |Im z_i|)
                   product_i max_(a,b)|r_sigma_i(a,b;z_i)|. (6d)

Every original cup/cap normalization and the explicit external chi in (2) is retained. This is a quantitative full-source statement, not a claim that separately cut Fourier sectors obey the same bound. On fixed-height cotangent contours, the projection cost in (6d) is O_T(1)^m. Their lengths, cotangent bounds and fixed word length add only polynomial factors. Thus the endpoint/interface aggregate does not create a new exponential envelope ON THOSE CONTOURS.

Conversely the macroscopic complex cost in (6b) cannot be replaced by a uniform polynomial for this same scalar interpolation. If z=N zeta, Im zeta!=0, and Re(b+z) lies in the physical output band, choose an allowed c with |b+Re z-c|<=1. Formula (3) then has an individual output coefficient of size

|r(a,b;z)| exp(2pi N|Im zeta|)/poly(N),           (6e)

provided its total-degree block contains that c. For example a+b=d contains every physical c. The denominator in (5) is O(N^2), while its numerator has the stated exponential size. Hence the rate 2pi|Im zeta| is sharp already for the retained physical kernel. This does not prove it survives the exterior knot pairing; it proves that such a cancellation must be retained in a sharp complex-contour argument rather than absorbed into a polynomial projection norm.

3. An exact remaining E-coproduct defect

Use the original complete scaled currents

Ecal|a>=(Q^-a-1)|a-1>, K|a>=x Q^-a|a>,
E=Ecal tensor K + I tensor Ecal,
q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, x=-q^-1.

The original complete R commutes with E. Its repaired interpolation B_+(z) does not. An exact entry at z=1/2 is

<0,0|[E,B_+(1/2)]|1,0>
  =(Q^-1-1) r(0,0;1/2) (4/pi^2) (1-Q+q^-1).     (7)

To check it, the two degree-one output weights in (3) are both 4/pi^2, while

r(1,0;z)/r(0,0;z)
      =x^-1 (1-Q)/(1-Q^(1-z)).

At z=1/2 this gives (7) directly. It is nonzero at EVERY N>=2: r(0,0;1/2)=kappa x^(1/2)/Fhat(-1/2) is nonzero, and 1-Q+q^-1=0 would imply q^3=q+1. The latter is impossible since |q+1|>=sqrt(2)>1. Therefore degree repair alone does not turn the pointwise five-function density into the complete crossing Ward solution.

This defect is visible to an ORIGINAL source. At N=2 use precisely the balanced cup and FULL marked row

p_(a,b)=2 x^(a-1) delta_(a+b,1),
m_(a,b)=x^b/4,  x=i.

A direct four-state calculation gives

m [E,B_+(1/2)] p
      =(-16-52i)/(9pi^2 Fhat(-1/2)) != 0.          (8)

All original kappa and x phases are included in this formula. A marked-fiber replacement must not be applied AFTER the degree-lowering insertion E; (8) uses the full marked row. In contrast, the complete cotangent integral of this commutator is [E,R]=0. Its compensating source currents are therefore essential to any argument based on pointwise Ward equations. This is not a new nonzero knot invariant with an arbitrarily inserted coupon; it tests the proposed density identity before the exact integral is assembled.

4. Tame projections exist; they do not supply a free phase theorem

No impossibility of a further tame algebraic repair follows from (7). For example the Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal projection onto the common E,F,K intertwiner space always exists, has HS norm one, and has operator-to-operator norm at most N on a two-head space of dimension N^2. It preserves the complete R integral and every added zero. It need not preserve the separate original transfer residues. More importantly E,F,K intertwiners need not implement the specified braid crossing: they also include other functions of the two-head Casimir. Their phases and canonical relations have to be derived afresh.

The same warning applies to the full Hurwitz intertwiner line. If the complete physical algebra makes that solution space span{R}, its HS projection is exactly

P_R(A)=<R,A>_HS R / ||R||_HS^2.                  (9)

It is still tame as a linear operator. At an integer transfer k the scalar coefficient is

lambda_k=||R_k||_HS^2/||R||_HS^2, sum_k lambda_k=1,

because the original transfer slices have disjoint matrix-entry supports. But the entire known exponential crossing matrix R is now a CONSTANT factor in the density P_R(Rhat(z))=lambda_N(z)R. The original word asymptotic is not obtained by analyzing the harmless scalar lambda alone. Such a projection changes the phase representation and packages its difficulty into the retained R factor; it does not contradict (7) or provide the requested sharp geometric bound.

5. Critical-path conclusion

The theorem removes both artificial OUTER transfer collars, with the physical projections and source retained. Formula (2) is an exact full-word representation, and (1) repairs its pointwise degree at no norm cost. This is substantive source control.

What is not yet proved is a bulk-only estimate with exponential rate at most the knot volume. The regular critical-regulator theorem applies to the genuine full crossing/gluing equations. To use it here one must either control the complete Fourier interval currents in (4)–(6), show their coupled stationary support belongs to that actual saturated graph, or give a different phase-preserving source transport. Keeping the finite physical head sums avoids these new complex-head seams, but then the remaining positive matrix bound still loses the global outer phase and is not sharp by knot volume. These alternatives are not supplied by the two local collar estimates alone.

Audit: exact degree-averaged columns and contraction, original R and E Ward, the all-level entry (7), the full original source (8), the retained triangle Fourier identity, and the exact full-intertwiner projection weights. No sharp bound for a knot family is inferred.
