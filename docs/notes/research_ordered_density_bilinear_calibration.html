A full-matrix inequality for bilinear composition

22 November 2026. Research note; the public manuscripts remain frozen.

A surjective bilinear tensor admits explicit affine lower bounds in the logarithms of arbitrary positive input and output metrics. The density matrices in these bounds belong to a specified spectral family. They are independent dual witnesses, rather than the actual marginals of a tensor after a common choice of gauges.

The proof applies to the original anchored Conway multiplication, including every Hermite channel. It supplies local inequalities for the bounded-window certificate, but does not construct matching geometric densities or evaluate their constants at the volume scale.

1. Ordered densities

All spaces are finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces. A density matrix is positive semidefinite with trace one. Say that a density ρ\rho is ordered with a Hermitian matrix KK if they have a common orthonormal eigenbasis in which K=diag⁡(ki),ρ=diag⁡(wi),(wi−wj)(ki−kj)≥0for all i,j.(1) K=\operatorname{diag}(k_i),\qquad \rho=\operatorname{diag}(w_i),\qquad (w_i-w_j)(k_i-k_j)\ge0\quad\text{for all }i,j. \qquad\text{(1)} Thus [ρ,K]=0[\rho,K]=0 is required. Repeated eigenvalues cause no ambiguity: diagonalize ρ\rho within each eigenspace of KK. Examples are the normalized identity, a normalized projection onto the largest eigenvalues, and ρ=eαK/tr⁡(eαK)\rho=e^{\alpha K}/\operatorname{tr}(e^{\alpha K}) for α≥0\alpha\ge0. More generally, a nonnegative nondecreasing function of KK, normalized to trace one, is allowed. An arbitrary density need not satisfy (1). Within a tied eigenspace (1) need not make ρ\rho a scalar function of KK.

We write tr\operatorname{tr}, ∥⋅∥op\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{ op}}, and ∥⋅∥HS\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{ HS}} for trace, operator norm, and Hilbert–Schmidt norm. All matrix logarithms below are those of strictly positive matrices.

2. Logarithms and compression

Scalar integration in a spectral basis gives the norm-convergent identity log⁡B=∫0∞{(1+t)−1I−(B+tI)−1}dt.(2) \log B=\int_0^\infty \bigl\{(1+t)^{-1}I-(B+tI)^{-1}\bigr\}\,dt. \qquad\text{(2)} Inversion reverses positive-matrix order, as follows by conjugating with a positive square root. Consequently (2) proves that 0<B≤C0<B\le C implies log⁡B≤log⁡C\log B\le\log C. No order-preservation assertion about exponentiation is needed.

Let V:𝒦→ℋV:\mathcal K\to\mathcal H be a coisometry: VV*=IℋVV^*=I_{\mathcal H}. For B>0B>0, a Schur complement gives V(B+tI)−1V*≥(VBV*+tI)−1(t≥0).(3) V(B+tI)^{-1}V^*\ \ge\ (VBV^*+tI)^{-1}\quad(t\ge0). \qquad\text{(3)} Indeed decompose 𝒦=ran⁡V*⊕ker⁡V\mathcal K=\operatorname{ran}V^*\oplus\ker V. The left side is the upper block of the inverse of B+tIB+tI, namely (B11+tI−B12(B22+tI)−1B21)−1(B_{11}+tI-B_{12}(B_{22}+tI)^{-1}B_{21})^{-1}. The subtracted Schur correction is positive, so this is at least (B11+tI)−1(B_{11}+tI)^{-1}. If ker⁡V=0\ker V=0, equality holds directly. Integrating (3) in (2), with the constant terms cancelled and VV*=IVV^*=I, proves log⁡(VBV*)≥V(log⁡B)V*.(4) \log(VBV^*)\ge V(\log B)V^*. \qquad\text{(4)} The differences in these integrals converge in norm.

3. A positive factor and an ordered density

Lemma 1. Let D=eK>0D=e^K>0, and let ρ\rho satisfy (1). For every B>0B>0, tr⁡{ρlog⁡(DBD)}≥tr⁡(ρlog⁡B)+2tr⁡(ρK).(5) \boxed{\quad \operatorname{tr}\{\rho\log(DBD)\} \ge \operatorname{tr}(\rho\log B) +2\operatorname{tr}(\rho K). \quad} \qquad\text{(5)}

Proof. Put Bs=esKBesKB_s=e^{sK}Be^{sK} and Rs,t=(Bs+tI)−1R_{s,t}=(B_s+tI)^{-1}. Differentiating (2), ddstr⁡(ρlog⁡Bs)=∫0∞tr⁡{ρRs,t(KBs+BsK)Rs,t}dt.(6) \frac d{ds}\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log B_s) =\int_0^\infty \operatorname{tr}\{\rho R_{s,t}(KB_s+B_sK)R_{s,t}\}\,dt. \qquad\text{(6)} In the common eigenbasis in (1), use BsRs,t=I−tRs,tB_sR_{s,t}=I-tR_{s,t} and ∫0∞(Rs,t−tRs,t2)dt=I. \int_0^\infty(R_{s,t}-tR_{s,t}^2)\,dt=I. The latter follows by scalar integration on the spectrum of BsB_s. Subtracting 2tr⁡(ρK)2\operatorname{tr}(\rho K) from (6) gives ddstr⁡(ρlog⁡Bs)−2tr⁡(ρK)=2∫0∞t∑i,jwi(ki−kj)|(Rs,t)ij|2dt=∫0∞t∑i,j(wi−wj)(ki−kj)|(Rs,t)ij|2dt≥0.(7) \begin{split} \frac d{ds}\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log B_s) -2\operatorname{tr}(\rho K) &=2\int_0^\infty t\sum_{i,j}w_i(k_i-k_j) |(R_{s,t})_{ij}|^2\,dt\\ &=\int_0^\infty t\sum_{i,j}(w_i-w_j)(k_i-k_j) |(R_{s,t})_{ij}|^2\,dt \ \ge0. \qquad\text{(7)} \end{split} The second equality exchanges i,ji,j in half the sum and uses Hermitian symmetry. The differentiations are justified uniformly for s∈[0,1]s\in[0,1]: the positive matrices BsB_s have common positive lower and finite upper spectral bounds. In the last integral only off-diagonal terms matter. Near infinity they have (Rs,t)ij=O(t−2)(R_{s,t})_{ij}=O(t^{-2}), whereas near zero the resolvent is bounded. Thus the integrand is integrable. Integrating (7) from zero to one proves (5). ▫\square

For an invertible polar decomposition A=UDA=UD, this also gives a unary bound. Put c=∥Ho1/2AHi−1/2∥op2c=\|H_o^{1/2}AH_i^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{ op}}^2. The inequality DU*HoUD≤cHiDU^*H_oUD\le cH_i, logarithmic order, and (5) give log⁡∥Ho1/2AHi−1/2∥HS≥tr⁡(ρlog⁡D)+12tr⁡(UρU*log⁡Ho)−12tr⁡(ρlog⁡Hi).(8) \log\|H_o^{1/2}AH_i^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{ HS}} \ge \operatorname{tr}(\rho\log D) +\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(U\rho U^*\log H_o) -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log H_i). \qquad\text{(8)} Here ρ\rho is ordered with log⁡D\log D. The rectangular result below does not require inserting a separate unary gate.

4. The original surjective binary tensor

Let A:ℋ1⊗ℋ2→ℋoA:\mathcal H_1\otimes\mathcal H_2\to\mathcal H_o be surjective. Define D=(AA*)1/2>0,V=D−1A,VV*=I.(9) D=(AA^*)^{1/2}>0,\qquad V=D^{-1}A,\qquad VV^*=I. \qquad\text{(9)} Choose a density ρ\rho ordered with log⁡D\log D, and put ω=V*ρV,ρ1=tr⁡ℋ2ω,ρ2=tr⁡ℋ1ω,β=tr⁡(ρlog⁡D).(10) \omega=V^*\rho V,\qquad \rho_1=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal H_2}\omega,\qquad \rho_2=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal H_1}\omega,\qquad \beta=\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log D). \qquad\text{(10)} Since VV*=IVV^*=I, ω,ρ1,ρ2\omega,\rho_1,\rho_2 are densities. The joint density ω\omega can be entangled; no product-density replacement is made.

Theorem 2. For arbitrary positive Ho,H1,H2H_o,H_1,H_2, log⁡∥Ho1/2A(H1−1/2⊗H2−1/2)∥HS≥β+12tr⁡(ρlog⁡Ho)−12tr⁡(ρ1log⁡H1)−12tr⁡(ρ2log⁡H2).(11) \boxed{\quad \log\|H_o^{1/2}A(H_1^{-1/2}\otimes H_2^{-1/2})\|_{\mathrm{ HS}} \ge \beta+\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log H_o) -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\rho_1\log H_1) -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\rho_2\log H_2). \quad} \qquad\text{(11)}

Proof. Write Xi=log⁡HiX_i=\log H_i, X=X1⊗I+I⊗X2X=X_1\otimes I+I\otimes X_2, and set T=Ve−XV*>0,c=∥Ho1/2DVe−X/2∥op2>0. T=Ve^{-X}V^*>0,\qquad c=\|H_o^{1/2}DVe^{-X/2}\|_{\mathrm{ op}}^2>0. The definition of cc gives Ho1/2DTDHo1/2≤cIH_o^{1/2}DTD H_o^{1/2}\le cI. Conjugating and inverting gives DHoD≤cT−1.(12) DH_oD\le cT^{-1}. \qquad\text{(12)} Use logarithmic order in (12), then trace against ρ\rho. Lemma 1 on the left, and (4) applied to e−Xe^{-X} on the right, give tr⁡(ρlog⁡Ho)+2β≤log⁡c−tr⁡(ρlog⁡T)≤log⁡c+tr⁡(ρVXV*).(13) \operatorname{tr}(\rho\log H_o)+2\beta \le \log c-\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log T) \le \log c+\operatorname{tr}(\rho VXV^*). \qquad\text{(13)} The last trace equals tr⁡(ρ1X1)+tr⁡(ρ2X2)\operatorname{tr}(\rho_1X_1)+\operatorname{tr}(\rho_2X_2). Since log⁡∥⋅∥HS≥12log⁡c\log\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}\ge\tfrac12\log c, rearrangement proves (11). ▫\square

The metric matrices need not commute. Only the fixed calibration density is restricted by (1). For bilinear tree contractions, the output half-edge density is ρ\rho, and the dual input half-edge densities are ρ1𝖳,ρ2𝖳\rho_1^{\mathsf T},\rho_2^{\mathsf T}. The parent tensor uses Hi−𝖳/2H_i^{-\mathsf T/2} on its dual index; tr⁡(ρi𝖳Xi𝖳)=tr⁡(ρiXi)\operatorname{tr}(\rho_i^{\mathsf T}X_i^{\mathsf T}) =\operatorname{tr}(\rho_iX_i).

5. The full anchored Conway product

For each integer N≥2N\ge2, use the complete Newton coefficient space HN=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2) H_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2) where TNT_N is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. Use the actual normalized Newton basis bib_i, with coefficient vectors ei=bie_i=b_i, and its Euclidean Hermitian norm. It retains all value and first-normal channels at repeated Casimir roots. Its actual multiplication MM has unit b0b_0, hence is surjective. The anchored compiler uses ĤN=ℂe⋆⊕HN,M̂((a,u),(b,v))=(ab,M(u,v)).(14) \widehat H_N=\mathbb C e_\star\oplus H_N,\qquad \widehat M((a,u),(b,v))=(ab,M(u,v)). \qquad\text{(14)} This is surjective because M̂((a,u),(1,b0))=(a,u)\widehat M((a,u),(1,b_0))=(a,u). Thus (9)–(11) apply to the literal tensor without changing a crossing leaf, a quarter-turn, or the original marked closing row. All original framing and peripheral scalars remain prescribed.

Its covariance satisfies M̂M̂*=diag⁡(1,MM*)≥I.(15) \widehat M\widehat M^*=\operatorname{diag}(1,MM^*)\ge I. \qquad\text{(15)} Indeed M(ei⊗e0)=ei=M(e0⊗ei)M(e_i\otimes e_0)=e_i=M(e_0\otimes e_i) in the orthonormal Newton basis. These input columns are distinct except at i=0i=0; their contribution gives MM*≥2I−e0e0*≥IMM^*\ge2I-e_0e_0^*\ge I. Consequently every allowed β\beta in (10) is nonnegative here.

The densities in (10) are independent dual data. They need not be the actual reduced densities of any one gauged tensor. For example the whitened map VV, after Hilbert–Schmidt normalization, has actual output density I/dim⁡ĤNI/\dim\widehat H_N; this does not require choosing that density in (10). No physical channel has been projected away.

There is also an explicit source-dependent change of reference that requires no unknown internal packet. At a literal crossing leaf put a±=(1,R±1)a_\pm=(1,R^{\pm1}) and use the anchored shear S±(a,u)=(a,u−aR±1),S±a±=e⋆,∥S±∥op,∥S±−1∥op≤1+N.(16) S_\pm(a,u)=(a,u-aR^{\pm1}),\qquad S_\pm a_\pm=e_\star,\qquad \|S_\pm\|_{\mathrm{ op}},\ \|S_\pm^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{ op}}\le1+\sqrt N. \qquad\text{(16)} The bound follows from the shear formula and the exact Newton norm ∥R±1∥=N\|R^{\pm1}\|=\sqrt N. Apply S±−1S_\pm^{-1} on the adjacent parent input to preserve the complete contraction. Each affected local logarithmic Hilbert–Schmidt norm changes by at most O(log⁡N)O(\log N); the new parent map remains surjective, so Theorem 2 applies to it. This is a known complex nonunitary reference change, rather than a unitary rephasing. The bound D≥ID\ge I in (15) concerns the original untransformed product, and is not asserted after this change. The shear neither constructs globally matched densities nor proves an upper volume rate.

6. Use on a fixed tree

For the exact anchored source YN=(1,KN)Y_N=(1,K_N), the three complete sources below give regularized minimizing metrics satisfying ∥log⁡He*∥op≤RN=O(N),∥YN∥≤∏v∥Tv,N∥H*≤2∥YN∥.(17) \|\log H_e^*\|_{\mathrm{ op}}\le R_N=O(N),\qquad \|Y_N\|\le\prod_v\|T_{v,N}\|_{H^*}\le2\|Y_N\|. \qquad\text{(17)} One can take RN=LN(I)/ε+log⁡Z(I)R_N=L_N(I)/\varepsilon+\log Z(I), where LN=∑vlog⁡∥Tv,N∥HL_N=\sum_v\log\|T_{v,N}\|_H, Z=1+∑etr⁡(He+He−1)Z=1+\sum_e\operatorname{tr}(H_e+H_e^{-1}), and ε=1/(512m2)\varepsilon=1/(512m^2) for a fixed tree with m≥2m\ge2 vertices. For completeness, regularized stationarity gives total actual marginal mismatch S≤2εS\le2\varepsilon. The capacity comparison is ∥YN∥/∏v∥Tv∥H*≥1−4mS−5mS>1/2\|Y_N\|/\prod_v\|T_v\|_{H^*} \ge1-4m\sqrt S-5mS>1/2 at this value of ε\varepsilon. Also LN(H*)≥log⁡∥YN∥≥0L_N(H^*)\ge\log\|Y_N\|\ge0; comparison of LN+εlog⁡ZL_N+\varepsilon\log Z at H*H^* and II gives log⁡Z(H*)≤RN\log Z(H^*)\le R_N. Each eigenvalue of He*H_e^* and its reciprocal is at most Z(H*)Z(H^*), proving the other part of (17).

Use (11) at products and valid literal leaf, turn, and closing lower bounds on this window. Let δe,N\delta_{e,N} be the trace-norm mismatch between the child density and the transposed parent density. Summing at H*H^* and bounding each residual trace gives log⁡∥YN∥≥∑vβv,N−RN2∑eδe,N−log⁡2.(18) \log\|Y_N\|\ge \sum_v\beta_{v,N}-\frac{R_N}{2}\sum_e\delta_{e,N}-\log2. \qquad\text{(18)} Thus total density error o(1)o(1) costs o(N)o(N) in this logarithmic certificate on a fixed tree. This is not a complex-source error bound.

Theorem 2 supplies an explicit valid family at the original product. It does not prove that this restricted family contains geometric data matching the actual leaves and closing with small error, or that its constants sum to the hyperbolic-volume rate. Source-adapted preconditioning may be needed. A separate full-metric upper trial is also required. No exact entropy dual or new volume theorem is asserted.
