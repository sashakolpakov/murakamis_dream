Pointed descent on the central Kashaev fiber

Research note, 25 October 2026. This supplies a nonzero meridian anchor for the actual literature state and uses it to remove the central comparison ambiguity on the fiber containing the Kashaev evaluation. It also gives a cofactor formula for that evaluation from a specified quantum A-operator when its rational solution line has rank one. It does not prove that rank hypothesis for every knot or establish the regulator norm comparison.

The subsequent all-level A-period theorem removes the rank and regular-orbit conditions from a different exact presentation. It uses cross-level polynomial rigidity, finitely many fixed small colors, and uniformly bounded q-jets. The meridian-jet theorem gives the corresponding integral-kernel bound without assuming rank one.

All conventions and the literature state are those independently fixed in the ADO pointing note. The torsor and classical target remain those of the primary A-polynomial formulation.

1. An anchor at the trivial quotient color

Fix N>=2, q=exp(pi*i/N), and use M=-q^(alpha+1). The reduced literature period F_N is Laurent polynomial in M. For every knot,

F_N(-q)=1, F_N(1)=<K>_N.                         (1.1)

More generally, for 1<=j<=N,

F_N(-q^j)=C_j(q),                                (1.2)

where C_j is the normalized reduced j-dimensional highest-weight period, with unknot value one. It is the usual reduced colored-Jones normalization already compared directly in the crossing note. Here only fixed small j is needed to specify initial values; no Jones asymptotic or literature comparison theorem is a premise.

Direct filtered-linear-algebra proof

At alpha=j-1 the N-dimensional color has

E v_a=[a]_q[j-a]_q v_(a-1), F v_a=v_(a+1),
K v_a=q^(j-1-2a)v_a.

The subspace U=span(v_j,…,v_(N-1)) is invariant under E,F,K,H. Its quotient W has dimension j and is exactly the standard j-color. The finite R and its inverse preserve U on each individual input strand and induce the usual R on the quotient. Terms of degree at least j vanish there. The pivotal matrix preserves U as well.

The following elementary trace fact is the relevant functoriality. Use a basis adapted to U and its quotient on each strand of a braid whose closure is a knot. Along an underlying strand, a state that has entered U cannot leave U, since the crossing factors preserve the subspace. In the induced cut operator on W, the open strand starts and ends in the quotient. Partial trace identifies the other endpoints. The braid permutation is a single cycle, so every traced state contributing to this quotient block stays in W throughout. Thus the induced cut operator is precisely the cut operator with all strands colored by W. This assertion would need modification for a separate closed link component not attached to the cut.

The two pivotal conventions on W differ by epsilon_j=(-1)^(j-1):

K^(1-N)=epsilon_j*K,
theta_ADO=epsilon_j*q^((j^2-1)/2).

For a b-strand knot braid of writhe w, w and b-1 have the same parity. The total convention factor is therefore epsilon_j^(b-1-w)=1. This proves (1.2). In particular j=1 is the trivial quotient and gives the anchor in (1.1).

For each fixed j, C_j(q) is analytic near q=1 and C_j(1)=1. This also follows directly from the finite matrices: [k]_q! tends to k!, R tends to the identity, the braid tends to its permutation, and the cut trace of a one-cycle permutation is the identity. These initial periods consequently have no level-dependent exponential normalization.

The compatibility of integer colors and ADO is existing coverage; Willetts, 2003.09854, states a more extensive unification. Its completion and recursion theorems are not used in the proof above.

2. Pointing kills the central scalar on the relevant fiber

Let sigma(M)=qM with q of order n, E=C(M), K=C(u), u=M^n. If h and g span the same nonzero rational solution line, then

g=c(u)h, c in K^*.                               (2.1)

Suppose h is regular at M_0, h(M_0)!=0, and the corresponding germ of g is regular with g(M_0)=h(M_0). It follows that c is regular at u_0=M_0^n and c(u_0)=1. At every other point of that sigma-orbit where h is regular, g has the same value as h.

This is an exact central-fiber statement, not a uniform growth bound. It permits arbitrarily level-dependent c; its value on this fiber has already been fixed by the pointing.

For the literature state, n=2N and

M_*=-q, M_+=1, M_* q^(N-1)=M_+,
M_*^(2N)=M_+^(2N)=1.                             (2.2)

Thus a comparison within the same rational solution line, normalized by the actual anchor F_N(M_*)=1, has no remaining central multiplier at the Kashaev point.

A sufficient operator condition for the solution line

Given a specified operator P and its cyclic module as in the intrinsic norm-torsor note, assume the row kernel of H-1 has dimension one over E. Then all rational solutions form a one-dimensional K-vector space, provided one nonzero solution exists.

Indeed a solution h defines the E-linear pairing ell_h by ell_h(T^j e)=sigma^j h. It satisfies ell_h(H-1)=0. Two such pairings are E-proportional by the kernel hypothesis, so g=c(M)h. Their compatibility with T gives sigma(c)=c, hence c belongs to K. The hypothesis concerns geometric multiplicity one; it does not require H to be semisimple. It must be checked for the actual operator.

An explicit period generator from Hilbert 90

This criterion also constructs a rational period from the operator. Write T=B*sigma on the cyclic module and choose a nonzero row w with wH=w. On this row line define

Gamma(w)=sigma(w)B^(-1)=a*w.

The identity sigma(H)=B^(-1)HB preserves the line, and Gamman(w)=wH(-1)=w, so Norm(a)=1. The explicit Hilbert-90 sum

f(c)=sum_(j=0)^(n-1)
       [a*sigma(a)*...*sigma^(j-1)(a)]*sigma^j(c)

has a choice of c for which it is a unit and asigma(f)=f. Consequently ell=fw obeys sigma(ell)=ell B. Its first component h is a nonzero rational solution of the original scalar operator. Changing c or the row frame changes h only by a central factor.

Normalize on the specified root germs M_+(u)=u^(1/n), M_(u)=-qu^(1/n). Then for the reduced state

<K>_N=[h(M_+(u))/h(M_*(u))]_(u=1), n=2N,        (2.3)

with common zeros and poles canceled as a whole germ. This is the explicit norm-torsor/Hilbert-90 version of the cofactor period below. It does not require equating D_n with the classical A-polynomial.

The one-dimensional solution-line hypothesis cannot simply be omitted. For even N, the equation (T^2-1)h=0 admits both h=1 and g=1+c*(M^N+1). They have the same value one at M=-q, but their values at M=1 differ by 2c. This is an abstract equation, not a knot counterexample. It explains the need for either the verified line hypothesis or the finite initial-value data in Section 6.

3. The full ADO state is anchored by a central residue

Write N_N(M)=d_N(M)F_N(M) and eta_N=i^(N+3), as before. At M_* the full state has a pole, so one must not replace the anchor by an undefined ordinary value. Instead

(u-1)N_N(M)=eta_N*(M-M^(-1))*M^N*F_N(M),
[(u-1)N_N]_(M=-q)=eta_N*(-1)^N*(q-q^(-1)).       (3.1)

The last quantity is nonzero. At M=1, by contrast, the whole full period is regular and N_N(1)=eta_N*<K>_N/N. The same central fiber therefore requires different meridian frames at its two marked points.

To verify the residue, M_N=(-1)(N+1) and M_-M_*(-1)=-(q-q(-1)). Equation (1.1) then gives (3.1). If a rational comparison of full states preserves this residue, (2.1) again gives c(1)=1. It therefore preserves the regular full state value at M=1, and hence the Kashaev value. This is the same pointed descent with its actual peripheral lattice.

4. The root period can be read from the quantum A-operator

Let P(q,M,T) be a specified reduced-state annihilator. At q=q_N, view it as a K-linear operator on E, using

E=K[M]/(M^n-u), n=2N,
basis 1,M,...,M^(n-1),
X=multiplication by M, S=diag(1,q,...,q^(n-1)),
A_n(u)=sum_j a_j(q_N,X)S^j.                       (4.1)

This is an n by n matrix over K. Assume its generic nullity is one. It is a direct checkable condition on the given root-level A-module. Then its kernel is exactly K*F_N, since F_N is a nonzero solution.

Work over the DVR O=C[u]_(u-1). The integral period lattice

Lambda=ker_K(A_n) intersection O^n

is free of rank one and saturated in O^n. The Laurent polynomial F_N is in this lattice and is primitive because F_N(M_*)=1. For any primitive generator w of Lambda, let

e_+=(1,1,...,1), e_*=(1,M_*,...,M_*^(n-1)).

The denominator e_* w(1) is nonzero and

<K>_N=e_+ w(1)/(e_* w(1)).                       (4.2)

This is the period pairing on a pointed projective kernel line. It contains no free normalization scalar.

Clear rational denominators of A_n by a central scalar and choose any nonzero column w_raw of its adjugate. Generically its rank is n-1, so such a column spans the kernel. Divide out the common (u-1)-valuation to obtain w. Equivalently compute

<K>_N=[(e_+ w_raw(u))/(e_* w_raw(u))]_(u=1),       (4.3)

as a whole central germ. This remains valid if all the displayed cofactors vanish at u=1 and the specialized matrix has a larger kernel. The saturated generic line, rather than the whole enlarged special kernel, specifies the period. A different nonzero column, or a central clearing denominator, gives the same ratio.

The construction also applies to a specified finite system of annihilators, hence to the joint kernel of generators of a quantum A-ideal. If that joint kernel has dimension one, choose n-1 independent rows from the stacked matrix and take their signed maximal minors. They give the same projective kernel line and the same pointed ratio. Independence of the row choice follows from uniqueness of a one-dimensional kernel. No arbitrary favorable generator of an ideal is being substituted for the actual system.

5. The full-state formula retains one meridian jet

The actual reduced/full gauge is multiplication by the nonzero rational function d_N over E. It is a K-linear isomorphism, so the generic rational kernel dimensions in these two normalizations agree. Their integral frames at u=1 differ, which is why the full state requires the jet below.

For a full-state annihilator with generic rational nullity one, its integral kernel is generated by the primitive vector for R_N=(u-1)N_N. At M=1 this vector has zero evaluation. Its first meridian jet, rather than that zero value, gives the full state.

Choose the root M_+(u)=u^(1/n) with value one, and M_(u)=-qu^(1/n). Let e_+(u),e_*(u) be their evaluation rows in the monomial basis. For a primitive generator w,

j_+(w)=sum_(j=0)^(n-1)[w_j'(1)+(j/n)w_j(1)],
<K>_N=N*(-1)^N*(q-q^(-1))*j_+(w)/(e_* w(1)).      (5.1)

This follows by differentiating R_N(M_+(u))=(u-1)N_N(M_+(u)) and using the nonzero residue (3.1) to fix the scalar at u=1. A derivative of that scalar does not contribute, since e_+w(1)=0.

For any nonzero adjugate column the equivalent whole-germ formula is

<K>_N=N*(-1)^N*(q-q^(-1))*
  [ e_+(u)w_raw(u) / ((u-1)e_*(u)w_raw(u)) ]_(u=1). (5.2)

The specified root germs are essential here. Equations (4.3) and (5.2) give the same period under the actual reduced/full ADO gauge. This explains precisely where a first jet and the peripheral factor enter an intrinsic A-operator formulation.

6. A finite initial-value presentation when the orbit is regular

There is another exact presentation that does not require a one-dimensional rational solution space. Suppose the scalar operator has order d and its coefficients and forward companion matrix are regular at the orbit points M_j=-q^j, j=1,…,N-d, with nonzero leading coefficient there. The equation propagates the initial vector

(F_N(M_1),...,F_N(M_d))=(C_1(q),...,C_d(q))        (6.1)

to F_N(M_N)=<K>_N by its ordered companion product. For fixed d the initial periods are the analytic, level-independent functions of q in Section 1. The quantum A-equation and this finite pointing specify the exact period on that orbit. At singular orbit points the stated propagation argument cannot simply divide by a vanishing coefficient; the integral lattice or additional compatibility must be retained.

The subsequent all-level theorem supplies this compatibility from the actual integer-color identities. It first propagates rational functions in q and then evaluates the whole q-germ at q_N. Its jet bound is D(s-1), with D the leading M-span and s its support size; it does not assume the fixed-q meridian jets are bounded.

7. Remaining volume theorem

Under the hypotheses of Section 4, (4.3) is an A-operator-only formula for the actual literature period. Consequently the requested volume conjecture is exactly the assertion that this pointed cofactor ratio has logarithmic growth Vol(S^3 minus K)/(2pi). No replacement invariant is being defined by a favorable quantization.

This is not a proof of that growth statement. A nonzero actual all-level knot operator is now supplied by the finite cut-state argument. Its geometric classical symbol and comparison of the pointed period with the marked regulator still require proof in the desired knot class. Solution-rank and orbit-regularity conditions remain hypotheses of the particular formulas above; the all-level whole-germ presentation no longer needs them. The reference anchor removes the residual central normalization once the comparison line has been identified; it does not identify a geometric period with that line by itself.

The independent checks in experiments/audit_root_orbit_pointing.py compare full and quotient colors directly and test the cofactor and lattice prescription, including a fiber where the matrix rank drops. They supplement the uniform arguments above.
