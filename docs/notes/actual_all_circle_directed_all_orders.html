All orders of the meridian profile under the directed criterion

The directed degree-and-twist criterion gives more than a leading volume limit. For every fixed knot presentation satisfying that criterion, the actual meridian state has an asymptotic expansion to every finite order on the microscopic scale M=qNsM=q_N^s. Its coefficients have a precise polynomial degree bound. This bound allows an exact quantum recurrence to select the preferred geometric meridian–longitude curve of the original knot complement.

The proof expands the actual normalized subtree integrals, retaining their full normal data and endpoint terms. It also works on the fixed translated parameter regions used by the recurrence. These are separate estimates from the previously established first-order profile.

The extension has an operator consequence. For every knot satisfying the directed certificate, the first nonzero classical symbol of every genuine fixed all-level reduced ADO annihilator contains the selected complex geometric meridian–longitude factor. The statement is about that factor, not the full A-polynomial, its multiplicity, or the entire operator ideal.

9. Proof dependencies and scope

The directed finite-source theorem supplies the following inputs:

  1. Equations (N1)–(N4), (13), (25)–(26): actual basis, full Frobenius functional, complete metric, physical turn, and exact finite recurrence. These fix types, normals and the outside scalar.
  2. Equations (14)–(24): full edge complement, conditional rectangle estimate, actual local wire contour and nonzero leading edge amplitude. Extending the compact changes only bounded H/WH/W factors.
  3. Equations (27)–(37): normalized conditional phase, its Hessian and Schur complement, first-charge drop and strict separation of retained charges.
  4. Equations (38)–(43): actual full-input strip estimate, exact charge coefficient, tail/endpoint/current bounds, and outgoing-envelope regeneration. These estimates are applied to exact messages outside the local saddle at every asymptotic order.
  5. Equations (44)–(46): backward restoration of the same joint critical point, common small-angular charge-path anchor and actual filled geometry.

The exact interpolation theorem and regular-circle calculation, §10, fix (13)–(17), including all midpoint and reflection factors. The direct-crossing trace, §§2–4, fixes the finite translated grids and their endpoint currents. The full source bounds, (D9)–(D14), supply the full polynomial norm used in regeneration, including constant normal channels; the extension to compact nonreal parameters is checked in §3 above. The exact scalar calculation fixes (20) and the one reduced cut.

The filtered characteristic theorem supplies the algebraic organization of the canonical insertion and the Ore gauge. Its analytic hypotheses for the directed certificate are proved here: §§3–5 give every finite asymptotic order on the required charts with a uniform remainder, and §6 identifies those actual coefficients with the joint two-parameter insertion. The published first-order profile alone is not used as a substitute for either step.

Finally the geometric-characteristic theorem, §§5–10, supplies the marked reconstructed branch and the preferred longitude, with §5’s complete filled-point anchor checked in §7 above for the new certificate. The positive geometric path depends on (23), not on the older summed or maximum-based source criterion. Thus no old analytic class restriction is silently used to identify the new source branch.

All work in this note is for the actual fixed finite tree. It does not give a multi-boundary replacement theorem for arbitrary short-power islands, nor an automatic result for a graph with cycles. Those would require their own conditional source estimates.
