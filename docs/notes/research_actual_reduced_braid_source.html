The actual reduced braid operators and their pointed trace

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The first-meridian-jet source formula can be used with an explicit finite braid representation. The reduced operator is not an unspecified quotient: its generators are the original crossings on the remaining factors and one triangular Euler operator. The same source formula proves cyclicity on the actual intertwiner algebra. No modified-trace theorem is imported.

The complete directs are C for the prefix quotient, H for its scalar source kernel, E for the original crossing matrices, and M for the marked Hurwitz convention. All additional generator, cyclicity, stabilization and norm arguments are below.

1. The actual quotient and its algebra map

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, Q=q2Q=q^2. Keep qq fixed and vary the common meridian MM near one. Write x=−M/qx=-M/q and fa=∏j=1a(1−Qj)f_a=\prod_{j=1}^a(1-Q^j), f0=1f_0=1. The original balanced factors have Kwa=xQ−awa,Fwa=(1−Qa+1)wa+1(a<d),Fwd=0.(1) K w_a=xQ^{-a}w_a,\qquad Fw_a=(1-Q^{a+1})w_{a+1}\quad(a<d),\qquad Fw_d=0. \qquad\text{(1)} The lowering matrix has coefficient MQ−a−M−1MQ^{-a}-M^{-1} for a>0a>0, and its vacuum arrow is zero.

For mm factors let Fr,KrF_{\mathrm{ r}},K_{\mathrm{ r}} be the ordered coproducts on factors 2,…,m2,\ldots,m. With the literal shift L1wa=wa−1L_1w_a=w_{a-1}, L1w0=0L_1w_0=0, C gives Sm=∑j=0d(x−1L1Fr)jfj,Sm−1FΣSm=F1,[Sm,KΣ]=0.(2) S_m=\sum_{j=0}^{d} \frac{(x^{-1}L_1F_{\mathrm{ r}})^j}{f_j},\qquad S_m^{-1}F_\Sigma S_m=F_1,\qquad [S_m,K_\Sigma]=0. \qquad\text{(2)} Its inverse has coefficient (−1)jQj(j−1)/2/fj(-1)^jQ^{j(j-1)/2}/f_j in degree jj. For one factor use S1=IS_1=I.

Let i0v=w0⊗vi_0v=w_0\otimes v and let p0p_0 extract first occupation zero. All statements concern the actual common-meridian family at the fixed quantum root.

2. Explicit braid generators

Assume m≥2m\ge2. Let RiR_i be the original positive checked crossing on factors i,i+1i,i+1, including its common Cartan scalar κ\kappa. E fixes the literal formula Ri=κ𝖯iDiΘi,(Di)a,b=x−a−bQab,(6) R_i=\kappa\,\mathsf P_iD_i\Theta_i,\qquad (D_i)_{a,b}=x^{-a-b}Q^{ab}, \qquad\text{(6)} where the Euler transfer lowers the first input occupation. The negative crossing is the actual inverse. These crossings are total intertwiners at the fixed root throughout the common-meridian family.

For i≥2i\ge2, the crossing is internal to the remaining tensor. It commutes with its ordered total FrF_{\mathrm{ r}} and KrK_{\mathrm{ r}}, hence with SmS_m. It also commutes with the exposed first-factor maps. Thus, with the remaining positions relabelled 1,…,m−11,\ldots,m-1, ℬm(Ri)=Ri−1.(7) \mathcal B_m(R_i)=R_{i-1}. \qquad\text{(7)} The right side is precisely the original physical crossing on those two remaining factors.

The crossing R1R_1 has a different explicit image. On input w0⊗wb⊗vw_0\otimes w_b\otimes v, its positive transfer is forced to be zero, so R1(w0⊗wb⊗v)=κx−bwb⊗w0⊗v.(8) R_1(w_0\otimes w_b\otimes v) =\kappa x^{-b}w_b\otimes w_0\otimes v. \qquad\text{(8)} In p0Sm−1p_0S_m^{-1}, only the degree-bb term can then reach the first row. Equation (4) gives ℬm(R1)(wb⊗v)=κ(−1)bQb(b−1)/2x−2bfbFrb(w0⊗v).(9) \mathcal B_m(R_1)(w_b\otimes v) =\kappa\frac{(-1)^bQ^{b(b-1)/2}x^{-2b}}{f_b} F_{\mathrm{ r}}^{\,b}(w_0\otimes v). \qquad\text{(9)} C’s finite prefix identity on the remaining m−1m-1 factors is Sm−1(wb⊗v)=fb−1Frb(w0⊗v).(10) S_{m-1}(w_b\otimes v) =f_b^{-1}F_{\mathrm{ r}}^{\,b}(w_0\otimes v). \qquad\text{(10)} Consequently define the diagonal matrix on the first remaining factor by Dredwb=(−1)bQb(b−1)/2x−2bwb=(−1)bqb(b+1)M−2bwb.(11) D_{\mathrm{ red}}w_b=(-1)^bQ^{b(b-1)/2}x^{-2b}w_b =(-1)^bq^{b(b+1)}M^{-2b}w_b . \qquad\text{(11)} The exact reduced generator is ℬm(R1)=κSm−1Dred,ℬm(R1−1)=κ−1Dred−1Sm−1−1.(12) \boxed{\quad \mathcal B_m(R_1)=\kappa S_{m-1}D_{\mathrm{ red}},\qquad \mathcal B_m(R_1^{-1}) =\kappa^{-1}D_{\mathrm{ red}}^{-1}S_{m-1}^{-1}. \quad} \qquad\text{(12)} For m=2m=2, Sm−1=IS_{m-1}=I. Every endpoint in (8)–(12) is the actual finite endpoint. No cyclic wrap or infinite series replaces it.

Equations (5), (7), and (12) give the original braid relations on the remaining space: they are images of the actual braid relations under an algebra map. They also give ℬm(Wβ)\mathcal B_m(W_\beta) by a literal finite product. All common crossing scalars and any separately prescribed zero-framing line remain. First meridian derivatives follow by the ordinary product rule for this finite product, including derivatives of Sm−1,Dred,κS_{m-1},D_{\mathrm{ red}},\kappa. No condition-number bound for the triangular factor is asserted.

3. Cyclicity with the original pointed and pivotal source

The original highest-cut source is Φ0(W)=Tr⁡((P0⊗Kr1−N)W).(13) \Phi_0(W)=\operatorname{Tr} ((P_0\otimes K_{\mathrm{ r}}^{1-N})W). \qquad\text{(13)} H proves the exact meromorphic identity Φ0(W)=Tr⁡r(DN(M)DN(MKr)ℬm(W)),DN(z)=zN−z−Nz−z−1,(14) \Phi_0(W)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ r}} \left(\frac{D_N(M)}{D_N(MK_{\mathrm{ r}})}\mathcal B_m(W)\right), \qquad D_N(z)=\frac{z^N-z^{-N}}{z-z^{-1}}, \qquad\text{(14)} with its prescribed removable continuation for an actual analytic intertwiner. The fraction is a function of the actual remaining Cartan, not a scalar Casimir choice.

For two such intertwiners X,YX,Y, their constant coefficients commute with that function of KrK_{\mathrm{ r}}. On the generic meridian set where its denominator is invertible, (5) and the ordinary matrix trace therefore give Φ0(XY)=Φ0(YX).(15) \Phi_0(XY)=\Phi_0(YX). \qquad\text{(15)} The two original finite source functions are analytic. Thus (15) extends through completeness, as an equality of their common-meridian germs. In particular Φ0(GWG−1)=Φ0(W)(16) \Phi_0(GWG^{-1})=\Phi_0(W) \qquad\text{(16)} for an invertible actual total intertwiner GG. This permits unmarked braid conjugation with the original highest cut and tail pivot. Braid conjugation also preserves the writhe, so its prescribed external zero-framing scalar is unchanged. This conclusion does not apply to arbitrary marked matrices failing the total intertwining equations.

4. A source bound on an actual meridian collar

Write B(M)=ℬm(W(M))B(M)=\mathcal B_m(W(M)). H proves, in the original remaining occupation norm, |Φ0(W(1))|≤Nm−1(∥B(1)∥+m−1∥B′(1)∥).(17) |\Phi_0(W(1))| \le N^{m-1}\big(\|B(1)\|+m^{-1}\|B'(1)\|\big). \qquad\text{(17)} Suppose the actual finite matrix BB is analytic on a neighborhood of |M−1|≤η/N|M-1|\le\eta/N. Matrix Cauchy estimates and the maximum principle give |Φ0(W(1))|≤Nm−1(1+Nmη)sup|M−1|=η/N∥B(M)∥.(18) |\Phi_0(W(1))| \le N^{m-1}\left(1+\frac{N}{m\eta}\right) \sup_{|M-1|=\eta/N}\|B(M)\|. \qquad\text{(18)} This is a polynomial cost at fixed m,ηm,\eta. Every external scalar line must still be included if the source convention places it outside WW. Neither (17) nor (18) bounds the actual reduced word. A large norm does not imply that its specified trace functional is nonzero.

5. A regular semisimple total product after at most three stabilizations

There is a useful presentation choice for an irreducible complete representation of the knot group. The tuple of its ordered noncentral parabolic meridians is denoted H1,…,HmH_1,\ldots,H_m in M’s sign convention, and P=Hm⋯H1P=H_m\cdots H_1. Positive Hurwitz motion sends an adjacent pair to (Hi,Hi+1)↦(Hi−1Hi+1Hi,Hi).(19) (H_i,H_{i+1})\longmapsto (H_i^{-1}H_{i+1}H_i,H_i). \qquad\text{(19)} It preserves PP. Applying positions j,j+1,…,m−1j,j+1,\ldots,m-1 therefore moves a chosen HjH_j to the last position unchanged. Conjugate the braid by that explicit Hurwitz word.

Append a new last meridian equal to the old last one, and positively stabilize the braid. Its last crossing fixes this equal pair, so the new tuple still satisfies every braid fixed-point relation. Its total product is HjPH_jP. Repeated stabilization on this last meridian gives HjtPH_j^tP. Topologically these are Markov stabilizations of the same knot.

If tr⁡(P)2≠4\operatorname{tr}(P)^2\ne4, no change is needed. If PP is noncentral parabolic, write P=ϵ(I+A),Hj=δ(I+B),A2=B2=0,ϵ,δ∈{1,−1}.(20) P=\epsilon(I+A),\qquad H_j=\delta(I+B),\qquad A^2=B^2=0,\qquad \epsilon,\delta\in\{1,-1\}. \qquad\text{(20)} Irreducibility supplies a meridian whose eigenline differs from the unique eigenline of PP. For this choice tr⁡(BA)≠0\operatorname{tr}(BA)\ne0: in a basis making AA strictly upper triangular, the lower-left entry of BB is nonzero and computes this trace. Thus tr⁡(HjtP)2=(2+ttr⁡(BA))2.(21) \operatorname{tr}(H_j^tP)^2 =(2+t\,\operatorname{tr}(BA))^2 . \qquad\text{(21)} For a nonzero number cc, the two numbers 2+c2+c and 2+2c2+2c cannot both belong to {2,−2}\{2,-2\}. One or two positive stabilizations therefore make this product regular semisimple. If PP was central, a first stabilization makes it noncentral parabolic; the preceding argument then uses at most two more. The enlarged tuple is still irreducible because it generates the same group. This proves the bound of three.

For completeness the exact quantum scalar of each such stabilization follows directly from (6), with no generic modified-trace assumption. Put g=K1−Ng=K^{1-N}. Integer-degree conservation makes Tr⁡2((I⊗g)R+)\operatorname{Tr}_2((I\otimes g)R_+) diagonal. For its input occupation aa, set t=Q1−ax2t=Q^{1-a}x^2. The literal finite crossing coefficients give Ta=κx1−Nx−2aQa(a−1)∑n=0a(−1)nQn(n−1)/2+(1−a)n[an]Q(t;Q)n.(22) \begin{split} T_a={}&\kappa x^{1-N}x^{-2a}Q^{a(a-1)} \sum_{n=0}^a(-1)^nQ^{n(n-1)/2+(1-a)n} {a\brack n}_Q(t;Q)_n . \end{split} \qquad\text{(22)} The finite sum is tat^a. To check it, its coefficient of tkt^k, k<ak<a, is a nonzero scalar factor times (Qk+1−a;Q)a−k(Q^{k+1-a};Q)_{a-k}, which contains 1−Q01-Q^0. This follows by expanding the products and using the finite product expansion for the Gaussian coefficients. Its leading coefficient is one. All products have length at most a<Na<N, so no root denominator was inverted. It follows that Tr⁡2((I⊗g)R+)=αI,α=κx1−N.(23) \operatorname{Tr}_2((I\otimes g)R_+) =\alpha I,\qquad \alpha=\kappa x^{1-N}. \qquad\text{(23)} The actual zero-framing compensation multiplies by α−1\alpha^{-1} when the positive writhe increases by one. It consequently cancels exactly the scalar in (23). Equivalently, define the normalized zero-framed raw braid source by its prescribed factor α−wr(β)\alpha^{-\mathrm{wr}(\beta)}; (23) verifies its positive Markov invariance directly. Any separately retained modified-dimension line is unchanged. Together with (16), these operations preserve the original normalized root-meridian source, with its actual meridian germ. This is not an off-root quantum-normal argument.

For the resulting regular semisimple PP, there is always an eigenline different from the selected meridian flag. It supplies a finite classical frame with P21=0P_{21}=0. This statement does not place every other shape in a common regular slit chart or put the geometric point on the original integration cycle. The stabilization lemma establishes presentation regularity only.

6. Scope and complete proof-source pins

The reduction is exact on the root-quantum common-meridian family. Quantum normals retain C’s additional prefix row JNJ_N; the root generator reduction is not differentiated as an ordinary-color quantum-normal identity. No assertion about a geometric integration cycle, source positivity, or a surviving geometric contribution is made.
