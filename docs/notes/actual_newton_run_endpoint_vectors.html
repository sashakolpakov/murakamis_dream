Finite Gaussian boundary data for arbitrary repeated twist/turn runs

29 October 2026. For every fixed integer m, the actual source of (T_2 P_rev)^m has explicit first AND last fixed-rank endpoint vectors. They reduce to original signed-twist sources with fixed Newton insertions. Each such source has a finite algebraic Gaussian leading coefficient, so testing a candidate recurrence’s boundary projection does not require an unknown knot invariant or an unspecified solution. No interior branch-survival theorem for all m is asserted.

1. Universal exact source endpoint identities

Use the full original Newton algebra/metric, Bhat_h=B_h/s_h^2, T=T_1,P=P_rev,R=kappa r. The actual Cartan identities give

kappa^2(-q)^d=1,
theta=kappa^3(-q)^d=kappa.

Thus the coherent modular reduction simplifies, for every integer m, to

v_m:=(T^2P)^m1=kappa^m T P T^(1-m)1.                 (1)

For a polynomial f, its Newton endpoint coefficient satisfies alpha_(d-j)(f)=phi(f Bhat_j). Lower triangularity of T therefore gives

(v_m)_h=kappa^m sum_(j=0)^h
              T_(h,j) phi(R^(1-m) Bhat_j).            (2)

There is an equally explicit right endpoint formula. Define

E_h=sum_(r=0)^h nu_h^(-1)nu_(h-r)(-q)^(-(h-r))
                                 [h choose r]_Q Bhat_r.

Then, on the FULL algebra and with every confluent normal retained,

mathscrR(R Bhat_h)=R^(-1)E_h,
(v_m)_(d-h)=kappa^m phi(R^(-m)E_h).                  (3)

Proof. TPT=kappa PT^(-1)P implies PT=kappa T(-1)PT(-1)P. Apply this to e_h. The middle vector PT^(-1)e_(d-h) is supported on indices0,…,h. The actual reflected inverse kernel gives

kappa (T^(-1))_(d-r,d-h)
   =nu_h^(-1)nu_(h-r)(-q)^(-(h-r))[h choose r]_Q.

This proves (3)’s first equality. The Frobenius identity phi(mathscrR(f)g)=phi(f mathscrR(g)) then proves its second. It is a full operator/source calculation, not a claim about eigenlines.

In particular

(v_m)_0=kappa^(m+1)phi(R^(1-m)),
(v_m)_d=kappa^m phi(R^(-m)).                          (4)

For fixed endpoint rank h, all sums in(2)–(3) have at most h+1 terms, with explicit coefficients. There is no inverse Clebsch minor.

2. Fixed endpoint insertions still have a finite Gaussian certificate

Keep a(s)=(1-2s)sin(2pi s), epsilon=k mod2, and s_l=(2l-epsilon)/(2k), as in the actual twist endpoint theorem. For every FIXED k>0 and FIXED h>=0, set

G_(k,h)(N)=sum_(0<s_l<1)(-1)^l a(s_l)sin(pi s_l)^(2h)
                     exp[-i pi N(l-epsilon/2)^2/k].    (5)

This is algebraic and periodic in N modulo8k. With C_(N,k) exactly the phase in that theorem, the original source obeys

phi(r^k Bhat_h)
   = C_(N,k) N^(3/2+2h)/(pi^(2h)(h!)^2) G_(k,h)(N)
                                          +O_(k,h)(N^(1+2h)). (6)

For odd k the error is O_(k,h)(N^(1/2+2h)). Negative k follows by coefficient conjugation. The statements concern fixed h<N as N tends to infinity; h may not be sent to a fraction of N here.

An own proof retains the full source derivative. Its exact folded form is

phi(r^k Bhat_h)=(-1)^(d+1)/2 sum_j nu_j^k {
   Bhat_h(c_j)[c_j-(k/2)(d-2j)A_j]+A_j^2 Bhat_h'(c_j)}. (7)

Uniformly with all fixed-order s derivatives,

Bhat_h(2cos(2pi s))
  =N^(2h)sin(pi s)^(2h)/(pi^(2h)(h!)^2)+O_h(N^(2h-2)).

This follows directly from its h factors and s_h2=(2pi/N)(2h)(h!)2[1+O_h(N(-2))]. Insert this into(7) and apply the proved half-grid Gaussian argument to the leading a(s) term. The remaining source terms have no extra N factor and are O(N^(1/2+2h)), except the possible even-k endpoint contributions already bounded by O(N^(1+2h)). This proves (6), including normals.

For each specified k,h, nonvanishing of(5) is a FINITE cyclotomic test; a zero leading coefficient is not silently discarded. For k3,

G_(3,h)=4^(-h)G_(3,0),

and for k4,

G_(4,h)=2^(-h)G_(4,0).

Both are nonzero on every N residue class. For k5 the two paired weights are

a_1 sin(pi/10)^(2h), a_2 sin(3pi/10)^(2h).

At h0 the first is larger; at every integer h>=1 the second is larger: a_2/a_1=cos(pi/5)>1/2 and sin(3pi/10)/sin(pi/10)=(3+sqrt5)/2>2. Their strict difference gives a uniform triangle lower bound exactly as in the scalar theorem. Thus fixed endpoint insertions for powers3,4,5 all have nonzero leading terms, with no parity restriction.

The exceptional powers have exact fixed-root boundary data instead:

phi(Bhat_h)=delta_(h,d),
phi(r Bhat_h)=(-q)^(d-h),
phi(r^2 Bhat_h)=(-q)^(d-h)F_(d-h).                   (8)

Negative powers are their conjugates. These follow from the literal r and r^2 source columns and the full Frobenius pairing. Thus the vanishing leading Gaussians at k0,1,2 leave no unknown endpoint data.

3. Every fixed twist power has nonzero resolved Gaussian boundary modes

There is a stronger UNIVERSAL vector statement even when the scalar Gaussian sum cancels. Fix any k>=3, put epsilon=k mod2 and rho=floor((k-1)/2), and define

s_j=(2j-epsilon)/(2k), t_j=sin(pi s_j)^2,
                  1<=j<=rho, 0<t_1<...<t_rho<1,
w_j(N)=2(-1)^j(1-2s_j)sin(2pi s_j)
                   exp[-i pi N(j-epsilon/2)^2/k].    (9)

Then, for EVERY integer N,

G_(k,h)(N)=sum_(j=1)^rho t_j^h w_j(N).               (10)

To prove it, pair the stationary index j with k+epsilon-j. Their positions are s and1-s, their a values and sin(pi s)^(2h) agree, and their (-1) signs agree. Their quadratic phase difference is pi N(k+epsilon-2j), an even integer multiple of pi. The remaining middle position s=1/2 has a=0. Thus every pair adds, proving(10). Moreover

|w_j(N)|=2(1-2s_j)sin(2pi s_j)>0                    (11)

independently of N or any coprimality condition.

Let V_(h,j)=t_j^h, 0<=h<rho. This fixed Vandermonde is invertible: its determinant is product_(i<j)(t_j-t_i)!=0. Form from the ACTUAL source entries the normalized vector

b_(h,N)=pi^(2h)(h!)^2
    phi(r^k Bhat_h)/(C_(N,k)N^(3/2+2h)).

Equation(6) is uniform over this fixed finite vector. Consequently

V^(-1)b_N=(w_j(N))_j+O_k(N^(-1/2)),                 (12)

with O_k(1/N) error for odd k. Every individual resolved coefficient therefore has a uniform positive lower bound for all sufficiently large N. Negative powers give its conjugate statement.

One may use an EXACT algebraic normalization at every root instead:

btilde_(h,N)=s_h^2 phi(r^k Bhat_h)
                             /(4^h C_(N,k)N^(3/2)). (12a)

Since s_h2/4h=pi(2h)(h!)2 N(-2h)[1+O_h(N(-2))], the same conclusion(12) holds with btilde. All its finite-level normalizing factors, and the Vandermonde entries, are algebraic.

The normalizations and V inverse are explicit, depend on k, and have only polynomial cost in N. They do not use an unknown knot invariant. Thus scalar residue-class cancellations do NOT leave unknown source incidence if one retains the full fixed-rank endpoint vector. These are resolved GAUSSIAN endpoint modes. Identifying them with a chosen interior Ward branch requires the exact boundary-to-Ward projection and a propagation estimate on the domain in question. Neither follows from the names or from nonzero individual entries; a finite endpoint projection alone does not control subsequent mode conversion.

4. What this gives a finite-depth source criterion

For any fixed m and any proposed finite-rank Ward recurrence for v_m, (2)–(7) specify its endpoint vectors by finitely many exact Gaussians and known N powers/phases. A dominant-eigenprojection test can therefore be made against actual source data. It must retain a possible linear combination/cancellation between those finite coefficients; nonzero individual entries do not imply a nonzero projection.

The repeated run is modularly PARABOLIC and conjugate to one twist. Its unbounded count of displayed turns is not a new general bridge family. The reusable result here is the actual finite boundary-data formula; it is not counted as completion of the broad source problem.

The next m4 vector and its exact rank-three inhomogeneous Ward are already displayed in the endpoint theorem. Its forward source has an interior modulus switch; distinct roots alone cannot select one branch. The data here remove an unknown boundary value from that problem, rather than asserting that the remaining conversion or global source gap has been solved.

All statements are ROOT-VALUE identities in the actual original source. Bhat insertions encode endpoint coordinates and full Casimir Hermite normals. They are not Taylor derivatives in q of an arbitrary generic turn family. No recorded Newton clock is promoted to a peripheral deformation or an additional actual knot coupon without its own proof.

Audit: full original endpoint matrices and finite Gaussian data. Its modal-residue checks use exact primitive cyclotomic-root images in finite fields, with the prime and image recorded in the results. They supplement the all-k pairing/Vandermonde proof above; a successful specialization is not itself a proof of an identity over the complex cyclotomic field.

The finite audit checks original source families, coefficient identities, Gaussian profiles and cyclotomic modal residue vectors. These checks supplement the universal pairing proof; they do not extrapolate it from the tested twist powers.
