A binomial cut and its polynomially bounded framing density

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

A single cut occupation near the middle of the physical module has an exact normal-residue description, but its coefficients at the two framing endpoints can grow exponentially with the level. A binomial average of cut occupations avoids that cost. It gives the same original unmarked knot invariant, and its entire density on the compact real framing interval has polynomial norm. The density is a Legendre polynomial evaluation kernel.

The construction below keeps the original marked-current defect. It does not select a geometric contribution to the invariant. The complete proof sources are C, the actual normal-residue and cut-moment map; D, its whole-clock extension; and I, the exact change of physical cut. Their pins are listed below. The orthogonal-polynomial calculations are proved here.

1. The cut and its exact relation to the original scalar

Fix N≥2N\ge2, put d=N−1d=N-1, and use the factorial basis e0,…,ede_0,\ldots,e_d of I. Let Ta=eaea*T_a=e_ae_a^* be the diagonal coordinate projection. Define πN,a=2−d(da),PNcut=∑a=0dπN,aTa.(1) \pi_{N,a}=2^{-d}\binom da,\qquad P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}=\sum_{a=0}^{d}\pi_{N,a}T_a . \qquad\text{(1)} The weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Positivity here is in the stated finite basis metric; no unitary structure on every quantum-group representation is assumed.

For the actual unmarked one-cut endomorphism, I proves CN(M)=FN(M)IC_N(M)=F_N(M)I, with the original framing and reduced scalar normalization. Therefore, exactly at every level, Tr⁡(PNcutCN(M))=FN(M)=Tr⁡(T0CN(M)).(2) \operatorname{Tr}(P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}C_N(M)) =F_N(M)=\operatorname{Tr}(T_0C_N(M)). \qquad\text{(2)} At M=1M=1 this is the original Kashaev scalar. In particular no factor such as 2−d2^{-d} is being added to the invariant after its evaluation; it is part of a trace-one cut probe in (1).

For an arbitrary marked endomorphism the comparison has a defect. Use I’s actual raising coefficients and currents, ϵr(M)=MQ−r−1−M−1,Q=e2πi/N,BN,a=−∑r<aϵr(M)−1Er+1,r. \epsilon_r(M)=M Q^{-r-1}-M^{-1},\quad Q=e^{2\pi i/N}, \qquad B_{N,a}=-\sum_{r<a}\epsilon_r(M)^{-1}E_{r+1,r}. Set BNcut=∑aπN,aBN,aB_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}=\sum_a\pi_{N,a}B_{N,a}. Then [ℰ,BNcut]=PNcut−T0,Tr⁡((PNcut−T0)C)=Tr⁡(BNcut[C,ℰ]).(3) [\mathcal E,B_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}]=P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}-T_0,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}((P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}-T_0)C) =\operatorname{Tr}(B_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}[C,\mathcal E]). \qquad\text{(3)} Its subdiagonal coefficients are −Pr⁡(A>r)/ϵr(M)-\Pr(A>r)/\epsilon_r(M), where AA has probabilities πN,a\pi_{N,a}. Their numerators have absolute value at most one. The complete bounds of I consequently give ∥BNcut∥≤N/3(|M−1|≤14N),∥∂MjBNcut∥≤CjNj+1(|M−1|≤18N)(4) \|B_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}\|\le N/3 \quad\left(|M-1|\le\frac1{4N}\right),\qquad \|\partial_M^j B_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}\|\le C_jN^{j+1} \quad\left(|M-1|\le\frac1{8N}\right) \qquad\text{(4)} for each fixed jj. The same physical tail pivots and marked word are retained on the right side of (3).

2. What a framing density must reproduce

For any finite matrix CC, write its diagonal coherent polynomial as f¯C(T)=∑a=0dCaaBa,d(T),Ba,d(T)=(da)Ta(1−T)d−a.(5) \overline f_C(T)=\sum_{a=0}^{d}C_{aa}B_{a,d}(T),\qquad B_{a,d}(T)=\binom da T^a(1-T)^{d-a}. \qquad\text{(5)} This polynomial has degree at most dd. It is the angular average of the full actual coherent symbol in C and D, including when CC has off-diagonal entries. The exact Toeplitz row is [𝒯N(P)]a=N∫01P(−T)Ba,d(T)dT.(6) [\mathcal T_N(P)]_a =N\int_0^1P(-T)B_{a,d}(T)\,dT . \qquad\text{(6)} The corresponding normal-residue pairing is 1NTr⁡(𝒯N(P)C)=∫01P(−T)f¯C(T)dT.(7) \frac1N\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal T_N(P)C) =\int_0^1P(-T)\overline f_C(T)\,dT . \qquad\text{(7)} This is a normalized ordinary trace on arbitrary matrices. As in C and D, its equality with the prescribed average cut uses diagonality of the actual position-conserving cut arrays. It is not an equality with that average on arbitrary off-diagonal Ward matrices. Equation (3) retains the physical comparison.

The density needed for (1) must therefore evaluate all degree ≤d\le d polynomials at T=1/2T=1/2: ∫01pN(T)q(T)dT=q(1/2).(8) \int_0^1p_N(T)q(T)\,dT=q(1/2). \qquad\text{(8)} Indeed (8) applied to Ba,dB_{a,d} gives 𝒯N(PNnor)=NPNcut\mathcal T_N(P_N^{\mathrm{ nor}})=N P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}, where PNnor(b)=pN(−b)P_N^{\mathrm{ nor}}(b)=p_N(-b). The factor NN is required by the original normalization (7).

3. An elementary construction of the evaluation kernel

Let 𝖫k\mathsf L_k denote the ordinary Legendre polynomial, to distinguish it from the physical circle operators. Define it by 𝖫k(x)=12kk!dkdxk(x2−1)k.(9) \mathsf L_k(x)=\frac1{2^kk!} \frac{d^k}{dx^k}(x^2-1)^k . \qquad\text{(9)} Integration by parts kk times shows that it is orthogonal to every polynomial of degree below kk on [−1,1][-1,1]. All boundary terms vanish because (x2−1)k(x^2-1)^k has a zero of order kk at both endpoints. Its leading coefficient is 2−k(2kk)2^{-k}\binom{2k}{k}, and the same integration by parts, using ∫−11(1−x2)kdx=22k+1(k!)2(2k+1)!, \int_{-1}^1(1-x^2)^k\,dx =\frac{2^{2k+1}(k!)^2}{(2k+1)!}, gives ∫−11𝖫k2dx=2/(2k+1)\int_{-1}^1\mathsf L_k^2\,dx=2/(2k+1). The displayed integral follows by the substitution x=2u−1x=2u-1 and the elementary factorial beta integral. Thus 2k+1𝖫k(2T−1)\sqrt{2k+1}\mathsf L_k(2T-1) is an orthonormal polynomial basis for Lebesgue measure on [0,1][0,1].

Set pN(T)=∑k=0d(2k+1)𝖫k(0)𝖫k(2T−1).(10) p_N(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(2k+1)\mathsf L_k(0) \mathsf L_k(2T-1). \qquad\text{(10)} Expanding qq in this orthonormal basis proves (8), without an approximation or an asymptotic passage. Consequently Tr⁡(PNcutC)=∫01pN(T)f¯C(T)dT=f¯C(1/2).(11) \boxed{\displaystyle \operatorname{Tr}(P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}C) =\int_0^1p_N(T)\overline f_C(T)\,dT =\overline f_C(1/2).} \qquad\text{(11)} The polynomial density can have either sign. The positive object in (1) is the physical cut matrix, not its contravariant density.

There is also an exact compact contour presentation. The full coherent symbol of C and D is fC(v,T)=∑i,j=0dCij(dj)vi−jTj(1−T)d−j. f_C(v,T)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d}C_{ij}\binom dj v^{i-j}T^j(1-T)^{d-j}. Taking its angular constant coefficient gives Tr⁡(PNcutC)=12πi∮|v|=1fC(v,1/2)dvv.(11a) \operatorname{Tr}(P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}C) =\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|v|=1}f_C(v,1/2)\,\frac{dv}{v}. \qquad\text{(11a)} Thus the compact polynomial density can be integrated out exactly to an interior latitude circle, for every finite matrix CC. This circle is a co-source presentation; it has not been identified with the remaining geometric centralizer quotient.

Here are the elementary bounds used next. Formula (9) gives 𝖫2m+1(0)=0,𝖫2m(0)=(−1)mcm,cm=4−m(2mm),𝖫k(1)=1,𝖫k(−1)=(−1)k.(12) \mathsf L_{2m+1}(0)=0,\quad \mathsf L_{2m}(0)=(-1)^m c_m,\quad c_m=4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m},\quad \mathsf L_k(1)=1,\quad \mathsf L_k(-1)=(-1)^k. \qquad\text{(12)} It also gives |𝖫k(x)|≤1|\mathsf L_k(x)|\le1 for real |x|≤1|x|\le1. For completeness, its generating series, obtained by expanding (9), is (1−2xz+z2)−1/2(1-2xz+z^2)^{-1/2}. The same series is obtained by summing the geometric series under 1π∫0π(x+i1−x2cos⁡φ)kdφ. \frac1\pi\int_0^\pi \bigl(x+i\sqrt{1-x^2}\cos\varphi\bigr)^k\,d\varphi. Indeed the normalized integral π−1∫0π\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi of (1−zx−iz1−x2cos⁡φ)−1(1-zx-iz\sqrt{1-x^2}\cos\varphi)^{-1} is ((1−zx)2+z2(1−x2))−1/2((1-zx)^2+z^2(1-x^2))^{-1/2}; the substitution tan⁡(φ/2)\tan(\varphi/2) verifies it near z=0z=0, with branch one there. Each base in the integrand has modulus at most one. Comparison of coefficients proves the bound.

4. Exact endpoint and norm formulas

Put m*=⌊d/2⌋m_*=\lfloor d/2\rfloor and AN=(2m*+1)cm*>0A_N=(2m_*+1)c_{m_*}>0. Then pN(0)=pN(1)=(−1)m*AN,∥pN∥L2(0,1)=AN.(13) \boxed{\displaystyle p_N(0)=p_N(1)=(-1)^{m_*}A_N,\qquad \|p_N\|_{L^2(0,1)}=A_N.} \qquad\text{(13)} Here is a direct verification. The endpoint sum is ∑m=0m*(4m+1)(−1)mcm\sum_{m=0}^{m_*}(4m+1)(-1)^m c_m. The ratio cm/cm−1=(2m−1)/(2m)c_m/c_{m-1}=(2m-1)/(2m) shows by subtraction that its partial sum is (−1)m*(2m*+1)cm*(-1)^{m_*}(2m_*+1)c_{m_*}. Orthogonality gives ∥pN∥22=∑m=0m*(4m+1)cm2=(2m*+1)2cm*2. \|p_N\|_2^2=\sum_{m=0}^{m_*}(4m+1)c_m^2 =(2m_*+1)^2c_{m_*}^2. The last equality telescopes by the same ratio. Induction on that ratio proves cm2≤1/(m+1)c_m^2\le1/(m+1). Hence AN≤2NA_N\le2\sqrt N, including N=2N=2, and ∥pN∥L1(0,1),∥pN∥L2(0,1)≤2N,∥pN∥L∞(0,1)≤NAN≤2N3/2.(14) \|p_N\|_{L^1(0,1)},\ \|p_N\|_{L^2(0,1)}\le2\sqrt N, \qquad \|p_N\|_{L^\infty(0,1)}\le N A_N\le2N^{3/2}. \qquad\text{(14)} The supremum bound follows from Cauchy–Schwarz in (10): the evaluation vector at TT has squared norm at most ∑k=0d(2k+1)=N2\sum_{k=0}^{d}(2k+1)=N^2, while that at 1/21/2 has norm ANA_N.

All fixed real derivatives also have polynomial bounds: ∥pN(r)∥∞≤(2N2)rNAN.(15) \|p_N^{(r)}\|_\infty \le(2N^2)^r N A_N . \qquad\text{(15)} To see this without importing a derivative inequality, expand 𝖫k′\mathsf L_k' in the Legendre basis. Integration by parts and (12) give coefficient 2ℓ+12\ell+1 at every ℓ<k\ell<k of parity opposite to kk, and zero otherwise. Their sum is k(k+1)/2≤N2k(k+1)/2\le N^2. Repeating this expansion at each derivative and using the factor two from 2T−12T-1 proves (15). Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the original coefficient sum in (10) by NANN A_N. In particular, on the complex distance-δ\delta neighborhood of [0,1][0,1], Taylor’s finite formula gives |pN(T)|≤NANexp⁡(2N2δ).(16) |p_N(T)|\le N A_N\exp(2N^2\delta). \qquad\text{(16)} This supplies a polynomial bound on collars of width O(N−2)O(N^{-2}), not on a fixed complex neighborhood.

5. The actual normal residue and its retained current

In the normal framing coordinates of C and D, bN=−a/ηb_N=-a/\eta, T=vaT=va, and νcut=−dηη∧Ω1−ηv. \nu_{\mathrm{ cut}}=-\frac{d\eta}{\eta}\wedge \frac{\Omega}{1-\eta v}. The exact finite residue is Res⁡E[PNnor(bN)νcut]=−pN(T)Ω.(17) \operatorname{Res}_E [P_N^{\mathrm{ nor}}(b_N)\nu_{\mathrm{ cut}}] =-p_N(T)\Omega . \qquad\text{(17)} With the original compact-cycle normalization, pairing (17) against the actual coherent source gives (11). The compatible south representative remains PNnor(bS−1)P_N^{\mathrm{ nor}}(b_S-1). No change of the affine clutch or deletion of 1−ηv1-\eta v before the residue is made.

The degree is 2m*2m_*, so the normal pole order is 2m*+12m_*+1: it is NN at odd levels and N−1N-1 at even levels. The polynomial compact residue bounds do not turn this into a bounded normal jet or identify the off-divisor source with its residue.

The two density endpoints in (13) agree, but the full current does not consequently vanish. For arbitrary diagonal CC, the actual integration-by-parts map is 1NTr⁡(𝒯N((PNnor)′)C)=(−1)m*AN(C00−Cdd)+f¯C′(1/2).(18) \boxed{\displaystyle \frac1N\operatorname{Tr} (\mathcal T_N((P_N^{\mathrm{ nor}})')C) =(-1)^{m_*}A_N(C_{00}-C_{dd}) +\overline f_C'(1/2).} \qquad\text{(18)} Indeed the endpoint formula in C gives the first term and the remaining integral is ∫01pN(T)f¯C′(T)dT\int_0^1p_N(T)\overline f_C'(T)\,dT. Here f¯C′\overline f_C' has degree at most d−1d-1, so (8) evaluates that integral exactly. Explicitly f¯C′(1/2)=d∑a=0d−12−(d−1)(d−1a)(Ca+1,a+1−Caa). \overline f_C'(1/2) =d\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}2^{-(d-1)}\binom{d-1}{a} (C_{a+1,a+1}-C_{aa}). This includes N=2N=2. For C=IC=I both terms vanish; for a marked source neither is to be omitted. The product-current correction of C is still present before pairing.

6. Scope of the new presentation

The cut probabilities are concentrated near the middle occupation. Precisely Pr⁡(|A−d/2|≥εd)≤2e−2ε2d.(19) \Pr(|A-d/2|\ge\varepsilon d) \le2e^{-2\varepsilon^2d}. \qquad\text{(19)} An elementary proof uses 𝔼et(A−d/2)=cosh⁡(t/2)d≤edt2/8\mathbb E e^{t(A-d/2)}=\cosh(t/2)^d\le e^{dt^2/8} and exponential Markov inequality with t=4εt=4\varepsilon. The hyperbolic-cosine bound follows by integrating tanh⁡x≤x\tanh x\le x. The estimate describes the probe weights; discarding their tails in a marked contraction also needs a bound on that contraction’s matrix entries.

Equation (11) gives a fixed interior latitude evaluation of the angularly averaged coherent source. In I’s existing classical cut-position chart, the central occupation has the noncentral complete parameter s=−2s=-2. This does not prove that the full new quantum source has just the old geometric critical point or the same stationary equations. An exponentially varying matrix kernel can shift a saddle in a binomial sum. Its entropy and all remaining variables must be retained in a new source analysis.

What is settled is a source-preserving choice of cut with a polynomially bounded compact framing density and exact current. The remaining centralizer coordinate, off-divisor normal transport, transverse source map, and geometric cycle coefficient are not supplied by this calculation. No new volume-conjecture filling case is claimed.
