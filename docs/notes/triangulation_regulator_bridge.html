Where the geometric ideal triangulation enters the period comparison

Research note, 26 October 2026. This answers the user’s question about the role of the ideal triangulation theorem in identifying the source extension with the marked Bloch regulator. It gives the common geometric input, an exact local quantum/central calculation, and the precise comparison still required. No new definition of the knot invariant or identification with a geometric-character state is made.

1. What the audited triangulation theorem supplies

The independent Ge audit, relative to the standard Epstein–Penner decomposition, supplies a complete positive geometric ideal triangulation T of every finite-volume cusped hyperbolic manifold. For the knot complement M, its tetrahedra have marked shapes z_j with Im(z_j)>0 and fundamental-class lift

beta_T=sum_j [z_j], D(beta_T)=Vol(M).             (1.1)

Thus the geometric starting point is available without alternatingness. The edge equations and marked peripheral monomials give, in the conventions already audited,

delta beta_T=-(1/2)m wedge l.                   (1.2)

The complete positive point selects the volume embedding and orientation. This is precisely the lift that the boundary equation alone omits. On an augmented representation chart, develop each ideal vertex using the representation and its chosen peripheral eigenflag and take tetrahedral cross-ratios. The face identifications give the edge equations and peripheral holonomies as identities. Positivity at the complete point ensures nondegeneracy there and hence on a Zariski open neighborhood of that point. This realizes the geometric peripheral branch in the gluing coordinates, keeping the augmented cover when one is required. The five-term calculation in the Bloch-lift note proves invariance under the oriented five-vertex chain relation. The regulator note gives

dR=-(Log(m)dLog(l)-Log(l)dLog(m))/4,
log||P_hyp||=2Vol(M)/pi.                         (1.3)

The same triangulation therefore supplies both the geometric gluing branch of the peripheral A-curve and its marked regulator period. An existence theorem for positive shapes does not specify a quantum boundary vector or source cycle.

2. The exact local quantum calculation

At a primitive n-th root omega, take the quantum torus

X Y=omega Y X,

and the tetrahedron relation (X+Y-1)v=0. The finite q-binomial expansion gives

(X+Y)^n=X^n+Y^n.                               (2.1)

For completeness, induction gives the ordered Gaussian-binomial coefficients. For 0<k<n their formula has a numerator 1-omega^n and only nonzero denominator factors 1-omega^r, 0<r<n. Those coefficients vanish even when n is composite. Hence

(X^n+Y^n-1)v=0.                                (2.2)

The root center has the ordinary tetrahedron equation s+t=1, s=Xn,t=Yn. This is an exact algebraic statement. It does not require a semiclassical estimate or an assumed quantum-Frobenius theorem for an arbitrary A-operator. Since the equation is central, it annihilates the whole cyclic tetrahedron module generated by v.

Here n is the order of the tetrahedral quantum-torus parameter. The actual ADO parameter q_N has order 2N, while tetrahedron conventions often use omega=q_N^2 of order N. A comparison must convert these parameters and the full peripheral monomial lattice explicitly. This local equation alone does not fix that conversion or the factor in the regulator action.

The monomial phases can also be retained at every parity. Choose lambda with

lambda^2=omega, lambda^n=(-1)^(n-1),
W(a,b)=lambda^(-ab) X^a Y^b.

Then direct commutation gives

W(a,b)W(c,d)=lambda^(ad-bc)W(a+c,b+d),
W(a,b)^n=(X^n)^a(Y^n)^b.                        (2.3)

For several tetrahedra use the product convention. Isotropic integral edge vectors therefore give commuting edge operators, whose n-th powers are exactly the corresponding classical monomials. If an edge equation is W(e)=c_e, its central equation is the monomial=c_e^n. The full c_e, including its chosen roots and phases, remains part of the gluing data.

Using z’‘=1-z^(-1) instead of 1-z also requires the sign in z’’=-(1-z)/z. It belongs in the classical edge character and its specified quantum root. Equations (2.2)–(2.3) do not erase it.

For the actual parameter omega=Q_N=q_N^2 there is a uniform compatible choice lambda=-q_N: lambda^2=Q_N and lambdaN=(-1)(N-1). Its inverse is exactly the constant character kappa=-q_N^(-1) in the full ADO shift gauge. This records a compatible root convention at every parity; it is not by itself a comparison of the quantum states. Using lambda=q_N at odd N instead would change W(a,b)^N by (-1)^(ab), as the local audit detects.

Concretely, start with the tensor product of the tetrahedron modules and form edge coinvariants by the images (W(e)-c_e)V. Use the centralizer of the edge operators for the descended action; it contains the marked peripheral operators. The central relation W(e)n-c_en acts by zero on these coinvariants, by factoring it through W(e)-c_e. Their central support therefore satisfies the classical gluing constraints and maps into their peripheral elimination curve. Localizing at the chosen geometric component selects the branch represented by (1.1). This is a support statement; by itself it does not prove nonvanishing of the global reduction, equality of two annihilator ideals, or identification of their periods. The later pointed tetrahedron gluing theorem does establish nonvanishing of the root reduction for S^3 knot exteriors: its saturated boundary module has dimension N at every level, including even N. Integral edge primitivity and strict shape-ordering maps remove a global branching requirement for this module construction. The later integral half-longitude theorem identifies its saturated boundary lattice using the marked meridian and half the preferred longitude. This gives TY=q^2 YT without dividing by two modulo an even level. Its scalar lift retains the edge character and the actual central longitude. The projected generator’s actual ADO period and regulator norm are not identified by these module and lattice statements.

3. Why the affine source is useful here

After converting to one quantum-parameter and peripheral-lattice convention, suppose the geometric gluing module is the homogeneous block C of an actual source extension

sigma(v)=C v+c, U=[C c;0 1].

At a root its augmented monodromy is [H s;0 1], so exactly

det(V-U_cyc)=(V-1)det(V-H).                      (3.1)

The actual rational ADO period has a V=1 sector. The geometric block need not: if I-H is invertible, its source-normalized period is (I-H)^(-1)s, as proved in the affine extension note. Thus the geometric block and the rational period can belong to one extension without being the same unpointed homogeneous module.

This avoids a literal identification that is generically impossible. If a geometric root-central curve satisfies A_geom(U,V)=0 with A_geom(U,1) not identically zero, it cannot equal the whole rational ADO V=1 sector over variable U. At the complete fiber U=V=1 the sectors may meet, and the extension and its integral pointing carry information that a generic central curve comparison would discard. The full A-polynomial’s abelian factor can mask this distinction.

Accordingly the triangulation is a candidate construction of the geometric block and the Bloch lift. One must still construct its relative source data, compare the resulting pointed extension with the actual knot operator and finite cut state, and identify the selected period’s norm with (1.3). These are comparisons of actual objects; the source cannot be chosen to force the desired volume.

The finite cut-state argument supplies a nonzero actual all-level meridian annihilator independently of a triangulation. It separates this existence question from the geometric block and source identification that T must help establish.

4. Keep central and semiclassical shapes distinct

At q->1, a tetrahedron’s commuting coordinates x,y obey x+y=1. At a root, its central coordinates are s=Xn,t=Yn and obey s+t=1. These are two uses of the same polynomial relation, with different coordinates and period data.

For one root coordinate, s=x^n. Fixing a classical small coordinate x=z as n changes makes that center vary. Fixing the center s=z instead gives x=exp(Log(z)/n), tending to one. Neither choice alone constructs globally compatible quantum gluing data. In particular the two copies of the gluing curve must not be identified merely because their displayed equations have the same form.

There is a quantitative local test. For omega=exp(2pi*i/n) define the cyclic dilogarithm by its algebraic product, with branches retained,

D_n(x)=product_(k=1)^(n-1)(1-omega^k*x)^(k/n).

With compatible branches near x=0, cancellation of adjacent factors gives

D_n(omega*x)/D_n(x)=(1-x)/(1-x^n)^(1/n).

The right side has cyclic norm one. This is the local Hilbert-90 primitive on its specified root cover, not an arbitrary scalar normalization of a knot period.

Its modulus is branch-independent. For fixed z!=0,1 and a chosen Log(z)=w,

log|D_n(exp(w/n))|=O_w(log n).                   (4.1)

To prove this, start from the exact identity |D_n(1)|=sqrt(n), obtained by pairing k and n-k and using product_(k=1)^(n-1) |1-omega^k|=n. For min(k,n-k) larger than a fixed multiple of |w|, integrating the logarithmic derivative along tw/n, 0<=t<=1, bounds each log-modulus change by C_w/min(k,n-k). The harmonic sum is O(log n). At the finitely many remaining endpoint indices, the ratios tend to nonzero constants |w+/-2piik|/(2pi*k), since z!=1. They give a bounded additional contribution. This proves both an upper and a lower polynomial bound in (4.1).

For real positive z!=1 there is the stronger exact identity

|D_n(z^(1/n))|^2=|(1-z)/(1-z^(1/n))|.           (4.2)

In contrast, for fixed |x|<1 a uniformly convergent Fourier series or its Riemann sum, using the logarithm normalized by Log D_n(0)=0, gives

(1/n)Log D_n(x) -> -Li_2(x)/(2pi*i).             (4.3)

Indeed integral_0^1 texp(2piirt)dt=1/(2piir), and Log(1-xexp(2piit))=-sum_(r>=1)x^r exp(2piirt)/r. The real part of (4.3) is -Im Li_2(x)/(2pi). The full Bloch norm also has the logarithmic correction in (1.3); a bare modulus cannot be substituted for that corrected regulator norm.

Thus holding geometric shapes as fixed root centers is not a proof of their tetrahedral volume exponent. Internal state sums and their selected cycles can still grow exponentially; (4.1) concerns the stated local normalization only. The exact trajectory and the source contraction must be established in the knot comparison.

5. Literature definitions also distinguish the state data

There is a useful coverage check, without adopting any comparison proof. The definitions of quantum hyperbolic link patterns in Baseilhac–Benedetti, pages 32–33 specialize the link pattern in S^3 to the trivial flat character; cusped geometric-character invariants have different input data. Consequently putting the hyperbolic shapes into a geometric-character state cannot be assumed to give the already fixed Kashaev state. Their claimed comparison theorems are not premises of this note. The actual Kashaev/ADO crossing comparison remains the independently checked one in this project, including even levels.

Likewise, positive vertex ordering on individual tetrahedra does not supply a global branching. The audited geometric theorem does not certify that extra quantum input. A comparison using face permutation intertwiners or weak branchings must check their normalizations and scalar coherence for the chosen state.

6. The remaining theorem

The triangulation theorem supplies (1.1), the geometric branch, and a positive classical representative for the desired norm. The local algebra (2.1)–(2.3) supplies an exact quantum gluing support calculation. The unresolved theorem is to identify the actual source extension and its pointed period with the relative gluing period retaining beta_T, and prove

(4/N)log|F_N(1)|-log||P_hyp|| ->0.

Existence of a positive triangulation does not itself prove this period identity or its norm estimate. Its role is to provide the common geometric representative on which that comparison can be constructed, without restricting to a small knot census.
