A nonzero microscopic endpoint insertion at a nonreal local saddle

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The explicit sharp endpoint baseline has the actual Gamma connection coefficients, uniformly on each prescribed fixed-height rectangle. At a genuinely nonreal point the two conjugate modes have unequal absolute values. This gives a polynomial lower bound, uniformly for bounded real endpoint parameters including zero, despite their oscillation in log⁡N\log N. The exact designated-half representatives inherit that bound.

The last section applies this insertion to a supplied nonzero local Gaussian saddle contribution. It neither supplies the geometric cycle of the original closing nor estimates its other currents.

1. Actual normalization and the tall connection formula

Write h=N−1,CN(x)=2cos⁡(2πx+πh),wN(α)=2cosh⁡(αh),BN(x,α)=KN(wN(α),CN(x)).(1) h=N^{-1},\qquad C_N(x)=2\cos(2\pi x+\pi h),\qquad w_N(\alpha)=2\cosh(\alpha h),\qquad B_N(x,\alpha)=K_N(w_N(\alpha),C_N(x)). \qquad\text{(1)} The endpoint kernel is the original canonical polynomial. Fix a real parameter bound |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A, a target x0∈(0,1/2)x_0\in(0,1/2), and a sufficiently small buffered real interval about x0x_0. Fix arbitrary finite upper and lower heights. The resulting rectangles, including all buffers below, have real parts strictly between 00 and 1/21/2.

Let VNV_N be the particular two-value-normalized baseline of the reflected sharp connection theorem. Thus its cutoff, mode pair and actual physical anchor are fixed as in that theorem. In particular, at a physical anchor xj*=j*/Nx_{j_*}=j_*/N in its fixed right buffer, VN(xj*)=BN(xj*),VN(xj*−h)=BN(xj*−h).(2) V_N(x_{j_*})=B_N(x_{j_*}),\qquad V_N(x_{j_*}-h)=B_N(x_{j_*}-h). \qquad\text{(2)} The anchor remains a fixed distance from both endpoints and the fold. No arbitrary homogeneous solution is added to this normalization.

Put ν=α/π,ℓ(x)=Log⁡sin⁡(πx),LN=log⁡(N/π),SN(x)=(N/π)sin⁡(πx).(3) \nu=\alpha/\pi,\qquad \ell(x)=\operatorname{Log}\sin(\pi x),\qquad L_N=\log(N/\pi),\qquad S_N(x)=(N/\pi)\sin(\pi x). \qquad\text{(3)} The logarithm is the continuation of the real logarithm. Indeed, ℜsin⁡(πx)>0\Re\sin(\pi x)>0 on 0<ℜx<1/20<\Re x<1/2, so this logarithm is single-valued on every rectangle under consideration.

For real ν≠0\nu\ne0, define k±(ν)=Γ(±iν)Γ(1/2±iν/2)4,C0(ν)=k++k−,C1(ν)=iν(k+−k−).(4) k_\pm(\nu)= \frac{\Gamma(\pm i\nu)}{\Gamma(1/2\pm i\nu/2)^4},\qquad C_0(\nu)=k_++k_-,\qquad C_1(\nu)=i\nu(k_+-k_-). \qquad\text{(4)} The removable values are C0(0)=2(γE+4log⁡2)π2,C1(0)=2π2.(5) C_0(0)=\frac{2(\gamma_E+4\log2)}{\pi^2},\qquad C_1(0)=\frac2{\pi^2}. \qquad\text{(5)} Use the specified logarithm to set gα(SN(x))=1SN(x){C0(ν)cos(ν(LN+ℓ(x)))+C1(ν)sin⁡(ν(LN+ℓ(x)))ν}.(6) g_\alpha(S_N(x))= \frac1{S_N(x)} \left\{C_0(\nu)\cos(\nu(L_N+\ell(x))) +C_1(\nu)\frac{\sin(\nu(L_N+\ell(x)))}{\nu}\right\}. \qquad\text{(6)} The sine quotient is removable at zero. For nonzero real ν\nu, this is equivalently gα(SN(x))=k+SN(x)−1+iν+k−SN(x)−1−iν.(7) g_\alpha(S_N(x)) =k_+S_N(x)^{-1+i\nu}+k_-S_N(x)^{-1-i\nu}. \qquad\text{(7)}

Theorem 1. On every smaller buffered rectangle of the prescribed fixed height there is ϵ0>0\epsilon_0>0 such that, for every fixed spatial derivative order kk, ∂xkVN(x,α)=∂xkgα(SN(x))+Ok(N−1−ϵ0).(8) \partial_x^k V_N(x,\alpha)= \partial_x^k g_\alpha(S_N(x))+O_k(N^{-1-\epsilon_0}). \qquad\text{(8)} Constants are uniform for real |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A, at both parities of NN, and also for NN-dependent real parameters in this compact interval. The exponent does not decrease merely because the prescribed height is larger; the constants and required buffers may depend on it. No endpoint-parameter derivative version of (8) is asserted.

Proof. The archived actual value connection theorem gives, at the anchor in (2), (BN(xj*)N(BN(xj*)−BN(xj*−h)))=(gα(SN(xj*))∂xgα(SN(xj*)))+O(N−1−ϵ).(9) \binom{B_N(x_{j_*})} {N(B_N(x_{j_*})-B_N(x_{j_*}-h))} = \binom{g_\alpha(S_N(x_{j_*}))} {\partial_x g_\alpha(S_N(x_{j_*}))} +O(N^{-1-\epsilon}). \qquad\text{(9)} Its error is uniform through the coalescence α=0\alpha=0 and at both parities. In particular the second entry is an actual two-value quantity; we have not replaced an independent spectral normal by a derivative.

Let H0,N,H1,NH_{0,N},H_{1,N} be the coalescence-safe mode pair used to define VNV_N, and let ψ0(x)=cos⁡(νℓ(x))sin⁡(πx),ψ1(x)=sin⁡(νℓ(x))νsin⁡(πx).(10) \psi_0(x)=\frac{\cos(\nu\ell(x))}{\sin(\pi x)},\qquad \psi_1(x)=\frac{\sin(\nu\ell(x))}{\nu\sin(\pi x)}. \qquad\text{(10)} The sine-factor estimates in the reflected theorem give Hi,N=ψi+Ohol,x(h)(11) H_{i,N}=\psi_i+O_{\mathrm{hol},x}(h) \qquad\text{(11)} on the whole prescribed fixed-height rectangle, uniformly on the real parameter compact. This follows there from the sharper centered estimate at x+h/2x+h/2. The same estimate holds for all fixed spatial derivatives on buffered rectangles. Consequently the balanced frame 𝒴N(x)=(H0,N(x)H1,N(x)[H0,N(x)−H0,N(x−h)]/h[H1,N(x)−H1,N(x−h)]/h)=(ψ0ψ1ψ0′ψ1′)+O(h)(12) \mathcal Y_N(x)= \begin{pmatrix} H_{0,N}(x)&H_{1,N}(x)\\ [H_{0,N}(x)-H_{0,N}(x-h)]/h& [H_{1,N}(x)-H_{1,N}(x-h)]/h \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\psi_0&\psi_1\\\psi_0'&\psi_1'\end{pmatrix} +O(h) \qquad\text{(12)} is uniformly invertible at the real anchor. The limiting determinant is πcos⁡(πx)/sin⁡3(πx)\pi\cos(\pi x)/\sin^3(\pi x), independently of ν\nu. The divided-color frame in the reflected theorem and (12) are related at the anchor by an invertible row scaling; hence they define exactly the same coefficient vector 𝒂N\mathbf a_N for VNV_N.

Define the explicit leading coefficients a0,N0=πN{C0cos(νLN)+C1sin⁡(νLN)ν},a1,N0=πN{−νC0sin(νLN)+C1cos(νLN)}.(13) a_{0,N}^{\,0}=\frac{\pi}{N} \left\{C_0\cos(\nu L_N)+C_1\frac{\sin(\nu L_N)}{\nu}\right\}, \qquad a_{1,N}^{\,0}=\frac{\pi}{N} \left\{-\nu C_0\sin(\nu L_N)+C_1\cos(\nu L_N)\right\}. \qquad\text{(13)} The addition formulas give exactly (ψ0,ψ1)𝒂N0=gα(SN(x)),(ψ0′,ψ1′)𝒂N0=∂xgα(SN(x)).(14) (\psi_0,\psi_1)\mathbf a_N^{\,0}=g_\alpha(S_N(x)),\qquad (\psi_0',\psi_1')\mathbf a_N^{\,0}=\partial_x g_\alpha(S_N(x)). \qquad\text{(14)} Uniformly on the real parameter compact, c/N≤|𝒂N0|≤C(1+log⁡N)/N.(15) c/N\le|\mathbf a_N^{\,0}|\le C(1+\log N)/N. \qquad\text{(15)} Here |sin⁡(νLN)/ν|≤LN|\sin(\nu L_N)/\nu|\le L_N, including its value at zero. For the lower bound at nonzero real ν\nu, the two entries of N𝒂N0/πN\mathbf a_N^{\,0}/\pi are 2ℜ(k+eiνLN)2\Re(k_+e^{i\nu L_N}) and −2νℑ(k+eiνLN)-2\nu\Im(k_+e^{i\nu L_N}). Their Euclidean norm is at least 2|k+|min⁡(1,|ν|)2|k_+|\min(1,|\nu|), uniformly bounded below on the real compact because the Gamma function has no zeros and |νk+|→1/π2|\nu k_+|\to1/\pi^2. At zero the second entry is C1(0)C_1(0). Combining (9), (12) and (14) proves 𝒂N−𝒂N0=O(N−1−ϵ)+O((1+log⁡N)/N2)=O(N−1−ϵ0),(16) \mathbf a_N-\mathbf a_N^{\,0} =O(N^{-1-\epsilon})+O((1+\log N)/N^2) =O(N^{-1-\epsilon_0}), \qquad\text{(16)} where one may take ϵ0=min⁡(ϵ,1/2)\epsilon_0=\min(\epsilon,1/2). In particular this is also a relative vector estimate |𝒂N−𝒂N0|/|𝒂N0|=O(N−ϵ0)|\mathbf a_N-\mathbf a_N^{\,0}|/|\mathbf a_N^{\,0}| =O(N^{-\epsilon_0}); no lower bound for either separate coalesced coordinate is required. Multiplying these coefficients by the bounded explicit modes and using (11) proves (8), including its derivatives. This proof transports two actual coefficients in an explicit global mode frame; it is not analytic continuation of a small real-axis error. ▫\square

2. A uniform nonreal lower bound

Let 𝒦\mathcal K be a compact subset of the prescribed rectangle with ℑx≠0\Im x\ne0, and reserve a slightly larger such compact neighborhood. Then β(x):=ℑℓ(x),infx∈𝒦|β(x)|=β0>0.(17) \beta(x):=\Im\ell(x),\qquad \inf_{x\in\mathcal K}|\beta(x)|=\beta_0>0. \qquad\text{(17)} Indeed, for x=a+iyx=a+iy in this strip, ℑsin⁡(πx)=cos⁡(πa)sinh⁡(πy)\Im\sin(\pi x)=\cos(\pi a)\sinh(\pi y), which is nonzero exactly when y≠0y\ne0.

Theorem 2. For all sufficiently large NN, uniformly for x∈𝒦x\in\mathcal K and real |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A, cN≤|VN(x,α)|≤C(1+log⁡N)N.(18) \frac{c}{N}\le |V_N(x,\alpha)|\le\frac{C(1+\log N)}N. \qquad\text{(18)} Every fixed spatial derivative has the upper bound Ck(1+log⁡N)/NC_k(1+\log N)/N. Moreover VN(x,α)gα(SN(x))=1+O(N−ϵ0).(19) \frac{V_N(x,\alpha)}{g_\alpha(S_N(x))} =1+O(N^{-\epsilon_0}). \qquad\text{(19)} The denominator is nonzero throughout this compact neighborhood.

Proof. For real ν≠0\nu\ne0, k−=k+¯k_-=\overline{k_+}. The reverse triangle inequality in (7), with Log⁡SN=LN+ℓ(x)\operatorname{Log} S_N=L_N+\ell(x), gives |gα(SN(x))|≥2π|k+(ν)|N|sin⁡(πx)||sinh⁡(νβ(x))|.(20) |g_\alpha(S_N(x))| \ge \frac{2\pi |k_+(\nu)|}{N|\sin(\pi x)|} |\sinh(\nu\beta(x))|. \qquad\text{(20)} This inequality eliminates the phase eiνlog⁡(N/π)e^{i\nu\log(N/\pi)}. The Gamma function is nonzero, and its expansion at zero gives |ν||k+(ν)|→1/π2.(21) |\nu|\,|k_+(\nu)|\longrightarrow1/\pi^2. \qquad\text{(21)} Thus the right side of NN times (20) has the strictly positive continuous limit 2|β(x)|π|sin⁡(πx)|.(22) \frac{2|\beta(x)|}{\pi|\sin(\pi x)|}. \qquad\text{(22)} At zero itself, (5)–(6) give g0(SN(x))=2πNsin⁡(πx){log⁡(N/π)+ℓ(x)+γE+4log⁡2}.(23) g_0(S_N(x))= \frac{2}{\pi N\sin(\pi x)} \{\log(N/\pi)+\ell(x)+\gamma_E+4\log2\}. \qquad\text{(23)} The imaginary part of the expression in braces proves the lower bound (22) directly. Compactness therefore makes (20)–(23) uniform for the entire real parameter interval, including sequences α=αN→0\alpha=\alpha_N\to0. This proves |gα|≥c0/N|g_\alpha|\ge c_0/N.

The coalescence-safe formula (6) and its fixed spatial derivatives are bounded by Ck(1+log⁡N)/NC_k(1+\log N)/N; the real parameter restriction prevents growth of its LNL_N-dependent oscillations. Now (8) proves (18)–(19) and the derivative assertions. ▫\square

The restriction to a nonreal compact is essential to this argument. No lower bound is asserted on the real interval, where the two conjugate terms may interfere with equal absolute values.

3. Exact physical representatives and shared insertions

Choose either sign σ∈{+1,−1}\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}. Use the sharp one-sided assembly on the same window, with any fixed positive error rate γ\gamma below its local sharp rate. It gives HNσ=VN+CNσ(24) H_N^\sigma=V_N+C_N^\sigma \qquad\text{(24)} with exactly the original physical values and first spectral normals, and, for every fixed spatial derivative, |∂xkCNσ|≤CNpe−Nγwhen σℑx≥0.(25) |\partial_x^k C_N^\sigma|\le C N^p e^{-N\gamma} \quad\text{when }\sigma\Im x\ge0. \qquad\text{(25)} Therefore (8), (18) and (19) also hold with HNσH_N^\sigma in place of VNV_N on every nonreal compact in its designated half. This is a lower bound for an exact local full-jet representative, not for the canonical polynomial evaluated away from its physical nodes. Nor is it a lower bound for its original closed trace.

For a fixed number rr of factors, let xi(z)x_i(z) be fixed holomorphic functions near a point z*∈ℂdz_*\in\mathbb C^d. Require that their images of a common compact neighborhood lie in the designated nonreal compacts just described. Let all αi\alpha_i be real and bounded, and set ℬN(z)=∏i=1rHi,Nσi(xi(z),αi).(26) \mathcal B_N(z)= \prod_{i=1}^r H_{i,N}^{\sigma_i}(x_i(z),\alpha_i). \qquad\text{(26)} Then uniformly on a smaller neighborhood, |ℬN(z*)|≥cN−r,|∂zλℬN(z)|≤Cλ(1+log⁡NN)r.(27) |\mathcal B_N(z_*)|\ge cN^{-r},\qquad |\partial_z^\lambda\mathcal B_N(z)| \le C_\lambda\left(\frac{1+\log N}{N}\right)^r. \qquad\text{(27)} The same statement holds with any or all factors replaced by their baselines. It follows directly from the product and chain rules. Repeated/shared colors are allowed: their physical first normals are retained by the ordinary product rule, not independent sampling. Endpoint-parameter derivatives or normal derivatives of the insertion are not asserted to have a nonzero lower bound.

4. Conditional local stationary-phase consequence

The following hypotheses describe an already supplied local saddle contribution. They are not conclusions about the original global integration cycle.

Let Γloc\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}} have a fixed real dd-dimensional parameterization z=z(u)z=z(u), with z(0)=z*z(0)=z_* and |z(u)−z*|≤C|u||z(u)-z_*|\le C|u|, contained in the neighborhood of (27). Suppose its bare amplitude and integration density satisfy a uniform absolute bound |aN(z(u))dμ|≤Cdu|a_N(z(u))\,d\mu|\le C\,du, and its phase obeys ℜ(Φ(z(u))−Φ(z*))≤−c|u|2.(28) \Re(\Phi(z(u))-\Phi(z_*))\le-c|u|^2. \qquad\text{(28)} Suppose the bare local integral has its specified nonzero asymptotic IN0:=∫ΓloceNΦ(z)aN(z)dμ=eNΦ(z*)N−d/2{A*+o(1)},A*≠0.(29) I_N^0:=\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}}e^{N\Phi(z)}a_N(z)\,d\mu =e^{N\Phi(z_*)}N^{-d/2}\{A_*+o(1)\},\qquad A_*\ne0. \qquad\text{(29)} Uniform versions use uniform constants in (27)–(29) and inf⁡|A*|>0\inf|A_*|>0. A common scalar carrier may be factored out of both integrals without changing the assertion.

Corollary. Under these supplied local hypotheses, ∫ΓloceNΦ(z)aN(z)ℬN(z)dμ=ℬN(z*)eNΦ(z*)N−d/2{A*+o(1)}.(30) \int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}}e^{N\Phi(z)} a_N(z)\mathcal B_N(z)\,d\mu =\mathcal B_N(z_*)\,e^{N\Phi(z_*)}N^{-d/2} \{A_*+o(1)\}. \qquad\text{(30)} In particular the endpoint insertion does not annihilate this already specified nonzero local coefficient, even when its own coefficient oscillates in log⁡N\log N.

Proof. By (27) and the mean-value estimate along short paths in the fixed neighborhood, |ℬN(z(u))−ℬN(z*)|≤C|u|(1+log⁡NN)r.(31) |\mathcal B_N(z(u))-\mathcal B_N(z_*)| \le C|u|\left(\frac{1+\log N}{N}\right)^r. \qquad\text{(31)} Equations (28) and the absolute amplitude bound show that subtracting ℬN(z*)IN0\mathcal B_N(z_*)I_N^0 from the integral in (30) has modulus at most CeNℜΦ(z*)N−(d+1)/2(1+log⁡NN)r.(32) C e^{N\Re\Phi(z_*)}N^{-(d+1)/2} \left(\frac{1+\log N}{N}\right)^r. \qquad\text{(32)} After division by |ℬN(z*)eNΦ(z*)N−d/2||\mathcal B_N(z_*)e^{N\Phi(z_*)}N^{-d/2}|, this is O((1+log⁡N)r/N)=o(1)O((1+\log N)^r/\sqrt N)=o(1), by (27). Equation (29) proves (30). ▫\square

The exact one-sided trace construction still retains its real interface, both exterior half-contours, their vertical sides, all grid aliases, the original crossing, all shared spectators, and all physical rows outside the window. Nothing in (30) estimates these other terms or rules out cancellation between distinct local cycles in their closing. In particular it does not identify the original geometric cycle coefficient or prove a whole-source lower bound or a parent-volume comparison.

5. Coverage and direct sources

The earlier fixed-strip endpoint asymptotics already continue the connection formula to a narrow strip and prove a local multiplier consequence for parameters of size 1/log⁡N1/\log N. That coverage is not used as a proof dependency here. The new point is the actual two-coefficient transport in the explicit tall mode frame, followed by the nonreal reverse-triangle estimate uniformly for all bounded real parameters. The local Gaussian comparison above is included in full to specify the remaining cycle hypothesis.
