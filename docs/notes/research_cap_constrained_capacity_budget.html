Absolute cap-star comparison: exact constrained capacity and its missing budget

31 October 2026. analytical research theorem. Unpublished. The original ordinary targets are one, the original protected MAIN pair is u,v, and Phi_D is the complete capacity in the original normalization Vol(Y_D)/(2pi). No general absolute cap bound is asserted.

The main conclusions are exact variational reductions. They explain why the leaf and spectral-star results do not yet prove an absolute cap-only gap. Section 5 gives a literal obstruction to the stronger inference from LOCAL star hypotheses alone; it is explicitly not a common-stationary counterexample.

1. The exact one-cap residual capacity

Name one original cap F=(u,A,v,C), with triangles uAC,uvC and matching edge uC. Its original cap degrees are q_A=1-s,q_C=s, with 0<=s<=1/2; u,v remain zero. Every OTHER port in this section is CROSS.

Delete only F. Let R(s) be the maximum remaining complete-triangle entropy with residual targets

rA=s,rC=1−s,rf=1(f other ordinary),ru=rv=0. r_A=s,\qquad r_C=1-s,\qquad r_f=1\ (f\text{ other ordinary}),\qquad r_u=r_v=0.\tag{1}

Put R(s)=-infinity when this fiber is empty. On its compact effective domain R is concave and upper semicontinuous, by the compact concave triangle-flow problem. The exact original cap-sector maximum is

Mcap=max0≤s≤1/2R(s). M_{\mathrm{cap}}=\max_{0\le s\le1/2}R(s).\tag{2}

The original complete capacity has a different interval and a local entropy term. In the complete row F, its first triangle has loads (0,t,1/2-t) and its second is the pure-C half-row. Hence q_A=t, q_C=1-t, 0<=t<=1/2, and its entropy is 2f(t). Restoring its original targets in (1) therefore gives

ΦD=max1/2≤s≤1{R(s)+2f(s−12)}. \Phi_D=\max_{1/2\le s\le1}\{R(s)+2f(s-\tfrac12)\}.\tag{3}

We used f(1-s)=f(s-1/2) on this interval. There is no change of mark, vertex, source pairing or target in (2)-(3). In particular concavity of R alone does not compare these two maxima: they use opposite halves of its effective domain. No reflection symmetry R(s)=R(1-s) is known for an arbitrary prescribed exterior.

At an actual finite-common-weight cap-only stationary point with 0<nu<1/2, the cap equation is L_A=L_C. The remaining CROSS allocations attain their local triangle Legendre formulas at these SAME weights. For every feasible s’, their dual affine upper differs from its value at nu by (s’-nu)log(L_C/L_A)=0. Consequently

R(ν)=max0≤s≤1R(s)=Mcap. R(\nu)=\max_{0\le s\le1}R(s)=M_{\mathrm{cap}}.\tag{4}

This needs neither differentiability of R nor a separate local maximality assumption for a Green row: all remaining rows are complete concave triangle rows. It does not assert existence of such a stationary point, and does not classify arbitrary boundary maxima of the cap-restricted sector.

2. The cap-forest quotient preserves every target exactly

Now allow the at most two residual caps of classified positive-value data. Their ordinary incidence edges form a forest, as proved in the cap-component theorem, section 2. Let p be the number of caps, and relax EACH cap row from its prescribed overloaded orientation to ANY nonnegative split of unit supply between its two ordinary vertices. Its entropy is still zero. All other ports remain CROSS. Call this relaxed maximum M_rel.

For a tree component with n vertices and n-1 cap edges, deleting those edges leaves total residual target n-(n-1)=1. Every nonnegative residual vector (r_a) on that component with sum r_a=1 is realizable by the relaxed cap rows. To see this, first let r be concentrated at one root. Give each edge’s full unit to the vertex farther from that root; every nonroot vertex receives one and the root receives zero. Take the convex combination of these allocations with coefficients r_a. The restored cap load at vertex a is exactly 1-r_a.

Thus deleting the relaxed cap rows and merging each component is an EXACT identification of the classical flow problem. Every merged target is one; unmerged ordinary targets remain one; protected targets remain zero. This is a flow quotient only, not a triangulated-sphere edge contraction or an identity coupon. The true cap orientations impose additional inequalities and are NOT silently discarded from the definition of M_cap.

Write the original complete weighted potential as

FD(L)=∑t original triangleQt(L)−∑a ordinarylog⁡La,ΦD=infL>0FD(L),C0=2f(14)=voct2π. \begin{aligned} F_D(L)&=\sum_{t\text{ original triangle}}Q_t(L)-\sum_{a\text{ ordinary}}\log L_a,\\ \Phi_D&=\inf_{L>0}F_D(L),\qquad C_0=2f(\tfrac14)=\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{2\pi}. \end{aligned}\tag{5}

Let E_cap be the linear subspace of log weights constant on every cap-tree component. Each cap’s two ordinary weights then equal a common B, and its COMPLETE weighted value is exactly

Q(0,B,B)+Q(0,0,B)=C0+log⁡B. Q(0,B,B)+Q(0,0,B)=C_0+\log B.\tag{6}

In a component with n vertices and n-1 caps, the n target logs and n-1 cap logs leave exactly one target log. Compact concave duality for the remaining CROSS rows therefore gives the exact relaxed identity

Mrel=inflog⁡L∈EcapFD(L)−pC0=ΦD+Δcap−pC0,Δcap:=inflog⁡L∈EcapFD(L)−ΦD≥0. \begin{aligned} M_{\mathrm{rel}}&=\inf_{\log L\in E_{\mathrm{cap}}}F_D(L)-pC_0 =\Phi_D+\Delta_{\mathrm{cap}}-pC_0,\\ \Delta_{\mathrm{cap}}&:=\inf_{\log L\in E_{\mathrm{cap}}}F_D(L)-\Phi_D\ge0. \end{aligned}\tag{7}

The relaxed fiber is nonempty: start with an original feasible complete flow, remove the cap rows, and merge their components. Each cap has unit ordinary supply, so the same component totals are one. This also verifies the normalization in (7). Compact hypograph separation proves the dual equality even if a boundary multiplier is not attained.

For the actual closed cap sector one only has M_cap<=M_rel. However, at classified cap-only stationary data with all cap splits strict and finite common ordinary weights, equality DOES hold. The cap equations place these weights in E_cap, the complete rows attain Q, and the original demands cancel all logarithms. The actual feasible flow attains the dual value in (7), hence

Mcap=Mrel=ΦD+Δcap−pC0. M_{\mathrm{cap}}=M_{\mathrm{rel}}=\Phi_D+\Delta_{\mathrm{cap}}-pC_0.\tag{8}

This is an exact identification of the classical stationary envelope, not a claim that Delta_cap<=p C0. The latter inequality is the missing unsigned absolute comparison in this formulation.

3. What the new leaf/star mechanism proves, and what it still needs

If there are no Green ports, a nontrivial maximal-weight cap component has a nonroot leaf whose other incident ports are all CROSS. The star theorem therefore applies at ANY cap-child valence, with 0<nu<1/2 from the original demand. This removes the earlier degree-two limitation for a RELATIVE star improvement.

At the common stationary weights in (8), its weighted block envelope touches the original sharp block action. Indeed the CROSS neighbors supply nu to the internal A face, while the cap supplies nu to boundary C. Their log-weight discrepancy is nu(log L_A-log L_C)=0. With the unchanged exterior included, the full weighted envelope is precisely the value in (8), not an unrelated independently optimized number.

The star theorem gives some eta>0 below that envelope. Other cap rows at their equal ordinary weights have polynomial weighted size at +2, and only an arbitrarily small extra exponential cost on a sufficiently small complex +2 disk. Other CROSS rows have the global-real coefficientwise endpoint majorant and its thin-strip continuation. Thus, when using these common stationary weights and retaining the whole internal A^d contraction, the relevant source bound has rate at most

ΦD+Δcap−pC0−η. \Phi_D+\Delta_{\mathrm{cap}}-pC_0-\eta.\tag{9}

after reducing eta to absorb those small strip costs. Shared boundary variables and original ordinary targets are retained by the ONE final Cauchy extraction. A second cap does not involve this leaf A, so it belongs legitimately to the unchanged exterior.

Formula (9) is an absolute gap below Phi_D IF

Δcap−pC0<η. \Delta_{\mathrm{cap}}-pC_0<\eta.\tag{10}

In particular the nonstrict variational inequality Delta_cap<=p C0 would suffice, because the star gives a strict positive eta. But no current theorem compares eta to Delta_cap-p C0 in general. The leaf mechanism alone therefore has NOT closed cap-only high maxima.

For localizations around a stationary datum inside a larger source, one must use the genuine boundary coefficients of the contracted star and exterior-array fibers. Cutting individual internal cap indices would destroy the sum controlled by the theorem. The original block-contraction theorem gives that precise localization interface.

These statements make no claim about a full filling period: an absolute fixed-strip gap on the entire relevant source complement is still required before the existing whole-period theorem applies.

4. The tempting exterior packing contact is not present

Deleting F removes BOTH triangles uAC,uvC and their common MATCHING edge uC. The remaining positive CROSS disk has boundary u-A-C-v-u. It gives contacts uA,AC,Cv and the marked parallel-line edge uv. It does NOT give a contact uC. Consequently L_A=L_C does not force A to touch v by copying C’s radius: the assertion that C already touches both lines has used a missing cap contact.

There is a direct boundary-coordinate check. Suppose a positive exterior disk development has the prescribed boundary fan loads nu and 1-nu. Put theta=2pi nu, equal circle radius r, centers A=(0,0), C=(2r,0), and n=(cos theta,sin theta). Let the two lines be

u:n⋅z=r,v:n⋅z=2rcos⁡θ−r. u:\ n\cdot z=r,\qquad v:\ n\cdot z=2r\cos\theta-r.\tag{11}

They are parallel; A touches u, C touches v, and A,C are tangent. The counterclockwise fan from the AC ray at A to its u ray has angle theta. At C the fan from its v ray to the CA ray has angle 2pi-theta. Thus the normalized exterior loads are exactly nu,1-nu. The line separation is

H=2r(1−cos⁡θ)=4rsin⁡2(πν), H=2r(1-\cos\theta)=4r\sin^2(\pi\nu),\tag{12}

not the extra-contact value 2r. For 1/4<nu<1/2 both entire disks fit between the two lines, and neither has the other line contact. Their distance to that other line is r-2r cos theta>r. At nu=1/4 the extra contacts first occur.

This is a literal local boundary configuration, not a claim that every such boundary has an embedded finite interior CROSS packing, or that an arbitrary stationary disk develops injectively. It is enough to identify the missing step in the proposed immediate extra-contact contradiction. A global disk theorem could impose further restrictions; none is inferred here from the unfilled cap triangle.

5. A literal warning: local star hypotheses do not bound its exterior

The following fixed matched sphere proves that LOCAL star hypotheses alone cannot bound the full weighted envelope by Phi_D. Start with the five-vertex simple sphere having ports

(u,A,v,C),(A,u,C,Z),(v,C,u,Z). (u,A,v,C),\qquad(A,u,C,Z),\qquad(v,C,u,Z).

Stellar-subdivide the last port’s triangle vCZ by a new ordinary W, retaining its vZ pairing and pairing the other new triangles across CW. The resulting FOUR original ports are

F=(u,A,v,C),E=(A,u,C,Z),G′=(v,u,W,Z),G″=(C,v,Z,W). F=(u,A,v,C),\quad E=(A,u,C,Z),\quad G'=(v,u,W,Z),\quad G''=(C,v,Z,W).\tag{13}

Their eight triangles are uAC,uvC,uAZ,ACZ,uvZ,vWZ,vCW,CZW. Each edge is in two triangles, the sphere is simple, and the displayed paired edges uC,AZ,vZ,CW are distinct. Deleting them leaves a simple bipartite MAIN quadrangulation with four distinct vertices per port. The general Morse realization therefore supplies actual sources for this recorded incidence; no flat-plat word or knot equivalence is asserted. Mark the original MAIN pair u,v.

Use F as cap, all other ports CROSS, and choose ordinary weights

LA=LC=1,LZ=4,LW=W>0. L_A=L_C=1,\qquad L_Z=4,\qquad L_W=W>0.

The entire A-star consists of E. Its endpoint A-load is exactly

ν=1π(arctan12+arctan13)=14. \nu=\frac1\pi\left(\arctan\frac12+\arctan\frac13\right)=\frac14.\tag{14}

The last equality uses the positive tangent-addition branch. In particular the local star hypotheses and its positive loss constants do NOT depend on W. Let X_E=Q(1,0,4)+Q(1,1,4), a fixed finite number. The original full weighted cap-only envelope, including every target, is

ℰ(W)=XE−12log⁡4+Q(0,W,4)+Q(1,0,W)+Q(1,4,W)−log⁡W. \mathcal E(W)=X_E-\tfrac12\log4+Q(0,W,4)+Q(1,0,W)+Q(1,4,W)-\log W.

Each of the last three triangle potentials admits its pure-W half-load. Hence

ℰ(W)≥12log⁡W+XE−12log⁡4→+∞. \mathcal E(W)\ge\tfrac12\log W+X_E-\tfrac12\log4\longrightarrow+\infty.\tag{15}

The actual complete capacity Phi_D of this fixed finite sphere is finite. Thus an arbitrarily large excess of this weighted envelope over Phi_D is compatible with all LOCAL star hypotheses and a fixed positive relative loss.

The exterior is NOT stationary in this example. It is therefore NOT a counterexample to Delta_cap<=p C0, to the cap-only maximum inequality, or to the volume conjecture. At W tending to infinity its original W demand plainly fails the stationarity equation. The example only rules out promoting a local relative estimate to an absolute theorem without using the global demands/stationarity or a genuine positive reference comparison. Equations (1)-(10), rather than this warning, are the main reduced absolute problem.

Dependencies

The whole CROSS-star spectral theorem supplies the genuine relative loss and fixed spectral neighborhoods. The cap-component and stellar theorem supplies the forest and leaf selection, and the exact refinement used in section 5. The full-fiber stationary classification supplies the original finite common weights and strict residual caps. The triangle entropy normalization and complete-capacity duality fix the constants and the compact concave optimization convention. The matched-sphere Morse construction supplies actual sources for (13); it does not identify them with another filled knot. The cap flow quotient in section 2 is classical only and does not change any quantum tensor.

The explicit divergent-envelope example is nonstationary. It is not a counterexample at a stationary cap-only maximum.

No numerical audit or scan is needed or claimed. There is no constructed bad common-stationary maximum, coefficient lower-rate assertion, new finite-source rewiring, or general prescribed-diagram volume theorem.
