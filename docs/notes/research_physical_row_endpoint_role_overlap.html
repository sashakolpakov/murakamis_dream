Comparing the two endpoint roles on common positive charts

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The regular continuation theorem constructs a source-normalized mode by treating either nonzero endpoint as the degree variable. Here the two constructions are compared on a common domain. Exact joint physical data give an exponentially small normalized difference on a two-variable real seed neighborhood. Holomorphic propagation then compares them on every compact subset of a connected common domain where both action bounds hold.

The shared seed and common phase are hypotheses. This result does not identify arbitrary off-grid representatives merely because their physical jets agree. It neither constructs a global glued scalar nor removes window interfaces, endpoint packets or exterior currents.

1. The two constructions and the shared seed

Put h=N−1,Xj=(j+12)h,Yk=(k+12)h,C(X)=2cos⁡(2πX),t(X)=π−2πX.(1) h=N^{-1},\quad X_j=(j+\tfrac12)h,\quad Y_k=(k+\tfrac12)h,\qquad C(X)=2\cos(2\pi X),\qquad t(X)=\pi-2\pi X . \qquad\text{(1)} Take one representative from the pinned regular continuation theorem with the first endpoint as degree variable, and one with the roles interchanged. In common centered coordinates write them as AN(X,Y)=ℱN,1σ1(X,t(Y)),BN(X,Y)=ℱN,2σ2(Y,t(X)).(2) A_N(X,Y)=\mathcal F_{N,1}^{\sigma_1}(X,t(Y)),\qquad B_N(X,Y)=\mathcal F_{N,2}^{\sigma_2}(Y,t(X)). \qquad\text{(2)} The choices of anchors, first-degree windows and designated signs may differ. Every representative is the actual normalized construction with its exact one-sided repair; it is not a raw Sears cutoff.

Require two fixed intervals IX,IY⋐(0,1/2)(3) I_X,I_Y\Subset(0,1/2) \qquad\text{(3)} and fixed complex neighborhoods UX,UYU_X,U_Y of the respective intervals, with buffers, such that:

  1. IXI_X lies in the matched first window of ANA_N, and IYI_Y lies in the matched first window of the transposed construction BNB_N. These intervals have positive length, independent of NN.
  2. Both constructions are holomorphic on a neighborhood of UX¯×UY¯\overline{U_X}\times\overline{U_Y}. In particular the local second-parameter domain for ANA_N contains t(UY)t(U_Y), and that for BNB_N contains t(UX)t(U_X).
  3. Their continued phases agree there with the original symmetric phase Ψ(X,Y)=ψ(t(X),t(Y)).(4) \Psi(X,Y)=\psi(t(X),t(Y)). \qquad\text{(4)} Both phase-normalized representatives have uniform eo(N)e^{o(N)} bounds on this buffered seed product.

The third condition holds on sufficiently thin real seed products when the two regular constructions use the same original phase: each designated-half estimate also supplies a fixed thin opposite collar. The intervals in (3) must nevertheless be shared as stated. A local second-parameter chart need not contain any other physical row.

At every joint seed node, the exact full-jet identities give ∂Xa∂YbAN(Xj,Yk)=∂Xa∂YbBN(Xj,Yk)=∂Xa∂YbKN(C(X),C(Y))|(Xj,Yk),0≤a,b≤1.(5) \partial_X^a\partial_Y^b A_N(X_j,Y_k) =\partial_X^a\partial_Y^b B_N(X_j,Y_k) =\left.\partial_X^a\partial_Y^b K_N(C(X),C(Y))\right|_{(X_j,Y_k)}, \qquad 0\le a,b\le1. \qquad\text{(5)} Here Xj∈IX,Yk∈IYX_j\in I_X,Y_k\in I_Y. These are double physical spectral rows for all sufficiently large NN.

To verify (5), the first construction has the exact holomorphic identities AN(Xj,Y)=Fj(C(Y)),∂XAN(Xj,Y)=C′(Xj)Ĝj(C(Y)).(6) A_N(X_j,Y)=F_j(C(Y)),\qquad \partial_XA_N(X_j,Y)=C'(X_j)\widehat G_j(C(Y)). \qquad\text{(6)} The source normal Ĝj\widehat G_j has the value and first spectral normal of the original GjG_j at every physical second row. Differentiate (6) in YY and use this identity. The transposed construction gives the same four entries by symmetry of the original kernel. In particular the mixed entry is C′(Xj)C′(Yk)∂z∂wKN(cj,ck).(7) C'(X_j)C'(Y_k)\, \partial_z\partial_wK_N(c_j,c_k). \qquad\text{(7)} No assertion that a first normal follows from values alone is used. The equality also survives multiplication by any common holomorphic spectator, with its full mixed product rule.

2. Two successive dense-grid estimates

We record the analytic estimate used at each stage. Let fNf_N be holomorphic on a fixed complex neighborhood of a real interval, bounded there by eo(N)e^{o(N)}. Suppose on its centered nodes |fN(Xj)|≤e−κN(8) |f_N(X_j)|\le e^{-\kappa N} \qquad\text{(8)} for a fixed κ>0\kappa>0. Then fN=O(e−κ′N)f_N=O(e^{-\kappa' N}) on a smaller open neighborhood of a shorter interior interval, for some κ′>0\kappa'>0. All statements are uniform in any buffered holomorphic parameters.

For clarity, subtract the finite cardinal correction qN(X)=∑jfN(Xj)sinc⁡(N(X−Xj)).(9) q_N(X)=\sum_j f_N(X_j) \operatorname{sinc}(N(X-X_j)). \qquad\text{(9)} On a sufficiently thin fixed neighborhood, |qN(X)|≤CNe−κN+πN|ℑX|≤e−κN/2.(10) |q_N(X)|\le CN e^{-\kappa N+\pi N|\Im X|} \le e^{-\kappa N/2}. \qquad\text{(10)} Thus fN−qNf_N-q_N has exact zeros at every selected node and is still bounded by eo(N)e^{o(N)}. Take a disk inside that thin neighborhood and cNcN nodes in a smaller concentric disk. The corresponding disk Blaschke factors have modulus at most a fixed ϑ<1\vartheta<1 on a still smaller disk. Dividing out these factors and using the maximum principle gives |fN−qN|≤eo(N)ϑcN.(11) |f_N-q_N|\le e^{o(N)}\vartheta^{cN}. \qquad\text{(11)} Finite covering and buffered Cauchy estimates prove the assertion, including all fixed derivatives. For exact nodal zeros omit (9). This is the dense-zero argument of the pinned normal theorem, with its approximate-zero step made explicit.

Set DN(X,Y)=e−NΨ(X,Y)(AN−BN).(12) D_N(X,Y)=e^{-N\Psi(X,Y)}(A_N-B_N). \qquad\text{(12)} It is holomorphic and bounded by eo(N)e^{o(N)} on the seed product. Fix a physical Yk∈IYY_k\in I_Y. Equation (5) gives exact zeros DN(Xj,Yk)=0D_N(X_j,Y_k)=0 for Xj∈IXX_j\in I_X. The exact-zero case of (11), uniformly in kk, gives a fixed open UX′⋐UXU_X'\Subset U_X and c1>0c_1>0 such that supX∈UX′|DN(X,Yk)|≤e−c1N.(13) \sup_{X\in U_X'}|D_N(X,Y_k)| \le e^{-c_1N}. \qquad\text{(13)}

Now fix X∈UX′X\in U_X' and regard DN(X,Y)D_N(X,Y) as a holomorphic function of YY. Its values at the second grid are exponentially small by (13), although they need not be zero. Apply (9)–(11) in YY, using XX as a uniform holomorphic parameter. On a fixed smaller product, |DN(X,Y)|≤Ce−c2N.(14) |D_N(X,Y)|\le C e^{-c_2N}. \qquad\text{(14)} All fixed mixed derivatives obey this estimate after further fixed buffers. This proves a two-complex-variable seed estimate; an estimate merely on the real product would not suffice for the propagation below.

Only the value entries in (5) are needed for (14). The remaining entries establish that the compared constructions still represent the original full physical source. No independent jet has been discarded to obtain the analytic comparison.

3. Propagation on a connected common domain

Theorem. In addition to the shared seed conditions, let 𝒲⊂ℂ2\mathcal W\subset\mathbb C^2 be a connected open common domain containing the seed product and a target compact set. Require a single holomorphic phase Ψ\Psi on 𝒲\mathcal W, equal to (4) on the seed, and assume that AN,BNA_N,B_N are holomorphic there with supK(|e−NΨAN|+|e−NΨBN|)≤eoK(N)for every K⋐𝒲.(15) \sup_K\bigl(|e^{-N\Psi}A_N|+|e^{-N\Psi}B_N|\bigr) \le e^{o_K(N)} \quad\text{for every }K\Subset\mathcal W. \qquad\text{(15)} For the one-sided representatives this means that the common domain stays in their designated half-domains and permitted opposite collars, or elsewhere where (15) has separately been proved. Then for every K⋐𝒲K\Subset\mathcal W there is cK>0c_K>0 such that supK|∂Xa∂Yb[e−NΨ(AN−BN)]|≤CK,a,be−cKN(16) \sup_K\left| \partial_X^a\partial_Y^b \bigl[e^{-N\Psi}(A_N-B_N)\bigr]\right| \le C_{K,a,b}e^{-c_KN} \qquad\text{(16)} for every fixed a,ba,b. The exponent may be decreased when derivatives are taken.

Proof. Equation (14) supplies one open seed ball. Cover compact paths from that ball to KK by finitely many buffered Euclidean balls in 𝒲\mathcal W. The three-balls estimate for a holomorphic function is the usual three-circles estimate on each complex line through the center: a bound on an inner ball and a bound on an outer ball give the interpolated bound on an intermediate ball. Thus an inner e−cNe^{-cN} bound and the eo(N)e^{o(N)} outer bound from (15) give an e−c′Ne^{-c'N} intermediate bound, with c′>0c'>0. Choose the next center in the known smaller ball and repeat. A finite chain reaches each compact target neighborhood; compactness gives a finite collection of chains. Every step multiplies the positive exponential constant by a fixed positive number, while its o(N)o(N) term stays negligible. Buffered Cauchy estimates prove (16). ▫\square

The statement is uniform for buffered holomorphic exterior parameter families when the domains, phase, source identities and bounds above are uniform. Their fixed derivatives follow by Cauchy on the larger parameter compacts.

The same conclusion holds for the two unrepaired baselines under the same common-domain bounds. Their actual nodal value errors from the regular continuation theorem are exponentially small relative to the physical rows. On a buffered real bulk seed the normalized physical rows have eo(N)e^{o(N)} size. Hence the first stage uses the approximate-zero argument (9)–(11), rather than the exact-zero case; the rest of the proof is unchanged.

4. Scope for source transport

The theorem compares two actual endpoint-role constructions. It allows different anchors and different matched windows, provided the fixed shared seed intervals in (3) remain. It cannot be applied when the local second domains omit the other seed window, when the target component is disconnected from the seed, or when the continued phases differ by an unrecorded branch or constant.

On any overlap satisfying the theorem, a common holomorphic spectator multiplies (16) by its actual bound. Thus an interface primitive or contour integral still needs that bound and its original orientation; the theorem does not declare its integral zero.

In particular the exact window identity in the continuation source uses ΦN,I♯\Phi^\sharp_{N,I}, the finite residue expression with Ĝj\widehat G_j. Its difference from the original free-endpoint window trace is PN(w)P_N(w) times a holomorphic function. Equality with the original follows only after the prescribed complete second trace, with all spectator normals. The present overlap estimate does not change that scope.

No first-angle evenness of the one-sided repairs is asserted. No full-lens substitution, whole-grid interpolation, simultaneous fold construction, global Cousin gluing or global current bound follows from (16). All unmatched rows, angular endpoint packets, opposite-side closures and exterior currents remain in the original problem.
