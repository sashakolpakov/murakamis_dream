The whole selected period: its polar character controls the volume rate

Research theorem and scope audit, 27 October 2026. The user’s proposal is to construct the function or torsor first and regard quantum jets as its restrictions. The classical part of that proposal is already proved in the doubled-phase optimizer and whole cyclotomic reconstruction. This note gives a different sufficient quantum target: a meromorphic connection on the whole selected period line, with the correct polar character. Algebraicity of every quantum amplitude is unnecessary for this criterion. Existence of that line for the actual knot period is not asserted here; the general volume conjecture remains unproved.

Applicability audit. The subsequent actual atom theorem proves that neither the original unfolded Ward factorial nor its two-nome completion is an ordinary meromorphic H-line at H=0. Accumulating cyclotomic singularities and exact interior zeros give the obstructions. Thus this sufficient criterion is not an established model for the actual local quantum factors. The usable whole object keeps its difference transports, cyclotomic divisors and independent power/source normal. A completed selected word could still have additional cancellations, but those have not been proved here.

1. What the classical optimizer supplies

For a positive ideal triangulation at a nondegenerate complete point, the monomial shape equations and peripheral equations are defined over Q, with their sign characters retained. The fixed-meridian shape Jacobian is invertible. Therefore the normalized selected shape germ is algebraic and etale over its meridian germ. Its shapes are algebraic over the function field of the marked geometric A-curve, and the complete shapes are algebraic numbers. Here M and L satisfy A(M,L)=0; they are not two independent deformation parameters. At completeness the specified SL2 meridian lift has M=+1 or -1 and the longitude lift retains its prescribed sign.

Strict concavity of the volume on its affine positive angle slice selects this geometric solution. It does not assert that an arbitrary ideal triangulation has a positive interior optimum. The independent arguments, including the nonzero Jacobian, are in the linked notes. For literature coverage of the critical-point formulation, see Futer–Gueritaud, 1004.0440; no proof from that paper is used as a premise here.

The algebraic coordinates are the shapes, not their arguments or the dilogarithm value. The latter is a marked regulator period of an algebraic connection. The fundamental-class and peripheral marks fix the additive constant in the action. In the actual q convention used here,

h = Log q,    h_N = pi*i/N,
Im S_geo = Vol(S^3 minus K)/2.                 (1.1)

Consequently the character exp(S_geo/h_N) has precisely the desired volume rate. This character and an ordinary leading Gaussian coefficient are different data. The question is whether the original selected quantum period has this character, with only lower-rate factors remaining.

2. A finite polar-character theorem for a whole rank-one line

Let L be a rank-one meromorphic connection at h=0. After a specified finite ramification h=s^e, choose a meromorphic frame and write the horizontal scalar equation as

dP = omega P,
omega = sum_(j=-m)^infinity a_j s^j ds.         (2.1)

Meromorphic here means a convergent Laurent germ with FINITE polar part. Fix one sector and logarithmic sheet containing the physical ray. Suppose P is a nonzero horizontal section there. Integrating (2.1), exactly rather than asymptotically, gives

P(s) = C s^rho exp(B(s) + H(s)),
rho = a_(-1),
B(s) = sum_(r=1)^(m-1) b_r s^(-r),
b_r = -a_(-r-1)/r,                           (2.2)

where C is nonzero and H is holomorphic at zero. If m<2 the sum is empty. These equations follow simply by differentiating the right side. No summation of a divergent formal series is involved.

Choose the marked root kappa with kappa^e=pi*i and put

s_N = kappa N^(-1/e),   s_N^e = h_N.          (2.3)

Then the exact norm expansion is

(2*pi/N) log|P(s_N)|
  = 2*pi sum_r Re(b_r kappa^(-r)) N^(r/e-1)
    + O(log N/N).                            (2.4)

Indeed B(s_N) is the finite displayed sum; H(s_N) is bounded; and log|s_N^rho| equals Re(rho) log|s_N| minus the bounded marked argument times Im(rho). The constant C contributes O(1/N).

Thus the rate depends only on a finite polar character of the whole connection. In particular:

  • Terms r<e have zero limiting contribution, even though their exponentials need not be algebraic or polynomially bounded.
  • The r=e term contributes 2 Im(b_e), since kappa^e=pi*i.
  • Terms r>e contribute a divergent real power unless their real coefficients on the marked physical ray vanish. Distinct r give distinct powers, so they cannot cancel each other.

It follows that the limit equals a specified V if and only if

Re(b_r kappa^(-r))=0 for all r>e,
2 Im(b_e)=V,                                 (2.5)

with b_e=0 when absent. This is a criterion for the norm on this particular marked ray. It allows higher polar terms which are pure phase there; no uniform assertion on adjacent sectors is inferred.

3. The criterion belongs to the line, not to a chosen Taylor frame

A fixed nonzero meromorphic frame change P -> g(s)P changes omega by dlog g. Write g=s^nu u(s), where nu is an integer and u is a holomorphic unit. Then

dlog g = nu ds/s + dlog u.

It has no polar term of order two or higher. Consequently B(s) in (2.2) is unchanged, and the scalar norm changes by O(log N). The criterion is invariant under every such frame change, including zeros and poles of fixed order. Changing the ramification root changes B and kappa compatibly and leaves B(s_N) unchanged.

The quotient by the geometric character is particularly useful. The character has

P_geo = exp(S_geo/h) = exp(S_geo s^(-e)),
omega_geo = -e S_geo s^(-e-1) ds.             (3.1)

If L tensor P_geo^(-1) admits a logarithmic connection at s=0 in a meromorphic frame, then its entire B is zero. For a nonzero selected horizontal section the theorem therefore gives

(2*pi/N) log|P(s_N)|
      = Vol(S^3 minus K) + O(log N/N).        (3.2)

A logarithmic quotient is a clean sufficient condition, stronger than necessary. A quotient whose polar primitive has degree below e still has zero limiting rate, with the finite fractional-power error given by (2.4). This weakens the earlier amplitude requirement that the whole comparison coefficient itself belong to a fixed finite algebraic extension. For example exp(1/s) with e=2 is not algebraic and grows as exp(O(sqrt N)) on a suitable ray, but it does not affect the volume limit.

4. Why this is a substantive quantum theorem to prove

The hypothesis concerns the WHOLE selected period, not only the nonzero rank-one Gaussian germ at a geometric critical point. The source and co-source must put the actual finite Kashaev values in the proposed line, with the original finite endpoint and polar data. A local block, or the existence of an unpointed abstract line, does not imply that statement.

More precisely, the application requires ONE common horizontal section v(s) and a FIXED nonzero meromorphic cut lambda(s), with the original scalar equal to lambda(s_N)v(s_N). The theorem is applied to that scalar in its induced meromorphic frame. A nonzero anchor fixes its integration constant. Arbitrarily varying level rows, or an essential exponential hidden in lambda, are not covered.

Also the differential equation supplied by a quantum A-operator is in the meridian direction. A factor exp(C/h), independent of the meridian, preserves that operator equation and changes the h connection by -C dh/h^2. It changes the normalized rate by 2 Im C. The already specified Kashaev source does not admit this arbitrary renormalization; the example shows why the full period connection and its action anchor contain data beyond the A-operator alone.

All of (1.1)–(3.2) uses the fixed complete peripheral slice. With a moving meridian one must first pull back the FULL connection along the actual marked peripheral path. One must also retain S_geo(M(s))/h, not replace its differential by the fixed-meridian partial derivative. Even the flat extra term d((M-M_0)/h^2) restricts to zero on M=M_0 but becomes d(1/h) along M=M_0+h, changing a leading character. If S_geo(M(h))-S_geo(M_0)=O(h), the action replacement changes the norm by O(1); a finite-Puiseux motion may give larger, still sublinear, corrections that (2.4) must retain.

Marked root phases also remain part of the comparison. For example q(N2)=exp(-pi^2/h) on h=pi*i/N. Its polar character is nonzero but pure phase on that ray, and hence has zero norm contribution. It satisfies the weaker real-character test (2.5); it need not give a logarithmic quotient before that recorded phase is absorbed.

It is also not legitimate to define omega=dlog P for any interpolated function and declare the hypothesis automatic. For example, if A!=B and C_1 C_2!=0, the entire punctured function

P(h) = C_1 exp(A/h) + C_2 exp(B/h)

has infinitely many zeros accumulating at h=0:

h_k = (A-B)/(Log(-C_2/C_1)+2*pi*i*k),

omitting a possible zero denominator. Its logarithmic derivative therefore has infinitely many poles accumulating at zero and is not a meromorphic germ at zero. A finite cover does not remove this obstruction. Two local rank-one characters are not a single whole meromorphic selected line. One may instead need a higher-rank period object and its actual source projection.

Nor does finite type of an algebraic torsor imply a logarithmic quotient. The previously proved whole unit example

Q_N=exp(2*pi*i/N),
u(Q)=1+i(Q-1),     C_N=u(Q_N)^(N^2)

has u(1)=1 but (1/N)log|C_N| -> -2*pi. In the present coordinate its one whole logarithm is

F(h) = -pi^2 h^(-2) Log(1+i(exp(2h)-1)).

Its polar primitive is -2ipi^2/h. Thus its connection really has an additional leading irregular character, and (2.4) detects the rate change -4pi^2 in the normalization 2pi/N. It is not a counterexample to the theorem. This example is not claimed to preserve the actual knot source.

5. The revised cyclotomic rigidity target

The proposed route can now be phrased without computing an infinite list of quantum Taylor coefficients:

  1. Use the normalized geometric A-curve, its algebraic optimizer, and its marked regulator connection as the whole geometric object.
  2. Construct the whole period object selected by the actual quantum source and cut; carry its endpoints, peripheral units and pole lattice. Determine whether its selected geometric contribution descends to a rank-one line of the type above.
  3. Prove equality of the leading polar character with (3.1), and prove the selected pairing is nonzero. A logarithmic quotient would give the stronger error estimate (3.2).
  4. Use exact cyclotomic restrictions to certify identities of the whole algebraic transport and source maps. The existing reconstruction theorem applies once their fixed presentation is supplied. It does not by itself assert existence of this period connection or preservation of its marked polar character.

This is a different sufficient target from proving all amplitude coefficients algebraic and then estimating them. It has a weaker amplitude requirement conditional on the stronger structural input of a whole selected rank-one line. It does not establish that the actual knot object satisfies the target. If only a local geometric summand is obtained, its global coefficient and the other summands must still be controlled; formula (3.2) cannot be applied to the entire selected invariant in that situation.
