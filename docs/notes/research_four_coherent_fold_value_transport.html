Anchored source transport reaches the central fold value

22 November 2026. Research note. Author-final. Earlier final notes and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The original four-coherent scalar has the positive ideal-cube rate uniformly along its entire anchored half-grid up to the central fold. No fixed distance from that fold is required. If the grid lands on the fold, the estimate includes its actual value, without using the singular boundary-equation row there.

An explicit sine factor removes the potentially shrinking first difference. The resulting positive flux has a bounded total correction, and converting back costs at most a polynomial factor. No source, seam, or repeated-root coordinate is replaced.

1. The original source and the extended range

Let N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and Q=q2Q=q^2. In the full centered basis put Ua=∑ℓ=0daℓfℓ,ΦN(y,z;s,t)=ϕ(UyUzUsUt).(1) U_a=\sum_{\ell=0}^d a^\ell f_\ell,\qquad \Phi_N(y,z;s,t)=\phi(U_yU_zU_sU_t). \qquad\text{(1)} This is the original Frobenius contraction, including all internal Hermite derivative coordinates and its original phases.

Fix a compact subset KK of the three-parameter domain b,c>0,S=b+c<1,1−S<R<1−max⁡(b,c).(2) b,c>0,\qquad S=b+c<1,\qquad 1-S<R<1-\max(b,c). \qquad\text{(2)} Set ε=N−1\varepsilon=N^{-1} and y=−e−2πib,z=−e−2πic,r=e−2πiR,hj=(j+12)ε,kj=R−hj,sj=−q−1Q−j,tj=−qrQj,Φj=ΦN(y,z;sj,tj),ωN=e−πid(S+R).(3) \begin{gathered} y=-e^{-2\pi ib},\quad z=-e^{-2\pi ic},\quad r=e^{-2\pi iR},\\ h_j=(j+\tfrac12)\varepsilon,\quad k_j=R-h_j,\quad s_j=-q^{-1}Q^{-j},\quad t_j=-qrQ^j,\\ \Phi_j=\Phi_N(y,z;s_j,t_j),\qquad \omega_N=e^{-\pi i d(S+R)} . \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(3)} The allowed targets are all integers J≥0,0<hJ≤R/2.(4) J\ge0,\qquad 0<h_J\le R/2. \qquad\text{(4)} Thus the target can be O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) from the fold or exactly at it. The entries −1,0-1,0 initialize the same anchored orbit.

Use the actual source action L(x)=∫0xlog⁡|2sin⁡πv|dv,m(x,y,u)=L(x+y)+L(u)−L(x)−L(y)−L(u−x)−L(u−y)−L(x+y−u),(5) \begin{split} L(x)&=\int_0^x\log|2\sin\pi v|\,dv,\\ m(x,y,u)&=L(x+y)+L(u)-L(x)-L(y)\\ &\hspace{1cm}-L(u-x)-L(u-y)-L(x+y-u), \end{split} \qquad\text{(5)} where LL has its odd, one-periodic extension. Put Ih=(max⁡(b,c,1−R),min⁡(S,1−h,1−R+h)),ℱ(b,c,R,h)=maxu∈Ih{m(b,c,u)+m(h,R−h,1−u)}.(6) \begin{split} I_h&=(\max(b,c,1-R),\ \min(S,1-h,1-R+h)),\\ \mathcal F(b,c,R,h)&= \max_{u\in I_h}\{m(b,c,u)+m(h,R-h,1-u)\}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(6)} The actual cube theorem identifies πℱ\pi\mathcal F with the volume of its convex ideal cube. This remains a strict cube at h=R/2h=R/2: all six external pair sums are below one, and their total is S+R>1S+R>1. The fold is in the boundary parametrization, not a degeneration of this ideal cube.

Theorem. There are constants CK,NKC_K,N_K such that for every N≥NKN\ge N_K, every (b,c,R)∈K(b,c,R)\in K, and every target (4), ΦJ=ωNPN,J,PN,J>0,|log⁡PN,J−Nℱ(b,c,R,hJ)|≤CKlog⁡N.(7) \boxed{\quad \Phi_J=\omega_N P_{N,J},\qquad P_{N,J}>0,\qquad |\log P_{N,J}-N\mathcal F(b,c,R,h_J)|\le C_K\log N . \quad} \qquad\text{(7)} The estimate is uniform even when hJh_J tends to zero or to the fold at a level-dependent rate. Both parities and composite levels are included. The normalized original closing of the two turned products is ΦJ/N2\Phi_J/N^2, and has the same rate.

2. Exact source initialization and the rows actually used

The anchored-grid theorem supplies the exact common carrier in (3), the two original readouts, and their calibrated asymptotics. Consequently Hj=ωN−1Φj∈ℝ,H0>0,Δ0:=H0−H−1>0,log⁡H0=NM(b,c,R)+OK(log⁡N),M(b,c,R)=m(b,c,1−R)>0,|H−1|≤CK/N,Δ0/H0=1+OK(e−cKN).(8) \begin{gathered} H_j=\omega_N^{-1}\Phi_j\in\mathbb R,\qquad H_0>0,\qquad \Delta_0:=H_0-H_{-1}>0,\\ \log H_0=N M(b,c,R)+O_K(\log N),\qquad M(b,c,R)=m(b,c,1-R)>0,\\ |H_{-1}|\le C_K/N,\qquad \Delta_0/H_0=1+O_K(e^{-c_KN}). \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(8)} Only the compact parameters in (2) enter these bounds. For a fixed target JJ, put δj=R−2hj,αj=sin⁡π(R−2jε)=sin⁡π(δj+ε).(9) \delta_j=R-2h_j,\qquad \alpha_j=\sin\pi(R-2j\varepsilon) =\sin\pi(\delta_j+\varepsilon). \qquad\text{(9)} For every row 0≤j<J0\le j<J, δj=δJ+2(J−j)ε≥2ε,0<δj−ε<δj+ε≤R<1.(10) \delta_j=\delta_J+2(J-j)\varepsilon\ge2\varepsilon,\qquad 0<\delta_j-\varepsilon<\delta_j+\varepsilon\le R<1. \qquad\text{(10)} Therefore every boundary row used in the proof is regular. At the terminal point, cK/N≤αJ≤1,(11) c_K/N\le\alpha_J\le1, \qquad\text{(11)} including αJ=sin⁡(π/N)\alpha_J=\sin(\pi/N) when δJ=0\delta_J=0. No boundary equation is imposed at row JJ.

Write the positive numerators of the sine-converted coefficients as ℬj=sin⁡2π(kj+ε/2)sin⁡π(b+hj−ε)sin⁡π(c+hj−ε),𝒟j=sin⁡2π(hj+ε/2)sin⁡π(b+kj−ε)sin⁡π(c+kj−ε).(12) \begin{split} \mathcal B_j={}& \sin^2\pi(k_j+\varepsilon/2) \sin\pi(b+h_j-\varepsilon)\sin\pi(c+h_j-\varepsilon),\\ \mathcal D_j={}& \sin^2\pi(h_j+\varepsilon/2) \sin\pi(b+k_j-\varepsilon)\sin\pi(c+k_j-\varepsilon). \end{split} \qquad\text{(12)} Then the exact source recurrence, with Δj=Hj−Hj−1\Delta_j=H_j-H_{j-1}, is Δj+1=ρjΔj+ΛN𝖽jHj,𝖻j=ℬjsin⁡πδjαj,𝖽j=𝒟jsin⁡πδjαj+1,ρj=𝖻j/𝖽j,ΛN=sin⁡(π/N)sin⁡π(S−ε).(13) \begin{gathered} \Delta_{j+1}=\rho_j\Delta_j+ \frac{\Lambda_N}{\mathsf d_j}H_j,\\ \mathsf b_j=\frac{\mathcal B_j}{\sin\pi\delta_j\,\alpha_j}, \quad \mathsf d_j=\frac{\mathcal D_j}{\sin\pi\delta_j\,\alpha_{j+1}}, \quad \rho_j=\mathsf b_j/\mathsf d_j,\\ \Lambda_N=\sin(\pi/N)\sin\pi(S-\varepsilon). \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(13)} All coefficients in these used rows are positive for large NN. This proves (14) without bounding the possibly large divided coefficients near the fold.

3. Removing the shrinking sine from the first difference

Set qj=ℬj/𝒟j,Ej=Δj/αj.(15) q_j=\mathcal B_j/\mathcal D_j,\qquad E_j=\Delta_j/\alpha_j . \qquad\text{(15)} The exact relation between the two transfer factors is ρj=qjαj+1/αj.(16) \rho_j=q_j\,\alpha_{j+1}/\alpha_j . \qquad\text{(16)} Unlike ρj\rho_j, the factor qjq_j stays above one throughout the used half-path. For a quantitative bound define fN(v)=sin⁡2πvsin⁡π(b−3ε/2+v)sin⁡π(c−3ε/2+v).(17) f_N(v)=\frac{\sin^2\pi v} {\sin\pi(b-3\varepsilon/2+v)\sin\pi(c-3\varepsilon/2+v)} . \qquad\text{(17)} Then qj=fN(kj+ε/2)/fN(hj+ε/2)q_j=f_N(k_j+\varepsilon/2)/f_N(h_j+\varepsilon/2). Its logarithmic derivative is the sum of two positive cotangent differences. The identity cot⁡x−cot⁡(x+a)=sin⁡a/[sin⁡xsin⁡(x+a)]\cot x-\cot(x+a)=\sin a/[\sin x\sin(x+a)] gives a lower bound cK>0c_K>0, even when the first argument is of order N−1N^{-1}. Since the two arguments differ by δj\delta_j, qj−1≥cKδj.(18) q_j-1\ge c_K\delta_j . \qquad\text{(18)} Here the shifted b,cb,c are positive for large NN, and all shifted arguments remain in (0,1)(0,1), by (2).

We next bound Hj/EjH_j/E_j without a fixed fold margin. For every j<Jj<J, (10) and (18) imply αjEj≤CKδjEj≤CK(Ej+1−Ej).(20) \alpha_jE_j\le C_K\delta_jE_j \le C_K(E_{j+1}-E_j). \qquad\text{(20)} For a target 1≤n≤J1\le n\le J, use Hn=H0+∑j=1nαjEj.(21) H_n=H_0+\sum_{j=1}^n\alpha_jE_j. \qquad\text{(21)} Apply (20) up to n−1n-1, and bound the final term by EnE_n. 4. The same geometric action, uniformly at the terminal fold

The product now contains no divided fold factor: qj=sin⁡2π(R−jε)sin⁡2π((j+1)ε)sin⁡π(b+(j−12)ε)sin⁡π(c+(j−12)ε)sin⁡π(b+R−(j+32)ε)sin⁡π(c+R−(j+32)ε).(25) q_j=\frac{\sin^2\pi(R-j\varepsilon)} {\sin^2\pi((j+1)\varepsilon)} \frac{\sin\pi(b+(j-\tfrac12)\varepsilon) \sin\pi(c+(j-\tfrac12)\varepsilon)} {\sin\pi(b+R-(j+\tfrac32)\varepsilon) \sin\pi(c+R-(j+\tfrac32)\varepsilon)} . \qquad\text{(25)} All its factors are smooth log-sine samples on fixed strict intervals, apart from the explicit denominator at the initial endpoint. The usual integral comparison for log⁡(J!)\log(J!), together with log⁡sin⁡πv=log⁡v+\log\sin\pi v=\log v+ a smooth function near zero, gives uniformly up to hJ=R/2h_J=R/2, ∑j<Jlog⁡qj=N∫0hJlog⁡ρ0(v)dv+OK(log⁡N),ρ0(v)=sin⁡2π(R−v)sin⁡π(b+v)sin⁡π(c+v)sin⁡2πvsin⁡π(b+R−v)sin⁡π(c+R−v).(26) \begin{split} \sum_{j<J}\log q_j &=N\int_0^{h_J}\log\rho_0(v)\,dv+O_K(\log N),\\ \rho_0(v)&= \frac{\sin^2\pi(R-v)\sin\pi(b+v)\sin\pi(c+v)} {\sin^2\pi v\sin\pi(b+R-v)\sin\pi(c+R-v)} . \end{split} \qquad\text{(26)} The bounded shifts cost OK(1)O_K(1); changing J/NJ/N to hJh_J costs at most OK(log⁡N)O_K(\log N) after multiplication by NN. There is no singularity at the upper endpoint: ρ0(R/2)=1\rho_0(R/2)=1.

The anchored-grid proof identifies, on 0<h<R/20<h<R/2, ℱ(b,c,R,h)=M(b,c,R)+∫0hlog⁡ρ0(v)dv.(27) \mathcal F(b,c,R,h) =M(b,c,R)+\int_0^h\log\rho_0(v)\,dv . \qquad\text{(27)} This identity extends to h=R/2h=R/2 by continuity. The actual cube theorem applies there as well: its two prism actions still have a unique strict stationary maximum. Combining (8), (23), and (26)–(27) proves (7). No singular target row was used to obtain that value.

5. What has and has not been extended

This removes the fixed fold margin for actual values on the anchored orbit. In particular every closest prefold grid point is covered, and an exact central grid value is covered whenever it occurs. The proof uses only the original finite boundary equation in regular rows and its original two source anchors.

It does not estimate a derivative normal to the folded boundary coordinate, nor does it apply the pole-cancelled target equation to identify such a derivative. It does not prove transport to a fixed postfold interval, use a reflected fractional orbit, or remove the anchored-grid and initial pair-wall restrictions in (2)–(4). The original scalar and its N−2N^{-2} coherent normalization are unchanged. No general Conway attachment or finite-exception knot-volume theorem follows.
