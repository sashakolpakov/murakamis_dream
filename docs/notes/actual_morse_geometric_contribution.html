The original complement and its geometric contribution

A simple triangulated sphere with a perfect matching of its cubic dual determines an actual augmented link diagram when the cyclic orders, strand pairings and crossing disks are retained. This note follows that diagram through its exact finite source, its geometric critical point, and the filling of its original auxiliary cusps.

For independently sufficiently long fixed signed twists, the geometric contribution to the original invariant is nonzero and has the exponential rate of the actual filled hyperbolic volume. The proof keeps the primitive peripheral words, the finite scalar factors and both parities of the quantum level. No terminal tangles are inserted, and no local balance assumption is imposed.

The name CROSS denotes the central recurrence-path piece of each original homogeneous numerator. Selecting it at every crossing region is an algebraic decomposition of the original evaluation, not a new invariant. The analytic companion proves its full Fourier asymptotic. To conclude the same limit for the full invariant of an undecorated diagram, one must additionally control the remaining pieces.

1. Precise diagram and filling scope

Let 𝒯\mathcal T be a finite simple triangulated oriented sphere with at least four vertices. Choose a perfect matching of its cubic dual. The two triangles in each matched pair form a quadrilateral with four distinct white vertices. Record a cyclic order (xe,be,ye,ce)(x_e,b_e,y_e,c_e); its common diagonal is xeyex_ey_e, its input middle is beb_e, and its output middle is cec_e. There are RR matched pairs and R+2R+2 white vertices. Mark a nonmatching edge uvuv. It is a strand edge in the link diagram.

There are two equivalent permitted inputs:

  • an actual connected augmented diagram in S3S^3, whose projection and crossing-disk cut has exactly these labels and orders; or
  • the literal inverse-cut construction in section 2 below.

Fix the retained half twist ϵe∈{−1,0,1}\epsilon_e\in\{-1,0,1\} and its sign at every disk. Its auxiliary peripheral basis (μe,λe)(\mu_e,\lambda_e) is the original disk-framed primitive basis. The longitude λe\lambda_e is represented by the ordered two-head product Pe=BeAeP_e=B_eA_e. Integer filling coordinate pep_e means filling the slope μe+peλe\mu_e+p_e\lambda_e; the main crossing power is ne=ϵe−2pe. n_e=\epsilon_e-2p_e. \tag{1} The original outside terminal matching and the even/odd pairing at each disk determine the main components. When their terminal graph is connected, denote the filled knot by K𝒑K_{\boldsymbol p}. We keep this original parity test. We do not change the parity vector to obtain a different knot.

A supplied labeled positive right-angled cut certificate is sufficient for everything below. In this simple-sphere class such a certificate also follows from the constructive packing theorem in the balanced-fan note, section 2. That section constructs the packing before any fan is inserted. Only that initial geometric construction is used here.

2. Why the manifold and its complete anchor are the original ones

Take disjoint small neighborhoods of the matched edges of the cubic dual. Each is a four-terminal disk. In cyclic terminal order 0,1,2,30,1,2,3, the two shaded halves group (1,2)(1,2) and (3,0)(3,0). Draw the physical untwisted strands instead with pairs (0,1),(2,3)(0,1),(2,3), and connect outside terminals along the nonmatching dual edges. Retained odd half twists use the physical pairing (0,2),(1,3)(0,2),(1,3), with their original signed collar paths.

The two main pairings and the shaded grouping must not be confused. The graph of main components is the union of the outside terminal matching and these physical pairings. Every terminal has degree two. Its connectedness is exactly the knot test, and adding full twists does not change it.

Cut along the projection sphere. In each projection ball a crossing circle semicircle is boundary parallel by its original half disk. Drilling that semicircle and opening that same half disk cancels the introduced handle. These operations occur in disjoint collars, so opening all of them leaves one ball in each projection copy. The boundary cellulation is the prescribed medial sphere. Thus cycles in the matched graph create no unidentified handle or extra gluing.

For completeness, the initial simple-sphere packing theorem does not require the absence of separating triangles. Remove one triangular face and pool its three boundary angle demands.
