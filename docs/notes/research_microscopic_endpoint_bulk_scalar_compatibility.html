Scalar compatibility of the actual microscopic endpoint in the physical bulk

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The actual value and first spectral normal of a microscopic endpoint kernel admit one polynomially bounded holomorphic scalar representative on every buffered physical bulk interval. The two source coefficients in the prior normal-defect transport are exponentially small. The argument combines its moderate separate channels with an exact alternating analytic expression for their difference; both parity subgrids are essential.

Locally the scalar representative can be chosen to be an actual partial Newton sum. Its bounded complex neighborhood can be thin. No endpoint extension, whole-period representative, contour connector estimate, or filled-source rate follows.

6. Shared products and original trace placement

For any fixed number of microscopic endpoint factors, take their scalar representatives on a common smaller domain and multiply them before tracing. Their product has polynomial norm and its value and first spectral normal agree exactly with those of the original shared product, by the ordinary product rule. All fixed derivatives in the separate bounded complex endpoint parameters are retained on smaller parameter sets.

Only the covered bulk rows have been replaced. The odd simple node, both physical endpoint regions, external connectors, and all remaining source currents stay in the original expression. In particular there is no conflict with the lower bound for a full-period even Hermite representative: the domains and the required endpoint coverage are different.

7. Coverage and direct sources

The prior two-channel theorem supplied moderate separate value and normal interpolants, while the two-mode theorem isolated their unknown true compatibility coefficients. The general radical-domain theorem supplied exact high normals with analytic control, but had not combined its locally varying cutoff with those two channels. The new step is (18)–(22): exact physical termination on a suitable chart at every bulk point, followed by the two dense parity subgrids. It proves smallness of the actual compatibility coefficients and gives the scalar representatives (26), (28), and (31).
