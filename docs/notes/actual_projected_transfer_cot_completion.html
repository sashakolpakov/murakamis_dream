Original output Fourier projection removes the colliding negative-transfer poles

28 October 2026. Keeping the ORIGINAL finite physical output projection resolves the simultaneous endpoint collisions left by the scalar simple-seam calculation. There is an exact matrix-valued total-transfer interpolant. At EVERY newly crossed negative integer its projected operator has a zero, including when its unprojected scalar coefficient has a pole. Consequently the complete cotangent density is holomorphic across the whole negative normal collar. Its operator norm and contour mass cost are exp(o(N)) on any collar of width N^alpha, alpha<1, uniformly in ALL physical input basis states.

This is an actual full-source normal-boundary operation, not a favorable scalar interpolation with its output tails deleted. The Fourier/sinc factors are retained explicitly. It neither discards the original k=0 residue nor bounds an unrestricted bulk exterior by hyperbolic volume.

1. Literal physical Fourier projection and the interpolated crossing

Use the original balanced complete heads, N>=2, d=N-1, Q=exp(2pii/N), x=-exp(-pii/N), with orthonormal basis |j>,0<=j<=d. In L^2 of the unit circle use normalized measure dtheta/(2pi) and the specified logarithmic lift 0<=theta<=2pi. The orthogonal projection of exp(i nu theta) to the physical Fourier band is the ENTIRE vector

v_N(nu)=sum_(j=0)^d s_j(nu)|j>,
s_j(nu)=(exp(2pi*i*nu)-1)/(2pi*i*(nu-j)),             (1)

with its removable value one at nu=j. Thus v_N(j)=|j> inside the physical band, and v_N(j)=0 at every other integer. At an invalid integer its vector zero is at least simple. Orthogonality proves

||v_N(nu)||<=||exp(i nu theta)||_L2
                     <=exp(2pi |Im nu|).             (2)

No N-dependent conditioning or a nonpositive pivotal metric is used.

Let the scalar r_N(a,b;z), 0<=a,b<=d, be the EXACT factorial interpolation of the ORIGINAL positive crossing:

r_N(a,b;z)=kappa x^(-a-b+z)Q^(b(a-z))
           *Fhat(b+z)F_a/[Fhat(z)F_b Fhat(a-z)].     (3)

The normalizers, Gaussian, Cartan and completed factorial are those of the total-transfer theorem. Define the operator-valued continuation by its actual input columns,

Rhat_N(z)|a,b>=r_N(a,b;z)
                       v_N(b+z) tensor v_N(a-z).    (4)

This is precisely the contraction of the two original output Fourier projections with the scalar monomials of the physical crossing kernel. It is not an arbitrary change of its output source. At an integer 0<=k<=d it equals the ORIGINAL kth transfer slice: valid outputs give their literal basis vectors and forbidden transfers have their exact factorial zeros. The opposite crossing uses its actual conjugate mirror.

Therefore the half-integer closed cotangent rectangle gives an OPERATOR identity before applying any knot closure,

R_plus=integral_Gamma pi cot(pi z) Rhat_N(z) dz/(2pi*i), (5)

where Gamma encloses precisely 0,…,d. The full original kappa is in (3); all physical head projections and any external pivotal, framing, peripheral or marked source are unchanged. Fubini on finite contours applies (5) simultaneously to every crossing of a fixed signed word.

2. Exact cancellation at a simultaneous negative endpoint collision

Fix z=-s, with 1<=s<N. In this normal range Fhat has a simple pole at each negative integer, and a simple zero at N,…,2N-2. These orders follow directly from its convergent Gamma product or its shift continuation. Put

I_1=1 if b<s, otherwise 0,
I_2=1 if a+s>=N, otherwise 0.

The scalar coefficient (3) has local order

ord_(z=-s) r_N = 1-I_1-I_2.                         (6)

The first invalid output is b-s<0, and the second is a+s>d. Formula (1) supplies a simple vector zero at EACH invalid output. Thus the complete column in (4) has order at least

(1-I_1-I_2)+I_1+I_2=1.                              (7)

In particular the double collision I_1=I_2=1 is not discarded: its scalar pole is canceled by TWO actual physical projection zeros, leaving another zero. This is exactly the source factor lost if one studies the scalar factorial coefficient alone.

All scalar singularities in this collar lie at these integers. Consequently Rhat is holomorphic on the negative collar and vanishes at all its integers. Its complete density pi cot(pi z)Rhat_N(z) is holomorphic there too. No derivative residue or logarithmic source term has been omitted; the ENTIRE sinc vectors in (1) are retained before taking the removable limits.

3. Uniform polynomial base bound at every physical input endpoint

Fix T>0 and let 0<=Re nu<=1, |Im nu|<=T. Uniformly in 0<=n<=d,

|Fhat(n+nu)/F_n|<=C_T N^(C_T),
|F_n/Fhat(n-nu)|<=C_T N^(C_T),
|1/Fhat(nu)|<=C_T N.                               (8)

Here is a direct estimate, including the input endpoints. On the fixed nu rectangle, its defining product gives Fhat(nu) bounded by C_T N^(-Re nu), and its reciprocal by C_T N^(Re nu). For the first ratio use the exact product

Fhat(n+nu)/F_n=Fhat(nu)
         product_(j=1)^n (1-Q^(j+nu))/(1-Q^j).

Each ratio is at most 1+C_T/min(j,N-j); the sum of their logarithms is O_T(log N). For the second ratio when n>=1, start at Fhat(1-nu) and use factors j=2,…,n. Its ratios are bounded by 1+C_T/min(j-1,N-j), and |F_1/Fhat(1-nu)| is polynomially bounded. For n=0 use 1/Fhat(-nu)=(1-Q^(1-nu))/Fhat(1-nu), which is regular and satisfies the same bound. Thus (8) does not hide a large F_n or its inverse at the physical endpoints.

Since the Cartan multiplier in (3) is bounded on this fixed-height rectangle at physical a,b, (8) gives |r_N(a,b;nu)|<=C_T N^(C_T), uniformly for ALL a,b. Together with (2), this is the required bounded normal base for the actual projected operator.

4. A subexponential-cost compensated collar for the entire word

The same exact ratio used for the sourced Ward equation gives

r_N(z+1)/r_N(z)
   =c(1-Q^(b+z+1))(1-Q^(a-z))/(1-Q^(z+1)),
c=xQ^(-b).                                         (9)

Take the rectangle -s-1/2<=Re z<=-1/2, |Im z|<=T, with s<N/2. On either half-integer vertical side or either nonzero-height horizontal side, write z=nu-M, 0<=Re nu<=1. Inverting (9) M times costs at most (C_T N2)M: each denominator 1-Q^(integer+nu) has magnitude at least c_T/N there, while the numerator is bounded. The distance from an integer is bounded below by min(1/2,T). The base estimate (8) and (2) therefore give on this ENTIRE boundary

||pi cot(pi z)Rhat_N(z)||
                    <=N^(C_T)(C_T N^2)^(s+1).         (10)

The matrix operator norm costs only another factor N from its N^2 input columns. The cotangent is bounded on these edges. Its apparent interior poles have already been removed in Section 2. The maximum principle applied to every scalar matrix coefficient <u,Rhat v>, or to this holomorphic complete density, extends (10) through the interior with the same bound.

The added contour has length O_T(s+1). Moving the original left edge from -1/2 to -s-1/2 and retaining BOTH finite-height connecting pieces changes (5) by zero: all enclosed new poles are removable. Thus this is an exact source-preserving collar move with quantitative operator cost

exp[O_T((s+1)log N)].                               (11)

For s<=N^alpha with any fixed alpha<1 this is exp(o(N)). The number of crossings and physical heads of a fixed diagram does not grow with N, so finitely many such original operator collar operations have the same subexponential additional cost. Every original cup, cap, pivot, phase and external line remains as prescribed.

For an artificial positive split k=R the adjacent contours have opposite interface currents; moving them together retains the same integer residues with opposite signs. Equations (5)–(11) address the ACTUAL outer normal source as well, including the simultaneous collisions which a scalar split had left uncontrolled.

5. Scope of the advance

This removes an artificial negative-transfer exit of normal width N^alpha at the full finite physical source, with explicit tame cost. The original integer residues 0,…,d remain enclosed. In particular it does not contradict the full-rate low Markov packet: no original residue has been declared zero or individually lower-rate.

The physical input labels in (4) are the ORIGINAL finite basis labels. The bound is uniform over all of them, so it is an operator/source statement rather than a selected fixed-clock phase claim. If those input indices are themselves replaced by independent complex contour variables, one must retain the corresponding input projections and their joint sources; (7) is not an assertion for an arbitrary scalar continuation with those physical projections removed.

Nothing here assigns a sharp geometric bound to an unrestricted bulk exterior, a real radial cutoff face, or a transfer collar of macroscopic width whose cost in (11) is no longer subexponential. It does not identify every stationary branch of a split sinc density with a knot representation: the entire physical projection must stay. The theorem is a quantitative full-source normal-boundary filling, not the global sublevel transport or volume conjecture.
