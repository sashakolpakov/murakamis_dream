Exact star–triangle reduction with its retained degree defect

28 October 2026. The balanced root-of-unity star–triangle identity in the general source fusion theorem has an exact extension to stars below balance. It factors off the same cyclotomic pair constraints and retains a polynomial whose degree is exactly the balance defect. Its normalized coefficient norm has the explicit bound (e+1)^5 binom(N+e,e)^6, so EVERY sublinear defect e=o(N) has only subexponential cost. No claim is made that the original knot state has defects in this range, or that contracting its remaining polynomial cannot cancel the geometric contribution.

1. Polynomial factorization, including an unbalanced star

Fix a primitive Nth root Q, put d=N-1, and write

B_L(X,Z)=product_(r=0)^(L-1)(X-Q^r Z).

Let 0<=L_i<=d, S=L_1+L_2+L_3, and suppose

S<=2d,                 L_i+L_j>=d for every i!=j.       (1)

Define

e=2d-S,       k_i=d-L_i,       a_i=k_i-e.

Then a_i>=0, sum k_i=d+e, sum a_i=d-2e. In particular 0<=e<=d/2. The actual charged contraction is

F_L(X_1,X_2,X_3)=[Z^d] product_i B_(L_i)(X_i,Z).

There is an EXACT homogeneous polynomial R_L of degree e such that

F_L=Delta_L R_L,                                      (2)

Delta_L=
   product_(r<a_3)(X_1-Q^(k_2+1+r) X_2)
  *product_(r<a_1)(X_2-Q^(k_3+1+r) X_3)
  *product_(r<a_2)(X_3-Q^(k_1+1+r) X_1).               (3)

This holds in the ordinary polynomial ring, BEFORE nilpotent source reduction. Arbitrary nonzero coefficients in front of Z are allowed by substituting X_i -> X_i/b_i and multiplying by product b_i^L_i. One can subsequently identify neighbor variables as required by the actual graph.

Proof. Set X_i=Q^p X_j with

N-L_j <= p <= L_i.

The two incident products have, as roots in Z, intervals with exponents [p-L_i+1,p] and [-L_j+1,0]. The conditions on p make these intervals overlap or touch, and their union contains at least N consecutive integers. Their product is divisible by X_jN-ZN, up to a nonzero scalar. The remaining product has Z-degree S-N<=d-1. Therefore its product with X_jN-ZN has ZERO Z^d coefficient: the constant term would need degree d in the remainder, while the Z^N term is already too large. Thus every linear factor displayed in (3) divides F_L. They are pairwise nonassociate factors in the three-variable polynomial ring. Their total degree is d-2e. Since F_L has degree S-d=d-e, the quotient has degree e. The nonzero coefficient below also proves that F_L and R_L are not identically zero. This argument uses only the full cyclotomic product and polynomial divisibility.

2. A nonzero marked coefficient fixes the normalization

Put T(n)=n(n-1)/2 and use the Gaussian binomial with base Q. The coefficient of X_2^e in R_L is exactly

c_L=(-1)^(d-a_3)
    Q^[T(L_1)+T(k_1)-(k_2+1)a_3-T(a_3)]
    {L_3 choose k_1}_Q.                              (4)

In particular c_L!=0: L_3=k_1+a_2<=d and 0<=k_1<=L_3.

To prove (4), set X_1=0. The first block contributes (-1)^L_1 Q^T(L_1) Z^L_1. To get the largest X_2 exponent in the remaining Z^k_1 coefficient, take no Z from the second block and k_1 powers from the third. The resulting coefficient is (-1)^d Q1 {L_3 choose k_1}_Q at X_2^(a_1+a_3+e) X_3^a_2. The corresponding leading coefficient of Delta_L is (-1)^a_3 Q2. Division gives (4).

At e=0 the quotient is the single scalar c_L, recovering the balanced star–triangle theorem, with all phases retained. At e>0, replacing R_L by this one coefficient is NOT an exact source identity.

3. Growing defect jets have an explicit uniform exponential scale

For EVERY admissible e, not just a fixed degree, the coefficient norm satisfies the explicit uniform estimate

|c_L| <= ||R_L||_1 <= (e+1)^5 (2N)^(4e) |c_L|.       (5)

The norm is the sum of the ordinary complex coefficient moduli, not the value after contraction against an exterior knot source. This already covers e_N log N=o(N). The refinement (5a) below extends the conclusion to EVERY e_N=o(N).

Here is a direct proof that does not hide constants growing with e. Set a=X_1/X_3, b=X_3/X_2. If c_D is the unit-modulus leading coefficient of Delta_L, then

Delta_L=c_D X_2^(a_1+a_3) X_3^a_2 D(a,b),

where D is a product of a_2 factors (1-unita), a_1 factors (1-unitb), and a_3 factors (1-unitab). The quotient R_L/X_2^e has monomials a^u b^(u+w), u+w<=e. All needed a and b exponents therefore lie between zero and e.

Also F_L/(c_D X_2^(d-e) b^a_2) is an ORDINARY polynomial in a,b: in each monomial of F_L the X_2 degree is at most L_2, and d-e-L_2=a_2. Its coefficient at a^i b^j, for i,j<=e, comes from the original three Z indices

L_1-i,           j,           k_1-j+i.

The first two binomials have moduli <=N^i and <=N^j. The third differs from {L_3 choose k_1}_Q by |i-j| adjacent-index steps. Each ratio or its reciprocal along valid indices has modulus <=N: all its nonzero factors satisfy 4/N<=|1-Q^r|<=2. Thus these coefficients of F_L, after dividing by c_D, are bounded by

|c_L| N^(i+j+|i-j|) <= |c_L| N^(2e).

Invalid indices give zero; no vanishing factorial is inverted. The same bound holds for any complex primitive Nth root, since its nonzero factors are a permutation of the standard ones.

Expand 1/D formally at (0,0). Its coefficient of a^u b^v is a sum over at most e+1 choices t for the number of factors of type ab. Each term is bounded by a product of three complete homogeneous coefficients of lengths <=d and degrees u-t,v-t,t. Their elementary counting bounds give, for u,v<=e,

|[a^u b^v](1/D)| <= (e+1)(2N)^(u+v).

Convolving with the needed rectangle of F_L uses at most (e+1)^2 terms. There are at most (e+1)^2 coefficients of R_L. Combining these bounds proves the upper estimate (5); (4) gives its lower estimate. This proof permits e to grow with N throughout.

The counting can be sharpened uniformly, including e=0. Put B=binom(N+e,e). Then

||R_L||_1/|c_L| <= (e+1)^5 B^6
             <= (e+1)^5 (2 exp(1) N/e)^(6e), e>=1. (5a)

The first bound applies also at e=0, when B=1 and R_L=c_L.

Indeed the kth smallest modulus among the N-1 nonzero root factors is at least 2k/N: distance min(j,N-j) occurs at most twice and 2 sin(pi*j/N)>=4 min(j,N-j)/N. Therefore the product of ANY r distinct such factors is at least (2/N)^r r!, at every primitive root. A Gaussian-binomial index shift of r steps has numerator bounded by 2^r and r distinct denominator factors, so its ratio is at most N^r/r!, in either direction when the indices are valid.

The first two binomials in the rectangle have moduli at most their ordinary binomial coefficients: their elementary-symmetric expansion is a sum of binom(L,i) unit terms. These counts are <=B for i<=e and L<=d. For the third relative ratio, r<=e gives

N^r/r! <= N^e/e! <= B.

Thus every rectangle coefficient is bounded by |c_L|B^3. Each complete homogeneous coefficient of degree r<=e and length <=d is a sum of at most binom(d+r-1,r)<=B unit terms. The three groups in 1/D therefore give (e+1)B^3. The convolution and quotient-support counts each contribute at most (e+1)^2, proving (5a). Finally B<= [exp(1)(N+e)/e]^e<= [2 exp(1)N/e]^e for e>=1. No constant in these bounds depends on e.

Consequently log(||R_L||_1/|c_L|)=o(N) for every e=o(N), since e log(N/e)=o(N). This includes all sublinear windows, not just a fixed normal depth or an O(sqrt N) window. At e=0, R_L=c_L exactly.

For the ACTUAL standard root Q=exp(2pii/N), the exact modulus of (4) is N/(|F_(k_1) F_(k_2-e) F_(k_3)|). The uniform endpoint factorial estimate and (5a) consequently give, for e>=1,

log ||R_L||_1 = (N/pi) sum_i Lambda(pi*k_i/N)
                     +O(e log(N/e)+e+log N).          (6)

The replacement of F_(k_2-e) by F_(k_2) obeys the same error bound: the intervening e factors have product between (2/N)^e e! and 2^e. At e=0 the error in (6) is simply O(log N).

Here Lambda(theta)=-integral_0^theta log|2 sin u| du. The last formula uses the uniform endpoint-aware factorial estimate already proved for the original state, together with reflection about N. In particular if e=o(N) and k_i/N -> t_i>0, then sum t_i=1. The corresponding limiting ideal tetrahedron has angles pi*t_i, and

lim (2pi/N) log ||R_L||_1 = 2 Vol(tetrahedron).       (7)

The factor TWO is essential. A single extracted scalar must not be counted as one tetrahedron in the volume-conjecture normalization. The entire transformed source, including its new triangle factors, is what preserves the knot period.

4. What this permits in the proposed leading/jet reduction

Equations (2)-(5) are a precise, source-compatible local meaning of “a geometric factor with a controlled quantum correction.” The correction is retained as a homogeneous polynomial; it is not erased by an unsupported assertion of nilpotence or rigidity.

The degree defect is the exact angular source-phase frequency

There is an analytic interpretation on the original unit-radius anchor torus. Let a charged product, including all its explicit constant normalizers, be

P(X)=gamma product_f (alpha_f X_(i_f)-beta_f X_(j_f)),
|alpha_f|=|beta_f|=1, X_a=exp(i theta_a).

Let S_a count the incident linear factors at anchor a, counting a self-loop twice. The source density is P(X) product_a X_a^(-d). On fixed real argument lifts, the elementary identity

exp(i A)-exp(i B)=2i exp(i(A+B)/2) sin((A-B)/2)

gives EXACTLY

P(exp(i theta)) product_a exp(-i*d*theta_a)
   =C exp[(i/2)sum_a (S_a-2d)theta_a] A(theta),       (8)

where C is an explicit theta-independent nonzero scalar and A(theta) is the real product of the displayed sine factors. A(theta) may change sign; this is not a positivity assertion. In particular, away from its zero set the angular phase derivative equals

partial_(theta_a) Arg(source density)=(S_a-2d)/2.    (9)

Equation (8) is global on the lifted angles. Its sine signs account for the apparent phase jumps and the periodicity of the original integer-degree density. Self-loops satisfy the same formula because their common anchor contributes one full theta_a phase.

Thus the additive fusion defect S_a-2d is precisely the source’s angular frequency, not only a formal incidence count. A full-block contraction adds the two endpoint defects at the identified anchor; a balanced star–triangle move removes a zero-frequency direction and preserves every outer defect. These are the exact incidence identities proved in the general fusion note. Removing an explicit self-loop monomial reduces both S_a and 2d by the same amount, when the remaining source charge is written correctly.

For the below-balance central star here, S=2d-e, so this exact central frequency is -e/2. The retained degree-e polynomial and (5) quantify its actual cost, including every growing e=o(N) by (5a). A defect of order N is a macroscopic source phase. Its bound in (5) is not subexponential. At nonunit anchor radii, the real part of each logarithmic factor derivative need not be 1/2; (8)-(9) are not an exclusion theorem for complex critical points on deformed contours.

There are two necessary limitations for the actual conjecture. First, the original clocks range across O(N), so a balance defect need not satisfy e=o(N); (5a) then gives no subexponential comparison. Second, a nonzero polynomial with a large norm can pair to zero with the exterior source. Equations (5)-(7) do not give a scalar lower bound after that pairing. A global theorem controlling defects and preserving the geometric contribution remains necessary.

The coefficient identities are independently audited in the defect-jet audit. The separate exact source fusion note gives the full-length reproducing kernel and the consequent contraction of the original closure adapters. No new broad-class volume theorem is asserted here.
