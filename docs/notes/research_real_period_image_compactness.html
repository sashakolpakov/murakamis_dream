Finite real-period image boundaries and their source contours

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The original positive period image already has an exact description by tilted minima of the compact angle energy. This note strengthens its finite-boundary conclusion: on every bounded real-period set, all angular heights, returns and square covectors are uniformly bounded. Every boundary approach therefore has finite sharp corner data. For a proper selected subset of a connected original tree, complete outside vertices also prevent vanishing half-angle observation factors.

These statements do not make the inverse image a whole quantum contour. A general real family in that image meets genuine trace poles at infinitely many levels. That fact diagnoses a required detour problem, not an impossibility of transporting the original cycle. The component containing the geometric center is not assumed to contain this real family.

2. Uniform finite-period bounds

Fix R≥0R\ge0, put cmax=max⁡vcvc_{\max}=\max_v c_v, and define HR=Emax+2πRα,KR=max⁡{2R+(2cmax+1)HR,arsinh(cosh(HR/2))},LR=KR/2+HR/4.(11) \begin{aligned} H_R&=\frac{E_{\max}+2\pi R}{\alpha},\\ K_R&=\max\left\{2R+(2c_{\max}+1)H_R, \operatorname{arsinh}\bigl(\cosh(H_R/2)\bigr)\right\},\\ L_R&=K_R/2+H_R/4 . \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(11)}

Theorem 1. 3. Every finite image-boundary approach

Corollary 2. Suppose uj∈Ξcu_j\in\Xi_c tends to a finite u*u_*. The primal assignments tend to the unique closed minimizer zu*z_{u_*}. Heights, returns and square covectors have a jointly convergent subsequence, with (12). If zu*z_{u_*} is on the angle boundary, every noninterior square is a corner, and at each such square (A,y)=(εAπ/4,εyπ/4),U=εAx>0,V=εyB>0,sinh⁡(2U)sinh⁡(2V)=1.(22) (A,y)=(\varepsilon_A\pi/4,\varepsilon_y\pi/4),\qquad U=\varepsilon_Ax>0,\quad V=\varepsilon_yB>0,\qquad \sinh(2U)\sinh(2V)=1. \qquad\text{(22)} The original charge, real-period and matching rows pass to the limit without resetting their branches.

Proof. Continuity in (7) gives the primal convergence; Theorem 1 and the bounded real coordinates give subsequential compactness of all remaining quantities. An open-side square would make one of x=12arsinh⁡(tan⁡(2A)cos⁡(2y)),B=12arsinh⁡(tan⁡(2y)cos⁡(2A))(23) x=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(\tan(2A)\cos(2y)),\qquad B=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(\tan(2y)\cos(2A)) \qquad\text{(23)} diverge, contrary to (12). At a corner, pass to the exact identity sinh⁡(2x)sinh⁡(2B)=sin⁡(2A)sin⁡(2y).(24) \sinh(2x)\sinh(2B)=\sin(2A)\sin(2y). \qquad\text{(24)} The signs of x,Bx,B are those of A,yA,y; bounded limits in (24) therefore give exactly (22). All period and matching rows are finite linear identities in these coordinates. ▫\square

This strengthens the finite-boundary statement in the earlier inverse-image theorem for complete outside data. It does not assert uniqueness of the reconstructed corner covectors when only the closed primal minimizer has been specified.

Corollary 3 (proper-selection observation units). If SS is proper in the connected original tree, then for each RR there is δR>0\delta_R>0 such that, at every solution covered by Theorem 1 and every selected vertex, |cos⁡(tv/2)|,|sin⁡(tv/2)|,|sin⁡tv|≥δR.(25) |\cos(t_v/2)|,\quad |\sin(t_v/2)|,\quad|\sin t_v|\ \ge\delta_R. \qquad\text{(25)} No nonzero lower bound on |hv||h_v| is asserted.

Proof. If the cosine bound failed, choose a sequence of solutions with that cosine tending to zero. Theorem 1 and the compact real-angle ranges give a joint subsequential limit. The limiting angle at this vertex is tv=πt_v=\pi or −π-\pi. Use the division-free original identity sinh⁡rv,ecos⁡(tv/2)=sin⁡(tv/2)cos⁡(tw/2).(26) \sinh r_{v,e}\cos(t_v/2) =\sin(t_v/2)\cos(t_w/2). \qquad\text{(26)} Its bounded factors pass to the limit. Since the limiting sine is 11 or −1-1, every neighbor has cos⁡(tw/2)=0\cos(t_w/2)=0. This propagates along the selected component to a complete outside neighbor, where the cosine equals one. Every selected component has such a neighbor because the original tree is connected and SS is proper. This is a contradiction.

If a half-angle sine tends to zero, its limiting angle must be tv=0t_v=0. Equation (26) makes every incident sinh⁡rv,e=0\sinh r_{v,e}=0. The closed range |ℑrv,e|≤π/2|\Im r_{v,e}|\le\pi/2 forces rv,e=0r_{v,e}=0. The limit of (8) would have imaginary part 0=−πcv0=-\pi c_v, again impossible. The only additional zeros of sin⁡tv\sin t_v in the closed angular range are tv=±πt_v=\pm\pi, already excluded. Compactness and finiteness of the vertices prove uniformity. ▫\square

The tangent variables are consequently bounded, and the original relations e±rv,e=cos⁡(tw/2)(qe±av)(27) e^{\pm r_{v,e}}=\cos(t_w/2)(q_e\pm a_v) \qquad\text{(27)} bound qe±avq_e\pm a_v away from zero. These conclusions do not exclude qe=±iq_e=\pm i at a general tilted corner. Thus they do not prove boundedness of xe=12πilog⁡qe+iqe−i.(28) x_e=\frac1{2\pi i}\log\frac{q_e+i}{q_e-i}. \qquad\text{(28)} In particular the radial endpoint exclusion and two-square matching theorem cannot be imported into this tilted image. 4. A classical gap away from the center

Suppose in addition that 0∈Ξc0\in\Xi_c, so the original charged positive solution t0t^0 is supplied. Retain the exact action Φc(t)=∑eψ(tv,tw)+i4π∑v∈Snvtv2−i∑v∈Scvtv.(29) \Phi_c(t)=\sum_e\psi(t_v,t_w) +\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{v\in S}n_vt_v^2 -i\sum_{v\in S}c_vt_v. \qquad\text{(29)} The original edge identity of the inverse-descent theorem gives, with M∞=ψ(0,0)=4GCat/πM_\infty=\psi(0,0)=4G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/\pi, on the entire positive inverse slice, ℜΦc(t(u))=mM∞−ℰ(zu)π.(30) \Re\Phi_c(t(u)) =mM_\infty-\frac{\mathcal E(z_u)}{\pi}. \qquad\text{(30)} Indeed summing πℜψ+4Ee−gvhv−gwhw=πM∞\pi\Re\psi+4E_e-g_vh_v-g_wh_w=\pi M_\infty and using ∑gv=nvsv/2−πcv\sum g_v=n_vs_v/2-\pi c_v gives (30). For every 0<r≤R<∞0<r\le R<\infty, Δr,R:=minr≤∥u∥1≤R{ℰ(zu)−ℰ(z0)}>0.(31) \Delta_{r,R} :=\min_{r\le\|u\|_1\le R} \{\mathcal E(z_u)-\mathcal E(z_0)\}>0. \qquad\text{(31)} To prove it, use continuity of the closed minimizers and compactness of the parameter annulus. Since z0z_0 is interior, it could also minimize ℰ−u⋅s\mathcal E-u\cdot s only if u⋅ds=0u\cdot ds=0 on every feasible tangent. The projection to selected ss is surjective: change one incidence per selected vertex to restore its row. Hence u=0u=0, excluded in (31).

Thus finite image-boundary approaches away from the center retain a strict calibrated classical action gap. At a regular single-square image boundary satisfying the already proved proper-selected-set Jacobian criterion, the inverse equations continue locally through the wall. The continued cells need not be positive, so the original convex argument does not control an unrestricted continuation beyond that neighborhood. At more general finite corners, (22) alone supplies no such continuation. No conclusion about the quantum source follows from the classical gap without its actual contour estimates.

5. The real slice and actual trace poles

The full positive image is not generally confined to lower angular heights. This is an exact general statement:

Proposition 4. Suppose nv≠0n_v\ne0 and |nv|>2cv|n_v|>2c_v for every selected vertex. Then the real configuration sv=2πcvnv,tv=sv,(32) s_v=\frac{2\pi c_v}{n_v},\qquad t_v=s_v, \qquad\text{(32)} with complete outside angles, lies on the positive inverse slice for the real parameter uv=∑e∋vrv,e(s)u_v=\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}(s). Near that parameter the heights hvh_v, as functions of the real period coordinates, attain a neighborhood of zero.

At unit charge, if all |nv|≥3|n_v|\ge3 are odd, every odd level NN divisible by all |nv||n_v| makes (32) an original angular grid point at every selected vertex. The literal unit-coupon factorial density has genuine trace double poles there.

Proof. The angles in (32) lie in (−π,π)(-\pi,\pi). All tangents ava_v are real, and qe=1+av2+aw2>av2+aw2q_e=\sqrt{1+a_v^2+a_w^2}>\sqrt{a_v^2+a_w^2}; thus every original edge is positive and its returns are real. The imaginary part of PvP_v is −nvsv/2=−πcv-n_vs_v/2=-\pi c_v, proving (8).

At this real point the complex period Jacobian is J=A−iD,A=A𝖳>0,D=diag⁡(nv/2).(33) J=A-iD,\qquad A=A^{\mathsf T}>0,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(n_v/2). \qquad\text{(33)} The real return matrix AA is positive definite by original edge monotonicity. The inverse-period height derivative is the real symmetric matrix ∂h∂u=ℑ(J−1)=J−*DJ−1.(34) \frac{\partial h}{\partial u} =\Im(J^{-1})=J^{-*}D J^{-1}. \qquad\text{(34)} It is invertible since DD is. The real inverse-function theorem proves the local height assertion.

For the stated odd common-multiple levels, the integers 2jv+1=N−2Nnv(35) 2j_v+1=N-\frac{2N}{n_v} \qquad\text{(35)} are positive, odd and less than 2N2N. Thus (32) equals τjv=π−(2jv+1)π/N\tau_{j_v}=\pi-(2j_v+1)\pi/N, the actual physical angular grid. The factor WN(t)=e−iNt(1−ηe−iNt)2,η=(−1)N−1,(36) W_N(t)=\frac{e^{-iNt}}{(1-\eta e^{-iNt})^2}, \qquad \eta=(-1)^{N-1}, \qquad\text{(36)} has a double pole there.

There is no cancellation of these poles by the literal local factorials. At the real stationary wire, π2−πxe=arctan⁡qe>|sv|/2,0<12−xe±sv2π<1.(37) \frac{\pi}{2}-\pi x_e=\arctan q_e>|s_v|/2, \qquad 0<\tfrac12-x_e\pm\frac{s_v}{2\pi}<1. \qquad\text{(37)} Consequently every shifted factorial argument N(1/2−xe±sv/(2π))−1/2N(1/2-x_e\pm s_v/(2\pi))-1/2 lies in (−1,N)(-1,N), and Nxe∈(0,N)Nx_e\in(0,N). All the original BNB_N’s are exact units. Also sin⁡sv≠0\sin s_v\ne0, the unit coupon is one, and the crossing Gaussian is a unit. This proves the last assertion about the specified density. ▫\square

Proposition 4 does not show that this real point belongs to the component of Ξc\Xi_c containing u=0u=0. Nor does the presence of poles forbid a valid cycle: it requires prescribed detours and retention of their residue or endpoint currents. On a compact regular phase chart, displacements of size O(1/N)O(1/N) change its exponential phase by only O(1)O(1); this observation is not an estimate for the entire original factorial network. Competing modes and the actual meromorphic factors still require their own coherent bounds.

For the original source the allowed relative boundary remains the one in the joint-lens theorem: fixed angular endpoint faces and fixed wire seams, with free cardinal ends unchanged. A finite corner image is not one of those prescribed source faces merely because the classical positive cells degenerate there. The compactness and gap proved above therefore leave a precise global task: construct the connecting relative chain, including all necessary pole detours and wire faces, and control its complete source. No such homology or remainder statement is inferred in this note. A literal factorial density is deployed as the original source only under its traced-end convention or a separately proved boundary adapter.
