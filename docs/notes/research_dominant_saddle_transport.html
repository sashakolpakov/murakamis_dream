Persistence of a dominant saddle and the conditions it needs

Research note, 2 November 2026. This note proves a continuation principle for a controlled holomorphic period. It also gives an explicit counterexample to replacing dominance along the path by dominance at its two endpoints. It does not establish the required global hypotheses for arbitrary short fillings.

The useful object is a one-dimensional relative homology group: after contours supported at lower real action are discarded, the selected saddle supplies one remaining generator. Transport preserves its coefficient. Ordinary Stokes changes only add lower generators to this one.

1. What the period and its leading coefficient mean

Let XtX_t be a complex manifold of dimension dd, depending smoothly on t∈[0,1]t\in[0,1]. Any poles or branch divisors have been removed; alternatively, a specified covering has been chosen on which the phase is single valued. Consider

IN(t)=∫ΓteNft(z)ωN,t(z),N→+∞.(1) I_N(t)=\int_{\Gamma_t}e^{Nf_t(z)}\omega_{N,t}(z), \qquad N\longrightarrow+\infty. \qquad\text{(1)}

Here ftf_t is holomorphic, Γt\Gamma_t is an oriented real dd-cycle with permitted decaying ends, and ωN,t\omega_{N,t} is a holomorphic dd-form on all chains used in the argument. The cycle must be transported as a relative homology class. Replacing it at each parameter by a convenient real contour without recording crossed poles is a different operation.

Write ht=Re⁡fth_t=\operatorname{Re}f_t. Suppose p(t)p(t) is a nondegenerate critical point. Choose continuous local Morse coordinates and the corresponding orientation so that

ft=ft(p(t))−12(w12+⋯+wd2).(2) f_t=f_t(p(t))-\frac12(w_1^2+\cdots+w_d^2). \qquad\text{(2)}

Near p(t)p(t), suppose

ωN,t=(at(w)+O(N−1))dw1⋯dwd,at(0)≠0,(3) \omega_{N,t}= \bigl(a_t(w)+O(N^{-1})\bigr)\,dw_1\cdots dw_d, \qquad a_t(0)\ne0, \qquad\text{(3)}

uniformly with the derivatives needed for the local integral. In arbitrary original coordinates, at(0)a_t(0) includes the chosen square root of the inverse Hessian determinant. Continuous transport can change its orientation sign around a loop, but cannot make it zero.

The integer multiplying the local descending cycle is denoted k(t)k(t). It is distinct from at(0)a_t(0): the former comes from the integration cycle, the latter from the integrand. Both must be nonzero to obtain the usual nonzero leading term.

2. A precise controlled-transport hypothesis

A statement merely saying that the critical points remain finite does not control the ends of a contour. The following are the specific topological and analytic assumptions used below.

Choose continuous regular levels c(t)c(t) and δ>0\delta>0 such that

ht(p(t))≥c(t)+2δ,ht(q)≤c(t)−2δfor every other critical point q.(4) h_t(p(t))\ge c(t)+2\delta, \qquad h_t(q)\le c(t)-2\delta \quad\text{for every other critical point \(q\)}. \qquad\text{(4)}

Put At={ht≤c(t)}A_t=\{h_t\le c(t)\}, together with the permitted exit ends. Require the following facts. For homology, truncate each decaying end once it lies in {ht<c(t)}\{h_t<c(t)\}; the remainder must be a finite chain whose boundary lies in AtA_t. Thus Hd(Xt,At;ℤ)H_d(X_t,A_t;\mathbb Z) below means ordinary relative homology of these truncated chains, with the vanishing tail integrals restored afterwards. No infinite formal sum of contours is included without a separate convergence argument.

Morse deformation above the cutoff. Lowering hth_t deforms the region above AtA_t to AtA_t with one real dd-handle, the handle at p(t)p(t). There are no additional handles from finite boundaries, deleted divisors, or infinity. The deformation and this handle identification are uniform in tt.

This condition has a concrete flow formulation. Outside a small Morse neighborhood, the normalized negative gradient has dht/ds=−1dh_t/ds=-1. Every part of a chain above the cutoff must follow this flow until it reaches AtA_t or the Morse neighborhood, without reaching a pole or escaping to infinity at finite height. This must hold uniformly on finite height intervals. Inside the Morse neighborhood, the quadratic model (2) attaches exactly its descending dd-disk. The corresponding parameter transport of the level boundary and exit ends must also exist without an escape. A proper Morse cobordism is one sufficient way to verify these requirements. For a noncompact period, they are facts to prove, not consequences of nondegeneracy.

Transport of the original cycle. The family of pairs (Xt,At)(X_t,A_t) and the original admissible cycle classes have compatible continuous identifications along the path. In particular no pole is crossed, no unrecorded residue cycle is added, and no integration boundary passes through the upper region.

Bounds on the lower chains. The deformations of the original cycle give lower chains RtR_t supported in AtA_t, with weighted integrability bounded by a power of NN. One precise sufficient estimate is, for a fixed N0>0N_0>0,

∫RteN0ht|ωN,t|≤CNb(5) \int_{R_t} e^{N_0h_t}\lvert\omega_{N,t}\rvert \le C N^b \qquad\text{(5)}

uniformly in tt. The portions of the selected descending cycle outside its local Morse neighborhood satisfy the corresponding estimate, with a fixed drop from ht(p(t))h_t(p(t)). All Stokes deformations have vanishing boundary integrals at the permitted ends. Polynomial volume growth and uniform exponential decay at those ends can establish (5), but must be checked for the particular period.

These hypotheses explicitly exclude a critical contribution at an end whose real action is as large as that of p(t)p(t). An exact cancellation showing that such an end represents no cycle can also remove it; an upper bound on finite critical values alone cannot.

3. The persistence theorem

Theorem 1. Under (2)–(5) and the controlled transport hypotheses of Section 2, the selected cycle coefficient k(t)k(t) is constant after its local orientation is transported. If k(0)≠0k(0)\ne0, then

IN(t)=k(0)(2πN)d/2at(0)eNft(p(t))(1+O(N−1)),(6) I_N(t)= k(0)\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^{d/2} a_t(0)e^{Nf_t(p(t))} \bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr), \qquad\text{(6)}

uniformly on the compact parameter path. In particular the leading coefficient never vanishes.

Proof. The Morse deformation identifies

Hd(Xt,At;ℤ)≃ℤ.(7) H_d(X_t,A_t;\mathbb Z)\simeq\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(7)}

Indeed, outside the one critical neighborhood the normalized flow lowers the height to the cutoff. In the quadratic model, the change from the lower sublevel to the upper sublevel is the attachment of one dd-disk with its boundary already in the lower sublevel. Its relative homology in degree dd has one generator. The no-escape condition ensures that this procedure has not missed another component at a divisor, finite boundary, or infinity.

The selected descending cycle JtJ_t projects to that generator. The original cycle projects to k(t)[Jt]k(t)[J_t]. The identifications of pairs transport both classes continuously. The integer k(t)k(t) is consequently locally constant, and hence constant on the interval. This argument uses the group (7), not a choice of a complete thimble basis for all lower critical points.

Subtracting k(0)Jtk(0)J_t from Γt\Gamma_t gives a cycle whose image in (7) is zero. Its integral therefore equals the integral of a lower chain RtR_t, up to boundaries on which Stokes’ theorem applies. The form eNftωN,te^{Nf_t}\omega_{N,t} is closed: it is a holomorphic top-degree form. There are no residue terms because the deformations stay in XtX_t. For N≥N0N\ge N_0, (5) gives

|∫RteNftωN,t|≤CNbe(N−N0)c(t).(8) \left|\int_{R_t}e^{Nf_t}\omega_{N,t}\right| \le C N^b e^{(N-N_0)c(t)}. \qquad\text{(8)}

The selected cycle outside its local neighborhood has the same type of exponentially smaller estimate. Inside it, use (2), rescale w=N−1/2vw=N^{-1/2}v, and integrate the Taylor expansion in (3) against the real Gaussian. Odd terms integrate to zero; the first relative error is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). The leading integral is (2π/N)d/2at(0)(2\pi/N)^{d/2}a_t(0). Uniform nondegeneracy and compactness of the parameter interval give a common neighborhood and common constants. The strict gap (4) makes (8) exponentially smaller than this nonzero term, proving (6). ▫\square

This formulation separates the topological conclusion from the analytic estimates. A proof of a relative homology identification alone does not supply (5); a critical-value inequality alone supplies neither.

4. Why ordinary Stokes walls do not remove a dominant term

There is an elementary equivalent description when the descending cycles form a finite basis. A downward gradient trajectory preserves Im⁡f\operatorname{Im}f and strictly decreases Re⁡f\operatorname{Re}f. An ordinary Stokes event is a connection from one saddle php_h to another plp_l, with

Im⁡f(ph)=Im⁡f(pl),Re⁡f(ph)>Re⁡f(pl).(9) \operatorname{Im}f(p_h)=\operatorname{Im}f(p_l), \qquad \operatorname{Re}f(p_h)>\operatorname{Re}f(p_l). \qquad\text{(9)}

Assume this is an isolated connection, all critical points remain nondegenerate, and no pole, boundary, or end event occurs at the same parameter.

Cut the connecting descending cycle just before it enters a Morse neighborhood of plp_l. On the two sides of the wall, the two ways of continuing the cut differ by a local descending disk at plp_l. Continuing that disk downwards gives the lower descending cycle. Thus, with consistent orientations,

Jh+=Jh−+mJl−,Jl+=Jl−,(10) J_h^+=J_h^-+mJ_l^-, \qquad J_l^+=J_l^-, \qquad\text{(10)}

where mm is the signed count of the connections; at a single transverse elementary event it is 11 or −1-1. Cycles not involved in the event are unchanged. This is a cut-and-paste description of the usual Picard–Lefschetz change, with its direction fixed by the decreasing real action.

Writing the same transported cycle in the two bases gives

kh+=kh−,kl+=kl−−mkh−.(11) k_h^+=k_h^-, \qquad k_l^+=k_l^- -m k_h^-. \qquad\text{(11)}

It is the coefficient of the lower saddle that can change. A strictly dominant saddle is never the lower endpoint, so its coefficient survives every such wall. The filtered proof in Section 3 avoids any difficulty caused by several changes among lower saddles.

There is a useful weaker test: a selected coefficient can change only when it receives a term from a higher saddle whose coefficient is nonzero. One could prove persistence by excluding these particular connections, without proving that the selected saddle has the largest real value among all critical points.

If every critical branch has one fixed strict real ordering along a path, all these matrices preserve the same triangular filtration. Their off-diagonal parts are nilpotent. By contrast, knowing that each individual wall matrix is unipotent is insufficient. Matrices

(1101),(1011)(12) \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix} \qquad\text{(12)}

are individually unipotent, but their product has eigenvalues (3+5)/2(3+\sqrt5)/2 and (3−5)/2(3-\sqrt5)/2. A common ordering, or a common invariant one-dimensional quotient, is the relevant extra property.

5. Endpoint dominance is insufficient: an entire cubic example

Let Γ\Gamma run from infinity on the ray arg⁡z=−π/3\arg z=-\pi/3 to the origin, then from the origin to infinity on the ray arg⁡z=π/3\arg z=\pi/3. Put

IN(λ)=∫Γexp⁡[N(z33−λz)]dz,λ=eiθ,2π3≤θ≤2π.(13) I_N(\lambda)=\int_\Gamma \exp\left[N\left(\frac{z^3}{3}-\lambda z\right)\right]\,dz, \qquad \lambda=e^{i\theta},\quad \frac{2\pi}{3}\le\theta\le2\pi. \qquad\text{(13)}

The contour is fixed. Its ends have uniform cubic decay for all these λ\lambda. The integrand is entire, and its two critical points are distinct and nondegenerate throughout the path. Continue the selected branch by

p(θ)=eiθ/2,fθ(p(θ))=−23e3iθ/2.(14) p(\theta)=e^{i\theta/2}, \qquad f_\theta(p(\theta)) =-\frac23e^{3i\theta/2}. \qquad\text{(14)}

At both endpoints, its critical value is 2/32/3; the other critical value is −2/3-2/3. Thus the selected point is uniquely dominant at each endpoint, with the same fixed gap 4/34/3.

At the first endpoint, substitute z=eiπ/3wz=e^{i\pi/3}w. Then

fθ(z)=−w33+w.(15) f_\theta(z)=-\frac{w^3}{3}+w. \qquad\text{(15)}

The transformed contour consists of an incoming ray of argument −2π/3-2\pi/3 and the positive real axis. On the incoming ray w=re−2πi/3w=re^{-2\pi i/3}, Re⁡f=−r3/3−r/2\operatorname{Re}f=-r^3/3-r/2, so its integral is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). On the positive real axis, the unique maximum is at w=1w=1, with value 2/32/3 and second derivative −2-2. Ordinary real Gaussian estimation gives

IN(e2πi/3)=eiπ/3πNe2N/3(1+O(N−1)).(16) I_N(e^{2\pi i/3})= e^{i\pi/3}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{N}}\, e^{2N/3}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr). \qquad\text{(16)}

At the final endpoint λ=1\lambda=1, deform the contour to the vertical line z=1+itz=1+it, oriented upwards. This deformation is legitimate because the integrand is entire. In the coordinate w=z−1w=z-1, the joining arcs have π/3≤|arg⁡w|≤π/2\pi/3\le|\arg w|\le\pi/2; both Re⁡w3≤0\operatorname{Re}w^3\le0 and Re⁡w2≤−|w|2/2\operatorname{Re}w^2\le-\lvert w\rvert^2/2 there, so the large joining integrals tend to zero. On the vertical line,

(1+it)33−(1+it)=−23−t2−it33.(17) \frac{(1+it)^3}{3}-(1+it) =-\frac23-t^2-\frac{i t^3}{3}. \qquad\text{(17)}

Rescaling t=N−1/2st=N^{-1/2}s gives

IN(1)=iπNe−2N/3(1+O(N−1)).(18) I_N(1)= i\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{N}}\, e^{-2N/3}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr). \qquad\text{(18)}

The selected branch at this endpoint is p=−1p=-1, with value 2/32/3, but its coefficient is zero. The integral comes from the other saddle p=1p=1.

There is no pole, amplitude zero, critical collision, or change of the decaying ends in this example. The missing hypothesis is precisely dominance along the path: Re⁡fθ(p(θ))<0\operatorname{Re}f_\theta(p(\theta))<0 for π<θ<5π/3\pi<\theta<5\pi/3. At the interior ordinary Stokes wall θ=4π/3\theta=4\pi/3, the selected saddle is the lower one and its coefficient can change as in (11). The endpoints lie on ordinary Stokes rays, but the real gap and the displayed leading coefficients are unambiguous. Small perturbations of the endpoints into their adjacent sectors give the same example with endpoints off those rays.

6. Other hypotheses that cannot be dropped

Nonzero cycle coefficient does not guarantee nonzero leading amplitude. For example,

∫ℝ(t+z)e−Nz2dz=tπN(19) \int_{\mathbb R}(t+z)e^{-Nz^2}\,dz =t\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{N}} \qquad\text{(19)}

has one nondegenerate saddle and fixed decaying ends, but vanishes at t=0t=0. Here the local amplitude vanishes.

Poles can contribute a rate not represented by any critical point. For f(z)=−z2f(z)=-z^2, a small positively oriented circle around z=iz=i gives

∫|z−i|=εe−Nz2z−idz=2πieN.(20) \int_{|z-i|=\varepsilon} \frac{e^{-Nz^2}}{z-i}\,dz=2\pi i e^N. \qquad\text{(20)}

The only finite saddle has critical value zero. A description using that saddle alone has omitted the pole cycle. Moving poles or finite integration boundaries require their corresponding terms to be tracked just as explicitly.

Equal real actions also require a separate argument. Two local Gaussian contributions with actions MM and M+iπM+i\pi and equal coefficients have sum

πNeNM(1+(−1)N).(21) \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{N}}\,e^{NM} \bigl(1+(-1)^N\bigr). \qquad\text{(21)}

Each local coefficient is nonzero, but the sum vanishes at every odd integer level. One can realize (21) on two components of a holomorphic period. The example is relevant when different logarithmic lifts or periodic aliases correspond to the same geometric representation. A volume bound on representations does not distinguish those lifts.

Finally, a family with NN-dependent divisors, phase branches, or cycles requires uniform versions of all the hypotheses. The identity of its classical limiting phase does not supply them. If a common top relative homology line exists at every level, its coefficient must be shown nonzero at every sufficiently large level, with subexponential normalization; a computation at one level is insufficient.

7. What representation-volume rigidity would supply

The papers of Francaviglia–Klaff and Francaviglia–Savini state rigidity results for representations of the fundamental group of a fixed complete finite-volume hyperbolic manifold. The latter also treats sequences whose volumes approach the maximum. These are relevant coverage references, not proofs imported into Theorem 1.

Even if those statements are independently verified, applying them at an integer hyperbolic filling first requires that every competing critical solution define an actual representation of that filled group, and that its real action equal the correctly normalized representation volume. An exponential meridian equation alone may not kill the full meridian matrix. Uncalibrated action constants and logarithmic lifts must also be accounted for.

After those checks, rigidity can help order the real critical values at that endpoint. A compactness statement for representations near maximal volume can help exclude character-variety ends there. Neither statement proves dominance along a deformation, as the cubic example shows. Nor does character compactness prove properness of a chosen quantum chart: decoration coordinates or logarithmic lifts may escape while the represented character stays bounded.

For a primitive cone deformation, the meridian equation is

U+pV=−2πiλ,0<λ<1.(22) U+pV=-2\pi i\lambda, \qquad 0<\lambda<1. \qquad\text{(22)}

Such a representation generally belongs to the unfilled exterior group; it does not factor through the ordinary filled manifold group. Complete representation-volume rigidity for the integer filling therefore does not apply at these intermediate parameters. Rigidity for the complete parent would give the parent’s volume as an upper bound, whereas the geometric cone branch has already decreased below that value. This does not compare a competitor with the cone branch itself.

One would need a relative or cone-angle maximality statement, with the same primitive constraints and the correct boundary correction, or another proof excluding incoming Stokes contributions to the selected branch. In addition, the geometric branch must remain nondegenerate along the chosen path. Hyperbolicity of the final integer filling supplies neither assertion.

The completed adjacent two-crossing calculation is a useful warning. Its escaping alias has the same real action as the finite geometric saddle, as proved in the Bloch identity. The successful proof removes that end by the exact full-circle cancellation. It does not obtain a strict inequality between those two critical values. A general period-continuation approach must allow this kind of exact removal before claiming that the remaining top relative group is one-dimensional.

8. Attribution and the resulting research target

The Stokes interpretation by relative cycles and intersection numbers is established asymptotic analysis. A directly relevant reference, including integration boundaries, is Delabaere–Howls, Global asymptotics for multiple integrals with boundaries, Duke Mathematical Journal 112 (2002), 199–264. Its institutional record describes its treatment of generalized Picard–Lefschetz changes and boundary contributions. The arguments in Sections 3–5 were written directly here; no theorem from that article is needed for them.

For the knot problem, the potentially useful general theorem is now precise: construct the actual period of the restored finite source, prove that its top relative homology quotient is a transported line after the genuine lower or null end cycles are removed, and prove a nonzero local amplitude with uniform subexponential normalization. A starting nonzero coefficient would then survive by Theorem 1.

This could replace many separate contour certificates. It has not yet been established for arbitrary short fillings. Endpoint volume rigidity, or unipotence of individual Stokes matrices, does not by itself supply the missing transported line.

9. An exact analytic coupon family and the proved initializer

The small-charge source theorem now supplies an actual nonzero initial source on a fixed small positive charge box. Its scope includes the original positive paths, and the specified same-index finite graph sums treated there. This does not initialize the source at charge zero. The following interpolation makes the finite parameter family precise without asserting that its top relative quotient has been controlled.

Fix NN, put D=N−1,η=(−1)DD=N-1,\ \eta=(-1)^D, and use the original physical angles and spectral variables τa=π(D−2a)N,ca=−2cos⁡τa.(23) \tau_a=\frac{\pi(D-2a)}N,\qquad c_a=-2\cos\tau_a . \qquad\text{(23)} For a complex parameter χ\chi, let 𝒞N,χ\mathcal C_{N,\chi} be the unique polynomial of degree less than NN with the following full physical data. The exact trace contour continues to hold with angular representative cos⁡(N(1−χv)tv)\cos(N(1-\chi_v)t_v). Consequently, for each NN, the surrounding rectangles and their grid-pole divisors can be chosen independent of 𝝌\boldsymbol\chi. All vertical currents remain included. This is an exact analytic family in the original algebra, not a new assertion about a topological invariant at a nonintegral charge.

In particular the all-zero-charge source equals ηm\eta^m times the uninserted source, where mm is the number of traced vertices. It is not a known complete bare-edge value. At that endpoint the formal zero-charge central phase is even in every selected angle, while each trace contributes its odd factor sin⁡tv\sin t_v. Its symmetric local Gaussian observation vanishes. The already proved initializer is instead a box bounded away from zero.

There is no analogous cancellation from the companion at the other endpoint. More quantitatively, on a compact neighborhood with |ℜt|≤A,−ℑt≥h0>0|\Re t|\le A,\ -\Im t\ge h_0>0, the numerator is uniformly nonzero for real 0<χ≤10<\chi\le1, at every NN. If s≥s0s\ge s_0, its modulus is at least 1−e−2s0h0>01-e^{-2s_0h_0}>0. The case A=0A=0 is immediate. Every fixed derivative in tt is uniformly bounded, using sups≥0ske−2sh0<∞.(27) \sup_{s\ge0}s^k e^{-2s h_0}<\infty . \qquad\text{(27)} The denominator in (26) and its derivatives are likewise uniformly controlled on this neighborhood.

The transition layer 1−χ=O(N−1)1-\chi=O(N^{-1}) must nevertheless retain its NN-dependent factor 1+e−2ist1+e^{-2ist}. It does not have one NN-independent limiting amplitude uniformly through χ=1\chi=1. The local proof of Theorem 1 has the following direct variant: one may replace ata_t in (3) by aN,ta_{N,t} if its spatial holomorphic derivatives are uniformly bounded and |aN,t(0)||a_{N,t}(0)| has a uniform positive lower bound. The same Gaussian Taylor proof gives (6) with aN,t(0)a_{N,t}(0), with the same relative O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) error. No derivative bound in the external parameter is required for this local estimate. Formula (26) satisfies this local amplitude requirement wherever the remaining actual source factors do.

Thus an exact analytic finite family and a nonzero starting observation are now available. The Stokes conclusion of Sections 3–4 is unchanged: a flat top relative line keeps its coefficient through ordinary lower-saddle Stokes events, without a scalar almost-invariance defect estimate. What is still missing for general charge continuation is a uniform identification of that line for the actual NN-dependent quantum period. In particular one must control all competing cycles, including divisor and infinity cycles, and verify the lower-chain bounds of Section 2. The finite edge kernels cannot simply be placed in a subexponential amplitude on an unproved global geometric contour. Fixed grid poles in the trace representation do not settle the other ends or aliases of a factorial-wire representation. Analyticity of the coupons and strict positivity of the geometric critical point alone do not discharge these conditions.
