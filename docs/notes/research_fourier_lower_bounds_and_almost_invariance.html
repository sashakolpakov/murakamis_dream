Fourier lower bounds and almost-invariant vectors

Research note, updated 4 November 2026.

There are two different uses of a positive norm in this problem. For the complete-color Fourier state, its norm differs from the squared Kashaev invariant by a proved polynomial remainder. A lower bound for that norm would therefore prove a lower bound for the invariant. For the positive sums obtained by replacing local terms by their absolute values, the available comparison goes in the other direction. Positivity of the comparison sum does not supply the missing lower bound.

There is now an actual initial family for changing the Fourier charge. The small-charge theorem proves a nonzero leading contribution for every fixed positive path with crossing powers at least three, after inserting TN−jv(−wv/2)T_{N-j_v}(-w_v/2) in its original traces and taking jv/N∈[1/640,1/320]j_v/N\in[1/640,1/320]. The coefficient is computed from the exact trace contour; every competing charge and endpoint term is bounded. Continuing this coefficient to jv=Nj_v=N, where the insertions disappear, remains the lower-bound problem. A nonzero starting contribution and a continuous geometric critical point do not themselves prove that continuation.

On [1/4000,1/2000][1/4000,1/2000], the statement extends to any fixed finite graph carrying these edge kernels, with no isolated selected vertex, and allows arbitrary fixed signed integer crossing powers, including zero. Section 16 verifies the Gaussian analogue of almost-invariance for this initialized source: its rescaled integrand has a nonzero independently computed norm and an O(N−1/2)O(N^{-1/2}) relative annihilation residual. These estimates currently hold on the small charge box; a knot realization of an arbitrary such graph is not part of the assertion.

The exact charge recurrence retains the free boundary color and has nonvanishing outer coefficients. With the physical period and reflection relations imposed, its solution space has dimension two at every free color. The original endpoint condition selects the actual source line. The extra solution has zero physical values and only first-normal data. This removes an ambiguity in the finite equation, but line uniqueness does not bound a particular Fourier observation of that line.

The charge-increment norm now gives an exact positive identity and a sharp asymptotic for every fixed graph carrying these same-index kernels. Its increments discard the normals by an exact period difference, and Parseval removes the unit crossing phases from their squared norm. The resulting growth is the sum of the unfilled edge rates, independent of the twists. For the proved three-crossing terminal source, the central coefficient divided by this norm is exponentially small. The full charge vector therefore has a rigorous lower bound without a corresponding lower bound for its specified central entry. This is an actual-source limitation of this particular positive Fourier norm.

There is already a stronger local lower bound in the all-color theorem. For a fixed filled tree with crossing powers of one sign, suppose QG=diag⁡(|nv|)−Adj⁡(G)Q_G=\operatorname{diag}(|n_v|)-\operatorname{Adj}(G) is positive definite. At each physical color assignment, the complete first-normal factor is a determinant with a positive definite imaginary part after the stated normalization. Its modulus is bounded below by a power of NN times the positive kernel product, and bounded above at polynomial additional cost. Thus cancellation within one color assignment is already controlled under this condition. The outstanding cancellation is between different assignments, which retain their original crossing and determinant phases. The tree determinant estimate is an established input, not a new consequence of the charge-increment calculation.

The explicit source reconstruction connects these facts for one charged edge with real free color. The period increment determines its double-node values by inverse Fourier transformation. A final Jacobi recurrence recovers the odd simple node, and the existing relative normal formula reconstructs all derivatives with polynomial cost. The resulting map from the increment vector to all original charge observations has polynomial operator norm. Its particular closing row can still be exponentially small on the source; the positive norm theorem supplies an example.

The positivity now extends to an exact boundary problem with a complex geometric free color. The cosine-phase maximum principle places every such color in ℜw<7/4\Re w<7/4 along the positive-path charge cube. For N≥16N\ge16, the ordinary Jacobi inverse has norm at most 55. Its Fourier image is coercive in an explicit positive metric of condition number at most N/2N/2, with the original endpoint forcing. A Ward representative bounds the first normals by a polynomial in NN inside subsequent original traces, preserving the mixed derivatives. This is a positive transport equation; its closing row is still the original signed observation.

The Riccati estimate also proves a nonzero local transport factor directly from the normalized physical solution. Its consecutive ratios contract in a right half-plane and approach the growing root uniformly, even with complex free color. The product over an interior row interval has the original edge-phase difference as exponent, with a factor bounded above and below independently of NN. This establishes the local coefficient for physical rows. The endpoint extension now reaches the last physical row, with logarithmic error and all fixed free-color derivatives. At odd levels this row is the complete color. At even levels the Chebyshev interpolation argument proves that the original low-index complete-end kernel divided by the last row is 1+O(N−1/2)1+O(N^{-1/2}). The bare-kernel parity example shows that the restriction to the low sector matters: at an explicit complex color the full even-level kernel has a strictly larger exponential rate than its odd-level counterpart. This is a kernel evaluation before tracing, not a statement about a knot invariant.

This distinction can be tested on the original source itself. The central-readout polynomial has a zero in the positive Jacobi half-plane at every level and power. At that zero, the row values remain nonzero and the Jacobi inverses are bounded by one. Hence those two facts alone cannot force a positive overlap with the closing row. The zeros have not been located on the actual geometric locus, so this does not exclude a more specific lower-bound theorem there.
