The full wire–angle phase at a two-square fold

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The original stationary wire can cease to be a holomorphic function of its endpoint angles when its square root vanishes. At the radial limits classified in the pinned two-square theorem, this does not make the full unsummed wire–angle phase degenerate. Its critical equations are locally equivalent, by explicit holomorphic units, to the regular lifted primitive system. The full holomorphic Hessian is therefore nonsingular, including at every admitted qe=0q_e=0.

This is a statement about the retained original phase and its local holomorphic germ. It neither assigns its coefficient in the finite source nor constructs a completed cone metric at the two-square limit. The phase of a reversed-index kernel or of a genuinely free cardinal boundary factor is not substituted by a same-index factorial phase.

1. The original phase and its marked germ

Use the hypotheses and limiting data of the two-square primitive lift: a finite connected original ordered circle tree, a nonempty proper selected set SS, complete outside angles, fixed integer original powers nv=ηv−2pvn_v=\eta_v-2p_v, and a supplied positive unit-charge solution. A sequence of actual positive common-charge solutions has a finite corner limit at 0<χ*<10<\chi_*<1.

All limiting selected angles have tv=sv−iHv,Hv>0,|sv|<π.(1) t_v=s_v-iH_v,\qquad H_v>0,\qquad |s_v|<\pi. \qquad\text{(1)} Every included edge is positive, a regular single-square corner, or a two-square matching edge whose endpoints each have another positive edge. On a two-square edge a=tan⁡(tv/2)=−iA,b=tan⁡(tw/2)=−iB,0<A,B<1,q=iQ,Q2=A2+B2−1≥0,(2) a=\tan(t_v/2)=-iA,\quad b=\tan(t_w/2)=-iB,\quad 0<A,B<1,\quad q=iQ,\quad Q^2=A^2+B^2-1\ge0, \qquad\text{(2)} so |Q|<min⁡(A,B)<1|Q|<\min(A,B)<1. A fold has Q=0Q=0.

For each original same-index edge retain its wire coordinate xex_e and the literal incidence phase b0(x,t)=−J(1/2−x+t/(2π))−J(1/2−x−t/(2π))−2J(x),J′(z)=Log⁡(2sin⁡πz).(3) b_0(x,t)=-J(1/2-x+t/(2\pi)) -J(1/2-x-t/(2\pi))-2J(x),\qquad J'(z)=\operatorname{Log}(2\sin\pi z). \qquad\text{(3)} Here b0b_0 denotes a phase, not the endpoint tangent bb. On the positive approach the original stationary wires satisfy qe=cot⁡(πxe),qe2=1+a2+b2.(5) q_e=\cot(\pi x_e),\qquad q_e^2=1+a^2+b^2. \qquad\text{(5)} The inverse through q=1,x=1/4q=1,x=1/4 is x(q)=12πiLog⁡q+iq−i,dxdq=−1π(1+q2).(6) x(q)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Log}\frac{q+i}{q-i},\qquad \frac{dx}{dq}=-\frac1{\pi(1+q^2)}. \qquad\text{(6)} For a positive or single-square edge, ℜq>0\Re q>0 and 0<ℜx<1/20<\Re x<1/2. Every sine appearing in (3) is nonzero at this stationary limit. Indeed sin⁡πx≠0\sin\pi x\ne0, and sin⁡π(1/2−x+t/(2π))=sin⁡πxcos⁡(t/2)(q+a),sin⁡π(1/2−x−t/(2π))=sin⁡πxcos⁡(t/2)(q−a).(8) \begin{aligned} \sin\pi(1/2-x+t/(2\pi)) &=\sin\pi x\,\cos(t/2)(q+a),\\ \sin\pi(1/2-x-t/(2\pi)) &=\sin\pi x\,\cos(t/2)(q-a). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(8)} All factors on the right are units by (1)–(2) and the pinned classification of the other edges.

There is no branch-existence assertion hidden here. The finite sharp square limits have bounded strip coordinates, with limiting disk points away from 1,−1,i,−i1,-1,i,-i. 2. Exact equivalence of the critical equations

Temporarily make qeq_e an independent variable for every edge, using (6) to change the wire coordinates. Write fe=qe2−1−a2−b2,de=(1+a2)(1+b2),γe=1+qe2de.(10) f_e=q_e^2-1-a^2-b^2,\qquad d_e=(1+a^2)(1+b^2),\qquad \gamma_e=\frac{1+q_e^2}{d_e}. \qquad\text{(10)} These denominators are units near the limiting point. For the holomorphic function germ L(z)=Log⁡(1+z)z,L(0)=1,(11) L(z)=\frac{\operatorname{Log}(1+z)}z,\qquad L(0)=1, \qquad\text{(11)} use its local branch at zero.

The exact exponentiated original wire equation is exp⁡Fe,x=(qe2−a2)(qe2−b2)(1+a2)(1+b2)=1+γefe.(12) \begin{aligned} \exp F_{e,x} &=\frac{(q_e^2-a^2)(q_e^2-b^2)} {(1+a^2)(1+b^2)}\\ &=1+\gamma_e f_e. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(12)} For the first equality, differentiate (3), multiply the two incidence sine ratios, and use sin⁡2πx=1/(1+q2)\sin^2\pi x=1/(1+q^2). The elementary factor identity cos⁡2(tw/2)(qe+a)(qe−a)=qe2−a21+b2=1+fe1+b2(17) \cos^2(t_w/2)(q_e+a)(q_e-a) =\frac{q_e^2-a^2}{1+b^2} =1+\frac{f_e}{1+b^2} \qquad\text{(17)} proves r̂v,e−r̃v,e=12Log⁡(1+fe1+b2).(18) \widehat r_{v,e}-\widetilde r_{v,e} =\frac12\operatorname{Log}\left(1+\frac{f_e}{1+b^2}\right). \qquad\text{(18)} There is no additional logarithmic constant: both sides vanish on the continued stationary sheet. 3. Nonsingularity of the full Hessian

Theorem. At the supplied radial corner limit, the full holomorphic Hessian of (4) in all retained wire variables and selected angular variables is nonsingular. The conclusion includes every fold edge qe=0q_e=0.

Proof. First consider the Jacobian of (f,G)(f,G) in all independent (q,t)(q,t). On a nonfold edge ∂fe/∂qe=2qe≠0\partial f_e/\partial q_e=2q_e\ne0, so its constraint can be solved for the original local square root. Eliminating these constraints leaves exactly the lifted fold and primitive system of the pinned two-square theorem. That system has invertible Jacobian. The full constraint/primitive Jacobian is therefore invertible as well.

At a critical point, differentiation of (20) multiplies this Jacobian by the invertible triangular matrix in (21). Terms differentiating that matrix vanish because f=G=0f=G=0. Thus Hess⁡(q,t)Φ̃c\operatorname{Hess}_{(q,t)}\widetilde\Phi_c is invertible. The coordinate change (6) has invertible differential, and a critical Hessian transforms by congruence. This proves the assertion in the original (x,t)(x,t) coordinates. ▫\square

For an explicit local check, the wire-only negative second derivative is the familiar original expression −Fe,xx=2πqe(1+qe2)2(1+a2)(1+b2)when fe=0.(22) -F_{e,xx}= \frac{2\pi q_e(1+q_e^2)^2}{(1+a^2)(1+b^2)} \quad\text{when }f_e=0. \qquad\text{(22)} It vanishes at a fold. There, with the notation of (2) and A2+B2=1A^2+B^2=1, the mixed wire–angle row is instead Fe,x(tv,tw)=iAB(B,A)≠0.(23) F_{e,x(t_v,t_w)} =\frac{i}{AB}(B,A)\ne0. \qquad\text{(23)} Indeed dfe=iAB(Bdtv+Adtw)df_e=iAB(B\,dt_v+A\,dt_w), and (14) at the fold has coefficient 1/(A2B2)1/(A^2B^2). The same vector is the real constraint normal used in the lifted primitive proof. The positive edges forced beside each two-square edge control the complementary angle directions. This is why the joint system stays regular even though the wire-only Hessian vanishes.

More precisely, with the ordering in (21) and d=|E|+|S|d=|E|+|S|, at the critical point det⁡Hess⁡(q,t)Φ̃c=(−1)dπd∏ededet⁡D(q,t)(f,G).(24) \det\operatorname{Hess}_{(q,t)}\widetilde\Phi_c =\frac{(-1)^d}{\pi^d\prod_e d_e} \det D_{(q,t)}(f,G). \qquad\text{(24)} Passing to (x,t)(x,t) multiplies this determinant by ∏e{π(1+qe2)}2\prod_e\{\pi(1+q_e^2)\}^2, a nonzero factor. No lower bound uniform over unspecified radial limits is inferred from this local determinant identity.

The holomorphic Morse lemma consequently gives local coordinates ξ1,…,ξd\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_d, with nonzero Jacobian, in which Φc=Φc(x*,t*)−12∑j=1dξj2.(25) \Phi_c=\Phi_c(x_*,t_*)-\frac12\sum_{j=1}^d\xi_j^2. \qquad\text{(25)} Real ξ\xi give a strictly descending local real dd-disk. These coordinates generally mix wire and angle variables. They do not assert concavity on the original horizontal product contour. They also do not repair the singular operation of eliminating the fold wire as a function of arbitrary endpoint angles.

4. Relation to the exact factorial source

For N≥2N\ge2, D=N−1D=N-1, the actual halfwire interpolation is BN(x,t)=NGN(D/2−Nx+Nt/(2π))GN(D/2−Nx−Nt/(2π))GN(Nx)2.(26) B_N(x,t)=\frac{N}{ G_N(D/2-Nx+Nt/(2\pi)) G_N(D/2-Nx-Nt/(2\pi))G_N(Nx)^2}. \qquad\text{(26)} The exact divisor result used in the pinned product-contour theorem implies that BN(x,t)B_N(x,t) and BN(D/N−x,t)B_N(D/N-x,t) are holomorphic near every compact subset of −12N≤ℜx≤1−12N,|ℜt|<π,(27) -\frac1{2N}\le\Re x\le1-\frac1{2N},\qquad |\Re t|<\pi, \qquad\text{(27)} at arbitrary finite imaginary heights. This uses the canonical factorial itself, not an asymptotic continuation of Stirling’s formula.

Every stationary wire considered here has 0<ℜxe≤1/20<\Re x_e\le1/2, by (6)–(7). The selected angles have the strict margin (1), and outside angles equal zero. Since there are finitely many edges, a small fixed neighborhood of the target satisfies (27) for every N≥2N\ge2. For example, choose all wire real parts between a fixed positive number and a number strictly below 3/43/4; the latter is the upper endpoint of (27) at N=2N=2. Thus the exact halfwire factors have no pole in that neighborhood, including at x=1/2x=1/2.

Where the source is traced, the exact joint-lens form still contains ∏vsin⁡tve−iNtv(1−ηe−iNtv)2einvNtv2/(4π)cos⁡(N(1−cv)tv),η=(−1)N−1,(28) \prod_v \sin t_v\, \frac{e^{-iNt_v}}{(1-\eta e^{-iNt_v})^2}\, e^{i n_vNt_v^2/(4\pi)} \cos(N(1-c_v)t_v),\qquad \eta=(-1)^{N-1}, \qquad\text{(28)} with the actual factors N|E|∏v−N2CN,nvπi,CN,n=eπinD2/(4N),(29) N^{|E|}\prod_v\frac{-N^2 C_{N,n_v}}{\pi i}, \qquad C_{N,n}=e^{\pi i nD^2/(4N)}, \qquad\text{(29)} and every prescribed spectator. On selected lower angular neighborhoods Hv>0H_v>0, there is no physical trace pole. The primitive branch in (4) uses the charge-cvc_v exponential: it is the product of e−iNtve^{-iNt_v} in (28) and 12eiN(1−cv)tv\tfrac12 e^{iN(1-c_v)t_v} from the original cosine. The other cosine exponential and the denominator in (28) remain in the exact source. At cv=1c_v=1 the two cosine terms coincide and their coefficients add to one. No branch or current is deleted by the phase calculation.

The exact source equalities retain all first Hermite normals, the odd simple node, and the original closing functional. At a genuine free spectral boundary the compiler requires its ordinary cardinal polynomial representative instead of (26); its asymptotic phase has not been identified with (3). Likewise, a reversed-index kernel retains its different original phase. The Hessian theorem concerns the same-index phase (4) with its stated fixed outside angular parameters.

Holomorphy in (27) does not imply that (26) is nonzero, that one continued factorial asymptotic branch dominates there, or that the descending disk (25) has nonzero coefficient in the prescribed relative cycle. Exact factorial zeros, branch frequencies, connectors and all angular sectors must still be retained. The established gain is the removal of a local full-phase degeneracy: a vanishing wire-only Hessian at these admitted folds is not a caustic of the complete stationary system.
