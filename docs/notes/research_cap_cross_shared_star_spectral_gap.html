A full spectral-strip gap for an original cap and its whole CROSS star

31 October 2026. The cap varies in a small complex disk about +2; every neighboring CROSS spectrum may range over the entire real fold with a sufficiently thin fixed complex strip. This is a relative weighted-star theorem, not an absolute Phi_D bound. The face targets and both parity cutoffs are unchanged.

1. Original source and theorem

Let F=(u,A,v,C) be an original marked cap, with u=v=0 and A,C ordinary. Include EVERY other original A-incident port in a product of k genuine CROSS coupons. The valence k is fixed with the original diagram but otherwise arbitrary. Remove the fixed common raw signs and write

PN𝜹(A,Y)=∏ePe,CROSS(A,Y;σ(δe)),σ(δ)=−2cos⁡(2πδ),PN0(A,Y)=PN𝜹(A,Y)|𝜹=0. \begin{aligned} P_N^{\boldsymbol\delta}(A,Y)&=\prod_eP_{e,\mathrm{CROSS}}(A,Y;\sigma(\delta_e)),\\ \sigma(\delta)&=-2\cos(2\pi\delta),\\ P_N^0(A,Y)&=P_N^{\boldsymbol\delta}(A,Y)\big|_{\boldsymbol\delta=0}. \end{aligned}\tag{1}

The endpoint product has nonnegative coefficients at both parities. All shared variables remain identified. The actual finite CROSS degree projections are unchanged as the spectra vary.

Fix positive ordinary radii, protected variables exactly zero, with L_A=L_C. Write P_N+(a)=P_N0(a,L_Y). The actual endpoint weighted action has the locally uniform real limit

1Nlog⁡PN+(LCet)→F(t),F differentiable at 0,0<ν:=F′(0)<12. \frac1N\log P_N^+(L_Ce^t)\longrightarrow F(t),\qquad F\text{ differentiable at }0,\qquad0<\nu:=F'(0)<\frac12.\tag{2}

The derivative condition is the substantive scalar hypothesis. At a classified stationary cap with only CROSS neighbors it follows from the SAME original demand: L_A=L_C and F’(0)=sum_(e!=F) q_(e,A)=1-q_(F,A)=nu in (0,1/2).

Put d=N-1 and M=floor(N/2)-1. For cap spectrum sigma the original projected polynomial and the extracted star are

HF,cap(A,C;σ)=∑j=0M(−1)jbj(σ)Ad−jCj,BN(Y;σ,𝜹)=[Ad]HF,capPN𝜹. \begin{aligned} H_{F,\mathrm{cap}}(A,C;\sigma)&=\sum_{j=0}^M(-1)^jb_j(\sigma)A^{d-j}C^j,\\ B_N(Y;\sigma,\boldsymbol\delta)&=[A^d]H_{F,\mathrm{cap}}P_N^{\boldsymbol\delta}. \end{aligned}\tag{3}

Theorem. There are fixed epsilon_>0, h_>0, eta>0 and N_0 such that

sup|σ−2|≤ε*,|Im⁡δe|≤h*Re⁡δe∈ℝ,|Yf|=Lf|BN(Y;σ,𝜹)|≤e−ηNPN+(LC),N≥N0. \sup_{\substack{|\sigma-2|\le\varepsilon_*\,,\ |\operatorname{Im}\delta_e|\le h_*\\ \operatorname{Re}\delta_e\in\mathbb R\,,\ |Y_f|=L_f}} |B_N(Y;\sigma,\boldsymbol\delta)| \le e^{-\eta N}P_N^+(L_C),\qquad N\ge N_0.\tag{4}

Both parities and every remaining face phase are included. This extracts exactly the original A^d against the WHOLE neighboring product; it is not a shifted demand or an independent allocation at each neighbor.

2. Root-safe cap factors and the exact global CROSS majorant

Set theta=pi/N and

cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)θ),sj=2sin⁡(jθ),Bj=∏r=1jsr2,bj(σ)=Bj∏r=j+1d(σ−cr),Uj=bj(2),γj=2−cj>0. \begin{aligned} c_j&=2\cos((2j+1)\theta),&s_j&=2\sin(j\theta),\\ B_j&=\prod_{r=1}^js_r^2,&b_j(\sigma)&=B_j\prod_{r=j+1}^d(\sigma-c_r),\\ U_j&=b_j(2),&\gamma_j&=2-c_j>0. \end{aligned}

These are the full original unused-row products, also at repeated spectral roots. They satisfy the polynomial relation

(σ−cj+1)bj+1(σ)=sj+12bj(σ). (\sigma-c_{j+1})b_{j+1}(\sigma)=s_{j+1}^2b_j(\sigma).\tag{5}

For j<=M, the explicit increasing-sine ratios give U_j<=U_0=csc^2(theta/2)<=C N^2. Since product gamma_j=4 and chi_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2, for |sigma-2|<=epsilon_s,

|bj(σ)|≤Uj∏r>j(1+εsγr)≤CN2eNκs,κs=arcosh⁡(1+εs/2). |b_j(\sigma)|\le U_j\prod_{r>j}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_s}{\gamma_r}\right) \le CN^2e^{N\kappa_s},\qquad \kappa_s=\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\varepsilon_s/2).\tag{6}

There is no division by b_j, chi_N, or a varying spectral factor. This polynomial estimate is used near every endpoint or sampled zero outside the strict row interval below.

The original cofactor interpolation theorem supplies the stronger, GLOBAL coefficientwise CROSS bound, on any fixed strip |Im delta|<=T,

|Pe,CROSS(σ(δ))|coeff≤CTlog⁡2(N+1)e2πd|Im⁡δ|Pe,CROSS(−2). |P_{e,\mathrm{CROSS}}(\sigma(\delta))|_{\mathrm{coeff}} \le C_T\log^2(N+1)e^{2\pi d|\operatorname{Im}\delta|} P_{e,\mathrm{CROSS}}(-2).\tag{7}

This is equation (27) of the original cofactor factorization, section 8, not an inferred coefficient lower asymptotic. Its nodal positivity covers ALL j,a<=J: for j<=a it is the direct positive original recurrence; for j>a it is the retained physical tail adapter. Thus the plain coefficient majorant includes j=J-1 at all nodes. Its central coefficientwise comparisons use (x+y)^2>=2xy, with the original forced outer units retained.

Taking products in (7), even when face variables are shared, yields

|PN𝜹|coeff≤QNexp⁡(2πd∑e|Imδe|)PN0. |P_N^{\boldsymbol\delta}|_{\mathrm{coeff}} \le Q_N\exp\!\left(2\pi d\sum_e|\operatorname{Im}\delta_e|\right)P_N^0.\tag{8}

where Q_N is a fixed polynomial upper bound in N on a fixed strip. For later rate estimates put

ω=κs+2π∑e|Im⁡δe|. \omega=\kappa_s+2\pi\sum_e|\operatorname{Im}\delta_e|.\tag{9}

3. Actual MAIN-edge angular loss on the whole neighbor fold

The triangle on the opposite side of original MAIN edge AC from (u,A,C) belongs to a different port in P_N^delta. It contains the two supported positive variables A,C. Fix phi_0 in (0,pi/8) and a small radial interval |t|<=T_0. The cofactor angular theorem gives gamma>0 and a FIXED strip height h_0>0 such that

|PN𝜹(Cet+iϕ,Y)|≤CNKexp⁡(2πd∑e|Imδe|−γN)PN+(LCet),ϕ0≤|ϕ|≤π,|t|≤T0,|Im⁡δe|≤h0,Re⁡δe∈ℝ,|Yf|=Lf. \begin{aligned} |P_N^{\boldsymbol\delta}(Ce^{t+i\phi},Y)| &\le CN^K\exp\!\left(2\pi d\sum_e|\operatorname{Im}\delta_e|-\gamma N\right)P_N^+(L_Ce^t),\\ \phi_0\le|\phi|\le\pi,\quad |t|&\le T_0,\quad |\operatorname{Im}\delta_e|\le h_0, \quad\operatorname{Re}\delta_e\in\mathbb R,\quad |Y_f|=L_f. \end{aligned}\tag{10}

Here C on the left is the actual complex face value. Thus A and C have precisely the stated phase difference regardless of other face phases. No phase is assigned to a protected zero.

To verify (10) against the actual source, put u=-4sin^2(pi delta). At even N=2m the central formula is

sm2(x+y)DbDc+smsm−1xy(DbEc+EbDc),Db=Rm−1(b;u),Eb=(am−1+u)Rm−2(b;u). \begin{gathered} s_m^2(x+y)D_bD_c+s_ms_{m-1}xy(D_bE_c+E_bD_c),\\ D_b=R_{m-1}(b;u),\qquad E_b=(a_{m-1}+u)R_{m-2}(b;u). \end{gathered}

At odd N=2m+1 it is

GbGc+usmsm+1xyDbDc,Gb=Rm(b;u),Db=Rm−1(b;u). G_bG_c+u s_ms_{m+1}xyD_bD_c,\qquad G_b=R_m(b;u),\quad D_b=R_{m-1}(b;u).\tag{11}

The skipped-central odd term is retained. Every central summand has a cofactor on the actual AC side, at index J or J-1. On that side the actual angular theorem gives C N^K exp(-gamma N)R_j(positive;0), uniformly on the entire real fold and its fixed thin strip. The J-1 case includes the theorem’s omitted-node extra-factor/division argument and its polynomial cost; no logarithmic angular prefactor is being claimed. On the other side apply the exact coefficient interpolation/Bernstein bound.

The remaining central factors are bounded on that strip. At positive radii use, at even levels, D_b(0)>=s_(m-1)^2(x+y)R_(m-2)(b;0) and (x+y)^2>=2xy; at odd levels use G_b(0)>=s_m^2(x+y)D_b(0) and G_b(0)G_c(0)>=2s_m^4 xyD_b(0)D_c(0). These compare every central term to the actual positive endpoint kernel without division by a small diagonal or central zero. This proves (10); the other star ports use (7). Shrinking the strip later preserves all these fixed constants.

4. Total-degree tails and fixed choices of radii

Choose delta_j>0 such that

0<ν−2δj<ν+2δj<12,l=⌈N(ν−δj)⌉,r=⌊N(ν+δj)⌋. 0<\nu-2\delta_j<\nu+2\delta_j<\frac12,\qquad l=\lceil N(\nu-\delta_j)\rceil,\quad r=\lfloor N(\nu+\delta_j)\rfloor.

For sufficiently large N, 0<l<=r<r+1<=M. Differentiability in (2) gives fixed t_-<0<t_+, as small as desired, and beta>0 with

F(t−)−(ν−δj)t−≤F(0)−β,F(t+)−(ν+δj)t+≤F(0)−β. \begin{aligned} F(t_-)-(\nu-\delta_j)t_-&\le F(0)-\beta,\\ F(t_+)-(\nu+\delta_j)t_+&\le F(0)-\beta. \end{aligned}\tag{12}

Choose them also within (-T_0,T_0), and so small that

F(t)−vt≤F(0)+γ/4, F(t)-vt\le F(0)+\gamma/4,

for t between 0 and either chosen radius, and v in {nu-delta_j,nu+delta_j}. Continuity gives this extra condition. The integer rounding of l/N and (r+1)/N costs only O(1) in logarithms.

Write P_N^delta=sum_j p_jdelta(Y)Aj. Formula (3) gives the exact total-degree split j<l, l<=j<=r, and j>r, all with j<=M. Positivity of P_N^0, (6),(8), and the two Chernoff radii show that the exterior parts have bound

|∑0≤j≤Mj<lorj>r(−1)jbj(σ)Cjpj𝜹(Y)|≤poly⁡(N)eN(F(0)−β+ω+o(1)). \left|\sum_{\substack{0\le j\le M\\j<l\ \mathrm{or}\ j>r}} (-1)^jb_j(\sigma)C^jp_j^{\boldsymbol\delta}(Y)\right| \le\operatorname{poly}(N)e^{N(F(0)-\beta+\omega+o(1))}.\tag{13}

For example, the lower positive tail is bounded by exp(-(l-1)t_-)P_N^+(L_C exp(t_-)); the upper one by exp(-(r+1)t_+)P_N^+(L_C exp(t_+)). No individual neighbor A allocation is restricted. Endpoint or zero spectral rows are controlled by (6), not by solving a divided recurrence there.

5. An exact finite analytic antidifference on the interior rows

Put h=1/N. For a complex log-radius zeta set

wj(ζ)=(−1)jbj(σ)e−jζ,aN(t,ζ,σ)=−e−ζ4sin⁡2(π(t+h))σ−2cos⁡(2π(t+3h/2)). \begin{aligned} w_j(\zeta)&=(-1)^jb_j(\sigma)e^{-j\zeta},\\ a_N(t,\zeta,\sigma)&=-e^{-\zeta} \frac{4\sin^2(\pi(t+h))}{\sigma-2\cos(2\pi(t+3h/2))}. \end{aligned}\tag{14}

Then w_(j+1)=a_N(j/N)w_j EXACTLY by (5). On a fixed complex tube about the strict row interval and a fixed rectangle |Re zeta|<T_1, |Im zeta|<2phi_0 with T_1>T_0, the denominator stays away from zero for sigma in a sufficiently small complex disk about 2. At sigma=2, a_N converges uniformly there to -exp(-zeta). After shrinking these fixed neighborhoods if necessary, a_N-1 has a uniformly bounded analytic inverse. The original b_j can still be zero; it is never divided out. All divisions in this construction are confined to the stated bulk interval, where both the denominator in (14) and a_N-1 are uniformly nonzero.

Here is the finite construction, including its error. Put

KN=(aN−1)−1,(ℱhH)(t)=−aN(t)aN(t)−1[H(t+h)−H(t)],HN=∑q=0mN−1ℱhqKN. \begin{aligned} K_N&=(a_N-1)^{-1},\\ (\mathcal F_hH)(t)&=-\frac{a_N(t)}{a_N(t)-1}\,[H(t+h)-H(t)],\\ H_N&=\sum_{q=0}^{m_N-1}\mathcal F_h^qK_N. \end{aligned}\tag{15}

Let rho be the row-tube radius, K a uniform bound on |a_N/(a_N-1)|, and K_0 a uniform bound on |K_N| on the larger tube and fixed parameter rectangle. Choose

mN=⌊ρN8(K+1)⌋≥1. m_N=\left\lfloor\frac{\rho N}{8(K+1)}\right\rfloor\ge1.

Use m_N nested tubes from radius rho to rho/2. Cauchy’s derivative bound makes each forward difference cost at most 2h/s, with s=rho/(2m_N), so each F_h costs at most 4Khm_N/rho<=1/2. All literal shifts stay in the preceding tube. The finite geometric identity now gives

aN(t)HN(t+h)−HN(t)=1−eN(t),eN(t)=(aN(t)−1)ℱhmNKN(t),|HN|≤2K0,|eN|≤Ce−cN. \begin{aligned} a_N(t)H_N(t+h)-H_N(t)&=1-e_N(t),\\ e_N(t)&=(a_N(t)-1)\mathcal F_h^{m_N}K_N(t),\\ |H_N|&\le2K_0,\qquad |e_N|\le Ce^{-cN}. \end{aligned}\tag{16}

All functions are analytic in zeta and sigma on the fixed parameter rectangle; the bounds are uniform. This constructs an exact finite antidifference, not an asymptotic solution for a discrete coefficient.

Consequently the original window kernel satisfies EXACTLY

∑j=lrwj=wr+1HN((r+1)/N)−wlHN(l/N)+∑j=lreN(j/N)wj. \sum_{j=l}^rw_j =w_{r+1}H_N((r+1)/N)-w_lH_N(l/N) +\sum_{j=l}^re_N(j/N)w_j.\tag{17}

BOTH endpoint terms remain. Each w_j retains the full b_j unused-row product, including every common sampled zero.

6. Exact A contour and both radial side currents

The actual window coefficient is

12π∫−ππPN𝜹(Ceiϕ,Y)∑j=lrwj(iϕ)dϕ. \frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} P_N^{\boldsymbol\delta}(Ce^{i\phi},Y) \sum_{j=l}^rw_j(i\phi)\,d\phi.\tag{18}

This is a literal coefficient contour, not a finite grid. No alias or bound on the total degree of P_N relative to N is needed.

For |phi|>=phi_0, (10) and (6) give rate at most F(0)+omega-gamma. For |phi|<=phi_0, insert (17). The residual contributes at most

poly⁡(N)eN(F(0)+ω−c+o(1)). \operatorname{poly}(N)e^{N(F(0)+\omega-c+o(1))}.\tag{19}

by (6),(8). No analytic interpolation of p_j has been used.

Consider separately the boundary terms j=l and j=r+1. Apart from the actual scalar (-1)^j b_j(sigma), their integrand is

Wj(ζ)=PN𝜹(Ceζ,Y)e−jζHN(j/N;ζ,σ). W_j(\zeta)=P_N^{\boldsymbol\delta}(Ce^\zeta,Y) e^{-j\zeta}H_N(j/N;\zeta,\sigma).

It is holomorphic on the small rectangle of (14)-(16). Deform the segment zeta=i[-phi_0,phi_0] to Re zeta=t_- for j=l, and to Re zeta=t_+ for j=r+1. The exact contour identity retains BOTH joining segments, at Im zeta=+phi_0 and Im zeta=-phi_0.

On the shifted vertical segment, bounded H_N, (6),(8),(12) give rate at most F(0)+omega-beta. On either joining segment, the actual A/C phase difference is exactly +/-phi_0, so (10) applies. The small-radius choice after (12) bounds the rate there by

F(0)+ω−3γ/4. F(0)+\omega-3\gamma/4.\tag{20}

Thus BOTH endpoint terms have a strict rate improvement, and both original radial currents have been controlled, not discarded. All bounds are uniform in the other face phases.

7. Closing the fixed spectral neighborhoods

The row interval, angular threshold, radii and positive losses beta,gamma,c were fixed using the endpoint profile before the final spectral neighborhoods were chosen. Take epsilon_* within the initial cap disk, and h_* within the initial CROSS strip, such that

arcosh⁡(1+ε*/2)+2πkh*<14min⁡{β,γ/2,c}. \operatorname{arcosh}(1+\varepsilon_*/2)+2\pi k h_* <\frac14\min\{\beta,\gamma/2,c\}.\tag{21}

All these numbers are fixed before N tends to infinity. Equations (13),(18)-(20) give a uniform upper rate strictly below F(0). Since log P_N^+(L_C)=NF(0)+o(N), reducing the gap once more proves the actual finite comparison (4) at all sufficiently large levels.

This includes arbitrary real parts of every CROSS fold parameter. No cap moment sequence at a varied spectrum, unknown degree interpolation, or finite-order adiabatic extrapolation has entered. The finite high-order construction (15)-(17) supplies the stated exponential residual, and all source cutoff endpoints remain.

8. Exterior attachment and precise remaining scope

As the whole A-star was included, the unchanged exterior G_N has no A incidence. For the remaining original target monomial Y^t, (4) and Cauchy give

|[Y𝒕]BN(Y;σ,𝜹)GN(Y)|≤e−ηNPN+(LC)sup|Yf|=Lf|GN(Y)|∏fLftf. \big|[Y^{\mathbf t}]B_N(Y;\sigma,\boldsymbol\delta)G_N(Y)\big| \le e^{-\eta N}P_N^+(L_C) \frac{\sup_{|Y_f|=L_f}|G_N(Y)|}{\prod_fL_f^{t_f}}.\tag{22}

Here ordinary t_f=d and protected exponents zero are unchanged. Boundary variables may be shared arbitrarily. All raw source scalar signs are fixed unit factors; there is no new N normalization.

This is a genuine spectral-neighborhood RELATIVE star improvement. Its displayed weighted star/exterior envelope is not generally known to be at most exp(N Phi_D). The hypothesis that every other A-incident port is CROSS also remains explicit. Consequently no universal cap elimination, full source gap, or volume theorem is inferred.

9. A shorter fixed-spectrum proof using only finite positive moments

At the fixed spectra cap +2 and all neighbors -2, there is a separate proof of the relative star gap that avoids analytic antidifferencing. It uses the exact positive cap-moment law, not its disproved squared-norm strengthening. Put

PN0(A,Y)=∑j≥0pN,j(Y)Aj,RN(x;Y)=∑j=0MpN,j(Y)(−Cx)j. P_N^0(A,Y)=\sum_{j\ge0}p_{N,j}(Y)A^j,\qquad R_N(x;Y)=\sum_{j=0}^Mp_{N,j}(Y)(-Cx)^j.

There is a positive random variable XNX_N with

𝔼XNj=Uj/U0(0≤j≤d),BN(Y;2,0)=U0𝔼RN(XN;Y). \mathbb E X_N^j=U_j/U_0\quad(0\le j\le d),\qquad B_N(Y;2,0)=U_0\mathbb E R_N(X_N;Y).\tag{23}

Its support is unbounded. Only the displayed finite moments are used. The original full sine products give Bd=N2B_d=N^2 and ∏r=0dγr=4\prod_{r=0}^d\gamma_r=4, hence Ud=N2U_d=N^2 at both parities. Thus

md:=𝔼XNd=N2sin⁡2π2N≤π24,U0≤CN2. m_d:=\mathbb E X_N^d=N^2\sin^2\!\frac{\pi}{2N} \le\frac{\pi^2}{4},\qquad U_0\le CN^2.\tag{24}

Fix a small δ0>0\delta_0>0. The actual AC-cofactor angular theorem at phase difference π\pi gives constants C0,K,γ>0C_0,K,\gamma>0 with

|PN0(−Cx,Y)|≤C0NKe−γNPN+(LCx),1−δ0≤x≤1+δ0,|Yf|=Lf. |P_N^0(-Cx,Y)|\le C_0N^Ke^{-\gamma N}P_N^+(L_Cx), \quad 1-\delta_0\le x\le1+\delta_0,\quad |Y_f|=L_f.

The central formulas (11) retain every term at both parities; the ordinary AC variables are supported in one actual triangle. This bound is therefore uniform in every other face phase.

Choose 0<δ<δ00<\delta<\delta_0 and R>1+δR>1+\delta. Split the expectation in (23) into the lower, middle and upper ranges separated by 1−δ1-\delta and 1+δ1+\delta. In the lower range, positivity of the endpoint product gives |RN(x;Y)|≤PN+(LC(1−δ))|R_N(x;Y)|\le P_N^+(L_C(1-\delta)). In the upper range the TRUE total cap cutoff gives

|RN(x;Y)|≤xMPN+(LC),𝔼[XNM𝟏XN≥1+δ]≤(1+δ)−(d−M)md. |R_N(x;Y)|\le x^M P_N^+(L_C),\qquad \mathbb E\!\left[X_N^M\mathbf1_{X_N\ge1+\delta}\right] \le(1+\delta)^{-(d-M)}m_d.

On the bounded middle range, use the finite identity RN(x;Y)=PN0(−Cx,Y)−∑j>MpN,j(Y)(−Cx)jR_N(x;Y)=P_N^0(-Cx,Y)-\sum_{j>M}p_{N,j}(Y)(-Cx)^j. Its first term has the angular gap. The second has the finite Chernoff bound (x/R)M+1PN+(LCR)(x/R)^{M+1}P_N^+(L_CR). The full product is evaluated only on this bounded interval: neither its higher powers nor its square is integrated against the moment law. Combining the three ranges gives

sup|Yf|=Lf|BN(Y;2,0)|≤U0[PN+(LC(1−δ))+md(1+δ)−(d−M)PN+(LC)+C0NKe−γNPN+(LC(1+δ))+(1+δR)M+1PN+(LCR)]. \begin{aligned} \sup_{|Y_f|=L_f}|B_N(Y;2,0)|\le U_0\bigg[&P_N^+(L_C(1-\delta))\\ &+m_d(1+\delta)^{-(d-M)}P_N^+(L_C)\\ &+C_0N^Ke^{-\gamma N}P_N^+(L_C(1+\delta))\\ &+\left(\frac{1+\delta}{R}\right)^{M+1}P_N^+(L_CR)\bigg]. \end{aligned}\tag{25}

This is an explicit finite inequality for the actual positive star values; no individual neighbor A allocation is made. Let F0=F(0)F_0=F(0). Choose t>0t>0 with F(t)−F0<t/2F(t)-F_0<t/2, set R=etR=e^t, and then take δ>0\delta>0 small enough that all four deficits are positive:

η1=F0−F(log⁡(1−δ)),η2=12log⁡(1+δ),η3=γ−F(log⁡(1+δ))+F0,η4=12(t−log⁡(1+δ))−F(t)+F0. \begin{aligned} \eta_1&=F_0-F(\log(1-\delta)),\\ \eta_2&=\tfrac12\log(1+\delta),\\ \eta_3&=\gamma-F(\log(1+\delta))+F_0,\\ \eta_4&=\tfrac12(t-\log(1+\delta))-F(t)+F_0. \end{aligned}\tag{26}

The first uses F′(0)>0F'(0)>0; the fourth uses F′(0)<1/2F'(0)<1/2 with the radius tt fixed first. Continuity gives the third. At even levels d−M=M+1=N/2d-M=M+1=N/2; at odd levels these quantities are (N+1)/2(N+1)/2 and (N−1)/2(N-1)/2. The bounded offsets, (24), and the endpoint weighted limit therefore turn (25) into a strict exponential gap relative to the actual PN+(LC)P_N^+(L_C).

This proof does not extend the positive moment sequence to a varying cap spectrum. Sections 2–7 provide that separate analytic extension. The fixed-spectrum argument also does not supply a coefficientwise comparison with the paired endpoint reference: it is the same weighted, phase-uniform star estimate as the main theorem.
