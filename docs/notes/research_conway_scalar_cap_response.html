Separating the closing functional in the convex Conway energy

21 November 2026. The final closing functional in the normalized Conway energy has complex rank one. This note separates it by an exact scalar dual problem. The scalar response is a contraction, and its first nonlinear correction has a bound independent of the quantum level and the size of the expression tree. Keeping this rank-one term in the quadratic minorant also improves the explicit lower certificate.

The internal minimization still retains all physical boundary packets. None of the formulas below evaluates their geometric asymptotic rate.

1. The original closing row and the residual energy

Use the complete boundary spaces, fixed local budgets, and normalized maps of the convex Conway source. Write the product of unit balls as 𝒟\mathcal D, the true normalized field as aa, and the full residual map as GG. Put R(x)=∥G(x)∥2,cx=Lxv0,E(x)=R(x)+|cx|2,k=ca=KN/Btot.(1) R(x)=\|G(x)\|^2,\qquad cx=Lx_{v_0},\qquad E(x)=R(x)+|cx|^2,\qquad k=ca=K_N/B_{\mathrm{ tot}}. \qquad\text{(1)} Here v0v_0 is the proper vertex immediately below the closing root, and LL is the original marked closing row divided by its recorded local budget. The full scalar BtotB_{\mathrm{ tot}} is unchanged. In particular, a framing phase or recorded power of NN in the physical closing row stays in (1).

Set σ=1/16\sigma=1/16 and ℓ=∥L∥≤σ\ell=\|L\|\le\sigma. The coefficient norms are the Euclidean norms in the complete Newton basis. Repeated-root derivatives and both original closing directions remain present. All gradients and Hessians below are real, for the inner product Re⁡∑vxv*yv\operatorname{Re}\sum_vx_v^*y_v. Thus a scalar complex coordinate has two real coordinates. The complex-linear row cc satisfies cc*=ℓ2I2cc^*=\ell^2I_2 in these real coordinates.

We use the following estimates, proved in the complete source theorem: G(a)=0,∥av∥≤σ,|k|≤σ2,I≤D2R≤3I,I≤D2E≤3Ion 𝒟.(2) G(a)=0,\quad \|a_v\|\le\sigma,\quad |k|\le\sigma^2, \qquad I\le D^2R\le3I,\qquad I\le D^2E\le3I \quad\text{on }\mathcal D. \qquad\text{(2)} The stated estimate for RR follows from that theorem’s residual Hessian proof before adding the nonnegative closing Hessian. If e*=min⁡𝒟Ee_* =\min_{\mathcal D}E, the original source comparison is Btot2e*≤|KN|2≤γBtot2e*,γ=197/196.(3) B_{\mathrm{ tot}}^2e_*\le |K_N|^2 \le\gamma B_{\mathrm{ tot}}^2e_*,\qquad \gamma=197/196. \qquad\text{(3)}

2. An exact scalar dual problem

For λ∈ℂ\lambda\in\mathbb C, define h(λ)=minx∈𝒟{R(x)+2Re⁡(λ‾cx)},q(λ)=h(λ)−|λ|2.(4) h(\lambda)=\min_{x\in\mathcal D} \{R(x)+2\operatorname{Re}(\bar\lambda cx)\},\qquad q(\lambda)=h(\lambda)-|\lambda|^2. \qquad\text{(4)} This definition uses only the known local maps and the prescribed closing row. It does not take KNK_N or the true field aa as an input.

Theorem 1. The exact minimum is e*=maxλ∈ℂq(λ).(5) \boxed{\quad e_* =\max_{\lambda\in\mathbb C}q(\lambda). \quad} \qquad\text{(5)} The maximum is unique. It lies in |λ|≤σ|\lambda|\le\sigma, where the inner minimum has a unique interior minimizer x(λ)x(\lambda). Writing s(λ)=cx(λ)s(\lambda)=cx(\lambda), the maximizer is characterized by λ*=s(λ*),∥Ds(λ)∥≤2ℓ2≤1/128.(6) \lambda_*=s(\lambda_*),\qquad \|Ds(\lambda)\|\le2\ell^2\le1/128. \qquad\text{(6)} Thus the iteration λj+1=s(λj)\lambda_{j+1}=s(\lambda_j) converges to λ*\lambda_* from any point in this disk.

Proof. For any x,λx,\lambda, |cx|2−2Re⁡(λ‾cx)+|λ|2=|cx−λ|2≥0. |cx|^2-2\operatorname{Re}(\bar\lambda cx)+|\lambda|^2 =|cx-\lambda|^2\ge0. Consequently q(λ)≤e*q(\lambda)\le e_*. Let x*x_* be the unique interior minimizer of EE, whose existence and stationarity were proved in the source theorem. Put λ*=cx*\lambda_*=cx_*, so |λ*|≤ℓ≤σ|\lambda_*|\le\ell\le\sigma. The stationary equations give ∇R(x*)+2c*λ*=0. \nabla R(x_*)+2c^*\lambda_*=0. Since the objective in the inner problem is strongly convex, this equation makes x*x_* its unique minimum at λ*\lambda_*. The displayed square vanishes there, proving (5).

For |λ|≤σ|\lambda|\le\sigma, the inner half-gradient map has, at a nontop vertex vv, the same component as the residual energy: Φv−(DvΦparent⁡(v))*ℛparent⁡(v). \Phi_v-(D_v\Phi_{\operatorname{parent}(v)})^* \mathcal R_{\operatorname{parent}(v)}. Its norm is at most σ+σ(1+σ)=33/256\sigma+\sigma(1+\sigma)=33/256. The top component is Φv0−L*λ\Phi_{v_0}-L^*\lambda, with norm at most σ+σ2=17/256\sigma+\sigma^2=17/256. The Hessian is D2RD^2R, so this map is a contraction with factor 1/21/2. It maps 𝒟\mathcal D strictly inside itself. Its unique fixed point is the unique interior inner minimum. The implicit function theorem therefore gives a smooth real response x(λ)x(\lambda) on a neighborhood of the closed disk.

Differentiating its stationary equation gives Dx(λ)=−2H(λ)−1c*,Ds(λ)=−2cH(λ)−1c*,H(λ)=D2R(x(λ)).(7) Dx(\lambda)=-2H(\lambda)^{-1}c^*,\qquad Ds(\lambda)=-2cH(\lambda)^{-1}c^*,\qquad H(\lambda)=D^2R(x(\lambda)). \qquad\text{(7)} Equation (2) implies (6). Also |s(λ)|≤σ(σ+σ2)<σ|s(\lambda)|\le\sigma(\sigma+\sigma^2)<\sigma, because the top fixed-point equation has that same component bound. The disk is therefore preserved by the scalar contraction.

The function hh is concave on the whole scalar plane, being an infimum of affine functions of λ\lambda. Hence subtracting |λ|2|\lambda|^2 makes qq strictly concave, so its global maximum is unique. On the disk, ∇q=2(s−λ),−(2+4ℓ2)I2≤D2q≤−2I2.(8) \nabla q=2(s-\lambda),\qquad -(2+4\ell^2)I_2\le D^2q\le-2I_2. \qquad\text{(8)} This proves the remaining assertions. ▫\square

The reduction is scalar at the outside. Each evaluation of ss still solves a problem involving the actual internal packets. It has not converted their coherent value into a known local norm.

3. Positive response and its first nonlinear correction

Since x(0)=ax(0)=a, integrating (7) along the scalar segment gives s(λ)=k−𝖡(λ)λ,𝖡(λ)=2∫01cH(tλ)−1c*dt.(9) s(\lambda)=k-\mathsf B(\lambda)\lambda,\qquad \mathsf B(\lambda)=2\int_0^1 cH(t\lambda)^{-1}c^*\,dt. \qquad\text{(9)} This is a real 2×22\times2 response matrix, with 23ℓ2I2≤𝖡(λ)≤2ℓ2I2.(10) \tfrac23\ell^2I_2\le\mathsf B(\lambda)\le2\ell^2I_2. \qquad\text{(10)} In particular k=(I2+𝖡(λ*))λ*k=(I_2+\mathsf B(\lambda_*))\lambda_*. The response correction is positive and close to the identity. It is not asserted to be a complex-holomorphic scalar function.

There is a uniform expansion at the exact residual field. Set T=DG(a),ν=∥cT−1∥2≤1/196.(11) T=DG(a),\qquad \nu=\|cT^{-1}\|^2\le1/196. \qquad\text{(11)} The inverse exists because T=−I+A(a)T=-I+A(a), with ∥A(a)∥≤1/8\|A(a)\|\le1/8. Theorem 2. With the normalizations above, |e*−|k|21+ν|≤|k|34096,|λ*−k1+ν|≤|k|24096.(12) \left|e_*-\frac{|k|^2}{1+\nu}\right| \le\frac{|k|^3}{4096},\qquad \left|\lambda_*-\frac{k}{1+\nu}\right| \le\frac{|k|^2}{4096}. \qquad\text{(12)} The constants are independent of NN, the expression tree, and its fixed filling coefficients after the recorded budgets are retained.

Proof. The full residual is quadratic. For a displacement ee, G(a+e)=Te+Q(e),∥Q(e)∥≤σ2∥e∥2,∥DQ(e)∥≤σ∥e∥.(13) G(a+e)=Te+Q(e),\qquad \|Q(e)\|\le\tfrac\sigma2\|e\|^2,\qquad \|DQ(e)\|\le\sigma\|e\|. \qquad\text{(13)} Indeed Qv(e)=Φv(eL,eR)Q_v(e)=\Phi_v(e_L,e_R) at a product and is zero at other vertices. The quadratic model is E0(e)=∥Te∥2+|k+ce|2. E_0(e)=\|Te\|^2+|k+ce|^2. 4. A stronger explicit lower certificate

The closing Hessian can also be kept exactly in the quadratic minorant. For g=∇E(x)g=\nabla E(x), define Dcap(x)=E(x)−12∥g∥2+|cg|21+2ℓ2.(17) \boxed{\quad D_{\mathrm{ cap}}(x)=E(x)-\tfrac12\|g\|^2 +\frac{|cg|^2}{1+2\ell^2}. \quad} \qquad\text{(17)} This is a real polynomial of degree at most six. Its coefficients use only the prescribed source maps and closing row.

Since D2E≥I+2c*cD^2E\ge I+2c^*c, strong convexity with this constant matrix gives the lower bound Dcap(x)≤e*≤E(x).(18) D_{\mathrm{ cap}}(x)\le e_*\le E(x). \qquad\text{(18)} Indeed the unconstrained minimum of the corresponding quadratic minorant is E−12⟨g,(I+2c*c)−1g⟩E-\tfrac12\langle g,(I+2c^*c)^{-1}g\rangle. The identity (I+2c*c)−1=I−2c*c1+2ℓ2 (I+2c^*c)^{-1}=I-\frac{2c^*c}{1+2\ell^2} gives (17). At the interior minimizing field g=0g=0, so maxx∈𝒟Dcap(x)=e*.(19) \max_{x\in\mathcal D}D_{\mathrm{ cap}}(x)=e_*. \qquad\text{(19)} No concavity of this polynomial is asserted. It improves the earlier certificate by the nonnegative term |cg|2/(1+2ℓ2)|cg|^2/(1+2\ell^2).

Equivalently, apply the quadratic minorant to the inner problem in (4) at a trial field xx, then maximize its explicit scalar lower bound. With b=∇R(x)b=\nabla R(x) and s=cxs=cx, that maximum is attained at λtrial=s−cb1+2ℓ2, \lambda_{\mathrm{ trial}}=\frac{s-cb}{1+2\ell^2}, and its value is R(x)−12∥b∥2+|s−cb|21+2ℓ2=Dcap(x).(20) R(x)-\tfrac12\|b\|^2 +\frac{|s-cb|^2}{1+2\ell^2}=D_{\mathrm{ cap}}(x). \qquad\text{(20)} This calculation is a lower bound even when the displayed trial scalar lies outside the disk used for the smooth response theorem.

Combining (3) and (18), every feasible trial field gives Btot2max⁡(0,Dcap(x))≤|KN|2≤γBtot2E(x).(21) B_{\mathrm{ tot}}^2\max(0,D_{\mathrm{ cap}}(x)) \le |K_N|^2\le\gamma B_{\mathrm{ tot}}^2 E(x). \qquad\text{(21)} This is an explicit lower witness for the original marked source. A positive witness at the hyperbolic volume rate is still required; the scalar reduction does not establish it.
