A volume theorem with a twist bound independent of the number of tangles

A knot in this theorem is built from a finite ordered tree of integer two-strand braids. Turn each child tangle through a quarter-turn before stacking it on its specified side of the parent braid. Close at a terminal vertex by the prescribed cylindrical closure. Each vertex, including each terminal vertex, has its own crossing circle in the augmented diagram. The construction and strand test fix the endpoint orders and require that all the main strands form one knot, with no additional component closed inside a tangle.

The number of vertices, their degrees, and the nesting depth may be arbitrary. We prove a sufficient condition at each vertex that does not depend on any of those global quantities. Write dvd_v for the number of incident tree edges and nvn_v for the signed number of half-twists in its braid. If maxvdvdv2+nv2≤2−120, \max_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2}\le2^{-120}, then the original knot complement is hyperbolic and limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K)|=Vol⁡(S3\K). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K)| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K). In particular, requiring every |nv|≥2119|n_v|\ge2^{119} suffices for every finite tree in this construction. This very coarse constant is a sufficient bound, not an estimate of the shortest twists for which the equality holds. The tree and all powers are fixed before NN tends to infinity.

The proof evaluates a subtree while leaving its attaching edge unevaluated. The result is a polynomial in the variable at the parent vertex. Dividing by its value at zero factors out the contribution already supplied by that subtree. When the next crossing trace is taken, its error can therefore be compared with the change at that one vertex. The proof below establishes the nonvanishing needed for this division and bounds the entire finite polynomial, including its high indices and repeated-root derivatives.

The earlier summed criterion remains useful and has a much better numerical constant. The two criteria are alternatives. The local criterion removes a restriction on the total number of vertices; it does not subsume the earlier criterion for every individual diagram.

8. Structural consequences and limitations

This removes the sum over vertices from the sufficient analytic condition. For example dd2+n2≤12|n|(n≠0) \frac{d}{d^2+n^2}\le\frac1{2|n|}\quad(n\ne0) \tag{47} shows that every |nv|≥2119|n_v|\ge2^{119} satisfies (2), regardless of the number or degrees of the vertices. The constant is very coarse; its purpose is a size-independent sufficient hypothesis. Zero or short powers are also allowed whenever their own degree makes (1) small. This is not the claim that the graph can grow with NN: every resulting diagram is fixed first.

The proof is specific to the literal all-circle tree. It uses the exact degree-N−1N-1 boundary message and the one-edge kernel factorization. Arbitrary bare junctions, graphs with cycles, and rational slopes have not been included. The former summed criterion can remain a less restrictive sufficient test for individual diagrams; no comparison of optimal constants is claimed.

The main analytic point is conditional normalization at the finite-polynomial level. Expanding child errors as absolute remainders at the augmented action, or using 2G0+O(r2)2G_0+O(r^2) instead of ψ(0,u)\psi(0,u) in (12), would not prove this theorem. The proof above keeps the exact child messages until the next local saddle and retains all source factors and normal channels.
