What cyclotomic descent can identify with the regulator connection

Focused applicability audit, 27 October 2026. The marked Bloch regulator has an algebraic logarithmic connection, but its exact finite quantum step is not thereby rational in Q,Y. An absolute rational unipotent family with identity cyclotomic curvature cannot supply that regulator connection in a regular Q->1 limit. This remains true even if its initial rational gauge has poles at Q=1. The appropriate possible use of zero-curvature descent is a relative comparison between two already specified period realizations, with their sources and markings.

No construction of that actual comparison or volume theorem is asserted here.

1. A rational-coboundary confluence lemma

Let k be an infinite characteristic-zero field and put h=Q-1, D=Y partial_Y. Suppose a rational matrix C(Q,Y) is regular at Q=1 as a matrix over k(Y)[[h]], with

C(Q,Y)=I+h A(Y)+O(h^2).                     (1.1)

Assume it is a generic rational coboundary in the column convention:

C(Q,Y)=G(Q,QY)G(Q,Y)^(-1),
G in GL_d(k(Q,Y)).                          (1.2)

Then the differential equation D v=A(Y)v has a fundamental matrix in GL_d(k(Y)). A pole of the initial G at Q=1 does not evade this conclusion.

Proof. Introduce an independent anchor Z, fixed by sigma and D, and normalize on the right:

Ghat(Q,Y;Z)=G(Q,Y)G(Q,Z)^(-1).              (1.3)

It obeys the same transport and Ghat(Q,Z;Z)=I. Its Laurent expansion in h has finite principal part. Its coefficients are rational in Y,Z and regular at Y=Z over k(Z). To see the last point, take a common rational denominator before expanding. Its first nonzero h coefficient as a polynomial in Y is nonzero at the generic value Y=Z. The extra normalization denominator is independent of Y and has the same property at generic Z. Thus expansion and evaluation at Y=Z commute.

If Ghat had a negative leading order -r, r>0, the coefficient G_(-r) would satisfy

D G_(-r)=A G_(-r), G_(-r)(Z;Z)=0.           (1.4)

This follows by comparing order h^(1-r) in sigma(Ghat)=C Ghat. The coefficients of A(Y)/Y are regular at the generic anchor. Expanding at Y=Z, the formal differential equation uniquely determines all coefficients from the constant term, so the zero initial value makes G_(-r)=0. This contradicts its leading-order choice. Hence Ghat is regular at Q=1.

Its constant matrix G_0(Y;Z) has G_0(Z;Z)=I and satisfies

D G_0=A G_0.                                (1.5)

It is generically invertible. Specialize Z to a value in k avoiding the finitely many rational denominator and determinant exceptions. This gives a fundamental matrix over k(Y), proving the lemma.

The same argument can be expressed analytically at a generic complex anchor. The independent-anchor proof avoids an assumption of uniform Laurent expansion at an accidentally degenerate numeric anchor.

The anchor condition matters. For the scalar G=Y-Q one has C=Q(Y-1)/(Y-Q)=1+h Y/(Y-1)+O(h^2). Normalization at the fixed anchor Y=1 gives Ghat=1-(Y-1)/h and remains singular. That anchor is a pole of the limiting connection; it is excluded by the generic construction. At an independent Z the normalized limit is (Y-1)/(Z-1), the required rational fundamental solution.

2. Consequence of full cyclotomic curvature identity

The full rank-one and upper-triangular descent theorems construct a rational G satisfying (1.2) when the specified rational triangular transport has identity whole cyclotomic curvature. If that transport also has the regular form (1.1), the lemma shows that its entire classical differential limit is rationally gauge trivial. Here the coefficient-field and full-root hypotheses are those in the descent theorem: principal roots suffice for rational coefficients by Galois descent; over a number field one must supply the additional whole-root or coefficient-conjugate marking stated there.

The statement is about the full differential module, not the residue of every displayed matrix entry in every gauge. A rational singular gauge can change individual residues and higher pole terms. What is excluded is a nontrivial marked logarithmic extension such as the regulator’s first additive logarithm block below.

There is an exact root-normal version as well. For every sufficiently large root where G is defined, take the module T=C^(-1)sigma_Q. The gauge v=G w changes it to sigma_Q. By the gauge law of the first quantum-normal construction, the descended operator pi L_1/N^2 is therefore the trivial connection U partial_U in that invariant frame, U=Y^N. This assertion does not need a regular Q=1 gauge. It holds at each regular finite root.

It does not make an arbitrary pointed source section horizontal. Nor does it identify generic kernels with special central-fiber kernels at rank jumps. Those remain source/lattice questions.

3. The marked regulator is not a rationally trivial connection

On the normalized marked A-curve let m be the nonconstant meridian function and l the longitude function in the recorded conventions. The Heisenberg period equations are

dU=dm/m, dV=dl/l, dH=U dl/l.                (3.1)

More precisely Pi=[1 U H;0 1 V;0 0 1] obeys the right equation dPi=Pi Omega, with Omega_12=dm/m, Omega_23=dl/l, and other entries zero. In the column convention the fundamental matrix is Pi^t and its connection matrix is Omega^t. These equations define an algebraic flat connection on the curve, with its fundamental-class mark fixing the central period. Already dm/m has no rational primitive on the curve: at a zero or pole of m its residue is ord_p(m)!=0, whereas an exact differential of a rational function has residue zero. A nonconstant meromorphic function on the compact normalized curve has such a divisor.

The same obstruction survives every finite algebraic cover. The pullback residue is multiplied by the nonzero ramification index; it remains nonzero in characteristic zero. Thus Log m is not an algebraic function on any finite cover.

Equivalently the rank-two additive block

d(v_1)=v_2 dm/m, d(v_2)=0                   (3.2)

has no rational fundamental matrix, even on a finite cover. A horizontal rational vector with v_2!=0 would supply the forbidden primitive. This block obstructs rational triviality of the full marked Heisenberg connection. Directly in its recorded right convention, suppose K were a rational fundamental matrix for dK=K Omega. Its first column f_1 is a nonzero constant vector, and its second column obeys d f_2=f_1 dm/m. A constant row functional a with a f_1=1 would give the forbidden rational primitive a f_2. This avoids any choice of a splitting of the rank-three filtration.

Consequently the regulator connection cannot be identified, preserving its flat structure and meridian marking, with an absolute rational triangular family satisfying both identity cyclotomic curvature and the regular confluence hypothesis (1.1). The finite-root normal connection of an absolute rational coboundary is likewise trivial and does not already give the marked regulator connection.

These are global function-field obstructions. In a small analytic disk at m=1, or its formal completion, Log m is a perfectly valid primitive. Local formal triviality is not generic rational or finite-algebraic triviality. Algebraicity of the complete gluing shape does not remove this distinction.

4. A rational deformation can carry logarithms if curvature is retained

The obstruction is not to a rational deformation of the differential connection. A simple Heisenberg example makes the missing condition explicit. On the torus chart take m=Y, l=Y^r for an integer r, and

A=E_12+r E_23, A^3=0.

The logarithmic connection is d v=A v dlog Y. The rational matrix

C_rat(Q)=exp((Q-1)A)
  =I+(Q-1)A+(Q-1)^2 A^2/2                 (4.1)

has that regular differential limit. Its exact root curvature is

C_rat(zeta_N)^N
  =exp(N(zeta_N-1)A),                      (4.2)

which is not identity for N>=2 when A!=0. Its limit is exp(2pi*i A), the nontrivial logarithmic monodromy. All central Heisenberg terms are retained in this formula.

The exact horizontal step of that same differential connection, on the chosen logarithmic sheet, is instead

C_exact(Q)=exp((Log Q)A).                   (4.3)

It contains Log Q and is not rational or algebraic in Q. Already its first superdiagonal is Log Q. This cannot be made a rational function by a rational additive gauge: such a change adds a rational function to that entry. The residue of dQ/Q on a finite cover also directly excludes an algebraic Log Q.

Thus a rational algebraic connection does not furnish a rational exact q-step. Formula (4.1) gives a genuine confluence construction, while (4.3) gives exact connection transport; they are distinct whole objects. This torus example isolates the logarithmic issue and is not a replacement knot state or a hyperbolic volume model.

5. The precise possible use of comparison descent

The zero-curvature theorems can be applied to a specified relative comparison whose own transport has identity curvature. They should not be applied to trivialize the absolute regulator period. Two period realizations may carry the same nontrivial logarithmic and central periods while their relative algebraic comparison descends. Its source, cut anchor and integral lattice still must be matched.

Equality or conjugacy of the two absolute curvature matrices is insufficient. On a Hom module its curvature acts on a comparison matrix P by P |-> H_act P H_geo^(-1); even H_act=H_geo does not make this action identity on all matrices. One needs a specified comparison line or filtered subobject on which that relative action is identity, or an actual curvature-intertwining section together with the necessary descent data. Nor is the naive product C_geo^(-1) C_act in general the transport matrix of this Hom module. The descent theorem applies only after this relative zero-curvature object has been constructed.

Likewise the actual geometric quantum action has an exponential scale exp(W/Log Q). It need not be a regular unipotent confluence of the form (1.1). Singular or derived limits outside the lemma’s hypotheses require their own maps and normal source complexes; they are not justified by a scalar norm equation.

The actual first-normal source constructed from the finite-sum central derivative and the Fourier–Mellin source are genuine data available for that comparison. No equality with the regulator source or its nonzero selected period is inferred here.
