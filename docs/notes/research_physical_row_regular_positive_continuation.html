Actual physical-row windows on regular positive continuation charts

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The full-slit physical-row theorem fixes the actual value and first-normal normalization on a real bulk window. Here that normalization gives a moderate scalar mode on a buffered complex degree chart, even where the selected characteristic root ceases to dominate in modulus. The proof constructs the mode there; it does not propagate an exponentially small source error through an uncontrolled growing solution.

An exact one-sided Hermite repair preserves the original window data without a loss proportional to the designated complex height. The positive product-domain theorem supplies such charts near every positive pair whose first angle is nonzero. Connected overlaps with a common physical seed have exponentially small normalized differences. All original exterior window currents remain. No whole-grid kernel or bound on a raw Sears cutoff transition is asserted.

5. Positive reachability and coherent overlaps

The regular-domain hypotheses above are available at actual positive configurations, not only on a small rectangle about real angles. Fix a positive pair (t*,u*)(t_*,u_*), in the original |ℜt|,|ℜu|<π|\Re t|,|\Re u|<\pi strips, with t*≠0t_*\ne0. The phase in (9) is the original branch on this path, since it agrees at the real seed and its derivative is (10). No additive phase is selected from an off-grid factorial endpoint. If t*=0t_*=0 but u*≠0u_*\ne0, one can interchange the roles for a local construction. This observation does not make a single first-degree chart cross its fold. The simultaneous complete point and a whole first-grid fold neighborhood require their existing separate constructions.

There is a useful quantitative overlap statement for two charts using the same first-degree coordinate. Suppose their constructions have a connected buffered common domain, the same continued phase, and a common real seed window containing cNcN physical nodes. Suppose the compact target overlap is connected to that window through this common domain where both normalized representatives have the bounds just proved. Then e−NΨ(ℱN,1−ℱN,2)=O(e−κ12N)(35) e^{-N\Psi}(\mathcal F_{N,1}-\mathcal F_{N,2}) =O(e^{-\kappa_{12}N}) \qquad\text{(35)} on that compact overlap, with fixed derivatives.

To prove this, the normalized difference has exact zeros at every common physical node and has norm eo(N)e^{o(N)} on a fixed real tube. The disk Blaschke estimate used in the pinned normal theorem therefore gives e−cN+o(N)e^{-cN+o(N)} on a smaller open seed set. A finite chain of buffered three-circles estimates propagates this to the specified compact overlap. The o(N)o(N) upper exponents remain negligible at each of finitely many steps. Work uniformly on a slightly larger exterior parameter compact and then use Cauchy for its derivatives. The same argument applies to the baselines using their exponentially small nodal errors.

This statement requires the connected common domain and its shared seed, or an already compared open subset. It does not control a disconnected overlap, continuation around an unrecorded branch, or an arbitrary raw cutoff transition merely because its physical jets vanish. It is a controlled transport of the constructed normalized modes. Comparison of the two constructions obtained by interchanging the endpoint roles requires its own joint seed and overlap argument; it is not included in (35).
