Source growth near the complete main meridian

The general component theorem extends this result to every fixed strict Conway tree. The proof below establishes the original separated-junction case and its physical input estimates. The general theorem retains the same invariant, normalization and radius restrictions.

Fix a knot construction made by stacking and turning two-strand tangles, with H≥2H\ge2 original pieces. Before inserting their long twists, keep the crossing circle around each piece. The resulting augmented complement is the complement with these crossing circles left unfilled. Their integer colors ji∈{1,…,N}j_i\in\{1,\ldots,N\} label the ordinary representations used in the quantum calculation; they have not yet been summed with filling coefficients.

A product junction, also called a hub below, is a place where several tangle operators are multiplied without intervening turns. Combine adjacent product junctions until every remaining internal edge carries a quarter-turn. Mark a junction when one of its original crossing-circle operators occurs there without that outer turn; such a junction is called flagged. A junction without this operator is called bare. We assume that no two bare junctions are adjacent. The number of junctions and their valences may be any fixed finite numbers.

The construction must also satisfy the existing strict Conway conditions: its retained angle inequalities have an interior solution, its prescribed endpoint tracing gives one main component, and it has no internal closed component. These conditions refer to the original ordered tangle diagram. For two pieces, retain the knot-producing half-twist choices; those contain the pure leaf needed by the two-leaf asymptotic theorem.

4.7. The remaining scope

The separation condition is essential to this proof’s recursive invariant. A general adjacent bare hub receives an A module whose low coefficient profile is not g+cg+c; a small spectral remainder also need not retain the full saving required by the next bare hub. Nothing above resets that module for free. Conversely, every bare hub in the stated class is fed only L modules, so this missing step is never used.

For this same separated-junction class, the auxiliary-color gap theorem additionally proves a strict exponential loss below the rate F0F_0 whenever at least one original color satisfies ji≤(1−δ)Nj_i\le(1-\delta)N. It retains every other color, both parities, and the actual half-twist and cut factors. Its parameter disks satisfy R<1/2R<1/2, whereas (T31)’s upper bound holds for R<1R<1.

Both results concern the unsummed auxiliary-colored source at the complete augmented action. They do not supply a cancellation-preserving filling contour, a lower bound for a filled invariant, or a bound at the generally smaller filled-knot volume. They also do not prove convergence of the ordinary-color-subtracted microscopic meridian profile. Those conclusions require additional control of the signed filling transform or normalized profile.

The subsequent component theorem provides the intermediate-profile estimate under an explicit condition on paths through adjacent bare junctions. The filled-meridian theorem combines that estimate with a joint Fourier calculation and the original marked filling geometry. For the resulting class it proves a pointwise normalized profile and a uniform knot-complement volume limit on every |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1. These conclusions use those additional arguments, not just the supremum in (T33).
