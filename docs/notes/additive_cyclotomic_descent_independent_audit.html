Independent proof of additive full-cyclotomic descent

Research audit and proof, 27 October 2026. The additive analogue of the full rank-one theorem is valid with the weighted exponential–Puiseux uniqueness lemma. The pole-order induction needs the whole orbit function, not just its highest principal coefficient. The proof below makes that cancellation explicit.

This concerns additive comparison torsors with trivial homogeneous action. It does not by itself solve a residual twisted by a nontrivial geometric companion matrix, or construct the selected knot comparison. The volume conjecture remains unproved.

1. Statement and full-root propagation

Let c(Q,Y) belong to ℚ(Q,Y), sigma(Y)=QY. Suppose that for every sufficiently large N,

sum_(j=0)^(N-1)c(zeta_N,zeta_N^jY)=0        (1.1)

as a rational identity in Y. Then there is b in ℚ(Q,Y) such that

c(Q,Y)=b(Q,QY)-b(Q,Y).                     (1.2)

For coefficients in a field K contained in C the same conclusion over K(Q,Y) holds if the hypothesis is supplied at every primitive root for the fixed coefficient embedding. The rational coefficient case obtains these conjugate identities by Galois action. An appropriate sufficient condition over a number field is the full coefficient-conjugate marking of the multiplicative theorem.

For fixed k, xi_N=exp(2piik/N) has order m=N/gcd(k,N). Its true m-step sum is zero for all sufficiently large N; the N-step sum repeats it gcd(k,N) times. Thus these scaled roots are available without a coprimality restriction on k,N.

2. Exact pole-cluster cancellation

Over a finite algebraic splitting field write the unique partial fraction expansion

c(Q,Y)=sum_l c_l(Q)Y^l
        +sum_(mu,r) a_(mu,r)(Q)/(1-mu(Q)Y)^r, (2.1)

with finitely many Laurent monomials, distinct nonzero algebraic mu, and positive pole orders r. The Laurent coefficients belong to the original rational field. Choose the common algebraic coefficient field large enough for all partial fractions.

At a primitive m-th root, the universal orbit function is

Psi_(m,r)(u)=sum_(j=0)^(m-1)(1-zeta_m^j z)^(-r),
u=z^m,
Psi_(m,r)(u)=m sum_(l>=0)
                   binom(ml+r-1,r-1)u^l.   (2.2)

This follows from expansion at z=0 and root orthogonality. The binomial is a polynomial in l of degree r-1. Hence Psi is rational and has an order-r pole at u=1 with highest coefficient

Psi_(m,r)(u)=m^r/(1-u)^r
                     +lower pole orders.  (2.3)

For example this coefficient follows inductively from Psi_(m,r+1)=Psi_(m,r)+(m*u/r) partial_u Psi_(m,r). Equivalently use the leading coefficient m^(r-1)/(r-1)! of the binomial polynomial in (2.2) and the order-r pole of sum l(r-1)ul.

Fix a root specialization outside the finite bad set and a cluster of poles for which mu(Q)^m has the same value lambda. Let R be the largest pole order still present. At U=Y^m=1/lambda, the order-R coefficient of the averaged rational function is

m^R sum_(mu^m=lambda) a_(mu,R)(Q).

It must vanish. Crucially, the entire contribution of that order is the same universal function Psi_(m,R)(lambda U) multiplied by this sum. It therefore vanishes identically, including all its lower pole orders. One may now descend to R-1. Induction proves

sum_(mu^m=lambda) a_(mu,r)(Q)=0
                     for every r,lambda.  (2.4)

This avoids mixing an order-R lower principal part with a genuine order-(R-1) coefficient. Moving poles and their partial-fraction weights are algebraic, so accidental collisions or poles of the weights exclude only finitely many sufficiently near Q=1 roots, unless the algebraic branches are identical and were already combined.

For large m all nonconstant Laurent monomials average to zero. The pole terms tend to zero at infinity. Comparing the remaining constant there gives m c_0(Q)=0. Infinitely many root values therefore give c_0(Q)=0 identically.

3. Weighted scaled moments detect the generic Q-orbits

At xi_N, use (2.4) at its true order m, then raise mu^m to the gcd(k,N)-th power. For every fixed r this gives

sum_mu a_(mu,r)(xi_N) mu(xi_N)^N=0.          (3.1)

Choose the common ramification and Puiseux forms of the full multiplicative proof:

mu(exp(2pi*i*t))=a t^nu exp(lambda_mu(t^(1/e))),
A_mu(t)=exp(lambda_mu(t^(1/e))/t).

The algebraic weights a_(mu,r)(exp(2piit)) are meromorphic in t^(1/e). They are precisely allowed by the weighted uniqueness lemma. At each fixed scaled root k that lemma separates the groups (nu,a), giving, after rescaling the argument,

sum_(mu in (nu,a)) a_(mu,r)(Q(t)) A_mu(t)^k=0. (3.2)

The weights are not raised to the k-th power. Within one group, partition the mu by identical functions A_mu. Let the distinct nonzero functions be A_1,…,A_h and let W_l(t) be the sum of their weights. For k=1,…,h, equation (3.2) is

sum_l W_l(t) A_l(t)^k=0.

This matrix is a Vandermonde matrix times a diagonal matrix of the nonzero A_l, and is invertible over the common function field on the ramified punctured disk. Hence every W_l=0 identically.

For branches with the same (nu,a), equality A_mu=A_eta is equivalent to

mu(Q)/eta(Q)=Q^j for an integer j.          (3.3)

One direction is immediate. For the other, their logarithmic difference divided by t has exponential one and therefore is a constant 2pi*i j, exactly as in the multiplicative proof. Thus the identical-A classes are the generic Q-shift orbits of the pole parameters. We have proved zero total weight in each such orbit, separately for every pole order r.

4. A finite additive flow constructs the rational primitive

On an orbit mu=Q^u eta write the order-r weights as a_u(Q). Their total is zero. Define the finite cumulative flow

e_u=-sum_(v<=u) a_v,
b_(eta,r)=sum_u e_u/(1-Q^u eta Y)^r.        (4.1)

Then sigma b_(eta,r)-b_(eta,r) is the required pole contribution, because the coefficient at index u is e_(u-1)-e_u=a_u. For a nonzero Laurent exponent l, use the primitive

c_l(Q)Y^l/(Q^l-1).                         (4.2)

The constant Laurent coefficient has already been shown to vanish. The sum of (4.1)–(4.2) solves (1.2) over the splitting field.

The pole flow is the unique finite-support flow on its infinite Q-orbit. Since c is rational over ℚ(Q), Galois permutations preserve the resulting whole pole part. The Laurent part is already rational. Choosing the free constant in that Laurent part to be zero therefore gives b in ℚ(Q,Y). The same descent works over K(Q). Any two rational solutions differ by a rational sigma-invariant, hence by a function of Q alone.

This is generic rational descent. Pole divisors of b at the ideal meridian remain part of its lattice. A source-compatible marked cut or normal residue cannot be inferred from (1.2) alone.

5. The finite nilpotent extension consequence

Combining the full multiplicative and additive results gives a direct finite induction for upper-triangular comparison transport. Suppose an upper-triangular rational matrix B(Q,Y) has identity cyclic monodromy at every sufficiently large primitive root. Its diagonal coefficients have norm one, so the multiplicative theorem trivializes them by rational diagonal gauges. The matrix is now unipotent and its root monodromy remains identity.

The first superdiagonal of its monodromy is the cyclic sum of that superdiagonal of B. The additive theorem removes it by a rational unipotent gauge. After this removal the second superdiagonal has the same property; continue by distance above the diagonal. Only finitely many steps occur, at most the nilpotence class. This proves rational triviality for the stated upper-triangular comparison object.

It does not assert nilpotence of the original braid image, nor triangularity or identity curvature for the unknown actual/geometric comparison. Those inputs must be established. A twisted source residual (sigma-C)b=Delta is not automatically the untwisted problem (1.2).
