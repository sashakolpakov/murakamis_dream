Independent audit of the actual two-head cyclic sewing

28 October 2026. The exact five-term resolvent and the two-sector SMALL-CIRCLE period reduction in the sewing theorem are correct, including the coincident-wall fibre. A balancing gauge gives a useful local matrix estimate. It does not turn the original period into a positive pairing or license the old Hankel deformation term by term.

There is an explicit source-visible pole witness in the original positive crossing: its two sewn cross pieces have nonzero double poles absent from the nilpotent resolvent. Their contributions must accompany any subsequent cut deformation.

1. Resolvent order and the coincident wall

On a total-index residue fibre choose basis b_n, n=0,…,N-1. Let S be cyclic raising, A=aS, z=a/t, and h=k+1. For h<N the actual truncated transfer deletes the arrows N-1 to 0 and h-1 to h. With U=[b_0,b_h], its deleted operator is UU^t A. Woodbury therefore gives

R_T=R_A-R_A U (I+U^t A R_A U)^-1 U^t A R_A.

Direct cyclic summation gives

(I+U^t A R_A U)^-1
    =C_h(z)=[[1,-z^(N-h)],[-z^h,1]].

All orders in the displayed formula matter. At h=N the two columns of U coincide, so a rank-two Woodbury inverse is NOT available. Nevertheless the SAME compressed expression remains correct, because

U C_N(z) U^t=(1-z^N)b_0 b_0^t.

This is exactly the single-deleted-edge formula. Thus the global five-term expression and its two cross terms are valid for every fibre without double-counting the physical wall.

R_A and the two diagonal sewing terms are holomorphic at t=0. Multiplying by the marked analytic inverse-D germ and integrating on rho<min(1,|a|) kills those three terms entrywise. Other word factors do not alter that conclusion, since they use different integration variables. Consequently the at most 2^(3m) surviving cross-sector networks retain the original source exactly on the original product of small circles.

2. The balancing gauge and its quantitative scope

Retain a continuous Log z and put

D_h=diag(1,z^(h-N/2)), w=z^(N/2).

Then exactly

D_h^-1 C_h D_h=[[1,-w],[-w,1]],
det C_h=1-z^N.                                (1)

The original physical scalar is a=x^-1 or Q^-1 x^-1. On an actual inverse-D cut t=Q^(-epsilon j)r and at M=1,

z^N=(-1)^(N+1) r^(-N).                        (2)

For odd N, w is one of the two real values +/-r^(-N/2). The balanced C has positive eigenvalues 1+/-r^(-N/2) for r>1. For even N, w is purely imaginary, and the Hermitian part of the balanced C is EXACTLY identity. Both statements retain the root phase; z^N has not been replaced by a positive number.

The gauge condition number is

kappa(D_h)=r^|h-N/2|.

On the actual radial scale r=exp(s/N), it is bounded by exp(s/2), uniformly in h and N when s is bounded. This includes h=N, although U then has repeated columns. For odd N the smallest balanced eigenvalue is 1-exp(-s/2), which tends to zero at the radial endpoint. At fixed r>1 the gauge can instead have exponential condition number; the stated bound is a scaled local estimate, not a uniform estimate on the entire radial ray.

Moreover the two-sector Cauchy reduction keeps only C’s OFFDIAGONAL part. Its balanced version is [[0,-w],[-w,0]]: indefinite for odd N and with zero Hermitian part for even N. Thus the positive diagonal of full C belongs to terms whose original small-circle periods vanish. Positivity of full C does not itself prove a lower bound for their remaining cross-sector contraction. The actual co-source is also a pivotal linear pairing, not the positive quadratic form used by an accretivity estimate.

3. Added cyclic poles change the required relative cycle

Every cyclic R_A has poles at tN=aN, while R_T has only its nilpotent pole at zero. The five-term formula cancels all added poles. Discarding its analytic-at-zero terms preserves the small circle period but does NOT preserve global holomorphy.

At completeness aN=(-1)(N+1). Thus for odd N the added poles lie at N-th roots of unity: most hit D-cut endpoints, and t=1 is an added pole where D itself is regular. For even N they lie at the interlaced half-roots, off those cuts. Separate cross pieces can have double poles. Their old radial-cut integrals may be nonintegrable when the pole hits a branch endpoint. Pole residues or the corresponding marked finite-part chains must be retained. Matrix balancing in Section 2 does not remove this change of relative cycle.

4. An exact original positive-crossing source witness

Take N=2, M=1, Q=-1, q=i, x=i. In the opposite-root realization of the actual U_+ factor, a=Q^-1 x^-1=i. Use the original fusion C and Gaussian D=I from the strict crossing factorization, and the original averaged first-cut functional Phi with pivotal entries (-i,i). Keep its common Cartan scalar and external line separately, unchanged.

Let R_cross(t) be the sum of the two cross terms, and R_T the actual transfer resolvent. Exact source contraction gives

Phi(C R_T(t) C^-1)=2i/t^2,
Phi(C R_cross(t) C^-1)
    =-2i(t-1)(t+1)/[t^2(t^2+1)^2].             (3)

The leading double-pole coefficient of the second expression at EACH of t=i and t=-i is i. These poles are source-visible inside an actual positive crossing, rather than only in an arbitrary matrix entry. The original inverse-D germ here is f(t)=(1+t)^(-1/2). Both small-circle periods are exactly -i.

The uncorrected old D-cut integral for the sewn expression, on t=-r, would instead be

(2i/pi) integral_1^infinity
     (r^2-1)/[r^2(r^2+1)^2] (r-1)^(-1/2)dr.

It is purely POSITIVE imaginary and therefore cannot equal -i. The missing cyclic-pole contributions are essential, even in this smallest actual-source example. The original normalization multiplies both expressions by the same retained nonzero factor; it cannot correct the mismatch.

The missing ordinary residues are explicitly

Res_(t=i) f(t)B(t)dt=(-9-i)/(4sqrt(1+i)),
Res_(t=-i) f(t)B(t)dt=(9-i)/(4sqrt(1-i)).

Their sum is approximately 1.0596435755300515788i. The raw cut integral is approximately 0.0596435755300515788i. The correct contour identity is cut MINUS these pole residues, which gives the original value -i.

5. Audit conclusion

The exact small-circle sewing is a real finite-sector reduction. The balanced 2-by-2 matrix has a controlled condition number on the natural radial scale and the stated parity-dependent sign. Neither statement supplies a convergent termwise old-cut sewing or a positive selected scalar. A usable geometric comparison must carry the new cyclic poles and the original cross-sector source through the transform, including its endpoint corrections.

There is a canonical linear repair when cyclic poles have order at most two, as they do in each transfer variable. At a D-cut endpoint alpha, put x=r-1 and write the remaining cut integrand as x^(-u)f(x), where f(x)=b_-2 x^(-2)+b_-1 x^(-1)+O(1). Its finite part is

integral_0^1 x^(-u)[f-b_-2 x^(-2)-b_-1 x^(-1)]dx
  +integral_1^infinity x^(-u)f dx
  -b_-2/(u+1)-b_-1/u.

Use these marked finite parts on the branch cuts and subtract the ordinary residues at unbranched cyclic poles. This gives the original small-circle period exactly. The constants follow from analytic continuation of the two elementary power integrals. The small endpoint arcs cancel their fractional divergences and have no additional finite residue: on the normalized cover a residue exponent would require j to be a multiple of N. Coordinatewise finite parts commute for this word because its ordered matrix source is a finite sum of products of one-variable rational factors. The tensor prescription retains the required corner subtractions. It preserves the source, while still not making its resulting scalar a positive quadratic pairing.

The regularized cut theorem has positive derivative factor 1/[u…(u+p-1)] for p=1,2. At every integration-by-parts step the boundary term at infinity is O(x^(-(N-1)/2)), and the zero-end finite constant vanishes because u is nonintegral. The final derivative integral converges at both ends. The whole-word tensor prescription is a finite sum of products of one-variable factors, with only coordinate pole loci. The unit-circle/completeness hypothesis excludes additional poles inside a radial cut. Geometric coupling remains separate.
