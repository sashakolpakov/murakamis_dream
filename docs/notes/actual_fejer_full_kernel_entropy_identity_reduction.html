Continuous full-kernel entropy and exact identity reduction of maxima

Completion: the general endpoint theorem assembles this local result into the actual augmented-volume equality. Its unique geometric maximum gives concentration; the original filling conjecture remains open.

31 October 2026. full-kernel entropy and identity-reduction theorem. This uses the proved uniform closed local coefficient limits. It does not assume that an infeasible fixed half-label pattern becomes feasible. It sums the full positive local kernel before forgetting its type.

1. The full local coefficient entropy is continuous

For q=(X,B,C,Y)>=0, X+B+C+Y=1, define h(q) as follows.

If C>=1/2, put h=E(B,1-C,X). If B>=1/2, put h=E(C,1-B,X). If B,C<=1/2, put

h(q)=max_s { Ctri(s,B,1/2-B-s) +Ctri(X-s,C,1/2-C-X+s) }, (1)

where max(0,X+C-1/2)<=s<=min(X,1/2-B), and Ctri(alpha)=V(2pi alpha)/(2pi) is the proved triangle entropy. The conventions agree on every overlap. At C=1/2 the second triangle is pure-middle, of entropy zero, and (1) is Ctri(X,B,Y), which is EXACTLY E(B,1/2,X). The B=1/2 boundary is reflected. At B=C=1/2 all entropies are zero. The interval in (1) has continuous endpoints, so compact maximization and continuity of Ctri prove h is continuous on the whole simplex. The closed half-block theorem proves the same for its two one-sided pieces. In particular

h(0,B,1-B,0)=0 for every 0<=B<=1. (2)

Let p_N(n) be the coefficient of the FULL positive polynomial P in a nonnegative local exponent vector n of total degree d=N-1. Every such coefficient is positive. The exact supports partition all these vectors into cross (both middle<=m-1), left (c>=m), and right (b>=m). The uniform half-block limits, positive cross convolution and B_cross<=P_cross<=3B_cross give

sup_(n>=0, sum n=d) |N^-1 log p_N(n)-h(n/d)| ->0. (3)

For the cross convolution the allowed outer-split interval has integer endpoints. Every limiting split has an O(1/N) integer approximation; there are O(N) summands. Uniform continuity of Ctri therefore makes its log-sum rate uniformly the maximum (1), including the terminal faces. The finite forced outer unit is retained in this argument. The changes from i/N,j/N to n/d are O(1/N) in the closed parameters, so continuity justifies that normalization. This is NOT a bound on an individual cross term by H1 at zero outer degree.

2. A shared variational theorem without fixed half labels

Take any fixed finite incidence graph of these homogeneous degree-d polynomials, allowing repeated global labels among a port’s four separate incidences. Give every ordinary face target d and each protected face target zero. Let P be the nonnegative real flow polytope with port supplies1, ordinary face demands1 and protected demands0. If its total supplies/demands disagree, or the polytope is empty, the coefficient is zero. Otherwise

lim_(even N) N^-1 log [original target] product_e P_e =max_(q in P) sum_e h(q_e). (4)

The upper bound follows by (3) and the polynomial number of arrays. For the lower bound, take a maximizing q and multiply by d. This gives EXACT integer node balances with real edge values. Fractional-cycle rounding inside each floor/ceiling interval gives an integral flow, with error at most one per incidence. The local polynomial contains EVERY degree-d monomial, so this rounded array is an actual positive term, regardless of which half label it receives at that level. Continuity of h and (3) give its desired rate. Repeated incidences remain parallel arcs in the rounding graph.

Thus (4) agrees with the previously proved W_D on the original diagram, since both are limits of the SAME source. It does not deny the original eventual-feasibility test for FIXED patterns; it avoids assigning a fixed pattern at the seams, where h has the same continuous value. The formulation also applies to actual identity-smoothed incidence maps even when they are not simplicial right-angled certificates. No arbitrary graph-to-original-plat adapter is asserted.

The same argument permits some local H1 factors. At such an identity port remove its two outer incidence arcs; the remaining middle loads sum to one and its coefficient entropy is identically zero. All its allowed coefficients are one, and fractional-cycle rounding respects this reduced incidence graph. Thus the corresponding mixed flow maximum is the ACTUAL mixed positive coefficient rate as well.

3. A maximum with pure-middle ports is an exact lower-port problem

Let q attain (4), and let D be ANY collection of its pure-middle ports, including ordinary one-sided diagonals and a pure-middle cross endpoint (0,1/2,1/2,0). Replace those full local P factors by H1. The SAME q is feasible in the mixed identity flow polytope and has the same entropy by (2). Therefore the mixed maximum is at least W_D. Conversely, coefficientwise H1<=P_diag<=P, so its actual mixed coefficient rate is at most W_D. Hence

W_D = rate of the actual mixed source with H1 on D. (5)

This equality is about the FULL summed local kernel and its rate. It makes no false finite comparison between a cross boundary monomial and H1. In particular it repairs the pure-middle cross seam only after (3)–(4), without relabeling an infeasible fixed source pattern.

Feasibility forces the diagonal middle graph to be a forest: every possible original even-gap cycle contains the protected exterior, and its unit edge supplies exceed that component’s ordinary demands. A tree cannot contain two protected vertices either. The exact subtree coefficient identity therefore identifies the mixed H1 coefficient with the true connected identity smoothing, with all face targets and no multiplicity. Thus (5) is a genuine reduction to fewer remaining positive P ports.

What it does NOT yet prove is that the resulting non-simple smoothed incidence graph has a regular entropy maximizer or that its W is bounded by the original complete volume. Matching/matching cuts can still carry asymmetric Green loads; their complete-source block factorization is not assumed. The remaining geometry must retain the actual smoothing and, when restoring deleted cusps, its literal identity meridians.

The rate-level identity reduction does not give a volume bound for every nonsimple smoothed source or a quantum filling asymptotic.
