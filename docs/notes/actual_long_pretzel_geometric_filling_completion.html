Positive geometric deformation and smooth filling of the selected source

Subsequent quantum scope extension. The improved full-color bound lowers the required odd width to s>=9. The geometric argument below already applies there and is unchanged. Its diagram-independent generalization is the general geometric theorem.

29 October 2026. The selected long-filling representation is realized by a complete, positively oriented finite-volume hyperbolic metric on the ACTUAL filled pretzel complement. The construction starts with the explicit four-antiprism cellulation and uses finite face and cell centers. It does not assume that an arbitrary ideal topological triangulation has a positive geometric realization.

Together with the already proved full original-source asymptotic and marked regulator comparison, this gives full hyperbolic-volume equality for every fixed odd width s≥25s\geq25, with all signed twist parameters independently sufficiently large and fixed before N→∞N\to\infty. The proof below identifies the selected representation geometrically; a representation-volume UPPER bound or convergence of its volume alone would not prove this conclusion.

1. The precise geometric and source inputs

The explicit augmented geometry constructs the original half-twisted augmented link from two convex ideal polyhedra 𝒫s\mathcal P_s, each the double of an ideal right-angled antiprism. Its labeled face pairings, edge cycles and cusp tori are those of the original source, and the complete volume is VsV_s.

The literal port and anchor theorem supplies a family ρz\rho_z of representations of that ACTUAL augmented-link group, with its specified main and auxiliary peripheral eigenflags. It proves:

  • ρ0\rho_0 is the positively oriented complete geometric holonomy, by every white and both shaded face maps;
  • the main meridian stays noncentral parabolic;
  • the auxiliary meridians and preferred disk longitudes are Ci,PiC_i,P_i, with a common specified eigenflag and the retained SL sign;
  • the selected source filling point approaches this complete point uniformly as max⁡i|pi|−1→0\max_i|p_i|^{-1}\to0, and kills exactly the primitive slopes μi+piλi\mu_i+p_i\lambda_i in PSL(2,C).

This family is a direct verified group/peripheral construction, not an inference from a scalar eigenphase or an unspecified character-variety component. The original cap order and the orientation-preserving reverse traversal are retained.

The primitive-period theorem fixes the continuous LOG SIMILARITY holonomies near completeness:

Ui=Log⁡Mi2=−zi,Vi=Log⁡λi2 centered at completeness=−2ℓi, U_i=\operatorname{Log} M_i^2=-z_i,\qquad V_i=\operatorname{Log}\lambda_i^2\text{ centered at completeness}=-2\ell_i, Ui+piVi=−2πimi,|mi|=1. U_i+p_iV_i=-2\pi i m_i,\qquad |m_i|=1. \tag{1}

The source’s selected all-unit charge, not just its exponentiated relation, is used in (1). The SL longitude sign is not counted as an additional uncentered 2πipi2\pi i p_i.

2. A fixed positive triangulation of the ACTUAL complete cellulation

Choose a finite point OPO_P strictly inside each of the two convex complete polyhedra. For every paired original face, choose one finite point FF in the relative interior of one face and put the center of its partner at the point prescribed by that face isometry. This is one choice per face ORBIT, not two unrelated choices. Each original face is a convex ideal polygon. Join its center to every ideal edge, and cone every resulting triangle to OPO_P.

The resulting finite tetrahedra have vertices

(OP,F,vj,vj+1), (O_P,F,v_j,v_{j+1}), \tag{2}

with two finite and two ideal vertices. Every tetrahedron is strictly positive with its cell’s induced orientation: FF is interior to its face, vjvj+1v_jv_{j+1} is an edge, and OPO_P is strictly on the inner side of that face plane. Convexity makes these face fans and cones a decomposition of the original polyhedron. The paired face fans match EXACTLY, including shaded outer-label exchanges. There are 24s24s tetrahedra in the two polyhedra. Their only ideal vertices are the original cusp lifts.

This is a geometric triangulation of the actual complete manifold, allowing finite vertices. No assertion about a positive all-ideal subdivision is needed. Finite centers may, for example, be chosen by normalized positive Lorentz sums of ideal vertex lifts in a chosen face/cell and then transferred to a paired face by its isometry. All choices are fixed before deformation.

3. Equivariant deformation gives a locally hyperbolic core

Work in the topological universal cover of this fixed triangulation. Choose one representative of each FINITE vertex orbit and give it its old finite point, or any continuous nearby point. For every other lift define its position by ρz\rho_z-equivariance. Finite vertices have trivial deck stabilizer, so this assignment is well defined. Assign each ideal vertex its specified peripheral fixed flag, and propagate it equivariantly. This is well defined because its entire peripheral subgroup fixes that flag.

The preferred flags vary continuously, indeed analytically on the marked chart, at the complete point. For the auxiliary cusp the given eigenvalue is Mi=Hi−1/2M_i=H_i^{-1/2}; its eigenspace is the kernel of Ci−MiIC_i-M_iI, a rank-one matrix even at the complete NONCENTRAL unipotent limit. Thus no singular diagonalizing frame is used. The same statement holds for the noncentral main parabolic meridian.

There are finitely many tetrahedra, faces and local stars modulo the deck group. Only finitely many group words occur in their vertex assignments. Their developed vertices are consequently uniformly close to the old ones for zz in one fixed sufficiently small neighborhood. Every tetrahedron in (2) stays strictly positive and nondegenerate. Straighten it to the convex geodesic tetrahedron with its assigned vertices.

Shared faces match by EXACT hyperbolic isometries: their three vertices are the same equivariant points, including every finite face center. In a fundamental-domain description the transition is precisely the corresponding ρz\rho_z face word. Positivity and closeness keep the two tetrahedra on opposite sides of a common face. Thus there is no fold in a face interior.

Here are the remaining local checks; positivity alone would not justify ignoring them.

Edges. Develop the finite cyclic star of a nonideal edge into ℍ3\mathbb H^3. It returns to the SAME developed face and edge point because its contractible edge-loop word is the identity in the actual manifold group, hence also under ρz\rho_z. More precisely the ENTIRE face-transition product telescopes to ρz(1)=1\rho_z(1)=1; this is stronger than merely returning one edge endpoint or one normal ray. There is no residual translation or dilation along the edge. In a normal disk, its positive sectors therefore have total angle 2πk2\pi k, with integer kk. Their angles depend continuously on the finitely many developed vertices. At completeness k=1k=1, so it remains one in the chosen neighborhood. This includes finite–finite, finite–ideal and ideal–ideal edges; dihedral angles are constant along each geodesic edge. The full edge star is an ordinary hyperbolic neighborhood, without a cone or screw defect.

Finite vertices. The finite topological vertex link is a 2-sphere. Its positive spherical triangles glue isometrically, and the preceding edge check gives angle 2π2\pi at every link vertex. Its development is therefore a local isometry to the unit sphere. Compactness and geodesic path lifting make it a covering of that sphere. Since the sphere is simply connected, it is one-sheeted. Hence the vertex star is an ordinary hyperbolic ball; no hidden branched spherical link occurs.

It follows that every finite point of the ideal-vertex-deleted manifold has a genuine oriented metric of curvature −1-1. Its holonomy is the original ρz\rho_z, by construction. This proves the required local developing geometry directly, without assuming a deformation or local-rigidity theorem.

4. The actual ideal stars have positive similarity links

Send a selected cusp flag to infinity by a Mobius frame chosen continuously near its complete frame. In each incident tetrahedron, the three faces through infinity are vertical planes. The horizontal projections of the three remaining vertices form a NONDEGENERATE Euclidean triangle, because the tetrahedron is positive. They give its horospherical link triangle.

The face transitions fixing infinity are orientation-preserving complex similarities ζ↦aζ+b\zeta\mapsto a\zeta+b. Thus these positive triangles form a similarity structure on the compact topological cusp torus. At its vertices the same developed edge star has winding one and angle 2π2\pi, so this is a local Euclidean coordinate structure even at those vertices. There is no cone or branch point in the affine link.

In the continuously chosen flag frame, all vertices of a fixed finite fundamental union of link triangles remain finite and uniformly bounded. The finite-center vertices stay finite; the other ideal vertices remain distinct from the chosen flag. Their projected triangles and any fixed meridian/longitude edge paths consequently have uniformly bounded developed images and Euclidean lengths. This verifies the geometric input used to identify the small logarithmic periods in the primitive-period theorem, including for independently unequal filling lengths.

The opposite face of an incident tetrahedron is not vertical: otherwise it would also contain the ideal point at infinity and all four vertices would be coplanar. It is a hemisphere, so over each projected link triangle the tetrahedron contains a vertical ray above a bounded lower height. These finitely many epigraphs provide the ACTUAL end, not merely a formal link at infinity.

5. Primitive tube completion, with no hidden covering multiplicity

Apply the proved compact-log and tube-completion lemma, sections2–4, to the similarity links just constructed. Its argument is recalled to specify every needed input.

At an auxiliary end a meridian has affine form d↦ad+bd\mapsto a d+b, with a→1a\to1 and complete b→W(1+ic)≠0b\to W(1+ic)\ne0. The common second fixed point q=b/(1−a)q=b/(1-a) therefore exists and escapes to infinity near completeness. The bounded fundamental link triangles miss it; equivariance then shows that the ENTIRE link development misses it.

On the simply connected link cover take f=Log⁡(d−q)f=\operatorname{Log}(d-q). The form dfdf is invariant under deck translations and is everywhere nonzero. Hence |df|2|df|^2 is a nonsingular flat metric on the COMPACT link torus. It is complete. Lifting straight Euclidean segments makes ff a global isometry of its universal cover onto ℂ\mathbb C. Its periods form an INJECTIVE rank-two lattice, rather than an unspecified covering of a punctured plane.

After translating qq to zero, d=efd=e^f. 6. A complete finite-volume metric on the original filled topology

Choose all slopes sufficiently long that their selected source parameters lie in the fixed positive geometric neighborhood of sections2–4. The source solution is holomorphic in all ϵi=1/pi\epsilon_i=1/p_i near zero, so a common threshold works whenever every |pi||p_i| exceeds it, with either sign and NO comparability requirement. The source parameters are fixed before the quantum-level limit is taken.

Complete every auxiliary end as in section5. The underlying topological operation is exactly Dehn filling by its prescribed primitive slope, and leaves the original complete main cusp. This is the actual pretzel knot with boxes R−(2pi+1)R^{-(2p_i+1)}, by the literal tangle and preferred-peripheral certificates. All original cap, spin and marking data agree.

The resulting Riemannian metric is smooth of curvature −1-1. Its complement of the standard ends is compact; the added solid tori are compact at bounded tube radius and the main horoball quotient is a complete cusp. Thus the whole metric is complete. Its volume is finite: there are finitely many finite-volume partially ideal tetrahedra, standard finite-radius tubes, and one finite-volume cusp. Its holonomy is exactly ρ𝒑\rho_{\boldsymbol p}.

The complete simply connected developing cover is ℍ3\mathbb H^3. Indeed geodesic path lifting for a complete local isometry gives a covering of ℍ3\mathbb H^3, which is simply connected. Hence the developing map is a global isometry and the holonomy is discrete and faithful. This conclusion has been PROVED from the positive core and primitive tube; it was not inferred from killing a peripheral word or from proximity of two volumes.

There is no need for a separate character-variety local-uniqueness theorem. The actual source critical solution is already the unique analytic selected solution in its marked small parameter chart. For that fixed holonomy the completed structure is ℍ3/ρ𝒑\mathbb H^3/\rho_{\boldsymbol p}, so changing auxiliary finite centers cannot produce a different complete metric. No assertion that all algebraic character components are this branch is used.

7. Hyperbolic-volume equality and the original invariant

The marked regulator comparison and the finite compatible filling-chain argument prove

2πℜ𝒜s(𝒑)=Vol⁡(ρ𝒑). 2\pi\Re\mathcal A_s(\boldsymbol p) =\operatorname{Vol}(\rho_{\boldsymbol p}).

Since the constructed complete metric has that holonomy and the same oriented fundamental chain, its geometric volume is precisely this value. If one starts with another complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric on the same oriented filled topology, apply the independently proved sharp representation-volume inequality in BOTH directions, with the identity-marked holonomies. The two inequalities force equal volumes. Thus no additional unproved volume-rigidity assertion or imported Mostow theorem is needed to identify this with the usual topological hyperbolic volume.

Finally the whole original-source asymptotic has a nonzero leading coefficient at BOTH level parities and all composite levels, with the actual original normalization. Combining it with the geometric completion gives

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|⟨Pretzel(2p1+1,…,2ps+1)⟩N|=Vol⁡(S3\Pretzel⁡(2p1+1,…,2ps+1)). \boxed{\quad \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N} \log\left|\langle\operatorname{Pretzel} (2p_1+1,\ldots,2p_s+1)\rangle_N\right| =\operatorname{Vol}\bigl(S^3\setminus \operatorname{Pretzel}(2p_1+1,\ldots,2p_s+1)\bigr). \quad} \tag{4}

Here s≥25s\geq25 is any fixed odd integer, and the independently signed integer slopes are all sufficiently long and fixed before N→∞N\to\infty. The construction itself proves hyperbolicity in this sufficiently long range. No statement for short slopes, smaller widths, growing width, or arbitrary knots is inferred.

This completion is a geometric equality certificate. A Casson–Rivin ideal-angle interpretation requires its own positive ideal shape chart and modified filling constraints; it cannot be obtained by maximizing the old unfilled cusp slice. The direct positive finite-center metric and primitive end completion above do not need that additional chart.

8. Scope of the completion argument

The edge transition is the full identity, including zero axial dilation. The proof uses degree-one spherical links, positive similarity-link triangles, bounded generator paths and actual epigraph ends; finite samples cannot replace these neighborhood and degree conditions.
