A relative packet for the cleared cofactor at a protected middle face

The whole-source local packet now assembles these results for the actual full numerator. The original global spectral filling period remains open.

31 October 2026. . This concerns the actual fixed-root lower cofactor with b=0. It is not a whole-source saddle, filling-cycle or genuine-q theorem. All products below stay at level N.

1. Exact recurrence and statement

Let x,y range in a sufficiently small fixed complex neighborhood of ANY fixed positive pair, let u range in a sufficiently small complex disk about 0, and put A=x+y, P=xy. Write h=1/N, s_j=2sin(pij/N), a_j=4cos^2(pi(j+1/2)/N). The original cleared cofactor recurrence at b=0 is

R_0=1, R_(-1)=0, R_j=s_j^2 A R_(j-1)+s_j s_(j-1)(a_(j-1)+u)P R_(j-2).

Set L_j=R_j/(product_(r=1)^j s_r). Then EXACTLY

L_j=s_j A L_(j-1)+(a_(j-1)+u)P L_(j-2), (L_0,L_(-1))=(1,0). (1)

The normalization causes no loss at j=1: its second term multiplies L_(-1)=0. Fix 0<t_0<1/2 and take t_0 N<=n<=N/2. Define

T_h(t)=[[2sin(pit) A, P(4cos^2(pi(t-h/2))+u)],[1,0]], lambda_+(t), lambda_-(t)=its two eigenvalues, g(t)=lambda_+(t)-lambda_-(t).

The square-root branch has g>0 at real x,y>0,u=0. Write lambda_(j,+) for lambda_+(j/N), and similarly for minus and g. The matrix at t=0 is only a reference eigenbasis; it does not change the initial vector. For all sufficiently large N these branches are holomorphic and

C_j=(lambda_(j-1,+)-lambda_(j,-))/g_j =1+O(h), A_(N,n)=lambda_(n,+)/g_0 product_(j=1)^n C_j, E_(N,n)=product_(j=1)^n [s_j lambda_(j,+)].

Then uniformly on the stated FIXED complex neighborhood,

R_n(0;x,y;u)=A_(N,n) E_(N,n) [1+O(N^-1)]. (2)

The explicit amplitude A_(N,n) is holomorphic, uniformly bounded and bounded away from zero. The same relative error has fixed parameter derivative bounds on every smaller neighborhood, by Cauchy. The finite-N product E retains all endpoint factors and parity dependence; no scalar normalization is discarded.

2. The eigenvalues have an algebraic gap even at the lower endpoint

For positive real x,y, u=0 and h=0, the discriminant is

4sin2(pi*t)A2+16cos^2(pi*t)P>0, 0<=t<=1/2.

Its minimum and lambda_+ are positive on this compact interval. They remain uniformly separated from zero for sufficiently small complex parameter perturbations and small real h>=0. In particular |g|>=c and |lambda_+|>=c. All derivatives needed below are uniformly bounded on a small extension of this interval. lambda_- may vanish; no inverse of lambda_- is used.

Put r(t)=lambda_-(t)/lambda_+(t). At t=0, r(0)=-1 EXACTLY, also for complex parameters. Since lambda_++lambda_-=2sin(pi*t)A,

r’(0)=2pi A/lambda_+(0).

Its real part is uniformly positive near the positive real parameters. Therefore |r(t)|<=exp(-c t) for 0<=t<=epsilon with uniform c>0, after shrinking the parameter neighborhood. On [epsilon,1/2], the real unperturbed |r| is strictly less than1; compactness extends a bound |r(t)|<=exp(-c t) there as well. These estimates are uniform in sufficiently small h. Equality of the eigenvalue MODULI at t=0 therefore does not imply coalescence: |1-r| stays uniformly positive.

3. A one-step normal form, including the initial negative branch

Use E(t)=[[lambda_+(t),lambda_-(t)],[1,1]]. For the state vector v_j=(L_j,L_(j-1))^T, set w_j=E(jh)^(-1)v_j. The exact update is

w_j=diag(lambda_(j,+),lambda_(j,-)) B_j w_(j-1), B_j=E(jh)^(-1)E((j-1)h).

Here (B_j)(++)=C_j. Divide out product lambda(j,+) C_j, so the normalized update has the form

M_j=diag(1,d_j)+F_j, d_j=r(jh)(B_j)_(–)/C_j, F_j diagonal entries 0, offdiagonal entries O(h).

These matrices are samples of smooth functions of t, with F’(t)=O(h), |1-d(t)|>=c, and |d_j|<=exp(Ch-cjh). Choose the offdiagonal near-identity correction X_j by

(X_j)_12=-(F_j)_12/(1-d_j), (X_j)_21= (F_j)_21/(1-d_j), diag X_j=0.

Then X_j=O(h), X_j-X_(j-1)=O(h^2). Direct multiplication gives

(I+X_j)^(-1) M_j (I+X_(j-1)) =diag(1,d_j)+O(h^2). (3)

Only 1-d_j is divided out. This remains valid when d_j=0 and when r(0)=-1. The diagonal product norms are at most exp(Cnh), and the same holds for the corrected matrices, since each step has norm at most exp(Ch)+O(h^2). Telescoping their n-fold products in operator norm gives an O(nh^2)=O(h) error from the diagonal product. Meanwhile

|product_(j=1)^n d_j|<=exp(Cnh-c h n(n+1)/2)=O(exp(-c’ N))

for t_0 N<=n<=N/2. Thus the initial negative branch is retained and then proved exponentially suppressed; it is not discarded by fiat.

The initial eigenvector coordinates are exactly (1/g_0,-1/g_0). The near-identity corrections change them by O(h). Inverting the final basis yields

L_n=[product lambda_(j,+) C_j] lambda_(n,+)/g_0 [1+O(h)],

uniformly, since lambda_(n,+)/g_0 is bounded away from zero. This is (2). The amplitude product is bounded and nonzero because every C_j=1+O(h) and n=O(N). Uniform holomorphy proves the asserted Cauchy bounds.

4. Scope and exact degenerate outer faces

If b=0 and exactly one of x,y vanishes, the original recurrence is instead the EXACT monomer product

R_n=(x+y)^n product_(j=1)^n s_j^2,

independent of u. No limiting inverse of xy is used. If both outer faces vanish and n>0, the same original recurrence is zero; this is not part of the positive geometric triangle chart.

For x,y>0 the terminal ratios following from (2), uniformly on a smaller neighborhood, are

R_n/R_(n-1)=s_n lambda_(n,+) [1+O(N^-1)]

when n is m or m-1, m=floor(N/2). The limiting ratio is

Lambda_*=2[A+sqrt(A^2+uP)].

Consequently E(b)/D(b) tends to u/Lambda_* at this protected middle, with absolute O(N^-1) error. The case u=0 retains its actual small finite-N factor a_(m-1); the displayed limit does not replace that factor in any exact source identity.

No claim is made here for an independently summed positive starting row b>0, the original shared face torus, or the original Gaussian filling period. Those require additional arguments.
