An octahedral decomposition for ordered trees of crossing circles

1 November 2026.

A crossing circle surrounds two strands of a link and bounds a disk punctured by them. Cutting along the projection regions and these disks opens the augmented link exterior into two balls. For the ordered tangle constructions considered here, each ball can be assembled from octahedra, with one octahedron for each edge of the tangle-operation tree. The proof identifies every boundary vertex and face, so it also identifies the original meridians and disk longitudes after the pieces are joined.

The tangles have two strings and four ordered endpoints. Their operations are vertical stacking, quarter-turns in the projection plane, and the specified cylindrical closure. Every product junction in the reduced operation tree carries an original crossing circle; there are no junctions without a circle. The tree, the order of its branches, and all endpoint frames are retained throughout. Faces created by opening crossing disks are called shaded faces; the pieces of the projection regions are called white faces.

This is the topological input for the quantitative volume theorem. The angular cell calculation provides positive shapes for its octahedra, and the completion theorem uses the path supplied by the analytic filling criterion. The present proof concerns the augmented exterior before filling.

1. The actual ordered object and the statement

At each vertex vv of a finite tree put an original two-strand crossing-circle tangle PvP_v. It retains the crossing RϵvR^{\epsilon_v}, where ϵv∈{−1,0,1}\epsilon_v\in\{-1,0,1\}, and its auxiliary component bounds the original twice-punctured disk. Every edge is an actual quarter-turn between two ordered product junctions. There are no product junctions without their own original circle. Terminal circles are included as vertices of degree one. The tree has at least one edge. Write TT for the actual quarter-turn, CC for the cylindrical closure, and RR for one positive braid crossing in the original ordered terminal frame.

The product order, the four endpoint frames, every rotation of a child ball, and every original disk orientation are part of the data. A representative recursive description has Wv=Pv∏w child of vT(Ww). W_v=P_v\prod_{w\text{ child of }v}T(W_w). \tag{1} The product here is in its specified order. At the closing sphere one may use the actual collar move to expose a root circle. A double turn then leaves its actual half-turn decoration on the affected subtree; it is not replaced by the scalar identity T2=1T^2=1. The relevant moves are the literal closed-turn and cyclic collar isotopies in the original ordered tangle moves, equations (14)–(18). Their four collar sectors, and any reversal of the child order, are retained. Thus (1), with those transported frames, describes the original all-circle ordered operation tree. It does not introduce a commutativity move on open two-tangles.

Write EE for the number of tree edges, V=E+1V=E+1 for the number of circles, and dvd_v for the number of edges incident to circle vv. The exterior admits the following labeled cut:

  • There is one pair of octahedral balls per original tree edge.
  • At a circle vv, its incident edge blocks are joined in the original cyclic order. There are dvd_v disk-side pages, and exactly one of their cyclic seams retains the original disk identification and its ϵv\epsilon_v crossing. All other page seams are the untwisted labeled identity seams supplied by the ordered insertion.
  • Cutting open those distinguished disk seams and the projection faces gives two balls. Each is a tree of EE octahedra attached along E−1E-1 triangular faces. Its remaining boundary has exactly the two original shaded triangles of every crossing circle and the original white face cycles.

Here an octahedron means the explicitly labeled topological cell. The angular charts give its positive shapes. The topological proof does not require the union of several cells to be convex.

4. Why this covers every ordered tree

Start with one actual tree edge. The two-leaf matching gives exactly the octahedral boundary (7), in both projection copies. This is the labeled two-circle block, after the recorded endpoint-frame rotation. Its proof is a disk/projection cut, not an equality of two scalar sources.

For a general tree, choose a terminal vertex as root and start with its unique incident edge. Expose that root by the actual closed-collar moves from section 1, retaining all transported frames and orders. The initial root then has no further children to insert; at the other endpoint the only existing edge is its parent edge. At each vertex add the children on the right of PvP_v in reverse final order, always immediately after PvP_v. Add the children on its left in their final order, always immediately before PvP_v. This is a construction of the specified word, not a permutation of an existing word: successive insertions produce exactly the two ordered lists in (1b), including the special case (1). A new child initially has only its own crossing-circle tangle; its children are subsequently inserted inside its own ball. Its actual quarter-turn carries the whole local insertion, including the two projection sides and all four endpoint labels. Any previously recorded half-turn or input/output reversal similarly carries the complete insertion and its order. It does not remove the frame decoration.

Induction now gives exactly the original recursive word and its full boundary ribbon graph. Every step glues a ball along a triangular disk, so each projection copy stays a ball. There are E−1E-1 attachments after the initial octahedron. The shaded face count is 4E−2(E−1)=2V. 4E-2(E-1)=2V. \tag{8} The count is only a consistency check: the insertion proof has already identified each of these two faces with its particular original circle and each main-segment vertex with its literal pair.

The equality of oriented ribbon graphs gives a label-preserving homeomorphism of the final boundary spheres. Extend it over the balls by a ball extension. All original white and disk-face pairing maps are matched, hence the quotient is the original link exterior. This is stronger than an identification of the unmarked manifold: the main arcs, individual auxiliary circles, disk sides, and their specified collar paths are preserved.

The extra triangular interfaces provide the promised pages. In the whole exterior an upper interface triangle and its lower partner form a two-triangle, thrice-punctured sphere. The ends are the same original circle and its two physical main punctures. Its compactification is a twice-punctured spanning disk for that circle. These pages are obtained from the explicit cell subdivision and the preceding homeomorphism; we did not assume that a single original disk cut somehow creates all dvd_v pages. The insertion order places the pages consecutively in exactly the original cyclic order at that circle. There are dv−1d_v-1 new seams there, plus the original distinguished disk seam.

5. Retained half twists and reversed disk frames

First perform the projection construction with the disk cuts open. A retained crossing changes their regluing, not the half-segment compiler of the open cut. At each v keep its original distinguished seam throughout all insertions. Its original disk map is used once, after the untwisted page attachments. Thus no crossing is distributed among several edges, and no crossing is silently added at a new seam.

For ϵv=0\epsilon_v=0 the original pure disk map pairs the two sides within the same projection copy. For ϵv=+1\epsilon_v=+1 or −1-1 use its actual odd pairings: the punctures exchange and the two projection copies are permuted as in the original A,B disk maps. The signs +1 and -1 have their distinct primitive meridian words even though their endpoint permutation has the same parity. The explicit collar permutation is also recorded in the original half-twist collar, section 3a. A full twist changes the peripheral marking and must not be discarded simply because its endpoint permutation is trivial.

A physical rotation or reflection of a disk frame transports its puncture labels, its two pages, and its chosen meridian and longitude together. Apply the insertion table in that transported frame. In particular a reversal that exchanges the projection copies is applied to both halves of every incident block. There is no use of an arbitrary puncture permutation justified only by the rigidity of an abstract thrice-punctured sphere.

These conventions cover the original signed and reversed diagrams. The local geometric block may be relabeled by these same actual rotations. Its shape formula is not used to determine the topology.

6. Exact primitive peripheral concatenation

Fix one auxiliary circle vv and orient its original disk-framed longitude λv\lambda_v and meridian μv\mu_v as in the original marked leaf theorem. After the page cut, a small cusp torus of vv consists of dvd_v annular sectors, one from each incident edge block. Every page boundary on this torus is the same primitive disk-longitude class. A small meridian crosses these sectors once in their cyclic order. The insertion (5) subdivides one such transverse passage into two consecutive passages; it neither winds the meridian a second time nor changes its longitude class.

This can also be read as a face-word statement. Each insertion replaces one original disk-face passage by two passages separated by the new triangular interface. In the final word all internal interface changes of coordinates occur in inverse consecutive pairs. After eliminating them, the retained word is the original disk meridian. Its ordered factors are one endpoint passage for each incident block, followed at the distinguished seam by the single original half-twist correction. This is a statement about the actual marked loop before evaluating its holonomy.

Use the local angular chart, with common oriented longitude variable τv\tau_v at all pages of this circle. Its incoming/outgoing edge-ratio tables give exactly the inverse seam maps with the just-established puncture matching. The longitude conjugates to the same element on all pages. Whenever it is loxodromic, its two fixed points are therefore carried to the same two marked points. 7. From the cut to the geometric filling

This proof applies to the actual ordered circle-only tree, with all its inherited frames and disk collars. It does not assert such a cut for an arbitrary scalar tensor network, for arbitrary branching of six independent boundary slots, or for a graph with cycles. It uses neither commutativity of pair-algebra polynomials nor equality of source actions. No auxiliary component is filled or deleted in the topological identification.

The explicit terminal substitution (5), its ribbon-face realization (7), and the primitive path concatenation supply the ordered topological identification. The local angular charts give the positive cells and their full complex seam ratios. The analytic criterion constructs the charge path, and the geometric completion proves that its endpoint is a smooth complete metric on the stated filled topology.
