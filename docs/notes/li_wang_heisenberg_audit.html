An independent audit of the Li–Wang regulator line

Research audit, 26 October 2026. The source is Weiping Li and Qingxue Wang, On the Generalized Volume Conjecture and Regulator, arXiv:math/0610745v1, the nine-page 2006 version. Its version-specific HTML was checked against the PDF. The earlier math/0604057 is not needed here. This is a correctness and terminology audit, not a claim about novelty or a proof of colored-Jones asymptotics.

The core flat regulator line is valid with logarithms continued along paths. The paper does not construct our root-of-unity quantum norm torsor. There are two distinct branch qualifications: a literal use of the printed discontinuous logarithm fails, while an endpoint-only scalar reading of its later special Chern–Simons integral requires additional trivialization data. Neither qualification disproves the continued-log regulator.

1. What the source actually supplies

The terminology is a flat line bundle: Introduction, p. 1; §3.2, equation (3.6), pp. 4–5; and Remark 2(iii), p. 7. The universal Heisenberg bundle’s explicit construction is referred to Bloch and Ramakrishnan, rather than written out. The inspected text contains neither “torsor” nor “principal.” Proposition 3.2, p. 5, supplies a K2-torsion assertion; Theorem 3.3, p. 6, deduces exactness and rational periods. Equation (3.9), p. 7, remains a conjecture. Primary text, §§3.2–3.3.

Removing the zero section of a complex line gives an analytic principal C* bundle. Calling that associated object a torsor is mathematically legitimate. It is a translation of the line-bundle construction, not a separate scalar norm equation or an identification with a quantum object. The calculations below independently check the relevant construction.

2. Rebuild the logarithmic quotient and its connection

Set t=2πi. On the logarithmic cover of (C*)² write f=exp(F), g=exp(G). The following deck transformations define an analytic line:

D_(a,b)(F,G,z)=(F+t a,G+t b,exp(aG) z),
a,b in Z.                                           (2.1)

Indeed, applying D_(a,b) and then D_(c,d) gives the product multiplier exp((a+c)G+t cb)=exp((a+c)G). Thus the action is an actual action. Local branches F_j=F_i+t a_ij, G_j=G_i+t b_ij give local fiber-coordinate transitions z_j=exp(a_ij G_i)z_i; the same calculation checks their cocycle.

Declare

A=F dG/t,        nabla=d-A.                          (2.2)

Then A changes by a dG, the logarithmic differential of exp(aG). Consequently (2.2) descends. In this declared convention its curvature is

F_nabla=-dA=-dF wedge dG/t.                          (2.3)

On a smooth complex curve with f,g invertible, dF and dG are locally holomorphic multiples of the same local parameter differential. Their wedge is zero. The pullback connection is therefore flat. No K2 torsion is needed for this flatness.

Lift a based loop γ starting with logarithms F0,G0. Write its endpoint as (F0+t a,G0+t b). Parallel transport in (2.2) multiplies z by exp(∫γ F dG/t). Comparing the endpoint fiber to the initial fiber requires exp(−aG0), by (2.1). The holonomy is exactly

r(f,g)(γ)=exp((∫γ F dG−G0 ∫γ dF)/t).                 (2.4)

This reproduces the source’s (3.6), with continued logarithms. Shifting F by t r and G0 by t s changes the numerator by t²(rb−sa), so the exponential is branch independent. Concatenation adds an extra t²ab’ to the numerator, also invisible after exponentiation. Flatness proves homotopy invariance; rank-one holonomy also gives the usual base-point independence under transport.

There is a normalization issue in the printed curvature statements, Proposition 3.1(1), p. 4, and Remark 2(iii), p. 7. With (2.4) fixed as holonomy, the actual connection curvature in convention (2.2) is (2.3), not the unnormalized dF wedge dG. The discrepancy is invisible after restriction to a curve, where both vanish. A nonzero universal curvature or Chern class must retain the connection convention and its factors of 2πi. We do not use the source’s unnormalized formula as such a universal Chern–Weil identity.

3. Verify that the holonomy descends through the K2 relations

Bilinearity follows directly from (2.4). For antisymmetry, the sum of the two numerators is

∫γ d(FG)−G0 ∫γ dF−F0 ∫γ dG=t²ab,

whose exponential after division by t is one.

The local tame-symbol calculation can also be done without a cited proof. At a puncture with parameter w, let

f=w^a u(w),  g=w^b v(w),  u(0)v(0)!=0.

On a small positive circle, continuous logarithms give the limiting numerator/t as bF0−aG0+tab/2. Its exponential is

(−1)^(ab) u(0)^b/v(0)^a.                            (3.1)

The radius-dependent powers cancel. Flatness on the punctured disc identifies this limit with the actual small-loop holonomy.

For the Steinberg relation, first use f=w,g=1−w on P1 minus {0,1,infinity}. The small-loop values at both 0 and 1 are one by (3.1). These loops generate its fundamental group, so the character is trivial. Pulling back along f proves r(f,1−f)=1 on its domain. Thus the homomorphism out of the symbol presentation of K2 is verified here, including its tame signs. One need not assume a published dilogarithm argument for these particular checks.

4. Exactly what torsion gives

Suppose q{l,m}=0 in the function-field K2. Then (2.4) has q-th power one. If the relation is initially realized after deleting extra points, the inclusion of that smaller punctured curve surjects on the fundamental group of the larger one; the already-defined character therefore has q-th power one on the source’s Y_h as well. Its image has order dividing q. Equality with the K2 order is not required and is not implied by applying a homomorphism.

The transfer arithmetic in Proposition 3.2 is correct: if restriction to a degree-n field extension kills 2{l,m}, then transfer back gives 2n{l,m}=0. This audit has not independently rederived, from a knot presentation, the existence of that finite extension or the cited order-two statement in it. Those are the geometric K2 inputs to the paper’s knot-wide application. They are explicitly kept separate from the directly verified regulator construction and its conditional consequences.

For those consequences write F=log l=x+iu and G=log m=y+iv, continuing u,v on the path. Integration by parts on a closed loop gives

Im(∫ F dG−G0 ∫ dF)=∫(x dv−y du),
Re(∫ F dG−G0 ∫ dF)=−∫(y dx+u dv)+v0∫du.           (4.1)

The boundary contribution in the first identity cancels the real part of G0 ∫dF. At the paper’s complete base point m0=1, choose G0=0. Finite-order holonomy then gives

∫γ (x dv−y du)=0,
(q/(4π²))∫γ(y dx+u dv) is an integer.              (4.2)

The first integrand is a globally defined closed real form; all its periods vanish, so it is exact. The second is a continued-branch integral. Changing the initial branch u by 2πk changes its normalized value by an integer multiple of q. This verifies the substantive quantization deduction under the K2-torsion hypothesis. It gives finite monodromy and a trivial q-th tensor power of the flat line, not a pointing of the original line and not a quantum asymptotic estimate.

5. A literal fixed-cut reading fails; the continued-log reading survives

Immediately after (3.6), p. 4, the source fixes 0≤arg z<2π and describes the discontinuity on the positive real axis. A literal pointwise insertion of this discontinuous log into the integral cannot define the asserted regulator. Continuous path logs, or equivalent transition contributions at cuts, are necessary.

An exact diagnostic uses the algebraic curve

l=−z, m=z, z in C*, base point z=1.                 (5.1)

Its symbol {−z,z} is zero: the Steinberg relation applied to z and z^(-1) gives {z,z}={z,−1}, hence {z,−z}=2{z,−1}=0. Thus its K2 order is q=1. On z=exp(iθ), 0≤θ≤2π, continuous logarithms give arg l=π+θ and log m0=0. Therefore

∫ log l dlog m=−∫_0^(2π)(π+θ)dθ=−4π²,
r(l,m)=1.                                         (5.2)

With pointwise 0≤arg l<2π, arg l instead jumps at θ=π. The same integral becomes

−∫_0^π(π+θ)dθ−∫_π^(2π)(θ−π)dθ=−2π²,
literal_fixed_cut_r(l,m)=−1.                       (5.3)

The corresponding normalized real period is 1/2 rather than an integer. This is a counterexample to the literal fixed-cut reading of the general construction and quantization inference. It is not a knot A-curve counterexample and does not contradict (2.4) or (4.2). Interpreting (3.6) using continued logs repairs this issue.

6. The later special invariant needs its path or endpoint frame

After Remark 2(iii), p. 7, the paper defines, for a path c from the complete base point to (l,m), the quantity

U_c=−q∫c(y dx+u dv).                               (6.1)

Remark 3, on the same page, describes U/(2π)² as well-defined in R/Z. The path is indeed present in the preceding definition. On that marked path domain (6.1) is a meaningful quantity. The assertion cannot be inferred as a single-valued function of an unmarked endpoint from the closed-loop statement (4.2) alone.

To see the obstruction, insert a based loop γ before c and let a=wind_γ(l). The l logarithm on the following copy of c has changed by 2πia. Consequently

U_(γ*c)−U_c=U_γ−2πq a∫c darg m,
(U_(γ*c)−U_c)/(4π²)
   =−q a Δ_c arg(m)/(2π) mod Z.                   (6.2)

The right side is generally not integral. For (5.1), let c_θ run from angle 0 to θ with continuous arguments. Direct integration gives

U_(c_θ)=−πθ−θ²/2,
U_(c_(θ+2π))−U_(c_θ)=−4π²−2πθ.                    (6.3)

At θ=π/2 the difference divided by 4π² is −5/4. Thus even the valid continued-log interpretation does not make this integral a function of the unmarked endpoint on a general torsion-symbol curve. The same obstruction (6.2) applies on any curve possessing the stated winding loop and a path with the indicated nonintegral endpoint phase; we do not assert a specific knot example from (5.1).

There is a precise section interpretation. Put

rho=−y dx−u dv+i(x dv−y du)
   =F dG−d(yF).                                   (6.4)

Then d rho=dF wedge dG. Under u→u+2πa it changes by −2πa dv. Thus rho is a local connection potential, not a single global scalar form. The smooth gauge z_tilde=exp(−yF/t)z changes (2.2) to rho/t and has deck multiplier exp(i a v). Its q-th tensor power retains exp(iq a v), exactly the endpoint phase missing from an endpoint-only reading of (6.1). A path-cover value or a local coordinate of a bundle section is consistent. A chosen global endpoint frame, or explicit cut corrections, is additional data. The vanishing of the exterior derivative in Remark 2(iii) does not itself supply it.

7. Keep the categories and our normalization separate

The universal object in (2.1) is analytic. Its nonzero vectors form an analytic principal C* bundle. Its restriction to a curve with (2.2) is a flat bundle; the sheaf of horizontal vectors is a rank-one local system. H1 with constant C* coefficients classifies these flat objects, rather than just their underlying holomorphic lines.

The universal analytic bundle is not an algebraic G_m torsor on (G_m)². Integrating (2.3)/(2πi) on the real two-torus gives −1 in our declared convention, so the underlying topological line is nontrivial. In contrast C[f(±1),g(±1)] is a factorial regular ring, and its algebraic Picard group is zero. The universal line therefore cannot be the analytification of an algebraic line on that torus. This obstruction concerns the universal torus bundle, not every restriction of it. In particular a finite-monodromy restriction can be studied with a finite-cover algebraic model, but that is further category/comparison information, not the displayed analytic quotient.

The associated Heisenberg group can be written

(F,G,H)*(F',G',H')
  =(F+F',G+G',H+H'+F G').                         (7.1)

Its center is additive and the quotient is the additive (F,G) plane. Left multiplication by (ta,tb,t²c) sends H to H+taG+t²c. Exponentiating H/t gives precisely (2.1); dH−F dG is invariant. The noncommutative central extension, its integral lattice quotient, and the resulting analytic C* bundle are related constructions, not interchangeable names for one algebraic G_m torsor over the A-curve.

There is also a factor of two in a direct comparison with our boundary line. On a common torus with U=log m,V=log l, the regulator potential is V dU/t. The gauge z_sym=exp(−UV/(2t))z changes it to

A_sym=(V dU−U dV)/(2t),
z_sym'=(−1)^(ab)exp((bU−aV)/2)z_sym.               (7.2)

Squaring removes the sign and gives exactly our deck multiplier exp(bU−aV) and potential (V dU−U dV)/t. Thus, on the same torus and with these conventions, our boundary line is the square of the symmetrized r(l,m) line. Matching the actual papers additionally requires transporting squared eigenvalues and complete-point shifts; the literal variable names are not that transport. Reversing (l,m) dualizes the regulator. These constants should not be hidden inside a generic statement that the two lines are the same.

Our common period space has an explicit algebraic unipotent connection and further exponential periods. Nothing here identifies their selected periods with the knot state. Our quantum norm construction instead begins with a specialized q-difference module, a finite etale cyclic algebra S/k, and N_(S/k)(a)=v. That solution variety is a torsor under the norm-one torus, while its scalar intertwiners form a separate G_m torsor. Neither follows from (2.4) or from calling a line’s nonzero vectors a torsor.

8. Reproducible scope

The exact audit script checks the deck cocycle, connection transformation, curvature constant, antisymmetry, branch shifts, Heisenberg horizontal form, local secondary-potential transformation, factor-of-two comparison, and both explicit branch diagnostics. Recorded results.

python3 experiments/audit_li_wang_heisenberg.py

The verified result is the analytic regulator line, its continued-log holonomy, and the quantization deduction conditional on K2 torsion. The knot K2 input has not been independently reproved here. The endpoint-only reading of the later special invariant needs correction or additional marking. No quantum norm-torsor comparison or volume conjecture is proved by this audit.
