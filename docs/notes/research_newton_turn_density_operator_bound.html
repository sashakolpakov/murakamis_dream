A polynomial Newton operator bound for the original turn-trace density

Written proof, 24 November 2026. Earlier finals and public manuscripts are unchanged.

The original diagonal turn kernel provides a canonical weight for the full-primary compression of the genuine turn. Every selected compression is invertible, with inverse norm at most m(N+2)m(N+2) when mm distinct Casimir nodes are retained. This holds for every cluster size and includes both directions at each double root.

For the whole algebra it gives a polynomial operator bound for the original turn-trace density. Its known small nodal weights and indefinite signature remain; no positive scalar closing or geometric lower bound is inferred.

1. Original algebra, turn and density

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and η=(−1)d\eta=(-1)^d. Use the full original algebra and normalized Newton basis [A,T]: 𝒜N=ℂ[z]/PN(z),PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N), {\mathcal{ A}}_N=\mathbb C[z]/P_N(z),\qquad P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2, \qquad c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N), S0=1,Sr=∏h=1r2sin⁡(πh/N),br(z)=Sr−2∏j<r(cj−z),0≤r≤d.(1) S_0=1,\qquad S_r=\prod_{h=1}^r2\sin(\pi h/N),\qquad b_r(z)=S_r^{-2}\prod_{j<r}(c_j-z),\quad 0\le r\le d. \qquad\text{(1)} Here TNT_N is Chebyshev of the first kind. Newton coefficients carry their usual positive Hermitian norm. The basis polynomials are real. All first normals at double roots remain independent. The original turn and cylindrical closing satisfy 𝒯br=bd−r,𝒯*=𝒯=𝒯−1,ϕ(f)=ηN2[zd]rem⁡PNf,ϕ(brbs)=δr+s,d.(2) {\mathcal{ T}}b_r=b_{d-r},\quad {\mathcal{ T}}^*={\mathcal{ T}}={\mathcal{ T}}^{-1}, \qquad \phi(f)=\eta N^2[z^d]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}f, \qquad \phi(b_rb_s)=\delta_{r+s,d}. \qquad\text{(2)} Thus ϕ(fg)=f𝖳𝒯g\phi(fg)=f^{\mathsf T}{\mathcal{ T}}g on coefficient vectors. The other original row is λ0=ϕ𝒯\lambda_0=\phi{\mathcal{ T}}. No ordinary cap is substituted for the prescribed mark.

Write Z=MzZ=M_z. The Newton product recurrence gives Zbr=crbr−4sin⁡2(π(r+1)/N)br+1(r<d),Zbd=cdbd,∥Z∥≤6.(3) Zb_r=c_rb_r-4\sin^2(\pi(r+1)/N)b_{r+1}\quad(r<d), \qquad Zb_d=c_db_d,\qquad \|Z\|\le6. \qquad\text{(3)} The faithful full minimal polynomial is PNP_N.

Define the literal diagonal kernel and its canonical quotient inverse: ℬN(z)=∑r=0dbr(z)2,𝔟N=ℬN−1in 𝒜N.(4) {\mathcal{ B}}_N(z)=\sum_{r=0}^d b_r(z)^2,\qquad \mathfrak b_N={\mathcal{ B}}_N^{-1}\ \text{in }{\mathcal{ A}}_N. \qquad\text{(4)} Since ℬN(x)≥1{\mathcal{ B}}_N(x)\ge1 on the real axis, it is a unit in the full repeated-root algebra. The inverse retains its prescribed first derivatives, not only its distinct-root values. The existing turn-trace theorem [T] proves the exact original-source identity ϕ(f)=Tr⁡(𝒟NMf),𝒟N=𝒯M𝔟N.(5) \phi(f)=\operatorname{Tr}({\mathcal{ D}}_N M_f),\qquad {\mathcal{ D}}_N={\mathcal{ T}}M_{\mathfrak b_N}. \qquad\text{(5)} The trace acts on the NN-dimensional Newton algebra. All original framing and external factors remain attached to the original source when (5) is used.

2. Full-primary Newton frames and their source normalization

The distinct double nodes are indexed by 0≤i≤⌊(N−2)/2⌋0\le i\le\lfloor(N-2)/2\rfloor. There is one additional simple node −2-2 at odd levels. Let ℐi{\mathcal{ I}}_i be its full primary ideal, of dimension two or one respectively.

For a double node c=cic=c_i, put ai=η(4−c2),χi=c4−c2,vi=(br(c))r=0d,wi=(br′(c))r=0d, a_i=\eta(4-c^2),\qquad \chi_i=\frac{c}{4-c^2},\qquad v_i=(b_r(c))_{r=0}^d,\quad w_i=(b_r'(c))_{r=0}^d, Ai=∥vi∥2=ℬN(c),αi=vi𝖳wiAi,δi=∥wi−αivi∥Ai.(6) A_i=\|v_i\|^2={\mathcal{ B}}_N(c),\qquad \alpha_i=\frac{v_i^{\mathsf T}w_i}{A_i},\qquad \delta_i=\frac{\|w_i-\alpha_iv_i\|}{\sqrt{A_i}}. \qquad\text{(6)} These are real Newton coordinate vectors and a real positive normal scale, not magnetic Clebsch columns in a physical pair degree.

The local source coefficients can be checked directly. The Chebyshev equation is (4−z2)PN″−zPN′+N2(PN−2)=0. (4-z^2)P_N''-zP_N'+N^2(P_N-2)=0. For Qi=PN/(z−c)2Q_i=P_N/(z-c)^2, this equation and its derivative give Qi(c)=N24−c2,Qi′(c)Qi(c)=c4−c2. Q_i(c)=\frac{N^2}{4-c^2},\qquad \frac{Q_i'(c)}{Q_i(c)}=\frac{c}{4-c^2}. The original residue ηN2Res⁡(h/PN)\eta N^2\operatorname{Res}(h/P_N) at cc is therefore aih′(c)−ηch(c).(7) a_i h'(c)-\eta c\,h(c). \qquad\text{(7)} At the odd simple node, PN′(−2)=N2P_N'(-2)=N^2, so its residue is h(−2)h(-2).

Let pip_i be the full primary idempotent and ni=(z−ci)pin_i=(z-c_i)p_i. Testing (7) on an arbitrary original spectator, and using the nondegenerate pairing (2), proves ni=ai𝒯vi,pi=𝒯(−ηcivi+aiwi).(8) n_i=a_i{\mathcal{ T}}v_i,\qquad p_i={\mathcal{ T}}(-\eta c_i v_i+a_iw_i). \qquad\text{(8)} These identities prove that the following frame spans the entire actual primary ideal: Ei=(𝒯viAi,𝒯(wi−αivi)Aiδi),Ei𝖳Ei=I2,ZEi=Ei(ciδi−10ci).(9) E_i=\left(\frac{{\mathcal{ T}}v_i}{\sqrt{A_i}}, \frac{{\mathcal{ T}}(w_i-\alpha_iv_i)} {\sqrt{A_i}\delta_i}\right),\qquad E_i^{\mathsf T}E_i=I_2, \qquad ZE_i=E_i\begin{pmatrix}c_i&\delta_i^{-1}\\0&c_i\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(9)} In particular δi>0\delta_i>0. The first vector is the nilpotent eigenvector direction; the second retains the generalized direction. The Jordan identity follows from (Z−ci)pi=ni(Z-c_i)p_i=n_i, not from differentiating a root-only equality.

At the odd simple node define vi=(br(−2))rv_i=(b_r(-2))_r, Ai=∥vi∥2A_i=\|v_i\|^2, and Ei=𝒯vi/AiE_i={\mathcal{ T}}v_i/\sqrt{A_i}. Its primary idempotent is 𝒯vi{\mathcal{ T}}v_i. Distinct primary ideals have zero product, hence Ei𝖳𝒯Ej=0(i≠j).(10) E_i^{\mathsf T}{\mathcal{ T}}E_j=0 \qquad(i\ne j). \qquad\text{(10)} All frames are real, so their transpose here is also their Hermitian adjoint. This is full-primary Frobenius orthogonality, not Hermitian orthogonality between the primary ideals themselves.

Two existing kernel estimates control the scales in (6). Writing KN(z,w)=∑rbr(z)br(w)K_N(z,w)=\sum_r b_r(z)b_r(w), one has δi2=∂z∂wlogKN(z,w)|(ci,ci). \delta_i^2= \left.\partial_z\partial_w\log K_N(z,w)\right|_{(c_i,c_i)}. The all-color first-normal theorem [H, Theorem 1] gives 18≤δi≤γi,γi=N−2i−14sin⁡θi,θi=(2i+1)π/N.(11) \frac18\le\delta_i\le\gamma_i,\qquad \gamma_i=\frac{N-2i-1}{4\sin\theta_i},\qquad \theta_i=(2i+1)\pi/N. \qquad\text{(11)} The lower bound follows separately from (3),(9): δi−1≤∥Z−ci∥≤8\delta_i^{-1}\le\|Z-c_i\|\le8. The diagonal-kernel derivative bound [T, (12)] also gives −N2/8≤αi≤0,ℬN′(ci)=2Aiαi.(12) -N^2/8\le\alpha_i\le0,\qquad {\mathcal{ B}}_N'(c_i)=2A_i\alpha_i. \qquad\text{(12)}

3. Canonical weighted turn blocks

Formula (7) applied to (8),(9) yields Ei𝖳𝒯Ei=1aiAiδi(011(χi−2αi)/δi).(13) E_i^{\mathsf T}{\mathcal{ T}}E_i =\frac1{a_iA_i\delta_i} \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&(\chi_i-2\alpha_i)/\delta_i\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(13)} The original diagonal kernel, including its first normal (12), acts by MℬNEi=EiAi(12αi/δi01). M_{{\mathcal{ B}}_N}E_i =E_i A_i\begin{pmatrix}1&2\alpha_i/\delta_i\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. Consequently its shear cancels exactly: Ci:=Ei𝖳𝒯MℬNEi=1aiδi(011χi/δi),Ci−1=(−aiχiaiδiaiδi0).(14) C_i:=E_i^{\mathsf T}{\mathcal{ T}}M_{{\mathcal{ B}}_N}E_i =\frac1{a_i\delta_i} \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&\chi_i/\delta_i\end{pmatrix}, \qquad C_i^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-a_i\chi_i&a_i\delta_i\\ a_i\delta_i&0\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(14)} This uses the canonical source kernel ℬN{\mathcal{ B}}_N, not a scalar replacement of its jets. Omitting its derivative would leave the αi\alpha_i term in (13).

Now aiχi=ηcia_i\chi_i=\eta c_i, and (11) gives |aiδi|≤4sin⁡2θiγi=(N−2i−1)sin⁡θi≤N. |a_i\delta_i| \le4\sin^2\theta_i\,\gamma_i =(N-2i-1)\sin\theta_i\le N . Splitting the last matrix in (14) into its diagonal and off-diagonal parts proves the universal bound ∥Ci−1∥≤N+2.(15) \|C_i^{-1}\|\le N+2. \qquad\text{(15)} At the odd simple node the same weighted block is exactly Ci=1C_i=1. Cross blocks in (14) vanish for distinct nodes, since multiplication by ℬN{\mathcal{ B}}_N preserves each full primary ideal.

4. Every selected compression and the global density bound

Select any nonempty set JJ of mm distinct nodes, keeping each full primary ideal. Set ℐJ=⨁i∈Jℐi,V=(Ei)i∈J,G=V𝖳V>0,C=diag⁡i∈J(Ci). {\mathcal{ I}}_J=\bigoplus_{i\in J}{\mathcal{ I}}_i,\quad V=(E_i)_{i\in J},\quad G=V^{\mathsf T}V>0,\quad C=\operatorname{diag}_{i\in J}(C_i). Primary decomposition proves that VV has full column rank. Its individual blocks are orthonormal, so ∥V∥2≤m\|V\|^2\le m, without a lower Gram bound. Let PJ=VG−1V𝖳P_J=VG^{-1}V^{\mathsf T} be the Newton orthogonal projection onto this selected sum and define its measured weighted turn by HJ=PJ𝒯MℬN|ℐJ.(16) H_J=P_J{\mathcal{ T}}M_{{\mathcal{ B}}_N}|_{{\mathcal{ I}}_J}. \qquad\text{(16)} For every coefficient vector xx, HJ(Vx)=VG−1CxH_J(Vx)=VG^{-1}Cx. Hence the inverse on the selected sum is HJ−1=VC−1V𝖳|ℐJ,∥HJ−1∥≤m(N+2),∥HJu∥≥∥u∥m(N+2).(17) H_J^{-1}=VC^{-1}V^{\mathsf T}|_{{\mathcal{ I}}_J},\qquad \boxed{\quad \|H_J^{-1}\|\le m(N+2),\qquad \|H_Ju\|\ge\frac{\|u\|}{m(N+2)}.\quad} \qquad\text{(17)} Multiplying both displayed maps on VxVx verifies both inverse identities. This argument needs no sublinear restriction, no estimate on ∥G−1∥\|G^{-1}\|, and no ambient spectral-projector bound.

For all qN=⌈N/2⌉q_N=\lceil N/2\rceil distinct nodes, the selected sum is the whole algebra. Then PJ=IP_J=I, and (𝒯MℬN)−1=M𝔟N𝒯=Vdiag⁡(Ci−1)V𝖳. ({\mathcal{ T}}M_{{\mathcal{ B}}_N})^{-1} =M_{\mathfrak b_N}{\mathcal{ T}} =V\operatorname{diag}(C_i^{-1})V^{\mathsf T}. The order of these factors matters. The original density in (5) is 𝒟N=𝒯(𝒯MℬN)−1𝒯{\mathcal{ D}}_N={\mathcal{ T}}({\mathcal{ T}}M_{{\mathcal{ B}}_N})^{-1}{\mathcal{ T}}, not the inverse itself. Since the turn is unitary, ∥M𝔟N∥Newt→Newt=∥𝒟N∥≤⌈N/2⌉(N+2).(18) \boxed{\quad \|M_{\mathfrak b_N}\|_{{\mathrm{ Newt}}\to{\mathrm{ Newt}}} =\|{\mathcal{ D}}_N\| \le\lceil N/2\rceil(N+2). \quad} \qquad\text{(18)} This is an operator estimate on the entire original Newton algebra, including arbitrary coherent inputs and every first normal. It strengthens the earlier pointwise and real-boundary estimates for 𝔟N\mathfrak b_N; it does not bound its inverse polynomially.

For a fixed number rr of contracted algebra copies, [T, (9)] gives ϕ⊗r(F)=Tr⁡(𝒟N⊗rMF)\phi^{\otimes r}(F)=\operatorname{Tr}({\mathcal{ D}}_N^{\otimes r}M_F). Put CNdens=⌈N/2⌉(N+2)C_N^{\mathrm{ dens}}=\lceil N/2\rceil(N+2). For every operator error ΔA\Delta A on that full tensor algebra, with ∥A∥1=Tr⁡A*A\|A\|_1=\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{A^*A}, one has |Tr⁡(𝒟N⊗rΔA)|≤(CNdens)r∥ΔA∥1≤(CNdens)rNr∥ΔA∥op.(18a) |\operatorname{Tr}({\mathcal{ D}}_N^{\otimes r}\Delta A)| \le(C_N^{\mathrm{ dens}})^r\|\Delta A\|_1 \le(C_N^{\mathrm{ dens}})^rN^r\|\Delta A\|_{\mathrm{ op}}. \qquad\text{(18a)} This is polynomial absolute source error with the actual density retained in the leading trace, not a relative scalar estimate.

5. The original closing under the canonical half-density

The original cylindrical row on each paired frame is exactly ϕEi=Ai−1/2(1,−αi/δi).(19) \phi E_i=A_i^{-1/2}(1,-\alpha_i/\delta_i). \qquad\text{(19)} Indeed ϕ(𝒯vi)=b0(ci)=1\phi({\mathcal{ T}}v_i)=b_0(c_i)=1 and ϕ(𝒯wi)=b0′(ci)=0\phi({\mathcal{ T}}w_i)=b_0'(c_i)=0. At the odd simple node the row is Ai−1/2A_i^{-1/2}.

Let sNs_N be the real Hermite unit with sN2=ℬNs_N^2={\mathcal{ B}}_N and positive nodal values. Its full prescribed jets are sN(ci)=Ais_N(c_i)=\sqrt{A_i} and sN′(ci)=Aiαis_N'(c_i)=\sqrt{A_i}\alpha_i at double nodes; at the simple node only its value is prescribed. This is the inverse of the positive-jet square root of 𝔟N\mathfrak b_N used in [T]. Thus MsNEi=EiAi(1αi/δi01),ϕMsNEi=(1,0).(20) M_{s_N}E_i=E_i\sqrt{A_i} \begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha_i/\delta_i\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi M_{s_N}E_i=(1,0). \qquad\text{(20)} The simple block gives ϕMsNEi=1\phi M_{s_N}E_i=1. This identifies the original row in a specified full-normal frame. Its kernel remains: (20) is not a scalar lower bound for an arbitrary incoming packet. The unweighted source is restored with MsN−1M_{s_N}^{-1}; neither its action factor nor the original mark has been removed.

6. Scope and complete sources

The weight in (14)–(18) is the actual diagonal kernel from the original turn-trace source. A double block has negative determinant. Also 𝒟N{\mathcal{ D}}_N is real symmetric, invertible, and has trace ϕ(1)=0\phi(1)=0, hence is indefinite. The operator upper bound (18) does not turn its original trace pairing into a positive norm.

The original nodal weights 𝔟N(ci)\mathfrak b_N(c_i) can still be exponentially small at central nodes, as [T] proves. No lower bound for the smallest singular value of 𝒟N{\mathcal{ D}}_N, an arbitrary original closing, or geometric coefficient is inferred. Nor is inserting PJP_J into a closed source authorized by (17): its omitted component and subsequent exterior remain. Actual turns, pivots, crossing phases and prescribed source rows retain their original meanings.

The closest physical middle-weight turn theorem [M] already gives an exact kernel and positive transported boundary Gram. Its flat physical coordinates and positive-metric transport are different from the full-primary Newton compression considered here. There is no new interpolation or abstract compression method claimed. The new estimates are (17)–(18) for the canonical original-source density, with the explicit normal and closing calibrations above. No new knot-volume or finite-exception theorem is proved.
