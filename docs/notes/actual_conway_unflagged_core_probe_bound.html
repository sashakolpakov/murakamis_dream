An action-compatible positive probe for an unflagged Conway core

31 October 2026. Internally reviewed coefficient theorem. The actual coupons, independent colors and normal coefficients remain in the source. Positive arrays introduced below give upper comparisons; they do not replace a physical leaf or add a circle to the invariant.

The theorem bounds the full-Hermite norm of an unflagged component at its intrinsic geometric pressure. It also proves strict losses for high input or output coordinates and transfers a local coefficient loss in any descendant. The analytic propagation theorem turns these estimates into spectral Gaussian bounds. No filling theorem is assembled here.

1. The component and the coefficient hypotheses

Use the full algebra, positive Newton multiplication and turn from the reduced-hub source theorem. Write er=B̂re_r=\widehat B_r, d=N−1d=N-1, and M=⌊d/2⌋M=\lfloor d/2\rfloor. Define wN,r={er(−2),0≤r≤M,1,M<r≤d,WN=∑r=0dwN,rer. w_{N,r}= \begin{cases} e_r(-2),&0\le r\le M,\\ 1,&M<r\le d, \end{cases} \qquad W_N=\sum_{r=0}^{d}w_{N,r}e_r. \tag{1} All weights are positive. The actual full-jet norm satisfies ∥er∥𝒥≤CNCwN,r, \|e_r\|_{\mathcal J}\le CN^Cw_{N,r}, \tag{2} uniformly at both parities, by the low-degree interpolation and high-normal estimates, §§2–3. Moreover, log⁡wN,r=Ng(r/N)+O(log⁡N)(0≤r≤M) \log w_{N,r}=Ng(r/N)+O(\log N)\qquad(0\le r\le M) \tag{3} uniformly on the closed low range. The high weight has zero logarithmic rate. The comparison coupon WNW_N is never inserted into the invariant.

Let AA be the fixed product-rooted expression of a maximal connected component of unflagged hubs. Its external children are n≥2n\ge2 type-LL modules. Its internal edges carry TT, and each product hub has valence at least three. The reduced-hub theorem places AA in the completed grammar E::=LL∣T(E)∣EE∣LE∣EL. E\ ::=\ LL\ \mid\ T(E)\ \mid\ EE\ \mid\ LE\ \mid\ EL. \tag{4} Each external occurrence is a distinct original module. Write its actual coefficient array as Fi,N=∑r=0dfi,N,rer,1≤i≤n. Assume fixed real constants cic_i and one error eN→0e_N\to0, uniform over the specified physical input parameters, such that |fi,N,r|≤{exp⁡{N[ci+g(r/N)+eN]},r≤M,exp⁡{N[ci+eN]},r>M. |f_{i,N,r}|\le \begin{cases} \exp\{N[c_i+g(r/N)+e_N]\},&r\le M,\\ \exp\{N[c_i+e_N]\},&r>M. \end{cases} \tag{5} These are coefficients of the whole module after its actual operations. The split at MM is a low/high coordinate split. It is not identified with that module’s physical core/alias decomposition.

Let AN=A(F1,N,…,Fn,N)A_N=A(F_{1,N},\ldots,F_{n,N}) and QN=T(AN)Q_N=T(A_N). Let A0A^0 denote the same expression with each atomic input replaced by the pure complete physical hN=T(LN)h_N=T(L_N). For n=2n=2 it is C(hhh)C(hhh); for n≥3n\ge3 its source reduces to the completed/completed-root grammar by the exact flag-free adapter. Using this reference does not modify ANA_N.

2. An exact full-normal probe inequality

Replace every input coefficient by its modulus and compile ANabsA_N^{\mathrm{abs}} from these nonnegative arrays using the same products and turns. The positive Newton product and exact index reversal imply coefficientwise |[er]AN|≤[er]ANabs. |[e_r]A_N|\le [e_r]A_N^{\mathrm{abs}}. \tag{7} No norm estimate for a turn enters (7). By the full pairing, [er]T(AN)=ϕ(erAN). [e_r]T(A_N)=\phi(e_rA_N). All basis products and the marked coefficient functional have nonnegative coefficients in this calculation. Therefore ∥T(AN)∥𝒥≤CNC∑r=0dwN,r|ϕ(erAN)|≤CNCϕ(WNANabs). \begin{aligned} \|T(A_N)\|_{\mathcal J} &\le CN^C\sum_{r=0}^{d}w_{N,r}|\phi(e_rA_N)|\\ &\le CN^C\phi(W_NA_N^{\mathrm{abs}}). \end{aligned}\tag{8} Every first normal is retained, including high Newton coordinates invisible at all nodes. The last scalar is nonnegative and its finite source has only polynomially many index states, because the component is fixed.

The same estimate holds sectorwise. Split each input into r≤Mr\le M and r>Mr>M arrays, and split WNW_N likewise. For each specified input band assignment and probe band, the positive closed contraction bounds the corresponding output contribution. In particular the high output part of T(AN)T(A_N) is bounded using the high probe WNhiW_N^{\mathrm{hi}}.

3. Why the block inequalities apply to arbitrary arrays

Retain the profiles J(u)=∫0ulog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt,g(x)=J(2x)−4J(x),g̃(x)=g(max⁡(x,1/4))(0≤x≤1/2),k(x)=−2J(x/2)(0≤x≤1). \begin{gathered} J(u)=\int_0^u\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt,\qquad g(x)=J(2x)-4J(x),\\ \widetilde g(x)=g(\max(x,1/4))\quad(0\le x\le1/2),\\ k(x)=-2J(x/2)\quad(0\le x\le1). \end{gathered} On these domains g≤g̃g\le\widetilde g and k≥0k\ge0. The input bounds (5) and probe (3) satisfy the earlier leaf upper hypotheses, with the constants cic_i added in every sector: low coordinates:rate≤g≤g̃,r/N≤1/2,high coordinates:rate≤0≤k. \begin{array}{ll} \text{low coordinates:}&\text{rate}\le g\le\widetilde g,\quad r/N\le1/2,\\ \text{high coordinates:}&\text{rate}\le0\le k. \end{array} \tag{9} The high coordinate range is a subset of the unrestricted range allowed by the earlier alias inequalities. No physical alias identity or complementary-color relation is needed for this comparison.

The precise input from those proofs is a scalar variational inequality. For a fixed closed flag-free expression, put g̃\widetilde g at each chosen low leaf, kk at each chosen high leaf, and the actual concave gate action m(x,y,z)=J(x+y)+J(z)−J(x)−J(y)−J(z−x)−J(z−y)−J(x+y−z) \begin{aligned} m(x,y,z)={}&J(x+y)+J(z)-J(x)-J(y)\\ &-J(z-x)-J(z-y)-J(x+y-z) \end{aligned} on its original domain D={x,y≥0:max⁡(x,y)≤z≤x+y≤1}. D=\{x,y\ge0:\max(x,y)\le z\le x+y\le1\}. For every nonempty chosen high subset, the supremum of the action is strictly below the pure complete action. The proof uses only the displayed profiles and domains:

  • In an A2A_2 seed, the uniform block inequalities bound the ACAC and AAAA envelopes by its complete profile tangent minus 1/1001/100. They allow the alias coordinate over its whole interval and thus also cover the present high band.
  • At a single attachment, the exact square certificate and the remaining leaf tangent bound k+mk+m against the child’s profile tangent with a strict loss. A child loss passes through the pure g̃+m\widetilde g+m step.
  • Turns reverse the output index and tangent sign. Products add the concave gate tangent. At the closed root all free linear terms cancel.
  • The isolated three-leaf case uses its separate endpoint A2A_2 regrouping and three-alias bound. It covers n=2n=2 here with the same pair-sum and total-sum constraints.

These statements are proved in the block source argument, §§3–5; uniform two-leaf inequality; single-leaf source induction, §§6–7, and its analytic companion; and the isolated three-leaf proof, §§1–3.

Their supporting-plane inequalities do not require the leaf arrays to attain their upper profiles or to arise from a particular physical color. The finite sums are bounded after taking coefficient magnitudes. They therefore give the same upper bounds for arbitrary arrays satisfying (5). The gap is not inferred by comparing two actual source values.

If C(WNANabs)C(W_NA_N^{\mathrm{abs}}) was not initially parenthesized in the completed-root grammar, apply the exact flag-free rerooting to each fixed band assignment. Every external array and its band remain intact. All intermediate kernels remain positive. The complete pure action is unchanged by the same exact source identity and its proved coefficient asymptotics. The isolated case with three effective leaves is handled separately as above.

Finite support still requires i+j≤di+j\le d; larger input sums are zero. Turn and closing relations retain d/N=1−1/Nd/N=1-1/N. Uniform factorial rates have O(log⁡N)O(\log N) errors on the whole closed support. The supporting-plane proof costs only O(1)O(1) under those affine shifts; compact continuity and uniform closed rates give the same limiting bound. The single uniform eNe_N, the fixed number of input arrays, and the polynomial number of states are absorbed by reducing a fixed positive gap.

4. The norm bound and strict high-sector loss

There is γA>0\gamma_A>0, depending on the fixed component but not its physical input parameters, such that ϕ(WNANabs)≤exp⁡{N[Ω+o(1)]}, \phi(W_NA_N^{\mathrm{abs}}) \le \exp\{N[\Omega+o(1)]\}, \tag{10} and every sector with at least one high input or a high probe satisfies positive sector≤exp⁡{N(Ω−γA)} \text{positive sector}\le \exp\{N(\Omega-\gamma_A)\} \tag{11} for all sufficiently large NN. Section 3 proves the nonempty high cases; the all-low sector uses the original pure complete maximum. Consequently ∥QN∥𝒥≤exp⁡{N[Ω+o(1)]}, \|Q_N\|_{\mathcal J}\le\exp\{N[\Omega+o(1)]\}, \tag{12} and, for a possibly smaller γA′>0\gamma_A'>0, ∥(QN)hi∥𝒥≤exp⁡{N(Ω−γA′)}. \|(Q_N)^{\mathrm{hi}}\|_{\mathcal J} \le\exp\{N(\Omega-\gamma_A')\}. \tag{13} Every contribution containing a high input satisfies the same strict bound. The superscript in (13) denotes the actual output Newton coordinates r>Mr>M, including their normals.

This is sharp as an action budget, not a lower statement for every arbitrary input array. For reference arrays eNcihNe^{Nc_i}h_N, the low probe is coefficientwise comparable to hNh_N within powers of NN. Both positive coefficient families have the uniform closed rate gg with O(log⁡N)O(\log N) error, so N−ChN≤WNlo≤NChN N^{-C}h_N\le W_N^{\mathrm{lo}}\le N^Ch_N coefficientwise. Positivity gives the same exponential rate ΦA\Phi_A for the reference low-probe contraction. The high sectors already have a strict loss. Thus Ω\Omega is the pure reference action, rather than the generic bare-turn cost 2G02G_0 added to an existing action.

5. Clean-state localization and local loss transfer

Let yy denote the original primitive variables of C(hA0)C(hA^0), including the scaled probe index u=r/Nu=r/N and the nn effective input indices aia_i. The all-low comparison has the uniform closed upper action ∑ici+g(u)+∑ig(ai)+∑retained gatesm. \sum_i c_i+g(u)+\sum_i g(a_i) +\sum_{\text{retained gates}}m. \tag{14} This is the genuine pure complete action of that expression. The strict-state/compiler theorem gives a unique strict optimizer y*y_*. Its probe index β\beta and effective input indices ai*a_i^* lie in (1/4,1/2)(1/4,1/2); in the isolated three-leaf case they are arctan⁡(2)/π\arctan(2)/\pi. The strict gate and angle Hessian gives local strict curvature. Hence for each fixed neighborhood UU of y*y_*, there is γU>0\gamma_U>0 such that all-low positive sum outside U≤exp⁡{N(Ω−γU)}. \text{all-low positive sum outside }U \le\exp\{N(\Omega-\gamma_U)\}. \tag{15} The effective parameter range is checked in the intrinsic probe geometry, §§1–3. Estimate (15) is uniform under (5), since only upper profiles are used. It is stated in the original resolution and coordinates of this component, even if another rooting was used in §3 for the high-sector bound. No transformed state index is identified with an original physical color.

Only a compact regular low-index window around β\beta can therefore attain Ω\Omega; the effective input indices lie in compact regular windows around their own ai*a_i^*. This gives an explicit coefficient loss-transfer statement. Suppose one fixed compact interval IiI_i contains ai*a_i^* in its interior, and for a specified physical parameter subfamily, |fi,N,r|≤exp⁡{N[ci+g(r/N)−δ+eN]}when r/N∈Ii,δ>0. |f_{i,N,r}| \le\exp\{N[c_i+g(r/N)-\delta+e_N]\} \quad\text{when }r/N\in I_i,\qquad \delta>0. \tag{16} Choose UU so its ii-th input coordinate lies in IiI_i. The clean sum inside UU loses δ\delta, its outside loses γU\gamma_U, and every high sector loses γA\gamma_A. By (8), after reducing the smallest margin, ∥QN∥𝒥≤exp⁡{N(Ω−η)},η>0, \|Q_N\|_{\mathcal J}\le\exp\{N(\Omega-\eta)\}, \qquad \eta>0, \tag{17} uniformly over that subfamily for all sufficiently large NN. Only coefficient upper bounds enter this proof. It does not infer a physical-color gap from positivity of the signed source or convergence of derivatives.

The regular windows and the same finite comparison are also inputs to canonical spectral localization. That analytic step is separate from the finite norm and loss-transfer estimates proved here.

6. Geometric pressure and the exact component interface

Suppose each boundary module has the rigid positive profile fi(b)=g(b)+cif_i(b)=g(b)+c_i. Independence of its internal optimization at a fixed boundary label gives fA(b)=fA0(b)+∑ici,fQ(b)=fA(1−b),Q=T(A). f_A(b)=f_{A^0}(b)+\sum_i c_i,\qquad f_Q(b)=f_A(1-b),\qquad Q=T(A). Its intrinsic X=1X=1 pressure is ΩQ=max0≤b≤1/2[g(b)+fQ(b)]=∑ici+ΦA=Ω. \Omega_Q=\max_{0\le b\le1/2}[g(b)+f_Q(b)] =\sum_i c_i+\Phi_A=\Omega. \tag{18} This is the pressure entering a rigid spine when QQ is a satellite. The probe in (8) estimates that boundary pressure; it has not added a physical crossing circle. The absolute constants and pressure identification use the independently proved intrinsic probe adapter, §4, the rigid-profile geometry, and complete-mask geometry.

Every maximal unflagged component of a strict reduced tree has only type-LL modules or ordinary leaves on its boundary: adjacent flagged hubs send type-LL messages, and the remaining original leaf flags are zero. Its parent-facing root has at least two other branches, so n≥2n\ge2. This is the component scope needed for alternating flagged and unflagged reduction.

The result does not turn the comparison bands into physical aliases, assume positivity of actual signed modules, or prove a local packet. No source equality is promoted to tangle isotopy.
