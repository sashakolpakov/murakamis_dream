Framing and the exact scalar in the main-meridian profile

1 November 2026.

Moving the main meridian changes the physical crossing matrices, the maps that bend strands, and the scalar used to correct the diagram to zero framing. These changes must be kept together. A factor that is harmless for the exponential volume rate can still contribute to the first correction of order 1/N1/N.

This note computes the scalar retained in the filled main-meridian profile. The answer is a single quadratic phase. Its exponent is determined by the actual crossing powers and the oriented writhe of the diagram. The proof uses the physical duality maps and crossing normalization; the preferred cusp shape is identified by a separate geometric calculation.

1. Statement and convention

Fix an oriented Conway knot diagram DD, built from ordered products, physical quarter-turns and two-strand crossing boxes. The order of its endpoints and its original reduced cut are retained. A product stacks compatible two-strand operators; a quarter-turn bends one incoming strand into an outgoing strand and one outgoing strand into an incoming strand.

The crossing boxes contain the integral words RniR^{n_i}. Here nin_i is the signed exponent of the actual checked crossing: the crossing matrix followed by the interchange of its two tensor factors. For example, if another diagram convention calls a pretzel integer aia_i but inserts R−aiR^{-a_i}, then ni=−ain_i=-a_i in the formulas below. Let w(D)w(D) be the oriented writhe, the sum of the oriented crossing signs in the one-component diagram. It is the linking number of the knot with its blackboard parallel. Planar joining arcs and quarter-turns add no crossings or curls. Any additional framing curl must be included in both the physical word and its writhe.

Put N≥2,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,d=N−1,α=d+s,M=−qqα=qs. It is the full modified-trace invariant divided by its modified dimension. The coordinate MM in (1) is the marked meridian; s=0s=0 is its complete value M=1M=1. 2. Closing, the mark, and the absence of another monomial

6. The moving profile and the preferred cusp

The scalar identity above requires no strict geometric saddle or long-twist assumption. To apply it to an asymptotic, impose the hypotheses of the filled main-meridian theorem, Section 1.

The general filled-meridian theorem uses this exact scalar for every fixed strict Conway knot presentation with independently sufficiently long signed twists. Its analytic proof now permits arbitrary bare components. The scalar identity itself follows from the finite crossing and duality calculation above.

For this class there is one presentation-dependent threshold BB such that the theorem holds for all fixed signed crossing powers ni=ϵi−2pin_i=\epsilon_i-2p_i with |ni|≥B|n_i|\ge B. The signs and magnitudes are independent; no ratio between the magnitudes is bounded. They are fixed before NN tends to infinity. The threshold is independent of the subsequent fixed disk 0≤R<10\le R<1. Both even and odd levels are included.

Substitution of (4) into that theorem’s second-order insertion formula gives FN(qs)FN(1)=HN(s)qa(D)s2/2(1−πs2N∑ecot(πxefill)+O(N−2)). \frac{F_N(q^s)}{F_N(1)} =H_N(s)q^{a(D)s^2/2} \left(1-\frac{\pi s^2}{N} \sum_e\cot(\pi x_e^{\mathrm{fill}}) +O(N^{-2})\right). \tag{27} All estimates are uniform on a fixed disk |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1. 7. Scope of the calculation

The exact scalar calculation uses the ribbon twist, actual duality and marked cut, six-type turns, mixed monodromy and the full shifted-grid crossing identity. The latter is derived in detail in the regular circle trace, Sections 1–3, and the direct crossing trace. The argument is finite-level algebra and applies at both parities.

Changing coherent reference maps can redistribute scalar phases among edges and odd vertices. Compatible composition and the actual closing cut preserve their total. Changing the framing changes the recorded twist factor. Changing the reduced cut normalization changes the invariant, so that change must be stated separately.

The complete-value volume theorem has broader coverage. In particular the strict-tree theorem establishes the long-twist volume equality at M=1M=1 without the additional product-junction restriction used by the moving theorem. The profile and first correction in Section 6 are asserted only for the moving theorem’s stated class. Exact knowledge of a framing factor does not supply the remaining estimates for other trees.

The geometric comparison in (31) uses the preferred peripheral curves of the same knot. It does not identify a classical geometric factor of a quantum annihilator, nor does it give a profile on a fixed nonshrinking disk in MM. The microscopic coordinate and the exclusion of ordinary resonances remain part of the statement.

8. Reproducing the finite identities

The exact scalar script starts from the two mixed positive crossings, obtains the inverse crossings, and composes signed words with their actual starting and ending orders. It also derives the integer grading from the six turn phases, checks compatible products and turns on finite expression trees, and compares the shifted-grid exponents as rational polynomials. Its recorded results list the inputs and the scope of the checks.

To run the scalar checks together with the geometric matrix identities, use the repository runner:

python3 repro/run_research_checks.py meridian-cusp

The scalar script uses Python’s standard library; the geometric script also requires SymPy. These computations check local algebra. The physical tensor formulas, the longitude’s topology, and the uniform asymptotic estimates are supplied by the proofs, rather than by a finite list of examples.
