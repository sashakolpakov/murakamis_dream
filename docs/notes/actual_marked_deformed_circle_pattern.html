The actual marked line-circle pattern near the complete source

30 October 2026. This note reconstructs a local analytic circle pattern on the ACTUAL labeled white-face sphere, including its two marked line faces. Its radii agree with the stationary limiting face action. It also retains the selected fixed-point germs of the local product of physical meridians at the degenerate marked triangles. The ordered auxiliary meridian and full regulator comparison remain separate until their actual collar words are calculated.

Follow-up: the primitive collar calculation and global geometric assembly now supply those steps. The full reflected-color filling estimate remains outside these geometric theorems.

1. Actual incidence and a finite reconstruction system

Use the labeled convex ideal right-angled certificate and original main mark of the matching geometry. The white-normal construction, section 2, gives a SIMPLICIAL triangulated sphere C. Its vertices are the R+2 actual white faces, and its 2R triangles are the shaded source vertices. Each triangle has two main edges and one matching edge. The two marked white faces u,v share the original MAIN edge. These are incidence hypotheses already supplied by this certificate; no arbitrary routed graph or combinatorial repair is introduced.

Make one common orientation-preserving normalization of the complete circle picture. The outward marked disks are the halfplanes Y<=0 and Y>=1. Their common ideal point is infinity. All other outward disks are finite disjoint circles inside the strip, with positive radii. Keep their original labels and choose one finite center’s horizontal coordinate as the last translation normalization.

For each finite face f use unknowns (X_f,Y_f,r_f). Let delta_i be one small complex parameter for each actual matching edge. On a main edge put I_e=1, and on matching edge i put I_e=cos(2pi delta_i). The equations are, with the ACTUAL signed line side,

(X_f-X_g)^2+(Y_f-Y_g)^2
                     =r_f^2+r_g^2+2I_e r_f r_g,       (1a)
Y_f=r_f I_e                 on the lower marked line, (1b)
1-Y_f=r_f I_e               on the upper marked line. (1c)

The marked line-line edge is automatic and has no equation. Fix the chosen X-coordinate at its complete value. There are 3R-1 remaining variables and 3R-1 equations, since C has 3R edges. These are holomorphic polynomial/trigonometric equations; their real version is the usual signed circle inversive-distance equation. Complexification uses X,Y,r as independent holomorphic coordinates, not complex conjugation.

The Jacobian in these variables is invertible at delta=0. Consequently (1) has a unique nearby holomorphic solution for all delta in one fixed complex polydisc. It is real for real delta and even separately in each delta_i. Radii remain nonzero. This assertion is local for each fixed certified diagram, at arbitrary fixed bridge width.

2. Proof of the full Jacobian rank, including the line faces

Consider an infinitesimal kernel vector of (1) at delta=0. The original center triangles have the following angle interpretations. A triangle with three finite circles is its nondegenerate Euclidean center triangle. For one marked line, the direction to its infinite center is the fixed outward vertical direction, and the two finite angles are the limits of the center-triangle angles. For two marked lines the two vertical directions are opposite: the finite angle is pi and the other two angles are zero. The side signs in (1b,c) are essential here.

In the actual cyclic order around a finite face these direction angles sum to 2pi. Their first variation therefore vanishes. At complete tangency the already proved source gradient is theta/(2pi). The positive finite-face Hessian and rank thus force every logarithmic radius variation to be the same constant: only the scale kernel remains. This uses the limiting Hessian on the original finite-face gauge slice, not an assumed parametrization by off-critical flat representations.

There are exactly two triangles containing the edge uv. In either, the finite circle is tangent to BOTH normalized lines, so r=1/2. Linearizing its two equations gives dr=0. Hence the common radius scale variation is zero: all dr_f=0.

With the radii fixed, an all-finite triangle has fixed three side lengths, so its center velocities are one infinitesimal Euclidean motion. A one-line triangle has dY_f=dY_g=0. At the complete point its horizontal separation has absolute value 2sqrt(r_f r_g)>0. Its fixed finite edge length therefore gives dX_f=dX_g: its two centers have one common horizontal translation, with zero rotation and vertical translation.

Delete the two triangles incident to uv. Their union is a topological disk with four-edge boundary; the remaining triangles form a triangulated disk with connected adjacency graph. This last statement follows by joining two triangle interior points by a path in the disk interior and perturbing that path to avoid vertices and cross edges transversely. Every finite vertex occurs in this remaining disk: its sphere link has at least three triangles, whereas only two triangles were deleted. There is a remaining one-line triangle, since the link of u has at least three triangles and only its two uv triangles were removed.

Two remaining triangles sharing a finite-finite edge have the same infinitesimal Euclidean motion, because they agree at two distinct centers. If their common edge contains a marked line, both are one-line triangles; agreement at their common finite center identifies their horizontal translations. Starting at a remaining one-line triangle and using the connected adjacency graph therefore leaves one horizontal translation for ALL finite centers. The fixed X-coordinate removes it. The Jacobian kernel is zero. Since the finite system is square, the holomorphic implicit-function theorem proves the assertion in section 1. No Bessel packet, finite-color stationarity, or new source-cycle hypothesis is used in this reconstruction.

3. The full local face force is the deformed center angle

Here all variables are curvatures, so l_f=1/r_f. For one ordered source triangle (x,b,y), put mu=1/2-delta and

s=sin(pi delta), c=cos(pi delta), A=x+y,
D=A^2-4xy s^2,
T_b=sqrt[bA+xy+b^2s^2], E_b=A+2bs^2+2sT_b.

Select every root by the positive complete value and continue analytically. Let Q_b be ONE determinant rate, ONE beta rate and HALF the forced-product rate from the actual channel action:

Q_b=I(2mu)-2I(mu)+integral_0^mu log lambda_b(t)dt
                                                +(1/2)J_mu(x,y). (2)

Thus Q_b+Q_c is exactly the full original circle rate, with its sine normalization retained. Direct differentiation cancels the sine factors:

partial_delta Q_b=-Log(E_b/sqrt D), Q_b(0)=F(x,b,y). (3)

For positive curvatures its center triangle has two main tangent side lengths r_x+r_b,r_b+r_y and matching side length

L_xy=sqrt[(r_x+r_y)^2-4r_xr_y sin^2(pi delta)].

The middle angle and the outer x-angle obey

theta_b=2atan[b c/T_b],
cos theta_x=[x(y-x)+b(y+x cos(2pi delta))]/[(x+b)sqrt D],
sin theta_x=2x c T_b/[(x+b)sqrt D].                 (4)

Exchange x,y for the third angle. These formulas also specify the selected complex angle germs. Differentiating (4) gives exactly

partial_delta(theta_j/(2pi))
                =-partial_log l_j Log(E_b/sqrt D). (5)

For the middle angle both sides are -b s/T_b. For the outer angle, differentiate its cosine and sine: their squared sum is one and (cos theta_x)(partial sin theta_x)-(sin theta_x)(partial cos theta_x) gives (5) after substitution. This is an elementary rational identity in x,b,y,s and the displayed roots. The complete source already fixes the integration constant, so integrating (5) proves

partial_log l_j Q_b=theta_j/(2pi).                 (6)

One marked zero curvature is obtained by the corresponding line limit. With two zero curvatures the single finite angle is pi and (6) is the single-variable homogeneous rate (1/2)log l. The geometry of that triangle’s strip is retained by (1), not by assigning extra information to this degenerate angle formula.

4. Identification with the stationary source radii

The real solution of (1) for small real delta preserves all original cyclic center-direction orders. Its main tangencies and prescribed matching intersections give exactly the angles (4), including the one-line and two-line conventions in section 2. Thus their sums at every finite circle are 2pi. Holomorphic continuation preserves these identities on the fixed complex polydisc.

By (6), its curvatures solve the actual limiting face-stationary equations of the complete Fourier-core action, with the two marked faces zero. After fixing the source’s one positive curvature scale, the complete face Hessian is invertible. Its holomorphic implicit-function theorem therefore identifies these normalized curvatures with the UNIQUE nearby source stationary radii, for every small delta. This is a full face-momentum identification, not only agreement of the cusp Hessian.

The line-side equations also prove the missing strip consistency explicitly. At either triangle containing uv, one finite circle is tangent to one marked line and has matching inversive cosine cos psi with the other, where psi=2pi delta_i. For strip height H,

H=r_f(1+cos psi), l_f=2cos^2(pi delta_i)/H.         (7)

The same H occurs on the adjacent uv triangle. It was fixed in (1), not chosen independently for two source factors. At complete delta0 this is the usual r_f=H/2. Formula (7) survives even though that triangle’s source characteristic quadratic has a repeated root.

5. Selected fixed-point germs at a marked triangle

The actual ordered product of two white reflections at matching edge i has trace -2cos(2pi delta_i) in the recorded negative-parabolic lift. At delta_i=0 it is a nontrivial parabolic, supplied by the original certificate. Choosing the eigenvalues

lambda_+=-exp(-2pi i delta_i),
lambda_-=-exp( 2pi i delta_i)                      (8)

therefore gives two holomorphic fixed-line germs. This is elementary: a nonzero off-diagonal entry of the complete parabolic remains a unit, so the eigenline can be solved explicitly in that affine chart using lambda_+ or lambda_-. Their collision at delta0 is retained. The internal source node is exactly -2cos(2pi delta), so (8) records its two actual eigenvalue branches; it is not a free square root of the collapsed scalar characteristic.

For clarity an actual two-line calculation resolves the 200 case. Take the finite circle centered at X+i r, tangent to the lower line, and the upper matching line at height H=r(1+cos psi). The matching product P=r_upper r_circle, with the original outward reflection signs, has trace -2cos psi. Its fixed points are

p_+=X+r sin psi+iH, p_-=X-r sin psi+iH.            (9)

The recorded lift may be written

P=(i/r) [[bar c+2iH, r^2-c bar c-2iH c],
                     [1, -c]], c=X+ir.

Here bar c means X-ir; after complexification it is this holomorphic expression. At p_+ its eigenvalue is -exp(-i psi), at p_- it is -exp(i psi). If the matching line is instead the LOWER line and the circle is tangent to the upper one, the selected lambda_+ line is the point X-r sin psi on that lower line. Reversing the ordered product exchanges its eigenvalues. These are literal orientation adapters, not independently reset choices of a local frame.

Equations (7)–(9) retain the actual selected line germs that the limiting 200 quadratic alone loses. They do not identify a finite-N Bessel logarithmic derivative with a single classical characteristic.
