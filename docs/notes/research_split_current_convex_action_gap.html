A general convex action gap for split endpoint currents

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

At an original positive primitive solution, every admitted deleted angle action associated with a nonzero endpoint current of a suitable even-factor Ward implementation is strictly smaller than the full action. The comparison does not require that the deleted assignment extend to the original unit-charge constraint face. Instead it uses the supporting affine function of the original angle energy on the whole angle body. Its charge correction has the favorable sign.

This is a general geometric comparison for arbitrary signed powers and arbitrary vertex subsets in a fixed original circle tree. It also compares every nonzero integer charge of the deleted forest. The exact finite current is a scalar times that forest, with all original closures retained. A bound on the complete quantum forest still has to be proved before the geometric comparison becomes a bound on its whole current; no contour or geometric coefficient is inferred here.

Lower-half placement

Every HvH_v is strictly positive. This already follows from the proper-prefix theorem: each actual positive return obeys Re⁡(rv,etv¯)>0\operatorname{Re}(r_{v,e}\overline{t_v})>0 when tv≠0t_v\ne0, and pairing (1) with tv¯\overline{t_v} gives ∑eRe⁡(rv,etv¯)=−πhv\sum_e\operatorname{Re}(r_{v,e}\overline{t_v})=-\pi h_v. 2. A supporting gap that allows different charge rows

Define the Bregman remainder at the original point by D0(z)=ℰ(z)−ℰ(z0)−dℰ(z0)(z−z0).(9) D_0(z)=\mathcal E(z)-\mathcal E(z_0) -d\mathcal E(z_0)(z-z_0). \qquad\text{(9)} It is continuous on X¯\overline X, zero at z0z_0, and strictly positive at every other point of X¯\overline X. For a boundary point this follows by restricting to the segment from z0z_0: its interior lies in XX, and the second derivative in the nonzero segment direction is strictly positive there. Consequently δ0=min∂XD0>0.(10) \delta_0=\min_{\partial X}D_0>0. \qquad\text{(10)} The boundary is relative to the ambient affine space with the outside sbs_b’s fixed. It is not the boundary of the unit-charge feasible polytope.

If a weak angle assignment zz has rows ρv(z)=−πcv\rho_v(z)=-\pi c_v, then (7)–(9) give the exact formula ℰ(z)−ℰ(z0)=D0(z)+π∑v∈SHv(cv−1).(11) \boxed{\quad \mathcal E(z)-\mathcal E(z_0) =D_0(z)+\pi\sum_{v\in S}H_v(c_v-1). \quad} \qquad\text{(11)} The cvc_v’s in this identity can be any real numbers. They are the row values of a comparison assignment. No Fourier alias, peripheral change, or source equality is asserted by changing them. In particular, if all cv≥1c_v\ge1, the supporting correction is nonnegative, and a boundary comparison has at least the gap δ0\delta_0.

3. Every deleted forest and every nonzero charge

Action normalization for charged positive points

If z′z' comes from a positive solution with its original canonical logarithms and charges cw′c'_w, its charge action is F𝒄′(t)=∑esurvivesψ(tv,tw)+i4π∑w∈S\Cnwtw2−i∑w∈S\Ccw′tw.(21) F_{\mathbf c'}(t) =\sum_{e\ {\mathrm{ survives}}}\psi(t_v,t_w) +\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{w\in S\setminus C}n_wt_w^2 -i\sum_{w\in S\setminus C}c'_wt_w. \qquad\text{(21)} Then Re⁡F𝒄′=Mdel\operatorname{Re}F_{\mathbf c'}=M_{\mathrm{ del}} exactly. Indeed the pinned edge calibration is πRe⁡ψ+4(EP+EQ)−gvhv−gwhw=πM∞.(22) \pi\operatorname{Re}\psi+4(E_P+E_Q) -g_vh_v-g_wh_w=\pi M_\infty. \qquad\text{(22)} After summing it, the charged row ∑g=nwsw/2−πcw′\sum g=n_ws_w/2-\pi c'_w cancels the real parts of the quadratic and linear terms in (21), exactly as at unit charge. Thus Theorem 1 compares the actual real classical action of every positive deleted charged solution, including every nonzero integer charge vector, to the original primitive action.

For an isolated surviving selected vertex, a charged critical point must obey nwtw=2πcw′n_wt_w=2\pi c'_w. With |cw′|≥1|c'_w|\ge1, this requires nw≠0n_w\ne0 and gives real twt_w, so its terms in (21) have zero real part. A weak isolated row without a critical interpretation is still covered as an angle assignment. No quantum estimate for an isolated trace is assumed.

The linear higher-charge term in (15) is a classical comparison, not by itself an estimate of an infinite quantum charge sum.

Uniformity under small changes of outside angles

The gap is uniform on a compact continuous family of positive original unit-charge solutions with the same tree, selected set and powers. Indeed Hv>0H_v>0 has a positive family minimum. For the boundary remainders, consider pairs consisting of a family parameter and an assignment on the boundary of its ambient body XX. All angle coordinates are bounded. Along a convergent sequence of such pairs, one of the finitely many weak affine inequalities is active on a subsequence, and remains active at its limit. The limiting body has an interior positive point, so this is still its boundary. Hence the set of pairs is compact. The square energy, outside work and gradient at the continued original solution are continuous; D0D_0 is strictly positive at every pair. Its family minimum is therefore positive as well.

In particular, the original positive-period implicit-function theorem continues any fixed solution under sufficiently small complex changes of the prescribed outside angles. Restricting to a small closed neighborhood gives a compact family of positive solutions. Shrinking it if necessary, (15) holds uniformly with δ0/2\delta_0/2 and Hv/2H_v/2 from its center. This is applicable near complete outside angles whenever the fixed short component has the required original positive solution there. It supplies no contour transport for the outside quantum variables.

4. An auxiliary even split with no exceptional small-power current

For each original integer nvn_v, choose the auxiliary factor ϵv*={0,nveven,sgn⁡(nv),nvodd,mv*=nv−ϵv*∈2ℤ.(23) \epsilon_v^*= \begin{cases} 0,&n_v\ {\mathrm{ even}},\\ \operatorname{sgn}(n_v),&n_v\ {\mathrm{ odd}}, \end{cases} \qquad m_v^*=n_v-\epsilon_v^*\in2\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(23)} This is an algebraic factorization of the same original RnvR^{n_v}. The auxiliary ϵv*\epsilon_v^* is not a replacement for the physical marking ηv\eta_v; the original relation nv=ηv−2pvn_v=\eta_v-2p_v stays fixed. It is possible that the auxiliary factor differs from that marking, for example at a total power nv=1n_v=1 with ηv=−1\eta_v=-1. The operation then changes the chosen conjugated Ward operator, not the crossing power or peripheral curve.

To verify its exact validity, use the full original algebra 𝒜N=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2),q=eπi/N,ρ=q−q−1,κ=q(N−1)2/2,σ=(−1)N.(24) \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2),\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad \rho=q-q^{-1},\quad \kappa=q^{(N-1)^2/2},\quad \sigma=(-1)^N. \qquad\text{(24)} Let fjf_j represent rNjr_N^j, with R=κrNR=\kappa r_N. For every signed integer split n=m+ϵn=m+\epsilon with mm even, fn=fmfϵf_n=f_mf_\epsilon, including all repeated-node normals. Conjugating the original even Ward identity by the unit fϵf_\epsilon gives, for the original trace and every polynomial spectator AA, ϕN(fn𝒟m,1ϵA)=σmNρA(c0),c0=q+q−1.(25) \phi_N\!\left(f_n\mathcal D_{m,1}^{\epsilon}A\right) =\sigma mN\rho\,A(c_0),\qquad c_0=q+q^{-1}. \qquad\text{(25)} Its proof uses only this factorization and fϵΞ0=Ξ0f_\epsilon\Xi_0=\Xi_0, not an identification of ϵ\epsilon with the marking. Hence the adopted full-algebra theorem applies unchanged to (23). The original carrier is still κn\kappa^n.

For a simultaneous set CC of these currents on the original circle tree, its exact source is 𝒥C,N*=(∏v∈Cκnvσmv*Nρ)ZG\C,N.(26) \mathcal J_{C,N}^* =\left(\prod_{v\in C}\kappa^{n_v}\sigma m_v^*N\rho\right) Z_{G\setminus C,N}. \qquad\text{(26)} All surviving traces, outside variables, and their mixed normal data are retained. This follows from KT(c0,w)=1K_T(c_0,w)=1 before any exterior trace; no independent-component factorization is assumed. The formula is for the original turn edges. An identity kernel or another exterior coupon must retain its actual endpoint evaluation.

If nv∈{0,1,−1}n_v\in\{0,1,-1\}, then mv*=0m_v^*=0, so every current containing that vertex is zero. Conversely mv*≠0m_v^*\ne0 implies |nv|≥2|n_v|\ge2. Thus every nonzero scalar prefactor in (26) has a removed set covered by Theorem 1. This holds at both parities for every N≥2N\ge2. For fixed powers, |∏v∈Cκnvσmv*Nρ|≤∏v∈C2π|mv*|,(27) \left|\prod_{v\in C}\kappa^{n_v}\sigma m_v^*N\rho\right| \le\prod_{v\in C}2\pi|m_v^*|, \qquad\text{(27)} so these exact prefactors introduce no exponential rate. The deleted source itself can of course vanish.

For comparison, retaining the original ηv\eta_v as the split factor can leave a nonzero even current at total nv=±1n_v=\pm1. One must not infer (12) from that different choice. Formula (23) is the precise auxiliary choice that removes this issue.

5. Consequence and the remaining contour problem

No Conway reduction and no charge alias are needed for this geometric gap whenever the original positive primitive solution exists. The old endpoint-face condition remains exact for an extension within the same unit-charge rows. The present comparison uses a different boundary assignment only as a competitor in the ambient convex body, and keeps its affine charge correction explicitly. Thus it does not contradict or alter the original face criterion.

For a complete contour induction this removes a particular geometric comparison problem: every nonzero chosen split current is exactly a deleted source whose admitted nonzero-charge positive action is uniformly below the full action. If the original, fully normalized deleted source has already been bounded by those actions with subexponential loss, (26)–(27) transfer that bound immediately to this current, with the strict margin (15).

The outstanding statement is genuinely about the original source. One must show that the actual deformation produces currents of the controlled form (26), and bound each entire deleted source, including its nonpositive sectors, lattice modes, endpoint seams and exterior closing. A deleted forest can lack a positive critical point; no action or quantum bound is assigned to it by assumption. Nor does the present proof supply a positive original chart for an arbitrary hyperbolic short filling after Conway reductions. The relative-cycle coefficient of the original geometric block remains a separate obligation.

The chosen half-factor has the canonical analytic unit from the even-source theorem. Replacing it by an unwrapped Gaussian across a logarithmic cut still incurs that theorem’s exact rank-two seam. Those seam currents are not silently identified with (26).
