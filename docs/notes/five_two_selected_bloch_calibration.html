Exact Bloch and hyperbolic calibration of the selected five-crossing phase

28 October 2026. This identifies the critical value of the EXACT original two-index source in the finite transform. It does not infer a volume limit from the existence of that critical point. Selection of the finite contour is separate.

1. The algebraic point and its logarithmic marks

Let rho be the positive real root of rho3+rho2-1=0, and put

v=(1+rho)/2+i sqrt(1/rho-(1+rho)^2/4).

Then v is the upper nonreal root of v3-v2+1=0. In particular

1-v=v^(-2), v^3=v^2-1, v^5=-(1+v).
X=1/(1+v^2), Y=-v, Z=X/Y,
x=1+Log(X)/(2pi i), y=1+Log(Y)/(2pi i).       (1)

The logarithms on the right are principal; X,Y lie in the lower half-plane. Thus 1/2<Re y<Re x<1. Use the marked primitive

J(z)=integral_0^z Log(2sin(pi t))dt,
S(x,y)=3J(x)-J(y)-J(x-y)
                +i pi(3x+2y-x^2-xy-y^2).                (2)

All three arguments have real part strictly between zero and one. The stationary equations, with the actual marks, are

3Log(1-X)-Log(1-Z)-2(2pi i x)-(2pi i y)+4pi i=0,
-Log(1-Y)+Log(1-Z)-(2pi i x)+2pi i=0.                   (3)

Their exponentials are verified by reducing in Q[v]/(v3-v2+1):

(1-X)^3=X^2 Y(1-Z),  1-Z=X(1-Y).                       (4)

There is no unnoticed integer in (3). For example rho is between 7548/10000 and 7549/10000 by direct polynomial signs and strict monotonicity. Formula (1) then isolates v in the first quadrant near .87744+.74486i, X near .38150-.41040i, and Z near -.02193+.48634i. Principal arguments give

Arg(1-X) in (.57,.61), Arg(1-Z) in (-.50,-.40),
Im(2pi i x) in (5.44,5.50),
Im(2pi i y) in (3.82,3.86).

The imaginary part of the first expression in (3) is therefore between -.19 and .20; its real part is zero by (4), so the integer multiple of 2pi i is zero. The second expression is handled by 1-Z=X(1-Y): both Log(1-Z)-Log(1-Y) and principal Log X have arguments in (-pi,0). This proves the marked stationary equations exactly.

2. The critical action is its Bloch regulator

Write D for the Bloch–Wigner dilogarithm,

D(z)=Im Li_2(z)+log|z| Arg(1-z).

With l_X=2pi i x and l_Y=2pi i y, direct differentiation and evaluation at zero give

2pi i S=Li_2(1)-3Li_2(X)+Li_2(Y)+Li_2(Z)
                 -l_X^2-l_X l_Y+2pi i(2l_X+l_Y).        (5)

All dilogarithms have the branches inherited from 0<Re z<1; their arguments avoid [1,infinity). Set a=Re l_X, b=Re l_Y, A=Im l_X, B=Im l_Y. The imaginary part of the polynomial term in (5) is

a(4pi-2A-B)+b(2pi-A).

Taking imaginary parts of (3) shows that this equals

-3log|X| Arg(1-X)+log|Y| Arg(1-Y)
                              +log|Z| Arg(1-Z).

Consequently, with no unrecorded additive constant,

2pi Re S(x,y)=-3D(X)+D(Y)+D(Z).                         (6)

3. An explicit finite Bloch identity

The right side of (6) is exactly 3D(v). Here is a short proof using only the six cross-ratio symmetries, duplication, and one five-term relation.

First 1-v=v^-2 gives D(v^2)=D(v). Duplication D(z^2)=2D(z)+2D(-z) gives

D(-v)=-D(v)/2.
D(-v^2)=D(X), since X=1/(1+v^2).
D(v^4)=2D(v)+2D(X).                                    (7)

The same cubic equation gives the literal field identities

-v^4=(-v-1)/(-v),
A_0=v(1+v)/(1+v^2)=v^8/(v^8-1).                        (8)

Hence D(-v^4)=D(-v), D(A_0)=-D(v^8), and duplication once more gives

D(v^8)=2D(v^4)+2D(-v^4)=3D(v)+4D(X).                   (9)

The five-term identity in multiplicative form at (v,-v) is

D(v)+D(-v)
  =D(-v^2)+D(v(1+v)/(1+v^2))
                      +D(-v(1-v)/(1+v^2))
  =D(X)-D(v^8)+D(Z),                                  (10)

because 1-v=v^-2. Substitution of (7)-(9) into (10) yields

-3D(X)+D(-v)+D(Z)=3D(v).                               (11)

The regulator identities used here can be checked directly: their exterior derivatives vanish using dD=log|z| dArg(1-z)-log|1-z| dArg z, and their real degenerate values fix the constants. Thus (11) is not a numerical rational relation or a conclusion from the degree of the trace field.

4. Why 3D(v) is the hyperbolic volume of the marked complement

Use the explicitly paired three-tetrahedron p=0 odd family in the face and angle audit and its independent topological and peripheral audit. That marked family is the 7/3 five-crossing knot complement. Its positive symmetric angle coordinates are

U=(A,B,C), V=(A,B,C), W=(B,C,A),
A+3C=pi, A+B+C=pi.                                   (12)

The face audit proves that averaging into this symmetric locus preserves all edge equations and does not decrease total volume. The full volume is strictly concave. Thus an interior stationary point of the one-dimensional symmetric volume is its full maximum.

Put theta=Arg v. From 1-v=v^-2 and the stated branch one has

C=theta, B=2theta, A=pi-3theta,

all positive. These are the three angles of the ideal shape v, up to cyclic order. The Euclidean sine rule gives

|v|=sin B/sin A, |1-v|=sin C/sin A.

The modulus relation |v|^2|1-v|=1 therefore says exactly

sin^3 A=sin^2 B sin C.                                (13)

Equation (13) is the derivative-zero equation for

3[Lambda(pi-3theta)+Lambda(2theta)+Lambda(theta)].

It is the interior maximum by strict concavity. The explicit multiplier construction in Section 3.5 of the face audit glues the cusp triangles isometrically, gives trivial edge dilation and complete torus cusps, and hence supplies a complete hyperbolic metric. Its three tetrahedra each have volume D(v). The topological marking fixes the complement, and the positive orientation fixes its volume sign. Therefore

Vol(S^3 minus 5_2)=3D(v),
Re S(x,y)=Vol(S^3 minus 5_2)/(2pi).                     (14)

This step uses the already audited classical face geometry; it assumes no quantum state identity for that triangulation.

5. Scope

The selected algebraic point has the exact marked volume action. This resolves the calibration of that point. The actual finite source contour and the exclusion of all competing source pieces still have to be established before (14) implies an asymptotic statement for the original invariant.
