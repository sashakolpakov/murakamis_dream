A quantitative phase loss for the original charged block source

Private written proof, 14 November 2026. No computation, import, test or manuscript change is used.

The exact bridge reduction of the original signed braid gives root intervals with positive sine-binomial magnitudes and a specified phase on each block. Here their phase loss is bounded explicitly, at every block length and every positive radius. The resulting coefficient estimate retains the original shared charges, all clock factors and the closing pivots. It improves the positive radial upper bound by a weighted graph phase energy.

The positive radial bound has a precise constrained tetrahedral-volume dual. Neither that dual nor the phase loss is identified here with the hyperbolic volume of the original partially filled tangle. No lower bound for its coherent clock sum is asserted.

2. The actual shared coefficient and its graph phase energy

Consider a fixed finite directed multigraph, including parallel edges and loops, and the ordinary polynomial PN(X)=ΓN∏e:u→vXuLeBLe,N(weXv/Xu),|we|=1,0≤Le<N.(9) P_N(X)=\Gamma_N\prod_{e:u\to v} X_u^{L_e}B_{L_e,N}(w_eX_v/X_u), \qquad |w_e|=1,\quad 0\le L_e<N. \qquad\text{(9)} The Laurent notation represents an ordinary polynomial. 3. Source normalization and the coherent clock sum

The original-source identity in the charged bridge-flow theorem, equations (12), (17)–(19), is KN=χNN∑hover-monotoneWh[X1N−1⋯XsN−1]Ph(X).(14) K_N=\frac{\chi_N}{N} \sum_{h\ {\mathrm{ over\text{-}monotone}}} W_h[X_1^{N-1}\cdots X_s^{N-1}]P_h(X). \qquad\text{(14)} It retains integer-weight conservation, all original closure adapters and pivots, and the homogeneous alias cancellation. Each fixed-clock block is exactly ∏ℓ=0L−1(αXu−QℓβXv)=αLXuLBL,N(−(β/α)qL−1Xv/Xu).(15) \prod_{\ell=0}^{L-1}(\alpha X_u-Q^\ell\beta X_v) =\alpha^L X_u^L B_{L,N}\bigl(-(\beta/\alpha)q^{L-1}X_v/X_u\bigr). \qquad\text{(15)} The actual propagated root phases have modulus one. Its wew_e’s are the source theorem’s equation (18), including the unpivoted cut and every pivotal closing block. They are not selected by optimizing (10). Every WhW_h, including its sine-factorial magnitudes and Gaussian phases, remains.

4. The constrained tetrahedral entropy of the positive bound

This identifies the positive optimization, not the knot geometry. Put J(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt,vλ(x)=J(λ)−J(x)−J(λ−x).(17) J(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt,\qquad v_\lambda(x)=J(\lambda)-J(x)-J(\lambda-x). \qquad\text{(17)} For 0≤x≤λ≤10\le x\le\lambda\le1, reflection gives vλ(x)=1π{Λ(πx)+Λ(π(λ−x))+Λ(π(1−λ))},(18) v_\lambda(x)=\frac1\pi \{\Lambda(\pi x)+\Lambda(\pi(\lambda-x)) +\Lambda(\pi(1-\lambda))\}, \qquad\text{(18)} where Λ(a)=−∫0alog⁡|2sin⁡t|dt\Lambda(a)=-\int_0^a\log|2\sin t|\,dt. 5. Contribution and remaining geometric comparison

The earlier cycle-packing theorem already turns supplied local losses into a shared coefficient bound. The additions here are the explicit loss for the actual sine-binomial blocks, uniformity through all allowed lengths and all positive radii with polynomial cost, and (11) for the original charged bridge source. No asymptotic replacement of a crossing or deletion of an endpoint is used to obtain the phase loss.

Equations (18), (23), (24) connect the bound to a concave tetrahedral entropy problem. In (14), however, the lengths and block phases depend on the remaining clocks. Their Gaussian feedback and WhW_h remain part of the source. The coefficient flow equations have not been proved to give all marked geometric gluing and peripheral equations for arbitrary short Conway pieces. The maximum in (24) must not be called their hyperbolic volume.

At specified geometric radii, (11) supplies the positive radial envelope minus the full arc-loss energy, bounded below by the explicit graph cosine energy. Proving a strict gap below the geometric action on a whole complementary family still requires that numerical-free analytic comparison. Nontrivial holonomy alone, or positive energy alone, does not supply it. Compatible sectors and cancellation among original clock phases remain. No general partial-filling volume theorem is claimed.
