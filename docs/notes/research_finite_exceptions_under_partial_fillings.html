From long fillings to finitely many exceptions

Research note, 2 November 2026. The manuscripts remain frozen. This note proves elementary consequences of their stated hypotheses and a limitation of the printed directed criterion. It does not prove a new volume theorem for short fillings.

1. The target and the present quantifiers

The target is Kashaev’s original volume identity for a hyperbolic knot K:

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|⟨K⟩N|=Vol⁡(S3\K). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|\langle K\rangle_N| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K).

There are two different finite-exception questions:

  1. For one fixed marked tangle template, are only finitely many integer filling assignments that give hyperbolic knots untreated?
  2. Among all hyperbolic knot types, are only finitely many untreated?

The second statement does not follow from the first for infinitely many templates. Parameter assignments also need not give distinct knot types.

For a fixed admissible template with d filling parameters, the current theorem supplies a finite nonnegative integer B such that every assignment with |p_i| > B for every i satisfies the volume identity. All parameters are fixed before the level N tends to infinity. The threshold can depend on the template. A retained half-twist pattern is fixed, so integer surgery parameters already preserve the relevant crossing parities.

2. The density consequence

For an integer H > B, the fraction of assignments in [-H,H]^d covered by this sufficient condition is exactly

(2(H−B)2H+1)d. \left(\frac{2(H-B)}{2H+1}\right)^d.

Indeed each coordinate has 2(H-B) choices outside [-B,B]. The number not certified by this condition is at most

d(2B+1)(2H+1)d−1, d(2B+1)(2H+1)^{d-1},

by the union bound over coordinates. Thus the covered assignments have density one in this fixed parameter space. For d >= 2, the complement of this sufficient condition is nevertheless infinite: p_1 can remain zero while p_2 tends to infinity. This is a statement about untested assignments, not about failures of the volume conjecture or necessarily about infinitely many distinct untested knot types.

For a single free parameter the corresponding exceptional assignments are already finite. No counting statement here concerns knots ordered by crossing number.

3. A finite-exception lemma for one template

Let D be any subset of Z^d. In the intended application, D consists of the assignments giving hyperbolic knots with the prescribed marking. Let P(p) be a property defined for p in D; here P is the volume identity.

For I a proper subset of {1,…,d} and a in Z^I, fixing p_i=a_i for i in I is called a partial assignment. The coordinates outside I remain free.

Proposition. Suppose that every partial assignment (I,a) has a finite integer B(I,a) >= 0 such that

𝒑∈D,pi=ai(i∈I),|pj|>B(I,a)(j∉I)⇒P(𝒑). \mathbf p\in D,\quad p_i=a_i\ (i\in I),\quad |p_j|>B(I,a)\ (j\notin I) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(\mathbf p).

Then D contains only finitely many points where P fails.

Proof. Begin with I empty. A point where P fails has a free coordinate j with |p_j| <= B(I,a). Branch over the finitely many choices of j and of the integer p_j in that interval, adding this coordinate to I. Apply the same argument at each new partial assignment. There are finitely many children at every node, and every step fixes one previously free coordinate. After at most d steps each remaining branch specifies one complete tuple. A finite tree of bounded depth has finitely many leaves. Every point of D where P fails belongs to this finite set of complete tuples. QED.

The thresholds may depend arbitrarily on the previously fixed values; no uniform threshold over all partial assignments is assumed. If the thresholds and applicable source theorems are effective, the argument also gives an effective finite list. It does not settle the points on that list.

The lemma only needs the partial assignments visited by the finite branching procedure. Proving a theorem stable under all fixed partial fillings is a convenient sufficient hypothesis, stronger than that finite collection of needed cases.

4. The missing stability theorem

The next substantive target is the following statement, currently unproved in this project:

Fix an admissible marked parent, choose one of its crossing circles, and give that circle any fixed integer filling, provided at least one crossing circle remains unfilled. For all independently sufficiently long fillings of the remaining crossing circles that produce hyperbolic knots, prove the original Kashaev volume identity. Establish the same statement for the partially filled sources so that it can be applied repeatedly.

Stop the iteration when no free coordinate remains. Those finitely many fully assigned leaves are the possible exceptions; no theorem for them is being smuggled in through a vacuous longness condition.

One application alone is insufficient in three or more parameters: there can be two or more short coordinates. The class of sources must be stable under iteration, or each required partial assignment must be treated separately.

The existing filling-functional norm estimate is already valid for every fixed integer crossing power n:

|ℒNnH|≤(1+|n|/2)∥H∥[−2,2]. |\mathcal L_N^n H|\le(1+|n|/2)\|H\|_{[-2,2]}.

Its prefactor has zero exponential rate as N tends to infinity. Therefore an upper bound for the short filling is not the main missing step. The problem is to identify the geometric stationary point after the partial filling, prove that its contribution is nonzero, and bound the other contributions below it. A closed-tangle volume limit does not provide the uniform open-boundary estimates needed for further gluing.

Intermediate partial fillings need not remain in the current geometric class. One cannot silently discard an intermediate nonhyperbolic exterior without proving that its relevant descendants are absent or already controlled. The abstract proposition uses only the final domain D and hides no assumption about intermediate hyperbolicity.

5. A concrete limitation of the current directed estimates

The directed criterion in the circle-tree manuscript fixes k_0=2^{-16} and t_0=2^{-14}. At a nonroot leaf, the degree is one and there are no child contributions. Its quantities are

A=2k0π2,C=16+n232k0,|D|2=1+n24π2,β=420π361(1+n2). A=\frac{2k_0}{\pi^2},\qquad C=16+\frac{n^2}{32k_0},\qquad |D|^2=\frac{1+n^2}{4\pi^2},\qquad \beta=\frac{420\pi}{361(1+n^2)}.

The printed contour-height and error estimates give

y≤t0|D|4C,η≤t0|D|y2≤t02|D|28C. y\le\frac{t_0|D|}{4C},\qquad \eta\le\frac{t_0|D|y}{2} \le\frac{t_0^2|D|^2}{8C}.

For n nonzero, the required inequality beta < eta/2 therefore implies

n2+2+n−2>840π3361t02k0. n^2+2+n^{-2}> \frac{840\pi^3}{361t_0^2k_0}.

Since pi > 31/10 and t_0^2 k_0=2^{-44}, the right side exceeds 69 times 2^{44}. If 1 <= |n| <= 2^{25}, the left side is at most 2^{50}+3, which is smaller. For n=0 the separate required condition t_0|D| >= 2 fails. Thus:

Proposition. No nonroot leaf with |n| <= 2^{25} satisfies the printed directed criterion, for any choice of its adjustable decay parameters.

Every finite tree with at least two vertices has a nonroot leaf, whatever root is selected. This limitation concerns this sufficient criterion alone. It is not a necessary condition for the volume identity, nor a limitation on all other methods in the project. It shows why optimizing the final 2^{27} rounding cannot reach ordinary short twists without changing the analytic estimates.

6. The additional issue for all hyperbolic knots

To pass from fixed templates to finitely many excluded knot types, one sufficient statement would be a universal C such that every hyperbolic knot either has an established volume proof or has crossing number at most C. There are finitely many knot types of bounded crossing number. The current degree-and-twist criteria supply no such bound on untreated knots.

Two weaker conclusions must not be mistaken for this statement:

  • Finitely many short local tangle types can form arbitrarily large diagrams.
  • Finitely many unresolved reduced cores can have infinitely many unresolved extensions. A reduction that only transports a proof from a core to its extensions does not control those extensions when the core remains unresolved.

Nor can all hyperbolic knots be fillings of a finite list of fixed hyperbolic parents: filling decreases hyperbolic volume, whereas hyperbolic knot volumes are unbounded. Explicit weaving-knot bounds are given by Champanerkar–Kofman–Purcell. The general finite-exception target needs a theorem handling unbounded diagram complexity, in addition to the partial-filling theorem above.

7. Attribution and novelty

Kashaev’s original conjecture is the target. Chen and Yang explicitly credit it in the introduction to their different conjectures for Turaev–Viro and Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants. A theorem about those invariants does not automatically prove the original Kashaev evaluation. The long-filling theorem stated by Shen belongs in related work rather than direct coverage of our main knot theorem. A reflection or cancellation argument taken from it must nevertheless be credited, and its existing scope matters to any claim that a particular analytic technique is new.

The counting and finite-branching propositions above are elementary deductions. They are not being advertised as new knot theorems. The research contribution still sought is the stability under short partial fillings and the control of arbitrarily long compositions of short tangles.

8. A consequence of counting assignments: strict padding

The literal finite-assignment target is stronger than a statement about distinct knot types. The following observation was obtained while pursuing the active goal. It uses the ordered conventions and the explicit cap isotopies of Ordered tangle reductions and long net twists. Here “strict” means the enlarged literal grammar in the Conway asymptotics manuscript: the closing children may be elementary or turned elementary boxes. The construction below is not asserted to belong to the older, narrower grammar requiring two completed closing children.

Write T for a physical quarter-turn, h(n)=T(R^n) for an elementary leaf, C for cylindrical closure, D for cap closure, and tau=T^2 for the physical half-turn. An unframed cap closure D(R^a) is an unknot for every integer a: its crossings are removable curls. The zero leaf h(0) closes an adjacent tangle by caps. Consequently

C(h(0)F)≃D(F),D(FG)≃D(F)#D(G),D(TQ)≃C(τQ). C(h(0)F)\simeq D(F),\qquad D(FG)\simeq D(F)\mathbin\# D(G),\qquad D(TQ)\simeq C(\tau Q).

The connected sum uses the two specified cap components; internal components are retained. If C(Q) is a knot, the application below contains no extra component. Rotating the entire cylindrical closure identifies C(tau Q) with C(Q).

Proposition. For the class of strict Conway templates, these two statements are equivalent:

  1. Each fixed admissible template has only finitely many hyperbolic filling assignments at which the original Kashaev volume identity fails.
  2. Every hyperbolic knot represented by a strict Conway template satisfies that identity, at every integer assignment of its powers.

Proof. The second statement implies the first with no exceptions. For the converse, fix an arbitrary strict core K=C(Q), with all its powers fixed, and assume K is hyperbolic. Add two new leaves and form

E(a,b)=C(h(b)[RaT(Q)]). E(a,b)=C\bigl(h(b)[R^aT(Q)]\bigr).

The factor R^a can be written T^3(h(a)), so the displayed expression uses precisely the permitted leaves, turns, and products.

This is formally strict. When Q is inserted as an open tangle, its former closing product becomes an ordinary retained product. This is allowed: each permitted closed pair AA, AL, AH, LH (in either order) also has a nonempty open product, respectively of type A, A, H, H. All products below it were already strict. Thus Q, now considered open, has output type A or H in the original type table. This is an existence argument for a new strict state; it does not reuse the old closed optimizer. Consequently T(Q) has type A or L, while Ra=T3(h(a)) has type H. Their product has type H, and the outer closing pair consists of types L and H, which is allowed. None of these formal type assertions depends on the numerical powers.

At b=0 the cap identities give

E(a,0)≃D(RaT(Q))≃D(Ra)#D(T(Q))≃U#C(τQ)≃K E(a,0)\simeq D(R^aT(Q)) \simeq D(R^a)\#D(T(Q)) \simeq U\#C(\tau Q)\simeq K

for every integer a. Fix either parity for a, use even parity at b, and retain the core parities. These are infinitely many distinct filling assignments in one fixed admissible marked template, all giving the same hyperbolic knot. After the prescribed zero-framing correction they have the same reduced invariant and volume. If K failed the identity, the first statement would be contradicted. Therefore K satisfies it. Since the core was arbitrary, the second statement follows. QED.

More explicitly, choose retained carriers ηa=ηb=0\eta_a=\eta_b=0 at the two new circles and keep the fixed core carriers. In the original primitive surgery coordinates, the assignments (pa,pb,𝒑Q)=(r,0,𝒑Q),r∈ℤ, (p_a,p_b,\mathbf p_Q)=(r,0,\mathbf p_Q), \qquad r\in\mathbb Z, give a=−2ra=-2r, b=0b=0, and the same fixed core. They are distinct tuples of the original template. Its retained pattern passes the knot test: changing a braid power by an even integer changes no endpoint pairing or internal component, and every displayed closure is the knot KK. The physical half-turn τ\tau permutes the cylindrical closing pairs {0,3},{1,2}\{0,3\},\{1,2\}, carrying the prescribed empty outside arcs to one another. Thus the last isotopy above is the rotation of the entire closed picture, with no unrecorded boundary Dehn twist.

This does not disprove the requested theorem. It identifies its actual strength: a proof for every strict template cannot leave any unresolved hyperbolic strict core behind a finite list of assignments. Replacing assignments by distinct knot types, or excluding inessential parameters, would change the goal and is not done here.

The same construction explains why a short partially filled exterior that is nonhyperbolic cannot simply be ignored. Its remaining circle can control a removable curl while all its fillings still produce the fixed hyperbolic knot K. The proposed partial-filling theorem must handle this possibility rather than assume that every intermediate exterior remains in the original augmented geometric class.
