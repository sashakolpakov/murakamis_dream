Confluent physical rows, the original closing, and the turn-invariant positive quotient

Private written proof, 17 November 2026.

The full physical value and normal rows give an exact block formula for the original Frobenius closing. They also identify the largest positive quotient on which every original VnV_n is an isometry. The prescribed closing has dual norm one on this quotient, but one literal multiplication crossing does not descend to it. In fact the same obstruction applies to every positive semidefinite form invariant under V1V_1 which controls that closing.

These are statements about the original finite source and its full normal channels. They give neither a lower bound for a prescribed filled scalar nor a source-specific geometric asymptotic.

For the coefficient reflection f♯(Z)=f(Z‾)¯f^\sharp(Z)=\overline{f(\bar Z)}, the Hermitian form ϕN(f♯g)\phi_N(f^\sharp g) has the same real blocks (6). Every double block has determinant −βj2<0-\beta_j^2<0. Its total signature is (⌈N/2⌉,⌊N/2⌋)(\lceil N/2\rceil,\lfloor N/2\rfloor). In particular the span of the value rows is totally isotropic at even levels. At odd levels its only nonzero pairing is the simple-row square. These conclusions also follow from the degrees in (3), but (6) retains the missing normal pairings.

3. Actual Jordan phases and all invariant positive forms

Write νj=(−1)jqj(j+1),λn,j=νj−n,αj=R′(cj)R(cj)=i(D−2j)4sin⁡((2j+1)θ)(j∈𝒟N).(14) \nu_j=(-1)^j q^{j(j+1)},\qquad \lambda_{n,j}=\nu_j^{-n},\qquad \alpha_j=\frac{R'(c_j)}{R(c_j)} =\frac{i(D-2j)}{4\sin((2j+1)\theta)} \quad(j\in\mathcal D_N). \qquad\text{(14)} The original crossing values are R(cj)=κνjR(c_j)=\kappa\nu_j. The number αj\alpha_j is purely imaginary and nonzero at every double node, at every N≥2N\ge2. The retained Jordan action from the signed-turn source is VnFj=λn,jFj,VnGj=λn,j(Gj−nαjFj).(15) V_nF_j=\lambda_{n,j}F_j,\qquad V_nG_j=\lambda_{n,j}(G_j-n\alpha_jF_j). \qquad\text{(15)} The odd simple row has only its first formula. Thus (7) transforms by (Aj,Bj)↦λn,j(Aj−nαjBj,Bj).(16) (A_j,B_j)\longmapsto \lambda_{n,j}(A_j-n\alpha_jB_j,B_j). \qquad\text{(16)} The normal coefficient is not discarded. The Hermitian indefinite form (6) is preserved by (16), since αj+αj¯=0\alpha_j+\overline{\alpha_j}=0 and |λn,j|=1|\lambda_{n,j}|=1.

Define the surjective value map and its seminorm by 𝒰Nf=((Tf)(cj))0≤j≤J,∥f∥𝒬N2=∑j=0J|(Tf)(cj)|2.(17) \mathcal U_N f=((Tf)(c_j))_{0\le j\le J},\qquad \|f\|_{\mathcal Q_N}^2 =\sum_{j=0}^{J}|(Tf)(c_j)|^2 . \qquad\text{(17)} Surjectivity follows from ordinary value interpolation, followed by TT; prescribing no normals is required for it.

Theorem 2. The form (17) has exactly the kernel ℒN=span⁡{Fj:j∈𝒟N}.(18) \mathcal L_N=\operatorname{span} \{F_j:j\in\mathcal D_N\}. \qquad\text{(18)} It gives a positive definite norm on 𝒜N/ℒN\mathcal A_N/\mathcal L_N, and every VnV_n induces an isometry there. The original closing descends and satisfies |ϕN(f)|≤∥f∥𝒬N(19) |\phi_N(f)|\le\|f\|_{\mathcal Q_N} \qquad\text{(19)} with exact dual norm one.

Moreover, every positive semidefinite Hermitian form 𝔥\mathfrak h on 𝒜N\mathcal A_N satisfying 𝔥(V1f,V1g)=𝔥(f,g)\mathfrak h(V_1f,V_1g)=\mathfrak h(f,g) kills ℒN\mathcal L_N. Precisely all such forms are 𝔥(f,g)=(𝒰Nf)*B(𝒰Ng),B≥0,Bjk=0if λ1,j≠λ1,k.(20) \mathfrak h(f,g)=(\mathcal U_N f)^* B(\mathcal U_N g), \quad B\ge0,\quad B_{jk}=0\ \text{if }\lambda_{1,j}\ne\lambda_{1,k}. \qquad\text{(20)} Possible repeated crossing phases are retained in the blocks of BB, including at composite levels.

Proof. Equation (8) proves (18). Also, directly from (2), 𝒰N(Vnf)j=κnR(cj)−n(Tf)(cj)=λn,j𝒰N(f)j.(21) \mathcal U_N(V_nf)_j =\kappa^nR(c_j)^{-n}(Tf)(c_j) =\lambda_{n,j}\mathcal U_N(f)_j . \qquad\text{(21)} This proves isometry. Equation (9) identifies the closing with the zeroth coordinate of (17), proving (19). Equality is achieved by f=β0−1G0f=\beta_0^{-1}G_0, whose 𝒰N\mathcal U_N vector is the zeroth standard vector.

For any invariant 𝔥\mathfrak h, iterate (15) with n=mn=m and mm an arbitrary positive integer: 𝔥(Gj−mαjFj,Gj−mαjFj)=𝔥(Gj,Gj).(22) \mathfrak h(G_j-m\alpha_jF_j,G_j-m\alpha_jF_j) =\mathfrak h(G_j,G_j). \qquad\text{(22)} The coefficient of m2m^2 is |αj|2𝔥(Fj,Fj)|\alpha_j|^2\mathfrak h(F_j,F_j), so it is zero. Invariance under the unit diagonal matrix in (21) is precisely the block condition in (20). Conversely that condition proves invariance, and also invariance under every Vn=V1nV_n=V_1^n. ▫\square

The quotient in (18) is a quotient of vector spaces with the specified VnV_n action. It is not an algebra quotient: it kills F0=1F_0=1. It retains the normal coefficients BjB_j through (Tf)(cj)=βjBj(Tf)(c_j)=\beta_j B_j, but forgets their value-row partners. No assertion about serial products follows from its positive norm.

For N≥3N\ge3, (19) has no converse lower bound on the full quotient. A coordinate of 𝒰Nf\mathcal U_N f other than zero can be nonzero while ϕN(f)=0\phi_N(f)=0; take GjG_j with j>0j>0 when available, or the odd simple row. At N=2N=2 the quotient has dimension one, but the crossing obstruction below still holds.

4. One original crossing leaves every such null quotient

The original finite crossing formulas are Rσ=κσ∑r=0D(−q)σrer,σ∈{1,−1}.(23) R^\sigma=\kappa^\sigma \sum_{r=0}^{D}(-q)^{\sigma r}e_r,\qquad \sigma\in\{1,-1\}. \qquad\text{(23)} They imply the exact polynomial identities TRσ=R−σ,ϕN(Rσ)=κσ(−q)σD,|ϕN(Rσ)|=1.(24) TR^\sigma=R^{-\sigma},\qquad \phi_N(R^\sigma)=\kappa^\sigma(-q)^{\sigma D}, \qquad |\phi_N(R^\sigma)|=1 . \qquad\text{(24)} For the first identity reverse (23) and use κ2(−q)D=1\kappa^2(-q)^D=1. All original phases are retained.

Theorem 3. There is no positive semidefinite Hermitian form 𝔥\mathfrak h satisfying all three properties 𝔥(V1f,V1f)=𝔥(f,f),|ϕN(f)|2≤CN𝔥(f,f), \mathfrak h(V_1f,V_1f)=\mathfrak h(f,f),\qquad |\phi_N(f)|^2\le C_N\mathfrak h(f,f), 𝔥(Rσf,Rσf)≤BN𝔥(f,f)for every f∈𝒜N,(25) \mathfrak h(R^\sigma f,R^\sigma f) \le B_N\mathfrak h(f,f) \quad\text{for every } f\in\mathcal A_N, \qquad\text{(25)} where CN,BNC_N,B_N are finite and σ\sigma is either one of the two original crossing signs. This holds separately at each level N≥2N\ge2.

Proof. Theorem 2 forces 𝔥(1,1)=0\mathfrak h(1,1)=0. The last inequality in (25) then forces 𝔥(Rσ,Rσ)=0\mathfrak h(R^\sigma,R^\sigma)=0, and the second forces ϕN(Rσ)=0\phi_N(R^\sigma)=0. This contradicts (24). ▫\square

The contradiction uses the actual unit input and one literal crossing; it does not require a coupon depending on an unknown closed scalar. In particular no finite crossing bound exists for (17), even if a level-dependent exponential constant is allowed.

The full prescribed one-crossing observation in the confluent basis is also explicit: ϕN(RσFj)=R(cj)−σ,ϕN(RσGj)=−σαjR(cj)−σ.(26) \phi_N(R^\sigma F_j)=R(c_j)^{-\sigma},\qquad \phi_N(R^\sigma G_j) =-\sigma\alpha_j R(c_j)^{-\sigma}. \qquad\text{(26)} Indeed ϕN(Fjf)=(Tf)(cj)\phi_N(F_jf)=(Tf)(c_j) and ϕN(Gjf)=(Tf)′(cj)\phi_N(G_jf)=(Tf)'(c_j), so (26) follows from (24). The odd simple row uses only the first formula. Combining (16) with (26), for every integer nn, ϕN(RσVnf)=κ−σ∑j∈𝒟Nλn+σ,j{Aj−(n+σ)αjBj}+𝟏Noddκ−σλn+σ,*A*.(27) \phi_N(R^\sigma V_nf) =\kappa^{-\sigma} \sum_{j\in\mathcal D_N} \lambda_{n+\sigma,j} \{A_j-(n+\sigma)\alpha_jB_j\} +\mathbf1_{N\ {\mathrm{ odd}}}\kappa^{-\sigma} \lambda_{n+\sigma,*}A_* . \qquad\text{(27)} Thus the crossing which exposes the null value rows also retains the normal shears and their original quadratic phases. Equation (27) is a coherent scalar sum, not a positive sum or a lower estimate.

5. Scope and complete direct sources

The general indefinite-closing obstruction, the positive Newton metric, and twist observation norms already have separate proofs. The added statements here are the explicit full F/GF/G Gram and coordinate closing, the classification (20) with its largest quotient (18), and the source-specific incompatibility (25). Positive low-degree linearization alone does not determine the G0G_0 coefficient extracted in (9). The positive Newton multiplication cone is also already known; no new positivity theorem for that cone is claimed here.

Exact identities hold with arbitrary ordinary free spectator coefficients. The positive forms and inequalities mean a fixed complex specialization of those coefficients. Subsequent products, turns, and traces remain the original ones. Theorem 3 concerns exact V1V_1 isometry on the whole space; it does not exclude controlled distortion on a restricted received source or a geometric norm with retained exponential costs.
