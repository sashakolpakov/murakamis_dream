Exact contraction of the full high Newton radical

Research note, 3 November 2026. The entire nilpotent high-index sector of the original edge kernel contracts to physical values with uniformly bounded coefficients. At both ends of an edge these coefficients give an explicit real positive-definite Gram matrix. Original crossing phases remain in the physical color sum, so this is an exact grouping and an upper bound, not a positivity assertion for the filled source.

The coefficient identity below is the signed form of the divided differences already used in the original low-color bound, equations (5)–(6). Square-zero nilpotence is also recorded in the arbitrary-power compiler, Proposition 2. The purpose here is their full high-index edge contraction, including a uniform bound through the last double node. This differs from the first-normal product-rule expansion in the grouped Ward-current note: a radical insertion consumes the local normal and leaves only the spectator’s value.

3. The whole radical edge becomes a physical Gram matrix

Separate the exact ordinary kernel as KN(z,w)=KNlow(z,w)+HNrad(z,w),HNrad(z,w)=∑r=0m−1eD−r(z)eD−r(w),KNlow(z,w)=∑j=0D−mej(z)ej(w).(17) \begin{split} K_N(z,w)&=K_N^{\mathrm{ low}}(z,w)+H_N^{\mathrm{ rad}}(z,w),\\ H_N^{\mathrm{ rad}}(z,w)&=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}e_{D-r}(z)e_{D-r}(w),\\ K_N^{\mathrm{ low}}(z,w)&=\sum_{j=0}^{D-m}e_j(z)e_j(w). \end{split} \qquad\text{(17)} At even levels the radical sector is every index j≥N/2j\ge N/2. At odd levels it is j≥(N+1)/2j\ge(N+1)/2; the middle polynomial stays in the low sector.

Let 𝖡=(bra)0≤r,a<m\mathsf B=(b_{ra})_{0\le r,a<m}, and set W=𝖡T𝖡,Wab=∑r=max⁡(a,b)m−1brabrb.(18) W=\mathsf B^T\mathsf B,\qquad W_{ab}=\sum_{r=\max(a,b)}^{m-1}b_{ra}b_{rb}. \qquad\text{(18)} Then for arbitrary original spectators f,gf,g and signed powers n,pn,p, (ϕN⊗ϕN)[Rn(z)Rp(w)HNrad(z,w)f(z)g(w)]=∑a,b=0m−1WabR(ca)nR(cb)pf(ca)g(cb).(19) \boxed{\quad (\phi_N\otimes\phi_N) \bigl[R^n(z)R^p(w)H_N^{\mathrm{ rad}}(z,w)f(z)g(w)\bigr] =\sum_{a,b=0}^{m-1}W_{ab}R(c_a)^nR(c_b)^p f(c_a)g(c_b). \quad} \qquad\text{(19)} The same identity holds with f(z)g(w)f(z)g(w) replaced by any joint spectator F(z,w;𝒖)F(z,w;\mathbf u), by replacing its final product of values with F(ca,cb;𝒖)F(c_a,c_b;\mathbf u). No independence assumption on outside variables is required.

Thus positive definiteness here supplies no uniform lower frame bound in the ordinary physical-value Euclidean norm. It cannot be used as an identity metric or as a spectral-gap lower bound for a specified source coefficient.

All nilpotence assertions here concern the physical relation PNP_N. They are not assertions about a scalar-smoothed algebra Pσ≠PNP_\sigma\ne P_N. Likewise, two radical factors multiply to zero at a contracted or canonically reduced vertex, but their ordinary product need not vanish in an unreduced free-boundary variable. The free-variable identities and their derivatives in (19) remain literal polynomial identities without imposing such a reduction.

4. Grouping all radical edges in a coupled source

This is the relevance to the power-three kernel obstruction. The high sector can be exponentially large at an off-grid complex geometric point before contraction. Its exact contribution after contraction is instead bounded by physical-color sources at polynomial cost. The next analytic estimate therefore concerns those sources, with their remaining traces and edge couplings. The all-color normal bound controls the first-normal determinant at each color, but explicitly leaves the coherent color sum unestimated. A positive local Gram matrix cannot replace that missing estimate.

For reflected conjugate data a Gram pairing can give a squared norm, but a general matching contribution is a bilinear sum, not a Hermitian quadratic form.

5. The single top mode and why removing it is insufficient

The special term eD(z)eD(w)e_D(z)e_D(w) has an especially simple interpretation. Equations (6) and (9) at r=0r=0 give ϕN(RneDf)=κnf(c0),KN(c0,w)=1.(31) \phi_N(R^n e_D f)=\kappa^n f(c_0),\qquad K_N(c_0,w)=1. \qquad\text{(31)} The first identity already appears in the free-boundary crossing identity. Let ZΓ∘Z^\circ_\Gamma use KN−eD⊗eDK_N-e_D\otimes e_D on its selected–selected edges and the original kernel elsewhere. Expansion as above now gives ZΓ=∑M⊆EImatchingκ∑v∈V(M)nvZΓ\V(M)∘.(32) Z_\Gamma= \sum_{M\subseteq E_I\ {\mathrm{ matching}}} \kappa^{\sum_{v\in V(M)}n_v} Z^\circ_{\Gamma\setminus V(M)}. \qquad\text{(32)} Here the graph on the right loses the matched vertices and every edge incident to them, since all those edges evaluate to one. An isolated surviving selected vertex still carries its original factor ϕN(Rnv)\phi_N(R^{n_v}). The deletion in (32) is an algebraic contraction, not an asserted topological move on the knot complement.

This deletion rule is special to the single top mode. For the full radical, the physical evaluations in (24) must remain. Moreover, at the power-three point in the linked obstruction theorem, the next term eD−1(z)eD−1(w)e_{D-1}(z)e_{D-1}(w) differs from the top by a factor of order N−4N^{-4}, and has the same exponential rate. Removing any fixed number of top terms therefore does not restore the bare geometric expansion. The full radical contraction in (25) addresses the entire sector instead.

The normalization in (39) uses the actual surviving spectral normal. Thus the Fourier estimate includes both parities and does not discard derivative data. It explains the distinction between the real physical estimates and the complex obstruction: the radical is polynomially bounded on the entire real period, but (36) allows exponential growth at a fixed nonzero height. No polynomial bound on a fixed complex strip, or lower bound for a coherent traced source, follows from (34).
