An all-transfer logarithmic collar for the actual boundary Ward source

Written research proof, 23 November 2026.

The complete boundary Ward sum is one coefficient of an actual Euler matrix times a finite logarithmic derivative. No inverse of a nilpotent lowering operator is needed. On a coefficient circle approaching the unit circle at distance of order (log⁡log⁡N)/N(\log\log N)/N, that logarithmic derivative has only its original vacuum kernel. After one literal lowering shift and its explicit scale of order NN are extracted, the remaining matrix and its inverse have logarithmic and bounded norms. A diagonal gauge with polylogarithmic conditioning extends inverse control to the full unit activity disk. The physical degree grading then extracts every Ward coefficient by a unitary Fourier projection, with no small-activity hypothesis and no additional coefficient-contour loss.

This controls an entire normal factor before geometric evaluation. The original factorial, Euler matrix, crossing, marked co-source and coefficient integral remain. No lower bound for that coefficient or for the closed scalar is inferred.

3. Normalizing the q-log factor

4. Accretivity and the genuine remaining endpoint

5. Polylogarithmic conditioning on the full unit disk

The near-unit collar can be transported to unit activity by a stated physical diagonal gauge, whose cost is polylogarithmic. On the head where LL acts put Sρ|j⟩=ρ−j|j⟩,SρLSρ−1=ρL,∥Sρ∥∥Sρ−1∥=ρ−d.(21) S_\rho|j\rangle=\rho^{-j}|j\rangle,\qquad S_\rho L S_\rho^{-1}=\rho L,\qquad \|S_\rho\|\|S_\rho^{-1}\|=\rho^{-d}. \qquad\text{(21)} Tensor with identities on all other heads. This is only a comparison of the indicated matrix factors; no source or cap is changed. With ρ=ρN\rho=\rho_N, one has ρN−d=(log⁡N)4d/N≤(log⁡N)4\rho_N^{-d}=(\log N)^{4d/N}\le(\log N)^4. Polynomial functional calculus in (21), followed by the inverse bound of (18), proves the inverse bound below. Its upper bound is even simpler: (12) gives ∑n=1N−1|rN,n|≤Clog⁡N\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}|r_{N,n}|\le C\log N, so the triangle inequality proves it directly on the full unit disk. Thus ∥UN(zL)∥≤Clog⁡N,∥UN(zL)−1∥≤C(log⁡N)4,|z|≤1,N≥N0.(22) \boxed{\quad \|U_N(zL)\|\le C\log N,\qquad \|U_N(zL)^{-1}\|\le C(\log N)^4, \qquad |z|\le1,\quad N\ge N_0. \quad} \qquad\text{(22)} Thus there is no small physical activity assumption in this estimate. At |z|=1|z|=1, equations (10),(19),(22) show that the nonzero singular values of ℓN(zL)\ell_N(zL) lie in [cN/(log⁡N)4,CNlog⁡N][cN/(\log N)^4,CN\log N]. For every compatible left matrix BB, in either the physical operator norm or the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, cN(log⁡N)4∥BL∥≤∥BℓN(zL)∥≤CNlog⁡N∥BL∥,|z|=1,N≥N0.(23) \frac{cN}{(\log N)^4}\|BL\|\le\|B\ell_N(zL)\| \le CN\log N\,\|BL\|, \qquad |z|=1,\quad N\ge N_0. \qquad\text{(23)} Its kernel and image are still exactly (19). The same proof as (20) applies, now using the bounds of (22). The gauge has not introduced an unknown metric inverse or an exponential conditioning factor.

6. Physical Fourier extraction at unit activity

Let 𝗇\mathsf n be the total integer occupation on the remaining rr heads and Dθ=e−iθ𝗇D_\theta=e^{-i\theta\mathsf n}. The operators EE and LL both lower that degree by one. The literal reduced crossing bb preserves it: each original crossing and every term of the prefix and its inverse preserve total occupation, and the first-head zero extraction retains the remaining degree. Therefore DθEDθ−1=eiθE,DθLDθ−1=eiθL,DθbDθ−1=b.(24) D_\theta E D_\theta^{-1}=e^{i\theta}E,\qquad D_\theta L D_\theta^{-1}=e^{i\theta}L,\qquad D_\theta bD_\theta^{-1}=b. \qquad\text{(24)} All these diagonal matrices are unitary in the original Euclidean basis. Set F(ξ)=ℰN(ξE)bℓN(aξL)F(\xi)=\mathcal E_N(\xi E)b\ell_N(a\xi L). Equation (24) gives the exact operator identity F(eiθ)=DθF(1)Dθ−1,[ξt]F=𝖯tF(1),𝖯tB=12π∫02πe−itθDθBDθ−1dθ.(25) F(e^{i\theta})=D_\theta F(1)D_\theta^{-1},\qquad [\xi^t]F=\mathsf P_tF(1),\quad \mathsf P_tB=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{-it\theta}D_\theta BD_\theta^{-1}\,d\theta. \qquad\text{(25)} The map 𝖯t\mathsf P_t keeps precisely the entries whose output degree is the input degree minus tt. Its operator norm as a map on operator norm is at most one, by unitary averaging and the triangle inequality. In Hilbert–Schmidt norm it is an orthogonal projection: the different degree differences are disjoint matrix-entry supports. In particular they obey Parseval on this full matrix space. This projection is not an assertion about a selected geometric window.

Combining (6),(23),(25) proves the uniform actual Ward estimate ∥𝔇t∥≤Cm|Ft|Nlog⁡N∥ℰN(E)bL∥,1≤t<N,N≥N0,(26) \boxed{\quad \|\mathfrak D_t\| \le C m|F_t|N\log N\, \|\mathcal E_N(E)bL\|, \qquad 1\le t<N,\quad N\ge N_0, \quad} \qquad\text{(26)} in either of the two stated physical norms. Thus all transfers are resummed before estimation, and extraction has norm at most one. The same unit-activity q-log factor estimate (23) permits any left exterior inside the norm, while the extraction identity (25) is applied before such an exterior unless it respects the degree action. The factorial FtF_t, which can carry exponential size, is explicitly retained. Terms of FF with degree at least NN can occur; (25) projects them out for the specified observation and does not replace the full matrix F(1)F(1) by a low-degree truncation.

The equality (9) keeps the original co-source on this normal insertion. Its contour can have cancellation, and its arbitrary co-source can annihilate a nonzero matrix. Consequently (23) does not give a lower bound after coefficient extraction or for the marked scalar. The full-activity Euler matrix, the crossing factor and their geometric contour remain separate analytic objects. The small-activity factorization [C] and coherent chart [F] are not being extended to unit activity by (26).

The exact negative-boundary and word cocycle laws of [W, (21)] may be applied to (6), retaining their actual inverse matrices and every normal insertion. No bound for those inverses or a general word’s remaining co-source is supplied by (18). All identities are at fixed quantum root; no quantum-normal derivative or identification of an off-circle geometric clock with a physical weight is used.
