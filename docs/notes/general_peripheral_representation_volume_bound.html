The representation-volume bound with general peripheral holonomy

28 October 2026. The independently proved smearing argument for boundary-parabolic representations also bounds representations whose peripheral images preserve a geodesic axis. Its proof needs integrable pullback volume and integrable finite- radius face errors, rather than a globally bounded derivative of the developing map. Axis-valued cusp maps supply exactly those properties.

Consequently the marked regulator of EVERY actual SL2(C) representation of a finite-volume hyperbolic knot group has absolute value at most the manifold volume. This includes unit meridian and nonunit longitude. It supplies a geometric bound for a genuine peripheral-curve primitive; it does not identify a quantum-A component with that curve, or estimate the actual sourced quantum Green function.

1. Peripheral classification and an equivariant cusp development

Let X=Gamma be an oriented complete finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold and rho:Gamma->SL2(C), followed by its PSL2 action. Each peripheral image consists of commuting matrices. If one has distinct eigenvalues, both common eigenlines are preserved by every commuting matrix, so the image preserves their joining geodesic axis. Otherwise the image is central or has a nontrivial unipotent, whose commuting matrices preserve its unique eigenline and act parabolically there. Thus each cusp is either of axis type or of the boundary-parabolic type already treated. Central images may be treated as axis type with zero translation. No discreteness or irreducibility is required.

For an axis-type cusp use domain coordinates (w,y), with w in C, y>Y and peripheral translations w->w+lambda. Choose an oriented target axis gamma(t), parametrized by hyperbolic distance. Its peripheral action is

rho(gamma_0) gamma(t)=gamma(t+chi(lambda_gamma0)),

where chi is the real translation character. The rotations about the same axis act trivially ON the axis. Extend chi from the lattice to a real-linear function of w and set

f(w,y)=gamma(chi(w)+log y).                        (1)

This is exactly peripheral-equivariant. It is smooth on finite cusp truncations and its image has dimension at most one. Hence

f^*(vol_H3)=0 on the ENTIRE axis cusp.              (2)

It need not be globally Lipschitz: its horizontal derivative in the hyperbolic domain metric can grow like y. Equation (2), not a false global Lipschitz assertion, is what is used below.

On parabolic cusps retain the old affine conical map, which has a uniform hyperbolic derivative bound and integrable pullback volume. Extend over the compact truncated core equivariantly by geodesic interpolation on a finite triangulation and a collar. Contractibility of the target gives the extension. The resulting f is locally Lipschitz, has a finite derivative bound on every fixed metric neighborhood of the truncated core, and has absolutely integrable pullback volume on X. Define

Vol(rho)=integral_X f^*(vol_H3).                   (3)

2. Choice independence and the actual regulator normalization

Two axis-cusp maps for the same noncentral peripheral image can be joined by an equivariant axis-valued homotopy. Its boundary-torus homotopy has zero 3-volume because it lies in that axis. Reversing the chosen axis endpoint is also such a homotopy. For a central peripheral image the maps may be independent of w; a homotopy then has zero horizontal derivatives and again zero boundary 3-volume. Parabolic-cusp choices have the vanishing boundary estimate already proved in the linked note. Stokes on a truncated core therefore proves that (3) is independent of these choices.

Map (1) tends to a peripheral fixed ideal endpoint as y tends to infinity. Truncate an ideal fundamental chain and compare its developed straightening with f by the same face-compatible geodesic homotopy on the compact pieces. Internal faces cancel. The comparison with the IDEAL straightening is not generally axis-valued on a cusp: the other developed vertices need not lie on that axis. Its boundary term instead vanishes by the following explicit estimate.

Put the selected fixed endpoint at infinity and the preserved axis at horizontal coordinate zero. An ideal straightening can be parametrized conically near that vertex, along the vertical geodesic rays to infinity. On a finite triangulation of a fundamental cusp cross-section its map has form

f_cone(w,y)=(a(w),y b(w)), b(w)>0.                 (4a)

Here a,b and their piecewise first derivatives are bounded on the fundamental cross-section, and b is bounded below there. Extend these functions using peripheral equivariance. If that peripheral acts by (z,y)->(l_gamma z,|l_gamma|y), their transformation rules are

a(w+lambda)=l_gamma a(w),
b(w+lambda)=|l_gamma|b(w).

The conical parametrization is chosen on the common cusp cross-section and its faces before extending over the finite tetrahedral interiors; thus adjacent straight faces use the same tail parametrization. It is an allowed proper straightening of the same developed ideal chain. For a tetrahedron with vertex infinity, its faces incident to that vertex are vertical, and its deep tail is foliated by these vertical rays; this gives the claimed conical choice after subdivision.

Join the axis map to (4a) by the EXPLICIT equivariant homotopy

H_t(w,y)=(t a(w), y exp((1-t)chi(w)+t Log b(w))).

On a fundamental cross-section the horizontal coordinates and their w,t derivatives are O(1), while its height is comparable to y and its height derivatives are O(y). The hyperbolic volume form is height^-3 dx_1 wedge dx_2 wedge dheight. Hence

integral_(cusp torus at y times [0,1]) |H^*vol|
                                  <=C/y^2 ->0.   (4b)

This estimate is independent of any assertion of global Lipschitzness or a zero comparison volume. On parabolic cusp tails use the old vanishing horospherical estimate. Thus, with the SAME marked fundamental-cycle convention,

Vol(rho)=sum_Delta oriented Vol(straight_rho Delta)
        =sum_Delta D(z_Delta).                    (4)

Auxiliary ideal flags and degenerate tetrahedra are handled by the finite-chain approximation used in the linked proof. This identifies the absolute regulator normalization; it is not an arbitrarily shifted primitive of a peripheral differential.

3. Finite-radius smearing without a global Lipschitz bound

Use the finite regular tetrahedron T_R and symmetric Lorentz straightening/homotopy of the linked smearing proof. For each fixed R its diameter is finite. That proof compares the developed straight tetrahedron with integral_(g T_R) f^*vol by a sum of FOUR oriented face-prism errors b_rho(g,face).

Their integrability is the only new issue. On an axis cusp, if a domain face lies entirely in one lifted cusp horoball, both f on that face and the straight interpolation of its three vertex images lie on the SAME target axis. The complete face-prism homotopy is axis-valued, so

b_rho(g,face)=0 there.                            (5)

A cusp horoball is convex. For fixed R, every remaining domain face which encounters an axis cusp and is not contained in it has its frame basepoint in a FIXED metric neighborhood of the truncated core. That neighborhood is compact modulo Gamma. On it f has some finite Lipschitz bound L_R, so the finite-radius face-prism estimate of the old proof bounds b there. On parabolic cusps the old global cusp bound applies. Hence every fixed-R face error is absolutely integrable on Gamma+(H3).

No bound uniform in R is asserted or needed. For EACH fixed R the four ordered regular faces are congruent by orientation-preserving isometries. Right Haar invariance makes their integrated error values identical; their alternating sum is exactly zero. Fubini for f^*vol is justified by its absolute integrability. With frame-fiber Haar mass normalized to one, this gives

Vol(T_R) Vol(rho)
   =integral_(Gamma\G) Vol(straight f(g T_R)) dg. (6)

Every target straight tetrahedron has absolute volume at most v3, by the independently proved ideal-tetrahedron bound. The Haar mass of the frame quotient is Vol(X). Therefore

|Vol(rho)|<=v3 Vol(X)/Vol(T_R).

Let R tend to infinity, so Vol(T_R) tends to v3. This proves

|Vol(rho)|<=Vol(X).                              (7)

There is no interchange of an unbounded family of face errors in this last step: their cancellation was exact separately at every finite R. Any joint projective shape limit of actual representation families has the same bound, because each D extends continuously through 0,1,infinity.

4. Consequence and limitation for the actual A-curve route

On a genuinely identified peripheral representation curve, retain the actual eigenvalues M and ell and the marked Bloch chain. Its already proved boundary identity is

delta beta=-2 M wedge ell.

The Bloch–Wigner differential gives, along |M|=1,

dR=2 log|ell| dArg M
   =log|ell| dArg Y, Y=M^2.                      (8)

Thus R is an ABSOLUTELY normalized primitive of the logarithmic longitude modulus on a specified representation branch, and (7) bounds it by the knot volume, including along nonunit-longitude arcs. For two points on that same branch,

|integral log|ell| dArg Y|<=2Vol(X).              (9)

If the initial marked regulator is independently known to be zero, the bound relative to it improves to Vol(X). A unitary meridian alone does not make that initial regulator zero; the complete hyperbolic representation is an immediate example.

This does NOT bound the integral of the pointwise largest root of an arbitrary quantum-A symbol: that prescription may switch branches and forgets their regulator constants. Nor does it identify the classical symbol of an arbitrary all-level annihilator with a genuine peripheral curve. The existing actual-state reciprocity theorem expressly makes no such identification. Finally a quantum Green response requires its original affine/normal source, finite-jet resonance and boundary matching determinant. Bound (7) is a proved geometric input to that problem, not a substitute for the missing sourced transfer estimate.
