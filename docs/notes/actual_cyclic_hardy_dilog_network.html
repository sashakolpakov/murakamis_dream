An exact scalar cyclic-dilogarithm network for the original knot source

The subsequent continuous Gaussian theorem completes this construction: its exact original-source period has ordinary Gaussian weights and no finite Gaussian scalar kernel either. The intermediate construction below retains the finite Gaussian while proving the cyclic/Hardy support and initial-cycle identities.

28 October 2026. For every actual signed braid word, the scalar Euler/Gaussian Cauchy representation can be replaced by one using the literature norm-one cyclic dilogarithm, its inverse, ordinary Hardy Cauchy kernels, and the full finite marked Gaussian. All finite Dirichlet numerators and all scalar finite Euler polynomials are absent from this presentation. The original period, including its source and peripheral/framing line, is exactly unchanged.

This is an exact function-and-cycle representation, not a replacement of the invariant by a positive model or an asymptotic substitution. The initial cycle and every norm root are specified below. Moving that cycle to a geometric Rivin chain remains unproved.

The audit and results check the full literature normalization, nonzero added cyclic tails, both actual signed crossings, and sixteen original whole-word pivotal contractions. Exact rational radius chambers are checked independently of those finite-field identities.

1. Fix the literature normalization as a function

Let Q=e2πi/NQ=e^{2\pi i/N}, N≥2N\ge2. Use precisely the alternate literature cyclic dilogarithm from four_cyclic_atoms.md:

𝒱N(t)=(1−tN)(N−1)/(2N)∏ℓ=1N−1(1−Qℓt)−ℓ/N,𝒰N(t)=𝒱N(t)−1. \mathcal V_N(t)= (1-t^N)^{(N-1)/(2N)} \prod_{\ell=1}^{N-1}(1-Q^\ell t)^{-\ell/N}, \qquad \mathcal U_N(t)=\mathcal V_N(t)^{-1}. \tag{1}

Every power is the analytic branch equal to one at t=0t=0. In particular the full factor (1−tN)(N−1)/(2N)(1-t^N)^{(N-1)/(2N)} is retained. This is the normalized function 𝔡N\mathfrak d_N, not the unnormalized weighted product often denoted DND_N. Even-level half roots use this same anchor.

On |t|<1|t|<1 its convergent logarithm is exactly

log⁡𝒱N(t)=∑m≥1N∤mtmm(Qm−1). \log\mathcal V_N(t)= \sum_{\substack{m\ge1\\N\nmid m}} \frac{t^m}{m(Q^m-1)}. \tag{2}

Indeed ∑ℓ=1N−1ℓQℓm=N/(Qm−1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\ell Q^{\ell m} =N/(Q^m-1) at nonresonant degrees. At m=Nkm=Nk the norm factor in (1) cancels the resonant logarithm of the weighted product. Thus

∏j=0N−1𝒱N(Qjt)=1 \prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathcal V_N(Q^jt)=1 \tag{3}

on the anchored component.

For the ACTUAL finite Euler polynomials

uN(t)=∑h=0N−1th/Fh,vN(t)=∑h=0N−1(−1)hQh(h−1)/2th/Fh, u_N(t)=\sum_{h=0}^{N-1}t^h/F_h,\quad v_N(t)=\sum_{h=0}^{N-1} (-1)^hQ^{h(h-1)/2}t^h/F_h,

comparison of their nilpotent logarithms gives

𝒰N(t)−uN(t)∈tNℂ[[t]],𝒱N(t)−vN(t)∈tNℂ[[t]]. \mathcal U_N(t)-u_N(t)\in t^N\mathbb C[[t]],\qquad \mathcal V_N(t)-v_N(t)\in t^N\mathbb C[[t]]. \tag{4}

This finite-jet observation alone is not a period theorem. The ordered support argument below proves where ALL the additional coefficients disappear in the actual source.

2. Three different support mechanisms

Work on nonnegative polynomial heads and use the Hardy Cauchy kernel K+(t)=1/(1−t)K_+(t)=1/(1-t), as in the exact Hardy replacement. Keep the original strict crossing order and original coefficients.

Transfer atoms

Before EVERY transfer in the strict actual crossing and boundary fusion words, both active indices lie in 0,…,N−10,\ldots,N-1. For a transfer J−⊗J+J_-\otimes J_+, every added power of order h≥Nh\ge N lowers the first index below zero. Hardy projection annihilates it. The reversed transfer has the same property on its second index. Therefore

𝒰N(x−1J−⊗J+),𝒱N(x−1J−⊗J+) \mathcal U_N(x^{-1}J_-\otimes J_+),\quad \mathcal V_N(x^{-1}J_-\otimes J_+)

act exactly as the original finite transfer atoms on these actual inputs. The infinite series terminates on each such input by its LOWERING head; no upper truncation is imposed.

Reciprocal factorial diagonals

The scalar kernel uN(z/w)u_N(z/w) is replaced by 𝒰N(z/w)\mathcal U_N(z/w) at |z/w|<1|z/w|<1. It now has genuinely nonzero diagonal coefficients at indices above the original head. However, the active input of every reciprocal-factorial diagonal in the strict word is already in the head:

  • the initial second head of inverse fusion is in the head;
  • after its lowering transfer the first head is still in it;
  • in the final fusion, the central transfer has only lowered its second head.

Thus none of the added diagonal coefficients is ever used.

Positive factorial diagonals

The actual factorial kernel is

∑a=0N−1Fata=NtN−1vN(Q/t). \sum_{a=0}^{N-1}F_a t^a=N t^{N-1}v_N(Q/t).

Replace it by the full Laurent function

ℱN(t)=NtN−1𝒱N(Q/t),|t|>1. \mathcal F_N(t)=N t^{N-1}\mathcal V_N(Q/t), \qquad |t|>1. \tag{5}

Its powers tat^a for 0≤a<N0\le a<N have exactly the original coefficients FaF_a. It has NO power tat^a with a≥Na\ge N. All added terms have a<0a<0.

In a diagonal Cauchy contraction, a term ta=(z/w)at^a=(z/w)^a can pair only with input index aa. Nonnegative polynomial inputs therefore do not see any of the added negative powers. Consequently (5), despite being a different scalar function, acts exactly as the original finite factorial diagonal on all nonnegative polynomial inputs. In particular it still kills every upper overflow. All factors NN and tN−1t^{N-1} in (5) are retained.

3. The support induction is simultaneous, not a circular assumption

Let ℱ\mathcal F denote the operator represented by (5), and let ℱ−\mathcal F^{-} be the operator with scalar kernel 𝒰N(z/w)\mathcal U_N(z/w). On head inputs use the completed fusions

𝒞=ℱ2𝒰−(x)ℱ1ℱ2−,𝒞¯=ℱ2ℱ1−𝒱−(x)ℱ2−. \begin{split} \mathcal C&=\mathcal F_2\, \mathcal U_-(x)\,\mathcal F_1\,\mathcal F_2^{-},\\ \overline{\mathcal C}&=\mathcal F_2\mathcal F_1^{-} \mathcal V_-(x)\,\mathcal F_2^{-}. \end{split} \tag{6}

Starting from a head input, the first reciprocal diagonal in 𝒞¯\overline{\mathcal C} has a head input. Its transfer lowers the first index, so all added powers vanish and the first index remains in the head. The next reciprocal diagonal is therefore also exact. The last positive factorial kills any second-index overflow. This proves

𝒞¯i=iC−1. \overline{\mathcal C}i=iC^{-1}. \tag{7}

For 𝒞\mathcal C, its first reciprocal diagonal is exact on a head second index. The positive factorial on head one enforces that head’s support BEFORE the transfer. The transfer is then exactly finite, and the last positive factorial kills its possible second-head overflow. Moreover 𝒞\mathcal C kills any nonnegative input outside the head pair: an oversized first index is killed before transfer; an oversized second index can only increase before the final factorial kills it.

Hence 𝒞=P𝒞P\mathcal C=P\mathcal CP and 𝒞i=iC\mathcal Ci=iC. The central transfer in either signed crossing receives a head input by (7); its added high powers vanish. Its possible first-head overflow is killed by 𝒞\mathcal C. The full marked Gaussian is kept exactly as before. Therefore the two completed signed operators satisfy

ℛ±i=iR±. \boxed{\qquad \mathcal R_\pm i=iR_\pm. \qquad} \tag{8}

The same proof applies to the typed unequal-color formula, using the original input/output fusions and its prescribed Euler argument. No common Cartan scalar is reset.

By induction, for EVERY signed word, with any original finite-head coupons BETWEEN complete crossings,

𝒲(β)ib=ibW(β). \mathcal W(\beta)i_b=i_bW(\beta). \tag{9}

This includes the full original source and Hopf Ward pairings. It does not authorize arbitrary insertions between factors inside (6), nor infer a source identity for an independently chosen cyclic/geometric sector.

4. A simultaneous, explicit initial radius chamber

The scalar kernels in this representation are:

5. The original fixed charged period

Use the existing full fused-plat source

$$ P_{\beta,N}=\chi_{\beta,N}N^{1-k} \langle e_{\rm out},B_\beta e_{\rm in}\rangle, $$

with its specified (d,0)(d,0) input pairs, (0,d)(0,d) ordinary output pairs and (d,0)(d,0) marked output pair. The full-word Ward corner is used only after assembly, as in its original proof. Expand BβB_\beta into the actual factors, replace them by (10), and insert the reset identities above.

If the resulting scalar kernels are 𝒦ℓ\mathcal K_\ell, the same ORIGINAL period is exactly

$$ \boxed{\quad P_{\beta,N}=\chi_{\beta,N}N^{1-k} \operatorname{CT} (z^0)^{a_{\rm in}}(z^{L})^{-a_{\rm out}} \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \mathcal K_\ell(z^\ell,z^{\ell-1}). \quad} \tag{12} $$

Its contours are the product circles just constructed, with the anchored lifts from (1). All original peripheral, Cartan, pivot and framing factors are included exactly once. The equality holds at all primitive levels, even or composite, and in the original root-meridian family.

Consequently any specified root-specialized quantum A-operator annihilating that original meridian period also annihilates (12), with its full peripheral factors. This is equality of the selected function, not a new assertion identifying an operator’s classical symbol with the geometric A-polynomial.

There are no scalar finite uN/vNu_N/v_N truncations and no finite Dirichlet numerator factors in (12). This does not erase boundary information: it specifies which global functions and initial cycle reproduce that information. Their norm divisors and matched residue/branch corrections must be retained in any subsequent contour transport.

The integer source charge is unchanged: total plat degree is k(N−1)k(N-1), and after the ordinary caps the remaining pair has D+1=ND+1=N. That is the actual cap charge. Equation (12) does not identify an unassigned geometric Pachner edge with that class.

An exact internal three-index charge is already available

There is also a genuine internal affine charge in this particular network. In (5), a nonnegative head index aa and the selected cyclic Taylor index rr satisfy exactly

a+r=N−1,a+r+1=N. a+r=N-1,\qquad a+r+1=N. \tag{13}

The +1+1 is forced by the original reflected monomial tN−1t^{N-1}, not chosen by a flattening convention. For an actual fusion coefficient write

h=a−u=v−b,r1=N−1−a,r2=N−1−v. h=a-u=v-b,\qquad r_1=N-1-a,\qquad r_2=N-1-v.

The two positive-factorial kernels in that fusion then give

(u+1)+h+r1=N,(b+1)+h+r2=N. \boxed{\quad (u+1)+h+r_1=N,\qquad (b+1)+h+r_2=N. \quad} \tag{14}

All entries are nonnegative, and the first entry of each triple is positive. Interior routes with h,ri>0h,r_i>0 give strictly positive triples. The inverse fusion retains the second equation, from its positive output factorial.

Thus actual fusion-network clocks can form an exact three-index charge-one sector, with its original affine shift. This is a more specific dictionary than total-degree conservation alone. It does not yet identify either triple in (14) with the three angles around the last geometric Pachner edge. Such an identification must come from the typed five-term/source map, not from renaming these index variables.

The remaining conjectural step is therefore genuinely global: transport this marked scalar cyclic-dilogarithm/Gaussian cycle to the positive ideal geometric chain, with all norm divisors, compatible root frames and original source charges retained, and prove its geometric coefficient and dominance. No such transport or volume limit is inferred from the exact identity (12).

In the stronger continuous version just linked, the Gaussian is also an ordinary integral over a specified fibered Cauchy cycle. Joint absolute convergence and uniform initial-cycle leading symbols are proved there. The remaining global-cycle statement is unchanged; a finite Gaussian endpoint sector is no longer an unresolved part of the scalar representation.
