The saturated four-port factorial action is a hyperideal tetrahedron volume

29 October 2026. The positive FACE-NORMALIZED Racah partition has an explicit local geometric action. Six folded Casimir labels determine the six dihedral angles of a truncated hyperideal tetrahedron; the four supported face sums are precisely its four vertex-angle conditions. The optimized action equals its actual hyperbolic volume divided by pi. This is proved here from a Lorentz Gram construction, finite rational identities and a direct first-variation calculation. No asymptotic 6j volume theorem is an input.

The face normalization is essential. This note does not identify an arbitrary physical recoupling energy with that normalization, discard high partners/normals, or infer a global knot triangulation from one local tetrahedron. The actual four-port source kernel and its weighting must be matched separately.

1. The exact local angle dictionary and positive scalar action

Order the six colors as (a,b,c,d,e,f). The four vertex triples and three opposite quadrilateral sums are

F=(a+b+e, c+d+e, a+d+f, b+c+f),
Q=(a+b+c+d, a+c+e+f, b+d+e+f).                (1)

For the geometric theorem first take real scaled colors in

0<a,b,c,d,e,f<1/2, every F_i>1.               (2)

Put alpha_a=pi-2pi a, and similarly for the other five. These angles are in (0,pi), and at each of the four indicated vertices

sum_incident alpha=3pi-2pi F_i<pi.            (3)

Thus each vertex will be hyperideal, with a geodesic polar truncation triangle of area pi-sum alpha=2pi(F_i-1). All triangle deficits F_i-2(color in that face) are strictly between 0 and 1.

The source-compatible quantum scaling is EXACTLY

a=(j_a+1/2)/N, alpha_a=pi-(2j_a+1)pi/N,
c_(j_a)=q^(2j_a+1)+q^(-2j_a-1)=-2cos alpha_a. (4)

If the six folded integer labels are <=floor((N-1)/2) and every face sum is >=N-1, then (3) is at most pi-pi/N. Its truncation triangle area is (2pi/N)(faceSum-(N-1)+1/2). The only zero edge angles are the odd simple Steinberg labels. They are treated by continuous limiting geometry, not as strictly positive interior tetrahedral angles. These are internal Casimir angle coordinates; (4) is not an auxiliary-meridian identification.

Let L(t)=integral_0^t log|2sin pi u|du, with period 1. On the interval max F_i<Z<min Q_k define

A(Z)=L(Z-1)-sum_(i=1)^4 L(Z-F_i)
                     -sum_(k=1)^3 L(Q_k-Z),
M=(1/2)sum_faces [sum_(edges in face)L(F_i-2edge)-L(F_i-1)],
Psi=max_Z A(Z)+M.                            (5)

The interval is nonempty: every Q_k-F_i is a positive triangle deficit from one of the other faces. It lies in (1,2).

For reference the finite positive scalar producing (5) is

R_N=N sum_(max faceSum<=z<=min quadSum)
   s_(z-(N-1)) /
     {product_faces s_(z-faceSum) product_quads s_(quadSum-z)},
D_face=product_(edges in face)s_(faceSum-2edge)
                                   /[N s_(faceSum-(N-1))],
W_N=R_N sqrt(product_faces D_face).           (6)

All factors in (6) are positive. It is the root of the explicitly normalized squared generic factorial expression, not the bare Racah sum. The phase-coherent root sum follows because its numerator has exactly one [N] zero and its seven denominator indices are <N. The finite phase/metric statements are proved in the supported Racah theorem. For the shifted scaled variables (4), use Z=(z+7/4)/N:

Z-F_i=(z-faceSum+1/4)/N,
Q_k-Z=(quadSum-z+1/4)/N.

Uniform all-index factorial estimates then give (5) as the limiting logarithmic action, with O(log N) endpoint errors. The geometric identification of this action is independent of a proposed source normalization. At six colors 1/3 the bare optimized A is 3v3/pi, whereas Psi=v3/pi; omitting M would already give the wrong volume. In these conventions log W_N/N has action Psi; the SQUARED weight W_N^2 has action 2Psi. That factor must be retained in a later energy or doubled-source comparison.

2. An own existence/uniqueness construction of the tetrahedron

Number the vertices by the four triples in (1). The edge endpoints are

a:13, b:14, c:24, d:23, e:12, f:34.

Let n_i be its four outward face normals, face i opposite vertex i. Their prescribed Lorentz Gram matrix is

G_ii=1,
G_12=-cos alpha_f, G_13=-cos alpha_c, G_14=-cos alpha_d,
G_23=-cos alpha_b, G_24=-cos alpha_a, G_34=-cos alpha_e. (7)

We prove that G has signature (3,1), rather than assuming an angle realization theorem. Every three-by-three principal minor corresponds to three angles A,B,C at a vertex, A+B+C<pi. Its determinant is

-4 cos((A+B+C)/2)cos((A+B-C)/2)
       cos((A-B+C)/2)cos((-A+B+C)/2)<0.        (8)

Its two-by-two minors are sin^2(alpha)>0, so that block has signature (2,1) and can be realized in Lorentz three-space. Its inverse has all entries negative: its off-diagonal cofactors have numerator cos C+cos A cos B>sin A sin B>0 and its determinant is negative.

If the three inner products of the fourth normal with this block are nonpositive, its projection to the block has nonpositive square. Adding a new spacelike coordinate makes its square one, with a nonzero fourth component. This constructs G with signature (3,1).

Otherwise choose a nonacute dihedral A>=pi/2 among these products and call its two normals n_0,n_1. At most two dihedrals can be nonacute, and two such edges must be opposite, because any two meeting at a vertex have sum <pi. The other products of n_0 are -cos B,-cos C, and those of n_1 are -cos D,-cos E. The three old normals have last mutual angle F, with D+E+F<pi. The vertex conditions imply

B+D<pi-A, C+E<pi-A.

In n_1-perpendicular Lorentz space define

m_2=(n_2+cos D n_1)/sin D,
m_3=(n_3+cos E n_1)/sin E,
<m_2,m_3>=-(cos F+cos D cos E)/(sin D sin E)<-1.

Write n_0=(-cos A)n_1+sin A u. The two required products of u with m_2,m_3 are both <-1: for example

<u,m_2>=(-cos B-cos A cos D)/(sin A sin D)<-1.

The inverse Gram matrix of m_2,m_3 has every entry negative. Thus their prescribed negative-product projection of u has negative square. An additional spacelike coordinate again makes u^2=1 and constructs the fourth normal independently. This proves det G<0 throughout (2), including the possible two opposite obtuse edges.

Let C=adj(G). Its diagonal entries are negative by (8), so the dual vectors v_i=-sum_j(G^-1)_ij n_j have positive squares. Their off-diagonal products are strictly negative. Indeed Jacobi’s minor identity gives

(G^-1)_ii(G^-1)_jj-(G^-1)_ij^2
                   =sin^2(alpha_complement)/det G<0.

No off-diagonal inverse entry can therefore vanish. The angle domain is convex and connected, and at a small regular angle all those entries are negative; continuity fixes their sign everywhere.

Normalize the v_i to square one. Every pair has product <-1, so their projective edge crosses the Klein ball. Their sum is timelike, and has negative product with every v_i. It supplies an interior point of the four face halfspaces AND the four vertex-polar halfspaces. Cutting the six projective edges by those polar planes gives twelve finite hyperbolic vertices. Each lies strictly on the proper side of the other two polar planes, by the negative products. The result is a compact convex truncated tetrahedron, with four triangular polar faces and four right-angled hexagonal original faces. Its six original dihedrals are (7); every new truncation edge is right-angled. There are no assumed end or completeness conditions in this compact local construction. Its ordered face-normal Gram fixes it up to isometry.

For an explicit compactness check, suppose a remaining point were ideal and write it in the vertex cone as x=sum_i t_i v_i, t_i>=0. The polar inequalities <x,v_i><=0 and x^2=0 force <x,v_i>=0 on every active coefficient. This would put the nonzero active vector t in the kernel of its principal vertex Gram block. All such blocks are nonsingular: their determinants are complementary principal minors of G divided by det G (size one and two were checked above; size three uses G_ii=1). Thus no ideal point remains after truncation.

The length along original edge a, between its polar truncations, is

ell_a=arccosh[ C_13/sqrt(C_11 C_33) ]>0.       (9)

The cofactor in (9) is positive since det G<0 and (G^-1)_13<0. The formula follows by restricting to the Lorentz plane spanned by its two normalized hyperideal endpoints. Other edges are obtained by vertex relabeling.

3. The optimized derivative is EXACTLY that truncated edge length

Here is a finite rational certificate for the derivative, with all branches fixed by the positive interval. Set u_a=exp(2pi i a), etc., P=product_edges u. Let U_i be the four products of u on the faces in (1), and V_k the three products on the quads. Then product U_i=product V_k=P^2. Write n=exp(2pi i Z). The stationarity equation A’(Z)=0 is exactly

(n-1)product_k(n-V_k)-product_i(n-U_i)
              =n(A2 n^2+A1 n+A0)=0,
A2=sum U_i-1-sum V_k,
A1=sum_(k<l)V_kV_l+sum V_k-sum_(i<j)U_iU_j,
A0=sum_(i<j<l)U_iU_jU_l-P^2-sum_(k<l)V_kV_l. (10)

Expansion gives the independent identity

A1^2-4A2A0=16P^2 det G.                       (11)

In (11), G has off-diagonals cos(2pi color), which is exactly (7). Also A0=P^2 conjugate(A2) and A1/P is real. Thus the two roots of (10) are distinct and on the unit circle. A2 cannot vanish, since that would make (11)/P^2 a nonnegative real square instead of 16det G<0. The function A’ tends to +infinity at the lower positive interval endpoint and to -infinity at the upper one. It is continuous inside, and (10) permits at most two simple critical points there. The number is odd, so there is EXACTLY one, Z_*. It is a strict global maximum, with nonzero negative second derivative. This also fixes a smooth selected branch on the entire connected domain (2).

For edge a let

B(n)=u_c (n-U_1)(n-U_3)/[(n-V_1)(n-V_2)],
R_a= sin pi(a+e-b)sin pi(a+b-e)
         /[sin pi(b+e-a)sin pi(F_1-1)]
     *sin pi(a+f-d)sin pi(a+d-f)
         /[sin pi(d+f-a)sin pi(F_3-1)].

All displayed sines in R_a are positive. Differentiating (5) at its selected stationary Z_* gives

E_a:=exp(partial_a Psi)=B(n_*)sqrt(R_a)>0.     (12)

To verify (12), the bare derivative is the ratio of the two sin(Z-F) factors to the two sin(Q-Z) factors incident to a. Their canceled phase is u_c. The four face derivatives give R_a.

Let n_+,n_- be the two roots of (10). Direct rational products give

B(n_+)B(n_-)=1/R_a,
B(n_+)+B(n_-)=C_13/(2K_a),
K_a=sin pi(a+e-b)sin pi(a+b-e)
                sin pi(a+f-d)sin pi(a+d-f),
sqrt(C_11 C_33) sqrt(R_a)=4K_a.               (13)

These are uniform algebraic identities, not tests at selected colors. For an explicit cleared-polynomial verification, put

A=-A1-A2(U_1+U_3), B=A2 U_1U_3-A0,
C=-A1-A2(V_1+V_2), D=A2 V_1V_2-A0,
Den=C^2 A0-CD A1+D^2 A2.

The sum/product of the two B(n) are respectively

u_c[2AC A0-(AD+BC)A1+2BD A2]/Den,
u_c^2[A^2 A0-AB A1+B^2 A2]/Den.

With edge variables written a,…,f temporarily for u_a,…,u_f, the claimed product is

(be-a)(abe-1)(df-a)(adf-1)
         /[(ae-b)(ab-e)(af-d)(ad-f)],

and the claimed sum is

8a^2bdef C_13 /[(ae-b)(ab-e)(af-d)(ad-f)].

Substitute (10), multiply these explicit denominators, and expand: both polynomial differences are identically zero in six independent variables. The final identity in (13) is the elementary three-face Gram determinant (8), with its sine factors. The accompanying audit performs these symbolic zero checks; no interpolation is involved.

Consequently the two possible E_a values are reciprocal and have sum 2C_13/sqrt(C_11C_33). They are exp(+ell_a), exp(-ell_a). Near the regular ideal limit all colors approach 1/3 from above; the metric factor in (12) diverges while its bare ratio remains finite and positive. Thus the selected E_a is the positive-length branch there. It cannot change branch on (2), since ell_a never vanishes. By connectedness and tetrahedral relabeling,

partial_edge Psi=ell_edge for all six edges.  (14)

4. An elementary volume-variation proof and its constant

For completeness the needed first variation can be derived directly. On the hyperboloid the coordinate vector restricted to a totally geodesic face satisfies Delta_face x=2x. It follows by differentiating the usual cosh/sinh coordinates on H^2. A face with moving outward unit normal n has normal speed -<dot n,x>. The divergence theorem within that face therefore gives

dot V=-(1/2)sum_faces sum_edges
                    integral_edge <dot n,nu_edge> ds.

For an edge between face normals n_i,n_j and angle alpha, the outward conormal within face i is (n_j+cos alpha n_i)/sin alpha. Adding its two face contributions uses d<n_i,n_j>=sin alpha d alpha, and gives

dV=-(1/2)sum_edges ell_edge d alpha_edge.     (15)

The new truncation edges have fixed angle pi/2, hence contribute zero. Using alpha=pi-2pi color, (15) becomes dV=pi sum ell_edge d(color). Equations (14),(15) show that V-pi Psi is constant on the connected domain (2).

Calibrate at the regular ideal boundary a=…=f=1/3. Then F_i=1, Q_k=4/3, Z_*=7/6, and

A(Z_*)=-6L(1/6)=3v3/pi,
M=6L(1/3)=-2v3/pi,
Psi=v3/pi.                                   (16)

The Lobachevsky duplication identity gives Lambda(pi/6)=(3/2)Lambda(pi/3), so (16) has no numerical calibration. The Gram construction converges to the regular ideal tetrahedron, whose volume is v3=3Lambda(pi/3).

One can justify volume convergence here without a realization or rigidity theorem. For the regular family take fixed Euclidean regular tetrahedral unit vectors t_i with mutual products -1/3 and use n_i=(T,S t_i), T^2=(3cos alpha-1)/4, S2=1+T2. At alpha=pi/3 their Klein faces have distance 1/3 from the center, giving the four ideal vertices -t_i. The finite polar truncation vertices converge to those same vertices. Here are exact uniform end inequalities. Set c=cos alpha, t=T/S and

rho=3t=sqrt((9c-3)/(c+1))>1.

The projective hyperideal vertex at -t_i is rho(-t_i). In a fixed upper-half-space frame sending w=-t_i to infinity, its polar cut is

|z|^2+y^2<=(rho+1)/(rho-1).

For every incident j!=i, t_j dot w=1/3. Its original face inequality therefore gives exactly

2(t_j)_perp dot z
   <=(t+1/3)+(t-1/3)(|z|^2+y^2)
   <=2(rho+1)/3.

The three fixed perpendicular normals surround the origin at angles 120 degrees. Hence |z|<=C uniformly as rho decreases to 1. The cross-sectional area is bounded, and its volume above y=Y is <=C/Y^2. Do this in the four disjoint ideal-end frames; the remaining compact regions converge. This proves volume convergence to the ideal tetrahedron and fixes the constant in (15), with no O(epsilon) end estimate left as an assumption.

We have therefore proved the LOCAL GEOMETRIC IDENTITY

pi Psi(a,b,c,d,e,f)=Vol(truncated hyperideal tetrahedron). (17)

Both sides extend continuously to the closed angle chamber. For Psi this follows directly from continuity of L and maximization on a continuously varying compact interval. The corresponding geometric extension is the limit from the interior; no assertion that every boundary point retains a nondegenerate truncated-tetrahedron cell is needed. In particular at six colors 1/2, Z_*=7/4 and Psi=v8/pi; the six original edge lengths tend to zero at the escaping ideal corners. The limiting cell is the regular ideal octahedron, not a tetrahedron with positive edges. One can check its geometry directly: G=2I-J and its four normalized polar normals are m_i=-n_i+(1/2)sum_j n_j. Then distinct n_i,n_j and distinct m_i,m_j have product -1, while <n_i,m_j> is -2 for i=j and zero otherwise. These are exactly the eight regular-octahedron face normals (1,sigma_1,sigma_2,sigma_3)/sqrt(2), partitioned by the product of the three signs. Thus the octahedral constant is attached to an explicit geometric cell, not only a limiting dilogarithm number.

5. Exact stationarity data, and what a global source still needs

The local action supplies more than a constant fit. The colors give the actual face-normal Gram (7), its polar triangle areas are (3), and the optimized derivatives are the six metric edge lengths (14). For a specified gluing of such cells, (15) gives its first variation on EVERY allowed dihedral-angle tangent, not merely on a chosen one-parameter source family. In particular variations exchanging two angles around one fixed total-angle edge force equality of their corresponding truncated lengths. More generally a linear constraint matrix C gives the exact stationarity condition

length vector in image(C^T).                 (18)

If the ONLY angle constraints are sums around actual common edges, (18) forces one common length per glued edge. Three such matched alternating lengths determine a right-angled hexagon by elementary hyperbolic cosine laws, so the prescribed original hexagonal faces then match isometrically. Actual angle sums, link degree/winding, boundary markings and end completeness must still be checked. Additional source constraints can reduce the tangent space, and then stationarity in that smaller space does not imply this full metric gluing. No global strict-concavity or complete-manifold maximum theorem is asserted solely from the local derivative identity.

There is a concrete obstruction to using just the four support inequalities as a complete volume-maximization certificate. Equation (14) gives partial_edge Psi>0 at every strict point. Increasing any color preserves those four inequalities. Along the line toward six colors 1/2, Psi therefore increases to v8/pi. By continuity that is the maximum over the CLOSED support chamber, and there is NO interior stationary point under only these inequalities. A geometric interior critical point for a larger graph needs the actual additional edge constraints, boundary potentials or their full source counterparts; the local face supports alone do not supply them.

The generic actual four-port theorem now gives an explicit separable row/column gauge relating its literal inverse kernel L_(G,E) to the seven-factorial sum S. Its four Delta face factors give exactly the normalization in (6) after root-zero removal. Thus this finite scalar/generic normalization is explicit; it is not being taken from a named 6j evaluation. But identifying the resulting weight with a useful FULL marked-source Cauchy energy still requires controlled gauge transport, complete colliding partners and root normals, and the graph’s actual port/edge incidence in (18). No individual partner value or semisimple normalization may replace that comparison. The bare Racah term is already wrong at the regular ideal tetrahedron by the factor in (16).

6. Uniform local quantum-volume theorem, including moving boundary colors

The geometry identity has the following UNIFORM finite-level consequence. There is an absolute constant C such that, for EVERY N and every six folded integer labels satisfying all four face sums >=N-1, the positive normalized scalar W_N in (6) obeys

|log W_N-(N/pi)Vol_ext(T(a_N))|<=C log(N+1),
a_(N,edge)=(j_edge+1/2)/N.                    (19)

The domain is empty at any level with no supported sextuple. The symbol Vol_ext is the continuous geometric extension of (17); at strict angles it is the ordinary compact truncated-tetrahedron volume, while zero-angle boundary cells are interpreted by their geometric limits. Constants in (19) do not depend on a distance from any face, edge, corner or the octahedral endpoint. Both level parities and composite N are included.

Here is the full uniform estimate. Write F_iint,Q_kint for the integer forms (1), z_-=max F_i^int and z_+=min Q_k^int. All true factorial indices are in [0,N-1]. The elementary uniform estimate

log s_n=N L(n/N)+O(log(N+1)), 0<=n<=N-1,

has an absolute constant. Applying it to the fixed eight factorials in each positive R_N term changes its logarithm by O(log N). At its actual point Z_z=(z+7/4)/N, the action arguments differ from those true indices divided by N by exactly

numerator: 3/(4N),
each of seven denominators: 1/(4N).

For a face metric the three deficits and its excess differ by exactly 1/(2N). The shifted and original arguments are all in [0,1]. The uniform modulus

|L(t+epsilon)-L(t)|
         <=C0 |epsilon|(1+|log|epsilon||)

therefore changes N times these actions by O(log N), including when an original factorial index is zero. The explicit N factors in R_N and D_face add only O(log N).

The geometric maximization interval is

I=[(z_-+3/2)/N,(z_++2)/N],

whereas the true grid runs from (z_-+7/4)/N to (z_++7/4)/N in steps 1/N. Its first and last points are inset by exactly 1/(4N) from the two respective endpoints. Every point of I is within 1/N of a true grid point, even when z_-=z_+. The same modulus of L, now for its eight action terms, shows that the continuous maximum and the true-grid maximum differ by O(log N/N), uniformly. There are at most N positive terms: z_+-z_-<=N-1 by the complementary triangle-deficit bounds. The largest term bounds their sum from below, and N times it bounds the sum from above. Thus

log W_N=N Psi(a_N)+O(log N)

with one universal error constant. Equation (17) proves (19). No nonzero Hessian, separation from a boundary, selected complex cycle or asymptotic interpolation of an unobserved normal was used in this argument.

In particular ANY convergent supported color sequence a_N->a in the closed chamber satisfies

lim (pi/N)log W_N=Vol_ext(T(a)).               (20)

This is a genuine broad LOCAL quantum-volume theorem for the explicitly gauged scalar/residue. Its squared weight has twice the logarithmic action. It does not turn (19) into a full marked recoupling norm or authorize replacing a partner germ by its positive value core; those source requirements in section 5 remain.
