A weighted actual Ward lift of the cut centralizer field

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

Three original coproduct Ward generators span simultaneous infinitesimal conjugation of the ordered classical meridians on every regular Gauss chart. Explicit field-dependent coefficients select the centralizer of the physical cut meridian. The same coefficients have ordered finite matrix lifts, with an exact cut/pivot boundary and, for a marked word, an exact internal defect. Neither boundary is discarded.

This identifies a principal geometric field of an actual sourced current. It does not transport the highest-cut central augmentation to a noncentral parabolic chart, construct a geometric relative cycle, or establish a nonzero coefficient in the physical knot period.

3. Selecting the physical cut centralizer

For any traceless matrix of regular functions X=(abc−a) X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&-a\end{pmatrix} put αE=c/K*,αF=b,αK=(2a+bf*)/K*.(12) \alpha_E=c/K_*,\qquad \alpha_F=b,\qquad \alpha_K=(2a+b f_*)/K_* . \qquad\text{(12)} Only K*K_*, already a Gauss unit, is inverted. Equations (8) imply that the vector field 𝒱X=αE{e*,⋅}+αF{f*,⋅}+αK{K*,⋅}(13) \mathscr V_X=\alpha_E\{e_*,\,\cdot\,\} +\alpha_F\{f_*,\,\cdot\,\} +\alpha_K\{K_*,\,\cdot\,\} \qquad\text{(13)} satisfies 𝒱X(Hj)=[X,Hj]\mathscr V_X(H_j)=[X,H_j] for every jj. This is a linear combination of vector fields with function coefficients. It is not asserted to be the Hamiltonian field of αEe*+αFf*+αKK*\alpha_Ee_*+\alpha_Ff_*+\alpha_KK_*; the latter would have additional coefficient derivatives.

Choose the first physical meridian μ=μ1=−H1\mu=\mu_1=-H_1, and set Xcen=−(μ−12tr(μ)I).(14) X_{\mathrm{ cen}}=-\left(\mu-\tfrac12 \operatorname{tr}(\mu)I\right). \qquad\text{(14)} Then [Xcen,μ]=0[X_{\mathrm{ cen}},\mu]=0. For a noncentral meridian, (14) spans its traceless centralizer: a vector vv for which v,μvv,\mu v are independent shows that every commuting matrix is a linear combination of I,μI,\mu. On an irreducible framed representation chart the resulting field is nonzero, since a nonscalar matrix commuting with every holonomy would supply a common invariant line. Word relations and the cut eigenflag are preserved. No braid-axis product is identified with the physical cut.

4. An ordered finite lift and its complete boundary

Here is one explicit quantization of (12)–(14). On the first head use the ordered matrix μ̂=−(K1ℰ1K1F1K1−1+ℰ1F1). \widehat\mu=-\begin{pmatrix} K_1&\mathcal E_1\\K_1F_1&K_1^{-1}+\mathcal E_1F_1 \end{pmatrix}. 5. Scope and the remaining source comparison

Equations (8), (12), and (17)–(23) supply an actual weighted Ward current whose principal bulk field is the physical cut centralizer. They hold with the original marked word and finite endpoint data. They do not identify its finite boundary with a particular geometric relative-chain endpoint, nor show its pairing vanishes. No finite flow or group-scheme isomorphism is inferred from the field-dependent linear combination.

The noncentral condition matters. At the literal highest complete classical augmentation the cut meridian is II, so (14) is zero. The theorem does not manufacture a noncentral parabolic limit from that augmentation. On an irreducible framed representation with noncentral parabolic cut it instead gives the nonzero vector s∂rs\partial_r of (15). Relating these source situations remains an additional boundary transport problem.

All finite wall matrices in (1), (3), (19)–(21) precede the principal comparison and remain whole in MM. The interior symbol argument is not an estimate of those walls, a Gaussian pushforward of the full current, or a lower bound for the selected knot coefficient. The original closing, peripheral line, and highest cut have not been replaced.
