A global spectral gap for short-moving source sectors

Independently reviewed analytical theorem, 31 October 2026. This retains the actual full numerator, original two protected targets and entire Fourier period. It extends the reviewed diagonal/CROSS sector lemma to a fixed small outer-degree corridor. It does not bound remote one-sided paths of arbitrary macroscopic displacement. Here “moving” refers to the original triangular face path degree; it is not an identification with physical input/output head displacement.

1. Exact path divided differences, including repeated nodes

Write d=N−1d=N-1, χN(σ)=2TN(σ/2)+2\chi_N(\sigma)=2T_N(\sigma/2)+2 and cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N)c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N). In the original triangular face path expansion, a path from jj to ii has kk one- or two-row steps, displacement

D=j−i,k≤D≤2k, D=j-i,\qquad k\leq D\leq2k,

and k+1k+1 distinct visited row indices v0,…,vkv_0,\ldots,v_k. Its scalar factor is

∏rnotvisited(σ−cr)=χN[cv0,…,cvk,σ]. \prod_{r\ \mathrm{not\ visited}}(\sigma-c_r) =\chi_N[c_{v_0},\ldots,c_{v_k},\sigma].\tag{1}

The right side is the divided difference of order k+1k+1. Repeated spectral values among the cvlc_{v_l} mean the exact Hermite divided difference. The identity remains polynomial at all removable nodes: χN\chi_N vanishes with at least the required multiplicity at the listed nodes, so Newton’s divided-remainder identity proves (1). It also follows by polynomial division, with no inverse spectral gap.

For real σ∈[−2,2]\sigma\in[-2,2], the iterated fundamental theorem of calculus on the divided-difference simplex bounds (1) by

sup[−2,2]|χN(r)|r!=2NN+r(N+r2r),r=k+1. \frac{\sup_{[-2,2]}|\chi_N^{(r)}|}{r!} =\frac{2N}{N+r}\binom{N+r}{2r},\qquad r=k+1.\tag{2}

Here is an elementary proof of the derivative value used in (2). Each derivative of TNT_N has nonnegative Chebyshev coefficients, since Tj′=jUj−1T_j'=jU_{j-1} and Uj−1U_{j-1} is a nonnegative sum of cosine characters. Therefore its absolute maximum on [−1,1][-1,1] occurs at 11. Differentiating (1−z2)TN″−zTN′+N2TN=0(1-z^2)T_N''-zT_N'+N^2T_N=0 at z=1z=1 gives

TN(r)(1)=N2∏a=1r−1(N2−a2)(2r−1)!!. T_N^{(r)}(1)=\frac{N^2\prod_{a=1}^{r-1}(N^2-a^2)}{(2r-1)!!}.

The scaling χN(σ)=2TN(σ/2)+2\chi_N(\sigma)=2T_N(\sigma/2)+2 and division by r!r! give (2). The divided-difference simplex has volume 1/r!1/r!, including the limit of coincident nodes, so the same bound applies there.

The remaining original step weights have absolute value at most 44. A one-step move contributes a multiple of x+yx+y; a two-step move a multiple of xyxy. 2. Shared target continuity, rather than an identity reset

Fix the actual certified diagram DD and a nonempty set II of ports. At II take the projection LN,ηL_{N,\eta}; elsewhere take the exact CROSS projection. Keep the original ordinary targets dd and protected targets 00. The global CROSS coefficient bound from the reviewed cofactor interpolation lemma is

|Pcross(σ)|coef≤Clog⁡2(N+1)HFN, |P_{\mathrm{cross}}(\sigma)|_{\mathrm{coef}} \leq C\log^2(N+1)H_{F_N},

on the real interval, where FNF_N is the actual even positive endpoint or odd regularized Fejer reference. Its strip version has the same degree-dd exponential factor as (4). Hence the absolute original sector is bounded by KN,η|I|[Clog⁡2(N+1)]R−|I|K_{N,\eta}^{|I|}[C\log^2(N+1)]^{R-|I|} times

[original targets]∏i∈ITN,η;i∏j∉IHFN;j. [\text{original targets}]\, \prod_{i\in I}T_{N,\eta;i}\prod_{j\notin I}H_{F_N;j}.\tag{5}

The comparison does not change the positive outer degrees into zero. They remain in (5) with the exact shared incidences and protected targets. In particular, it does not apply the old identity theorem to a finite shifted target or to a physically different coupon.

Let 𝒫I(η)\mathcal P_I(\eta) be the compact continuous flow polytope on the original port-to-face incidence graph: each port supplies 11, each ordinary face demands 11, each protected face demands 00, and at a port in II the total outer load is at most η\eta. Let

WI(η)=max𝒒∈𝒫I(η)∑j∉Ih(𝒒j). W_I(\eta)=\max_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal P_I(\eta)} \sum_{j\notin I}h(\mathbf q_j).\tag{6}

with value minus infinity for an empty polytope. Here hh is precisely the continuous full-simplex local entropy of HFNH_{F_N}, the same at both parities. Selected ports have entropy zero because TT has coefficient 11. The uniform local coefficient theorem, and the polynomial number of degree-dd monomial assignments for fixed DD, give the upper bound

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡(coefficient in (5))≤WI(η). \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\bigl(\text{coefficient in (5)}\bigr) \leq W_I(\eta).\tag{7}

Only an upper bound is needed: no boundary rounding or eventual fixed-label feasibility is asserted here. Normalize the integer exponents by dd, retaining the exact face balances; replacing d/Nd/N by 11 is uniform because hh is continuous on its compact simplex.

At η=0\eta=0 this is exactly the identity-inserted compact flow problem, so WI(0)<ΦD=Vol⁡(YD)/(2π)W_I(0)<\Phi_D=\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)/(2\pi) by the proved strict identity theorem for the original drilled group. Moreover

WI(η)→WI(0)as η↓0. W_I(\eta)\longrightarrow W_I(0)\qquad\text{as }\eta\downarrow0.\tag{8}

To see this, take a convergent subsequence of maximizers in the nested compact polytopes. Its limit lies in 𝒫I(0)\mathcal P_I(0); continuity of the entropy gives the upper inequality, and inclusion gives the reverse inequality. If 𝒫I(0)\mathcal P_I(0) is empty, compactness instead implies 𝒫I(η)\mathcal P_I(\eta) is empty for every sufficiently small η\eta.

Combining (4),(7),(8), choose a fixed ηD>0\eta_D>0 so

WI(ηD)+|I|ω(ηD)<ΦD−2γD. W_I(\eta_D)+|I|\omega(\eta_D)<\Phi_D-2\gamma_D.\tag{9}

There are finitely many nonempty II, so ηD\eta_D and γD\gamma_D can be chosen for all of them at once, ignoring identically zero cases. Shrinking a fixed complex 𝜹\boldsymbol\delta strip absorbs 2π∑j|Im⁡δj|2\pi\sum_j|\operatorname{Im}\delta_j|. For all sufficiently large NN, at both parities the sector satisfies

|ZI,ηD;cross(−2cos(2π𝜹))|≤eN(ΦD−γD). \left|Z_{I,\eta_D;\mathrm{cross}}(-2\cos(2\pi\boldsymbol\delta))\right| \leq e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma_D)}.\tag{10}

uniformly on that strip. Constants depend on the original fixed diagram, independently of the eventual fixed signed crossing powers.

3. Whole original period and precise remaining scope

The sector in (10) is still a polynomial of spectral degree at most dd in every coordinate. The three-integration-by-parts argument of the global diagonal/CROSS sector theorem, §4, therefore applies verbatim to its entire original Fourier period. In particular, sine factors give B(±1/2)=0B(\pm1/2)=0 and matching first derivatives; the second-derivative endpoint current is retained. Cauchy differentiates the source on its fixed strip, while the Gaussian is differentiated only on the real cube. Summing all nonzero integer charges with the actual charge weights gives

|IN,𝒏;shortI,crosselsewhere|≤CD∏j(1+|nj|)3eN(ΦD−γD). \left|I_{N,\mathbf n;\,\mathrm{short}\ I,\,\mathrm{cross\ elsewhere}}\right| \leq C_D\prod_j(1+|n_j|)^3e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma_D)}.\tag{11}

Thus a fixed small outer-degree corridor, including genuinely moving near-diagonal paths, is exponentially negligible relative to the proved local unit-charge contribution for independently sufficiently long fixed signed twists. No sharp local cube splits an infinite charge sum. If disjoint sectors are required, project overlapping classes further; coefficient projections preserve every upper bound.

This does not control a macroscopic one-sided path whose outer load stays outside this small corridor. In particular the known σ=3\sigma=\sqrt3 counterexample with outer load tending to 1/41/4 is compatible with (11). Nor does the statement bound a partial-far-charge source with an uncontrolled original RnR^n coupon at another port. Those remain separate global phase-sensitive questions.
