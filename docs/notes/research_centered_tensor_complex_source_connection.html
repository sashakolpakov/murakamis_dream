Two contributions to the original source near the real cube

21 November 2026. Research note. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The real three-boundary source has two different continuations in its asymptotic expansion. The initialized interior saddle contributes the known dilogarithmic exponential. The ends of the full Fourier contour, after all Fourier charges have been summed, contribute a nonzero term of order N−3/2N^{-3/2}. Its exponential phase is constant.

Both contributions can be estimated on a fixed complex neighborhood of each compact real cube. This proves a nonzero saddle coefficient on one side of the real cube and identifies the endpoint contribution that prevents a single saddle formula on a whole neighborhood. No assertion places an arbitrary short-filled component in this neighborhood.

5. The unit charges: a saddle and two endpoint rays

It suffices to treat ℓ=1\ell=1, by (20). At real parameters, φ1\varphi_1 is strictly convex and has one nondegenerate critical point in (0,2π)(0,2\pi), uniformly separated from the endpoints. Let θc(𝒕)\theta_c(\mathbf t) be its analytic continuation and α(𝒕)=φ1(θc,𝒕)\alpha(\mathbf t)=\varphi_1(\theta_c,\mathbf t). It exists on a fixed complex neighborhood. Its imaginary part can be made uniformly small there.

Here is the contour construction that separates its saddle from its endpoints. It follows that yy has a univalent holomorphic extension on a smaller neighborhood: local invertibility is uniform by compactness, while injectivity away from the diagonal persists from strict real monotonicity. 6. Summing the endpoints and restoring the original phases

Unlike a bound on the nonunit charges alone, (23) and (30) identify the same endpoint coefficient for every charge. Their source-weighted sum is absolutely convergent: S(a)=∑ℓ∈ℤ(−1)ℓℓ{(a−ℓ)−2−(−a−ℓ)−2},a=3/2.(31) S(a)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^\ell\ell \{(a-\ell)^{-2}-(-a-\ell)^{-2}\},\qquad a=3/2. \qquad\text{(31)} The combined summand is O(|ℓ|−2)O(|\ell|^{-2}). 7. Why a whole-neighborhood single saddle formula fails

The endpoint branch is visible immediately on one side of every real point. To verify that the two sides really occur, at a real point write z*=2cos⁡θ*∈(−2,2),bi=1+tiz*+ti2.(37) z_*=2\cos\theta_*\in(-2,2),\qquad b_i=1+t_i z_*+t_i^2 . \qquad\text{(37)} The envelope derivative of the initialized action is ∂tiΨ=log⁡bi−2log⁡(1+ti)2πti<0.(38) \partial_{t_i}\Psi= \frac{\log b_i-2\log(1+t_i)}{2\pi t_i}<0. \qquad\text{(38)} For ti≠0t_i\ne0, its sign follows from bi/(1+ti)2=1+ti(z*−2)/(1+ti)2b_i/(1+t_i)^2=1+t_i(z_*-2)/(1+t_i)^2. At ti=0t_i=0, its continuous value is (z*−2)/(2π)<0(z_*-2)/(2\pi)<0.

Moving a small fixed distance in a negative imaginary coordinate direction therefore gives Im⁡Ψ>0\operatorname{Im}\Psi>0. At each such fixed point the first term of (5) decays exponentially, whereas the actual source has the nonzero endpoint asymptotic (8). Consequently no fixed complex neighborhood of any real point supports the single-saddle relative formula (7) throughout. On the opposite side, (7) proves that the initialized saddle coefficient is nonzero without assigning it by hand.

The connection estimate does not decide cancellation in the shrinking transition region where the two terms have comparable size.
