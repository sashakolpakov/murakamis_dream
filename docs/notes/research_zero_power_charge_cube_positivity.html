Positive geometry on the zero-power charge cube

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

For a connected zero-power selected tree with original degrees at least three, the original positive primitive solution exists on the entire charge cube 0<cv≤10<c_v\le1, except at the upper corner of the selected trivalent pair. In particular a supplied positive unit-charge endpoint is joined to completeness by its positive radial charge branch. This sharpens the sufficient degree condition in the archived convex height potential. The zero-power knot family was already treated by the separate Newton and Conway constructions; no new quantum asymptotic or knot family is asserted here.

1. Original data and statement

Let TT be a finite original circle tree and let S≠⌀S\ne\varnothing be a connected proper set of vertices. Write dvd_v for the degree of vv in TT, including its edges to the outside. Include every edge incident to SS once, put every outside angle equal to zero, and set every selected original crossing power equal to zero. Keep the original complete branches and primitive equations ∑e=vwrv,e(𝒕)=−πicv,rv,e=arsinh⁡(tan⁡(tv/2)cos⁡(tw/2)),0<cv≤1.(1) \sum_{e=vw}r_{v,e}(\mathbf t)=-\pi i c_v,\qquad r_{v,e}=\operatorname{arsinh} \bigl(\tan(t_v/2)\cos(t_w/2)\bigr),\qquad 0<c_v\le1. \qquad\text{(1)} The sum uses the original incidences. Positivity means that all eight tetrahedra of each included original edge block have positive imaginary shapes on the branch from completeness, not positivity after replacing the subdivision.

Theorem 1. Suppose dv≥3d_v\ge3 for every v∈Sv\in S.

  1. For every charge vector in (0,1]S(0,1]^S, (1) has exactly one positive solution, except when SS consists of two adjacent vertices of original degree three and both charges equal one. Every existing solution has tv=−iHvt_v=-iH_v, Hv>0H_v>0, and is locally holomorphic in the full complex charge vector.
  2. The positive configurations depend continuously on the charges and stay in a compact subset of the original positive domain on every compact subset of the cube avoiding the exception. For each fixed charge vector 𝒄0\mathbf c^0 at which the solution exists, the entire radial path 𝒄=λ𝒄0\mathbf c=\lambda\mathbf c^0, 0≤λ≤10\le\lambda\le1, is positive and analytic, including its complete endpoint.
  3. At the exceptional pair’s upper corner the unique limiting heights are Hv=Hw=2arsinh⁡1.(2) H_v=H_w=2\operatorname{arsinh}1. \qquad\text{(2)} The internal edge has q=0q=0, its two selected return angles are −π/2-\pi/2, and the four outside edges remain positive. There is no positive solution at that corner.

Thus the exact unit-charge criterion for an arbitrary connected proper all-zero selected subtree is dv≥3 at every selected vertex, and S is not a selected trivalent pair.(3) \boxed{\quad d_v\ge3\text{ at every selected vertex, and } S\text{ is not a selected trivalent pair}.\quad} \qquad\text{(3)} The pair has its other four original edges to complete outside vertices. There is no bound on the size of SS.

2. The charge-scaled height minimum

Use the original height potential from the first pinned source: J(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πs)ds,B(x,h)=∫0xlog⁡(2cos⁡2πs+2cosh⁡h)ds−2J(x),V(h,g)=max0≤x≤1/2{B(x,h)+B(x,g)},h,g≥0.(4) \begin{split} J(x)&=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi s)\,ds,\\ B(x,h)&=\int_0^x\log(2\cos2\pi s+2\cosh h)\,ds-2J(x),\\ V(h,g)&=\max_{0\le x\le1/2}\{B(x,h)+B(x,g)\}, \qquad h,g\ge0. \end{split} \qquad\text{(4)} Endpoint integrals have their continuous values. The maximizing index is unique. Write r=sinh⁡(h/2)r=\sinh(h/2), s=sinh⁡(g/2)s=\sinh(g/2). If rs<1rs<1, the maximum is interior, and πVh(h,g)=α(h,g)=arcsin⁡r1+s21+r2.(5) \pi V_h(h,g)=\alpha(h,g) =\arcsin\frac{r\sqrt{1+s^2}}{\sqrt{1+r^2}}. \qquad\text{(5)} If rs≥1rs\ge1, the maximum is x=1/2x=1/2, and V(h,g)=(h+g)/2,πVh=π/2.(6) V(h,g)=(h+g)/2,\qquad \pi V_h=\pi/2. \qquad\text{(6)} The first derivatives match continuously at rs=1rs=1. Call an edge regular when rs<1rs<1, and saturated otherwise. An edge to a complete outside vertex is always regular.

The source proves convexity of VV on the nonnegative quadrant. At a regular edge its two-variable Hessian is positive definite; on an outside edge the remaining scalar Hessian is Vhh(h,0)=12πcosh⁡(h/2)>0.(7) V_{hh}(h,0)=\frac1{2\pi\cosh(h/2)}>0. \qquad\text{(7)} At h=0h=0, with the other height finite, its one-sided height derivative is zero. Define ℒ𝒄(𝑯)=∑vwincludedV(Hv,Hw)−∑v∈ScvHv,Hw=0(w∉S).(8) \mathcal L_{\mathbf c}(\mathbf H) =\sum_{vw\ {\mathrm{ included}}}V(H_v,H_w)-\sum_{v\in S}c_vH_v, \qquad H_w=0\quad(w\notin S). \qquad\text{(8)}

Lemma 2. For every 𝒄∈(0,1]S\mathbf c\in(0,1]^S, (8) has a unique minimizer on [0,∞)S[0,\infty)^S. All its coordinates are finite and positive, every selected vertex meets a regular edge, and ∑e∋vαv,e=πcv.(9) \sum_{e\ni v}\alpha_{v,e}=\pi c_v. \qquad\text{(9)}

Proof. Choosing x=1/2x=1/2 in (4), where B(1/2,h)=h/2B(1/2,h)=h/2, gives the uniform coercive bound ℒ𝒄(𝑯)≥∑v∈S(dv/2−cv)Hv≥12∑v∈SHv.(10) \mathcal L_{\mathbf c}(\mathbf H) \ge\sum_{v\in S}(d_v/2-c_v)H_v \ge\frac12\sum_{v\in S}H_v. \qquad\text{(10)} Continuous sublevel sets are compact, so a minimum exists. At Hv=0H_v=0 its one-sided derivative is −cv<0-c_v<0. Hence every minimizing height is positive, and stationarity gives (9). If every incidence at vv were saturated, its derivative sum would be dv/2>cvd_v/2>c_v, a contradiction.

The regular edges therefore cover all selected vertices. Their Hessians, using (7) on outside edges, sum to a positive definite form: any nonzero vector has a nonzero selected coordinate and a regular edge incident to it. This partial sum is locally strongly convex near the minimizer, while the remaining edge potentials are convex. Two minima would make their connecting segment constant, contradicting this local strictness. ▫\square

The minimizer varies continuously on the charge cube, including at the exceptional upper corner. The bound (10) is uniform and its value is at most ℒ𝒄(0)\mathcal L_{\mathbf c}(0), independent of 𝒄\mathbf c. Every convergent subsequence of minimizers is optimal at the limiting charge vector by continuity; uniqueness identifies its limit.

3. The saturated-edge inequality

At positive height, every incidence satisfies αv,e≥arctan⁡rv,rv=sinh⁡(Hv/2)>0.(11) \alpha_{v,e}\ge\arctan r_v,\qquad r_v=\sinh(H_v/2)>0. \qquad\text{(11)} There is equality on a complete outside edge and strict inequality on every edge to a selected neighbor. For a regular edge this follows from (5); for a saturated edge it follows from π/2>arctan⁡rv\pi/2>\arctan r_v.

Suppose vwvw is saturated at the minimizer. Its own contribution at each endpoint is exactly π/2\pi/2. Apply (11) only to the remaining dv−1d_v-1 and dw−1d_w-1 incidences: arctan⁡rv≤π(cv−1/2)dv−1≤π4,arctan⁡rw≤π(cw−1/2)dw−1≤π4.(12) \arctan r_v\le\frac{\pi(c_v-1/2)}{d_v-1}\le\frac{\pi}{4}, \qquad \arctan r_w\le\frac{\pi(c_w-1/2)}{d_w-1}\le\frac{\pi}{4}. \qquad\text{(12)} If either charge is at most 1/21/2, the first inequality already contradicts its positive height. Otherwise the displayed upper bounds follow from dv,dw≥3d_v,d_w\ge3 and cv,cw≤1c_v,c_w\le1. But rvrw≥1r_vr_w\ge1 implies arctan⁡rv+arctan⁡rw≥π/2.(13) \arctan r_v+\arctan r_w\ge\pi/2. \qquad\text{(13)} Every inequality in (12)–(13) must therefore be an equality. Consequently dv=dw=3,cv=cw=1,rv=rw=1.(14) d_v=d_w=3,\quad c_v=c_w=1,\quad r_v=r_w=1. \qquad\text{(14)} Neither vertex can have another selected neighbor, since that would make its incidence estimate strict. Connectedness makes SS exactly this pair.

Conversely, for this pair at charges (1,1)(1,1), rv=rw=1r_v=r_w=1 gives an internal contribution π/2\pi/2 and two outside contributions π/4\pi/4 at each vertex. It solves (9); convexity and Lemma 2 make it the unique minimum. This proves the full saturated-edge classification.

The improvement over the archived sufficient degree condition is the use of a saturated edge’s entire π/2\pi/2 contribution before estimating its remaining incidences. It is not an enumeration of short words.

4. Original geometry and continuation

At an all-regular minimizer set tv=−iHvt_v=-iH_v, with outside heights zero. Put Av=tanh⁡(Hv/2)=rv1+rv2,av=tan⁡(tv/2)=−iAv.(15) A_v=\tanh(H_v/2)=\frac{r_v}{\sqrt{1+r_v^2}},\qquad a_v=\tan(t_v/2)=-iA_v. \qquad\text{(15)} Then every original edge satisfies qe2=1−Av2−Aw2=1−rv2rw2(1+rv2)(1+rw2)>0.(16) q_e^2=1-A_v^2-A_w^2 =\frac{1-r_v^2r_w^2}{(1+r_v^2)(1+r_w^2)}>0. \qquad\text{(16)} The second source’s exact positivity criterion is ℜq>(ℜav)2+(ℜaw)2\Re q>\sqrt{(\Re a_v)^2+(\Re a_w)^2}. Thus the positive root in (16) gives the original eight positive shapes, with all original face denominators regular.

Simultaneously multiplying the heights by 0≤λ≤10\le\lambda\le1 keeps their hyperbolic-sine products below one. This fixes the complete branches, on which rv,eorig=−iarcsin⁡(tanh(Hv/2)cosh(Hw/2))=−iαv,e.(17) r_{v,e}^{\mathrm{ orig}} =-i\arcsin\!\left(\tanh(H_v/2)\cosh(H_w/2)\right) =-i\alpha_{v,e}. \qquad\text{(17)} Equation (9) is exactly (1). This height contraction fixes the logarithmic marking; it is not identified with the radial charge solution path.

The original global uniqueness theorem makes this solution unique among all complex positive configurations. Its complex period Jacobian has positive definite Hermitian part. The implicit-function theorem consequently gives a holomorphic solution near each charge vector with a regular minimizer. These branches agree by uniqueness. Together with continuity of the minimizer, this also gives the compact-uniform assertion.

To exclude an unobserved positive solution at the exceptional corner, first note that every positive solution of (1) is purely imaginary. The reflection 𝒕↦−𝒕¯\mathbf t\mapsto-\overline{\mathbf t} preserves positivity: av↦−av¯a_v\mapsto-\overline{a_v} and qe↦qe¯q_e\mapsto\overline{q_e} preserve the original criterion. Its canonical returns are −rv,eorig¯-\overline{r_{v,e}^{\mathrm{ orig}}}, so it preserves (1). Global uniqueness fixes the reflected point. At a purely imaginary positive edge the imaginary return has the same sign as its endpoint’s imaginary angle, because its argument is itanh⁡(ℑtv/2)cosh⁡(ℑtw/2)i\tanh(\Im t_v/2)\cosh(\Im t_w/2) in i(−1,1)i(-1,1). The negative row in (1) therefore makes every selected angle −iHv-iH_v with Hv>0H_v>0. Such a solution is all regular by (16) and stationary for (8), contradicting the unique saturated minimizer at the exceptional pair. No other positive solution exists there.

At any minimizer, (9) and (11) also give 0<Hv(𝒄)≤2arsinh⁡tan⁡(πcvdv).(18) 0<H_v(\mathbf c)\le 2\operatorname{arsinh}\tan\!\left(\frac{\pi c_v}{d_v}\right). \qquad\text{(18)} On a simultaneous radial charge path to zero the heights therefore tend to zero. The original complete period Jacobian is 12diag⁡(dv)\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(d_v); its analytic solution agrees with these solutions by uniqueness. For a fixed nonexceptional endpoint, the compact radial segment, including zero, is thus contained in the positive domain. A radial path ending at the exceptional corner is positive for λ<1\lambda<1, and its minimizer converges to (14). Equations (16)–(17) give (2), the internal q=0q=0, and the claimed return angles.

For a singleton, no internal edge is present and the solution is explicit: H(c)=2arsinh⁡tan⁡(πc/d).(19) H(c)=2\operatorname{arsinh}\tan(\pi c/d). \qquad\text{(19)} It exists through unit charge precisely when d>2d>2. At any positive zero-power unit solution the original canonical bound |ℑrv,eorig|<π/2|\Im r_{v,e}^{\mathrm{ orig}}|<\pi/2 likewise forces dv>2d_v>2 at every vertex. This proves the necessity in (3), and the theorem proves sufficiency. For disconnected selected sets the same classification applies separately to each selected component, since all outside angles remain zero.

Compact uniformity above concerns compact subsets of the positive charge cube avoiding its exceptional point, or fixed simultaneous radial segments with regular endpoints. It is not uniformity on all partially zero-charge faces: a zero-charge vertex can become complete and change the effective selected components.

5. Scope and full direct-source readback

This settles radial continuation for the all-zero class. It does not settle the same question for adjacent nonzero signed powers: their real angles need not vanish, and the nonnegative incidence estimate (11) is not supplied by their original equations.

The theorem concerns the original positive subdivision and full marked primitive periods. The trivalent-pair failure does not assert nonhyperbolicity of its partially filled parent; the archived Newton/Conway treatment uses other coordinates. No charged quantum asymptotic, original-cycle coefficient, or arbitrary-short hyperbolic coverage follows here.
