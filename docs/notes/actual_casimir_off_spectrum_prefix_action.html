The actual Casimir prefix has a selected off-spectrum action

30 October 2026. This is a theorem for the original physical Casimir matrix, with an arbitrary spectral variable. It supplies a forward/backward Green-kernel ingredient for the exact exceptional-block contour. It does not continue a finite color or a magnetic index, evaluate the full polynomial operator by its symbol, or prove that a geometric saddle contributes to the source contour.

1. Statement and the strict half-prefix condition

Use the original root matrix J_t of the physical generic-jet theorem, with q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, 0<=t<=N-1. Let J_[m] be its first m rows and columns. Suppose

t/N -> tau, m/N -> nu, 0<nu<tau/2, 0<tau<1.

All convergence below is uniform when the two real parameters stay in compact subsets of this strict domain. Put

beta=pi(1-tau+2nu) in (0,pi),
H_beta={z: Re[exp(-i beta/2)(2-z)]>0}.

On every compact subset of H_beta, the actual matrices J_[m]-zI are invertible for all sufficiently large N, with a uniformly bounded inverse. In particular this includes every fixed compact set of real spectral parameters z<2. The bound does not hold here at a central prefix nu=tau/2; that endpoint is deliberately excluded.

There is a canonical analytic logarithm of the determinant, selected from negative real infinity, for which

N^-1 Log det(J_[m]-zI) -> S_(tau,nu)(z),          (1)

locally uniformly on H_beta, with every fixed z derivative. Write

A(x)=-4 exp(i pi(2x-tau))
                     sin(pi(tau-x))sin(pi x),
w=2-z.

The limiting action is the explicit single integral

S_(tau,nu)(z)=integral_0^nu
   Log[(w+2A(x)+sqrt(w(w+4A(x))))/2] dx.          (2)

The square root and logarithm in (2) are selected at large positive w and continued in H_beta. They are not pointwise principal choices of a determinant or discriminant. Formula (1) is a leading action, not a relative-amplitude expansion.

2. An exact Dirichlet decomposition of the original matrix

The actual root entries, with U=Q^r and T=Q^t, are

D_r=U/T [2(q^-1+qT)-(q+q^-1)U],
A_r=(U-T)(1-q^2 U)/T.

Put A_(-1)=0. Direct algebra gives BOTH identities

D_r=q+q^-1+q^-1 A_r+q A_(r-1),                 (3)

R_r:=D_r-2-A_r-A_(r-1)
  =-U(q-1)^2(Tq^2-Uq^2-Uq-U+1)/(Tq^2).        (4)

Hence |R_r|<=C/N^2, uniformly over all physical r,t. The unitary diagonal sign matrix changes the offdiagonals of J_[m] to -A_r. After this conjugation, (4) says exactly

J_[m]-2I=L_A+diag(R_r),                        (5)

where L_A has diagonal A_(r-1)+A_r and adjacent entries -A_r. For v_m=0 its sesquilinear form is

<v,L_A v>=sum_(r=0)^(m-1) A_r |v_r-v_(r+1)|^2. (6)

The term r=m-1 is the retained Dirichlet boundary term. At the other endpoint A_(-1)=0 exactly; no omitted boundary term is being estimated.

The original coefficients also have the exact polar form

A_r=-4 exp(i(2r+1-t)pi/N)
                sin((t-r)pi/N)sin((r+1)pi/N).

For 0<=r<m, both sine factors are positive and their marked arguments obey

0<Arg A_r<=beta_N:=pi+(2m-1-t)pi/N<pi.         (7)

In the strict scaling range beta_N tends to beta<pi. If w=2-z, rotation of wI+L_A by exp(-i beta_N/2) gives

Re <v,exp(-i beta_N/2)(wI+L_A)v>
   >=Re[exp(-i beta_N/2)w] ||v||^2.             (8)

The perturbation (4) costs at most C/N^2 in (8). Every compact of H_beta has a positive margin in its right side; (5)–(8) therefore give the claimed uniform inverse bound for the ORIGINAL prefix. The sign conjugation is unitary and does not alter determinants.

3. Determining the actual logarithm, without an assumed WKB solution

For a finite N, restrict first to

Re[exp(-i beta_N/2)(2-z)]>2C/N^2.

There the rotated matrix in (8) is strictly accretive. Its principal matrix logarithm defines

Log det(J_[m]-zI)
   :=i m beta_N/2
      +tr Log[exp(-i beta_N/2)(J_[m]-zI)].       (9)

This agrees with m Log(-z)+O(m/|z|) as z tends to negative real infinity. These halfplanes exhaust every compact subset of H_beta. The logarithm in (9), rather than the principal logarithm of the scalar determinant, retains the actual accumulated phase.

The inverse bound gives

|N^-1 partial_z Log det(J_[m]-zI)|<=C_K m/N.

At one sufficiently negative fixed real z, the uniformly bounded entries and fixed bandwidth give a bounded normalized logarithm by its convergent trace series. The derivative bound then gives local boundedness throughout H_beta. These analytic functions form a locally bounded normal family there.

It remains to determine its limit on a nonempty open set. For each fixed integer k, multiplication of the tridiagonal matrices gives a finite sum over nearest-neighbor closed paths. Paths within k rows of an endpoint contribute O(k/N) after normalized trace, since their entries are uniformly bounded. On all other paths, the slowly varying entries may be replaced by their common local limits with error O_k(1/N). Thus

N^-1 tr(J_[m]^k) -> integral_0^nu (1/pi) integral_0^pi
        [2+2A(x)+2A(x)cos(phi)]^k dphi dx.       (10)

The coefficient of the zero Laurent power in the local tridiagonal symbol is precisely the inner integral in (10); this proves the path count directly. For |z| sufficiently large, the uniformly convergent logarithmic trace series and (10) show that (1) has limit

integral_0^nu (1/pi) integral_0^pi
       Log[w+4A(x)cos^2(phi/2)] dphi dx.         (11)

For every x in [0,nu], A(x) is zero or lies in the cone of (7). The argument of the logarithm in (11), after rotation, has strictly positive real part for z in H_beta. Consequently (11) is analytic on H_beta with the branch selected by negative infinity. The identity theorem and the normal-family bound make every subsequential limit equal to (11), proving convergence of the full sequence. Cauchy’s formula proves convergence of all fixed spectral derivatives.

Finally integrate the elementary logarithmic symbol. For large positive w, expansion in A/w (or differentiation in w followed by its value at infinity) gives

(1/pi) integral_0^pi Log[w+4A cos^2(phi/2)] dphi
   =Log[(w+2A+sqrt(w(w+4A)))/2].                (12)

Both sides continue analytically on H_beta. Indeed w and w+4A lie in the same rotated right halfplane; the selected product square root is nonzero there. The selected numerator in (12) is nonzero as well: a zero would force A=0 after squaring, whereas then it equals 2w. Equation (12) proves (2) with its source-selected branch.

4. What this gives to the exceptional-block problem

The forward resolvent numerator is exactly det(zI-J_[m]); it differs from the determinant in (1) by (-1)^m, whose marked logarithm must be retained. The actual trailing prefix is the reflected conjugate root matrix, so it has the conjugate statement with its own corresponding halfplane and branch. Thus suitable strict forward/backward portions of the original Green kernel have a selected off-spectrum leading action, including all real geometric target traces below 2.

This does not yet control prefixes reaching or crossing their midpoint, complex magnetic continuation, a relative nonzero amplitude, or the resolvent contour’s intersection with a geometric steepest-descent cycle. No large-degree polynomial has been replaced by scalar symbol evaluation in this theorem. The moving generic-q normal also remains the one in the exact contour formula; (1) concerns spectral derivatives of the root prefix, not generic-q derivatives of the whole source.
