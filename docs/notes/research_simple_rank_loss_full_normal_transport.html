Exact rank loss and the remaining physical normal

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

At a simple rank loss of an actual finite-level leading coefficient, the first-normal equation recovers a missing next value. It generally leaves one next normal undetermined. This note identifies that channel, gives the exact criterion for additional rows to remove it, and shows that the original closing trace sees it. A classical exceptional color alone does not imply such a finite-level rank loss.

1. The actual equations at a physical row

Use the original algebra and trace PN(Z)=2TN(Z/2)+2,𝒜N=ℂ[Z]/(PN),ϕN(f)=ηN2[ZN−1]can⁡(f),η=(−1)N−1.(1) P_N(Z)=2T_N(Z/2)+2,\qquad \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[Z]/(P_N),\qquad \phi_N(f)=\eta N^2[Z^{N-1}]\operatorname{can}(f), \quad \eta=(-1)^{N-1}. \qquad\text{(1)} The distinct double nodes are cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N),0≤j<⌊N/2⌋. c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N),\qquad 0\le j<\lfloor N/2\rfloor. When NN is odd there is also the simple node −2-2. Set h=N−1,CN(u)=2cos⁡(2πu+π/N). h=N^{-1},\qquad C_N(u)=2\cos(2\pi u+\pi/N). The full-step source equations in the first pinned source have ordinary residuals divisible by PNP_N, before their physical evaluation. In this coordinate, PN(CN(u))=4sin⁡2(πNu).(2) P_N(C_N(u))=4\sin^2(\pi Nu). \qquad\text{(2)} Consequently their values and first uu-derivatives vanish at u=j/Nu=j/N. These are derivatives of ordinary residuals, not a derivation of the physical quotient.

Consider one such finite-order row, possibly with several components. Move all already specified values and normals to the right. Suppose the only unknown value packet is x=F(u0+mh)∈ℂdx=F(u_0+mh)\in\mathbb C^d, and its unknown first-normal packet is y=F′(u0+mh)y=F'(u_0+mh). The two equations are exactly Ax=b,Ay+Ȧx=ḃ.(3) A x=b,\qquad A y+\dot A x=\dot b . \qquad\text{(3)} Here AA is the full finite-NN coefficient at u0u_0, Ȧ\dot A is its ordinary uu-derivative, and ḃ\dot b includes every derivative of the coefficients and known shifted values on the right. A dot in (3) never means that the unknown packet is held constant when the ordinary source equation is differentiated. The level and all exterior parameters are fixed in this calculation.

Equation (3) also applies to a larger future packet, with rectangular AA, after all its unknown shifted values are included. A row involving another unknown future value cannot be stacked as an equation for xx alone by treating that extra value as known.

2. A simple square rank loss

Theorem 1. Let A(u)A(u) be a holomorphic dd-by-dd matrix near u0u_0. Suppose rank⁡A(u0)=d−1,ker⁡A(u0)=ℂr,ker⁡A(u0)𝖳=ℂℓ𝖳,ℓȦr≠0.(4) \operatorname{rank}A(u_0)=d-1,\qquad \ker A(u_0)=\mathbb C r,\qquad \ker A(u_0)^{\mathsf T}=\mathbb C\ell^{\mathsf T}, \qquad \ell\dot A r\ne0. \qquad\text{(4)} The vectors ℓ,r\ell,r are nonzero, and no conjugation is used in ℓȦr\ell\dot A r. The system (3) is solvable if and only if ℓb=0\ell b=0. When it is solvable, its value xx is unique and its normal yy has precisely one undetermined scalar: Axp=b,x=xp+ℓ(ḃ−Ȧxp)ℓȦrr,Ayp=ḃ−Ȧx,y=yp+ζr,ζ∈ℂ.(5) \begin{split} Ax_p&=b,\\ x&=x_p+ \frac{\ell(\dot b-\dot A x_p)}{\ell\dot A r}\,r,\\ Ay_p&=\dot b-\dot A x,\qquad y=y_p+\zeta r,\quad \zeta\in\mathbb C. \end{split} \qquad\text{(5)} The condition in (4) is equivalent to a simple zero of det⁡A(u)\det A(u) at u0u_0.

Proof. The range of AA is ker⁡ℓ\ker\ell, so the value equation is solvable precisely when ℓb=0\ell b=0. Its solutions are xp+zrx_p+zr. The normal equation is solvable precisely when ℓ(ḃ−Ȧxp−zȦr)=0\ell(\dot b-\dot A x_p-z\dot A r)=0. This uniquely gives the displayed zz; the solutions for yy then differ by ker⁡A\ker A. Changing xpx_p changes zz by the opposite multiple, so xx is independent of that choice.

At a rank-(d−1)(d-1) matrix, its adjugate is a nonzero scalar multiple of rℓr\ell. Thus (det⁡A)′=tr⁡(adj⁡(A)Ȧ)(\det A)'=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A)\dot A) is a nonzero scalar multiple of ℓȦr\ell\dot A r. This proves the last assertion. ▫\square

In the scalar case, A(u0)=0A(u_0)=0, Ȧ(u0)≠0\dot A(u_0)\ne0. The exact equations reduce to b=0,x=ḃ/Ȧ,(6) b=0,\qquad x=\dot b/\dot A, \qquad\text{(6)} and leave yy arbitrary. Thus first normals supply the missing value row; they do not themselves supply a full next-state transfer.

The same fact has a local-algebra description. At a double spectral node cc, put ϵ=Z−c\epsilon=Z-c. In ℂ[ϵ]/(ϵ2)\mathbb C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2), multiplication by a coefficient with a simple zero is (a1ϵ)(f0+f1ϵ)=a1f0ϵ.(7) (a_1\epsilon)(f_0+f_1\epsilon)=a_1f_0\epsilon . \qquad\text{(7)} It detects the value and kills the remaining normal component. This is an exact statement about the retained nilpotent, not about a reduced value-only quotient.

3. All aligned rows and their precise recovery criterion

Theorem 2. Stack any collection of actual rows aligned on the same unknown value packet, obtaining Bx=b,By+Ḃx=ḃ,(8) Bx=b,\qquad By+\dot Bx=\dot b , \qquad\text{(8)} where B:ℂd→ℂpB:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^p. Assume that at least one solution exists, as it does for the original source with its actual previous data. Define K=ker⁡B,π:ℂp→coker⁡B,H=πḂ|K.(9) K=\ker B,\qquad \pi:\mathbb C^p\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}B,\qquad H=\left.\pi\dot B\right|_K . \qquad\text{(9)} Then the possible differences of value packets are precisely ker⁡H⊆K\ker H\subseteq K. For each fixed admissible value packet, the possible normals form a translate of KK. In particular:

  • the value is unique if and only if HH is injective;
  • the full value-and-normal packet is unique if and only if BB has full column rank.

Proof. Subtract two solutions. The resulting equations are Bδx=0,Bδy=−Ḃδx.(10) B\,\delta x=0,\qquad B\,\delta y=-\dot B\,\delta x . \qquad\text{(10)} The second is solvable precisely when πḂδx=0\pi\dot B\,\delta x=0. Every δx∈ker⁡H\delta x\in\ker H therefore occurs, and its corresponding δy\delta y’s differ by KK. For δx=0\delta x=0, every element of KK is an allowed δy\delta y, irrespective of HH. This proves both criteria. ▫\square

In Theorem 1, suppose further actual rows have coefficient matrix CC on the same future value packet. Once its value has been fixed by (5), the additional normal equations determine ζ\zeta if and only if Cr≠0.(11) Cr\ne0. \qquad\text{(11)} Indeed the stacked coefficient is (A𝖳,C𝖳)𝖳(A^{\mathsf T},C^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}, whose kernel is ℂr∩ker⁡C\mathbb Cr\cap\ker C. More generally, independent normal constraints applied after the value is known remove the remaining normal exactly when their restriction to KK is injective. All right sides must still be the original compatible source data.

These are statements about the equations at this row. Later rows, other recovery equations, or the original global closing conditions may determine the remaining scalar even when (11) fails locally. Theorem 2 neither discards those equations nor asserts a free parameter in the globally defined source.

4. The original trace sees the remaining normal

For every double node cc, including the sole node at N=2N=2, PN″(c)=2N24−c2.(12) P_N''(c)=\frac{2N^2}{4-c^2}. \qquad\text{(12)} Define the ordinary polynomial of degree N−1N-1 Rc(Z)=2PN(Z)PN″(c)(Z−c)=(4−c2)PN(Z)N2(Z−c).(13) R_c(Z)=\frac{2P_N(Z)}{P_N''(c)(Z-c)} =\frac{(4-c^2)P_N(Z)}{N^2(Z-c)}. \qquad\text{(13)} Division in this formula is exact ordinary polynomial division. The value of RcR_c at every physical node is zero. Its derivative is one at cc and zero at every other double node. At odd NN its value at the simple node −2-2 is also zero. These facts follow directly from the root multiplicities of PNP_N.

Proposition 3. For every original spectator g∈𝒜Ng\in\mathcal A_N, ϕN(Rcg)=η(4−c2)g(c).(14) \phi_N(R_c g)=\eta(4-c^2)g(c). \qquad\text{(14)} The identity holds coefficientwise with arbitrary ordinary exterior variables and their fixed derivatives retained.

Proof. At the double node cc, the only nonzero jet of RcgR_c g is its derivative g(c)g(c). All other physical jets vanish. Hence Rcg=g(c)RcR_c g=g(c)R_c in the full algebra. The polynomial PNP_N is monic, so the leading coefficient in (13) is (4−c2)/N2(4-c^2)/N^2. Apply (1). Formula (12), used above, also follows from the Chebyshev differential equation at a root with TN(c/2)=−1T_N(c/2)=-1 and TN′(c/2)=0T_N'(c/2)=0. ▫\square

Thus a spectral normal difference ζr\zeta r in a vector channel can be represented by ζrRc\zeta rR_c. For a vector spectator g=(g1,…,gd)g=(g_1,\ldots,g_d), its local contribution to the original closing is ∑ν=1dϕN(ζrνRcgν)=η(4−c2)ζ∑ν=1drνgν(c).(15) \sum_{\nu=1}^d\phi_N(\zeta r_\nu R_cg_\nu) =\eta(4-c^2)\zeta\sum_{\nu=1}^dr_\nu g_\nu(c). \qquad\text{(15)} This need not vanish. For the scalar channel, g=1g=1 already detects it. Original crossing phases can remain inside gg; their physical values are not dropped.

Formula (15) concerns vector components at one common physical color. For a future packet spanning several shifted colors, use the corresponding cardinal RcaR_{c_a} at each distinct color and sum its separate contribution (14), with the appropriate component spectator. Repeated occurrences of the same physical color must satisfy their original identification; they are not independent normal coordinates.

If the packet in (3) uses the uu-normal instead, then CN′(u0+mh)≠0C_N'(u_0+mh)\ne0 at a double node. The spectral normal in (15) is ζr/CN′(u0+mh)\zeta r/C_N'(u_0+mh). The conclusion is unchanged. There is no independent spectral normal in the quotient at the odd simple node. An angular first derivative there vanishes for an invariant spectral polynomial; it is not an additional free channel to which Theorem 1 should be applied.

The cardinal in (13) proves visibility in the original algebra. It does not say that adding this cardinal to an actual source continues to solve every shifted row. That is the distinction between a local missing transfer datum and a global homogeneous solution.

5. Why a second differentiated equation is not available for free

Let an ordinary residual of a cleared full-step row be ℛ(u)=PN(CN(u))HN(u),(16) \mathscr R(u)=P_N(C_N(u))\,H_N(u), \qquad\text{(16)} with HNH_N holomorphic near the physical point. Formula (2) gives the exact next derivative ℛ″(j/N)=8π2N2HN(j/N).(17) \mathscr R''(j/N)=8\pi^2N^2 H_N(j/N). \qquad\text{(17)} Thus only the value and first differentiated equations (3) are homogeneous at the grid. Passing to a second differentiated row would introduce the actual quotient residual HNH_N. Setting the right side of (17) to zero would delete source data. Equivalently, the original double-root relation does not become a triple-root relation.

Fixed exterior-parameter derivatives are different: they differentiate a PNP_N multiple without changing its physical root multiplicities. They may be retained in (3)–(17), with the usual product rules, wherever the coefficient/rank chart is defined.

6. Application and boundary of the conclusion

The leading matrix in these statements is the actual finite-level coefficient after retaining every quantum shift and every source channel. A limiting characteristic phase color, even when it equals an exact physical node, does not by itself establish that this matrix is singular. Terms of smaller asymptotic size can repair the coefficient, or can have greater shift order and change the appropriate unknown packet. No such terms are omitted in (3).

If an actual simple rank loss does occur, (5) gives its exact value repair and (11) gives the test for removing the residual normal with further aligned rows. Quantitative transfer also needs bounds for the inverse on the range of AA, for (ℓȦr)−1(\ell\dot A r)^{-1}, and, when used, for the extra restriction in (11). If these are polynomially conditioned, the determined data cost polynomially. No bound for the undetermined normal follows from that fact alone.

In particular this note supplies neither propagation across every classical exceptional color nor a growth rate for a general composed source. The original traces and global source constraints remain available to control the identified datum. Local physical jets do not give an off-grid polynomial bound for a canonical polynomial of growing degree.
