Coherent endpoint inputs have a controlled bulk crossing norm

Written proof, 24 November 2026.

A small endpoint input need not stay near the endpoints after an original crossing. Here the entire outgoing packet has an explicit positive exponential norm rate, uniformly for every coherent input in a sublinear endpoint band. This includes coefficients and phases chosen separately at each level. A bounded checked Hurwitz observable controls cancellation between the actual crossing columns.

The original binomial cut also has an exact weighted-cup realization. Its censored finite endpoint approximation gives the same outgoing norm rate for the full purified cut, with an arbitrarily small absolute error after crossing. The ordinary cap, pivot and all crossing phases remain explicit. No lower bound for a returned cap or an original closed knot scalar is inferred.

6. Relation to earlier endpoint models and source scope

[E] already proves subexponential factorial-chart costs on sublinear head bands, with the exact Fourier source and endpoint arrows. [T] proves bounded Euler transitions for compressed single-interval head blocks in appropriate factorial gauges. It explicitly keeps the complementary and cross-collar blocks. The present result estimates the full outgoing high/low column family, including the bulk reached by long transfers, and controls arbitrary coherent combinations before taking their norm.

[W]’s trace-class Poisson limit has error O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). That algebraic error is not multiplied through a growing crossing here. Only its exact finite censored model is used in (26)–(28). A complex parameter can be treated by a holomorphic source/co-source pair on a square-root chart, but no positive Hermitian purification or uniform nonzero norm theorem for such complex parameters is claimed.

Neither (6) nor (29) supplies a lower bound after a prescribed ordinary or marked cap. Nor do these estimates assert that arbitrary turns, branches, or neighboring short components preserve the selected endpoint spans or the exponent h*h_*. The returned source still has its original coherent contraction; its overlap has not been estimated. In particular this is not a knot-volume or finite-exception theorem.
