Rivin optimization, a regularized angle flow, and the quantum source

Research note, 26 October 2026, following the user’s suggestion to use Rivin’s geometrization and flow viewpoint. The classical geometric problem has a precise convex formulation. We give an exact constrained flow and prove global convergence when the triangulation is complete and positive geometric. We then identify the additional lift needed for the actual quantum period, retaining a newly computed paired source-normalization identity.

The volume conjecture is not proved. This is not a replacement positive quantum state or a claim that real convexity determines the original finite period.

Current source frontier (27 October): exact complete Fourier localization places the original period entirely at the resonant row; regular finite Fourier rows vanish exactly. The all-strand Euler/Cauchy theorem now expresses that original period as a specified finite coefficient or endpoint divided difference, with the whole word retained. Its physical-clock equation has a nilpotent apparent denominator. The full peripheral boundary calculation proves the required first pole cancellation and corrects a raw-prefix half-shift. These provide exact inputs for the geometric comparison; Rivin uniqueness still does not prove its selected nonzero image or the sharp norm. The critical-path audit keeps those three final gates open.

1. Existing scope and the correct optimization problem

Rivin, Combinatorial optimization in geometry explicitly develops linear programming and network-flow methods; the journal abstract states that scope. The triangulated Casson–Rivin formulation is stated in Futer–Gueritaud, 1004.0440: linear angle equations define a convex polytope, and an interior critical point of volume gives the complete hyperbolic metric. Luo–Yang, 1404.5365 uses the Fenchel dual of volume for decorated ideal metrics and curvature. For coverage of extended convergent flows on cusped triangulations, see the abstract of Feng–Ge–Hua, 2009.05842. No published flow or rigidity proof is used as a premise below.

For t tetrahedra let theta_(j,0),theta_(j,1),theta_(j,2)>0 be the angles on the three opposite edge pairs. Write C for the edge-incidence matrix: a column counts the two local edges in that pair, including multiplicity if their global edges coincide. The exact angle domain is

A={theta>0: sum_(a=0)^2 theta_(j,a)=pi,
                  C theta=2pi*1}.              (1.1)

Volume is strictly concave on A. The convex objective is its negative, rather than volume itself. An ideal triangulation alone does not guarantee an interior critical point. The positive complete triangulation from the audited geometric input does supply one; this is where that input is decisive.

2. An exact flow-network representation

Use a bipartite incidence graph with one node for each tetrahedron, one node for each global edge, and one arc for each of the six local edge occurrences, directed from its tetrahedron to its global edge. Give the arc the corresponding dihedral angle. Every tetrahedron has total outgoing flux 2pi; every global edge has total incoming flux 2pi. Impose equality of the two arcs belonging to each local opposite edge pair. These flow constraints are exactly (1.1), in both directions.

This graph is derived from the dual cell complex: tetrahedra are dual vertices and global edges are dual faces. The opposite-pair equalities are additional local constraints. Deleting them would give a different transportation problem. Likewise the bare dual one-skeleton does not by itself record which original edges are encircled or the peripheral holonomies.

Let A_0 be the full integer matrix of (1.1), and let B have columns forming a real basis of ker A_0. This description requires no invertible face-matrix chart. For quantum lattice applications one must retain the integral kernel and its peripheral marking as well; a real change of coordinates is not an integral saturation theorem.

3. Strict concavity and the geometric maximizer

Set Lambda’(a)=-log(2sin a), Lambda(0)=0, and

V(theta)=sum_j [Lambda(theta_j0)+Lambda(theta_j1)
                                  +Lambda(theta_j2)].

For alpha+beta+gamma=pi the negative Hessian in alpha,beta is

[[cot alpha+cot gamma, cot gamma],
 [cot gamma, cot beta+cot gamma]].             (3.1)

Its determinant is one and its first diagonal entry is sin beta/(sin alpha sin gamma)>0. Thus it is positive definite. This proves strict concavity on every affine angle domain.

The existing positive-factorial note already gives the independent stationary-point argument. Choose compatible cusp horospheres in a complete geometric triangulation. The truncated length of each global edge agrees across its incident tetrahedra. Summing the ideal Schlaefli identity gives

dV=-(1/2)sum_e length_e d(total angle_e)=0

on the angle domain; changes of decorations cancel at ideal vertices. Hence its geometric angle point theta_* is stationary. Strict concavity makes it the unique global maximizer, with

V(theta_*)=Vol(M).                            (3.2)

This argument proves the direction needed here from the supplied geometric point. It does not assume that an arbitrary nonempty angle polytope has an interior maximizer.

The same optimization generates the marked A-curve

This also gives a precise connection to the requested torsor formulation. Use logarithmic shape coordinates x=(u_j,v_j), with u_j=Log z_j, v_j=Log z_j’’, and their already audited symplectic form Omega. Let E be the isotropic space of independent edge Hamiltonians. Tangential angle structures are E^perp. The marked peripheral functions identify E^perp/E with a two-dimensional symplectic space, with

Omega_red=-(1/2)dU wedge dV,
U=Log m, V=Log l, m=M^2, l=L_intr^2.

All peripheral logs are centered at the chosen complete lift; its longitude character remains recorded separately. At a positive shape, the sine rule gives

dV_volume(delta theta)=Omega(Re x,delta theta). (3.3)

For example, in one tetrahedron with alpha=Im u, gamma=Im v, beta=pi-alpha-gamma, Re u=log(sin beta/sin gamma) and Re v=log(sin alpha/sin beta). Differentiating its Lobachevsky volume gives exactly Re u dgamma-Re v dalpha.

Prescribe the two real boundary angles a=Im U,b=Im V, and maximize volume on that affine slice of A. Its tangent space is E: fixing both peripheral angles removes the entire quotient E^perp/E. By (3.3), an interior critical point is equivalent to Re x in E^perp, which is precisely the real part of the edge gluing equations. Their imaginary part was imposed already. Thus the constrained maximizer solves the full complex gluing. Without fixing a,b, stationarity requires Re x in E, which also makes Re U=Re V=0 and gives completeness at a=b=0.

Near the complete geometric point define the value function

V_boundary(a,b)=max_(theta in A,
                        Im U=a,Im V=b) V(theta).

The positive geometric gluing branch exists there by the earlier rigidity proof. Its map to (a,b) is locally invertible because Im(dV/dU)>0. Hence these maxima are interior, unique and smooth. Reduction of (3.3) gives their exact differential

dV_boundary=(1/2)(Re V da-Re U db),
Re U=-2 partial_b V_boundary,
Re V= 2 partial_a V_boundary.                (3.4)

Consequently the marked geometric A-curve is locally the Legendre graph of this boundary volume function. This identifies the same regulator differential already used in the Heisenberg period space. It is a statement about the classical geometric branch, not a quantum-A symbol or selected-period theorem.

For clarity its boundary Hessian can be computed explicitly. If tau=dV/dU=p+i q_c at the complete point, q_c>0, then

Hess V_boundary=-(1/(2q_c))
                    [[|tau|^2,-p],[-p,1]],
det(Hess V_boundary)=1/4.                    (3.5)

Indeed Re U=(b-p a)/q_c and Re V=(p b-|tau|^2 a)/q_c to first order; insert them in (3.4). The negative Hessian is positive definite. The same constrained flow below applies to each nearby fixed-(a,b) slice, giving a variational construction of this marked A-curve germ.

There is an intrinsic boundary formulation. The calculation of (3.5) is pointwise on the positive gluing branch, with its varying tau=dV/dU and Im tau>0. Hence the convex potential

Phi(a,b)=-2 V_boundary(a,b)

satisfies the real Monge–Ampere equation

det(Hess Phi)=1,
U=partial_b Phi+i a,
V=-partial_a Phi+i b.                        (3.6)

Conversely a smooth strictly convex Phi with this determinant gives a positive complex curve by the last two formulas. Indeed dU wedge dV=(det(Hess Phi)-1)da wedge db=0, and Phi_bb>0 makes U a local real coordinate pair. Therefore V is holomorphic in U, with

dV/dU=-Phi_ab/Phi_bb+i/Phi_bb.

This is a direct local equivalence of the convex boundary potential and the positive marked complex A-curve germ. The fundamental-class mark still fixes its volume constant and the regulator’s central period. The Monge–Ampere equation alone does not specify that mark, a particular A-curve, or a quantum period. It makes the geometric target independent of a chosen tetrahedral coordinate chart.

4. A regularized flow with the exact same objective

Use h(theta)=-sum_i log theta_i as a metric potential. In coordinates theta=theta_0+B y define the constrained flow by

dot(theta)=B [B^T diag(theta_i^(-2)) B]^(-1)
                                 B^T grad V(theta). (4.1)

The matrix is positive definite whenever B has independent columns and theta>0. All angle equations are preserved because A_0 B=0. Moreover

dV/dt=(B^T grad V)^T
         [B^T diag(theta_i^(-2))B]^(-1)(B^T grad V)>=0. (4.2)

The barrier changes the metric, not the objective or its critical point. There is no regularization parameter to send to zero.

Here is a global convergence proof, given theta_*>0 from Section 3. Use the Bregman function with its arguments in the following order:

D(theta_*,theta)=sum_i [theta_*i/theta_i-1
                              -log(theta_*i/theta_i)]. (4.3)

Differentiating (4.3) and using theta_*-theta in im B gives

dot D=-(theta_*-theta)^T grad V(theta)
                          <=-[V(theta_*)-V(theta)]. (4.4)

The inequality is the tangent inequality for a concave function. Thus D is nonincreasing. It tends to infinity if any theta_i tends to zero, because every theta_*i is positive. The angle closure is compact, so the solution stays in a compact subset of A. The smooth vector field therefore exists for all time.

On a compact positive region containing this trajectory and its segments to theta_*, the restricted negative Hessian of V has a positive lower bound mu. Choose B orthonormal for this estimate. If theta_i>=delta there, the inverse metric in (4.2) is bounded below by delta^2. The strong-concavity inequality gives

||B^T grad V||^2>=2mu [V(theta_*)-V(theta)],
d/dt[V(theta_*)-V(theta)]
               <=-2mu delta^2[V(theta_*)-V(theta)]. (4.5)

Consequently the volume gap decays exponentially and theta(t) converges to theta_*. The constants depend on the triangulation and starting point; no uniform complexity estimate across knots is asserted. This proves a regularized geometric flow for every complete positive ideal triangulation, not a knot-by-knot criterion.

5. The convex dual makes the curvature residual explicit

Let Delta be the closed tetrahedral angle simplex, and define

F(lambda)=sum_j sup_(theta_j in Delta)
        [V_j(theta_j)-lambda^T C_j theta_j]
                        +2pi sum_e lambda_e.    (5.1)

It is convex as a supremum of affine functions of lambda. Where its local maximizers are unique and positive, direct differentiation gives

grad F=2pi*1-C theta(lambda).                  (5.2)

Thus the dual gradient is the edge-curvature residual. The edge potentials are the decorated-length variables, up to the fixed sign and factor from the Schlaefli formula. This is the co-volume viewpoint’s role in the proposed flow. Degenerate local maximizers require an extension or nonsmooth treatment; (4.1) avoids that issue by staying in the positive primal domain.
