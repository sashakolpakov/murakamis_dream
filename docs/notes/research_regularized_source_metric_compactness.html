Bounds on the regularized source metrics and their penalty

20 November 2026. Research note; previous finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The regularized source problem has a unique positive matrix minimizer. The earlier proof did not estimate its regularization penalty. Here we bound that penalty and the spectra of the optimizing metrics using only known input tensor norms and the ordinary trivial-color anchor. For a fixed original braid, the logarithms of the optimizing eigenvalues are of order at most the quantum level. The penalized minimum itself therefore has the same leading rate as the actual main-color energy.

This is spectral control. It does not identify the optimizing eigenvectors or matrix symbols with a hyperbolic angle structure. That quantum to classical comparison remains necessary.

1. An explicit near-minimizer on an anchored tree

Let a finite tree have m≥2m\ge2 vertices, edge spaces of dimensions nen_e, and one vector output YY. Use the bilinear contractions and full edge gauges of the regularized source theorem. Assume the known anchor ∥Y∥≥1\|Y\|\ge1. Put B=∏vmax⁡(1,∥Tv∥),D=∑ene,Am(B,D,η)=log⁡(1+2D)+(2+2m(m−1))log⁡B+(m−1)log⁡m2η.(1) B=\prod_v\max(1,\|T_v\|),\qquad D=\sum_e n_e,\qquad A_m(B,D,\eta)=\log(1+2D) +(2+2m(m-1))\log B+(m-1)\log\frac m{2\eta}. \qquad\text{(1)} Here 0<η≤10<\eta\le1, the input norms are the original coordinate norms, and the output vertex is the root. In particular B≥1B\ge1 and D≥1D\ge1.

Theorem 1. There are full edge metrics, specified by finite operations on the proper subtree messages, for which 0≤L(H)−log⁡∥Y∥≤η,log⁡Z(H)≤Am(B,D,η).(2) 0\le L(H)-\log\|Y\|\le\eta,\qquad \log Z(H)\le A_m(B,D,\eta). \qquad\text{(2)} No choice in the construction divides by the output YY.

Proof. Orient each edge toward the root, with its vector gauge acting on the child side. Reversing an earlier edge convention replaces HeH_e by H¯e−1\overline H_e^{-1}, preserving both the local norms and ZZ, so this choice changes no variational value. Let aea_e be the ordinary contraction of the proper subtree below that edge. It is a vector on that edge. Tree Cauchy gives both B−1≤∥ae∥≤B.(3) B^{-1}\le\|a_e\|\le B. \qquad\text{(3)} The upper bound contracts the subtree. For the lower bound, leave aea_e uncontracted and apply tree Cauchy to the rest: 1≤∥Y∥≤B∥ae∥1\le\|Y\|\le B\|a_e\|. In particular every such message is nonzero, using only the known anchor.

Choose an edge gauge geg_e sending aea_e to the first unit vector e0e_0, with ∥ge∥,∥ge−1∥≤B\|g_e\|,\|g_e^{-1}\|\le B. One construction first rotates ae/∥ae∥a_e/\|a_e\| to e0e_0, then scales its first coordinate by ∥ae∥−1\|a_e\|^{-1}. Applying all these gauges at once is consistent: gauges cancel inside a contracted subtree. Therefore each proper vertex tensor, when all its child indices are zero, is exactly e0e_0 on its parent edge. The corresponding root slice is exactly YY. All output-excited entries with zero child indices vanish. The gauged norm of any local tensor is at most ∥Tv∥Bdeg⁡(v)≤Bm.(4) \|T_v\|B^{\deg(v)}\le B^m. \qquad\text{(4)}

Let the root have depth zero. At an edge whose child has depth rr, apply the further child-side gauge De(t)=diag⁡(1,tr,…,tr)D_e(t)=\operatorname{diag}(1,t^r,\ldots,t^r), with t≥1t\ge1. Its inverse transpose acts on the parent’s input leg. An excited parent output at a vertex of depth rr, together with k≥1k\ge1 excited child indices, receives exponent r−k(r+1)≤−1r-k(r+1)\le-1. Without an excited output, any excited child also gives exponent at most −1-1. The excluded case of an excited output and no excited child was already zero. Leaves remain e0e_0. Thus every entry outside the zero-index slice is suppressed by at least t−1t^{-1}.

These entries are orthogonal in the coordinate norm to the unchanged zero-index slice. Its norm is one at a proper vertex and ∥Y∥≥1\|Y\|\ge1 at the root. By (4) and log⁡(1+x)≤x\log(1+x)\le x, L(H(t))−log⁡∥Y∥≤mB2m2t2.(5) L(H(t))-\log\|Y\| \le\frac{mB^{2m}}{2t^2}. \qquad\text{(5)} The complete edge gauge ge(t)=De(t)geg_e(t)=D_e(t)g_e has ∥ge(t)∥≤Btm−1,∥ge(t)−1∥≤B. \|g_e(t)\|\le Bt^{m-1},\qquad \|g_e(t)^{-1}\|\le B. Set He(t)=ge(t)*ge(t)H_e(t)=g_e(t)^*g_e(t). Removing the unitary factors of these gauges preserves all local norms, as in the direct source. The trace bound is Z(H(t))≤1+2DB2t2(m−1)≤(1+2D)B2t2(m−1).(6) Z(H(t))\le1+2DB^2t^{2(m-1)} \le(1+2D)B^2t^{2(m-1)}. \qquad\text{(6)} Take t=Bmm/(2η)≥1t=B^m\sqrt{m/(2\eta)}\ge1. Equations (5)–(6) give (2), including exactly the constants in (1). ▫\square

2. The penalty at the regularized minimum

Let H(ε)H^{(\varepsilon)} minimize Jε=L+εlog⁡ZJ_\varepsilon=L+\varepsilon\log Z, with 0<ε≤10<\varepsilon\le1. Apply Theorem 1 with η=ε\eta=\varepsilon. Since L(H(ε))≥log⁡∥Y∥L(H^{(\varepsilon)})\ge\log\|Y\|, comparison with that explicit competitor proves log⁡Z(H(ε))≤1+Am(B,D,ε),0≤min⁡Jε−log⁡∥Y∥≤ε[1+Am(B,D,ε)].(7) \boxed{\quad \log Z(H^{(\varepsilon)})\le1+A_m(B,D,\varepsilon), \qquad 0\le\min J_\varepsilon-\log\|Y\| \le\varepsilon[1+A_m(B,D,\varepsilon)]. \quad} \qquad\text{(7)} In particular this is a bound for the actual penalty, rather than an assumption that it disappears. Every optimizing eigenvalue satisfies e−1−Am(B,D,ε)≤λ(He(ε))≤e1+Am(B,D,ε).(8) e^{-1-A_m(B,D,\varepsilon)}\le \lambda(H_e^{(\varepsilon)}) \le e^{1+A_m(B,D,\varepsilon)}. \qquad\text{(8)} Indeed each eigenvalue and its reciprocal are at most ZZ. The bounds use no nonvanishing of a selected scalar coefficient.

3. Coarse uniform bounds for the actual input matrices

For completeness the input growth needed below follows directly from the original finite matrices. Let q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, Q=q2Q=q^2, θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N, and sk=∏r=1k2sin⁡(rθ),0≤k<N. s_k=\prod_{r=1}^k2\sin(r\theta),\qquad 0\le k<N. The factorization of (zN−1)/(z−1)(z^N-1)/(z-1) at z=1z=1 gives sN−1=Ns_{N-1}=N. Reflection of the sine factors then gives sksN−1−k=N,1sk≤2N−1−kN.(9) s_ks_{N-1-k}=N,\qquad \frac1{s_k}\le\frac{2^{N-1-k}}N. \qquad\text{(9)} No asymptotic factorial estimate is needed.

At main color α=j−1\alpha=j-1, 1≤j≤N1\le j\le N, the original unrolled module in the ADO pointing note has E|a⟩=[a]q[α−a+1]q|a−1⟩,F|a⟩=|a+1⟩,[r]q=sin⁡(rθ)sin⁡θ. E|a\rangle=[a]_q[\alpha-a+1]_q|a-1\rangle, \qquad F|a\rangle=|a+1\rangle, \qquad [r]_q=\frac{\sin(r\theta)}{\sin\theta}. Thus ∥Ek∥≤(sin⁡θ)−2k\|E^k\|\le(\sin\theta)^{-2k}, and ∥Fk∥≤1\|F^k\|\le1. The finite universal crossing in the original crossing definition, equation (2.1), is a unitary Cartan factor followed by the nilpotent sum whose coefficient magnitudes are |q−q−1|k/[k]q!|q-q^{-1}|^k/[k]_q!. Consequently its kk-th summand has operator norm at most (2sin⁡θ)k[k]q!(sin⁡θ)−2k=4ksk≤2N−1+kN≤4N−1N.(10) \frac{(2\sin\theta)^k}{[k]_q!}(\sin\theta)^{-2k} =\frac{4^k}{s_k} \le\frac{2^{N-1+k}}N \le\frac{4^{N-1}}N. \qquad\text{(10)} This estimate is uniform in the entire main-color orbit, including the reducible colors.

The inverse nilpotent sum has coefficients (−1)kq−k(k−1)/2(q−q−1)k/[k]q!(-1)^kq^{-k(k-1)/2}(q-q^{-1})^k/[k]_q!, of the same magnitudes. Multiplication with the positive sum gives coefficient zero in degree 1≤n<N1\le n<N by the finite binomial identity ∑k=0n(−1)kQk(k−1)/2[nk]Q=(1;Q)n=0\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^kQ^{k(k-1)/2}{n\brack k}_Q=(1;Q)_n=0. Degree NN and above vanish by nilpotence. The inverse Cartan factor is unitary too. Each input pair has at most NN possible transfer indices. Thus the physical N2N^2-dimensional crossing has at most N3N^3 nonzero entries, each bounded by the last expression in (10). Both signed crossings consequently obey ∥R±∥HS≤N4N−1.(11) \|R_\pm\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}\le\sqrt N\,4^{N-1}. \qquad\text{(11)} Checked permutations, original pivots, and the root-of-unity zero-framing scalars have unit modulus and add no exponential norm.

For the all-main-color compiler of a fixed braid on bb strands, tensoring with spectator identities and taking the direct sum over the NN main colors gives crossing leaf norm at most Nb/24N−1N^{b/2}4^{N-1}. Its componentwise matrix multiplication tensor has N3b+1N^{3b+1} unit entries and norm N(3b+1)/2N^{(3b+1)/2}. Its original root readout has N⋅Nb−1N\cdot N^{b-1} unit-modulus entries and norm Nb/2N^{b/2}. Therefore, for that fixed compiler, log⁡BN=O(N),log⁡DN=O(log⁡N).(12) \log B_N=O(N),\qquad \log D_N=O(\log N). \qquad\text{(12)} Constants may depend on the fixed word and width. Fixed signed powers or fixed short pieces may be contracted first; submultiplicativity gives the same type of bound. No estimate uniform in an unbounded word length is asserted.

4. Spectral compactness and the full variational limit

Take the actual regularized minimizer with εN=1/(m2N2)\varepsilon_N=1/(m^2N^2). For a fixed completed knot assignment, the number mm is fixed. Equations (7)–(12) yield log⁡Z(HN)=O(N),supe∥N−1log⁡He,N∥op=O(1),εNlog⁡Z(HN)=O(N−1).(13) \log Z(H_N)=O(N),\qquad \sup_e\|N^{-1}\log H_{e,N}\|_{\mathrm{ op}}=O(1), \qquad \varepsilon_N\log Z(H_N)=O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(13)} Thus not only the unpenalized objective but also the entire penalized minimum recovers the actual energy at that precision: 0≤minHJN,εN(H)−12log⁡EN=O(N−1).(14) 0\le\min_H J_{N,\varepsilon_N}(H) -\tfrac12\log E_N=O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(14)} For any prescribed positive VV, this gives the equivalent target limN2πNlog⁡|KN|=V⇔limN2πNminHJN,εN(H)=V.(15) \lim_N\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N|=V \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lim_N\frac{2\pi}{N}\min_HJ_{N,\varepsilon_N}(H)=V. \qquad\text{(15)} The ordinary-color remainder remains the one in the direct source. No eventual nonvanishing of KNK_N is a premise.

The bounded spectra in (13) do not provide compactness of the eigenvectors in a specified phase-space representation, convergence of matrix symbols, or the Casson–Rivin functional as a limiting objective. They also do not permit an unsupported interchange of an infimum and a limit. Those are the next substantive requirements for the geometric comparison in (15).

For a fixed short partial assignment, all statements apply to each completed remaining integer assignment which gives a one-component knot. The anchor is not asserted for an unfilled auxiliary link. The algebraic and flow constructions now have controlled metric spectra on every such completed fixed source. A uniform description of their classical limits is still required to prove the requested finite-exception theorem.

5. Main-color preserving gauges suffice at the leading scale

One exact restriction of the metric class is available. Suppose a tree is a direct sum of rr sectors: every local tensor has blocks Tv,jT_{v,j}, and a contraction can pair only equal sector labels. The root output is the orthogonal direct sum of vectors YjY_j. Restrict the edge gauges to preserve the sectors, and denote the resulting product-norm infimum by 𝒞block\mathcal C_{\mathrm{ block}}. Then 𝒞block=(∑j=1r∥Yj∥2/m)m/2.(16) \boxed{\quad \mathcal C_{\mathrm{ block}} =\left(\sum_{j=1}^r\|Y_j\|^{2/m}\right)^{m/2}. \quad} \qquad\text{(16)}

Here is a proof including sectors with zero output. At any finite choice of sector gauges, let av,j≥0a_{v,j}\ge0 be the local block norms. Tree Cauchy in each sector and generalized Hölder give ∏v(∑jav,j2)1/2≥(∑j(∏vav,j)2/m)m/2≥(∑j∥Yj∥2/m)m/2. \prod_v\left(\sum_j a_{v,j}^2\right)^{1/2} \ge\left(\sum_j\left(\prod_va_{v,j}\right)^{2/m}\right)^{m/2} \ge\left(\sum_j\|Y_j\|^{2/m}\right)^{m/2}. Conversely, first consider sectors with all local blocks nonzero. The full tree capacity theorem, applied separately in each such sector, gives gauges with products pj=∏vav,jp_j=\prod_va_{v,j} approaching ∥Yj∥\|Y_j\|, including zero. Positive scalar edge gauges can make every vertex norm in sector jj equal pj1/mp_j^{1/m}: the required logarithmic vertex rescalings sum to zero, and the incidence map of a tree maps onto the zero-sum vertex vectors. To verify that last statement, assign the required scalar at a leaf to its incident edge, remove the leaf, and repeat. These scalar gauges change neither the sector output nor its product of norms. The resulting whole product is (∑jpj2/m)m/2(\sum_j p_j^{2/m})^{m/2}. If a sector has a zero local block, its output is zero. Scalar edge gauges can make all its nonzero vertex norms tend to zero too: prescribe their target logarithmic rescalings, let one zero vertex absorb the compensating sum, and again use the incidence map. The zero block remains zero. Thus such sectors contribute nothing in the limit. This proves (16) in every case without division by a zero output.

If 𝒞full=∥⨁jYj∥\mathcal C_{\mathrm{ full}}=\|\bigoplus_jY_j\|, the elementary finite-dimensional norm inequalities give 𝒞full≤𝒞block≤r(m−1)/2𝒞full.(17) \mathcal C_{\mathrm{ full}}\le\mathcal C_{\mathrm{ block}} \le r^{(m-1)/2}\mathcal C_{\mathrm{ full}}. \qquad\text{(17)} For the actual all-main-color tree, r=Nr=N and Yj=cjY_j=c_j. For fixed mm, restricting the infimum to main-color preserving gauges therefore changes its logarithm by at most (m−1)log⁡N/2(m-1)\log N/2, which has zero volume rate.

This is a statement about infima. It does not assert that the canonical regularized full-matrix minimizer is block diagonal, or that pinching an arbitrary individual gauge improves the objective. The exceptional complete color is still retained as its own sector. A continuous symbol in the main-color parameter cannot erase it: the adjacent ordinary colors and the complete color have different source behavior even when their limiting meridians coincide.
