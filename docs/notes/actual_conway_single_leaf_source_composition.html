Exact source composition with single-leaf Conway attachments

31 October 2026. original-source theorem. For the completed grammar E::=hh∣T(E)∣EE∣hE∣EhE::=hh\mid T(E)\mid EE\mid hE\mid Eh and cylindrical closures C(FG)C(FG) with both root children completed, the exact source has a strict complete optimizer, a primitive wire lattice, the retained finite complex packet, and whole-source alias and physical-color gaps at both parities. No additional individual-leaf condition is required. All physical colors, original support constraints, Hermite normal data, phases and normalization are retained.

The proof below includes the exact finite support compiler and the single-leaf alias induction. Its analytic companion is the single-leaf alias composition theorem; the geometric companion retains the actual metric and primitive marked interfaces. The assembled long-filling theorem states their consequence for the original invariant. Root-leaf closures with a quarter-index turning point remain a separate regime. These are literal four-port ribbon sources, not formal rational fractions or replacement coupons.

1. The literal compiler and an exact all-level support recursion

Let h be the original turned complete-circle coupon, T the actual physical quarter-turn, and FG the original stack. Every copy is an independent box; its colors, signs, auxiliary circle and original mark are retained. For any finite expression in h,T,product the source is evaluated in the FULL algebra HN=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2)H_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2), d=N−1d=N-1 and M=⌊d/2⌋M=\lfloor d/2\rfloor. Write B̂r\widehat B_r for the actual normalized Newton basis and sr=∏t=1r2sin⁡(πt/N)s_r=\prod_{t=1}^r2\sin(\pi t/N), with s0=1s_0=1. The exact rules are

TB̂r=B̂d−r,B̂iB̂j=∑r=max⁡(i,j)i+jμi,j,rB̂r,μi,j,r=si+jsrsisjsr−isr−jsi+j−r>0,i+j≤d. \begin{aligned} T\widehat B_r&=\widehat B_{d-r},\\ \widehat B_i\widehat B_j &=\sum_{r=\max(i,j)}^{i+j}\mu_{i,j,r}\widehat B_r,\\ \mu_{i,j,r}&=\frac{s_{i+j}s_r} {s_i s_j s_{r-i}s_{r-j}s_{i+j-r}}>0,\qquad i+j\le d. \end{aligned}\tag{1}

and the product is zero if i+j>=N. This is polynomial divisibility with multiplicities: repeated-root normals are not removed. The complete h has strictly positive coefficients exactly on [0,M].

For every positive pure compiled box, its nonempty finite Newton support is an INTEGER INTERVAL. If I_F=[l_F,u_F], the exact support compiler is

Ih=[0,M],ITF=[d−uF,d−lF],IFG={⌀,lF+lG>d,[max⁡(lF,lG),min⁡(d,uF+uG)],lF+lG≤d. \begin{aligned} I_h&=[0,M],& I_{TF}&=[d-u_F,d-l_F],\\ I_{FG}&=\begin{cases} \varnothing,&l_F+l_G>d,\\ [\max(l_F,l_G),\min(d,u_F+u_G)],&l_F+l_G\le d. \end{cases} \end{aligned}\tag{2}

An empty support propagates through both operations. Every index displayed in (2) has strictly positive coefficient. To verify the product assertion, let max(l_F,l_G)<=r<=min(d,u_F+u_G). Choose i,j in their original intervals with i,j<=r and i+j=max(r,l_F+l_G). Such integers exist because min(u_F,r)+min(u_G,r)>=r, while both upper bounds exceed their respective lower bounds. The chosen sum is<=d and r lies in [max(i,j),i+j], so (1) supplies a positive contribution. The reverse inclusion is immediate. This proves (2) at every level, without a full-support hypothesis or parity approximation.

The actual closing scalar C_N(F)=phi(F) selects coordinate d; the cap scalar D_N(F)=phi(TF) selects coordinate zero. Hence exact closed support is read directly from (2), or for a cylindrical final product from

CN(FG)=∑r=0dfrgd−r. C_N(FG)=\sum_{r=0}^d f_r g_{d-r}.\tag{3}

All factors here have the existing physical normalization. For changed leaves T(R^epsilon L_j), use the same finite contraction (1),(3) with their actual signed core/alias arrays. Positivity and the interval conclusion are claimed only for the complete pure source; the compiler itself is valid without positivity.

2. An exact strict-state classification, separate from finite support

A strictly realized open output b means all underlying leaf labels lie in (0,1/2), and every product satisfies

max⁡(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \max(x,y)<z<x+y<1.\tag{4}

Turns impose y=1-x. For arbitrary finite h/T/product syntax, the set of strictly realized outputs is one of FOUR possibilities:

L=(0,1/2),H=(1/2,1),A=(0,1),⌀. L=(0,1/2),\qquad H=(1/2,1),\qquad A=(0,1),\qquad \varnothing. \tag{5}

Here h has type L; T exchanges L,H, fixes A, and preserves emptiness. The product table is symmetric:

LL↦A,LA↦A,AA↦A,HL↦H,HA↦H,HH↦⌀. \begin{aligned} LL&\longmapsto A,& LA&\longmapsto A,& AA&\longmapsto A,\\ HL&\longmapsto H,& HA&\longmapsto H,& HH&\longmapsto\varnothing. \end{aligned}\tag{6}

If either child has empty strict-state set, so does its parent. This table is exhaustive, not a necessary-only filter. For LL at any b in (0,1), choose both inputs a with b/2<a<min(b,1/2). For LA, choose a sufficiently small L input c and A input b-c/2. The AA case follows by using two inputs in L. For HL or HA, necessarily b>1/2; choose the H input b-c/2 and the other input c>0 sufficiently small. HH violates x+y<1. These constructions also prove every asserted interval is attained.

For a cylindrical final product C(FG), delete only its forced last gate. A strict reduced state exists exactly when

SF∩(1−SG)≠⌀. S_F\cap(1-S_G)\ne\varnothing.\tag{7}

where S is the strict output set. Thus LL and HH fail, while LA, HA, AA, and LH pass. Neither this classification nor an empty strict-state type asserts that the finite quantum source vanishes. For example T(h)T(h) has zero source at even levels, but at odd levels a nonzero boundary-only product supported at d; this follows directly from (2).

The completed grammar used here is

E::=hh∣T(E)∣EE∣hE∣Eh. E\ ::=\ hh\ \mid\ T(E)\ \mid\ EE\ \mid\ hE\ \mid\ Eh. \tag{8}

It may equivalently include any h^s, s>=2, as initial blocks. Every E in (8) has type A and admits a strict state at EVERY output b in (0,1). Consequently every fixed C(FG), F,G in (8), has a strict reduced state: choose both closing outputs1/2. A cylindrical closure with one bare leaf and one E also has strict states, but has a distinct packet issue in §5.

3. Primitive lattice, unique strict optimizer, and complete source

No old all-output finite-support statement is imported. Contract every turn chain by its exact relation r->d-r. At a cylindrical final product, eliminate one root input using i+j=d and delete that gate, whose coefficient is exactly one. The root subtrees are disjoint. Hence no equation2r=d is created. The remaining unturned wire outputs are free integer coordinates; all other indices are integer affine functions with coefficients+1 or-1.

If there are H original h leaves, there are H-1 binary products. Removing the last one leaves H-2 independent product outputs, H leaf outputs, and one closing relation. Thus the free dimension is

K=2H−3. K=2H-3.\tag{9}

Round d times a strict reduced state in these primitive coordinates and restore eliminated indices exactly. Errors stay bounded for a fixed tree, whereas each strict inequality has a fixed macroscopic slack. All original leaf and gate support constraints hold for every sufficiently large N, at BOTH parities. In particular all leaf indices lie strictly below M; the even A2 missing top coordinate is irrelevant to this interior argument. For an arbitrary expression, a prescribed open output must have r_N/d->b in its ACTUAL strict-output interval S_E. For the completed grammar (8), S_E=(0,1), so every interior b is allowed. Fix that output first and round the remaining coordinates in a strict state at r_N/d.

The original leaf/gate factorial bounds are uniform on the closed support and have O(log N) errors for a fixed tree. The usual leaf atoms (x,1/2-x,1/2), twice, and three gate atoms are unchanged. Any nonforced boundary maximum has a nonvertex-flat atom, possibly reached by descending through endpoint wires and turns. The segment to the constructed strict state has positive infinite entropy derivative there; no other atom has negative infinite derivative. Thus the reduced maximum is strict. The retained angle maps recover all free wires, so strict tetrahedral concavity gives a negative definite reduced Hessian and a unique optimizer. This is the original barrier/injectivity proof with the new strict-state construction.

Polynomially many states give the upper limit. Primitive rounding of the strict maximizer gives a genuine positive single-state lower limit at every sufficiently large N. The original complete source therefore has its full positive exponential rate, and fixed-output profiles are analytic:

limN→∞1Nlog⁡ZN0=max⁡𝒜,fE(b)=maxoutput b𝒜E. \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log Z_N^0=\max\mathcal A, \qquad f_E(b)=\max_{\text{output }b}\mathcal A_E.\tag{10}

This theorem is about the original positive source, not yet its mixed whole-source replacement or a new topological volume identification.

4. Normal forms and actual topological operations

There are three distinct levels of simplification.

  1. Literal stack associativity merely changes the heights and parentheses of fixed ordered boxes and is a boundary-preserving tangle isotopy. It retains every coupon, turn and frame. Flatten consecutive product vertices into an ORDERED list; do not move any factor through an intervening turn. This gives a useful actual compiler normal form with alternating product blocks and turned subexpressions.

  2. In the full source algebra multiplication is commutative and T^2=1. One may therefore additionally reorder a product block or cancel adjacent turn operators when computing the scalar, with all mark conventions still recorded. These source equalities do NOT prove relative-boundary tangle isotopies for arbitrary boxes. In particular T is not multiplicative, and there is no valid rule T(FG)=(TF)(TG). The exact Frobenius identity phi((TF)G)=phi(F(TG)) follows by coefficient reversal, but it too must not be promoted to an unproved physical tangle move.

For the open positive expression grammar, flattening product blocks, using source-only commutative reordering, and removing adjacent algebraic turns gives a syntactic criterion for the OLD A_s>=2 grammar: every leaf belongs to an unturned product block, and in each maximal product block the number of direct bare h factors is either zero or at least two; each turned child must recursively satisfy the same condition. In particular a lone h or T(h) is not an old completed expression. The new grammar (8) additionally permits one bare h beside a completed factor. Thus h T(hh), and chains built by E_(n+1)=h T(E_n) starting at hh, are new presentations under this restricted normalization. This is NOT a proof of a new knot isotopy class or of inequivalence under all possible source identities. Some closed instances regroup into old grammar: for example C((h T(A2))A2)=C(A3 T(A2)) as a source scalar. Keep the actual ordered diagram when drawing any geometric claim.

  1. The existing boundary cap reduction has a separate topology proof. With a closing flag C or D, a turn switches the flag; a D-product splits into its two D-children because

DN(FG)=DN(F)DN(G). D_N(FG)=D_N(F)D_N(G).\tag{11}

The physical empty collar exhibits the corresponding TWO-punctured sphere and componentwise connected sum, with the upper/lower cap components and mark transported. A C-product is retained, not factored. This gives an exact source and topological boundary reduction for arbitrary boxes, but is not a JSJ decomposition or an isotopy reducing every new tree to old robust ones. The entire rational angle polytope also splits at a D-product.

5. The precise high-index and packet interface

The original stationary port variables are B=cot(pi b), X2+Y2-B^2=1, X,Y>0. A leaf has (B,X,Y)=(X,X,1). Every completed E in (8) satisfies -Y<B<X at each interior optimized output. The A2A_2 base and ordinary completed product/turn steps are proved in the existing port theorem and the two-leaf base extension. For a single leaf attachment to a completed child, stationarity gives common X and, writing B for the child cotangent,

Bparent=X−1X+B,Yparent=YchildX+B,Ychild=1+B2−X2,X+B>0. \begin{aligned} B_{\mathrm{parent}}&=X-\frac1{X+B},\\ Y_{\mathrm{parent}}&=\frac{Y_{\mathrm{child}}}{X+B},\\ Y_{\mathrm{child}}&=\sqrt{1+B^2-X^2},\qquad X+B>0. \end{aligned}\tag{12}

This preserves -Y_parent<B_parent<X. If X>=1 this is immediate from B>0 and X^2-1>=0. If X<1, put h0=sqrt(1-X^2), alpha0=arccos X. The leaf has angle alpha0 in B=h0 cot(alpha); the completed child has beta>alpha0, and X+B>0 says alpha0+beta<pi. Their sum gives the strict parent inequality.

At completed/completed closure C(FG), the original matching of ports gives X,Y<1 at both root children. At any product with X<1, every child angle is at least alpha0, with equality only for a leaf; a completed child’s angle is strictly greater. The bound Y_parent<1 means alpha+beta<pi-alpha0, so each completed child also has Y<1. Turns exchange X,Y. Hence all completed ports in the closed grammar have X,Y<1, and every leaf has X<1, i.e. its Newton coordinate is strictly greater than1/4.

For a direct single attachment, Y_parent<1 and B<X sharpen this to

12<X<1,12X−X<B<X. \frac12<X<1,\qquad \frac1{2X}-X<B<X.\tag{13}

Indeed Y_child<X+B squares to1<2X(X+B), and B<X then forces X>1/2. The same lower X bound for the leaves in an A2 base follows from its Y=1/(2X)<1. Thus every leaf of a fixed completed/completed tree has a positive margin inside (1/4,atan(2)/pi). No uniform margin over all trees is asserted.

This supplies the exact LOCAL changed-source packet assumptions: fixed strict high-index window, the original relative exponential interpolation, primitive dimension (9), and all original affine shifts. Each leaf contributes N^(1/2), each of H-2 retained gates N^(-3/2), giving summand N^(3-H). Its Gaussian lattice factor N^(K/2) produces N^(3/2). The changed leaf and gate derivatives in the original integer-offset amplitude remain mandatory. At completeness the multiplier is nonzero, and the separately even color Hessian retains diagonal entries

−2πXl1−ϵliXl. -\frac{2\pi X_l}{1-\epsilon_l iX_l}.\tag{14}

with negative real part. These are local finite packet facts; the full mixed source still needs §6.

Root-leaf closures require an explicit exclusion from this automatic interface. At C(hE), the completed root may have Y=1, and a turn can pass that equality to X=1 deeper inside. In the exact example C(h T(A_s)), A_s at X=1 has B_s=Y_s=1/s. The turn gives (-1/s,1/s,1), which matches the closing leaf with X=1/s. Thus all s inner leaf indices are EXACTLY1/4, while the remaining leaf is arccot(1/s)/pi. Strict complete states exist, but the inherited strict-quarter packet cannot simply be quoted. The even simpler C(h T(h)) has exact scalar sum_r a_r^2 and a unique complete leaf coordinate1/4. These are source/packet limitations, not asserted topology or hyperbolicity obstructions. The isolated C(A3)C(A_3) theorem is separately proved and does not justify every root-leaf closure.

6. Exact reduction of the mixed gap to one new local dual inequality

Fix the actual closed complete optimizer of C(FG) in (8). For each completed subtree E let b_E be its optimized output and lambda_E=f’_E(b_E). The useful inductive statement is, uniformly in every physical color and finite output r and for EACH specified alias subset S_E,

|[B̂r]ESE|≤exp⁡{N[fE(bE)+λE(rN−bE)−δSE]+o(N)}. \left|[\widehat B_r]E_{S_E}\right| \le\exp\!\left\{N\left[f_E(b_E) +\lambda_E\left(\frac rN-b_E\right)-\delta_{S_E}\right]+o(N)\right\}. \tag{15}

where delta_empty=0 and delta_nonempty>0. No coefficient lower bound or sharpness of this envelope is asserted. A2 supplies this statement through the uniform block lemma. At a turn, exact coordinate reversal retains it with lambda sign reversed and an O(1) affine exponent cost. At a product of completed subtrees, their tangents and the concavity of the actual gate m give the parent’s tangent by stationary cancellation; child gaps add. Every state retains the original i+j<=d constraint.

The only new step is hE. At its actual common-X state let

a=arccot⁡Xπ,y=arccot⁡Bπ,z=arccot⁡(X−1/(X+B))π,λc=2log⁡(X/Ychild),λp=λc+2log⁡(X+B). \begin{aligned} a&=\frac{\operatorname{arccot}X}{\pi},\qquad y=\frac{\operatorname{arccot}B}{\pi},\\ z&=\frac{\operatorname{arccot}(X-1/(X+B))}{\pi},\\ \lambda_c&=2\log(X/Y_{\mathrm{child}}),\\ \lambda_p&=\lambda_c+2\log(X+B). \end{aligned}\tag{16}

All arccot values lie in(0,1) after division by pi. The parameter region is exactly (13). The local inequality needed for this induction, proved in §7, is the strictly positive margin

Δ(X,B)=g(a)+λcy+m(a,y,z)−λpz−maxD{k(u)+λcv+m(u,v,w)−λpw}>0,D={(u,v,w):u,v≥0,max⁡(u,v)≤w≤u+v≤1}. \begin{aligned} \Delta(X,B)&=g(a)+\lambda_cy+m(a,y,z)-\lambda_pz\\ &\quad-\max_D\{k(u)+\lambda_cv+m(u,v,w)-\lambda_pw\}>0,\\ D&=\{(u,v,w):u,v\ge0,\ \max(u,v)\le w\le u+v\le1\}. \end{aligned}\tag{17}

The actual alias index u is NOT restricted below1/2. The child output v also retains its whole original interval. The finite kernel and all normal directions have already been accounted for in (1).

If the new leaf is core, replace k(u) by gtilde(u), u<=1/2. Concavity and stationarity give equality of that maximum with the pure reference in (17). Thus a contaminated child transfers its existing gap. If the new leaf is alias, (17) supplies a gap whether the child is clean or already contaminated; in the latter case the child gap adds. This proves (15) inductively once (17) is proved at the finitely many actual attachment states. The fixed tree needs only positive margins there, not a universal lower bound throughout the open parameter region.

Finally the two root tangents cancel on the literal i+j=d. The N versus d shift gives only a fixed O(1) exponent. Summing polynomially many original states and finitely many sectors produces a whole-source alias gap. The all-core concave majorant has its unique strict maximum at the actual pure optimizer; compact outside-window and actual local far-color losses then give the whole physical-color complement gap. This is the same finite source-to-packet interface as the block proof, with (17) as its ONLY new analytic alias obligation. Section7 supplies it. The actual geometric and marked filling interfaces remain separately sourced; the finite contraction alone is not a new topological volume proof.

7. The exact square certificate discharges the remaining inequality

Write mu=lambda_c and lambda=lambda_p. Define the two exact variational quantities on the original product tetrahedron D from (17):

P(μ,λ)=maxD,u≤1/2{g(u)+m(u,v,w)+μv−λw},Q(μ,λ)=maxD{k(u)+m(u,v,w)+μv−λw}. \begin{aligned} P(\mu,\lambda)&=\max_{D,\,u\le1/2} \{g(u)+m(u,v,w)+\mu v-\lambda w\},\\ Q(\mu,\lambda)&=\max_D \{k(u)+m(u,v,w)+\mu v-\lambda w\}. \end{aligned}\tag{18}

The pure objective is concave. At the actual point(a,y,z), its three derivatives vanish by the original stationary equations, so its maximum is exactly the pure reference in (17). The same is true with gtilde in place of g: near a>1/4 they agree, and both resulting objectives are concave. Hence P-Q=Delta(X,B) for the actual slopes.

For 1/2<X<=2/3 and L=1/(2X)-X<B<X, the two slopes belong to [-2log2,0]. To check this directly, B is positive and

14X2<Ychild2<1,log⁡(X2)<μ<log⁡(4X4)≤log⁡(64/81)<0. \frac1{4X^2}<Y_{\mathrm{child}}^2<1,\qquad \log(X^2)<\mu<\log(4X^4)\le\log(64/81)<0.\tag{19}

For lambda(B)=log[X2(X+B)2/(1+B2-X2)], its derivative has the sign of 1-X^2-XB, which is positive throughout this region since 1-X2-XB>1-2X2>=1/9. At B=L and B=X its limits are log(X^2) and log(4X^4), respectively. These are both in[-log4,0]. Thus the following certificate applies to every actual low-X attachment.

8. Inputs

The original physical source/compiler and positive full-Newton multiplication supply the exact finite operations. The two-leaf source proof, §§1–2, supplies the predecessor strict-state and rounding arguments. The complete geometric proof, §§3–6, and the port inputs linked above retain the original leaf and prism data. The local changed source is the actual mixed complex packet. The exact finite support and strict-state tables are proved for arbitrary h/Th/T/product syntax. The all-output, strict-quarter and local-packet extension, and the automatic mixed gaps proved here, are restricted to (8) with completed/completed C(FG)C(FG) closure. The normal-form discussion distinguishes actual stack isotopy, algebraic scalar identities, and the separately proved cap connected-sum moves. It does not assert coverage of all algebraic tangles or knots, or exhibit a new isotopy class. The root-leaf quarter examples prevent extending the inherited packet from strict feasibility alone.

The rational certificate uses rigorous function enclosures; no continuum inequality is inferred from sampled or floating-point tests.
