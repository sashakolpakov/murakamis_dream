The actual finite-clock boundary determinant through the fold

30 October 2026. The original 222 boundary has two finite negative raw clocks, by the literal right-angled prefix sign theorem. At the ROOT this puts its actual white numerator in a positive definite determinant, including the maximal folded channel. This note proves a uniform relative perturbation formula which retains the order-one transport term, and an explicit leading logarithmic rate through the fold. The proof uses the finite determinant, matrix traces and elementary sums, not a saddle selection assumption.

These are numerator results. Scalar phases, partner products and all source normalization remain explicit. No genuine generic quantum derivative, full source cycle, or volume equality follows.

1. The literal source and its negative raw clocks

Put N>=2, d=N-1, theta=pi/N, q=exp(i theta), Q=q^2 and 0<=a<=floor(d/2). Let H_a^1(U,V) be the root white polynomial in the resonant boundary theorem. Its exact determinant, proved in the complementary boundary theorem, is

H_a^1(U,V)=V^a K_a
   det[I-(qU/2)H_--(q^-1/(2V))H_+],
K_a=[2a choose a]_Q.                                  (1)

The empty determinant for a=0 is one. The Hermitian positive definite matrices H_-,H_+ have common diagonal

d_n=1/[cos(n theta)cos((n+1)theta)],  0<=n<a,

and upper adjacent entries

sqrt(B_n)[sin(n theta) -/+ i cos(n theta)], 1<=n<a,
B_n=sin^2(n theta)/[cos^2(n theta)
                  sin((2n-1)theta)sin((2n+1)theta)].   (2)

Their positivity follows from the original exact Cholesky pivots; it holds at both level parities and through the folded endpoint.

Use the ACTUAL half-index arguments

U=q^-1 X, V=q^-1 Y, X<0, Y<0,
alpha=-X/2>0, beta=-1/(2Y)>0.

Then the matrix in (1) is EXACTLY

M=I+alpha H_-+beta H_+ > I.                           (3)

In particular its determinant is positive, while V^a and K_a retain their original phases. This statement concerns the raw clocks X,Y, not an independently rotated pair of physical heads. The original 222 geometry supplies precisely these limiting signs. The exact finite-r arguments differ from their limits, and are handled next; they are not silently set equal to their limits.

2. Uniform relative transport, with its first correction retained

Change the coefficients in (3) to alpha(1+e_-), beta(1+e_+), allowing complex e_-,e_+. Set eta=|e_-|+|e_+|<1 and

E=M^(-1/2)[alpha e_- H_-+beta e_+ H_+]M^(-1/2).

Both alpha M^(-1/2)H_- M^(-1/2) and beta M^(-1/2)H_+ M^(-1/2) are positive semidefinite, and their sum is I-M^-1<I. Hence ||E||<=eta, with no dependence on N,a, or a small eigenvalue of an individual H matrix. The marked logarithm, zero when e_-=e_+=0, therefore satisfies

Log(det(M+Delta M)/det M)=tr E+R,
|R|<=a eta^2/[2(1-eta)],
|Log(det(M+Delta M)/det M)|<=-a log(1-eta).          (4)

Indeed expand tr Log(I+E) in its absolutely convergent matrix power series, use |tr E^j|<=a eta^j, and sum the terms j>=2. This also proves nonvanishing and specifies the relative branch.

If X is changed to X(1+u) and Y to Y(1+v), the exact choices are

e_-=u, e_+=(1+v)^(-1)-1.                            (5)

Thus O(N^-1) relative clock corrections give

det(M+Delta M)/det M=exp(tr E)[1+O(N^-1)].          (6)

The trace need not tend to zero: it is an order-one source transport term and must stay. Corrections O(log N/N) have at most O(log N) logarithmic cost, with a nonzero relative factor. The estimates are uniform through the fold. The factors V^a, K_a and all other source factors remain outside this comparison and are kept EXACTLY. These are clock perturbations at one root, not the moving-Q or moving-B generic quantum derivatives.

3. The determinant’s leading rate

Suppose a/N tends to xi in [0,1/2] and alpha,beta tend to positive limits. The convergence below is uniform when alpha,beta stay in compact subsets of the positive reals. For 0<=t<1/2 write

c(t)=cos(pi t),
D(t)=1+(alpha+beta)/c(t)^2,
b(t)^2=[(alpha+beta)^2-4alpha beta c(t)^2]/[4c(t)^4],
lambda_+(t)=[D(t)
    +sqrt(1+[2(alpha+beta)+4alpha beta]/c(t)^2)]/2.

The square root here is positive real. Then

(1/N)log det M -> I(xi;alpha,beta),
I(xi;alpha,beta)=integral_0^xi log lambda_+(t) dt.    (7)

The endpoint xi=1/2 is included. The improper integral converges because log lambda_+(t)=O(1+|log(1/2-t)|). An a=0 subsequence has empty determinant and rate zero.

Here is a proof from the actual finite matrices. The diagonal of M is 1+(alpha+beta)d_n; the squared modulus of its upper entry is

B_n[(alpha+beta)^2 sin^2(n theta)
                  +(alpha-beta)^2 cos^2(n theta)].   (8)

Away from t=1/2 these converge to D(t), b(t)^2. On each fixed closed interval below 1/2, entries are uniformly bounded. The few initial n, for which the B_n continuum approximation is not uniform, do not affect the Riemann limit: after removing an arbitrarily short initial interval convergence is uniform, while all the removed entries have a common bound. Their contribution to each fixed matrix trace moment is bounded by that interval’s length times a constant depending only on the moment.

For a fixed integer j, expand tr M^j into closed nearest-neighbor walks of length j. Away from the two ends, each walk sees entries whose variation over j indices tends uniformly to zero. The constant-coefficient walk sum is the zeroth Fourier coefficient of [D(t)+2b(t)cos(phi)]^j; complex offdiagonal phases cancel because a closed walk traverses each edge equally in both directions. Endpoint walks contribute O(j/N) to the normalized trace. Consequently, for every polynomial P and xi<1/2,

(1/N)tr P(M) -> integral_0^xi (1/(2pi))
               integral_0^(2pi) P(D+2b cos phi) dphi dt. (9)

All eigenvalues of M lie in [1,C_xi] by (3) and the bounded row sums. The limiting symbol lies in the same type of interval: D-2b>=1. Approximate log on this interval by truncating

log z=log R-sum_(j>=1)(1-z/R)^j/j, R>C_xi.

The tail converges uniformly geometrically since z>=1. Its trace error divided by N is bounded by (a/N) times the uniform error. Thus (9) holds for log as well. Finally

D+2b cos phi=lambda_+ |1+(b/lambda_+)exp(i phi)|^2,

where b/lambda_+<1. Expanding the scalar logarithm in powers of exp(i phi) shows that its second factor has mean logarithm zero. This proves (7) below the fold, without invoking a limiting matrix theorem.

To include the fold, cut off the last h<=delta N+O(1) indices. Write M in these two principal blocks. Its Schur complement S on the last block satisfies I<=S<=M_tail: the lower bound follows by minimizing the quadratic form of M>=I over the first block. The upper bound is the explicit positive Schur subtraction. Hence

0<=log det M-log det M_bulk
   <=log det M_tail<=sum_tail log[1+(alpha+beta)d_n]. (10)

The last inequality follows, for example, from its Cholesky pivots being no larger than its diagonal entries. With j_n=N-2n-2>=1 at every allowed diagonal entry,

d_n<=C N^2/j_n^2.

The j_n have step two. Integral comparison for the sum of log j therefore bounds (10) by

C h[1+log(N/max(1,h))]+O(log N).

After division by N this tends to zero as delta tends to zero. The same integrable bound handles the tail of (7). This proves the endpoint, including every sequence of actual folded colors.

For varying positive parameters, (4) bounds the normalized logarithmic change by C(|Delta log alpha|+|Delta log beta|), uniformly in the channel and N on positive compact sets. Thus the fixed-parameter argument extends uniformly to compactly varying alpha,beta; the integral in (7) has the same continuity by its integrable fold bound.

4. The original scalar and large partner

Let Lambda(x)=-integral_0^x log|2sin v| dv. The exact magnitude of K_a is s_(2a)/s_a^2, where s_j=product_(r=1)^j 2sin(pi r/N). Elementary Riemann sums, splitting off the integrable endpoint logarithms, give

(1/N)log|H_a^1(q^-1X,q^-1Y)|
  -> xi log|Y|+[2Lambda(pi xi)-Lambda(2pi xi)]/pi
                  +I(xi;alpha,beta).                (11)

Equation (1), not just (11), retains its complex scalar phase. For the actual large channel m=d-a, keep the exact root identity

H_m^1(U,V)=H_a^1(U,V)
                     product_(r=a)^(m-1)(UV-Q^r).    (12)

For xi bounded away from zero and XY in a positive compact, the product in (12) is uniformly away from its factors’ zeros. Its additional logarithmic rate is

integral_xi^(1-xi) log|XY-exp(2pi i t)| dt.          (13)

The finite q^-2 in UV=Q^-1 XY and the exact endpoints stay in (12). At small colors, or under complex perturbations near any forced divisor, use that exact product, not a claimed uniform relative replacement of it. No derivative of (12) is used to assert a generic quantum normal.

This is a boundary extension of the earlier strict physical-tube theorem: the canonical raw negative clocks have limiting real total degree one, which lies on that tube’s boundary. Unlike the whole rational column, the numerator determinant has no omitted Delta_a(UV) denominator. This note estimates the numerator only.

The action in (7) has the original selected normalization. To check this directly, rotate both raw clocks to X_delta=e^(-i delta)X and Y_delta=e^(-i delta)Y, with fixed small delta>0. Their actual heads lie in the earlier strict physical tube for all sufficiently large N. The two determinant coefficients become alpha e^(-i delta) and beta e^(i delta), so (4) bounds the difference of their normalized marked logarithms from the negative-clock value by C delta, uniformly in N and through the fold. The strict-tube theorem gives the original selected action at each fixed delta. Taking N to infinity and then delta to zero identifies its boundary value with (7). The branch is transported by (4) from the positive determinant; at X=Y=-1 this is the old real center, where the determinant is positive, so the two branches agree there. Continuation through the connected negative-clock quadrant retains that equality. The exact factors V^a and K_a are still those of (1). No unrelated solution of the characteristic equation or new additive constant is being selected by the trace-walk calculation. This comparison uses the strict-tube theorem for xi>0. At xi=0, the present small-a/N trace bounds and scalar sums give rate zero; the selected boundary value there follows by continuity, without invoking the earlier fixed-positive-color packet at zero color.

It supplies one piece of the original boundary source, not its global cycle coefficient or the full complementary-color gap.
