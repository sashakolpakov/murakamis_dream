Right endpoint gaps for the full wide edge box

Research proof, 4 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

This note proves a uniform real-wire endpoint inequality for the original edge phase on |ℜt|,|ℜu|≤π/2,|ℑt|,|ℑu|≤3/10.(1) |\Re t|,|\Re u|\le\pi/2,\qquad |\Im t|,|\Im u|\le3/10. \qquad\text{(1)} It is one input to the finite-kernel contour proof, not a kernel asymptotic by itself.

1. Three fixed wire endpoints

Keep the original sine primitive and complete normalization J(z)=π2/6−Li⁡2(e2πiz)2πi+iπz(1−z)2,0<ℜz<1,b(x,t)=−J(1/2−x+t/(2π))−J(1/2−x−t/(2π))−2J(x),A(x,t,u)=b(x,t)+b(x,u),c=cos⁡t,d=cos⁡u,C=1−cd2+c+d,x*=arccos⁡C2π,x*(0,0)=1/4.(2) \begin{split} J(z)&=\frac{\pi^2/6-\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{2\pi iz})}{2\pi i} +\frac{i\pi z(1-z)}2,\qquad 0<\Re z<1,\\ b(x,t)&=-J(1/2-x+t/(2\pi))-J(1/2-x-t/(2\pi))-2J(x),\\ A(x,t,u)&=b(x,t)+b(x,u),\\ c&=\cos t,\quad d=\cos u,\quad C=\frac{1-cd}{2+c+d},\quad x_*=\frac{\arccos C}{2\pi}, \qquad x_*(0,0)=1/4 . \end{split} \qquad\text{(2)} The phase is ψ(t,u)=A(x*,t,u)\psi(t,u)=A(x_*,t,u), continued from ψ(0,0)=M∞\psi(0,0)=M_\infty. The dilogarithm in (2) is its principal branch: the exponential of the open strip avoids its cut.

By separate evenness and interchange of the parameters, it suffices to use the following three rectangles. In every row both imaginary parts range over [−3/10,3/10][-3/10,3/10]. patchℜtℜub0[0,23/20][0,23/20]3/101[11/10,7/5][0,7/5]27/1002[27/20,π/2][0,π/2]49/200(3) \begin{array}{c|c|c|c} \text{patch}&\Re t&\Re u&b\\ \hline 0 &[0,23/20]&[0,23/20]&3/10\\ 1 &[11/10,7/5]&[0,7/5]&27/100\\ 2 &[27/20,\pi/2]&[0,\pi/2]&49/200 \end{array} \qquad\text{(3)} If the larger absolute real angle is at most 23/2023/20, use patch 0. Lemma 1. On every patch in (3), ℜ{ψ(t,u)−A(b,t,u)}>110000.(4) \Re\{\psi(t,u)-A(b,t,u)\}>\frac1{10000}. \qquad\text{(4)} The bound persists, with a smaller positive gap, on a neighborhood of each closed patch.

2. Analytic branches and the harmonic reduction

The critical coordinate in (2) is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the whole box (1). Here is an elementary check of its inverse-cosine branch. Put a=1+ca=1+c, a′=1+da'=1+d. Their real parts are at least one and less than 41/2041/20, and their imaginary parts have magnitude less than 31/10031/100. The parallel-sum identity gives ℜaa′a+a′≥12,ℜaa′a+a′≤ℜ(a+a′)4+{ℑ(a−a′)}24ℜ(a+a′)<4140+96120000.(5) \Re\frac{aa'}{a+a'}\ge\frac12,\qquad \Re\frac{aa'}{a+a'} \le\frac{\Re(a+a')}4+ \frac{\{\Im(a-a')\}^2}{4\Re(a+a')} <\frac{41}{40}+\frac{961}{20000}. \qquad\text{(5)} The first inequality follows either by direct division or by completing the square. For the second use aa′/(a+a′)=(a+a′)/4−(a−a′)2/[4(a+a′)]aa'/(a+a')=(a+a')/4-(a-a')^2/[4(a+a')] and ℜ(z2/w)≥−(ℑz)2/ℜw\Re(z^2/w)\ge-(\Im z)^2/\Re w when ℜw>0\Re w>0. Since C=1−aa′/(a+a′)C=1-aa'/(a+a'), (5) places ℜC\Re C strictly between −1-1 and 11. Thus the chosen inverse cosine never meets its cuts.

3. Exact interval certificate

The checker certify_bare_kernel_wide_endpoint_gaps.py uses rational patch endpoints, outward Arb balls for π\pi, and outward complex balls for the trigonometric functions and dilogarithms. It evaluates DbD_b at each rational cell center. On the entire cell it encloses the exact derivatives (Db)t=atanh⁡(tan⁡(πb)tan⁡(t/2))−asinh⁡(tan⁡(t/2)cos⁡(u/2))π,(Db)u=(Db)t(u,t).(8) (D_b)_t= \frac{\operatorname{atanh}(\tan(\pi b)\tan(t/2)) -\operatorname{asinh}(\tan(t/2)\cos(u/2))}{\pi}, \qquad (D_b)_u=(D_b)_t(u,t). \qquad\text{(8)} There is no missing derivative of x*x_*, since Ax(x*)=0A_x(x_*)=0.

The inverse functions in (8) have their principal branches. For the first, an argument on a real cut would require ℑt=0\Im t=0; the strict factorial-wall inequality in (3) then gives |tan⁡(πb)tan⁡(t/2)|<1|\tan(\pi b)\tan(t/2)|<1. For the second, throughout (1) one has |ℜtan⁡(t/2)|≤1|\Re\tan(t/2)|\le1, |ℑtan⁡(t/2)|<3/10|\Im\tan(t/2)|<3/10, ℜcos⁡(u/2)<51/50\Re\cos(u/2)<51/50, and |ℑcos⁡(u/2)|<11/100|\Im\cos(u/2)|<11/100. Hence the imaginary part of its argument has magnitude less than 11/100+(3/10)(51/50)<111/100+(3/10)(51/50)<1. The original branches are therefore precisely the principal analytic branches. The checker additionally rejects any interval evaluation whose enclosing argument box meets an inverse-function cut.

For a cell with center s0s^0 and half-widths h1,h2h_1,h_2, the real mean-value formula gives the outward enclosure ℜDb(s)∈ℜDb(s0)+[−h1,h1]ℜ{(Db)t∂s1t}+[−h2,h2]ℜ{(Db)u∂s2u}.(9) \Re D_b(s)\in \Re D_b(s^0)+[-h_1,h_1]\, \Re\{(D_b)_t\,\partial_{s_1}t\} +[-h_2,h_2]\, \Re\{(D_b)_u\,\partial_{s_2}u\}. \qquad\text{(9)} Every accepted cell has the lower endpoint of (9) greater than 1/100001/10000, and satisfies (6). Otherwise the checker bisects its longer parameter side and repeats. All subdivisions and coverage data use exact rational numbers. A separate rational sweep verifies that the accepted rectangles cover each full unit square exactly, without gaps or overlaps.

The saved certificate records every accepted cell, its outward lower bound and strip margin, all 48 faces, precision and source hashes. Thus it certifies the full boundary faces, not a collection of samples. The harmonic argument in Section 2 then proves (4) on the entire complex parameter box. Strict compact margins prove the final neighborhood assertion. ▫\square

The lemma does not discard any finite Newton term or cotangent current. Those are separate parts of the original finite-kernel proof. Its purpose is to supply the real-wire endpoint gap that permits moving the constant cotangent integral to its saddle.
