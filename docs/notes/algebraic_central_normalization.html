Fixed algebraic central factors cannot change the volume rate

Research note, 25 October 2026. This is a normalization theorem for the A-module torsor program. It retains the central variable before root specialization and evaluates whole period germs. It does not assume that an unknown comparison of the Kashaev and geometric periods is algebraic, and does not prove the volume conjecture.

1. The specialization theorem

Fix nonzero c in C and an integer k>=1. At level N put

t_N=1/N, q_N=exp(c/N), z=M^(kN).

Let R be an algebraic function over the fixed field C(q,t,M,z), on any fixed finite algebraic cover. For each sufficiently large N, choose a branch after the displayed substitutions. Assume its whole germ at M=1 has a finite nonzero value r_N. A finite ramified germ is allowed, provided its value at M=1 is defined.

Theorem. There are constants B,C>0, independent of N and of the chosen branches, such that

C^(-1)N^(-B) <= |r_N| <= C N^B.                  (1.1)

Consequently log|r_N|=O(log N), so multiplying an already specified period by r_N does not change its exponential volume rate.

The assumption is a fixed finite algebraic presentation of the factor across levels. Being algebraic separately at every N is insufficient. The theorem also allows rational dependence on N itself, since N=1/t.

2. Proof by bounded central jets

Choose an algebraic relation, clear denominators, and write

P(q,t,M,z,Y)=sum_(i=0)^d G_i(q,t,M,z)Y^i=0,

where each G_i has finite Laurent support in M,z and rational coefficients in q,t. Introduce M=exp(y). After q=exp(ct), z=exp(ky/t), each coefficient is a finite exponential sum

G_i(t,y)=sum_(a,b) g_(i,a,b)(t) exp((a+kb/t)y).    (2.1)

The g coefficients are meromorphic germs at t=0. Its y-jets are

J_(i,j)(t)=sum_(a,b) g_(i,a,b)(t)(a+kb/t)^j,      (2.2)

also meromorphic germs. The exponent functions a+kb/t are distinct for all sufficiently small nonzero t after identical pairs are combined. A Vandermonde matrix shows that a nonzero coefficient with S monomials has some nonzero jet of order less than S.

There is no hidden identically zero relation caused by q=exp(ct): a nonzero rational function in q,t cannot vanish identically on that germ. After clearing denominators such a relation would be a polynomial exponential sum sum_j p_j(t)exp(jct)=0. On a ray with ct real and tending to positive infinity, the highest exponential dominates all lower ones, contradicting a nonzero polynomial p_j.

Let m be the smallest jet index for which some J_(i,m) is a nonzero meromorphic germ. All lower jets vanish identically. For sufficiently small nonzero t the order m is stable, because a nonzero meromorphic germ has no zeros accumulating at zero. Divide all coefficients in the relation by y^m and evaluate the whole branch at y=0. It follows that every r_N is a nonzero root of the same meromorphic polynomial

Q(t_N,Y)=sum_i J_(i,m)(t_N)Y^i.                   (2.3)

The common factorial m! is irrelevant. The polynomial Q is nonzero. Remove its power of Y so that its constant and leading coefficients are nonzero meromorphic germs. Ratios of its coefficients have at most a fixed power pole at t=0. The elementary root bound for a monic polynomial gives |r_N|<=C N^B. Apply the same bound to its reciprocal polynomial to obtain the lower bound. This proves (1.1) uniformly for every nonzero branch value.

This proof never evaluates separate vanishing numerator and denominator factors. The bounded jet m performs their common algebraic cancellation before specialization.

3. The actual ADO normalization is an example

For the literature meridian family, write f=M-M^(-1), k=2 and q_N=exp(pi*i/N). The reduced/full factor is

d_N(M)=eta_N f(M) M^N/(M^(2N)-1), |eta_N|=1.

After retaining z=M^(2N), its quotient by eta_N is the fixed algebraic function

R(M,z)=f(M)*sqrt(z)/(z-1),                         (3.1)

with the root sqrt(z)=M^N specified. Its polynomial relation is

Y^2(z-1)^2-f(M)^2 z=0.

After substitution, the lowest common y-jet is of order two and gives

4N^2 Y^2-4=0.

Thus Y=+/-1/N. The specified root germ selects +1/N and hence d_N(1)=eta_N/N, exactly as independently proved from the state definition. This is a fixed algebraic central factor despite its level-dependent powers in M.

Likewise 1+M^N=1+sqrt(z) evaluates to 2 on the specified root. It remains part of an exact comparison. The theorem says that such a factor has no exponential effect after its whole nonzero germ has been correctly specified; it does not authorize omitting it from a move identity.

4. How this fits the two torsors

The finite cyclic norm-torsor comparison permits central scalar factors. The formal A-curve connection retains the action and meromorphic connection coefficients, but actual difference periods can contain functions of the invariant

z=exp(2pi*i*Log M/h).

At h=pi*i/N this is M^(2N). The exact ADO factor illustrates this missing difference-constant datum; see the formal period construction.

If a genuine comparison between two selected quantum-period realizations has a factor from a fixed finite algebraic central cover, (1.1) controls that normalization uniformly. This strengthens the earlier bound for a fixed meromorphic germ in q alone.

The qualification matters. The levelwise unit (z/2)^N evaluates to 2^(-N) at z=1. It has no fixed finite algebraic presentation of the kind in the theorem; its exponential behavior is exactly excluded by that hypothesis. Hilbert 90 at separate levels does not exclude it.

No fixed algebraic comparison between the actual Kashaev period and a geometric quantum-period realization has been constructed here. In particular the theorem is not applied to an assumed algebraic ratio between the Kashaev invariant and the bare classical exponential of volume. The full period comparison and its geometric norm are still the outstanding theorem in the primary formulation.

experiments/audit_algebraic_central_normalization.py checks bounded central jets, canceled rational germs, the actual algebraic ADO factor, and its root selection. The growth bound itself is the proof above.
