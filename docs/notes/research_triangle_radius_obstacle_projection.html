Concave triangle radii and the overload obstacle projection

31 October 2026. The universal comparison remains open. The concavity, clipping and counterexample statements concern one fixed LEFT/CROSS/RIGHT sector and preserve its protected faces and targets. They do not establish a general filling theorem.

The finite-sector criterion explains the desired application. The fractional forest analysis records why a local completion cannot simply discard shared face loads.

1. An ordinary-radius concavity theorem

For the original triangle entropy C(α)C(\alpha), with ∑αi=1/2\sum\alpha_i=1/2, put

p=2α,K(p)=2C(p/2),H(p)=−∑ipilog⁡pi,G(p)=K(p)−H(p),F(w)=exp⁡(2Q(w))=exp⁡maxp∈Δ(K(p)+∑ipilogwi). \begin{aligned} p&=2\alpha,\qquad K(p)=2C(p/2),\qquad H(p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i,\\ G(p)&=K(p)-H(p),\\ F(w)&=\exp(2Q(w)) =\exp\max_{p\in\Delta}\left(K(p)+\sum_i p_i\log w_i\right). \end{aligned}\tag{1}

The triangle Shannon inequality gives G≥0G\ge0. Its ambient diagonal second derivatives satisfy

∂2G∂pi2=1pi−πsin⁡(πpi)<0. \frac{\partial^2G}{\partial p_i^2} =\frac1{p_i}-\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi p_i)}<0.\tag{2}

Thus GG is strictly concave and extends continuously to the closed simplex. A supporting plane at an interior point has the form G(p)≤a⋅p+bG(p)\le a\cdot p+b. The ordinary entropy variational formula gives

F(w)≤eb∑ieaiwi. F(w)\le e^b\sum_i e^{a_i}w_i.\tag{3}

For any positive w0w^0, choose the supporting point to be its optimizer in (1). Stationarity gives pi0∝wi0eaip_i^0\propto w_i^0e^{a_i}, so equality holds in (3) at w0w^0. Therefore FF is the infimum of positive linear forms. In particular it is concave in ordinary radii, homogeneous of degree one and coordinate-monotone. Equivalently,

F(w)≤F(w0)∑ipi0wiwi0. F(w)\le F(w^0)\sum_i p_i^0\frac{w_i}{w_i^0}.\tag{4}

Original zero-face continuity extends this to nonnegative coordinates, with F(0)=0F(0)=0. The absolute normalization also gives

F(w)≥∑iwi≥maxiwi, F(w)\ge\sum_iw_i\ge\max_iw_i,\tag{5}

strictly in the first inequality when at least two weights are positive. This is a stronger statement than convexity of QQ in logarithmic radii; the two statements are compatible.

2. A convergent, order-independent clipping procedure

At each surviving one-sided port let oeo_e be its overloaded ordinary middle and Ie=(x,p,y)I_e=(x,p,y) its retained triangle. Its actual weighted bound is

Ae(L)=max⁡(logLo,Q(LI)+12logLo). A_e(L)=\max\left(\log L_o,Q(L_I)+\frac12\log L_o\right). \tag{6}

A protected overloaded middle makes the original sector zero and is omitted. The input triangle has three distinct vertices and at least one ordinary vertex. The child oo is not an input vertex. The pure branch is active exactly when Lo≥F(LI)L_o\ge F(L_I).

Starting with any finite positive ordinary vector, choose a selected port and perform

Lo←min⁡{Lo,F(LI)}. L_o\longleftarrow\min\{L_o,F(L_I)\}.\tag{7}

Both protected coordinates remain exactly zero. If m,Mm,M are the minimum and maximum starting ordinary radii, every iterate lies in [m,M][m,M]: an input has an ordinary coordinate at least mm, and (5) bounds its new child below by mm. No marked-radius regularization is needed.

Let the full fixed-sector cost be

Vs(L)=∑e∈CXe(L)+∑e∉CAe(L)−∑f ordinarylog⁡Lf. V_s(L)=\sum_{e\in C}X_e(L)+\sum_{e\notin C}A_e(L) -\sum_{f\text{ ordinary}}\log L_f. \tag{8}

Every update (7) makes VsV_s nonincreasing. Along a nontrivial update the chosen row stays on its pure branch down to the tie, so its log⁡Lo\log L_o cancels the target −log⁡Lo-\log L_o. Every other local term is coordinate-monotone, including through changes of its maximizing branch. Thus decreasing LoL_o cannot increase the remaining expression. This also handles repeated overloaded children and arbitrary interactions between branches; no differentiability is required.

Visit each selected port infinitely often, in any fair asynchronous order. Every coordinate decreases to a limit L∞∈[m,M]L^\infty\in[m,M]. Continuity along the update subsequence at a fixed port gives

Lo∞≤F(LI∞)for every selected port. L_o^\infty\le F(L_I^\infty)\quad\text{for every selected port}.\tag{9}

In fact this limit is independent of the fair order. If BB is any feasible positive vector with B≤LstartB\le L^{\mathrm{start}}, monotonicity of FF preserves B≤LB\le L under every update. Hence B≤L∞B\le L^\infty. The limit is the greatest feasible vector below the starting vector, uniquely characterized this way.

The feasible domain is convex in ordinary radii, since each constraint in (9) is a hypograph of a concave function. It is also closed under coordinatewise maximum. At any coordinate that decreased, at least one constraint having it as child is tight: otherwise that coordinate could be raised slightly, improving input constraints and preserving its strict child constraints, contradicting greatestness. No uniform convergence rate is claimed.

Define the half-triangle potential

Vhalf(L)=∑e∈CXe(L)+∑e∉C(Q(LIe)+12logLoe)−∑f ordinarylog⁡Lf. V_{\mathrm{half}}(L)=\sum_{e\in C}X_e(L) +\sum_{e\notin C}\left(Q(L_{I_e})+\frac12\log L_{o_e}\right) -\sum_{f\text{ ordinary}}\log L_f.\tag{10}

At the clipped limit Vs=VhalfV_s=V_{\mathrm{half}}. Therefore the exact variational restriction is

infL>0Vs(L)=infL>0Loe≤F(LIe)∀eVhalf(L). \boxed{\quad \inf_{L>0}V_s(L) =\inf_{\substack{L>0\\L_{o_e}\le F(L_{I_e})\ \forall e}} V_{\mathrm{half}}(L). \quad}\tag{11}

The clipping procedure gives a finite positive admissible vector of no larger full cost from every starting vector. Attainment of either infimum is unnecessary.

3. Half descent fails even from a complete packing

The monotonicity in §2 applies to VsV_s, not to VhalfV_{\mathrm{half}}. An exact example uses the original reduced width-three word (1,3,2,1,3)(1,3,2,1,3), first cap mark, protected faces 0,10,1. Its actual complete packing has

(L2,L3,L4,L5,L6)=(1,φ2,φ2,φ3,1),φ=1+52. (L_2,L_3,L_4,L_5,L_6)=(1,\varphi^2,\varphi^2,\varphi^3,1), \qquad\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.

Select only port 00 LEFT. Its retained triangle is monomial, so clipping changes only c=L4c=L_4, from φ2\varphi^2 to 11. The exact derivative is

∂Vhalf∂log⁡c=−12+2πarctan⁡cc(1+φ3)+φ3+1πarctan⁡c. \frac{\partial V_{\mathrm{half}}}{\partial\log c} =-\frac12+\frac2\pi \arctan\frac{c}{\sqrt{c(1+\varphi^3)+\varphi^3}} +\frac1\pi\arctan\sqrt c.\tag{12}

At the starting packing it is 1/2−arctan⁡(φ)/π>01/2-\arctan(\varphi)/\pi>0, using φ2(1+φ3)+φ3=φ6\varphi^2(1+\varphi^3)+\varphi^3=\varphi^6. At the endpoint it is

−14+2πarctan⁡11+2φ3<0, -\frac14+\frac2\pi\arctan\frac1{\sqrt{1+2\varphi^3}}<0,\tag{13}

because 1+2φ3=5+25>(1+2)21+2\varphi^3=5+2\sqrt5>(1+\sqrt2)^2. Thus the half potential increases on a final subinterval of the clipping path, although the full sector cost continues to decrease. With one selected port there is no alternative update ordering that repairs this pointwise assertion.

4. The greatest geometric projection can end above volume

A stronger example uses the literal width-three word

(1,3,0,2,4,1,3),first cap mark, protected (0,6), (1,3,0,2,4,1,3),\qquad\text{first cap mark, protected }(0,6),

with complete curvatures

(L0,…,L8)=(0,16,9,25,1,4,0,1,9). (L_0,\ldots,L_8)=(0,16,9,25,1,4,0,1,9).\tag{14}

These are certified without a numerical packing solve. The original compiler gives seven ports, fourteen distinct triangles and twenty-one edges, each incident to two triangles. Every triangle discriminant D=∑i<jLiLjD=\sqrt{\sum_{i<j}L_iL_j} is an integer. At ordinary face ff, multiply D+iLfD+iL_f over its incident triangles, using ii for a monomial triangle. In face order 1,2,3,4,5,7,81,2,3,4,5,7,8, the products are

−6800,−994500,−24650,−1700,−490100,−100,−1170. -6800,\ -994500,\ -24650,\ -1700,\ -490100,\ -100,\ -1170.

Each angle is positive; elementary π/4\pi/4 or π/2\pi/2 bounds put each total below 2π2\pi (in fact at most 7π/47\pi/4). The negative real products therefore make every total exactly π\pi. All original ordinary angle loads equal one. The convex complete potential is stationary and hence minimizes at (14), giving its actual complete rate ΦD\Phi_D. Uniqueness modulo scale identifies the complete geometry.

Select only port 22 RIGHT. Its quartet is (0,1,6,4)(0,1,6,4), with sole ordinary retained input L4=1L_4=1. Greatest clipping changes only L1:16→1L_1:16\to1. After cancelling unchanged original factors, the endpoint half potential minus the complete rate is exactly

Δ=Q(1,0,9)+Q(1,1,9)−Q(16,0,9)−Q(16,1,9)−Q(0,16,1)+log⁡16. \begin{aligned} \Delta={}&Q(1,0,9)+Q(1,1,9)-Q(16,0,9)-Q(16,1,9)\\ &-Q(0,16,1)+\log16. \end{aligned}\tag{15}

A 192-bit Arb evaluation with 2,048 Fourier terms for each Lobachevsky function and the explicit tail 1/(2M)1/(2M) certifies

0.22616591009<Δ<0.22989612898,Δ>0.22. 0.22616591009<\Delta<0.22989612898,\qquad\Delta>0.22. \tag{16}

The finite check verifies the original chart, all seven exact angle products, the single clipping constraint and both the cancelled expression (15) and the full potential difference. Its inputs are exact integers; no optimized or rounded packing is used as proof.

There is only one constraint, so simultaneous or reordered clipping gives the same endpoint. Consequently the greatest projection of the geometric vector need not pass the desired bound. Equation (11) remains valid: a different feasible vector, potentially smaller than the greatest one or obtained by changing other faces, may still pass. This is not a fixed-sector capacity excess or an original quantum-rate counterexample.

5. The remaining global inequality

On the feasible domain,

Vhalf(L)=Vcomplete(L)−∑e selected(Q(Lx,Lo,Ly)−12logLo). V_{\mathrm{half}}(L)=V_{\mathrm{complete}}(L) -\sum_{e\text{ selected}} \left(Q(L_x,L_o,L_y)-\frac12\log L_o\right).\tag{17}

Every bracket is strictly positive: a surviving overloaded middle is ordinary, and the outer pair is MATCHING, so cannot be the two protected MAIN vertices. The deleted triangle has at least two positive coordinates. At the complete geometric vector this puts the half potential below ΦD\Phi_D. Equations (13) and (16) show why clipping alone does not preserve that certificate. The open step is to find an appropriate feasible vector whose deleted-triangle defect exceeds the complete-potential excess.

Equalizing middle radii also needs its original target correction. For one unprotected edge b=c=Bb=c=B, the old cost is the identity-smoothed remaining potential plus

Q(x,B,y)−12log⁡B≥0. Q(x,B,y)-\frac12\log B\ge0.\tag{18}

These terms add over a selected forest. A protected component cannot impose middle equality at positive ordinary radii at all: equality with its root sets those radii to zero and requires a separate target adapter. Neither (18) nor concavity of FF is an existing smoothing-volume upper bound.

The exact source and interval calculations establish (11) and refute the proposed monotonicity and greatest-projection bounds. The universal optimized fixed-sector inequality remains open.
