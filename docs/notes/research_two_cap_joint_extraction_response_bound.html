Actual two-cap extraction and an original-demand response certificate

31 October 2026. exact-source and analytical research theorem. Unpublished. The response inequality below is a sufficient certificate; its universal passage is not claimed. The source is the original simple matched sphere with the original protected MAIN pair u,v. There are two strict residual caps, all other coupons are CROSS at their actual endpoint -2, and both caps are at +2. The geometric classification has already proved that the two ordinary cap pairs are disjoint. Label child/outer pairs (A,C) and (B,D). The finite common weights satisfy L_A=L_C=a>0 and L_B=L_D=b>0. The exterior ordinary incidences are positive. This note does NOT prove that the resulting certificate always passes, and gives no varying-spectrum or filled-period theorem.

Inputs are the durable two-cap cylindrical-exterior theorem, the original one-cap negative-axis reduction and cap moment form, the actual odd CROSS determinant factorization, and the original positive determinant curvature. The two-cap geometry and the one-cap extraction retain the same source. There is no changed-diagram identification.

1. Four boundary variables and the two separate degree targets

Put d=N-1, M=floor(N/2)-1, H=M+1, and let I be the ordinary faces other than A,C,B,D. Protected variables are set to zero first. Remove the fixed common raw unit scalars and form the actual exterior polynomial

EN(L)=∏enotacapPe,CROSS(−2;L),PN(A,C,B,D)=[∏i∈ILid]EN(L). \begin{aligned} E_N(L)&=\prod_{e\ \mathrm{not\ a\ cap}}P_{e,\mathrm{CROSS}}(-2;L),\\ P_N(A,C,B,D)&=\left[\prod_{i\in I}L_i^d\right]E_N(L). \end{aligned}

There are R-2 exterior ports and R-4 extracted ordinary faces, so P_N is homogeneous of degree 2d. Its coefficients are nonnegative at both parities. The cap contractions impose MORE than this total degree:

QN(A,C,B,D)=[zd]PN(zA,zC,B,D)=∑0≤j,k≤dpN,jkAjCd−jBkDd−k. \begin{aligned} Q_N(A,C,B,D)&=[z^d]P_N(zA,zC,B,D)\\ &=\sum_{0\le j,k\le d}p_{N,jk}A^jC^{d-j}B^kD^{d-k}. \end{aligned}\tag{1}

The second pair degree is d by total homogeneity. Neither the cylinder construction nor the stationary classical pair loads makes the first projection redundant at finite N. All cap formulas below apply to Q_N, not to unprojected P_N.

With theta=pi/N and c_r=2cos((2r+1)theta), retain

Uj=(∏r=1j4sin⁡2(rθ))∏r=j+1d(2−cr),0≤j≤d. U_j=\left(\prod_{r=1}^{j}4\sin^2(r\theta)\right) \prod_{r=j+1}^{d}(2-c_r),\qquad 0\le j\le d.

After the literal reflected cap relabeling if necessary, the actual cap coefficient is (-1)^j U_j times child^(d-j) outer^j. Thus, up to ONE fixed unit scalar, the original two-cap sector coefficient is exactly

SLL=∑0≤j,k≤M(−1)j+kUjUkpN,jk. S_{LL}=\sum_{0\le j,k\le M}(-1)^{j+k}U_jU_kp_{N,jk}.\tag{2}

Every original ordinary degree d is now imposed. No independent signs are assigned to repeated face occurrences.

2. Completion has two mixed strips, not only a harmless double tail

Write S_** for the same sum over all 0<=j,k<=d, S_H* for j>=H and all k, S_*H for all j and k>=H, and S_HH for both indices >=H. Then

SLL=S**−SH*−S*H+SHH. S_{LL}=S_{**}-S_{H*}-S_{*H}+S_{HH}.\tag{3}

The exact cap moment law gives independent X,Y>0 with

𝔼Xj=𝔼Yj=UjU0(0≤j≤d),0<Uj≤N2,md=𝔼Xd=N2sin⁡2π2N≤π24. \begin{gathered} \mathbb E X^j=\mathbb E Y^j=\frac{U_j}{U_0}\quad(0\le j\le d), \qquad 0<U_j\le N^2,\\ m_d=\mathbb E X^d=N^2\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2N}\le\frac{\pi^2}{4}. \end{gathered}

Consequently

S**=U02𝔼QN(−X,1,−Y,1). S_{**}=U_0^2\mathbb E Q_N(-X,1,-Y,1).\tag{4}

This is absolutely integrable since Q_N has degree d in EACH pair. Interchanging an unprojected P_N, which can have individual degree 2d, with these moment integrals is not justified and is not done.

For v_-(t)=(-t,1), v_+(t)=(-1,t), define four actual suprema

Bϵ,η=sup0≤t,s≤1|QN(vϵ(t),vη(s))|. B_{\epsilon,\eta}=\sup_{0\le t,s\le1} |Q_N(v_\epsilon(t),v_\eta(s))|.

Homogeneity and the four regions X<=1/X>=1, Y<=1/Y>=1 give

|S**|≤U02[B−−+md(B+−+B−+)+md2B++]. |S_{**}|\le U_0^2\left[B_{--}+m_d(B_{+-}+B_{-+})+m_d^2B_{++}\right].\tag{5}

Let R(s,t) be the original exterior complete-triangle capacity with residual A,C loads s,1-s, B,D loads t,1-t, every i in I load one, and u,v load zero. Empty fibers have value minus infinity. The original complete capacity is exactly

ΦD=maxs,t∈[1/2,1][R(s,t)+2f(s−12)+2f(t−12)]. \Phi_D=\max_{s,t\in[1/2,1]} \left[R(s,t)+2f(s-\tfrac12)+2f(t-\tfrac12)\right].\tag{6}

This fills the two original cap half-triangles and leaves every other triangle and target unchanged. Therefore there is gamma_HH>0 with

maxs,t∈[1/2,1]R(s,t)≤ΦD−γHH. \max_{s,t\in[1/2,1]}R(s,t)\le\Phi_D-\gamma_{HH}.\tag{7}

For an interior coordinate its added entropy is positive. At the four remaining corners both added entropies vanish, but at least one original ordinary cap incidence is zero; the unique positive complete optimizer cannot occur there. Compactness and upper semicontinuity prove (7). The proved CLOSED local coefficient upper and polynomial shared-array count imply the corresponding uniform upper for p_(N,jk). Since U_j U_k are polynomially bounded, for all sufficiently large levels

|SHH|≤eN(ΦD−γHH/2). |S_{HH}|\le e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma_{HH}/2)}.\tag{8}

The finite thresholds satisfy H/d>=1/2 at both parities. In contrast, neither S_H* nor S_*H has this argument: its other residual coordinate may be below 1/2. Dropping those mixed strips would be an actual error. Sections 5-6 give separate original-demand bounds for them.

3. What stability survives, and the exact determinant inequality

On odd levels N=2m+1, every actual exterior CROSS coupon is

N2Dm(x,b,y)Dm(x,c,y),Dm(x,b,y)=det⁡[bI+x2H−+y2H+]. \begin{gathered} N^2D_m(x,b,y)D_m(x,c,y),\\ D_m(x,b,y)=\det\left[bI+\frac{x}{2}H_-+\frac{y}{2}H_+\right]. \end{gathered}\tag{9}

with the original positive Hermitian H_-,H_+. Products, shared-variable identifications, individual coefficient extractions and zero specializations show that P_N is real stable or zero. We do NOT conclude that Q_N is jointly real stable.

Two valid conditional statements remain. For fixed positive B,D, P_N(A,C,zB,zD) is stable in A,C,z; its coefficient [z^d] is precisely Q_N(A,C,B,D), so this binary homogeneous polynomial is stable or zero. Also every individual row

qN,j(B,D)=[AjCd−j]PN(A,C,B,D). q_{N,j}(B,D)=[A^jC^{d-j}]P_N(A,C,B,D).

is binary real stable or zero. These facts do not multiply conditional negative-axis losses, and do not identify the full joint polynomial with a product of Bernoulli factors.

Here is a different exact inequality which DOES retain the projection. For each determinant t in (9), at arbitrary positive ordinary weights, write its pencil as M_t=sum_f L_f K_tf>0 and set

Atf=Mt−1/2LfKtfMt−1/2,∑fAtf=I. A_{tf}=M_t^{-1/2}L_fK_{tf}M_t^{-1/2},\qquad\sum_fA_{tf}=I.

Protected zero terms are absent. For complex numbers |z_f|<=1, set Z_t=sum_f z_f A_tf. The unital completely positive map from diagonal scalars to sum_f z_f A_tf is contractive (equivalently insert the isometry v -> (sqrt(A_tf)v)_f), so Z_t^*Z_t<=I. Therefore

log⁡|det⁡Zt|≤−12Tr⁡(I−Zt*Zt)=−12∑f(1−|zf|2)Tr⁡Atf−12∑f<g|zf−zg|2Tr⁡(AtfAtg). \begin{aligned} \log|\det Z_t|&\le-\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(I-Z_t^*Z_t)\\ &=-\frac12\sum_f(1-|z_f|^2)\operatorname{Tr}A_{tf} -\frac12\sum_{f<g}|z_f-z_g|^2\operatorname{Tr}(A_{tf}A_{tg}). \end{aligned}\tag{10}

The inequality includes singular Z_t by continuity. All pair traces are real and nonnegative. There is no commutativity assumption.

Define the normalized original loads and conductances

rN,f=1N∑tTr⁡Atf,gN,fg=1N∑tTr⁡(AtfAtg)(f≠g),HN=Laplacian⁡(gN)=1NHess⁡log⁡Llog⁡EN(L). \begin{gathered} r_{N,f}=\frac1N\sum_t\operatorname{Tr}A_{tf},\qquad g_{N,fg}=\frac1N\sum_t\operatorname{Tr}(A_{tf}A_{tg})\quad(f\ne g),\\ H_N=\operatorname{Laplacian}(g_N) =\frac1N\operatorname{Hess}_{\log L}\log E_N(L). \end{gathered}\tag{11}

The scalar coupon factors N^2 remain in E_N and disappear only from these derivatives. The factor N, rather than d, in (11) agrees with the coefficient-rate normalization. The total loss in (10), divided by N, is

eN(z)=12∑frN,f(1−|zf|2)+12∑f<ggN,fg|zf−zg|2. e_N(z)=\frac12\sum_fr_{N,f}(1-|z_f|^2) +\frac12\sum_{f<g}g_{N,fg}|z_f-z_g|^2.\tag{12}

For each individual coordinate its quadratic coefficient is exactly -sum_t Tr(A_tf^2)/(2N)<=0. Thus, when z_f is constrained to a fixed ray and its modulus varies in [0,1], e_N is concave in that modulus. This finite identity will justify radial endpoint minimization BEFORE any Schur relaxation.

The exterior ordinary-pair graph is connected. Deleting the two cap patches removes no ordinary edge from that graph: their only ordinary edges AC and BD each have the opposite triangle still in the CROSS exterior; the two pairs are disjoint. The original ordinary graph was connected. Hence H_N has only the common scaling kernel. Its grounded principal submatrices are positive definite. The actual determinant derivative theorem gives r_N -> r and H_N -> H at fixed positive weights, where H is the original complete-triangle conductance Laplacian. No signed or projected coefficient limit is used here.

4. The four joint negative boxes have a finite response loss

Return to common cap weights A=C=a, B=D=b. Set

FN(L)=1Nlog⁡EN(L)−dN[∑i∈IlogLi+loga+logb]. F_N(L)=\frac1N\log E_N(L) -\frac dN\left[\sum_{i\in I}\log L_i+\log a+\log b\right].\tag{13}

To bound B_(epsilon,eta), the exact coefficient contour for (1) has the phases

(zA,zC)=eiθvϵ(t),(zB,zD)=vη(s),zi=eiθi(i∈I). (z_A,z_C)=e^{i\theta}v_\epsilon(t),\qquad (z_B,z_D)=v_\eta(s),\qquad z_i=e^{i\theta_i}\quad(i\in I).\tag{14}

The phase theta is the original PAIR-TOTAL projection [z^d]. Its Fourier multiplier has modulus one. It cannot be fixed independently or omitted. All measures are the original normalized torus measures. The positive prefactor after dividing by a^d b^d is exp[N F_N(L)].

For fixed theta and theta_i, separate concavity from (12) shows that the minimum over t,s occurs at their endpoints. Across all four boxes this leaves the three real vectors per pair

𝒱={(−1,1),(0,1),(−1,0)}. \mathcal V=\{(-1,1),(0,1),(-1,0)\}.

For v,w∈𝒱v,w\in\mathcal V, all other moduli are one. Thus the radial part of (12) is half the sum of r_(N,f) over zero boundary coordinates, and its remaining part is half the ordinary complex Dirichlet energy.

Let boundary order be (A,C | B,D), and take the exact Schur complement

KN=HN,bb−HN,bIHN,II−1HN,Ib. K_N=H_{N,bb}-H_{N,bI}H_{N,II}^{-1}H_{N,Ib}.

Relaxing the interior unit phases to arbitrary complex values lowers the Dirichlet energy, with minimum z_b^* K_N z_b. Write its pair blocks as K11,K12,K22. For z_b=(e^(i theta)v,w), the relative theta remains:

zb*KNzb=vTK11v+wTK22w+2cos⁡θvTK12w. z_b^*K_Nz_b=v^TK_{11}v+w^TK_{22}w +2\cos\theta\,v^TK_{12}w.

Consequently define the exact finite positive loss

δ12,N=12minv,w∈𝒱{∑f:(v,w)f=0rN,f+vTK11v+wTK22w−2|vTK12w|}. \delta_{12,N}=\frac12\min_{v,w\in\mathcal V} \left\{\sum_{f:(v,w)_f=0}r_{N,f} +v^TK_{11}v+w^TK_{22}w-2|v^TK_{12}w|\right\}.\tag{15}

There are nine expressions. Connectedness and positive boundary loads make their minimum positive: if there is a zero coordinate its radial cost is positive, while with no zero the pairwise opposite boundary values cannot be one common constant. The same argument applies to the limiting matrix, so delta_(12,N)->delta_12>0.

Equations (10)-(15) prove, uniformly over all four boxes,

Bϵ,η≤eN(FN(L)−δ12,N). B_{\epsilon,\eta}\le e^{N(F_N(L)-\delta_{12,N})}.\tag{16}

Here Q_N in the definition of B is at unweighted coordinates. Its bihomogeneity is exactly what canceled a^d b^d. The interior and pair-phase integrations introduce no exponential or polynomial loss. At the genuine common stationary weights, F_N(L)->M_2. Hence

limsupNodd1Nlog⁡|S**|≤M2−δ12. \limsup_{N\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac1N\log|S_{**}| \le M_2-\delta_{12}.\tag{17}

This is an explicit global simultaneous cancellation bound using the original pair targets. It is not a claim that its loss pays the full geometric budget.

5. Each mixed strip retains a one-pair loss and its high-degree cost

Fix j and write q_j(B,D) as in section 3. Its degree is d. Completion of only the SECOND cap gives

∑k=0d(−1)kUkpN,jk=U0𝔼qj(−Y,1). \sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^kU_kp_{N,jk}=U_0\mathbb E q_j(-Y,1).

Its two negative-axis suprema can again be bounded directly by (10), without appealing to binary roots. Use arbitrary positive weights

A=aez,C=a,B=D=b,z≥0. A=ae^z,\qquad C=a,\qquad B=D=b,\qquad z\ge0.

and retain all original I targets. Now A and C are individually extracted, to j and d-j, so their two phases are independent unit phases. Only B,D have the negative-axis boundary constraint. Let kappa_(2,N)(L)>0 be the effective conductance between B,D in H_N, eliminating ALL other ordinary vertices. The same radial endpoint and Dirichlet argument gives

δ2,N(L)=min⁡{2κ2,N,rN,B+κ2,N2,rN,D+κ2,N2}. \delta_{2,N}(L)=\min\left\{2\kappa_{2,N}, \frac{r_{N,B}+\kappa_{2,N}}2, \frac{r_{N,D}+\kappa_{2,N}}2\right\}.\tag{18}

Indeed the two-terminal Schur matrix is kappa[[1,-1],[-1,1]]; the opposite pair (-1,1) has half-energy 2kappa, and each one-zero case adds its half-load to half-energy kappa/2. These are the exact normalizations.

Use (13) with A=a exp(z) in the product E_N but keep its displayed subtractions log a+log b. Cauchy extraction of A^j C^(d-j) gives the ADDITIONAL factor exp(-zj). Both row negative-axis suprema obey

Bj,±≤eN(FN(L)−δ2,N(L))−zj. B_{j,\pm}\le e^{N(F_N(L)-\delta_{2,N}(L))-zj}.\tag{19}

This is uniform in every actual j, including zero row polynomials. The original moments and U_j<=N^2 therefore prove

|SH*|≤poly⁡(N)eN(FN(L)−δ2,N(L))−zH. |S_{H*}|\le\operatorname{poly}(N) e^{N(F_N(L)-\delta_{2,N}(L))-zH}.\tag{20}

The polynomial is uniform on fixed positive weight compacts; one may take the explicit factor (d+1)N4(1+pi2/4). No j-dependent saddle or lower coefficient asymptotic is required. Since H/N->1/2,

limsupNodd1Nlog⁡|SH*|≤F(L)−z2−δ2(L). \limsup_{N\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac1N\log|S_{H*}| \le F(L)-\frac z2-\delta_2(L).\tag{21}

The reflected statement for S_*H uses B=b exp(z), D=b, A=C=a, with delta_1 the effective A,C response. The mixed sums retain their original indices and signs throughout; taking their absolute values does not discard their other cap’s cancellation.

6. A finite classical sufficient criterion, with a local tilt improvement

The limiting positive functional in (13) is the original exterior triangle potential

F(L)=∑texteriorQt(L)−∑i∈Ilog⁡Li−log⁡a−log⁡b. F(L)=\sum_{t\ \mathrm{exterior}}Q_t(L) -\sum_{i\in I}\log L_i-\log a-\log b.\tag{22}

At the stationary weights it is M_2. In fact the same weights give R(s,t)<=F(L)=M_2 for every residual pair split: the two boundary log-weight contributions are always log a+log b. Equality holds at the actual (nu_1,nu_2) allocation. For the first mixed strip one may choose ANY fixed positive weights with A/C=exp(z)>=1 and B=D; then (21) is a valid upper bound. Thus one needs no global real saddle continuation to state or verify the following certificate.

Choose two such weight systems L(1),L(2), with respective z_1,z_2>=0, and compute their actual conductance matrices and loads. If

max⁡{M2−δ12,F(L(1))−z12−δ2(L(1)),F(L(2))−z22−δ1(L(2))}<ΦD. \max\left\{M_2-\delta_{12},\ F(L^{(1)})-\frac{z_1}{2}-\delta_2(L^{(1)}),\ F(L^{(2)})-\frac{z_2}{2}-\delta_1(L^{(2)})\right\}<\Phi_D.\tag{23}

then the original two-cap sector at the specified endpoint spectra has a strict exponential gap below Phi_D along odd levels. This follows from (3),(8),(17),(21), with only four terms. Replacing < by <= gives the corresponding weak upper bound. No sector has been moved to a different marked source.

There is a legitimate local optimization for these weights. Fix b=1, write A=a exp(z), C=a, B=D=1, and minimize (22) in log a and log L_I. At z=0 the common stationary weights solve these equations. Their Hessian is the restriction of the connected original conductance Laplacian, anchored at B,D, hence positive definite. The implicit function theorem gives an analytic local family L^(1)(z) and F_1(z), with

F1(0)=M2,F1′(0)=ν1<12. F_1(0)=M_2,\qquad F_1'(0)=\nu_1<\frac12.\tag{24}

The derivative is the original residual A load. Thus the high-degree Chernoff part F_1(z)-z/2 itself strictly improves for small z>0. The response delta_2(z) is continuous (a minimum of three smooth expressions); its change must still be retained when checking (23). The second mixed strip has the analogous family and derivative nu_2. This local construction does not assume global saddle differentiability.

The geometric theorem supplies

M2−ΦD≤W(ν1)+W(ν2)π−gD2π. M_2-\Phi_D\le\frac{W(\nu_1)+W(\nu_2)}{\pi}-\frac{g_D}{2\pi}.

One sufficient way to verify (23) is that all THREE explicit losses M_2 minus its entries exceed this available budget. No such universal comparison is claimed. In particular, a relative delta>0 alone does not give an absolute Phi_D bound.

There is a simpler, possibly weaker, test requiring no tilt. At the common stationary weights one has

δ12≥max⁡(δ1,δ2). \delta_{12}\ge\max(\delta_1,\delta_2).

For each state in (15), drop the other pair’s nonnegative radial cost and relax its boundary values as well as the interior values. The resulting two-terminal minimum is exactly one of the three expressions in (18), for the retained pair. This proves the claim for either pair, before taking the minimum over states. Consequently

min⁡(δ1,δ2)>M2−ΦD. \min(\delta_1,\delta_2)>M_2-\Phi_D.\tag{23a}

alone implies the strict odd endpoint two-cap bound. At stationarity the explicit two-terminal losses are

δi=min⁡{2κi,νi+κi2,1−νi+κi2}. \delta_i=\min\left\{2\kappa_i,\frac{\nu_i+\kappa_i}{2}, \frac{1-\nu_i+\kappa_i}{2}\right\}.

Condition (23) retains the potentially useful extra high-degree costs and does not require (23a).

7. Scope and precise remaining issue

The exact extraction, moment completion and high-high tail statement hold at both parities. The determinant response certificate is proved for ODD N, where (9) is the actual endpoint source, not a positive comparison substituted after signs. It controls actual globally extracted coefficients, including the pair-total Fourier phase.

The new finite algebraic inequality (10) and nine-state response bound do not require real stability of Q_N. Conditional binary stability is recorded only where valid. All normalizations use N in the exponential rate, d in the original finite degree targets, and actual odd matrices of size m. The source constants remain in F_N. No independent phase per occurrence, changed exterior target, omitted parity seam, or divergent Mellin integral is introduced.

What remains is quantitative: prove that (23), a stronger exact joint negative-axis criterion, or another cancellation estimate always pays the summed original geometric budget for actual cylinder exteriors. Neither the cylinder topology nor abstract stable-polynomial closure establishes that inequality here. Even levels, spectral neighborhoods, and whole filled periods are additional tasks.

8. A source-specific obstruction to restoring joint real stability

There is a stronger necessary condition than the abstract projection counterexample. Any nonzero nonnegative polynomial Q which is homogeneous of degree d in A,C and degree d in B,D, if jointly real stable, MUST factor across the two pairs:

Q(A,C,B,D)=Q1(A,C)Q2(B,D). Q(A,C,B,D)=Q_1(A,C)Q_2(B,D).\tag{25}

Here is an elementary proof, including absent endpoint coefficients. Put q(r,s)=Q(r,1,s,1) and Omega=C(-infty,0]. Every r in Omega is a ratio of two numbers in the upper half-plane, and the choices for r,s are independent. Bihomogeneity and stability therefore imply that q(r,s) never vanishes on Omega times Omega. Let l be its actual degree in r. Its leading coefficient a(s) is nonzero at every positive s, because its coefficients are nonnegative and not all zero. Near one positive s_0, a(s) stays nonzero. For EVERY complex s in this small disk, every root of the monic polynomial q(r,s)/a(s) lies on the nonpositive real axis, since no root can be in Omega. Its coefficients are consequently real. They are also holomorphic in s, so they are constant. Hence q(r,s)=a(s)p(r) there, and polynomial identity extends this to all r,s. Homogenizing to the specified degrees proves (25), with possible monomial factors retained. Both factors have their nonzero one-variable roots on the negative real axis. Conversely products of these binary stable factors are jointly stable.

The actual exterior provides an exact classical test against (25). Use relative log variables z,w and define the positive coefficient pressure of the SAME Q_N by

G(z,w)=minlog⁡a,log⁡LI{∑tQt(aez,a,ew,1,LI)−loga−∑i∈IlogLi}. G(z,w)=\min_{\log a,\log L_I} \left\{\sum_tQ_t(ae^z,a,e^w,1,L_I)-\log a-\sum_{i\in I}\log L_i\right\}.\tag{26}

The formula is needed only on one neighborhood of z=w=0. Its local minimizer is finite and analytic by the same grounded-Hessian argument as (24). The original coefficient contour extracts all I demands AND the pair-total a degree d. Its only positive angular maximum is the zero phase: A,C share the pair phase, B,D are anchored, and the connected ordinary graph propagates equality of phases. All characters are primitive incidence differences. Thus the already proved positive determinant packet argument, with this literal identified phase, gives a uniform local complex relative asymptotic and in particular

1N∂z∂wlogQN(ez,1,ew,1)|(0,0)→Gzw(0,0). \left.\frac1N\partial_z\partial_w\log Q_N(e^z,1,e^w,1)\right|_{(0,0)} \longrightarrow G_{zw}(0,0).\tag{27}

This is a positive coefficient saddle, not a signed-sector lower. The actual N^2 source factors and d targets are the same as in (13).

Let K be the four-terminal Schur matrix from section 4, at the actual stationary weights. Write its off-diagonal entries as -g_fg^eff, with g_fg^eff>=0, and put

T=gABeff+gADeff+gCBeff+gCDeff>0. T=g_{AB}^{\mathrm{eff}}+g_{AD}^{\mathrm{eff}} +g_{CB}^{\mathrm{eff}}+g_{CD}^{\mathrm{eff}}>0.

After eliminating I, the remaining extracted direction is e_A+e_C. Taking this ONE further Schur complement gives exactly

Gzw(0,0)=KAB−(KAA+KAC)(KAB+KCB)T=gADeffgBCeff−gABeffgCDeffT. \begin{aligned} G_{zw}(0,0)&=K_{AB} -\frac{(K_{AA}+K_{AC})(K_{AB}+K_{CB})}{T}\\ &=\frac{g_{AD}^{\mathrm{eff}}g_{BC}^{\mathrm{eff}} -g_{AB}^{\mathrm{eff}}g_{CD}^{\mathrm{eff}}}{T}. \end{aligned}\tag{28}

Therefore, if the explicit determinant in the numerator of (28) is nonzero for the actual finite-common-weight exterior, its actual Q_N fails joint real stability at every sufficiently large odd level. Indeed (25) would make the mixed logarithmic derivative identically zero, contradicting (27). This criterion refers to the original gluing solution and its original conductances; it is not a freely chosen polynomial counterexample.

No claim is made that (28) is always nonzero. A separating ordinary circle tangent to both protected lines can couple the boundary through a bottleneck; the cylinder theorem does not exclude that possibility. Nor does zero in (28) prove factorization. The response bound in sections 3-6 applies regardless of this diagnostic and never requires restoring joint stability.
