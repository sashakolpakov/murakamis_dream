The entire even low-demand range: a subexponential signed contraction

31 October 2026. analytical theorem. This extends the low-quarter estimate using its exact original-source recurrence. It retains the original two half-block cutoffs. The conclusion is subexponential, not a polynomial estimate, and does not cover odd levels or the remaining projected middle-demand range.

1. Statement and exact scalar reduction

Let N=2m≥4N=2m\ge4, M=m−1M=m-1, d=N−1d=N-1, and θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N. Use the original fixed-pair cap/CROSS coefficient SN,nS_{N,n}, endpoint comparator DN,nD_{N,n}, and coefficient C0,n>0C_{0,n}>0 of the Newton source theorem. In particular the original AdA^d demand, unused products, raw signs, and actual spectral pair (+2,−2)(+2,-2) have not changed.

Theorem. Uniformly over the whole original low range, limsupN→∞Neven1Nlog⁡max0≤n≤M|SN,n|DN,n≤0. \limsup_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\ {\mathrm{even}}}} \frac1N\log\max_{0\le n\le M} \frac{|S_{N,n}|}{D_{N,n}}\le0. \tag{1} Equivalently, for every ε>0\varepsilon>0, all sufficiently large even NN obey |SN,n|≤eεNDN,n|S_{N,n}|\le e^{\varepsilon N}D_{N,n} for every 0≤n≤M0\le n\le M. The threshold may depend on ε\varepsilon.

The exact source theorem gives Rn=γnSN,n4C0,n,R0=1,Rn=1−tnRn−1,tn=4sin⁡4(nθ)sin⁡2((2n−1)θ),γn=2−2cos⁡((2n+1)θ). R_n=\frac{\gamma_n S_{N,n}}{4C_{0,n}},\quad R_0=1,\quad R_n=1-t_nR_{n-1},\qquad t_n=\frac{4\sin^4(n\theta)}{\sin^2((2n-1)\theta)}, \quad\gamma_n=2-2\cos((2n+1)\theta). \tag{2} For this range the original cutoff is precisely Jn={max⁡(0,n−M),…,min⁡(M,n)}={0,…,n}J_n=\{\max(0,n-M),\ldots,\min(M,n)\}=\{0,\ldots,n\}. The auxiliary full slice therefore equals the original projected sum. No such identification is used above MM.

Write P0=1,Pj=∏r=1jtr,Aj=Pj−1.Rn=(−1)nPn∑j=0n(−1)jAj. P_0=1,\quad P_j=\prod_{r=1}^j t_r,\quad A_j=P_j^{-1}. \qquad R_n=(-1)^n P_n\sum_{j=0}^n(-1)^j A_j. \tag{3} The elementary sine products and monotonicity of trt_r give PM=N2sin⁡2(π/N)4,0<Pj≤π2/4(0≤j≤M). Thus log⁡Pj\log P_j is convex and its maximum occurs at an endpoint. This alone does not bound the signed sum in (3).

Replace the two sums in (5) by (7), with u=z/Nu=z/N, and call the resulting function 𝒜N(z)\mathcal{A}_N(z). 3. Uniform complex growth, including the endpoint layers

Put f(u)=−∫0ulog⁡tan⁡(πt)dt,0≤u≤1/2. f(u)=-\int_0^u\log\tan(\pi t)\,dt,\qquad0\le u\le1/2. Use its analytic continuation to 0<ℜu<1/2,ℑu>00<\Re u<1/2,\Im u>0, with the logarithm continued from the positive real tangent. Reflect by conjugation below the real axis. The primitive has continuous boundary values on both vertical edges of this strip.

The derivative bound (6) also shows, uniformly for complex uu in any fixed-height rectangle considered here, ℰ1/2,N(L;u)=N∫0uL+OY(N−1),ℰ1,N(L;u)=N∫0uL+12[L(u)−L(0)]+OY(N−1). \mathcal{E}_{1/2,N}(L;u)=N\int_0^uL+O_Y(N^{-1}),\qquad \mathcal{E}_{1,N}(L;u)=N\int_0^uL+\tfrac12[L(u)-L(0)] +O_Y(N^{-1}). \tag{10} For example the correction terms are bounded by constant multiples of N(2k−1)!/(2πρN)2kN(2k-1)!/(2\pi\rho N)^{2k}. Up to half the optimal truncation their successive ratios are bounded below one; the remaining terms are exponentially small. The differentiated corrections are bounded as well. No estimate uniform as Y→∞Y\to\infty is asserted.

Uniform Stirling estimates in the right half-plane, applied to the exact Gamma factors in (5), now give log⁡|𝒜N(x+iNy)|=Nℜ[2f(x/N+iy)]+OY(log⁡N),0≤x≤M,|y|≤Y. \log|\mathcal{A}_N(x+iNy)| =N\Re[2f(x/N+iy)]+O_Y(\log N), \quad0\le x\le M,\quad |y|\le Y. \tag{11} Here and below the lower half-plane uses the conjugate branch. 5. Scope

This is a cancellation estimate for the actual fixed spectral pair and every original even low-demand coefficient 0≤n≤M0\le n\le M. It is stronger than a coefficient-norm or residue-variation bound: those can be exponentially excessive in this same problem. The new argument retains the alternating sign through the contour.

The low-quarter theorem still provides its sharper polynomial factor there. The present proof does not establish a polynomial bound on the whole low range. It also does not identify the auxiliary full slice with the original projected sum when n>Mn>M, prove the analogous odd-level low bound, control nearby complex spectra, or give a whole source/filling theorem. Those are separate tasks.

The source input is the exact Newton recurrence with its original cutoff. The analytical estimates are (5)–(20). The odd extension proves the corresponding odd-source statement with its regularized positive comparator.
