Moving-period factorial normals along logarithmically escaping heads

30 October 2026. source estimate. The marked Gamma factorial remains controlled when a head has imaginary part of order N log N. This is a bound for the actual factorial factors and fixed quantum jets. It does not provide a packet for a polynomial whose critical points merge, or cross an original zero or pole divisor.

1. The precise source factors and growing charts

Use EXACTLY the interpolant Fhat_P and its logarithmic mark in the moving-period factorial theorem, with P=pi i/(pi i/N+epsilon), d=N-1 fixed. Put L_N=1+log N. Fix positive constants delta,C. The new interior arguments satisfy

delta <= Re(z/N) <= 1-delta,
|Im(z/N)| <= C L_N.                                  (1)

An exterior argument may instead have |Re(z/N)|<=C and delta<=|Im(z/N)|<=C L_N. Choose a fixed integer shift k_N=O(N) which puts its real part in a smaller interior strip, and use the original unit recurrence for that SAME function. The shift stays fixed when epsilon moves. Fixed physical integer arguments 0,…,d are also allowed, by their exact finite factorials.

For a fixed finite number of these arguments, there are constants c,C_1, independent of N and the arguments, such that on

|epsilon| <= rho_N=c/[N^2 L_N^2]                      (2)

every factor is holomorphic and nonzero, and its relative value and inverse are bounded by C_1. In particular, for every fixed j,

|partial_epsilon^j Fhat_(P(epsilon))(z)|_(epsilon=0)
  <= j! C_1 (C_1 N^2 L_N^2)^j |Fhat_N(z)|.            (3)

The same assertion holds for products and inverses of a fixed number of those factors, their normalized local square-root gauges, and retained powers zeta^E with |E|<=C N^2 L_N^2. All exponential root values and integer-linear characters remain part of the source.

2. A direct differentiated Gamma-product bound

The convergent product defining G_P gives, at FIXED z,

partial_P Log G_P(z)=-z/P
  +sum_(k>=1) { k psi(Pk+z+1)-k psi(Pk-z)
                                     -(2z+1)/P }.    (4)

It suffices first to work on |P-N|<=c/L_N. All Gamma arguments have positive real part there. Their real parts are at least c_delta N for k=1 and comparable to Nk for k>=2. Take

K=ceiling(C_2(1+|z|/N)), C_2 sufficiently large.

For k<=K, the uniform right-half-plane digamma expansion psi(w)=Log w+O(1/|w|) applies. Both arguments have size at least c_delta N. Their modulus ratio and the difference of their marked arguments are bounded uniformly: the large imaginary parts enter with opposite signs, and the real-part distance to the strip endpoints is fixed. Hence

|psi(Pk+z+1)-psi(Pk-z)|<=C_delta,

and the first K terms of (4) have total size O(K^2+K L_N)=O(L_N^2). This includes the case that z stays bounded in scaled coordinates.

For k>K, |z+1|/|Pk|<1/4. Expanding the two digammas about Pk shows that their 1/(Pk) term is (2z+1)/(Pk), while their 1/(Pk)^2 terms are EQUAL and cancel. More explicitly the latter coefficient of psi(w+a) is -a^2/2+a/2-1/12, which has the same value at a=z+1 and a=-z. The uniform next remainder gives

|k[psi(Pk+z+1)-psi(Pk-z)]-(2z+1)/P|
                  <=C(1+|z|)^3/(N^3 k^2).            (5)

One may obtain (5) directly by differentiating the convergent Gamma-ratio expansion on a fixed larger relative P disk; the same 1/k cancellation and uniform |z/(Pk)|<1/4 give its bound. Summing (5) costs O((1+|z|/N)3/K)=O(L_N2). The initial -z/P term is smaller. Therefore

|partial_P Log G_P(z)|<=C L_N^2.                      (6)

The marked elementary exponential converting G_P to Fhat_P has P derivative -pi i z(z+1)/(2P^2), also O(L_N^2). Since |P’(epsilon)|=O(N^2),

|partial_epsilon Log Fhat_P(z)|<=C N^2 L_N^2.         (7)

Disk (2) lies inside the P neighborhood just used. Integrating the actual logarithmic derivative along that disk proves the two-sided relative bound, and Cauchy’s formula gives (3). No principal logarithm is reset, and no root-only reflection is differentiated.

For completeness, the digamma estimates used here follow from the same uniform complex-sector Stirling expansion used to construct the original product. The Gamma arguments lie in the right half-plane, so its fixed sector can include angles up to pi/2. The finite sum and tail bounds above make explicit their dependence on the growing imaginary argument; a compact-set estimate is not silently reused.

3. Exterior rows, fixed endpoints and original gauges

For an exterior row the O(N) fixed unit shifts introduce factors 1-exp[2(z+j)Log zeta]. Their scaled imaginary parts remain at least delta/2 on (2). Thus

|exp[2(z+j)Log zeta]/(1-exp[2(z+j)Log zeta])|<=C_delta.

Each logarithmic epsilon derivative has size O(N L_N), so the whole shift product costs O(N^2 L_N), within (7). Large or small absolute magnitudes of individual factors are NOT bounded away: only their relative variation is being estimated. These magnitudes and all phases remain in the root value.

For a fixed physical integer n<=d, the original finite product has logarithmic derivative O(N^2 L_N), including n=d. Its known relative disk contains (2). This keeps the endpoint N-1 fixed rather than replacing it by P-1. Combining the bounds proves the statement for any fixed product, ratio and locally normalized square root. A marked monomial zeta^E has logarithmic derivative E, so it has precisely the additional cost claimed after (3).

Consequently the actual moving-factorial cup and cap factors at heads of size O(N log N) have polynomial fixed-jet cost whenever their arguments obey these hypotheses. The same is true of their specified boundary gauges. This does not give a relative estimate for a SUM of white-polynomial terms, and does not replace the actual large generic white by its root-colliding small partner.

4. What this resolves at the resonant framing boundary

For an actual clock X(r) asymptotic to c r^(2e), e=+1 or -1, on a marked ray r=N R with R in a compact set avoiding zero, its head z=Log X/(2Log q0) has |Im(z/N)|=O(log N). On a fixed logarithmic sheet with bounded real part it falls into the nonreal exterior class above for all sufficiently large N. Thus its large imaginary lift by itself is tame for the actual factorial normals. No bounded-lift hypothesis is falsely invoked.

In contrast, a clock tending to one can give a factorial argument approaching an integer zero or pole. Nor does this theorem control a white numerator’s endpoint saddle or its resonant coefficient sum. Those need their own exact small-argument products or turning analysis. The original finite support, generic normal, cotangent measure and selected global period remain unchanged and unproved by this estimate.

The focused audit and results retain 12 independent Gamma-tail comparisons, 48 two-sided relative disk checks, 12 genuine fixed-index normals, 12 exact marked unit recurrences and 12 actual opposite-head cup gauges, at 85 digits. The maximum identity error was 2.18e-75. These are diagnostics of the specified function; the uniform estimate is proved above.

The estimate concerns relative factorial/cup quantities and their genuine fixed jets. It does not bound an absolute magnitude, establish a turning packet or give a global period.
