The original inverse-period collar over a convex real-angle domain

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The compact inverse-period collar has a stronger description than its star-shaped real-period parametrization: it is the graph of the imaginary angles over a bounded convex domain of real angles. The convex function defining that domain is obtained by minimizing the original angle energy along its incidence fibers. This description retains the original signed powers, charges and complete peripheral branches. It also fixes the sign of a possible connector argument: moving vertically away from this graph increases the real action. Consequently the graph theorem does not itself complete the collar to the original quantum integration class.

1. Original coordinates and the closed fiber problem

Fix a finite connected original ordered circle tree, a nonempty selected set SS, and all edges incident to SS, counted once. Let k=|S|k=|S| and let mm be the number of included edges. Every selected vertex has positive degree. Outside angles are complete: tb=0t_b=0. Fix real powers nvn_v and positive real charges cvc_v. In an integral filling the original convention is nv=ηv−2pvn_v=\eta_v-2p_v; no carrier or slope is changed here.

Use the complete positive branches and write tv=sv+ihv,rv,e=kv,e+igv,e,Pv(t)=∑e∋vrv,e−inv2tv.(1) t_v=s_v+i h_v,\qquad r_{v,e}=k_{v,e}+i g_{v,e},\qquad P_v(t)=\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}-\frac{i n_v}{2}t_v. \qquad\text{(1)} The affine angle coordinates consist of the selected svs_v and all endpoint incidence variables gv,eg_{v,e}, with sb=0s_b=0 outside. Put ρv=∑e∋vgv,e−nv2sv=−πcv.(2) \rho_v=\sum_{e\ni v}g_{v,e}-\frac{n_v}{2}s_v=-\pi c_v. \qquad\text{(2)} The open positive polytope 𝒫c\mathcal P_c is defined by these rows and the inequalities |sv|+2|gw,e|<π,|sw|+2|gv,e|<π(e=vw).(3) |s_v|+2|g_{w,e}|<\pi,\qquad |s_w|+2|g_{v,e}|<\pi\qquad(e=vw). \qquad\text{(3)} Assume strict feasibility. Its compact closure is obtained by using weak inequalities. Indeed a segment from any weak assignment to a strict assignment is strict except at the weak endpoint. Interiors and boundaries of 𝒫c\mathcal P_c are relative to the affine space (2).

For one edge the original square coordinates are AP=sv/4−gw,e/2,AQ=sv/4+gw,e/2,yP=gv,e/2+sw/4,yQ=gv,e/2−sw/4.(4) \begin{aligned} A_P&=s_v/4-g_{w,e}/2,& A_Q&=s_v/4+g_{w,e}/2,\\ y_P&=g_{v,e}/2+s_w/4,& y_Q&=g_{v,e}/2-s_w/4. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(4)} The pinned variational theorem supplies the continuous convex energy ℰ=4∑e(EP,e+EQ,e)(5) \mathcal E=4\sum_e(E_{P,e}+E_{Q,e}) \qquad\text{(5)} on 𝒫¯c\overline{\mathcal P}_c. Its Hessian is positive definite on every nonzero affine tangent at an interior point. This follows from the positive square Hessians and the injectivity of (4). There is no outside imaginary-work term because the outside data are complete. The exact edge differential is 4d(EP,e+EQ,e)=kv,edsv+kw,edsw+hv,edgv,e+hw,edgw,e.(6) 4\,d(E_{P,e}+E_{Q,e}) =k_{v,e}\,ds_v+k_{w,e}\,ds_w +h_{v,e}\,dg_{v,e}+h_{w,e}\,dg_{w,e}. \qquad\text{(6)}

Let πs\pi_s denote projection to the selected real angles and set D¯=πs(𝒫¯c),ec(s)=min⁡{ℰ(s,g):(s,g)∈𝒫¯c}.(7) \overline D=\pi_s(\overline{\mathcal P}_c),\qquad e_c(s)=\min\{\mathcal E(s,g):(s,g)\in\overline{\mathcal P}_c\}. \qquad\text{(7)} Every fiber is nonempty and compact for s∈D¯s\in\overline D, so the value in (7) is defined. At this stage the notation does not assert uniqueness or interiority of a closed fiber minimizer.

The strict feasible real-angle base D=πs(𝒫c)D=\pi_s(\mathcal P_c) is explicitly |sv|<π,|nvsv−2πcv|+∑e=(v,w)|sw|<πdv(v∈S).(8) \boxed{\quad |s_v|<\pi,\qquad |n_vs_v-2\pi c_v|+ \sum_{e=(v,w)}|s_w|<\pi d_v\quad(v\in S).\quad} \qquad\text{(8)} Outside terms in the sum are zero; sums count incidences. This is the fixed-real-angle form of the existing feasibility criterion. For completeness, at fixed ss, each gv,eg_{v,e} independently varies in the interval (−(π−|sw|)/2,(π−|sw|)/2)(-(\pi-|s_w|)/2,(\pi-|s_w|)/2). Their sum must equal (nvsv−2πcv)/2(n_vs_v-2\pi c_v)/2, giving (8). Outside incidences have no sum constraint and can be chosen zero. Thus DD is a bounded convex open polytope, and D¯\overline D is its closure. The rows (2) are independent and their affine space projects surjectively onto the selected ss: an arbitrary change of svs_v can be offset in one incidence at that vertex.

The function ece_c is convex on D¯\overline D. Choose minimizers in the two fibers and take their convex combination; convexity of ℰ\mathcal E gives the convexity inequality for (7). It is also lower semicontinuous: for any convergent sequence of base points, a subsequence of their compact fiber minimizers attaining the lower limit has a limiting feasible assignment. No global strict-convexity assertion at boundary fibers is needed.

2. The regular fiber locus is exactly the positive period slice

Define Dreg={s∈D:a minimizer in (7) lies in 𝒫c}.(9) D_{\mathrm{reg}}= \{s\in D:\text{a minimizer in (7) lies in }\mathcal P_c\}. \qquad\text{(9)}

Proposition 1. The set DregD_{\mathrm{reg}} is open. Its fiber minimizer z(s)=(s,g(s))z(s)=(s,g(s)) is unique and real analytic. Its endpoint imaginary angles match, giving a real analytic graph t(s)=s+ih(s).(10) t(s)=s+i h(s). \qquad\text{(10)} With u(s)=∇ec(s)u(s)=\nabla e_c(s), this graph satisfies the original equations P(t(s))=u(s)−πic.(11) P(t(s))=u(s)-\pi i c. \qquad\text{(11)} Conversely every positive solution of P(t)=u−πicP(t)=u-\pi i c occurs exactly once on (10). Thus ∇ec\nabla e_c is a real analytic diffeomorphism from DregD_{\mathrm{reg}} onto the actual positive real-period image Ξc\Xi_c. Neither of these sets is assumed connected or convex.

Proof. An interior fiber critical point has positive Hessian on the homogeneous fiber tangent. It is the unique global fiber minimum, including against boundary points: its segment to any distinct feasible point is interior before the endpoint, and has strictly positive second derivative. The fiber stationarity equations have invertible Hessian, so the implicit-function theorem supplies an analytic interior critical point for all nearby ss. Its uniqueness as a global minimum proves the first assertions.

Attach multipliers hvh_v to (2) while holding ss fixed. By (6), incidence stationarity is precisely hv,e=hv(v∈S),hb,e=0(b∉S).(12) h_{v,e}=h_v\quad(v\in S),\qquad h_{b,e}=0\quad(b\notin S). \qquad\text{(12)} The original square dictionary therefore reconstructs the matched positive point (10). Along this fiber-minimum graph, (2) and (6) give dec=∑v∈S(∑e∋vkv,e+nv2hv)dsv.(13) de_c=\sum_{v\in S}\left( \sum_{e\ni v}k_{v,e}+\frac{n_v}{2}h_v\right)ds_v. \qquad\text{(13)} Combining (13) with (2) gives (11).

Alternatively the ambient identity at this point is dℰ=u⋅ds+∑vhvdρvd\mathcal E=u\cdot ds+\sum_v h_v\,d\rho_v. Consequently z(s)z(s) is the unique interior, hence global, minimum of ℰ−u(s)⋅s\mathcal E-u(s)\cdot s on 𝒫¯c\overline{\mathcal P}_c. This is the original tilted problem defining Ξc\Xi_c. Conversely its interior minimizer minimizes its fixed-ss fiber and satisfies (12), so it occurs on (10). Uniqueness of the tilted minimum gives injectivity. The period theorem supplies the analytic inverse u↦t(u)u\mapsto t(u); its real part is the inverse of ∇ec\nabla e_c. ▫\square

There is also a direct differential formula. Write the complex symmetric original Jacobian as J=∂P∂t=A+iB,A=A𝖳>0,B=B𝖳.(14) J=\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}=A+iB, \qquad A=A^{\mathsf T}>0,\qquad B=B^{\mathsf T}. \qquad\text{(14)} Differentiating (11) in real ss gives hs=−A−1B,∇2ec=A+BA−1B=(ℜJ−1)−1>0.(15) \boxed{\quad h_s=-A^{-1}B,\qquad \nabla^2e_c=A+B A^{-1}B =\bigl(\Re J^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}>0. \quad} \qquad\text{(15)} Indeed the imaginary part of (A+iB)(I+ihs)=us(A+iB)(I+i h_s)=u_s is B+Ahs=0B+A h_s=0, and the real part gives the second formula. The matrix hsh_s need not be symmetric; the graph assertion does not identify hh itself as the gradient of a function of ss.

The reduced energy also has strict supporting inequalities between any two distinct regular points, even if a segment between their base points is not known to stay regular. Apply the strict supporting inequality of ℰ\mathcal E at the first interior minimizer to the second. The dρd\rho terms vanish, giving ec(s2)>ec(s1)+∇ec(s1)⋅(s2−s1)(s1≠s2 in Dreg).(16) e_c(s_2)>e_c(s_1)+\nabla e_c(s_1)\cdot(s_2-s_1) \qquad(s_1\ne s_2\text{ in }D_{\mathrm{ reg}}). \qquad\text{(16)} In particular the gradient is strictly monotone between those points. This does not replace the interior-minimizer requirement in (9) by strict linear feasibility alone.

3. Exact convex projection of every low-energy collar

Assume now that the original charged positive solution at u=0u=0 is supplied. Write its angle assignment as z0z_0, its real angles as s0s_0, and E0=ℰ(z0)E_0=\mathcal E(z_0). It is the unique global minimum of ℰ\mathcal E. Put Δ=min∂𝒫cℰ−E0>0,0<δ<Δ,Dδ={s∈D¯:ec(s)<E0+δ}.(17) \Delta=\min_{\partial\mathcal P_c}\mathcal E-E_0>0, \qquad 0<\delta<\Delta, \qquad D_\delta=\{s\in\overline D:e_c(s)<E_0+\delta\}. \qquad\text{(17)}

Theorem 2. The set DδD_\delta is a bounded convex open domain with smooth boundary. Its closure is compactly contained in DregD_{\mathrm{reg}}, and Dδ¯={s:ec(s)≤E0+δ}=πs{z:ℰ(z)≤E0+δ},∇ec(Dδ)=Ωδ:={u:ℰ(zu)<E0+δ},Γδ:=t(Ωδ)={s+ih(s):s∈Dδ}.(18) \begin{aligned} \overline{D_\delta} &=\{s:e_c(s)\le E_0+\delta\} =\pi_s\{z:\mathcal E(z)\le E_0+\delta\},\\ \nabla e_c(D_\delta)&= \Omega_\delta:=\{u:\mathcal E(z_u)<E_0+\delta\},\\ \Gamma_\delta:=t(\Omega_\delta) &=\{s+i h(s):s\in D_\delta\}. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(18)} Here zuz_u is the unique closed tilted minimizer from the original inverse-period theorem. Thus (18) is the exact real-angle projection of that theorem’s collar, with its same marking and its same energy level, not a substitute contour.

Proof. The closed full sublevel Kδ={z:ℰ(z)≤E0+δ}K_\delta=\{z:\mathcal E(z)\le E_0+\delta\} is compact and lies in 𝒫c\mathcal P_c. Whenever ec(s)≤E0+δe_c(s)\le E_0+\delta, every fiber minimizer lies in KδK_\delta, so the fiber has its unique interior minimum. Thus the weak reduced sublevel is exactly πs(Kδ)\pi_s(K_\delta), is compact, and lies in the open set DregD_{\mathrm{reg}}. Likewise the strict sublevel is the projection of the strict full sublevel. It is open by surjectivity of the affine projection, or by Proposition 1, and is convex by convexity of ece_c. It contains a neighborhood of s0s_0.

Its closure is the weak sublevel. To approximate a weak-level point, take its segment toward s0s_0 and use convexity with ec(s0)=E0<E0+δe_c(s_0)=E_0<E_0+\delta. Lower semicontinuity gives the opposite inclusion. On a neighborhood of the weak sublevel the function is analytic and has positive definite Hessian (15). Its gradient vanishes only at s0s_0, by Proposition 1 and uniqueness at u=0u=0. The level E0+δE_0+\delta is therefore regular, proving smoothness of the boundary.

For s∈Dδs\in D_\delta, Proposition 1 identifies its fiber minimizer with z∇ec(s)z_{\nabla e_c(s)}, proving one inclusion in the second line of (18). Conversely any uu in that line has an interior minimizer in KδK_\delta; its real-angle projection lies in DδD_\delta and its gradient is uu. The third line now follows from the same inverse correspondence. ▫\square

In these coordinates the original calibrated action is particularly simple. Retain Φc(t)=∑e=vwψ(tv,tw)+i4π∑v∈Snvtv2−i∑v∈Scvtv,(Φc)t=−P+πicπ.(19) \Phi_c(t)=\sum_{e=vw}\psi(t_v,t_w) +\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{v\in S}n_vt_v^2 -i\sum_{v\in S}c_vt_v, \qquad (\Phi_c)_t=-\frac{P+\pi i c}{\pi}. \qquad\text{(19)} The exact edge energy identity gives, on the graph, ℜΦc(s+ih(s))=mM∞−ec(s)π,M∞=ψ(0,0).(20) \Re\Phi_c(s+i h(s))=mM_\infty-\frac{e_c(s)}\pi, \qquad M_\infty=\psi(0,0). \qquad\text{(20)} This uses arbitrary fixed charges, as in the finite-period compactness theorem, not only unit charge. The right side is strictly concave on DδD_\delta, has its unique maximum at s0s_0, and has exactly the boundary loss δ/π\delta/\pi. Compactness and (15) also give a constant κδ>0\kappa_\delta>0 such that ℜΦc(t0)−ℜΦc(s+ih(s))≥κδ2π|s−s0|2(s∈Dδ¯).(21) \Re\Phi_c(t^0)-\Re\Phi_c(s+i h(s)) \ge\frac{\kappa_\delta}{2\pi}|s-s_0|^2 \qquad(s\in\overline{D_\delta}). \qquad\text{(21)} To see this, take the minimum eigenvalue of ∇2ec\nabla^2e_c on the compact weak sublevel and integrate twice along the segment from s0s_0, which stays in that convex sublevel. For a lower-half-plane center, sufficiently small δ\delta keeps the entire closed graph in that half-plane, since the full energy sublevels shrink to their unique minimizer. No height sign is asserted on the whole regular locus.

4. The exact height cost of a vertical connector

Fix s∈Dregs\in D_{\mathrm{reg}}. Suppose a real vector vv is such that the entire segment tr=s+i(h(s)+rv),0≤r≤1,(22) t_r=s+i(h(s)+r v),\qquad 0\le r\le1, \qquad\text{(22)} stays in the original positive domain with the fixed outside data and continued original logarithms. This segment hypothesis is additional: no global vertical convexity of that domain is used. At its initial point (11) gives ∇hℜΦc=ℑP+πcπ=0,∇h2ℜΦc=ℜJπ>0.(23) \nabla_h\Re\Phi_c=\frac{\Im P+\pi c}{\pi}=0, \qquad \nabla_h^2\Re\Phi_c=\frac{\Re J}{\pi}>0. \qquad\text{(23)} Twice integrating along (22) therefore gives the exact identity ℜΦc(t1)−ℜΦc(t0)=1π∫01(1−r)v𝖳ℜJ(tr)vdr.(24) \boxed{\quad \Re\Phi_c(t_1)-\Re\Phi_c(t_0) =\frac1\pi\int_0^1(1-r) v^{\mathsf T}\Re J(t_r)v\,dr. \quad} \qquad\text{(24)} The increase is strictly positive for v≠0v\ne0. In particular the graph is a strict minimum in the imaginary-angle directions, despite being a strict maximum along its real-angle graph near the geometric center.

On a compact positive neighborhood with ℜJ≤ΛI\Re J\le\Lambda I, the cost in (24) is at most Λ|v|2/(2π)\Lambda|v|^2/(2\pi). For a point on ∂Dδ\partial D_\delta, a connector segment of this type consequently remains below the center’s action by at least δ−Λ|v|2/2π(25) \frac{\delta-\Lambda|v|^2/2}{\pi} \qquad\text{(25)} when the numerator is positive. Such a common compact neighborhood and a sufficiently small uniform displacement exist around the closed graph. Formula (25) is a local budget for a short side connector. It does not assert that the displacement needed to reach the original endpoint faces or wire seams fits that budget, or that such a displacement remains in a positive chart. Compactness of finite real-period data does not change the sign in (24).

There is nevertheless a valid local comparison with the constant geometric height h0=h(s0)h_0=h(s_0). On a sufficiently small compact convex real-angle neighborhood QQ of s0s_0, the graph, the constant-height slice and every segment between them stay positive. On that slice ∇s2ℜΦc(s+ih0)=−ℜJ/π<0\nabla_s^2\Re\Phi_c(s+i h_0)=-\Re J/\pi<0, and its gradient vanishes at s0s_0. Both endpoint chains therefore have their unique maximum at s0s_0. Vertical convexity bounds the intervening prism by the larger endpoint action, giving a strict uniform gap on ∂Q\partial Q. This is the original period-coordinate application of the local Legendre-prism argument already recorded in the general contour note, Section 5. It is not a connection to the real-height rim or the full source faces.

The real-height rim has a more definite limitation. Write ℰ(s,0)\mathcal E(s,0) for the ambient energy at zero incidence angles; it need not satisfy the charged rows (2). At fixed s∈(−π,π)Ss\in(-\pi,\pi)^S, all real original angles give positive edges and real returns. Thus the zero-incidence assignment has zero incidence gradient and is the strict minimum of the ambient energy in its convex fixed-ss incidence domain. The original edge energy identity consequently gives ℜΦc(s)=mM∞−ℰ(s,0)/π,ℜΦc(s)−ℜΦc(s+ih(s))={ec(s)−ℰ(s,0)}/π≥0.(26) \begin{aligned} \Re\Phi_c(s)&=mM_\infty-\mathcal E(s,0)/\pi,\\ \Re\Phi_c(s)-\Re\Phi_c(s+i h(s)) &=\{e_c(s)-\mathcal E(s,0)\}/\pi\ge0. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(26)} Equality holds exactly when the graph’s incidence assignment is zero, equivalently when h(s)=0h(s)=0. Equivalently, all rows satisfy nvsv=2πcvn_vs_v=2\pi c_v: in that case the zero-incidence assignment is feasible and is the fiber minimum. The reality assertions here follow either directly from the square dictionary or from the canonical real positive-edge formulas.

If h(s0)≠0h(s_0)\ne0, (26) gives ℜΦc(s0)>ℜΦc(t0)\Re\Phi_c(s_0)>\Re\Phi_c(t^0). By continuity and the shrinking of KδK_\delta to z0z_0, every sufficiently small projected collar has its entire real rim strictly above the target: ℜΦc(s)>ℜΦc(t0)(s∈Dδ¯).(27) \Re\Phi_c(s)>\Re\Phi_c(t^0) \qquad(s\in\overline{D_\delta}). \qquad\text{(27)} Thus vertical sides all the way to h=0h=0 cannot be lower-action connectors for such collars, even if those sides happen to stay positive. This endpoint conclusion does not require their positivity. It is the fixed-real-angle energy excess already underlying the physical-pressure theorem, applied here to the actual collar projection. It concerns the classical selected action, not the size of a signed trace remainder.

5. Exact remaining source task

The construction supplies the explicit convex real-angle domain (8), its regular fiber-minimum locus, and the exact collar projection (18). It proves neither that every strictly feasible real angle is regular nor that the full inverse-period image has a prescribed topology. The closed fiber problem and the closed tilted problem are different; uniqueness of every tilted minimizer is not an assertion of uniqueness of every boundary fiber minimizer.

For the original finite source, the relative boundary is still its angular endpoint faces and wire seams, with the retained full Hermite trace and free cardinal ends. The boundary of (18) is an interior energy level, not one of those prescribed source faces. The exact finite-period theorem also exhibits genuine trace poles on parts of the positive inverse slice; it does not place those parts in the component containing the center. Any deformation through such poles requires its actual residue/current terms. Small detours and a local action budget do not show that those terms have smaller complete source rate.

Thus the result gives a canonical real-angle graph on which to try the global contour construction and an exact test of one possible connector’s classical cost. It supplies no identification of the whole original relative class, no bound for the remaining joint factorial/wire source, and no nonzero quantum cycle coefficient.
