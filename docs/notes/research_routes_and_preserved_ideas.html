Research methods and their present roles

25 November 2026. The current status states the open finite-exception problem. The papers contain the established restricted volume theorems. The following methods have distinct roles.

Exact finite sources and orthogonality

The actual source includes a fixed closing functional and normal directions at repeated spectral roots. The ordinary-color Parseval calculation separates a remainder by exact orthogonality; it does not evaluate the resulting whole norm geometrically.

The two-dimensional Hermite source frame and cap-tree energy give positive forms that retain the marked observations. Their stated compatibility conditions matter: a direction invisible at one cut can become visible after another crossing.

The normalized source energy, orthogonal linear elimination and finite-fiber comparison give the general variational formulation. Its remaining minimum still needs a geometric exponential evaluation.

Algebraic periods, recurrences and torsors

The torsor status separates finite operator identities, algebraic branches, recurrence solutions and volume limits. A classical curve alone does not select the original quantum solution. The source, closing and normalization must survive every change of presentation.

The initialized coideal period is a finite realization of this viewpoint. The cyclic Hardy comparison provides a function-theoretic approach to coefficient norms. Neither calculation identifies an arbitrary closed source with a contributing geometric period.

Geometry and optimization

The marked-filling paper constructs the geometric data used by the volume arguments. The strict Conway geometry identifies their marked action with the absolute knot-complement volume. The geometric maximum and its rigidity do not by themselves prove survival of the original quantum contribution.

The Rivin presentation comparison retains the necessary boundary indices. The cover laws and filled-slope obstruction explain why a cover requires a separate filling and quantum descent argument.

Obstructions and other comparisons

The illustrated methods chapter explains weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, Fourier analysis, positive coefficient bounds and stationary phase. Its counterexamples distinguish upper bounds from noncancellation. The analytic transport examples show why distinct instantaneous eigenvalues do not give a single-branch asymptotic. The relative-homology criterion states sufficient additional conditions for persistence of a saddle.

The source and transport chapter retains the Racah, magnetic Fourier, reflection-double and positive-sewing approaches with their local conclusions and unresolved global steps. Earlier parameter pilots and superseded proof notes are in the pre-cleanup snapshot, without duplicate archive directories in the active tree.
