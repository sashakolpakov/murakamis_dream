A centered Fourier basis for the original Conway gluing

21 November 2026. Research note. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

A single unitary change of Newton coordinates makes the original bilinear gluing diagonal and makes both signed crossing coupons coordinate vectors. Every multiplication then uses one symmetric three-index tensor. The tensor is explicit, but its phases cannot all be removed while keeping this diagonal gluing and the full repeated-root algebra.

This is an exact compiler for the original finite source. It neither truncates a packet to a Gaussian window nor proves a geometric lower bound after short fillings.

1. The algebra and the two original closures

Fix N≥2N\ge2, and put d=N−1,q=eπi/N,κ=qd2/2,Sr=∏j=1r2sin⁡(πj/N),S0=1.(1) d=N-1,\qquad q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad \kappa=q^{d^2/2},\qquad S_r=\prod_{j=1}^r2\sin(\pi j/N),\quad S_0=1. \qquad\text{(1)} Powers of qq in this note mean qa=eπia/Nq^a=e^{\pi ia/N}, including half-integer exponents. This fixes the phases at even levels. The original algebra and its normalized Newton basis are ℋN=ℂ[z]/PN(z),PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N),er=Sr−2∏j=0r−1(cj−z).(2) \mathcal H_N=\mathbb C[z]/P_N(z),\quad P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\quad c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N),\quad e_r=S_r^{-2}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(c_j-z). \qquad\text{(2)} Here TN(cos⁡t)=cos⁡NtT_N(\cos t)=\cos Nt. Work in the entire quotient, including the first derivatives at its double roots.

The original Frobenius closing ϕ\phi, the ordinary marked closing mm, the genuine turn JJ, and coefficient reflection a♯(z)=a(z‾)¯a^\sharp(z)=\overline{a(\bar z)} satisfy ϕ(eres)=δr+s,d,ϕ(er)=δr,d,m(er)=δr,0,Jer=ed−r,er♯=er.(3) \phi(e_re_s)=\delta_{r+s,d},\qquad \phi(e_r)=\delta_{r,d},\qquad m(e_r)=\delta_{r,0}, \qquad Je_r=e_{d-r},\qquad e_r^\sharp=e_r. \qquad\text{(3)} The positive Newton norm is ∥∑rαrer∥2=∑r|αr|2\|\sum_r\alpha_re_r\|^2=\sum_r|\alpha_r|^2. The actual signed crossing elements are R+=κ∑r=0d(−q)rer,R−=κ−1∑r=0d(−q)−rer,R+R−=1.(4) R^+=\kappa\sum_{r=0}^d(-q)^re_r,\qquad R^-=\kappa^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^d(-q)^{-r}e_r,\qquad R^+R^-=1. \qquad\text{(4)} These identities, including the original cups, caps and Cartan scalar, are the direct inputs from the Newton source theorem.

2. Diagonal bilinear gluing and exact signed modes

For 0≤a≤d0\le a\le d, set ka=a−d/2,Ura=N−1/2exp⁡(2πiNka(r−d/2)),fa=∑r=0dUraer.(5) k_a=a-d/2,\qquad U_{ra}=N^{-1/2} \exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{N}k_a(r-d/2)\right), \qquad f_a=\sum_{r=0}^dU_{ra}e_r. \qquad\text{(5)} If α\alpha and β\beta are the Newton and Fourier coefficient columns of the same element, then α=Uβ\alpha=U\beta.

Proposition 1. The basis in (5) is Hermitian orthonormal in the positive Newton norm. It also satisfies the bilinear identities ϕ(fafb)=δab,Jfa=fd−a,fa♯=fd−a.(6) \boxed{\quad \phi(f_af_b)=\delta_{ab},\qquad Jf_a=f_{d-a},\qquad f_a^\sharp=f_{d-a}. \quad} \qquad\text{(6)} The two signed crossings are exactly R+=Nf0,R−=Nfd.(7) R^+=\sqrt N\,f_0,\qquad R^-=\sqrt N\,f_d . \qquad\text{(7)}

Proof. The differences kb−ka=b−ak_b-k_a=b-a are integers. The finite geometric sum in ∑rUra¯Urb\sum_r\overline{U_{ra}}U_{rb} therefore gives U*U=IU^*U=I, at both parities. Moreover Ud−r,b=Urb¯,Ur,d−a=Ura¯.(8) U_{d-r,b}=\overline{U_{rb}},\qquad U_{r,d-a}=\overline{U_{ra}}. \qquad\text{(8)} Combining the first equality with (3) proves ϕ(fafb)=∑rUraUrb¯=δab\phi(f_af_b)=\sum_rU_{ra}\overline{U_{rb}}=\delta_{ab}. The two reversal assertions follow from (8).

Since rr is an integer, (−q)r=e−πidr/N,NUr0=κ(−q)r,NUrd=κ−1(−q)−r.(9) (-q)^r=e^{-\pi idr/N},\qquad \sqrt N\,U_{r0}=\kappa(-q)^r,\qquad \sqrt N\,U_{rd}=\kappa^{-1}(-q)^{-r}. \qquad\text{(9)} Thus (4) proves (7) with no remaining phase factor. ▫\square

The distinction between bilinear and Hermitian pairing remains essential. If A=∑aAafaA=\sum_aA_af_a and B=∑aBafaB=\sum_aB_af_a, then ϕ(AB)=∑aAaBa,(10) \phi(AB)=\sum_aA_aB_a , \qquad\text{(10)} without conjugating either input. A physical turn on an attaching edge still inserts the reversal a↦d−aa\mapsto d-a.

The original closure rows are not discarded. Define ωa=N−1/2eπikad/N.(11) \omega_a=N^{-1/2}e^{\pi ik_ad/N}. \qquad\text{(11)} Then ϕ(fa)=ωa,m(fa)=ωa¯,1=∑a=0dωafa.(12) \phi(f_a)=\omega_a,\qquad m(f_a)=\overline{\omega_a},\qquad 1=\sum_{a=0}^d\omega_af_a . \qquad\text{(12)} These formulas follow by taking the ede_d and e0e_0 coefficients in (5), or by pairing the unit with faf_a. Both closure rows retain dual norm one. For example ϕ(1)=∑aωa2=0\phi(1)=\sum_a\omega_a^2=0, whereas m(1)=∑a|ωa|2=1m(1)=\sum_a|\omega_a|^2=1. Thus the diagonal gluing has not erased the original source’s exact zero or its marked closure.

3. One symmetric multiplication tensor

Define the real symmetric Newton tensor τijk=ϕ(eiejek).(13) \tau_{ijk}=\phi(e_ie_je_k). \qquad\text{(13)} Its exact support and coefficients are τijk={Si+jSi+kSj+kNSiSjSkSi+j+k−d,i+j,i+k,j+k≤d,i+j+k≥d,0,otherwise.(14) \tau_{ijk}= \begin{cases} \displaystyle \frac{S_{i+j}S_{i+k}S_{j+k}} {N S_iS_jS_kS_{i+j+k-d}}, &i+j,\ i+k,\ j+k\le d,\quad i+j+k\ge d,\\[6pt] 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(14)} All factorial indices in the nonzero case lie between 00 and dd; every displayed coefficient is strictly positive.

To derive (14), the actual positive multiplication theorem gives eiej=∑ℓ=max⁡(i,j)i+jSi+jSℓSiSjSℓ−iSℓ−jSi+j−ℓeℓ(i+j<N),(15) e_ie_j=\sum_{\ell=\max(i,j)}^{i+j} \frac{S_{i+j}S_\ell} {S_iS_jS_{\ell-i}S_{\ell-j}S_{i+j-\ell}}e_\ell \quad(i+j<N), \qquad\text{(15)} and gives zero when i+j≥Ni+j\ge N. By (3), only ℓ=d−k\ell=d-k contributes to (13). Its permitted range is equivalent to the four inequalities in (14). Finally use SrSd−r=NS_rS_{d-r}=N in its three complementary factorials. This proves the symmetric formula without evaluating only at the distinct Casimir roots.

In the Fourier basis put Cabc=ϕ(fafbfc)=∑i,j,k=0dUiaUjbUkcτijk.(16) C_{abc}=\phi(f_af_bf_c) =\sum_{i,j,k=0}^dU_{ia}U_{jb}U_{kc}\tau_{ijk}. \qquad\text{(16)} Thus CC is an explicit centered three-dimensional finite Fourier transform of (14). Equivalently the summand’s phase is exp⁡{2πiN[ka(i−d/2)+kb(j−d/2)+kc(k−d/2)]},(17) \exp\!\left\{\frac{2\pi i}{N} [k_a(i-d/2)+k_b(j-d/2)+k_c(k-d/2)]\right\}, \qquad\text{(17)} with the common factor N−3/2N^{-3/2}. The tensor is symmetric in all three legs, and (6) gives fafb=∑c=0dCabcfc.(18) f_af_b=\sum_{c=0}^dC_{abc}f_c . \qquad\text{(18)} It retains the conjugation rule Cabc¯=Cd−a,d−b,d−c\overline{C_{abc}}=C_{d-a,d-b,d-c}. In particular multiplication by the two actual signed crossings has matrices (A+)cb=NC0bc,(A−)cb=NCdbc,A−A+=I.(19) (A_+)_{cb}=\sqrt N\,C_{0bc},\qquad (A_-)_{cb}=\sqrt N\,C_{dbc},\qquad A_-A_+=I. \qquad\text{(19)} These matrices retain their repeated-root Jordan directions. They have not become diagonal phase multipliers.

4. The actual Casimir becomes tridiagonal

The tensor in (16) also satisfies finite three-term equations. For 0≤a≤d0\le a\le d, put Aa=Q−a−1−1,Ba=2q−1Q−a,Da=Q−a−1,Q=q2.(20) A_a=Q^{-a-1}-1,\qquad B_a=2q^{-1}Q^{-a},\qquad D_a=Q^{-a}-1,\qquad Q=q^2. \qquad\text{(20)} Then multiplication by the actual Casimir coordinate has the form zfa=Aafa+1+Bafa+Dafa−1.(21) zf_a=A_af_{a+1}+B_af_a+D_af_{a-1}. \qquad\text{(21)} The fa+1f_{a+1} and fa−1f_{a-1} terms are absent at a=da=d and a=0a=0, respectively: Ad=D0=0A_d=D_0=0. There is no cyclic endpoint arrow.

Here is a direct verification, including the endpoints. In Newton coordinates zer=crer+br+1er+1,br=Qr+Q−r−2,(22) ze_r=c_re_r+b_{r+1}e_{r+1},\qquad b_r=Q^r+Q^{-r}-2, \qquad\text{(22)} with the absent term at r=dr=d and b0=0b_0=0. Set x=qd=−q−1x=q^d=-q^{-1}. In the output row rr, the coefficient of zfazf_a is Ura{cr+brxQ−a}.(23) U_{ra}\{c_r+b_rxQ^{-a}\}. \qquad\text{(23)} Using cr=qQr+q−1Q−rc_r=qQ^r+q^{-1}Q^{-r} and Q±rUra=x±1Ur,a±1Q^{\pm r}U_{ra}=x^{\pm1}U_{r,a\pm1} gives (21). One may temporarily extend fa+N=(−1)dfaf_{a+N}=(-1)^df_a in this calculation, but the coefficients Ad,D0A_d,D_0 kill both wrap terms in the final identity.

Since f0=R+/Nf_0=R^+/\sqrt N and R+R^+ is invertible, (21) gives unique polynomials pa(z)p_a(z) of degree aa such that fa=N−1/2R+pa(z),p0=1.(24) f_a=N^{-1/2}R^+p_a(z),\qquad p_0=1 . \qquad\text{(24)} For a<da<d, solve (21) for pa+1p_{a+1}; its denominator AaA_a is nonzero. The monic rescaling 𝒫a=(−1)a(Q−1;Q−1)apa,(Q−1;Q−1)a=∏j=1a(1−Q−j),(25) \mathcal P_a=(-1)^a(Q^{-1};Q^{-1})_a p_a,\qquad (Q^{-1};Q^{-1})_a=\prod_{j=1}^a(1-Q^{-j}), \qquad\text{(25)} satisfies, for 0≤a<d0\le a<d, with 𝒫−1=0\mathcal P_{-1}=0, z𝒫a=𝒫a+1+2q−1Q−a𝒫a+(1−Q−a)2𝒫a−1.(26) z\mathcal P_a=\mathcal P_{a+1} +2q^{-1}Q^{-a}\mathcal P_a +(1-Q^{-a})^2\mathcal P_{a-1}. \qquad\text{(26)} This is the Al-Salam–Chihara recurrence with argument z/2z/2, base Q−1Q^{-1}, and both parameters q−1q^{-1}; compare Koornwinder (2004), equation (5). The comparison only names a standard recurrence. Its specialization here was derived above. Only 0≤a≤d0\le a\le d is rescaled in (25); its factorial is nonzero on that range. No rescaling by the vanishing factor (Q−1;Q−1)N(Q^{-1};Q^{-1})_N, positive measure, or generic-parameter orthogonality theorem is used.

The original complex linear functional ψ(g)=ϕ((R+)2g)/N(27) \psi(g)=\phi((R^+)^2g)/N \qquad\text{(27)} obeys ψ(papb)=δab,Cabc=N−1/2ψ(R+papbpc).(28) \psi(p_ap_b)=\delta_{ab},\qquad C_{abc}=N^{-1/2}\psi(R^+p_ap_bp_c). \qquad\text{(28)} These are bilinear identities; ψ\psi is not asserted to be a positive measure. They follow by substituting (24) in (6) and (16). Finally, moving zz among the three factors in ϕ(zfafbfc)\phi(zf_af_bf_c) proves AaCa+1,b,c+BaCabc+DaCa−1,b,c=AbCa,b+1,c+BbCabc+DbCa,b−1,c=AcCa,b,c+1+BcCabc+DcCa,b,c−1.(29) \begin{aligned} A_aC_{a+1,b,c}+B_aC_{abc}+D_aC_{a-1,b,c} &=A_bC_{a,b+1,c}+B_bC_{abc}+D_bC_{a,b-1,c}\\ &=A_cC_{a,b,c+1}+B_cC_{abc}+D_cC_{a,b,c-1}. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(29)} Every endpoint in these equations retains its zero coefficient. Thus the universal multiplication tensor comes with explicit finite difference equations, not only its Fourier sum.

5. The exact source compiler

Every specified finite Conway source can now be evaluated using:

  • the single tensor CC for each multiplication;
  • reversal a↦d−aa\mapsto d-a for each genuine turn;
  • the vectors Nδ0\sqrt N\,\delta_0 and Nδd\sqrt N\,\delta_d for signed crossing coupons;
  • the unit and the two original closing rows in (12).

A power RnR^n is a product of |n||n| signed unit crossings, or the unit for n=0n=0. Consequently J(R+)=NfdJ(R^+)=\sqrt N f_d and J(R−)=Nf0J(R^-)=\sqrt N f_0. An arbitrary fixed short filling can be retained exactly by this finite algebraic expansion. These auxiliary multiplication vertices are not new crossing circles or new independent twist parameters. There is no assertion that the expanded diagram is a new strict filling template, or that turns may be moved through products.

The source identity follows by induction on the original expression, using (6), (12), and (18) at its actual attachments. Open outputs are retained as full NN-component columns. No Casimir value or first derivative has been removed, because UU is invertible on the entire algebra.

The same change is unitary for the positive Newton packet norm. It preserves every vector norm, operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and multiplication-tensor Hilbert–Schmidt norm. For the latter, the ordinary output/input coordinate formula is U*M(U⊗U)U^*M(U\otimes U); equations (3) and (8) identify it with (16). Thus a previously recorded norm budget is unchanged if the same algebraic tree is written in these coordinates. In particular a normalized signed leaf is an exact endpoint mode, rather than an approximation obtained by deleting its other Newton coordinates.

6. The phases cannot all be removed

There is a structural limit to simultaneous diagonal gluing and real multiplication.

Proposition 2. For N≥2N\ge2, in any basis gag_a satisfying ϕ(gagb)=δab\phi(g_ag_b)=\delta_{ab}, the full triple tensor ϕ(gagbgc)\phi(g_ag_bg_c) cannot have all its entries in eiϑℝe^{i\vartheta}\mathbb R for one common phase ϑ\vartheta. In particular it cannot be entirely real or nonnegative.

Proof. Write 1=∑aℓaga1=\sum_a\ell_ag_a, so ℓa=ϕ(ga)\ell_a=\phi(g_a). Then ∑aℓa2=ϕ(1)=0.(30) \sum_a\ell_a^2=\phi(1)=0 . \qquad\text{(30)} Suppose the triple tensor were eiϑRabce^{i\vartheta}R_{abc} with all RabcR_{abc} real. The unit equations become ∑avaRabc=δbc,va=eiϑℓa.(31) \sum_av_aR_{abc}=\delta_{bc},\qquad v_a=e^{i\vartheta}\ell_a . \qquad\text{(31)} Their solution is unique: the difference of two solutions would give an element whose multiplication operator is zero, and applying it to 11 would show that element is zero. Real coefficients therefore force v=v‾v=\bar v. It is nonzero because the unit is nonzero. But then ∑aℓa2=e−2iϑ∑ava2≠0\sum_a\ell_a^2=e^{-2i\vartheta}\sum_av_a^2\ne0, contrary to (30). ▫\square

This statement concerns bases orthonormal for the original bilinear Frobenius form. It does not concern every Hermitian orthonormal basis. The original Newton basis already has real nonnegative multiplication, but its Frobenius pairing is anti-diagonal. The two facts are compatible. Here the signed leaves and gluing simplify, while necessary phases remain in CC and in the two explicit closing rows.

7. Scope and complete sources

The construction is at the fixed original root and complete main color. It transports the entire Casimir algebra, including its Hermite directions. It does not claim a generic-qq or moving peripheral extension without the corresponding original families. No positive geometric contour, nonzero global saddle coefficient, or additional volume-identity class follows from this basis change.
