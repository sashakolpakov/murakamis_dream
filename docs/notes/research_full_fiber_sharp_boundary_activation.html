Boundary reduction for a global full sharp-source maximum

The variational theorem concerns the entire original flow fiber with freely varying spectra. It does not assert finite-coefficient asymptotics, phase alignment or a volume bound.

1. Original fiber and explicit hypotheses

Fix an original simple-white certified plat. Its white sphere is a simple triangulation, the protected faces u,v are adjacent along the marked MAIN edge, and a port pairs two shaded triangles across a MATCHING edge. Keep every original incidence, including the protected zeros.

Let P be the FULL nonnegative transport fiber: each port supplies1, each ordinary face demands1, and each protected face demands0. No LEFT/CROSS/RIGHT label inequalities are imposed on P. A local vector q=(X,B,C,Y) with sum1 is written

mu=B, nu=1-C, xi=X, zeta=Y=nu-mu-xi.

The actual complete geometric flow qG belongs to P. It has positive load on EVERY ordinary incidence, zero on protected incidences, and both middle loads strictly less than1/2. Its two literal triangle allocations are positive on every ordinary triangle corner. Each port has at least one ordinary outer incidence: the two protected faces are joined by a MAIN edge, not by its outer MATCHING edge. Therefore

qG_x+qG_y>0 at every port.                         (1)

For 0<=delta<=1/2 define h_delta on the FULL interval by the original sharp functional

h_delta(q)=int_0^mu log|alpha_delta|
            +int_nu^1 log|alpha_delta|
            +inf_(x>0){int_mu^nu log Lambda_delta(t;x,1)dt
                          -xi log x},             (2)
alpha_delta=4[cos^2(pi delta)-sin^2(pi t)].

Here Lambda=beta rho, with beta(t)=4 sin^2(pi t) and rho the exact positive eta-word root, eta=cos^2(pi delta)/sin^2(pi t). Endpoint values are the continuous ones. In particular h_(1/2)=0 for ALL q. At delta=0, h_0 is the continuous full-kernel endpoint entropy. On CROSS (B,C<=1/2),

h_0(q)=max_(a+b=q) [Ctri(a)+Ctri(b)],              (3)

where the two original triangle rows have supply1/2 and their indicated middle loads B,C. This is the exact latent split of the original port, not independently reassigned angles. The all-CROSS geometric allocation has total endpoint entropy Phi_D>0.

Consider a GLOBAL maximum of sum_e h_(delta_e)(q_e), with q in P and independent real delta_e in[0,1/2]. Alternatively maximize over the closed non-CROSS union, but assume the maximum being studied has value at least Phi_D and at least one STRICT non-CROSS middle load greater than1/2. Such a port keeps the geometric segment below in that union for all sufficiently small perturbations. No analogous claim is made for a seam-only non-CROSS boundary maximum.

The statements use the sharp continuum functional directly. Where it is used as an upper functional for the actual source, this theorem does not supply a new coefficient-identification or cap-uniform N limit.

2. Fixed-load spectral exclusions

First, a positive-length port at strict 0<delta<1/2 cannot have an endpoint at either simple alpha pole. Write the poles as p=1/2-delta and p’=1/2+delta. Moving an endpoint pole into the active interval increases the two outside integrals by |s|log(1/|s|)+O(|s|). If both endpoints are poles, necessarily mu=p,nu=p’; decreasing delta moves both inside and gives twice this gain. All other outside-integral changes are O(|s|). The tilted tail changes only O(|s|), uniformly in the tilt: with eta=cos^2(pi delta)/sin^2(pi t),

0<=partial_(log eta)log rho_eta(x)<=1/2,

and partial_delta log eta is bounded on every strict delta compact. Taking a common infimum preserves that Lipschitz estimate, also for closed outer loads. Thus an arbitrarily small feasible spectral change increases (2). This excludes positive-length pole endpoints without assuming face stationarity or concavity in the row variables.

Second, at delta=0 a positive-length nonterminal one-sided interval cannot maximize in its free spectrum. For a lower interval nu<1/2, put z=sin^2(pi delta) and L_z=Lambda_z/alpha_z, with z small enough that alpha stays positive on the interval. Directly from the positive root,

-partial_z log Lambda_z <=1/[2(1-z)],
partial_z log L_z >=1/[2(1-z)].

The first inequality follows by bounding its square-root denominator below by 4xy(1-z); it remains valid at a monomial tilt. Hence, uniformly in every outer tilt and after the same infimum,

h_delta(q)-h_0(q)>=-(nu-mu)log(1-z)/2>0.          (4)

Reflection proves the upper one-sided case. Thus every positive-length delta=0 port at a maximum lies in CROSS, allowing its seams for now.

Finally classify a positive-length delta=1/2 port at fixed loads. The sharp entropy dominates its monomer action because J*>=0. If 0<mu<nu<1, choose a positive k=cos(pi delta) smaller than the minimum of sin(pi t) on that fixed interval. The entire active interval is then after the small lower pole and before its reflection, and its actual monomer action is

int_mu^nu -log[1-k^2/sin^2(pi t)]dt>0.           (5)

Thus every interval with both endpoints strictly inside(0,1) is excluded at the cap, uniformly over its closed outer split. Write I_k(0,a) for the initial monomer integral of log[sin2/|k2-sin^2|]. If mu=0 and 1/2<nu<1, the exact total and reflection identities give

I_k(0,nu)=-I_k(0,1-nu)>0                       (6)

for sufficiently small k, because the latter initial interval has negative monomer action by its regular positive suffix formula. Exact reflection treats nu=1 and 0<mu<1/2.

For mu=0,0<nu<1/2 and BOTH outer loads positive, the protected-middle cap lemma gives, at small positive k=cos(pi delta),

h_delta>=2 min(xi,nu-xi)log(1+k)
                    -(2/pi)k^2 cot(pi nu)>0.     (7)

The half-terminal extension has no baseline cost, and a full interval [0,1] has the same positive chord bound for strict outer loads. Exact reflection covers the protected output. Consequently a cap maximum with positive length can only have a monomial outer split and be

[0,nu], 0<nu<=1/2; its reflected interval;
or the whole interval[0,1].                     (8)

Every other fixed-load cap candidate has an available positive value at another spectrum, while the cap value is zero. This classification does not yet eliminate the zeros at (8).

3. One geometric segment, with source spectra retained or retuned

Use the EXACT full-fiber segment

q(eps)=(1-eps)q+eps qG.                          (9)

It preserves every ordinary target, protected zero and row supply. It activates every zero ORDINARY incidence. It is not required to preserve an old LEFT/CROSS/RIGHT label at a boundary: the full source fiber has no such imposed labels. If a strict non-CROSS port was specified, its strict inequality persists for small eps.

For a non-diagonal strict-spectrum port, keep delta fixed. The ordinary-zero barrier table and exact reflection give nonnegative eps log(1/eps) coefficients, strictly positive if an ordinary zero incidence is activated. Upper rows equal1/2 are regular units at a strict spectrum; there is no half-label inequality in this full fiber. At the full two-terminal interval mu=0,nu=1, write mu=eps B and 1-nu=eps C. With strict base outer loads use xi=xi0+eps dot_xi, 0<xi0<1; the two-end calculation gives B+C. At the x-zero corner instead use xi=eps X, and its coefficient is 2(B+C+X). Outer reflection is identical. These exhaust the positive-length full-row zero patterns. All other changes are O(eps).

For a delta=0 CROSS port choose an attaining split in (3), and mix its two triangle rows with the actual geometric triangle rows. Their sum is EXACTLY the local vector in (9). The optimized h_0 at that vector is at least the entropy of this mixed split. Each triangle has a nonnegative closed-corner singular coefficient, strictly positive at every activated zero ordinary corner. Thus an ordinary zero in the port gives a positive gain. At a seam nu=1/2 the output triangle is the pure-middle vertex, and its contribution is

2 dot_nu eps log(1/eps)+O(eps), dot_nu>0.         (10)

The input triangle has no negative singular contribution. The mu=1/2 seam is reflected and gives -2 dot_mu>0. This uses an actual latent complete split inside the FULL endpoint polynomial. It makes no infeasible fixed-pattern retagging or terminal KKT assertion.

For a diagonal mu=nu=a, retune its spectrum. At a in(0,1), (1) opens a positive interval; placing a simple pole inside it gives W eps log(1/eps)+O(eps), or 2W at a=1/2, uniformly in the outer split. At a=0 or1 use cos(pi delta_eps)=eps^2; its cost is at worst O(eps^2). These are the free-spectral retuning estimates.

For a cap port from (8), the cap-corner lemma gives the needed ordinary-zero gain. At [0,nu0] with nu0<1/2 and x load zero, choose cos^2(pi delta_eps)=eps K. Its singular coefficient is B+X, where B=dot_mu and X=dot_xi. It is positive if either zero incidence is ordinary. At a flat half-terminal monomial interval [0,1/2], the base value is zero for EVERY spectrum. Retune its base to delta=0 and use the actual latent CROSS split above. Its output triangle is the pure-middle vertex, so (10) gives a strictly positive gain because qG_c<1/2. This remains true when the input middle and the zero outer incidence are both protected. Thus the half-terminal monomial is excluded at every initial spectrum, not only at the cap. Exact reflection excludes the upper half-terminal monomial as well.

At the flat full terminal interval, retune to a fixed strict delta and use the corresponding corner table. A full [0,1] monomial interval has three zero incidences, at least one ordinary, so its gain is strict. If both zeros of a half-interval cap corner are protected, keep the cap value zero. That port has no ordinary zero to activate. Reflected and outer-exchanged cases use the same exact source symmetries.

All spectra are independent, so these choices can be made simultaneously without altering (9). There are only finitely many ports. Every listed singular coefficient is nonnegative; every strict gain dominates all O(eps) terms. Thus global maximality implies:

  • every non-diagonal port has positive load on every ordinary incidence;
  • no interior diagonal remains;
  • no positive-length delta=0 port lies on a CROSS seam;
  • no half-terminal monomial remains at any spectrum;
  • every remaining positive-length cap port has its two zero incidences both protected, and is a monomial corner [0,nu] with 0<nu<1/2, or its exact reflection.

This conclusion is for a global free-spectrum maximum. No such simultaneous retuning is inferred at a local fixed-spectrum maximum.

4. Endpoint diagonals form an impossible closed component

Only diagonals at a=0 or1 can now remain. Let D be their ports and F their occupied middle faces. Such a row sends its entire supply1 to one ordinary face. Protected demand0 excludes a protected occupied face, and demand1 makes the map D->F injective. Therefore |D|=|F|.

Every face in F is saturated by its diagonal row. By section3 every ordinary incidence of a non-diagonal row is positive. Thus no non-diagonal row can even be incident to F: N(F)=D in the full supported bipartite incidence graph B. Evaluate the same demands with qG. The faces F require |F|=|D|, and only D can supply them. All supplies of D are therefore used inside F. Because qG is strictly positive on every ordinary incidence, no D row has an arc to an ordinary face outside F. Hence D union F is a union of connected components of B.

The original graph B is connected. Delete u,v from the simple white triangulated sphere: the remaining white graph is connected. The elementary connectivity argument reroutes a path through the cyclic link of u, and then through the link of v with u deleted, which is a connected path or cycle. Every remaining white edge belongs to an original port quartet, so it induces a two-step path through that port in B. Every port has an ordinary incidence. These facts connect all vertices of B without creating any new source adjacency.

Consequently nonempty D would mean all ports are diagonal. Their total sharp entropy is zero. This contradicts the feasible geometric value Phi_D>0 for an unrestricted full-fiber maximum. In the strict non-CROSS variant, the comparison must instead assume the studied maximum has positive value (as is automatic for any candidate at or above Phi_D). The all-geometric point need not lie in that restricted set, so its value is not silently used as a feasible NC lower bound.

Thus D is empty under these stated value hypotheses. No actual identity smoothing or cover is needed for this tight-cut argument.

5. What the reduction proves, and what remains

An unrestricted global full sharp maximum on this original certified fiber has no diagonal and has positive load on every ordinary incidence. Positive-length strict-spectrum ports have no endpoint at a pole; delta=0 ports are strict CROSS. The only cap ports left by this argument are monomial intervals [0,nu], 0<nu<1/2, and their exact reflections, whose parent middle and one outer incidence are both the original protected faces. Half-terminal monomials at any spectrum have been excluded by their cost-free retuning to delta=0.

Those two zero incidences form the actual marked MAIN edge uv in one shaded triangle. The edge has two incident triangles, and they belong to distinct ports since ports pair across MATCHING edges. Thus at most two such positive-length cap ports remain, adjacent to the mark. This count does not identify their geometry, phase, or contribution.

The same boundary reduction holds for a competing maximum of value at least Phi_D over the closed non-CROSS union if at least one STRICT non-CROSS port is present. A seam-only constrained maximum requires its own documented argument; it is not covered by a feasible qG segment here.

Ordinary positivity is not an all-boundary theorem. Protected zero faces remain, the possible marked cap corners remain, and the full spectral/filling phase still has to be controlled. No finite positive ordinary multipliers, terminal geometric interpretation at a cap, new developing representation, bound by Phi_D, or quantum period conclusion is asserted at those residual cap configurations.

All arguments here concern the sharp continuum functional. They use the exact original row/face constraints, both unused products, the normalized eta word and the separately proved endpoint entropy. They do not replace a signed polynomial by a new positive model or interchange a shrinking spectral scale with N. Any later use for the original finite source must retain its exact scalar zeros and the independently proved coefficient upper/identification statements.

Scope

No lower coefficient limit or volume comparison follows from this activation argument. The finite-multiplier conclusion is proved in the separate stationary addendum.
