Original one-cap pressure: global real continuation and an algebraic boundary response

31 October 2026. analytical research theorem. Unpublished. The original source boundary curve has an atom-free, log-algebraic pressure-root density and an exact selected logarithmic period equal to the original drilled hyperbolic volume. No filled quantum limit or ordinary knot A-polynomial identification is asserted. The original simple matched sphere, protected MAIN edge uv and cap F=(u,A,v,C) remain fixed. Every other original port is CROSS. Assume a strict finite positive one-cap stationary exterior, with all ordinary incidences positive, L_A=L_C and 0<nu<1/2. The one-cap geometric double gives the stronger nu>1/4, but that stronger inequality is not needed below. Work on the ODD-level original binary polynomial P_N(A,C), with every other ordinary face extracted to its original demand d=N-1.

Inputs are the local complex pressure theorem, its root-measure reconstruction, the original half-triangle potential and compact flow duality. No signed coefficient lower asymptotic is assumed. This note does not prove chi_D >= M_cap-Phi_D.

1. The positive-real gluing branch exists for every finite boundary ratio

Put A=exp(z), C=1; let I be the other ordinary faces, with weights exp(w_i). For the original exterior triangles define

ℱ(z,w)=∑tQt(L)−∑i∈Iwi. \mathcal F(z,w)=\sum_tQ_t(L)-\sum_{i\in I}w_i.\tag{1}

Each supported half-triangle has supply 1/2. Its entropy is continuous and bounded on its closed supported simplex; its weighted potential is the maximum of entropy plus its load/log-weight pairing. On positive weights Q_t is real analytic. A pure supported corner contributes a linear term and no Hessian; every other supported triangle has Hessian kernel precisely common scaling of its supported coordinates.

The graph joining ordinary vertices that share an exterior triangle is connected. Deleting the cap triangles removes only the ordinary edge AC from those triangles, and the triangle on the other side of AC remains in the exterior. Thus no edge of the connected ordinary graph is lost. The grounded Hessian in the I variables is positive definite at EVERY finite positive weight assignment, not only at z=0.

Here is the required global coercivity. Extend a vector v in R^I by v_A=v_C=0. Write alpha^0 for the actual strict exterior allocation at the given stationary point. Its I demands are one. The recession function in the extracted directions is

rec⁡(v)=∑t12maxf∈supp⁡tvf−∑i∈Ivi=∑t[12maxf∈supp⁡tvf−∑f∈supp⁡tαt,f0vf]. \begin{aligned} \operatorname{rec}(v) &=\sum_t\frac12\max_{f\in\operatorname{supp}t}v_f-\sum_{i\in I}v_i\\ &=\sum_t\left[\frac12\max_{f\in\operatorname{supp}t}v_f -\sum_{f\in\operatorname{supp}t}\alpha^0_{t,f}v_f\right]. \end{aligned}\tag{2}

Every summand is nonnegative. Equality requires all supported v_f in each nonmonomial triangle to be equal, since all its supported alpha^0 are strictly positive. Connectedness and the two fixed anchors force v=0. Hence rec(v)>=c||v|| for some c>0, by compactness of the unit sphere. The pure-vertex choices in each local potential give

ℱ(z,w)≥c‖w‖−C(1+|z|). \mathcal F(z,w)\ge c\lVert w\rVert-C(1+|z|).\tag{3}

uniformly for z in compact real intervals. This also covers the empty I case with its empty minimization convention. Entropy boundedness is enough here; no global coefficient asymptotic is being invoked.

Consequently (1) has one finite minimizer w(z) for every real z. Its grounded Hessian is invertible, so the implicit-function theorem makes w(z) and

Fcl(z)=minwℱ(z,w). F_{\mathrm{cl}}(z)=\min_w\mathcal F(z,w).\tag{4}

real analytic on the WHOLE real line. This is actual positive gluing with the original I demands. It is not an assertion that its planar developing map remains embedded for all z.

Let mu_D be the probability measure reconstructed from the local pressure germ in the original pressure/root-measure theorem. The function

Fμ(z)=Mcap+∫[0,1]log⁡(1−p+pez)dμD(p). F_\mu(z)=M_{\mathrm{cap}}+\int_{[0,1]} \log(1-p+pe^z)\,d\mu_D(p).\tag{5}

is real analytic on all R (locally the denominator is uniformly away from zero). The local theorem says F_cl=F_mu near zero. The identity theorem for real analytic functions therefore gives

Fcl(z)=Fμ(z)(z∈ℝ). F_{\mathrm{cl}}(z)=F_\mu(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb R).\tag{6}

This continuation uses coercivity and the finite positive gluing system, not a remote every-degree coefficient limit or an assumed global complex saddle.

2. The exact residual interval is [0,1]

Let R(s) retain precisely the original residual targets A:s, C:1-s, all I demands one and both protected demands zero. Its full feasible interval is [0,1]. To see the endpoint assertion, multiply the transportation problem by two: each exterior triangle supplies one, each I face demands two, and A,C together demand two. This is an ordinary bipartite network-flow polytope with integral supplies and demands (one may route both A,C into a common final sink). Integral vertices attain the linear minimum and maximum of the A load. In the original normalization these two extrema lie in {0,1/2,1}.

The strict cap exterior realizes s=nu<1/2. The original complete packing realizes a residual s>1/2: the restored cap’s uAC triangle has A load alpha_A in (0,1/2), so its exterior A demand is 1-alpha_A in (1/2,1). Thus the two extrema are 0 and 1. Convexity supplies every intervening value. This uses the literal half-supply network and the original complete packing, not a new mark or source.

Compact concave duality now gives, including both endpoints,

Fcl(z)=max0≤s≤1{R(s)+sz},R(s)=infz∈ℝ{Fcl(z)−sz}. \begin{aligned} F_{\mathrm{cl}}(z)&=\max_{0\le s\le1}\{R(s)+sz\},\\ R(s)&=\inf_{z\in\mathbb R}\{F_{\mathrm{cl}}(z)-sz\}. \end{aligned}\tag{7}

The second formula follows also by closed concave biconjugacy; R is upper semicontinuous and concave on its compact feasible interval. In particular R(s)=M_cap-I_mu(s), where I_mu is the Legendre transform of the normalized integral in (5).

The limiting derivatives of (7) as z tends to minus/plus infinity are 0 and 1. Differentiating (5) and using dominated convergence gives

μD({1})=limz→−∞Fcl′(z)=0,1−μD({0})=limz→+∞Fcl′(z)=1. \begin{aligned} \mu_D(\{1\})&=\lim_{z\to-\infty}F_{\mathrm{cl}}'(z)=0,\\ 1-\mu_D(\{0\})&=\lim_{z\to+\infty}F_{\mathrm{cl}}'(z)=1. \end{aligned}\tag{8}

Thus neither endpoint carries a root atom. This is stronger than merely knowing the stationary mean nu or the finite polynomial’s nonzero endpoint coefficients.

3. The selected circle-star equations give an algebraic boundary phase

Write w=exp(z)>0 and m_D(w)=F_cl’(log w). For each nonmonomial exterior triangle t on supported positive weights a,b,c put

Tt2=ab+ac+bc,Tt>0,Zt,f=Tt+iLfTt−iLf=exp⁡(2πiαt,f). \begin{gathered} T_t^2=ab+ac+bc,\qquad T_t>0,\\ Z_{t,f}=\frac{T_t+iL_f}{T_t-iL_f} =\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{t,f}). \end{gathered}\tag{9}

The same formula applies with exactly two positive weights. A pure supported corner has alpha=1/2 and is represented separately by the constant factor -1; a zero corner contributes 1. No zero T is divided out. The exact extracted-face equations are

∏t∋fZt,f=1(f∈I). \prod_{t\ni f}Z_{t,f}=1\qquad(f\in I).\tag{10}

The branch is specified by the original load equations sum_t alpha=1, the positive T_t, and the actual stationary weights at w=1. The algebraic equations alone need not specify those integer lifts.

After denominators are cleared, (9)-(10), L_A=w, L_C=1 form a finite polynomial system with Gaussian-rational coefficients. The Jacobian in the extracted log weights of (10) is a nonzero diagonal factor times the grounded Hessian. The nonmonomial T equations have nonzero T derivatives. Hence the selected branch is a regular algebraic curve over the w line. Its weight coordinates and its boundary product

BD(w)=∏t∋AZt,A=exp⁡(2πimD(w)). B_D(w)=\prod_{t\ni A}Z_{t,A} =\exp(2\pi i m_D(w)).\tag{11}

are algebraic functions of w. Equivalently there is a nonzero source-boundary eliminant

𝒜D(w,BD(w))=0. \mathcal A_D(w,B_D(w))=0.\tag{12}

It may be obtained by saturating by the nonzero denominators and Jacobian and selecting the regular component carrying the positive branch. Eliminating a higher-dimensional extraneous component without this selection is not the asserted construction. Gaussian-rational polynomial equations suffice; taking Galois conjugates if needed gives an eliminant over their ground field.

This is an algebraic boundary ANGLE response of the original source disk. It is NOT identified here with the ordinary knot A-polynomial, nor with a primitive meridian/longitude pair of the drilled group. That additional peripheral dictionary has not been proved. The phase B alone forgets an integer; the actual lift m is fixed by its positive continuation and the endpoint values 0 and 1 from (8).

4. The root measure is absolutely continuous with a log-algebraic density

Push mu_D forward by r=(1-p)/p to a probability measure sigma_D on (0,infty); (8) removes the endpoint masses. For w off the negative real axis the pressure derivative has the actual Stieltjes form

mD(w)=∫(0,∞)ww+rdσD(r). m_D(w)=\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{w}{w+r}\,d\sigma_D(r).\tag{13}

This holomorphic function continues the positive-real m from (11). For Im w>0 it has strictly positive imaginary part, since Im[w/(w+r)]=r Im(w)/|w+r|^2. Thus

BD(w)=exp⁡(2πimD(w)),0<|BD(w)|<1. B_D(w)=\exp(2\pi i m_D(w)),\qquad 0<|B_D(w)|<1.\tag{14}

in the upper half-plane. The algebraic relation (12), valid near the positive axis, holds throughout that half-plane by holomorphic continuation. It fixes one selected algebraic branch there. On the positive axis |B|=1; the reflected branch obeys B(conj w)=1/conj(B(w)). No branch is reset on the negative axis.

There can be no atom at any finite r0>0. If an atom has mass a>0, then at w=-r0+i y, y>0,

Im⁡mD(−r0+iy)≥ar0y,|BD(−r0+iy)|≤exp⁡(−2πar0y). \operatorname{Im}m_D(-r_0+iy)\ge\frac{ar_0}{y},\qquad |B_D(-r_0+iy)|\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{2\pi ar_0}{y}\right).\tag{15}

A nonzero algebraic branch near a finite point has a Puiseux leading term c(w+r0)^beta and hence a power-law lower bound along this ray. It cannot satisfy (15). This contradiction excludes every interior atom, without assuming any smooth limiting density in advance.

Away from a finite set of algebraic branch, zero and pole points on the negative axis, the upper branch B is analytic and nonzero in a neighborhood. There one can choose Log B and write m=Log B/(2pi i)+an integer, with the SAME continued integer lift. Stieltjes inversion applied to m(w)/w gives the real-analytic density

dσD(r)dr=Im⁡mD(−r+i0)πr=−log⁡|BD(−r+i0)|2π2r. \frac{d\sigma_D(r)}{dr} =\frac{\operatorname{Im}m_D(-r+i0)}{\pi r} =-\frac{\log|B_D(-r+i0)|}{2\pi^2r}.\tag{16}

Explicitly, -Im[m(x+i y)/(x+i y)]/pi is the convolution of sigma_D with y/[pi((x+r)2+y2)]. Integrate it over a compact negative interval and let y decrease to0. The elementary Poisson approximate-identity limit gives that interval’s measure; the analytic boundary values there give exactly (16). Endpoint atoms have already been excluded.

There is no singular continuous part: outside that finite set the measure has (16), while a measure supported on the remaining finite set is atomic and has already been excluded. Returning to p yields

dμD(p)dp=−log⁡|BD(−1−pp+i0)|2π2p(1−p),0<p<1. \frac{d\mu_D(p)}{dp} =-\frac{\log\left|B_D\!\left(-\frac{1-p}{p}+i0\right)\right|} {2\pi^2p(1-p)},\qquad 0<p<1.\tag{17}

The density is nonnegative, integrates to one, and is piecewise real analytic. At an interior exceptional point it has at worst a logarithmic singularity: a Puiseux zero of B becomes a multiple of log|r-r0| in (16). A boundary pole is incompatible with (14).

For completeness, at r=0 and infinity, (8) gives m->0 and m->1, respectively, so B tends to the nonzero value1. Its nonconstant algebraic Puiseux expansion gives m=O(w^a) at0 and m-1=O(w^-b) at infinity for some a,b>0 on the selected branches. Consequently the endpoint density bounds are O(p^(b-1)) and O((1-p)^(a-1)). In particular both logarithmic endpoint moments are finite. These assertions concern the actual fixed disk; they give no uniform exponent over varying diagrams.

5. The same algebraic branch determines the exact missing comparison

Let f_D(w)=int log(1-p+p w) dmu_D(p), continued from positive w. Then f_D(1)=0, and on the slit plane

dfD=mD(w)dww=[Log⁡BD(w)2πi+the retained integer]dww. df_D=m_D(w)\frac{dw}{w} =\left[\frac{\operatorname{Log}B_D(w)}{2\pi i} +\text{the retained integer}\right]\frac{dw}{w}.\tag{18}

Thus the original pressure difference is a logarithmic integral on the selected algebraic boundary curve. Its normalization is fixed at w=1, not by an arbitrary integration constant. Calling (18) the volume of a knot representation would need a separate group and relative-chain proof.

Put q(z)=Q(0,exp(z),1). Its original normalization is

q(z)=2πTi⁡2(ez/2),q′(z)=arctan⁡(ez/2)π,Ti⁡2(x)=∫0xarctan⁡ttdt. \begin{gathered} q(z)=\frac2\pi\operatorname{Ti}_2(e^{z/2}),\qquad q'(z)=\frac{\arctan(e^{z/2})}{\pi},\\ \operatorname{Ti}_2(x)=\int_0^x\frac{\arctan t}{t}\,dt. \end{gathered}\tag{19}

Restoring the two original cap triangles and their two target logs gives the exact full-capacity formula

ΦD−Mcap=infz∈ℝ{fD(ez)+q(z)−z}. \Phi_D-M_{\mathrm{cap}} =\inf_{z\in\mathbb R}\{f_D(e^z)+q(z)-z\}.\tag{20}

It is equivalent to the original R(s)+2f(s-1/2) formula, by (7), and does not replace either face target.

There is an exact algebraic critical-point and absolute-value statement here, beyond an unspecified minimization. Put

Bcap(w)=1+iw1−iw,acap(w)=arctan⁡wπ. B_{\mathrm{cap}}(w)=\frac{1+i\sqrt w}{1-i\sqrt w},\qquad a_{\mathrm{cap}}(w)=\frac{\arctan\sqrt w}{\pi}.\tag{20a}

The derivative of the potential in (20) with respect to log w is m_D(w)+a_cap(w)-1. It is strictly increasing: the first derivative has positive Bernoulli variance and the cap derivative is strictly positive. Its limits are -1 and 1/2 by (8). Therefore there is exactly one positive critical point w_, and w_>1 because m_D(1)+a_cap(1)-1=nu-3/4<0. At this point

BD(w*)Bcap(w*)=1,mD(w*)+acap(w*)=1. B_D(w_*)B_{\mathrm{cap}}(w_*)=1,\qquad m_D(w_*)+a_{\mathrm{cap}}(w_*)=1.\tag{20b}

The second equality is the retained lift: the sum lies in (0,3/2) for positive w, so the only possible integer at a solution is1. The selected positive solution is algebraic, since (12),(20a) are algebraic and the critical point is isolated (the derivative is strictly positive). Its finite exterior weights restore the original complete A and C demands; every other original demand was already imposed. Thus it is the original complete circle packing.

With C0=q(0)=2G/pi, where q’s argument here is the log weight z and G is Catalan’s constant, the exact relative logarithmic period is

ΦD=Mcap+C0+∫1w*[mD(w)+acap(w)−1]dww. \Phi_D=M_{\mathrm{cap}}+C_0 +\int_1^{w_*}\bigl[m_D(w)+a_{\mathrm{cap}}(w)-1\bigr]\frac{dw}{w}. \tag{20c}

Equivalently the integrand is Log_lift(B_D B_cap)/(2pi i), with lift 2pi i[m_D+a_cap-1], not independently reset principal logarithms. The value is the original drilled hyperbolic volume divided by2pi by the already proved complete packing/capacity theorem. This is an absolute geometric consequence of that theorem and the specified constant M_cap+C0; it does not require a new representation-volume identification of (18), identify a knot A-polynomial, or prove a filled quantum limit.

For r>0 let U(r)=Re f_D(-r+i0). The finite original two negative-axis suprema correspond, in precisely the degree-one normalization, to

−χD=supr>0{U(r)−max⁡(0,log⁡r)}. -\chi_D=\sup_{r>0}\{U(r)-\max(0,\log r)\}.\tag{21}

with the continuous limiting values at r=0,infinity included. The endpoint power and interior logarithmic bounds after (17) justify these finite limits; the original supremum-rate theorem already handles them without needing this additional regularity.

Equations (18)-(21) put the proposed universal comparison on ONE selected, source-normalized algebraic boundary branch:

supr>0{Re⁡fD(−r+i0)−log⁡+r}≤infz∈ℝ{fD(ez)+q(z)−z}. \sup_{r>0}\{\operatorname{Re}f_D(-r+i0)-\log_+r\} \le\inf_{z\in\mathbb R}\{f_D(e^z)+q(z)-z\}.\tag{22}

This note does not prove (22). It proves that the previously arbitrary root measure is constrained by the actual finite circle-star system, has no atoms, and is reconstructed by (17). The known one-cap original chain only bounds the right-hand deficit by W(nu)/pi minus its fixed peripheral gap. It does not identify the logarithmic curve integral on the left with that representation volume or pay that deficit.

In particular no global embedding of the positive exterior for all real z, no analytic continuation of an individual complex saddle through every branch point, no knot A-polynomial identification, and no signed original coefficient lower rate is used or asserted.
