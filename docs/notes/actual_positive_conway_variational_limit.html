A whole-source variational limit for every positive Conway expression

Later geometric result, 29 October 2026: the actual cylindrical Conway theorem now identifies this maximum with the complete hyperbolic volume for every retained cylindrical factor after cap-sum reduction. Its proof supplies the actual ordered cell incidence and complete cusps. Cap products keep their connected-sum scope; arbitrary knots and fillings are not included. The earlier geometric gap below is retained as the scope of this original variational argument.

29 October 2026. Every fixed expression in the actual complete-circle Conway class has a full-sequence exponential limit. The limit is the maximum of an explicit concave sum of ideal-tetrahedron volumes on a rational polytope. This uses the original source, at both parities, including all circle colors and repeated-root normal directions.

The tetrahedral angle triples below are not yet a triangulation of the actual complement. Identifying their maximum with its hyperbolic volume remains a separate geometric task. In fact some expressions have zero exponential rate, so hyperbolicity cannot be inferred from their positive coefficients.

1. The actual expressions and the fixed rational polytope

Use the literal four-port compilation and exposed original closure of the Conway source theorem. Write h_N=Rturn L_N, A_N=h_N^3, d=N-1 and M=floor(d/2). Fix an expression generated by A, genuine quarter-turn and product. Expand each A into its three h leaves and two product gates. Repeated copies are distinct vertices of this finite expression tree.

Attach a real label x in [0,1] to every output wire. Define P_E by:

  • At an h leaf, 0<=x<=1/2.

  • At a turn with input x and output y, x+y=1.

  • At a product with inputs x,y and output z,

    max(x,y)<=z<=x+y<=1.

  • The final phi closure fixes its output label to 1.

This is a compact rational polytope. Its exact level-d lattice points are integer wire labels satisfying the SAME homogeneous inequalities after multiplying all constants by d. In particular 2r<=d at a leaf is exactly r<=M, and a product has i+j<=d, not i+j<=N. Thus

states at level N = (d P_E) intersect Z^(number of wires). (1)

Every state has strictly positive weight. Its leaf factor is the actual coefficient

a_(N,v)=N sum_(j=v)^M k_j s_(j+v)/(s_(j-v)s_v^2),
s_r=product_(u=1)^r 2sin(pi u/N),
k_j=2 at paired nodes, 1 at an odd simple node.

Its product factor is the actual full-Newton coefficient

mu_(i,j,k)=s_(i+j)s_k/
             (s_i s_j s_(k-i)s_(k-j)s_(i+j-k)). (2)

Turn and closure factors equal one on their stated constraints. The exact positive partition Z_E,N is phi(E_N), not a comparison model. The previously proved physical identities identify it with the whole original raw source of the compiled ribbon diagram.

2. A uniform action on the entire polytope

Put L(t)=integral_0^t log(2sin(pi u))du on [0,1], extended continuously to both endpoints. Set

g(x)=-2L(x)-2L(1/2-x),
m(x,y,z)=L(x+y)+L(z)-L(x)-L(y)
              -L(z-x)-L(z-y)-L(x+y-z).

Define A_E to be the sum of g over its leaves and m over its product gates. Turns contribute zero. This is one function on P_E with all shared labels retained; its factors are not maximized independently.

Uniformly for every exact state with scaled labels r/d,

log(weight)=N A_E(r/d)+O_E(log N).             (3)

For the leaf, z_j(v)=s_(j+v)/(s_(j-v)s_v^2) is increasing in j:

z_(j+1)(v)/z_j(v)
  =sin(pi(j+v+1)/N)/sin(pi(j-v+1)/N)>=1.

Indeed the difference of these sines is 2cos(pi(j+1)/N)sin(pi v/N)>=0 in the actual range. Therefore

N z_M(v)<=a_(N,v)<=N^2 z_M(v).                (4)

The uniform estimate log s_r=N L(r/N)+O(log N), including endpoints, gives the claimed g action from (4). Here M/N differs from 1/2 by O(1/N), and r/N differs from r/d by O(1/N). The modulus of continuity of L changes N L by O(log N) under these shifts, even at sine zeros. The same estimate applied directly to (2) gives its m action. There are a fixed number of factors, proving (3).

3. Integer support supplies an all-level lower bound

It is insufficient to approximate a maximum only along denominator subsequences. The exact support here is an additive semigroup in (d,wire labels): sums of two feasible integer labelings are feasible at the sum of their levels d. Every displayed constraint is linear and homogeneous in these variables, including 2r<=d and turn sums.

Moreover a feasible integer labeling exists for EVERY d>=2 and every prescribed root coordinate r in [0,d]. Here is a constructive proof. At a seed h^3, distribute r among three integers <=floor(d/2); this is possible since 3floor(d/2)>=d. Choose the upper output index at both products. At a turn use the child coordinate d-r. At a product of two already compiled expressions, use child coordinates r and 0 and output r. All chosen product sums are <=d. This proves feasibility by induction, including r=d for the final closure.

Take any rational y in P_E and a positive integer D such that Dy is an integer vector. For each residue modulo D choose d_r>=2 in that residue and a fixed feasible integer labeling b_r at level d_r. For all sufficiently large d in that residue, d=kD+d_r with k>=0, and

n_d=k(Dy)+b_r

is an exact integer state at level d. Its normalized label n_d/d tends to y as d tends to infinity through that residue. There are only finitely many residues. Thus every rational y is approached by actual states along the ENTIRE sequence of sufficiently large levels.

Rational points are dense in the nonempty rational polytope P_E: express a point as a convex combination of rational vertices and approximate its coefficients by rational simplex coefficients. Continuity of A_E therefore lets one choose rational y arbitrarily close to its maximum value, even when a maximum is on a boundary.

There are at most N to a fixed power states. Equation (3) gives the upper bound by max A_E. The single sequence n_d just constructed gives liminf at least A_E(y). Taking rational y with values approaching the maximum proves the unconditional full-source limit

lim_(N->infinity) (1/N)log Z_E,N=max_(P_E) A_E. (5)

No parity subsequence, Gaussian hypothesis, strict interior maximum, or lower-overlap conjecture is needed. Positivity and the additive integer support are what supply the every-level lower bound here.

4. A finite concave ideal-angle volume problem

Let V(a,b,c) be the ideal-tetrahedron volume with dihedral angles pi a,pi b,pi c, for nonnegative a+b+c=1, including its continuous zero-volume boundary. Then

pi g(x)=2V(x,1/2-x,1/2).

For a product put p=z-x, q=z-y, r=x+y-z, w=1-x-y. They are nonnegative and p+q+2r+w=1. The exact identity is

pi m(x,y,z)=V(p+r,q+r,w)
              +V(p,q,2r+w)+V(p+q,r,r+w).      (6)

It follows by substituting V=-pi sum L and L(1-t)=-L(t), as in the cuboctahedral period proof. All displayed triples have sum one on the whole allowed domain. Thus V_E=pi A_E is an explicitly prescribed sum of tetrahedral volumes with affine angles, and (5) becomes

lim_(N->infinity) (2pi/N)log Z_E,N=2 max_(P_E) V_E. (7)

Each tetrahedral volume is concave on its angle simplex; consequently V_E is concave on P_E. This is a finite convex optimization problem for -V_E. Strictness or an interior maximizer requires its own check; the all-level limit (7) does not depend on either assertion.

5. Algebraic critical equations and the exact geometric gap

On any selected face of P_E, omit angle terms identically zero there. At a critical point where the remaining angles are positive, write A_E=sum_b sigma_b L(ell_b(x)), with integer sigma_b and rational affine ell_b. For an integer basis vector v of that face’s tangent space, constrained stationarity is exactly

product_b (2sin(pi ell_b(x)))^[sigma_b dell_b(v)]=1. (8)

The exponents are integers in the unreduced wire coordinates. Set u_j=exp(pi i x_j). Each 2sin(pi ell_b) is a Laurent expression in the u_j with coefficients in Q(i), since the only affine constants are integers and half-integers. The linear face equalities likewise become monomial equations. Clearing denominators makes (8) polynomial. The actual angle branches and positive sine signs remain selected; the resulting algebraic equations alone do not choose them.

In particular an isolated complex solution of these finite equations has algebraic phase coordinates. Its displayed tetrahedral shapes, obtained from their angle sine rules, are algebraic as well. This is a precise finite algebraic critical-period construction. It does not by itself identify this circuit ideal with a knot’s A-polynomial.

The later quotient-Hessian theorem removes the extra isolation hypothesis at a global maximum: after restricting to its minimal face and removing the rational flat kernel, the Hessian supplies isolation automatically. Thus all nonflat shapes at every robust source maximum are algebraic, even with boundary or nonunique wire coordinates. Its conjugate-antisymmetrized symbol sum is moreover a rational Bloch class with this maximum as regulator. These statements still do not provide the actual manifold incidence.

Nor do (6)–(8) supply the missing global face identifications: they give positive angle triples, not yet the actual edge/peripheral equations of the diagram complement. A future geometric comparison must use the full source-volume certificate, including the absolute normalization and all angle tangent directions.

Two calibrations clarify the scope. The seed closure phi(h_N^3) is the whole untwisted theta source, already proved to have actual volume 4v8; hence its maximum V_E is 2v8. But phi(Rturn(h_N^3)) is the constant Newton coefficient of h_N^3, exactly (N2)3=N^6. Its limit and the maximum in (7) are zero. No claim that every expression is hyperbolic can be appended to this variational theorem.

Audit: exact support and positive partition checks and results supplement (1)–(8). They verify 800 additive integer support cases, 1008 all-residue approximations, and ten full positive-partition levels, including an independent whole-theta formula and the exact polynomial turned closures. Numerical rates are diagnostics, not the proof of (5).

No manifold-volume claim for arbitrary Conway expressions follows from this variational result.
