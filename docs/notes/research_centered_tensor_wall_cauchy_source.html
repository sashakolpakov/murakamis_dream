Exact wall values and a Cauchy representation of the centered source

21 November 2026. Research note. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

Fixing one generating leg at any physical norm-wall point gives an explicit product for the entire original three-boundary source. This product is nonzero at every level when the other two arguments lie in the open unit disk. Partial fractions then give a canonical finite Cauchy representation of the full source, without a derivative of a hypergeometric sum.

In the bulk of that wall, the product has a uniform asymptotic with the initialized dilogarithmic action and a known nonzero amplitude. The other two arguments may range over arbitrary compact complex subsets of the disk. This is additional actual source control; transport from this wall representation through the whole interior still requires a separate estimate.

2. Every wall slice is an explicit nonzero product

Theorem 1. For every 0≤j<N0\le j<N and y,z∈𝔻y,z\in\mathbb D, 𝒞N(Xj,y,z)=κNDN(y)DN(z)(Q;Q)j(Qyz;Q)j(−qy;Q)j+1(−qz;Q)j+1.(7) \boxed{\quad \mathcal C_N(X_j,y,z)= \frac{\kappa}{\sqrt N}D_N(y)D_N(z) \frac{(Q;Q)_j(Qyz;Q)_j} {(-qy;Q)_{j+1}(-qz;Q)_{j+1}} . \quad} \qquad\text{(7)} In particular each of these wall slices is nonzero on the whole 𝔻2\mathbb D^2, at every level and for both parities.

The two ends have simple checks in the original source: 𝒞N(X0,y,z)=κNDN(y)DN(z)(1+qy)(1+qz),𝒞N(Xd,y,z)=κN1−(yz)N1−yz.(11) \mathcal C_N(X_0,y,z)= \frac{\kappa}{\sqrt N} \frac{D_N(y)D_N(z)}{(1+qy)(1+qz)},\qquad \mathcal C_N(X_d,y,z)= \kappa\sqrt N\,\frac{1-(yz)^N}{1-yz}. \qquad\text{(11)} The second follows from (Q;Q)d=N(Q;Q)_d=N and the complete cyclic products in (7). These are the marked and unit end slices; the intervening products retain all original boundary directions.

The last wall step must not be made homogeneous. Extending the product in (7) formally to j=Nj=N would insert (Q;Q)N=0(Q;Q)_N=0, although XN=X0X_N=X_0 and (9) is nonzero. Thus a periodic interpolation of the factorial weights cannot silently erase this missing cyclic arrow.

3. A canonical derivative-free Cauchy period

For x,y,z∈𝔻x,y,z\in\mathbb D, put 𝒞̂N=𝒞NDN(x)DN(y)DN(z).(12) \widehat{\mathcal C}_N= \frac{\mathcal C_N}{D_N(x)D_N(y)D_N(z)} . \qquad\text{(12)} The exact finite representation is 𝒞̂N(x,y,z)=κN3/2(1+qy)(1+qz)∑j=0N−1(Q;Q)j(Qyz;Q)j(−q3y;Q)j(−q3z;Q)j11+q−1Q−jx.(13) \boxed{\quad \widehat{\mathcal C}_N(x,y,z)= \frac{\kappa}{N^{3/2}(1+qy)(1+qz)} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \frac{(Q;Q)_j(Qyz;Q)_j} {(-q^3y;Q)_j(-q^3z;Q)_j} \frac1{1+q^{-1}Q^{-j}x}. \quad} \qquad\text{(13)} For a proof, 𝒞N(x,y,z)\mathcal C_N(x,y,z) has degree below NN in xx. Thus the wall residues and the complete finite Cauchy period are prescribed, rather than fitted to an unknown source. Every pole in (13) has nonzero residue when y,z∈𝔻y,z\in\mathbb D. The full polynomial 𝒞N\mathcal C_N is regular at these points; one must use (7), not evaluate (12) without canceling its norm factor. Multiplication by all three DND_N’s recovers the original source inside the disk. No derivative, scalar phase, or first Casimir normal has been omitted in passing from (3) to this representation.

5. What the wall data do and do not control

Equation (13) is an exact, initialized Cauchy representation of the whole universal tensor. Equation (18) gives a known nonzero geometric principal amplitude on every strict bulk wall slice, even for arbitrary compact complex values of the other two legs. The result is not obtained by assigning a saddle coefficient.

It does not justify inserting only (18) term by term in (13) and then discarding cancellation among its NN terms. The two endpoint layers jj near zero or dd are outside the stated uniform wall asymptotic. A contour interpolation of the factorial weights must retain their finite initialization and the forced last arrow described after (11). An unchecked periodic quantum-gamma interpolation could change the source.

In particular no estimate here transports the growing packet along an arbitrary interior radial path. Nor does it move the assembled Conway coefficient cycle or prove a lower bound after general short fillings. Those questions require estimates for the complete Cauchy period and the original global gluing.
