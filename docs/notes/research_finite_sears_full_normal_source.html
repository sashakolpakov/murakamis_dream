A finite Sears certificate for the actual local full-normal source

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The terminating Sears expression for a physical kernel row has a four-factorial lift. An explicit finite telescoper identifies its degree derivative with the original consumed-normal representative. Consequently appropriate local cutoffs give all four physical jets of the original kernel, including its independent mixed normal. The diagonal factorial divisor is retained throughout.

This is an exact local source statement. It does not bound a tall wire contour, eliminate its endpoint packets, or assert that one cutoff is holomorphic on the whole angular box.

1. Original data and the canceled finite row

Set N≥2,D=N−1,θ=π/N,q=eiθ,Q=q2,sz=2sin⁡(θz),c(z)=2cos⁡((2z+1)θ),c0=q+q−1.(1) N\ge2,\quad D=N-1,\quad \theta=\pi/N,\quad q=e^{i\theta}, \quad Q=q^2,\quad s_z=2\sin(\theta z), \quad c(z)=2\cos((2z+1)\theta),\quad c_0=q+q^{-1}. \qquad\text{(1)} All powers of QQ use Qz=e2iθzQ^z=e^{2i\theta z}. The original kernel and physical rows are KN(z,w)=∑k=0Dek(z)ek(w),Fj(w)=KN(c(j),w),0≤j≤J=⌊D/2⌋.(2) K_N(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{D}e_k(z)e_k(w),\qquad F_j(w)=K_N(c(j),w),\qquad 0\le j\le J=\lfloor D/2\rfloor. \qquad\text{(2)} A first spectral normal is required only for 0≤j≤Jd=⌊(N−2)/2⌋0\le j\le J_d=\lfloor(N-2)/2\rfloor.

Let G=GNG=G_N be the specified canonical factorial in the pinned factorial source. Its normalization and exact shift are G(0)=1,G(z+1)=sz+1G(z).(3) G(0)=1,\qquad G(z+1)=s_{z+1}G(z). \qquad\text{(3)} It is a holomorphic unit on −1<ℜz<N-1<\Re z<N. Its finite logarithmic derivative formula also gives the exact reflection G(z)G(D−z)=N.(4) G(z)G(D-z)=N. \qquad\text{(4)} Indeed that formula is invariant under z↦D−zz\mapsto D-z, so the derivative of the logarithm of the left side is zero. At zero its value is G(D)=∏r=1N−12sin⁡(rθ)=NG(D)=\prod_{r=1}^{N-1}2\sin(r\theta)=N. Continuation proves (4) wherever defined. From (3), GG has a simple pole at each −1,…,−D-1,\ldots,-D, and its reciprocal is holomorphic with a simple zero there. These are local divisor statements, not an assertion that the reciprocal is entire.

For an angular variable uu, put Ck±(u)=∏r=0k−1cos⁡(u/2±(r+1/2)θ)sin⁡((r+1)θ),C0±=1.(5) C_k^\pm(u)=\prod_{r=0}^{k-1} \frac{\cos(u/2\pm(r+1/2)\theta)}{\sin((r+1)\theta)}, \qquad C_0^\pm=1. \qquad\text{(5)} Then the exact physical row has the canceled convolution Fj(−2cos⁡u)=∑k=0j[Ck+(u)Cj−k−(u)]2.(6) \boxed{\quad F_j(-2\cos u) =\sum_{k=0}^j[C_k^+(u)C_{j-k}^-(u)]^2.\quad} \qquad\text{(6)} In particular there are no spectral denominators in this finite formula. It is even in uu, by interchanging kk and j−kj-k.

Here is the normalization directly from the finite Sears identity. Write w=v+v−1w=v+v^{-1}, with v=−eiuv=-e^{iu}. Converting the original Newton products to Pochhammers gives Fj(w)=4ϕ3(Q−j,Qj+1,qv,q/v;Q,Q,Q;Q,Q).(7) F_j(w)={}_4\phi_3(Q^{-j},Q^{j+1},qv,q/v;Q,Q,Q;Q,Q). \qquad\text{(7)} The pinned finite identity, retaining the parameter qvqv, gives Fj(w)=(qv)j[(q/v;Q)j(Q;Q)j]2∑k=0jQk[(Q−j;Q)k(qv;Q)k(Q;Q)k(qvQ−j;Q)k]2.(8) F_j(w)=(qv)^j\left[\frac{(q/v;Q)_j}{(Q;Q)_j}\right]^2 \sum_{k=0}^j Q^k \left[\frac{(Q^{-j};Q)_k(qv;Q)_k} {(Q;Q)_k(qvQ^{-j};Q)_k}\right]^2. \qquad\text{(8)} This first holds away from its apparent spectral poles. The phase in each quotient of four factors 1−eiφ1-e^{i\varphi} is e−iθe^{-i\theta}; its square cancels QQ. The phase in the prefactor similarly cancels (qv)j(qv)^j. Its cosine product has indices 0,…,j−10,\ldots,j-1. The denominator cosine product of the kk-th term has precisely its last kk indices, in reverse order. Canceling them, and then canceling the corresponding sine factorials, gives (6). Thus (6) also holds at every removable spectral pole of (8). No phase or scalar carrier is left over.

2. The four-factorial lift and its necessary local domain

For independent complex degree variables α,β\alpha,\beta, define the meromorphic summand Tk(α,β)=G(α+β−k)2G(α−k)2G(β−k)2G(k)2,0≤k<N.(9) T_k(\alpha,\beta)= \frac{G(\alpha+\beta-k)^2} {G(\alpha-k)^2G(\beta-k)^2G(k)^2},\qquad 0\le k<N. \qquad\text{(9)} It is symmetric in α,β\alpha,\beta. By (4), equivalently Tk(α,β)=N2[G(k)G(α−k)G(β−k)G(D−α−β+k)]2.(10) T_k(\alpha,\beta)= \frac{N^2} {[G(k)G(\alpha-k)G(\beta-k) G(D-\alpha-\beta+k)]^2}. \qquad\text{(10)} These are identities of meromorphic functions; apparent factors are combined before a limiting value is taken.

For β=D/2−Nu/(2π)\beta=D/2-Nu/(2\pi), one has c(β)=−2cos⁡uc(\beta)=-2\cos u. The shift formula gives Ck+(u)=G(β)G(β−k)G(k),Cl−(u)=G(β+l)G(β)G(l).(11) C_k^+(u)=\frac{G(\beta)}{G(\beta-k)G(k)},\qquad C_l^-(u)=\frac{G(\beta+l)}{G(\beta)G(l)}. \qquad\text{(11)} Consequently (6) is exactly Fj(c(β))=∑k=0jTk(j,β).(12) F_j(c(\beta))=\sum_{k=0}^jT_k(j,\beta). \qquad\text{(12)} Its interpretation at exceptional factors is the holomorphic continuation supplied by (6).

The diagonal divisor must not be suppressed. For example at (α,β)=(0,0)(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0), a term with 1≤k<N1\le k<N has a nonzero holomorphic unit times α2β2(α+β)2.(13) \frac{\alpha^2\beta^2}{(\alpha+\beta)^2}. \qquad\text{(13)} Separate vanishing in either variable therefore does not give a joint holomorphic germ there.

We will use the finite cutoff SM(α,β)=∑k=0MTk(α,β).(14) S_M(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{k=0}^MT_k(\alpha,\beta). \qquad\text{(14)} At a physical pair (j,l)(j,l), the safe local condition is max⁡(j,l)≤M≤j+l,M<N.(15) \max(j,l)\le M\le j+l,\qquad M<N. \qquad\text{(15)} Every numerator argument in (9) then has nonnegative base value, and every reciprocal denominator is holomorphic near its base integer, which is less than NN. Thus (14) is jointly holomorphic on a neighborhood of that pair. Equivalently, a sufficient open restriction for the numerator is ℜ(α+β)>M−1\Re(\alpha+\beta)>M-1; all other factors retain their own local pole exclusions. No full-box assertion follows.

3. An explicit finite contiguous certificate

Use the original coefficients A(α)=sα+14s2α+1s2α+2,C(α)=sα4s2αs2α+1,C(0)=0.(16) A(\alpha)=\frac{s_{\alpha+1}^4}{s_{2\alpha+1}s_{2\alpha+2}}, \qquad C(\alpha)=\frac{s_\alpha^4}{s_{2\alpha}s_{2\alpha+1}}, \qquad C(0)=0. \qquad\text{(16)} The following is a meromorphic finite identity, so it can first be checked where all displayed denominators are nonzero.

Put X=Qα,Y=Qβ,z=Qk,s=QX,𝒟=(1−Xs)(1+s)(1+X),𝒦=q−1s2Y𝒟,ξ=z/s,(17) X=Q^\alpha,\quad Y=Q^\beta,\quad z=Q^k,\quad s=QX, \quad \mathcal D=(1-Xs)(1+s)(1+X), \quad \mathcal K=\frac{q^{-1}s^2}{Y\mathcal D},\quad \xi=z/s, \qquad\text{(17)} and define the Laurent polynomial 𝒫(z)=𝒦[2{(1+X)−(1+s)Y}ξ+(1+s)(1+X)(Y2−1)−4(X−s)Y+2Y{X(1+s)−s(1+X)Y}ξ−1].(18) \begin{split} \mathcal P(z)=\mathcal K\big[& 2\{(1+X)-(1+s)Y\}\xi\\ &+(1+s)(1+X)(Y^2-1)-4(X-s)Y\\ &+2Y\{X(1+s)-s(1+X)Y\}\xi^{-1}\big]. \end{split} \qquad\text{(18)} The exact flux is ℋk(α,β)=Tk(α,β)(1−Qk)2𝒫(Qk)(Qk−QX)2.(19) \mathcal H_k(\alpha,\beta)=T_k(\alpha,\beta) \frac{(1-Q^k)^2\mathcal P(Q^k)}{(Q^k-QX)^2}. \qquad\text{(19)} The summand and rational factor in (19) are combined before specializing. Proposition 1. Where its terms are defined, the finite sum obeys A(α)SM(α+1,β)+C(α)SM(α−1,β)−[A(α)+C(α)+c0−c(β)]SM(α,β)=ℋM+1(α,β).(20) \boxed{\begin{split} &A(\alpha)S_M(\alpha+1,\beta)+C(\alpha)S_M(\alpha-1,\beta)\\ &\quad-[A(\alpha)+C(\alpha)+c_0-c(\beta)]S_M(\alpha,\beta) =\mathcal H_{M+1}(\alpha,\beta). \end{split}} \qquad\text{(20)} It is enough here to use M+1<NM+1<N. The right side is the actual finite boundary, not an infinite-series limit.

Proof. The shift relation gives the three exact ratios Tk(α+1,β)Tk(α,β)=Y−1(z−QXY)2(z−QX)2,Tk(α−1,β)Tk(α,β)=Y(z−X)2(z−XY)2,(21) \frac{T_k(\alpha+1,\beta)}{T_k(\alpha,\beta)} =Y^{-1}\frac{(z-QXY)^2}{(z-QX)^2},\qquad \frac{T_k(\alpha-1,\beta)}{T_k(\alpha,\beta)} =Y\frac{(z-X)^2}{(z-XY)^2}, \qquad\text{(21)} Tk+1Tk=Q(z−X)2(z−Y)2(1−Qz)2(z−XY)2.(22) \frac{T_{k+1}}{T_k} =Q\frac{(z-X)^2(z-Y)^2}{(1-Qz)^2(z-XY)^2}. \qquad\text{(22)} Thus the flux difference divided by TkT_k is ℋk+1−ℋkTk=(z−Y)2𝒫(Qz)Q(z−XY)2−(1−z)2𝒫(z)(z−QX)2.(23) \frac{\mathcal H_{k+1}-\mathcal H_k}{T_k} =\frac{(z-Y)^2\mathcal P(Qz)}{Q(z-XY)^2} -\frac{(1-z)^2\mathcal P(z)}{(z-QX)^2}. \qquad\text{(23)} We verify that this equals the expression obtained from the left side of (20) using (21).

Here are explicit checks of the rational identity, including its simple poles. Write s=QXs=QX. The required values and logarithmic derivatives of 𝒫\mathcal P at its two relevant arguments are P0:=𝒫(s)=−AYs2(1−Y)2(1−s)2,P1:=𝒫(sY)=CQX2(1−Y)2Y(1−X)2,𝒫′(s)P0=2s(1−Y)+21−s,𝒫′(sY)P1=−2s(1−Y)+2QY(1−X).(24) \begin{split} P_0:=\mathcal P(s) &=-\frac{A}{Y}\frac{s^2(1-Y)^2}{(1-s)^2},\\ P_1:=\mathcal P(sY) &=\frac{CQX^2(1-Y)^2}{Y(1-X)^2},\\ \frac{\mathcal P'(s)}{P_0} &=\frac2{s(1-Y)}+\frac2{1-s},\\ \frac{\mathcal P'(sY)}{P_1} &=-\frac2{s(1-Y)}+\frac2{QY(1-X)}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(24)} They follow by substituting (18); equalities with a vanishing denominator mean their cleared versions. For completeness, the constant term of (23) is p0(Q−1−1)+2p1{Y(X−1)−(s−1)},𝒫(z)=p1z+p0+p−1/z.(26) p_0(Q^{-1}-1)+2p_1\{Y(X-1)-(s-1)\}, \qquad \mathcal P(z)=p_1z+p_0+p_{-1}/z. \qquad\text{(26)} Substituting (18) into (26) gives (C+q)Y+(A+q−1)Y−1−8qX(1+X)(1+QX).(27) (C+q)Y+(A+q^{-1})Y^{-1} -\frac{8qX}{(1+X)(1+QX)}. \qquad\text{(27)} Directly from (16), A=−q−1(1−QX)4(1−QX2)(1−Q2X2),C=−q(1−X)4(1−X2)(1−QX2),A+C+c0=8qX(1+X)(1+QX).(28) \begin{split} A&=-q^{-1}\frac{(1-QX)^4}{(1-QX^2)(1-Q^2X^2)},\\ C&=-q\frac{(1-X)^4}{(1-X^2)(1-QX^2)},\\ A+C+c_0&=\frac{8qX}{(1+X)(1+QX)}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(28)} Since c(β)=qY+q−1Y−1c(\beta)=qY+q^{-1}Y^{-1}, (27) is precisely the constant term required by (21). At a regular physical degree α=j\alpha=j, if M≥j+1M\ge j+1 and ℜ(α+β)>M\Re(\alpha+\beta)>M in the local neighborhood, the boundary in (20) is holomorphic and ℋM+1=O((α−j)2).(29) \mathcal H_{M+1}=O((\alpha-j)^2). \qquad\text{(29)} Indeed 1/G(α−M−1)21/G(\alpha-M-1)^2 has that double zero, the numerator in (9) is regular, and QM+1−Qj+1≠0Q^{M+1}-Q^{j+1}\ne0 for the stated finite range. The recurrence use below is for N≥3N\ge3, with all flux indices strictly below NN; the N=2N=2 source is handled directly.

4. The actual independent normal

Define the short degree derivative Hj(β)=∂α∑k=0jTk(α,β)|α=j.(30) H_j(\beta)= \left.\partial_\alpha\sum_{k=0}^jT_k(\alpha,\beta) \right|_{\alpha=j}. \qquad\text{(30)} This is not the derivative of a terminating sum whose upper limit has been differentiated. It is the derivative of the displayed fixed finite formula.

Let Q̃N\widetilde Q_N, RjR_j, and Ĝj\widehat G_j have exactly the normalization in the pinned physical residue source: Ĝj(w)=c′(0)Fj(w)Q̃N(w)+Rj(w)c′(j),c′(j)=−4θsin⁡((2j+1)θ).(31) \widehat G_j(w)= \frac{c'(0)F_j(w)\widetilde Q_N(w)+R_j(w)}{c'(j)}, \qquad c'(j)=-4\theta\sin((2j+1)\theta). \qquad\text{(31)} Here RjR_j is the common-shift Jacobi determinant derivative, with c0c_0 fixed, and c′(0)Q̃N(c(β))=2[gN′(β)−gN′(0)].(32) c'(0)\widetilde Q_N(c(\beta)) =2[g_N'(\beta)-g_N'(0)]. \qquad\text{(32)}

Theorem 2. For every double physical degree 0≤j≤Jd0\le j\le J_d, as an identity on the original β\beta-strip, Hj(β)=c′(0)Fj(c(β))Q̃N(c(β))+Rj(c(β))=c′(j)Ĝj(c(β)).(33) \boxed{\quad H_j(\beta) =c'(0)F_j(c(\beta))\widetilde Q_N(c(\beta)) +R_j(c(\beta)) =c'(j)\widehat G_j(c(\beta)).\quad} \qquad\text{(33)} In particular this retains the actual normal anchor, rather than only a formally differentiated homogeneous row equation.

Proof. At j=0j=0, (9) gives T0(α,β)=G(α+β)2G(α)2G(β)2,H0(β)=2[gN′(β)−gN′(0)],(34) T_0(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{G(\alpha+\beta)^2}{G(\alpha)^2G(\beta)^2}, \qquad H_0(\beta)=2[g_N'(\beta)-g_N'(0)], \qquad\text{(34)} which is exactly (32).

For a recurrence step at α=j\alpha=j, first choose M=j+1M=j+1 and an open β\beta-domain sufficiently far to the right of this finite cutoff, avoiding every displayed divisor. The finite shift identity (20) and (29) hold there with zero value and zero first α\alpha-derivative of the boundary. At each of the integer rows j−1,j,j+1j-1,j,j+1, the terms beyond that row have a double degree zero. Thus differentiating (20) gives A(j)Hj+1+C(j)Hj−1−[A(j)+C(j)+c0−c(β)]Hj=−A′(j)[Fj+1−Fj]−C′(j)[Fj−1−Fj],(35) \begin{split} A(j)H_{j+1}+C(j)H_{j-1} -[A(j)+C(j)+c_0-c(\beta)]H_j ={}&-A'(j)[F_{j+1}-F_j]\\ &-C'(j)[F_{j-1}-F_j], \end{split} \qquad\text{(35)} where all rows on the right are evaluated at c(β)c(\beta). The step at zero uses C(α)=O(α3)C(\alpha)=O(\alpha^3), so its backward contribution and its first derivative vanish. This is also obtained directly on the same regular initial domain; at j=0j=0 the backward terms in (35) are omitted, without assigning an additional F−1F_{-1} or H−1H_{-1} datum.

The common-shift determinant Fj(ε)F_j^{(\varepsilon)} obeys the same three-term recurrence with A(j+ε)A(j+\varepsilon), C(j+ε)C(j+\varepsilon), and fixed c0c_0. Its derivative RjR_j satisfies (35), with initial value zero. The physical row FjF_j solves the homogeneous equation. Therefore the right side of (33) satisfies (35) with the initial datum (34). Forward uniqueness proves (33). Only regular steps below the last needed row are used; no recurrence is imposed through the fold. For N=2N=2 the sole required row is the already proved initial identity.

Finally (30) is holomorphic for −1<ℜβ<N-1<\Re\beta<N. For k≤jk\le j, its numerator argument at α=j\alpha=j is β+j−k\beta+j-k, which has no factorial pole in this region. Possible zeros beyond the original factorial strip cause no pole in the numerator or its derivative. Each reciprocal G(β−k)−2G(\beta-k)^{-2} is holomorphic there; its possible negative integer factors are zeros, and its argument never reaches the positive zero divisor of GG. The other denominator is a nonzero physical factorial. This proves the claimed holomorphy, also after the single α\alpha-derivative. The right side of (33) is holomorphic on the same original angular window by its pinned source formula. Analytic continuation from the initial open domain proves the identity throughout that window. No joint continuation of an excessive cutoff through its diagonal pole is used. ▫\square

5. Exact local full jets and cutoff transitions

Fix a physical pair (j,l)(j,l) and a cutoff satisfying (15). In its joint holomorphic neighborhood, SM(j,β)=Fj(c(β)),SM(α,l)=Fl(c(α)).(36) S_M(j,\beta)=F_j(c(\beta)),\qquad S_M(\alpha,l)=F_l(c(\alpha)). \qquad\text{(36)} For a double first row, every excess term k>jk>j has a double α−j\alpha-j zero with holomorphic coefficient. Hence ∂αSM(j,β)=c′(j)Ĝj(c(β)).(37) \partial_\alpha S_M(j,\beta) =c'(j)\widehat G_j(c(\beta)). \qquad\text{(37)} The same assertion holds with the endpoints interchanged. The pinned representative preserves the value and required first normal at every physical second endpoint. Thus, when both rows are double, SM(j,l)=KN(cj,cl),∂αSM(j,l)=c′(j)Kz(cj,cl),∂βSM(j,l)=c′(l)Kw(cj,cl),∂α∂βSM(j,l)=c′(j)c′(l)Kzw(cj,cl).(38) \begin{split} S_M(j,l)&=K_N(c_j,c_l),\\ \partial_\alpha S_M(j,l)&=c'(j)K_z(c_j,c_l),\\ \partial_\beta S_M(j,l)&=c'(l)K_w(c_j,c_l),\\ \partial_\alpha\partial_\beta S_M(j,l) &=c'(j)c'(l)K_{zw}(c_j,c_l). \end{split} \qquad\text{(38)} No terminating-value differentiation has replaced the independent high Newton normal in this formula; it entered through (32)–(35).

At N=2N=2, the only physical pair is (0,0)(0,0), and (15) requires M=0M=0. The original kernel is K2(z,w)=1+zw/16K_2(z,w)=1+zw/16. The factorial formula gives ∂α∂βS0(0,0)=2g2″(0)\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta S_0(0,0)=2g_2''(0). The finite logarithmic-derivative formula yields g2″(0)=π2/8g_2''(0)=\pi^2/8, while c′(0)=−2πc'(0)=-2\pi; hence 2g2″(0)/c′(0)2=1/162g_2''(0)/c'(0)^2=1/16, as required. No isolated TNT_N or singular flux factor is evaluated in this check.

If an endpoint is the odd simple node, only its value and the other endpoint’s required normal are prescribed. There is no division by its vanishing c′(J)c'(J). To obtain a local spectral germ there, average the local angular germ under α↦D−α\alpha\mapsto D-\alpha, or under the corresponding β\beta reflection. Use both averages if both endpoints are simple. This preserves every prescribed datum and gives holomorphic descent through the spectral fold. The same operation does not assert global evenness of an unsymmetrized fixed cutoff.

If two cutoffs both satisfy (15) at the same physical pair, their difference is a sum of terms with k>max⁡(j,l)k>\max(j,l). Each is a holomorphic unit times a factor divisible by (α−j)2(β−l)2(\alpha-j)^2(\beta-l)^2, or has still higher vanishing. All four prescribed jets therefore agree on overlaps where the respective local cutoff conditions hold. The same holds after the odd-fold symmetrization. Products at a shared color retain these exact full jets by the ordinary product rule. They may be consumed by subsequent original traces, with their original crossings and spectators retained.

These transitions are local full-Hermite-null source changes. They do not establish an untraced free-polynomial identity, one global holomorphic kernel, a bounded relative primitive, or a small norm for the transition away from the physical grid. In particular the finite boundary (20) and the diagonal divisor (13) remain relevant to every attempted moving-wire contour.
