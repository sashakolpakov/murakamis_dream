Actual anchor critical points obey a graph flow and a cut maximum principle

28 October 2026. The actual binary-factor source has a direct critical flow equation. Combined with its exact cut factorization, it confines EVERY regular anchor critical point of each residual core to polynomially bounded radii. A macroscopic interior cut margin gives a bound independent of N. Exact balance is stronger: every critical point is on the original unit torus, with one nondegenerate point in each angular chamber.

These assertions concern each specified integer clock sector, with its original source. They do not assert criticality in the remaining clocks, nor extend unit-modulus hypotheses to complex clock deformations. The full invariant can still cancel between sectors or between chambers.

1. The actual source and its exact real critical equation

Take the original graph polynomial, before or after unit-factor-preserving source fusions,

P(X)=gamma product_f (alpha_f X_(i_f)-beta_f X_(j_f)),
|alpha_f|=|beta_f|=1,
f(X)=P(X) product_i X_i^(-q_i),
sum_i q_i=number of factors=M.                         (1)

The q_i are the nonnegative integer source charges, possibly reduced by forced endpoint monomials in the cut/core factorization. The extracted scalar gamma stays outside. A self-loop is evaluated as its exact scalar times X_i; an identically zero factor kills the period. Count a nonzero loop twice in the incidence S_i. Put

delta_i=S_i-2q_i,              sum_i delta_i=0.

A REGULAR anchor critical point has all X_i!=0, no zero factor, and X_i partial_i log f=0. Set rho_i=|X_i|^2. The elementary identity

Re(alpha X/(alpha X-beta Y))
   =1/2+(|X|^2-|Y|^2)/(2|alpha X-beta Y|^2)

proves the exact graph equation

sum_(f joining i,j) (rho_i-rho_j)/|alpha_f X_i-beta_f X_j|^2
                                               =-delta_i. (2)

The equation is independent of the orientation in which an edge is written. A loop contributes one to the logarithmic derivative and two to S_i, hence no flux. This is a weighted graph Laplace equation with POSITIVE weights at every regular critical point, even though the original source coefficients are complex. It is not a positivity assertion about their sum.

2. Cut inequalities give radial properness for every residual core

For a proper anchor subset I, let C_I be the number of crossing factors and e_I=q(I)-m_I the exact source cut deficit. Counting incidences gives

delta(I)=C_I-2e_I.

Summing (2) over I cancels all internal edges. Suppose every radius in I is larger than every radius outside, with separating logarithmic gap g=min_(i in I)log|X_i|-max_(j notin I)log|X_j|>0. For each crossing factor,

(rho_i-rho_j)/|alpha X_i-beta X_j|^2
   >=(|X_i|-|X_j|)/(|X_i|+|X_j|)>=tanh(g/2).

Consequently

C_I tanh(g/2)<=2e_I-C_I.                            (3)

In particular a high-radius cut must have e_I>C_I/2. If e_I<C_I, then

g<=log(e_I/(C_I-e_I)).                             (4)

Now take any nontrivial residual strongly connected core from the EXACT source factorization. It has no proper tight cut and admits a feasible endpoint assignment. The Hall inequalities and their complements therefore give integers

1<=e_I<=C_I-1                                    (5)

at every nonempty proper subset. Sort the k core radii and apply (4) to each positive consecutive gap. Since C_I<=M,

max_i log|X_i|-min_i log|X_i| <=(k-1)log(M-1).     (6)

Every such core has M>=2. After fixing the common C* scale, all its regular anchor critical radii thus lie between reciprocal polynomials in M. In an original fixed knot diagram, M=O(N) and k is bounded by the diagram, so (6) is a uniform POLYNOMIAL radius bound for all actual integer-clock residual cores. This uses no assumption that their defects are small. One-anchor cores have no radial variable after removing the common scale.

If the stronger margin epsilon C_I<=e_I<=(1-epsilon)C_I holds for all proper cuts, with 0<epsilon<=1/2, (4) instead gives

max log|X|-min log|X| <=(k-1)log((1-epsilon)/epsilon). (7)

Thus macroscopic interior cuts confine critical points uniformly even when their forcing delta is of order N. Degenerating margins are exactly the cut variables whose selected finite jets were retained in the preceding source theorem; no missing denominator or boundary source has been discarded here.

3. Balance and sublinear forcing

For a connected graph with delta_i=0 at every anchor, choose an anchor of largest radius in (2). All its summands are nonnegative, so every adjacent radius agrees. Connectivity propagates equality. Therefore EVERY regular critical point, up to common complex scale, has |X_i|=1. This is stronger than the pairing of conjugate critical points in a self-inversive polynomial.

More quantitatively let lambda be the minimum nontrivial cut size and D=(1/2)sum_i|delta_i|. If D<lambda, (3) bounds each consecutive gap by 2 artanh(D/lambda), hence

oscillation(log|X|)<=2(k-1)artanh(D/lambda).        (8)

In particular lambda>=cN and delta_i=o(N) force all regular critical radii into a shrinking tube around the unit torus. The bound does not give a source intersection number for a perturbed complex saddle.

4. In the balanced case the source itself reaches every critical chamber

Fix the common phase by X_k=1 and write X_i=exp(i theta_i). The exact sine factorization gives the original density on this torus as

f(exp(i theta))=C product_f 2sin((theta_i-theta_j+phi_f)/2),
                                                    (9)

with one constant complex phase C (and the retained scalar modulus). The signs vary across factor zeros. On each lifted arrangement chamber, log|f| has Hessian

H(v,v)=-(1/4)sum_f csc^2((theta_i-theta_j+phi_f)/2)
                                  (v_i-v_j)^2.       (10)

Connectivity makes this negative definite after removing the common phase. Each chamber is a convex bounded polytope in these lifted coordinates: every edge difference lies between two consecutive factor-zero levels, and a spanning tree bounds all differences. At every chamber boundary some factor vanishes, so log|f| tends to minus infinity. Thus each chamber has exactly one maximum, with a nondegenerate Hessian. The density has constant phase on that chamber; its angular critical point is therefore a full complex critical point. The maximum principle in Section 3 shows that there are no other regular critical points off this torus. Common-scale gauge directions are not counted as Hessian degeneracies here.

This proves accessibility from the ACTUAL coefficient torus for this balanced class. It does not choose the signs of different chamber contributions or assert that the remaining clock sum preserves them.

There is also a precise local source multiplicity. In logarithmic coordinates at a chamber maximum, the real tangent Hessian is negative definite, while its imaginary normal Hessian has the opposite sign. The actual torus therefore intersects the dual local Gaussian normal cycle transversely with degree +1 or -1, according to orientation. The critical point is present with local multiplicity one; its constant phase and Gaussian determinant are still retained. This local statement does not prevent cancellation between different chambers and does not compute a geometric coefficient of the full coupled clock sum.

5. A quantitative selected-coefficient theorem with a checkable gap

There is a useful uniform consequence for a sequence of balanced graphs with a fixed number r=k-1 of independent anchors and charges q_i=O(N). Put M_N=max_(unit torus)|f_N|. Suppose one sign chamber attains M_N and every chamber of the opposite sign has maximum at most M_N exp(-eta N), for a fixed eta>0. Chambers with the same sign are allowed to share the maximum. Then for all sufficiently large N,

c N^(-2r) M_N <= |[X^q]P_N| <= M_N.              (11)

The constant c depends on the fixed degree bounds and r. In particular the selected coefficient has exactly the exponential rate of M_N. No geometric thimble coefficient is assumed in this conclusion.

Proof. After removing its constant phase, (9) is a real trigonometric polynomial. Each variable has Fourier degree at most q_i: its polynomial degree is at most S_i=2q_i, before subtracting its source charge q_i. For a one-variable restriction whose sup norm is at most M_N, each Fourier coefficient has modulus at most M_N, so

|partial_(theta_i) f_N|<=q_i(q_i+1) M_N.

At a point attaining M_N, a box of side c_0 N^(-2) therefore has the same sign and modulus at least M_N/2. Its positive signed mass is at least c N^(-2r)M_N in normalized torus measure. All same-sign chambers add to that mass. The ENTIRE opposite-sign part has absolute integral at most M_N exp(-eta N), since the full normalized measure is one. This proves the lower bound, after reducing c. The upper bound is the integral triangle inequality. A polynomially small opposite-sign gap can replace the exponential hypothesis if its total bound is o(N^(-2r)M_N).

There is also an exact top-intersection statement in this balanced setting. Suppose the highest regular critical point is UNIQUE after the common-scale gauge and torus periods. By Sections 3–4 it lies on the original torus and is its unique global modulus maximum. An upward cycle of Re log f from that point has Re log f at least its critical height; the entire source torus has Re log f at most that height, with equality only at this point. Their only intersection is therefore this critical point. The negative-definite angular Hessian and holomorphy make the upward tangent space transverse to the angular source tangent space, giving local intersection number +1 or -1 with the specified orientations. Thus the highest relative coefficient is NONZERO here; it has not been inferred from a nonzero local density alone. This statement does not make the quantitative height-gap hypothesis in (11) automatic for an N-dependent sequence, or remove cancellation between opposite-sign chambers at tied height.

The gap is an extra hypothesis, not automatic. For example (X-Y)(X+Y) with charges (1,1) is balanced, has two nondegenerate unit-torus critical chambers of equal height and opposite sign, and has selected coefficient zero. Nor is an arbitrary unit-factor UNBALANCED core covered by (11): explicit such sources can exclude their unique highest critical action entirely.

6. Exact scope for the original knot and its A-period

Equations (2)-(8) apply to every actual fixed-clock binary-factor core, with the original source charges and normalizers. They give properness and a precise graph-theoretic condition for balanced torus accessibility. For an alternating one-component diagram in the present overbridge coordinates, exact balance forces all crossing clocks to agree; it is not an automatic consequence of alternation. The stronger actual alternating-source theorem shows that every balanced sector of the original figure-eight state is zero. Indeed every nonzero sector before further clustering has max_i|delta_i|>=(N-1)/2. Thus the pointwise balanced and sublinear defect regimes are ABSENT from this already known prototype’s nonzero source support in these variables. Further exact clustering can combine opposite defects; applying it is a distinct operation, not a consequence of (11).

The ALL-level original figure-eight source theorem makes this limitation sharp. Its nonzero fixed-clock sectors satisfy h_0=0 and h_1+h_3>=N-1; consequently EVERY balanced diagonal-clock sector is exactly zero, and at least one individual anchor defect has modulus >=(N-1)/2. Thus the balanced chamber theorem cannot on its own select that prototype’s volume contribution in these coordinates. Further exact clustering or fusion of macroscopic defects is required; sublinear individual-star jets alone do not cover that source.

The knot period still includes its W_h and the coupled h sum. Anchor criticality alone is not full gluing/holonomy criticality, so a bound on the latter’s regulator cannot be assigned to every chamber maximum of a fixed h. Complex h deformations can make the factor ratios nonunit, and require a separate argument. A retained deficient-star polynomial is not generally a unit binary product; its logarithmic derivative must remain unless one applies this theorem separately to its actual monomial source channels. A norm estimate does not permit dropping that derivative or its source pairing.

Thus this result proves a general critical flow and source-accessible balanced selection theorem. It does not yet prove the original geometric coefficient, the broad volume upper bound, or the volume conjecture. The exact source reductions and these bounds identify the remaining macroscopic forced flow and clock coupling as the next problem rather than an uncontrolled coordinate escape.
