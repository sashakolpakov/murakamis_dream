The exact spectral maximum of a full Green interval

31 October 2026. analytical research theorem. This identifies the spectral maximum of the original sharp coefficient UPPER functional. It does not assert a coefficient lower limit, phase compatibility, representation volume, or a shared maximum bounded by Phi_D.

1. Statement on the original degree simplex

Use the jointly continuous, absolutely normalized sharp action H_delta(q) of the global closed sharp coefficient upper theorem. Its row order is q=(q_x,q_b,q_c,q_y), with total one. On the closed LEFT domain q_c>=1/2 set

s_x=q_x, s_b=q_b, s_y=q_y, nu=s_x+s_b+s_y=1-q_c.

Let f be the actual triangle summand, f(0)=f(1/2)=0 and

f'(s)=log cot(pi s),
f''(s)=-2pi/sin(2pi s),        0<s<1/2.

Then the exact calculus identity is

max_(0<=delta<=1/2) H_delta(q)
   = G(q):=f(s_x)+f(s_b)+f(s_y)-f(nu).                 (1)

For strictly positive s_x,s_b,s_y and nu<1/2, the maximizing spectrum is unique and lies in (0,1/2). It satisfies

sin^2(pi delta_*)
  = cos(pi nu)cos(pi s_b)/[cos(pi s_x)cos(pi s_y)].    (2)

The reflected statement holds on the closed RIGHT domain, with its original overloaded middle as child. On the closed CROSS domain the closed CROSS comparison already gives max_delta H_delta=h_0. At the seams, (1) equals the actual complete triangle entropy, so these statements fit continuously. All spectra remain independent at the different original ports; this is a pointwise local identity, with no shared minimax exchange.

2. Construct the strict spectral stationary point from its degrees

Assume the three input loads are positive and their sum nu is below 1/2. Define dimensionless positive common weights

x=tan(pi s_x), b=tan(pi s_b), y=tan(pi s_y),
A=x+y, P=xy, S=1-bA-P.

The addition formula and nu<1/2 give S>0 and

c=tan(pi nu)=(A+b-bP)/S,
c-b=A(1+b^2)/S,
c-b-A=A(b+x)(b+y)/S>0.                               (3)

Put

z=S/(1+b^2),  k^2=1-z=(b+x)(b+y)/(1+b^2),
delta=(1/pi)arcsin(sqrt(z)).                          (4)

Both z and k^2 are strictly between zero and one. The last identity in (2) follows from cos(pi nu)=S cos(pi s_x)cos(pi s_b)cos(pi s_y).

The original root is

alpha_delta(t)=4[k^2-sin^2(pi t)],
Lambda_delta(t;x,y)
  =2sin(pi t)[A sin(pi t)
       +sqrt((x-y)^2 sin^2(pi t)+4k^2xy)],
L_delta=Lambda_delta/|alpha_delta|.

The exact pre-pole and post-pole row equations are

sin^2(pi mu)=k^2 b^2/[(b+x)(b+y)],
sin^2(pi nu)=k^2 c^2/[(c-x)(c-y)].                    (5)

Equations (3)–(4) give mu=s_b and the specified nu. For the second equation one may verify directly

(c-x)(c-y)/(1+c^2)=(b+x)(b+y)/(1+b^2).

The input is on the increasing pre-pole branch because k2-sin2(pi mu)=(bA+P)/(1+b^2)>0. The output is on the decreasing post-pole branch because k2-sin2(pi nu)=(P-Ac)/(1+c^2)<0. Thus this is a strict straddle, not a spurious squared solution. Its spectral stationary condition (c-b)z=A is exactly (3)–(4), with the original normalized branch derivative log(T_c/E_b) equal to zero.

3. Verify the actual outer coefficient, not just its row equations

The outer degree is the derivative of the literal integrated root at the minimizing tilt log(x/y). Write d=x-y and D=A^2-4Pz>0. Direct differentiation and integration give

a(t)=partial_(log x) log Lambda_delta(t;x,y)
    =1/2+d sin(pi t)/[2sqrt(d^2 sin^2(pi t)+4k^2P)],

int_mu^nu a(t)dt=(nu-mu)/2
  +[asin(d cos(pi mu)/sqrt D)
    -asin(d cos(pi nu)/sqrt D)]/(2pi).                (6)

All inverse sines here are their real principal values. To check their difference without selecting an unproved sign, put

B=bA+2P>0, V=sqrt(d^2+B^2),
R=sqrt((1+x^2)(1+y^2)), theta=atan x-atan y.

Then D=V2/(1+b2), and the two inverse-sine arguments in (6) are

I_b=d/V,       I_c=dS/(VR).

For I_c use sqrt(1+c2)=sqrt((1+x2)(1+b2)(1+y2))/S. Furthermore

sin theta=d/R, cos theta=(1+P)/R,
sin(asin I_b-theta)=d(1+P-B)/(VR)=I_c.

Since 1+P-B=S>0, tan(asin I_b)=d/B and tan theta=d/(1+P) have the same sign and |asin I_b|>=|theta|. Their difference remains in (-pi/2,pi/2), so

asin I_b-asin I_c=theta=pi(s_x-s_y).

The equality case d=0 is included directly. Equation (6) therefore equals s_x; its complementary outer degree is s_y. This verifies the original outer-coefficient Legendre equation and fixes the actual load vector q, not just a formal conic or two row roots.

4. Absolute action and uniqueness

Let H_*(s) denote H_delta(q) at the smooth stationary data just constructed. Every degree is interior, the minimizing outer tilt is finite and unique, and the spectrum is a strict nonpole stationary point. The ordinary envelope rule therefore gives

partial_(s_i) H_* = log(c/w_i),   i=x,b,y.             (7)

This is also obtained by adding the original face-weight term to the local simplex action: its derivative is zero, and increasing an input decreases the child load by the same amount. No common scalar is dropped. Since w_i=tan(pi s_i) and c=tan(pi nu), the right side of (7) is f’(s_i)-f’(nu). On the connected domain s_i>0, sum s_i<1/2, H_*-G is consequently constant.

Scale s_i toward zero in fixed positive ratios. Formula (2) gives delta_* ->1/2 and q tends to the pure-child vertex. Joint continuity of the original closed sharp action gives H_* ->0, as does G. Hence the constant is zero. This uses the proved continuous upper functional, not a coefficient limit or a collision claim about dilogarithms.

Conversely any strict spectral stationary point at these degrees must be the one constructed above. At its actual finite outer tilt define x,y>0 and b=L_delta(mu), c=L_delta(nu). The normalized pre/pre and post/post branch derivatives cannot vanish, so the point straddles the pole and (c-b)z=A. In particular c>b+A. Set

T^2=[Abc+P(c-b)]/[c-b-A]>0.

The exact row equations imply tan(pi mu)=b/T and tan(pi nu)=c/T. Rescale all four weights by T. The tangent-addition identity now gives atan(x/T)+atan(b/T)+atan(y/T)=atan(c/T); the angle sum is below pi/2 because T^2-bA-P=A(b+x)(b+y)/(c-b-A)>0. Repeating the exact outer calculation (6) shows s_x=atan(x/T)/pi and s_y=atan(y/T)/pi. Thus all ratios w_i/T, and then z, are fixed by q and agree with (2).

5. Why the stationary value is the whole spectral maximum

For fixed strict q, joint continuity makes the maximum on delta in [0,1/2] exist. The fixed-degree small-delta perturbation excludes delta=0 when the interval has positive length and nu<1/2. The cap value is zero, whereas strict concavity of f and f(0)=0 give G>0 for these three positive inputs. A spectral value at which an interval endpoint equals an alpha pole cannot maximize: the original unused-log perturbation into the interval has a positive epsilon log(1/epsilon) gain uniformly before the outer infimum. These are exactly the endpoint and pole exclusions for the same sharp functional and unchanged degree vector.

At every remaining spectral value the original coefficient tilt is finite, unique and smooth. A maximum is therefore a strict spectral stationary point. Section 4 proves that there is exactly one, and its value is G. This proves (1)–(2) on the strict LEFT degree domain.

The map q -> max_delta H_delta(q) is continuous on the entire closed simplex because H is jointly continuous and the spectral interval is compact. The right side of (1) is continuous as well. Approximation by the strict LEFT degrees thus proves (1) on the full closed LEFT domain, including zero ordinary inputs in the standalone local formula, protected specializations, diagonal intervals and the seam. No positivity or nonzero A_rest is inferred at any finite level.

For nu=1/2, f(nu)=0 and (1) is precisely the complete input triangle entropy; the output is its actual pure-middle triangle. This agrees with the independently normalized CROSS formula at the shared seam.

6. Exact reduced row Hessian and the remaining global problem

On the strict input simplex the Hessian of the optimized Green action is especially simple:

D^2 G = -2pi diag(1/sin(2pi s_i))
          +[2pi/sin(2pi nu)] 1 1^T.                 (8)

There is exactly one positive eigenvalue and two negative eigenvalues. Indeed the positive rank-one update crosses zero precisely when sum_i sin(2pi s_i)>sin(2pi nu), which is strict for positive inputs whose total angle is below pi. A protected-zero input is removed before differentiation; with two positive inputs the signature is one positive and one negative. This recovers the prior monomer Hessian.

Equivalently, for original local row displacements summing to zero,

D^2 G[v,v]
  =-2pi sum_(input i) v_i^2/sin(2pi q_i)
     +2pi v_child^2/sin(2pi(1-q_child)).               (9)

The full original constrained second variation after optimizing the strict Green spectra is the sum of (9) and the actual negative CROSS row Hessians, restricted to the original row and face balances; cap rows retain their zero action. Formula (9) is a diagonal incidence form before imposing these constraints. It does not itself produce a positive feasible direction: the shared repair problem is still real.

The general sharp upper variational problem can now maximize the explicit piecewise local function: actual CROSS entropy on CROSS degrees, and (1) on either overloaded-middle domain. Spectra optimize independently, so this elimination is legitimate; no face-weight infimum is exchanged with a shared maximum. A proof that this original shared maximum is at most Phi_D, or a cancellation-aware replacement when it exceeds Phi_D, remains separate. No universal full-Hessian instability or hyperbolic volume bound is claimed.

7. A general full-invariant criterion with no spectral variables

This corollary keeps the original labeled simple-white geometric certificate and full-source conventions. It is a sufficient criterion, not an assertion that every such diagram satisfies its inequality. Fix an actual width k>=2 flat-plat skeleton D, its permitted mark and fixed retained half twists epsilon_e in {0,+1,-1}, whose endpoint permutation gives a knot. Let Phi_D=Vol(Y_D)/(2pi) be its drilled volume rate. The common full-V source and Phi_D are the ones in the whole all-CROSS filling theorem, including the actual full crossing scalars and final framing character.

Define a continuous local function barH on the full degree simplex:

barH(q)=h_0(q),                    q_b,q_c<=1/2;
barH(q)=sum_(i=x,b,y) f(q_i)-f(1-q_c), q_c>=1/2;
barH(q)=sum_(i=x,c,y) f(q_i)-f(1-q_b), q_b>=1/2.       (10)

The seam agreements proved above make (10) unambiguous. Let P_D be the original compact transport fiber: each port supplies one, every ordinary face demands one, and the two protected faces demand zero. Repeated incidences retain their original labels. Put

P_NC={q in P_D : max_(e,middle b or c) q_(e,middle)>=1/2},
M_D^NC=max_(q in P_NC) sum_e barH(q_e).                 (11)

If P_NC is empty the corresponding upper condition is vacuous and its asymptotic non-CROSS arrays are absent. Otherwise suppose the fixed diagram satisfies the explicit, purely degree-variable inequality

M_D^NC < Phi_D.                                       (12)

Then there is a finite threshold T(D), including this certificate in the fixed data, such that for every independently signed integer twist vector p with all |p_e|>T(D), fixed BEFORE N tends to infinity,

lim_(N->infinity) (2pi/N) log|K_N(K_D(p))|
   =Vol(S^3 minus K_D(p)).                            (13)

Both level parities and composite levels are included. There is no comparability condition among the twist magnitudes, no growing-twist limit, and no threshold asserted uniformly over different diagrams. Equation (13) concerns the original filled knot invariant and its actual smooth filled exterior, not the drilled volume.

Proof. Spectra optimize independently at the original ports. The closed CROSS comparison and (1) give the exact pointwise identity

max_(delta_1,...,delta_R in [0,1/2])
    sum_e H_(delta_e)(q_e)=sum_e barH(q_e).             (14)

Take the literal non-CROSS remainder, namely the full-V source minus its actual all-CROSS path sector. Every original coefficient array in this remainder has a one-sided or diagonal port. Its d=N-1 normalized limiting degree lies in P_NC; possible finite half-mesh conventions only give a uniformly vanishing distance from this closed set. The global uniform coefficient LIMSUP theorem applies through all row, outer-degree and spectral boundaries, including sampled zeros. There are polynomially many original arrays at fixed D. Equations (12)–(14) and uniform continuity therefore give a fixed gamma_D>0 and, uniformly on the entire real spectral cube and at both parities,

|S_N^NC(-2cos(2pi delta))|
   <= exp[N(Phi_D-gamma_D)]                           (15)

for all sufficiently large N. The estimate also bounds the sum of absolute values of these arrays; no phase alignment or lower coefficient limit is required.

The remainder S_N^NC is still an actual polynomial of degree at most d in each sigma_e. Its degree/path projection is spectrum independent. Thus the cosine-Bernstein and whole-domain three-integration-by-parts argument of the global sector theorem, section 4 applies verbatim to (15). A sufficiently thin fixed complex spectral strip retains a positive loss. Source-only Cauchy estimates control the required derivatives; the original sine factors and Gaussian endpoint matching retain the second-derivative endpoint currents. The actual charge factors then leave the absolutely summable product of |ell_e|^(-2). Consequently some kappa_D>0 satisfies

|I_N^NC(p)| <= C_(D,p) exp[N(Phi_D-kappa_D)]             (16)

for each fixed signed p. This is the entire original Fourier period, not an infinite sum of sharply cut local charge cubes. The polynomial dependence on N and on the fixed slopes n_e=epsilon_e-2p_e is absorbed by retaining a smaller positive loss.

The proved all-CROSS whole period is eventually nonzero and has rate

lim_(N->infinity) N^(-1)log|I_N^CROSS(p)|
   =Phi_fill(p)
   =Phi_D-O_D(sum_e |n_e|^(-2)).                      (17)

Here Phi_fill(p)=Vol(S^3 minus K_D(p))/(2pi), with the same original normalization, half twists and framing. Choose T(D) large enough that this existing theorem applies and Phi_fill(p)>Phi_D-kappa_D/2 for all independent signed vectors in the stated range. Then (16)–(17) give I_NNC/I_NCROSS ->0 exponentially, at both parities. Adding the two literal sectors and retaining their common original normalization proves (13). The unproved general step is (12), not this conditional full-source implication.

8. Radial convexity and the exact local weighted transform

Take positive face weights (x,b,c,y), and write the LEFT inputs as s_i=t p_i, where p_i>=0, sum p_i=1 and 0<=t<=1/2. For at least two positive p_i, differentiation on the open radial interval gives

d^2/dt^2 G(t p)
  =2pi[1/sin(2pi t)-sum_(p_i>0) p_i^2/sin(2pi t p_i)]
  >0.                                                 (18)

Indeed u/sin u is strictly increasing on (0,pi), so each term in the sum is at most p_i/sin(2pi t), strictly when 0<p_i<1. If only one p_i is positive, G is identically zero. Thus G is convex on every closed radial segment, by continuity at its endpoints.

The added weight term is affine in t:

sum_f q_f log L_f=(1-t)log c+t sum_i p_i log w_i.

Its radial maximum is therefore attained at t=0 or t=1/2. At t=0 the value is log c. At t=1/2 the input has its actual triangle entropy, and maximizing its allocation gives

sup_(closed LEFT q) [barH(q)+sum_f q_f log L_f]
   =max{log c, Q(x,b,y)+(1/2)log c}.                   (19)

Here Q is the original weighted complete triangle action, with total triangle load 1/2; no degree normalization has changed. RIGHT has the reflected formula with b and c interchanged. On CROSS, the two genuine triangle allocations give the weighted supremum Q(x,b,y)+Q(x,c,y). Since a pure-middle triangle is allowed in its closed degree simplex, Q(x,c,y)>=(1/2)log c and Q(x,b,y)>=(1/2)log b. Hence the exact full simplex transform is

sup_q [barH(q)+sum_f q_f log L_f]
   =max{Q(x,b,y)+Q(x,c,y), log b, log c}.              (20)

All weights in (18)–(20) are positive; degree-boundary allocations are included by the established continuous extension. Formula (20) is exactly the local weighted transform of the original positive Fejer endpoint source in the complete Cauchy-capacity theorem. Thus replacing that source by the spectrally optimized sharp action does not improve the bound obtained by reoptimizing a single shared positive radius vector. The original face constraints still couple the radial motions. No shared maximum and infimum have been exchanged, and (20) proves neither (12) nor the universal volume comparison.

scope and dependencies

The weighted radial transform does not exchange the shared minimum and maximum. The non-CROSS filling implication retains its stated strict entropy hypothesis and fixed-twist quantifiers.

The dependencies are the closed global sharp upper functional; the strict stationary branch derivatives and outer tilt equation; the fixed-degree delta=0 exclusion; the pole-endpoint exclusion; and the proved closed CROSS comparison. All occur in the existing source notes. No numerical search is used in this proof.
