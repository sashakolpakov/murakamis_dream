Alternating physical-row linearization and odd-turn quadratic phases

Private written proof, 17 November 2026.

The ordinary product of two original physical rows has strictly alternating linearization coefficients throughout its full triangle support, provided its degree stays in the physical-row range. A finite positive recursion proves the signs and computes every coefficient. An odd signed turn removes precisely the alternating signs, retaining a sum with positive amplitudes and the original quadratic phases.

Above this degree range, an expansion in physical value rows alone is impossible. The original normal rows supply the missing degrees and have the retained Jordan action. No cancellation or lower bound for the resulting quadratic sum is inferred.

Scope and direct sources

The theorem is a general local composition identity for actual physical rows, with unrestricted indices in its stated low-degree range. It supplies a larger coherent class than phase-positive Laurent polynomials: the odd-turn expression (25) has positive amplitudes with an explicit quadratic phase. Bounding that sum after further original products, turns and closing remains a separate problem. No nonzero knot scalar or general filling asymptotic follows from the sign theorem alone.
