The original factorial amplitude through a two-square fold

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

At every two-square edge classified by the radial primitive theorem, both original same-index halfwire factorials are holomorphic units on one neighborhood independent of the level. They have a uniform all-order expansion with the retained original phase b0b_0 and a nonzero leading amplitude. This includes the square-root fold q=0q=0. The proof fixes the phase constant and logarithmic marking, rather than inferring an asymptotic expansion from finite-level holomorphy.

This result concerns the factors at the classified two-square edges. It does not assert a single-phase expansion for unrelated incidences, replace a free cardinal boundary by a factorial, or determine the coefficient of the local Morse disk in the original relative cycle.

1. A centered factorial expansion with a fixed constant

Write Ω=ℂ\((−∞,0]∪[1,∞)).(1) \Omega=\mathbb C\setminus\bigl((-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty)\bigr). \qquad\text{(1)} This domain is simply connected: u↦u/(1−u)u\mapsto u/(1-u) identifies it with the plane cut along the nonpositive real axis. Let J′(u)=Log⁡(2sin⁡πu),J(1/2)=0,(2) J'(u)=\operatorname{Log}(2\sin\pi u),\qquad J(1/2)=0, \qquad\text{(2)} where the logarithm is real on 0<u<10<u<1. This is the original sine primitive, since ∫01/2log⁡(2sin⁡πu)du=0\int_0^{1/2}\log(2\sin\pi u)\,du=0. Its reflection identities are J(1−u)=−J(u),J(2r)(1/2)=0(r≥1).(3) J(1-u)=-J(u),\qquad J^{(2r)}(1/2)=0\quad(r\ge1). \qquad\text{(3)} In particular its restrictions to the upper and lower half-planes are the marked branches of the archived half-plane expansions.

Use the original normalized gamma-product factorial GNG_N, N≥2N\ge2. Its exact logarithmic derivative and divisor are GN′(w)GN(w)=πN2∑k=0N−1(w−k)cot⁡π(w−k)N,ord⁡w=kGN(w)=⌊k/N⌋.(4) \frac{G_N'(w)}{G_N(w)} =\frac{\pi}{N^2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(w-k) \cot\frac{\pi(w-k)}N,\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{w=k}G_N(w)=\lfloor k/N\rfloor . \qquad\text{(4)} There are no other divisors. Terms with w=kw=k, 0≤k<N0\le k<N, are removable. Put HN(u)=GN(Nu−1/2).(5) H_N(u)=G_N(Nu-1/2). \qquad\text{(5)} Every divisor of (5) lies on one of the two removed real rays. Thus HNH_N has a holomorphic logarithm on Ω\Omega.

Its exact normalization is HN(1/2)=N>0.(6) H_N(1/2)=\sqrt N>0. \qquad\text{(6)} Indeed (4) gives GN′(N−1−w)/GN(N−1−w)=GN′(w)/GN(w)G_N'(N-1-w)/G_N(N-1-w)=G_N'(w)/G_N(w); hence GN(w)GN(N−1−w)G_N(w)G_N(N-1-w) is constant. At w=0w=0 it is ∏j=1N−12sin⁡(πj/N)=N\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}2\sin(\pi j/N)=N. Positivity of the defining gamma product on −1<w<N-1<w<N fixes the sign in (6). Choose log⁡HN(1/2)=12log⁡N\log H_N(1/2)=\tfrac12\log N.

Lemma 1. For every compact K⋐ΩK\Subset\Omega and integer R≥0R\ge0, log⁡HN(u)=12log⁡N+NJ(u)+∑r=1RB2r(1/2)(2r)!N2r−1J(2r)(u)+OK,R(N−2R−1).(7) \log H_N(u)=\tfrac12\log N+NJ(u) +\sum_{r=1}^{R} \frac{B_{2r}(1/2)}{(2r)!\,N^{2r-1}}J^{(2r)}(u) +O_{K,R}(N^{-2R-1}). \qquad\text{(7)} The remainder is holomorphic and has the same order under every fixed derivative on smaller compact sets. The apparent singularity at u=su=s is removable; every other possible pole would place uu on a removed ray.

The leading integral is ∫01Fu(s)ds=J′(u).(9) \int_0^1F_u(s)\,ds=J'(u). \qquad\text{(9)} Its derivative is Fu(0)−Fu(1)=πcot⁡πu=J″(u)F_u(0)-F_u(1)=\pi\cot\pi u=J''(u). At u=1/2u=1/2, integration by parts gives ∫−1/21/2πvcot⁡πvdv=−2∫01/2log⁡(sin⁡πv)dv=log⁡2, \int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\pi v\cot\pi v\,dv =-2\int_0^{1/2}\log(\sin\pi v)\,dv=\log2, which fixes (9). Midpoint Euler–Maclaurin, with enough remainder terms, gives ddulog⁡HN(u)=NJ′(u)+∑r=1RB2r(1/2)(2r)!N2r−1J(2r+1)(u)+OK,R(N−2R−1).(10) \frac d{du}\log H_N(u) =NJ'(u)+\sum_{r=1}^{R} \frac{B_{2r}(1/2)}{(2r)!\,N^{2r-1}}J^{(2r+1)}(u) +O_{K,R}(N^{-2R-1}). \qquad\text{(10)} The endpoint derivative difference used here is ∂s2r−1Fu(1)−∂s2r−1Fu(0)=J(2r+1)(u).(11) \partial_s^{2r-1}F_u(1)-\partial_s^{2r-1}F_u(0) =J^{(2r+1)}(u). \qquad\text{(11)} The odd Bernoulli polynomials at 1/21/2 vanish. Join KK to 1/21/2 by paths of uniformly bounded length in a larger compact subset of Ω\Omega. Such paths exist by a finite cover and connectedness of the domain. 2. A common exact unit neighborhood

At a classified two-square edge write t=−iH,u=−iK,A=tanh⁡(H/2),B=tanh⁡(K/2),q=iQ,Q2=A2+B2−1≥0,|Q|<min⁡(A,B)<1.(13) t=-iH,\quad u=-iK,\quad A=\tanh(H/2),\quad B=\tanh(K/2),\quad q=iQ,\quad Q^2=A^2+B^2-1\ge0, \quad |Q|<\min(A,B)<1. \qquad\text{(13)} These are the actual limiting data of the pinned radial theorem; in particular H,K>0H,K>0. The retained stationary wire is x*=12−iπartanh⁡Q.(14) x_*=\frac12-\frac{i}{\pi}\operatorname{artanh}Q. \qquad\text{(14)} Use D=N−1D=N-1 and the literal source factor BN(x,t)=NGN(Nz+−1/2)GN(Nz−−1/2)GN(Nx)2,z±=12−x±t2π.(15) B_N(x,t)= \frac{N}{G_N(Nz_+-1/2)G_N(Nz_--1/2)G_N(Nx)^2}, \qquad z_\pm=\frac12-x\pm\frac{t}{2\pi}. \qquad\text{(15)} At (13)–(14), z+=iπ{artanh⁡Q−H/2},z−=iπ{artanh⁡Q+H/2}.(16) z_+=\frac{i}{\pi}\{\operatorname{artanh}Q-H/2\}, \qquad z_-=\frac{i}{\pi}\{\operatorname{artanh}Q+H/2\}. \qquad\text{(16)} The first lies strictly below the real axis and the second strictly above it. The same assertion holds with HH replaced by KK.

Theorem 2. A neighborhood 𝒰\mathcal U of (x*,t,u)(x_*,t,u), independent of N≥2N\ge2, exists on which BN(x,t)B_N(x,t) and BN(x,u)B_N(x,u) are holomorphic and nonzero. It can be chosen so that all their normalized arguments x,z+,z−x,z_+,z_- lie in fixed compact subsets of Ω\Omega. The assertions hold simultaneously for every edge of a finite classified two-square matching, including every Q=0Q=0.

Proof. Shrink a relatively compact neighborhood so that 1/4<ℜx<3/41/4<\Re x<3/4 and the two signs in (16) are separated from zero, for each endpoint. Then the shifted arguments in (15) are nonreal at every level and have no divisor by (4). Also −1<ℜ(Nx)<N-1<\Re(Nx)<N, so GN(Nx)G_N(Nx) is a unit. The strict inequalities are independent of NN. They also give the compact subsets of Ω\Omega. Intersect the finitely many neighborhoods for the matching. ▫\square

For precision, the corresponding reversed factor BN(D/N−x,t)B_N(D/N-x,t) is an exact unit on a possibly smaller common neighborhood as well. Its shifted arguments are N(x−1/2±t/(2π))+1/2N(x-1/2\pm t/(2\pi))+1/2, still strictly nonreal; its unshifted argument is N(1−x)−1N(1-x)-1, whose real part lies strictly between −1-1 and NN for all N≥2N\ge2. This does not identify its different asymptotic phase with b0b_0.

3. Agreement with the retained geometric logarithms

The domain membership at the target alone does not determine a previously continued logarithmic marking. Here the actual positive approach and its allowed tangent contraction fix it.

Lemma 3. At each edge in Theorem 2, the primitive obtained by using (2) in bcan(x,t)=−J(z+)−J(z−)−2J(x)(17) b_{\mathrm{ can}}(x,t)=-J(z_+)-J(z_-)-2J(x) \qquad\text{(17)} is exactly the retained original b0(x,t)b_0(x,t) germ, including its additive constant and its logarithmic derivatives.

Proof. Let (aj,bj)(a_j,b_j) be its positive approach, tending to (−iA,−iB)(-iA,-iB). The pinned positive-wire theorem proves that (λaj,λbj),0≤λ≤1,(18) (\lambda a_j,\lambda b_j),\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1, \qquad\text{(18)} stays positive. Its original stationary branch has ℜqj(λ)>0\Re q_j(\lambda)>0, xj(0)=1/4x_j(0)=1/4. Thus continuation along (18) gives precisely the original marked phase, from completeness.

Put λ0=(A2+B2)−1/2≤1\lambda_0=(A^2+B^2)^{-1/2}\le1. The limiting root is nonnegative real on [0,λ0][0,\lambda_0], with qλ2=1−λ2(A2+B2),(19) q_\lambda^2=1-\lambda^2(A^2+B^2), \qquad\text{(19)} and for λ>λ0\lambda>\lambda_0 is the imaginary root whose sign is that of QQ. If Q=0Q=0, this latter interval is empty. The principal square roots along (18) converge uniformly to this continuous path. For Q≠0Q\ne0, the sign of their imaginary parts is eventually the sign of QQ; at the single zero of (19), continuity of the square root gives the same conclusion. The inverse cotangent has no singularity on this compact path, which avoids q=±iq=\pm i, so the wires converge uniformly too.

The limiting endpoint angle is tλ=−2iartanh⁡(λA).(20) t_\lambda=-2i\operatorname{artanh}(\lambda A). \qquad\text{(20)} Before λ0\lambda_0, xλx_\lambda is real in [1/4,1/2][1/4,1/2]. For 0<λ≤λ00<\lambda\le\lambda_0, the two arguments z±z_\pm therefore have opposite nonzero imaginary parts, with nonnegative real parts. At λ=0\lambda=0 all three arguments equal 1/41/4. After λ0\lambda_0, write qλ=iQλq_\lambda=iQ_\lambda. Then Qλ2=λ2(A2+B2)−1,|Qλ|<λA,|Qλ|<λB,(21) Q_\lambda^2=\lambda^2(A^2+B^2)-1,\qquad |Q_\lambda|<\lambda A,\quad |Q_\lambda|<\lambda B, \qquad\text{(21)} because λA,λB<1\lambda A,\lambda B<1. Formula (16), with these contracted values, again places z+z_+ strictly below and z−z_- strictly above the axis. The wire has real part 1/21/2.

Consequently each entire limiting argument path is a compact subset of Ω\Omega. Uniform convergence implies that the late actual positive paths (18) stay in this same domain. Along them the logarithm (2) is the original one, since it agrees at their common complete starting point. Its primitive constants agree there as well. Passing to the limit proves (17), and continuation to the small independent-variable neighborhood proves equality of the full germs. This argument does not assume that the radial charge path is connected, or deform fixed primitive equations along (18). ▫\square

4. The actual local amplitude

Theorem 4. Uniformly on compact subsets of the neighborhood in Theorem 2, with all fixed holomorphic derivatives after the displayed phase and prefactor are extracted, BN(x,t)=eNb0(x,t)2Nsin⁡πxexp⁡{∑r=1RCr(x,t)N2r−1+O(N−2R−1)},(22) \boxed{\quad B_N(x,t)=\frac{e^{Nb_0(x,t)}}{2N\sin\pi x} \exp\left\{\sum_{r=1}^{R}\frac{C_r(x,t)}{N^{2r-1}} +O(N^{-2R-1})\right\},\quad} \qquad\text{(22)} for every R≥0R\ge0, where Cr(x,t)=−B2r(1/2){J(2r)(z+)+J(2r)(z−)}+2B2rJ(2r)(x)(2r)!.(23) C_r(x,t)=- \frac{B_{2r}(1/2)\{J^{(2r)}(z_+)+J^{(2r)}(z_-)\} +2B_{2r}J^{(2r)}(x)}{(2r)!}. \qquad\text{(23)} In particular 2Nsin⁡(πx)e−Nb0BN→12N\sin(\pi x)e^{-Nb_0}B_N\to1 holomorphically, and the leading amplitude is a unit through the fold.

Proof. Apply (7) to the two shifted factors in (15), and (12) to its two unshifted factors. The four half-logarithms give 2log⁡N2\log N; subtracting them from the numerator log⁡N\log N leaves −log⁡N-\log N. The two unshifted half-logarithms of the sine give −J′(x)=−Log⁡(2sin⁡πx)-J'(x)=-\operatorname{Log}(2\sin\pi x). The action and all remaining terms are exactly (17), (23). Lemma 3 identifies the action with the retained b0b_0. The uniform remainder and derivative statements follow from Lemma 1. ▫\square

For the two endpoints of one affected edge this gives BN(x,t)BN(x,u)=eN{b0(x,t)+b0(x,u)}4N2sin⁡2πx(1+Ohol(N−1)).(24) B_N(x,t)B_N(x,u) =\frac{e^{N\{b_0(x,t)+b_0(x,u)\}}}{4N^2\sin^2\pi x} (1+O_{\mathrm{ hol}}(N^{-1})). \qquad\text{(24)} At its stationary limit the leading coefficient is explicitly 14sin⁡2πx*=1−Q24>0.(25) \frac1{4\sin^2\pi x_*}=\frac{1-Q^2}{4}>0. \qquad\text{(25)} It equals 1/41/4 at a fold. For a finite matching the products of (22)–(25) hold simultaneously, with their original shared angular variables. Compact families of the stated regular data have uniform constants after taking a finite cover; no height or regularity margin is claimed outside such compact families.

There is thus no Stokes or logarithmic wall obstruction to this specific local factorial amplitude. The proof does not delete exponentially small terms of the exact factorial as a source identity: (22) is an asymptotic expansion of that exact function. The full unsummed Hessian theorem supplies nonzero coordinate Jacobians through a fold, so these affected factors introduce no additional zero under its local Morse change of variables.

The other factors of the original source remain in place: unrelated halfwires, the full-Hermite angular kernels, both charged cosine terms, all constants and spectators, and any genuine free cardinal ends. A nonzero local factorial density does not establish the coefficient of the geometric exponential sector in their prescribed integral. In particular neither a global contour coefficient nor a metric completion at a two-square limit follows.
