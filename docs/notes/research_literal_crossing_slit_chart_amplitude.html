Literal crossing amplitudes on complex slit charts

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The exact root-factorial interpolant has a uniform nonzero local amplitude on a larger domain than its original vertical strip. The domain includes every nonreal scaled factorial length. Applied to the literal balanced signed crossing, this gives its actual five-factor amplitude without a coherent occupation sum or a change of the physical imaginary quantum ray.

The hypothesis is that all the affine factorial lengths lie in one compact part of the stated slit domain. Existence of such a simultaneous logarithmic chart at a geometric representation is not proved here. Neither an interpolation nor its nonzero amplitude selects the original finite support cycle.

The complete proof directs are I, the exact factorial interpolant; V, its moving-period version; and B, the literal balanced crossing. Their pins appear below.

2. Uniform asymptotics and bounded endpoint shifts

Uniformly on every K⋐𝒟K\Subset\mathcal D, Log⁡GN(Nz)=NJ(z)+12Log⁡(2Nsin⁡πz)+πcot⁡πz12N+OK(N−3).(8) \operatorname{Log} G_N(Nz) =NJ(z)+\tfrac12\operatorname{Log}(2N\sin\pi z) +\frac{\pi\cot\pi z}{12N}+O_K(N^{-3}). \qquad\text{(8)} All fixed holomorphic derivatives have the same remainder bound on a smaller compact set.

Here is why the strip proof extends without a new summation argument. For the finitely many small kk, the numbers z,k+z,k−zz,k+z,k-z, with z∈Kz\in K, avoid zero and the negative real ray. Compactness puts them in sectors |arg⁡w|≤π−δK|\arg w|\le\pi-\delta_K, with moduli bounded below. For the remaining kk, the arguments in (4) lie in a fixed smaller sector around the positive real axis and have size comparable to NkNk. The uniform Gamma expansion used and justified in I therefore has remainder OK(N−3)O_K(N^{-3}) for the initial term and OK(N−3k−3)O_K(N^{-3}k^{-3}) for the kk-th product term. To make its enlarged sector scope explicit, the Euler remainder after 1/(12w)1/(12w), initially on ℜw>0\Re w>0, can be integrated by parts twice. It is a fixed multiple of w−3w^{-3} plus a fixed multiple of ∫0∞B̃4(t)(w+t)−4dt\int_0^\infty\widetilde B_4(t)(w+t)^{-4}\,dt, where B̃4\widetilde B_4 is the bounded periodic fourth Bernoulli polynomial. This integral is holomorphic on the plane slit along the nonpositive real ray, so the same formula continues there. If |arg⁡w|≤π−δ|\arg w|\le\pi-\delta, splitting the integral at 2|w|2|w| bounds its absolute value by Cδ|w|−3C_\delta|w|^{-3}: on the first interval |w+t|≥cδ|w||w+t|\ge c_\delta|w|, and on the tail |w+t|≥t/2|w+t|\ge t/2. Summing (9), including the initial Gamma factor, proves (8). The proof uses the actual product, not continuation of an asymptotic identity without a remainder bound.

4. Physical meridian normals and genuine quantum normals

The estimates are joint in bounded α\alpha, on larger compacts before differentiation. Since ∂M=−2(hNM)−1∂α\partial_M=-2(h_NM)^{-1}\partial_\alpha, each fixed meridian normal has polynomial NN-cost. This differentiates the entire scalar in (18) or (20), including κ\kappa. It does not differentiate a specialized color product as if its meridian dependence had disappeared.

Moving-quantum coefficient normals can also be retained. Fix NN and all original indices, let q=qNeϵ,P=πilog⁡q,(24) q=q_Ne^\epsilon,\qquad P=\frac{\pi i}{\log q}, \qquad\text{(24)} and use V’s same Gamma product F̂P\widehat F_P, not a root reflection with a moving endpoint. In (15)–(21) replace the quantum parameters by these actual generic ones, keep d=N−1d=N-1 fixed, and use α(P)=−2log⁡M/(2log⁡q)\alpha(P)=-2\log M/(2\log q). The finite product identities remain exact as analytic coefficient identities. If the original finite-color normal ties MM to qq, that relation must be substituted before differentiation. A fixed-MM auxiliary coefficient germ is not relabeled as that entire representation family.

The sector argument of Section 2 works uniformly for PP in a sufficiently small relative complex neighborhood of NN, with every scaled argument divided by PP. At fixed indices its logarithmic PP-derivative is bounded on these compacts: differentiating PJ(ζ/P)P J(\zeta/P) gives J(y)−yJ′(y)J(y)-yJ'(y), and the remainder is differentiated on a larger relative disk. For fixed MM, the bounded color shift causes only bounded additional terms. Since P′(ϵ)=O(N2)P'(\epsilon)=O(N^2), every factorial ratio and exact monomial has logarithmic ϵ\epsilon-derivative O(N2)O(N^2). For κ\kappa this follows directly from log⁡κ=(log⁡x)2/(2log⁡q)\log\kappa=(\log x)^2/(2\log q), with log⁡x=πi−log⁡q+log⁡M\log x=\pi i-\log q+\log M.

Consequently, for all sufficiently large NN, there is a disk |ϵ|≤c/N2|\epsilon|\le c/N^2 on which the full local coefficient divided by its root value is bounded above and below by fixed constants. Cauchy’s formula gives, for every fixed jj, |∂ϵjR±|ϵ=0|≤CjN2j|R±|ϵ=0.(25) \left|\partial_\epsilon^jR_\pm\big|_{\epsilon=0}\right| \le C_jN^{2j}|R_\pm|_{\epsilon=0}. \qquad\text{(25)} Here all five lengths lie in the compact slit chart, so the root coefficient is nonzero. Fixed physical endpoint factorials outside this interior hypothesis require V’s separate endpoint bounds; (25) does not silently include them. The fixed-MM convention in (24) and the fixed endpoint d=N−1d=N-1 are part of this assertion.

The same proof includes a specified original parameter path M(ϵ)M(\epsilon) with M(0)=e−hNα0/2M(0)=e^{-h_N\alpha_0/2}, bounded α0\alpha_0, and log⁡M(ϵ)−log⁡M(0)=O(Nϵ)\log M(\epsilon)-\log M(0)=O(N\epsilon) holomorphically on a slightly larger disk, with its Cauchy derivative bounds. Then α(ϵ)\alpha(\epsilon) remains bounded and ∂ϵα=O(N2)\partial_\epsilon\alpha=O(N^2); its extra logarithmic derivative has the same cost as in (25). In particular the ordinary color relation M=−qNM=-q^N, based at M=1M=1, is covered. All its parameter derivatives, rather than a root-only reflection formula, must be taken.

5. The source cycle and the outstanding chart condition

For a literal finite word, replace only its original factorial products by (2), retaining the original affine matching lattice. I’s sum of small product cotangent circles about the supported integer points is then an exact period of that same finite sum. Equations (17)–(22) identify its local signed summands; they do not replace its support by independent head intervals. There are no extra coherent input/output occupation sums here.

Five-shape regularity says that certain exponentiated clocks avoid zero and one. It alone does not put all their compatible affine logarithmic lengths in 𝒟\mathcal D. On real exterior lengths the actual interpolant can have finite-level zeros or poles; no uniform nonzero amplitude on a neighborhood containing them follows from this note. Any whole-ratio cancellation must be verified before changing that conclusion.

Even on a paid common chart, the original cotangent cycles, physical support walls, and residual framing direction remain. No contributing contour, framing-fiber period, geometric coefficient, or scalar lower bound is asserted. This is an explicit adapter for the existing factorial interpolation, not a novelty claim for Gamma asymptotics.
