A fixed-radius smoothing bound and its explicit limitation

30 October 2026. ; source-coverage correction below. A one-variable test at the original positive face variables gives a strict source upper bound after a literal untwisted L_1 insertion. It needs neither a balanced split nor an attained smoothed capacity. The test is not universal: an explicit certified family below makes its best possible gap tend to minus infinity. This is a failure of fixed old radii, not a counterexample to a fully optimized smoothing gap or a volume conjecture. The family is proved as a labeled right-angled MATCHED-GRAPH capacity example. Its identification with the fixed flat-plat source coefficient is conditional on an additional source-preserving presentation adapter; section3 does not supply that adapter.

1. An exact sufficient source test

Use the original connected certified source, actual marked faces u,v, and the normalization of the smoothing capacity theorem. Let a specified UNTWISTED port have triangles (x,b,y),(x,c,y). Its replacement L_1=I deletes those two triangles and identifies b,c. Assume the resulting projection is connected, the marked faces remain distinct, and b,c are distinct unmarked faces. All repeated incidences after the identification are retained. The deleted port’s original half twist must be zero; a remaining R^epsilon is not an identity.

Let l^0 be the original positive finite face curvatures (reciprocals of Euclidean circle radii), with the two marked line variables zero. The established term “fixed-radius bound” refers to these face variables; it does not identify them with Euclidean radii. Write F for the source-normalized local fold rate, with

F(t l_0,t l_1,t l_2)=F(l_0,l_1,l_2)+(1/2)log t,
partial_(log l_j) F=theta_j/(2pi),
theta_j=2atan(l_j/sqrt(l_0 l_1+l_1 l_2+l_2 l_0)).   (1)

At a one-positive-variable triple the nonzero angle is pi. These are the continuous boundary values, not a different local model. The exact original action is

Phi_old=sum_(all triangles) F(l_t^0)
                     -sum_(old finite faces) log l_f^0.

For s real, replace BOTH b,c by z=exp(s), leaving every other finite face variable at l^0. Sum over the actual remaining triangles, and count the merged face only once in the target monomial. Define

P_sm(s)=sum_(remaining triangles) F(l_t(s))
                     -sum_(smoothed finite faces) log l_f(s),
G(s)=Phi_old-P_sm(s).                               (2)

If G(s_)=g>0 for one finite s_, then the original marked contraction with that L_1 insertion and all other circles complete satisfies

limsup_(N->infinity) N^-1 log|A_N^(L1)|
                                <=Phi_old-g.       (3)

Every retained half twist on another port stays in its exact source sum. Its channel phase has modulus one, so the upper bound is valid with those phases. The original mark, N-power measure, multiplicities, beta factors, and full range of every remaining channel are retained.

Proof: the exact marked face-coefficient theorem applies to the literal shorter diagram. For each label assignment its positive coefficient is bounded by evaluation at the shared radii in (2), divided by its actual target monomial. The degenerate local cases in the smoothing theorem bound every original beta^2 D D P factor by its fold rate at these same radii. Finite labels and original scalar factors contribute only a polynomial in N. Taking the sum in absolute value proves (3). No minimizer of the smoothed capacity is used. If a remaining source factor is identically zero, the bound is already stronger. The statement does not move the mark or substitute a second boundary trace.

Log-radius convexity of F makes P_sm convex, hence G concave. Its derivative is the explicit actual incidence sum

G'(s)=1-(1/(2pi))sum_(remaining b/c corners) theta_corner(s).
                                                           (4)

If a triangle acquires the merged label twice, both corners occur in this sum. Thus the best test in (2) is a one-dimensional convex minimization, with no split-side imbalance hypothesis. It may hold when the full smoothed minimizer escapes to a boundary. It is only a sufficient test; section3 proves that it need not hold at all.

2. Boundary regularization and the precise charged extension

The strict bound has a useful continuity margin. Set the two marked face variables to small FIXED positive values eta_u,eta_v before coefficient extraction. Keep their target exponents zero, and keep all other target exponents d=N-1. Each nonzero local triple in (2) has a continuous F limit as eta_u,eta_v tend to zero. Hence these values can be fixed sufficiently small that the new evaluation rate is at most Phi_old-g/2. This does not assign target degree d to a marked face and does not take a quantum-level-dependent limit.

More precisely, let P_N be one of the actual nonnegative-coefficient polynomial products after smoothing, including beta factors, and let n* be the actual target vector. At the fixed positive evaluation l*, any integer shift nu obeys

|[L^(n*+nu)]P_N| <= P_N(l*)/(l*)^(n*)
                              exp(C_l ||nu||_1).   (5)

A negative target exponent gives zero. Phased identifications such as c=Q^p b cost nothing in this bound: take absolute values of the polynomial coefficients before evaluation. A finite sum of such specified coefficients with total scalar norm at most N^C C_0^H and ||nu||_1<=C_1 H therefore costs only N^C exp(C_2 H) in addition to the regularized capacity. If H<=delta N with delta sufficiently small, it still has a strict exponential gap.

Equation(5) is a coefficient bound, NOT an already proved L_h source expansion. Actual L_h paths retain their unwrapped physical endpoints, their c=Q^p b identifications and all clock charges. In particular, after a charged modular face average a target congruence can cross N and admit a new exponent shifted by an ENTIRE N, not merely O(h). Those aliases are not controlled by (5) with H=O(h). They must either be excluded by the actual support or be estimated separately. The fixed positive marked radii resolve the elementary Laurent-coefficient bound; they do not silently remove these finite-source questions. No signed deleted-port R^epsilon norm is discarded.

3. A certified matched-graph family where the capacity test fails

The initial white triangulation is the octahedral sphere with equator 0,1,2,3 and poles4,5: its triangles are

(p,j,j+1 modulo4), p=4,5, j=0,1,2,3.

Mark the actual MAIN edge23. Choose the matching edges of the dual cubic source by the white edges

{01,12,34,35}.                                     (6)

They cover each triangle exactly once, and do not contain the marked edge. Starting with b_0=5, insert b_n into the triangular interstice (0,1,b_(n-1)), replacing that triangle by its three subdivisions. Add the matching white edge b_(n-1)b_n at each step; the matching edge01 stays. This is again a perfect matching: the new triangle (0,1,b_n) inherits01, while the other two new triangles are paired by b_(n-1)b_n. The complement is the actual union of main cycles.

To specify the marked diagram, use this EMBEDDED dual cubic graph, not just its abstract isomorphism class. Replace a small neighborhood of every selected matching edge by the standard untwisted complete crossing-circle box, with its four main half-edges in their inherited cyclic order. Join these half-edges along the complementary embedded edges, and put the original mark on edge23. This is an explicit augmented-link diagram. Each subdivision above replaces one trivalent source vertex in a small disk by a three-vertex triangle, retaining its three external ports and adding the specified matching edge. It therefore preserves this literal construction and its marking; no assertion that an arbitrary abstract cubic graph is an admissible source is needed. A Morse presentation gives its actual cups, caps, and complete-circle tensors. This construction does NOT prove that the diagram has the fixed flat-plat presentation required by the marked face-coefficient theorem, sections1–3, with the same designated circle ports and mark. That theorem’s gap sweep and marked conservation argument cannot simply be imported into an arbitrary Morse presentation. Neither a uniform bridge width over this family nor a source-preserving flat-plat adapter at a fixed depth is established here.

Here is the labeled geometric certificate, not just a combinatorial example. In the boundary plane use the two marked parallel lines y=0 and y=2 for faces3 and2. The four finite initial circles have

face0: center(0,1/2), radius1/2;
face1: center(0,3/2), radius1/2;
face4: center(-sqrt2,1), radius1;
face5: center(+sqrt2,1), radius1.

The inserted circles have center and radius

center(b_n)=((2n+sqrt2)/B_n,1), radius(b_n)=1/B_n,
B_n=4n^2+4sqrt2 n+1.                               (7)

They are tangent to circles0,1 and b_(n-1), and lie strictly inside that triangle’s old interstice. These statements follow directly by squaring the center distances; equivalently their curvatures satisfy the positive Descartes recurrence with the two fixed curvatures2,2. All other old circles and contacts are unchanged. Thus this is a labeled packing with precisely the stated triangulation at every n.

Its halfspace certificate can also be read directly. Take the geodesic planes of these white circles and the circles through the three contact points of each complementary triangle. The latter are orthogonal to its three white circles. Their inward halfspaces give the initial ideal right-angled cuboctahedron. Each insertion attaches the ideal right-angled octahedron with the three old and three new contact points along the old shaded triangle. Its adjacent white planes are the same planes, so the seams are flat and all remaining dihedral angles are pi/2. The three new outward shaded disks are contained in the old outward shaded disk and tangent to its boundary at the old contacts. Every new ideal vertex satisfies all the other old supporting inequalities, strictly when it is not incident to that facet. Thus the coplanar seams disappear and the union is the intersection of precisely the retained and new inward halfspaces; it is convex, rather than merely locally right-angled. Induction gives a convex ideal right-angled polyhedron with the exact new labeled facets. No unlabeled volume match is used as a certificate.

Consequently the labeled packing face curvatures are exactly

l_0=l_1=2, l_4=l_5=1, l_2=l_3=0, l_(b_j)=B_j.

The dual cubic graph is 3-connected: the initial cube is 3-connected, and replacing a vertex by its three-edge triangle preserves this property. Remove the matching port01 and its two triangles (0,1,b_n),(0,1,4), and merge b_n with4. The remaining projection is connected (already deleting the two old cubic vertices leaves a connected graph), and the marked faces2,3 remain distinct. Thus this is the literal opposite matching-edge smoothing of this marked graph. Applying section1 to its actual quantum source would additionally require the presentation adapter just specified. The geometric and capacity calculations do not require the main cycles to have one component.

Only five remaining triangle terms depend on the new common radius z: two are (2,B_(n-1),z), two are (2,z,0), and one is (z,0,0). The graph capacity functional (2) is therefore exactly

G_n(z)=C_n-2F(2,B_(n-1),z)-2F(2,z,0)+(1/2)log z,
C_n=F(2,B_n,2)+2F(2,B_(n-1),B_n)
              +F(2,1,2)+2F(2,1,0)-log B_n.       (8)

This includes both deleted triangles and the changed face-target monomial. In particular it is not just a comparison of local volumes.

We prove the obstruction uniformly over ALL z>0. Monotonicity and homogeneity of F give

2F(2,B_(n-1),z)>=log B_(n-1),
2F(2,z,0)>=log max(2,z).

Thus the z-dependent expression in (8) is bounded above by its n-dependent constant minus (1/2)|log(z/2)|. Evaluation at z=2 and the following limits show that its maximizers stay in one fixed compact subset of (0,infinity). On that compact, continuity and homogeneity give uniformly

F(2,B_n,2)=(1/2)log B_n+o(1),
F(2,B_(n-1),B_n)=(1/2)log B_n+F(0,1,1)+o(1),
2F(2,B_(n-1),z)=log B_(n-1)+o(1).

The limiting z-dependent function is -2F(2,z,0)+(1/2)log z. Its derivative vanishes only when the angle at z is pi/2, namely at z=2; strict log-radius convexity makes this its unique maximum. Substituting in (8) proves

sup_(z>0) G_n(z)=-log n+K+o(1),
K=F(2,1,2)+2F(2,1,0)-(3/2)log2.                  (9)

In particular the best fixed-old-radius test is NEGATIVE for every sufficiently large n. Full radius readjustment is essential here. This does not say that the fully optimized smoothed capacity exceeds the original capacity: the former is an infimum over ALL remaining radii, whereas (8) varies only the merged radius.

4. Remaining general comparison

Literal smoothing identifies opposite middle faces. It is not ordinary deletion of the shared white edge01, and a generic graph-minor volume inequality is not automatically its capacity inequality. Section3 also rules out promoting the one-variable test to a theorem forced by every positive labeled certificate.

The general connected untwisted smoothing gap and its near-complete small-color corridor are now proved separately in the original fixed flat-plat scope. That theorem does not provide the missing presentation adapter for the family in section3. The present note retains its exact sufficient source bound in section1 and its independent matched-graph functional limitation. Signed varied ports and far-color source bounds remain separate.

Focused audit: original source and labeled certificate checks, with saved results. At 80 digits it checks ten literal original marked L_1 contractions against the shorter face-coefficient source, retaining every remaining half twist and the original cups, mark, and measure. These original matrix fixtures test section1; they are NOT the Apollonian graphs of section3 and do not establish the missing adapter. The audit also checks two symbolic tangency identities; nine perfect matchings, three-connected graphs, and connected literal smoothings; 2,340 white and 3,816 shaded support values; and 432 original right-angle incidences. Seven finite optimized one-variable profiles and one positive-marked regularization check diagnose the exact normalization. The analytic argument, not those finite profiles, proves the all-n limitation in (9).

No full optimized smoothing gap or L_h alias bound is inferred.

Condition (3) is sufficient. The charge and alias qualifications after (5), and the fixed-old-face scope of the counterfamily, remain essential.

Source-coverage correction, 30 October 2026

The family in Section 3 proves the geometric matched-graph capacity obstruction (9). It is not a source counterexample to the fixed-radius strategy: the exact face identity proves a fixed flat-plat formula, whereas that inference would require a general Morse formula. A link-to-plat isotopy alone does not preserve the designated complete-circle tensors, mark or face-coefficient normalization.
