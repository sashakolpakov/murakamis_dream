One finite hypergeometric sum for the original multiplication tensor

21 November 2026. Research note. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The generating polynomial of the centered Conway multiplication tensor can be recovered from one finite, very-well-poised 6ϕ5{}_6\phi_5 partial sum. The original Frobenius source is a specified first derivative of that sum at the root. The sum itself vanishes there, so its value cannot replace the derivative.

The proof calibrates a generic-node interpolation at both values and first derivatives before taking the limit. It does not use Rogers’ infinite summation formula on the unit circle. No geometric noncancellation or volume bound is claimed.

Here TN(cos⁡θ)=cos⁡(Nθ)T_N(\cos\theta)=\cos(N\theta). The centered compiler supplies the basis fa=N−1/2Rpa(z)f_a=N^{-1/2}R\,p_a(z), where p0=1p_0=1, and ϕ(fafb)=δab,Cabc=ϕ(fafbfc),GN(t;z)=∑a=0dpa(z)ta.(2) \phi(f_af_b)=\delta_{ab},\qquad C_{abc}=\phi(f_af_bf_c),\qquad G_N(t;z)=\sum_{a=0}^dp_a(z)t^a . \qquad\text{(2)} The three-boundary polynomial of interest is exactly 𝒞N(t1,t2,t3)=∑a,b,c=0dCabct1at2bt3c=N−3/2ϕ(R3∏ν=13GN(tν;z)).(3) \mathcal C_N(t_1,t_2,t_3) =\sum_{a,b,c=0}^dC_{abc}t_1^at_2^bt_3^c =N^{-3/2}\phi\!\left(R^3\prod_{\nu=1}^3G_N(t_\nu;z)\right). \qquad\text{(3)} In particular this uses the original closing, not the bilinear functional ϕ(R2⋅)/N\phi(R^2\,\cdot)/N with its extra RR omitted.

2. Generic finite products recover the whole Hermite class

Keep NN fixed and let ξ\xi vary near qq. Write Qξ=ξ2,cj(ξ)=ξQξj+ξ−1Qξ−j,κξ=ξd2/2,ωj(ξ)=κξ(−1)jξj(j+1).(10) Q_\xi=\xi^2,\quad c_j(\xi)=\xi Q_\xi^j+\xi^{-1}Q_\xi^{-j},\quad \kappa_\xi=\xi^{d^2/2},\quad \omega_j(\xi)=\kappa_\xi(-1)^j\xi^{j(j+1)} . \qquad\text{(10)} A fixed holomorphic branch of the power defines κξ\kappa_\xi. Introduce the finite products Hj(ξ;t)=[1−(−ξt)N]∏ℓ=−j+10(1+ξ−1Qξℓt)∏ℓ=1j+1(1+ξ−1Qξℓt).(11) H_j(\xi;t)= \bigl[1-(-\xi t)^N\bigr]\, \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=-j+1}^{0} (1+\xi^{-1}Q_\xi^\ell t)} {\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{j+1} (1+\xi^{-1}Q_\xi^\ell t)} . \qquad\text{(11)} For fixed NN, these are holomorphic for ξ\xi sufficiently near qq and tt in any fixed closed disk inside the unit disk.

Lemma 1. Both polynomial families extend holomorphically to ξ=q\xi=q, and G̃q(t;z)=GN(t;z),R̃q(z)=R(z)in ℋN.(12) \widetilde G_q(t;z)=G_N(t;z),\qquad \widetilde R_q(z)=R(z) \quad\text{in }\mathcal H_N . \qquad\text{(12)}

Proof. The only coincident nodes at qq are the pairs j,k=d−jj,k=d-j. Their differences have nonzero first derivative: with a dot denoting ξ∂ξ\xi\partial_\xi, ċj(q)−ċk(q)=2Nsj.(13) \dot c_j(q)-\dot c_k(q)=2Ns_j . \qquad\text{(13)} The two values in each pair agree at the root. For HjH_j this follows from (4); for ωj\omega_j it follows directly from (10). Thus all corresponding divided differences have removable singularities. Grouping each pair in Lagrange interpolation proves regularity of the coefficients. Equivalently, one may use Newton interpolation with each coalescing pair adjacent: the only vanishing denominator is its simple difference (13).

It remains to check which Hermite polynomial these limits give. The common scalar in (11) cancels from a difference of logarithmic derivatives. For k=d−jk=d-j, the extra numerator indices of HkH_k are −k+1,…,−j-k+1,\ldots,-j; adding NN identifies them with the extra denominator indices j+2,…,N−jj+2,\ldots,N-j. Together with (4), this proves the first equality in (12), including every double-node derivative.

The original crossing has the nodal values R(cj)=ωj(q),R′(cj)R(cj)=2j−d2sj(15) R(c_j)=\omega_j(q),\qquad \frac{R'(c_j)}{R(c_j)} =\frac{2j-d}{2s_j} \qquad\text{(15)} at the paired nodes. From (10), ω̇j(q)−ω̇k(q)ωj(q)=j(j+1)−k(k+1)=N(2j−d).(16) \frac{\dot\omega_j(q)-\dot\omega_k(q)}{\omega_j(q)} =j(j+1)-k(k+1)=N(2j-d). \qquad\text{(16)} Equations (13) and (16) reproduce (15). These interpolating families are auxiliary. No claim is made that their off-root values equal an independently chosen moving-meridian or generic quantum-group source. Their entire root Hermite limits have been checked, which is what (3) needs.

3. A weighted node limit is the original Frobenius source

Write [m]ξ=ξm−ξ−mξ−ξ−1,ℒξ(f)=η[N]ξ∑j=0d[2j+1]ξf(cj(ξ)).(17) [m]_\xi=\frac{\xi^m-\xi^{-m}}{\xi-\xi^{-1}},\qquad \mathcal L_\xi(f)= \frac{\eta}{[N]_\xi} \sum_{j=0}^d[2j+1]_\xi f(c_j(\xi)). \qquad\text{(17)}

5. A reduced vertex on the original coefficient cycle

The scalar factors DN(tν)D_N(t_\nu) in (26) must remain in the polynomial identity. There is a separate exact simplification for the closed-tree coefficient period.

Reverse finite vertex indices, with the corresponding swaps of the signed inputs and original caps, as in the generating-section assembly theorem. Every edge then has anti-diagonal contraction. If VvV_v is the generating polynomial of its actual finite vertex array, the original scalar is [∏eted]∏vVv((te)e∋v).(31) [\,\prod_e t_e^d\,]\prod_v V_v((t_e)_{e\ni v}). \qquad\text{(31)} All powers in these vertex polynomials are nonnegative. On such a trivalent vertex define the analytic Taylor section 𝒞̂N(𝒕)=(−1)N(q−q−1)κ32N5/2∏ν=13(1+qtν)−1(ξ∂ξ)SN(ξ;𝒕)|ξ=q.(32) \widehat{\mathcal C}_N(\mathbf t)= \frac{(-1)^N(q-q^{-1})\kappa^3}{2N^{5/2}} \prod_{\nu=1}^3(1+qt_\nu)^{-1} \left.(\xi\partial_\xi)S_N(\xi;\mathbf t)\right|_{\xi=q}. \qquad\text{(32)} It is regular near the origin by (25), and (26) says exactly 𝒞N(𝒕)=∏ν=13DN(tν)𝒞̂N(𝒕).(33) \mathcal C_N(\mathbf t) =\prod_{\nu=1}^3D_N(t_\nu)\, \widehat{\mathcal C}_N(\mathbf t). \qquad\text{(33)} Since DN(t)=1+O(tN)D_N(t)=1+O(t^N), these two sections agree modulo the ideal generated by t1N,t2N,t3Nt_1^N,t_2^N,t_3^N. They therefore give the same original coefficient in (31): after expanding the difference one vertex at a time, each extra term has degree at least N>dN>d on some edge, and no other factor has negative powers to bring it back to degree dd. The coefficient-conjugate vertex has the conjugate Taylor section.

Equivalently, (31) has its prescribed small-circle representation 1(2πi)|E|∮⋯∮∏vVv((te)e∋v)∏edteteN,(34) \frac1{(2\pi i)^{|E|}} \oint\cdots\oint \prod_v V_v((t_e)_{e\ni v})\, \prod_e\frac{dt_e}{t_e^N}, \qquad\text{(34)} with all circles sufficiently small and positively oriented. The replacements (32) preserve this entire period, including its actual input and closing arrays. Thus every multiplication vertex can be written using the one finite sum in (25), its specified normal and the simple factors (1+qtν)−1(1+qt_\nu)^{-1}, on the original Taylor cycle.

This is a statement about these nonnegative auxiliary generating powers. It is not a rule for deleting a peripheral MNM^N factor, setting a parameter equal to one, reversing an untruncated Taylor series, or moving the cycle through a singularity.

6. What has been simplified

The three-boundary tensor is now a prescribed confluent finite hypergeometric source. No unknown interpolation coefficient or choice of cycle has been added. Both the simple node and all double-node derivatives survive.

The identity is initially proved on the unit polydisk, where every factor is regular. Since its left side is a polynomial, the full right side has the corresponding removable extension wherever an individual displayed denominator vanishes. This does not allow individual terms, or their derivatives, to be discarded.

Evaluating the sum only at the root would give zero by (30); it would lose the original source. Replacing it by an infinite Rogers product would require a separate argument and is not done. No nonzero leading coefficient, geometric saddle or lower bound for an arbitrary short component follows from (26) alone.
