A canonical finite splitting of the full Hermite filling kernel

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The physical circle basis gives an explicit endomorphism whose image is exactly the kernel of the original filling functional. Its one-dimensional complement has a specified period normalizer. The endomorphism and its inverse on that kernel have polynomial norm bounds, for every fixed signed crossing power.

This is finite algebra on the original full Hermite space. It neither replaces the closing functional nor supplies a lower bound for its value on an exterior source. In particular the conjugated endomorphism below is not identified with off-grid Gaussian averaging.

The sole complete proof directs are N, the physical finite Newton calculus, and F, the actual Fourier–Hermite crossing-circle kernel. Their exact paths and pins are recorded at the end.

1. The physical space, row and circle basis

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, and the original lifts q=eπi/N,κ=qd2/2,ℋN=ℂ[z]/(χN),χN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2.(1) q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad \kappa=q^{d^2/2},\qquad \mathcal H_N=\mathbb C[z]/(\chi_N),\qquad \chi_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2. \qquad\text{(1)} The quotient includes all first normals at double roots and the single value at the odd-level simple root. Let RR be the actual physical crossing of N, with its Cartan phase. Its values at every root are nonzero, so it is a unit of the full quotient. Negative powers below mean its actual inverse.

The original pivotal circle operators are the basis Lj=(−1)j−1Uj−1(z/2),1≤j≤N.(2) L_j=(-1)^{j-1}U_{j-1}(z/2),\qquad 1\le j\le N. \qquad\text{(2)} They are a basis because their degrees are j−1j-1, with nonzero leading coefficients. Use their coefficient norm ∥∑j=1NcjLj∥∘=∑j=1N|cj|.(3) \left\|\sum_{j=1}^N c_jL_j\right\|_\circ=\sum_{j=1}^N|c_j|. \qquad\text{(3)} This is a specified finite norm, not an energy defined using an unknown knot value.

Write ϕ\phi for the actual cylindrical closing functional of N. Its normalization and that of an integral filling are ϕ(f)=(−1)dN2[zd](fmod⁡χN),ϕn(f)=ϕ(Rnf),n∈ℤ.(4) \phi(f)=(-1)^dN^2[z^d](f\bmod\chi_N),\qquad \phi_n(f)=\phi(R^nf),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(4)} Thus ϕ(Lj)=0(j<N),ϕ(LN)=N2.(5) \phi(L_j)=0\quad(j<N),\qquad \phi(L_N)=N^2. \qquad\text{(5)} All peripheral or framing factors already outside this physical pair row remain outside it. Nothing is divided by a quantum dimension or by the possibly nilpotent operator LNL_N.

2. The zero-twist average and its exact inverse off one line

On z=−2cos⁡tz=-2\cos t, the actual circle basis has the identity Lj(−2cos⁡t)=sin⁡(jt)sin⁡t.(6) L_j(-2\cos t)=\frac{\sin(jt)}{\sin t}. \qquad\text{(6)} For the unique polynomial representative of degree <N<N, average its sine-weighted value over one grid cell: 1sin⁡t∫−1/21/2sin⁡(t+2πρ/N)f(−2cos⁡(t+2πρ/N))dρ.(7) \frac1{\sin t}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \sin(t+2\pi\rho/N)\, f(-2\cos(t+2\pi\rho/N))\,d\rho . \qquad\text{(7)} Equation (6) shows directly that (7) is again a polynomial of degree <N<N, and its action on the basis is K0Lj=λjLj,λj=sin⁡(jπ/N)jπ/N.(8) K_0L_j=\lambda_jL_j,\qquad \lambda_j=\frac{\sin(j\pi/N)}{j\pi/N}. \qquad\text{(8)} At sine zeros (7) is interpreted by this polynomial identity. Equivalently (8) defines K0K_0 intrinsically in the specified physical basis; it does not require choosing another representative.

For 1≤j<N1\le j<N, λj>0\lambda_j>0, whereas λN=0\lambda_N=0. Consequently ker⁡K0=ℂLN,im⁡K0=ker⁡ϕ.(9) \ker K_0=\mathbb C L_N,\qquad \operatorname{im}K_0=\ker\phi. \qquad\text{(9)} Define G0Lj={λj−1Lj,j<N,0,j=N,P0f=ϕ(f)N2LN.(10) G_0L_j= \begin{cases}\lambda_j^{-1}L_j,&j<N,\\0,&j=N,\end{cases} \qquad P_0f=\frac{\phi(f)}{N^2}L_N . \qquad\text{(10)} Then the complete exact identities are K0G0=G0K0=I−P0,G0P0=P0G0=K0P0=P0K0=0.(11) K_0G_0=G_0K_0=I-P_0,\qquad G_0P_0=P_0G_0=K_0P_0=P_0K_0=0. \qquad\text{(11)} The function sin⁡x/x\sin x/x decreases on 0<x<π0<x<\pi: the numerator of its derivative is xcos⁡x−sin⁡xx\cos x-\sin x, whose derivative is −xsin⁡x<0-x\sin x<0 and whose value at zero is zero. Since sin⁡(π/N)≥2/N\sin(\pi/N)\ge2/N, this proves ∥K0∥∘≤1,∥P0∥∘=1,∥G0∥∘≤πN2.(12) \|K_0\|_\circ\le1,\qquad \|P_0\|_\circ=1,\qquad \|G_0\|_\circ\le\frac{\pi N}{2}. \qquad\text{(12)} These are induced operator norms. The estimate includes N=2N=2.

3. Conjugation by the actual signed crossing

Let MfM_f denote multiplication by ff in the full quotient. For every signed integer nn, set K̃n=MR−nK0MRn,Gn=MR−nG0MRn,un=R−nLNN2,Pnf=unϕn(f).(13) \widetilde K_n=M_{R^{-n}}K_0M_{R^n},\qquad G_n=M_{R^{-n}}G_0M_{R^n},\qquad u_n=\frac{R^{-n}L_N}{N^2},\qquad P_nf=u_n\phi_n(f). \qquad\text{(13)} All operations in (13) are on quotient classes, with every Hermite normal retained. Equations (5), (9)–(11) give ϕn(un)=1,ker⁡K̃n=ℂun,im⁡K̃n=ker⁡ϕn,(14) \phi_n(u_n)=1,\qquad \ker\widetilde K_n=\mathbb C u_n,\qquad \operatorname{im}\widetilde K_n=\ker\phi_n, \qquad\text{(14)} K̃nGn=GnK̃n=I−Pn,GnPn=PnGn=K̃nPn=PnK̃n=0.(15) \widetilde K_nG_n=G_n\widetilde K_n=I-P_n,\qquad G_nP_n=P_nG_n=\widetilde K_nP_n=P_n\widetilde K_n=0. \qquad\text{(15)} In particular K̃n\widetilde K_n has rank N−1N-1, and GnG_n is its genuine two-sided inverse on ker⁡ϕn\ker\phi_n. Every original boundary class has the exact decomposition f=K̃nGnf+unϕn(f).(16) f=\widetilde K_nG_nf+u_n\phi_n(f). \qquad\text{(16)} Thus the sequence 0→ℂ→c↦cunℋN→K̃nℋN→ϕnℂ→0(17) 0\longrightarrow\mathbb C \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{c\mapsto cu_n}\mathcal H_N \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\widetilde K_n}\mathcal H_N \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\phi_n}\mathbb C \longrightarrow0 \qquad\text{(17)} is exact, with the displayed splitting. The last coordinate is the original period itself, not a substitute for it. This is a sequence of complex vector spaces: ker⁡ϕn\ker\phi_n is not asserted to be an ideal, and (17) is not a quotient-algebra or ℋN\mathcal H_N-module construction.

4. Polynomial conditioning with no deleted normal directions

Here is an elementary multiplication estimate in (3). Put Cr(z)=Ur(z/2)C_r(z)=U_r(z/2), r≥0r\ge0. Laurent expansion after z=w+w−1z=w+w^{-1} proves the polynomial fusion identity CrCs=∑j=0min⁡(r,s)Cr+s−2j.(18) C_rC_s=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,s)}C_{r+s-2j}. \qquad\text{(18)} For the basis products, r,s≤N−1r,s\le N-1, so every index on the right is at most 2N−22N-2. For 0≤v≤N−20\le v\le N-2, another Laurent identity gives 2TN(z/2)Cv=CN+v−CN−v−2. 2T_N(z/2)C_v=C_{N+v}-C_{N-v-2}. Using the full relation 2TN(z/2)=−22T_N(z/2)=-2, including multiplicities, one obtains CN+v=−2Cv+CN−v−2in ℋN.(19) C_{N+v}=-2C_v+C_{N-v-2} \quad\text{in }\mathcal H_N. \qquad\text{(19)} Each unreduced term of (18) therefore has circle coefficient mass at most three, and there are at most NN such terms. The signs in (2) do not affect this bound. It follows that ∥LiLj∥∘≤3N,∥fg∥∘≤3N∥f∥∘∥g∥∘,∥Mf∥∘≤3N∥f∥∘.(20) \|L_iL_j\|_\circ\le3N,\qquad \|fg\|_\circ\le3N\|f\|_\circ\|g\|_\circ,\qquad \|M_f\|_\circ\le3N\|f\|_\circ . \qquad\text{(20)}

F gives the original signed-power expansion, with its full Hermite data and retained unit phase, Rn=κn∑j=1Nβj−1(n)Lj,∑j|βj−1(n)|≤2N(1+|n|/2).(21) R^n=\kappa^n\sum_{j=1}^N\beta_{j-1}^{(n)}L_j,\qquad \sum_j|\beta_{j-1}^{(n)}| \le2N(1+|n|/2). \qquad\text{(21)} This bound is for every integer nn, positive, negative or zero; it is not obtained from a semisimple evaluation of the crossing. For clarity its finite Fourier input is the paired value/normal data |hk|=1|h_k|=1 and |dk|≤|n|N/2|d_k|\le |n|N/2. Their exact alias inverse gives first-kind coefficients |a0|≤1|a_0|\le1, |aj|≤1+|n|/2|a_j|\le1+|n|/2. Taking the actual second-kind differences (−1)j(aj−aj+2)(-1)^j(a_j-a_{j+2}), with aN=aN+1=0a_N=a_{N+1}=0, gives (21).

Let bn=1+|n|/2b_n=1+|n|/2. Since |κ|=1|\kappa|=1, (20)–(21) imply ∥MR±n∥∘≤6N2bn.(22) \|M_{R^{\pm n}}\|_\circ\le6N^2b_n. \qquad\text{(22)} Combining this with (12)–(15) yields explicit uniform bounds ∥K̃n∥∘,∥Pn∥∘≤36N4bn2,∥Gn∥∘≤18πN5bn2,∥un∥∘≤6bn.(23) \boxed{\quad \|\widetilde K_n\|_\circ,\ \|P_n\|_\circ\le36N^4b_n^2,\qquad \|G_n\|_\circ\le18\pi N^5b_n^2,\qquad \|u_n\|_\circ\le6b_n . \quad} \qquad\text{(23)} For fixed signed nn, the splitting is therefore polynomially conditioned in this recorded physical circle norm. No inverse of a confluent Vandermonde matrix or small Casimir-node separation was estimated. Formula (19), rather than distinct-root reduction, keeps the full normal directions.

5. A positive metric and coupled filling slots

Declare the basis (2) orthonormal and denote its Hilbert norm by ∥⋅∥2\|\cdot\|_2. Pull this metric back by the actual multiplier: ⟨f,g⟩n=⟨Rnf,Rng⟩2.(24) \langle f,g\rangle_n =\langle R^nf,R^ng\rangle_2 . \qquad\text{(24)} Then K̃n\widetilde K_n is self-adjoint and nonnegative, with the same eigenvalues (8). The projection PnP_n is orthogonal. Its normalized vector is R−nLNR^{-n}L_N, and |ϕn(f)|=N2∥Pnf∥n,∥f−Pnf∥n≤πN2∥K̃nf∥n.(25) |\phi_n(f)|=N^2\|P_nf\|_n,\qquad \|f-P_nf\|_n \le\frac{\pi N}{2}\|\widetilde K_nf\|_n . \qquad\text{(25)} Both assertions follow by writing RnfR^nf in the orthonormal circle basis; no Cauchy equality for the exterior source is assumed. Since ∥v∥2≤∥v∥∘≤N∥v∥2\|v\|_2\le\|v\|_\circ\le\sqrt N\|v\|_2, (22) gives (6N5/2bn)−1∥f∥2≤∥f∥n≤6N5/2bn∥f∥2.(26) (6N^{5/2}b_n)^{-1}\|f\|_2 \le\|f\|_n\le6N^{5/2}b_n\|f\|_2 . \qquad\text{(26)} This is polynomial equivalence to the specified circle coefficient metric, not to every physical tensor-space norm.

For rr filling slots retain an arbitrary coupled class H∈ℋN⊗rH\in\mathcal H_N^{\otimes r} and signed powers n1,…,nrn_1,\ldots,n_r. Use the tensor metric pulled back by ⨂iMRni\bigotimes_i M_{R^{n_i}}. Let Pi,KiP_i,K_i act as Pni,K̃niP_{n_i},\widetilde K_{n_i} in their own slot, and put P=∏iPi,ϕ𝒏=⨂iϕni. P=\prod_iP_i,\qquad \phi_{\boldsymbol n}=\bigotimes_i\phi_{n_i}. The PiP_i are commuting orthogonal projections. Expanding in the tensor circle basis gives the pointwise indicator inequality 1−∏ipi≤∑i(1−pi)1-\prod_i p_i\le\sum_i(1-p_i), pi∈{0,1}p_i\in\{0,1\}, and hence ∥H−PH∥𝒏2≤∑i∥(I−Pi)H∥𝒏2≤(πN2)2∑i∥KiH∥𝒏2,|ϕ𝒏(H)|=N2r∥PH∥𝒏.(27) \boxed{\quad \|H-PH\|_{\boldsymbol n}^2 \le\sum_i\|(I-P_i)H\|_{\boldsymbol n}^2 \le\left(\frac{\pi N}{2}\right)^2 \sum_i\|K_iH\|_{\boldsymbol n}^2,\qquad |\phi_{\boldsymbol n}(H)|=N^{2r}\|PH\|_{\boldsymbol n}. \quad} \qquad\text{(27)} No factorization of HH was used. Thus, if the actual source satisfies the additional estimate (πN2)2∑i∥KiH∥𝒏2≤δ2∥H∥𝒏2,0≤δ<1, \left(\frac{\pi N}{2}\right)^2 \sum_i\|K_iH\|_{\boldsymbol n}^2 \le\delta^2\|H\|_{\boldsymbol n}^2, \qquad 0\le\delta<1, orthogonality gives the exact consequence |ϕ𝒏(H)|≥N2r1−δ2∥H∥𝒏.(28) |\phi_{\boldsymbol n}(H)| \ge N^{2r}\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\,\|H\|_{\boldsymbol n}. \qquad\text{(28)} The source estimate in this implication is not proved here. Polynomial conditioning and a one-dimensional period space do not make it automatic. The metric in (24) is explicit and uses only the chosen filling powers; an exterior source can have a large component in the kernel of its original closing row.

6. Scope and coverage

The earlier Fourier–Hermite kernel and positive Gram already give polynomial coordinate bounds for the original circle packet. The additional statement here is the explicit singular endomorphism (8), its crossing conjugate, and the exact kernel/period splitting (14)–(17).

For n≠0n\ne0, K̃n\widetilde K_n is not claimed equal to 1sin⁡tfN,n(t)∫−1/21/2sin⁡(t+2πρ/N)fN,n(t+2πρ/N)H(−2cos⁡(t+2πρ/N))dρ.(29) \frac1{\sin t\,f_{N,n}(t)} \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \sin(t+2\pi\rho/N)f_{N,n}(t+2\pi\rho/N) H(-2\cos(t+2\pi\rho/N))\,d\rho . \qquad\text{(29)} Here fN,n(t)=qnd2/4exp⁡(−nt2/(2h))f_{N,n}(t)=q^{nd^2/4}\exp(-nt^2/(2h)), h=2πi/Nh=2\pi i/N, is the actual centered Gaussian lift of the crossing values and normals. Formula (29) uses that lift between nodes. In (13), RnHR^nH is first its full quotient class, whose canonical polynomial agrees with that lift only in the required nodal jets. Averaging intermediate values need not preserve that agreement. No equality of their principal symbols or microlocal interpolation estimate is asserted.

Likewise a polynomial bound for GnG_n does not estimate ϕn(f)\phi_n(f) from below. Equation (16) records that unknown coordinate exactly and allows it to vanish. The result applies to the original class supplied by any exterior contraction, but does not prove its overlap with the period line, select a geometric component, or remove relative boundary currents.
