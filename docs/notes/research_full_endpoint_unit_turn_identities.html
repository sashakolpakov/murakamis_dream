Exact unit-turn identities for a full microscopic endpoint kernel

Private written calculation, 16 November 2026.

The original unit-leaf coupon map has an exact all-degree formula. For a full endpoint kernel it is a finite root-of-unity hypergeometric sum, equivalently a canonical multiplication in the endpoint variable. Its complementary fixed-index limit has a beta-integral expression. None of these identities supplies a uniform bound through growing Newton indices. In particular this note does not permit turning a marked leaf in the adopted full-product forest theorem.

3. An ordinary resolvent and the endpoint-side adapter

The Frobenius representative of ordinary degree-DD evaluation is δw(Z):=(TKN(w,⋅))(Z)=PN(Z)−PN(w)ηN2(Z−w).(11) \boxed{\quad \delta_w(Z):=(TK_N(w,\cdot))(Z) =\frac{P_N(Z)-P_N(w)}{\eta N^2(Z-w)}. \quad} \qquad\text{(11)} The right side is an ordinary polynomial, including at Z=wZ=w. Indeed its leading coefficient is 1/(ηN2)1/(\eta N^2), so its trace is one. Multiplication by Z−wZ-w leaves the constant −PN(w)/(ηN2)-P_N(w)/(\eta N^2) modulo PNP_N. For any ordinary polynomial ff of degree at most DD, write f(Z)=f(w)+(Z−w)q(Z)f(Z)=f(w)+(Z-w)q(Z), with deg⁡q<D\deg q<D. Since ϕN(q)=0\phi_N(q)=0, this proves ϕN(δwf)=f(w)\phi_N(\delta_w f)=f(w). The same equality follows from the dual Newton expansion ∑ses(w)eD−s(Z)\sum_s e_s(w)e_{D-s}(Z), proving (11).

Transposing the multiplication matrix in (4) also gives ar,Nσ(w)=κσ[can⁡(R−σer)](w),(Uσ(Z)KN)(w,Z)=κσ[can⁡w(R(w)−σKN(w,Z))].(12) \boxed{\quad a_{r,N}^{\sigma}(w) =\kappa^\sigma [\operatorname{can}(R^{-\sigma}e_r)](w), \qquad (U_\sigma^{(Z)}K_N)(w,Z) =\kappa^\sigma [\operatorname{can}_w(R(w)^{-\sigma}K_N(w,Z))]. \quad} \qquad\text{(12)} The right side is the specified ordinary polynomial obtained after the indicated canonical multiplication, then evaluated at the ordinary endpoint ww. It is not the unreduced product κσR(w)−σKN(w,Z)\kappa^\sigma R(w)^{-\sigma}K_N(w,Z) off the physical spectrum. Equation (12) is a derived adapter, not permission to quotient an arbitrary original free boundary factor.

At a physical Z=cjZ=c_j, this reads (UσKN(w,⋅))(cj)=κσ[can⁡(R−σFj,N)](w),Fj,N(Z)=KN(cj,Z).(13) (U_\sigma K_N(w,\cdot))(c_j) =\kappa^\sigma [\operatorname{can}(R^{-\sigma}F_{j,N})](w), \qquad F_{j,N}(Z)=K_N(c_j,Z). \qquad\text{(13)} Thus the remaining estimate is an actual oscillatory finite crossing functional. Bounds for an arbitrary multiplication operator or for each term of (6) do not estimate it with the needed polynomial cost.

4. A complementary fixed-index anchor

Now put w=wN(α)=2cosh⁡(α/N)w=w_N(\alpha)=2\cosh(\alpha/N), with real α\alpha in a fixed compact interval, and write Ei,N(α)=(−1)iei(wN(α)),𝖤m(α)=(a0)m(b0)m(m!)2,a0=12+iα/(2π),b0=a0¯.(14) E_{i,N}(\alpha)=(-1)^i e_i(w_N(\alpha)),\qquad \mathsf E_m(\alpha)= \frac{(a_0)_m(b_0)_m}{(m!)^2},\quad a_0=\tfrac12+i\alpha/(2\pi),\quad b_0=\overline{a_0}. \qquad\text{(14)} For a fixed nonnegative integer mm, set r=D−mr=D-m. Sine reflection in (6) gives exactly, for either sign, aD−m,Nσ(wN(α))=η(−1)m∑ℓ=0m(−1)ℓq−σm(ℓ+1)SmSℓSm−ℓEN−1−ℓ,N(α).(15) a_{D-m,N}^{\sigma}(w_N(\alpha)) =\eta(-1)^m\sum_{\ell=0}^{m} (-1)^\ell q^{-\sigma m(\ell+1)} \frac{S_m}{S_\ell S_{m-\ell}}\, E_{N-1-\ell,N}(\alpha). \qquad\text{(15)} For the phase, write s=N−1−ℓs=N-1-\ell in (6). As N→∞N\to\infty, the finite sum (15) therefore gives η(−1)maD−m,Nσ(wN(α))→cosh⁡2(α/2)π2∫01∫01(1−tu)m(1−t)a0−1(1−u)b0−1dtdu.(17) \boxed{\quad \eta(-1)^m a_{D-m,N}^{\sigma}(w_N(\alpha)) \longrightarrow \frac{\cosh^2(\alpha/2)}{\pi^2} \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 (1-tu)^m(1-t)^{a_0-1}(1-u)^{b_0-1}\,dt\,du . \quad} \qquad\text{(17)} The convergence is uniform on compact real parameter intervals. To verify the integral, the beta identity gives exactly 1(ℓ+1)2𝖤ℓ+1(α)=∫01∫01(tu)ℓ(1−t)a0−1(1−u)b0−1dtdu.(18) \frac1{(\ell+1)^2\mathsf E_{\ell+1}(\alpha)} =\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 (tu)^\ell(1-t)^{a_0-1}(1-u)^{b_0-1}\,dt\,du . \qquad\text{(18)} Indeed its two beta factors are Γ(ℓ+1)Γ(a0)/Γ(ℓ+1+a0)\Gamma(\ell+1)\Gamma(a_0)/\Gamma(\ell+1+a_0) and the analogous b0b_0 factor. The sine binomial in (15) tends to (mℓ)\binom m\ell; summing its alternating finite powers gives (1−tu)m(1-tu)^m.

For real α\alpha, the absolute value of the integral in (17) is at most ∫01∫01(1−t)−1/2(1−u)−1/2dtdu=4.(19) \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 (1-t)^{-1/2}(1-u)^{-1/2}\,dt\,du=4. \qquad\text{(19)} This bounds the limiting anchor uniformly in mm. At α=0\alpha=0 the integrand is positive. No positivity is asserted for other real parameters.

The quantifiers remain important: (17) is proved for each fixed mm, followed by N→∞N\to\infty. Its bounded limiting expression does not prove a uniform finite-NN estimate when mm grows, and does not control the whole Newton reconstruction or its real spectral norm. Both signs have the same fixed-index limit after the displayed exact phase is retained.

5. Scope and direct sources

The question left open is a polynomial real-interval bound for the whole UσKN(wN(α),Z)U_\sigma K_N(w_N(\alpha),Z), including all its physical normals, uniformly in the level. The naive triangular bound can be exponential at full degree. Equations (10)–(13) identify the exact finite cancellation that would have to replace it. Equation (17) supplies only one endpoint anchor.

In particular no marked-leaf operation is added to a reduction certificate here. No whole-source or filled-action bound follows, and no infinite root-of-unity hypergeometric summation is used.
