The original crossing multiplier on the full microscopic boundary

Private written proof, 23 November 2026.

The canonical polynomial of each signed original crossing has an entire microscopic limit, with error O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) and every fixed holomorphic derivative. Both Newton endpoints and an oscillating bulk term survive. The limit retains the true first spectral normal at every fixed physical index. Its crossed Frobenius kernel also converges explicitly.

Canonical reduction remains essential: the two limiting multipliers are inverse only modulo the limiting physical polynomial. An explicit nonzero carry survives. These local results do not justify composing an arbitrary short component by pointwise multiplication or limiting kernel integrals.

1. Original signed crossings and the limiting functions

Put N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and

κ=eπid2/(2N),cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N),sm=2sin⁡(πm/N),Sm=∏r=1msr,S0=1, \kappa=e^{\pi i d^2/(2N)},\quad c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N),\quad s_m=2\sin(\pi m/N),\quad S_m=\prod_{r=1}^m s_r,\quad S_0=1, PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,em(z)=Sm−2∏j<m(cj−z),wN(α)=2cosh⁡(α/N).(1) P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\qquad e_m(z)=S_m^{-2}\prod_{j<m}(c_j-z),\qquad w_N(\alpha)=2\cosh(\alpha/N). \qquad\text{(1)} Here TNT_N is the ordinary Chebyshev polynomial. All canonical representatives have degree at most dd. The full repeated-root algebra is ℂ[z]/PN\mathbb C[z]/P_N, with its original functional ϕN(eiej)=δi+j,d\phi_N(e_i e_j)=\delta_{i+j,d} and genuine turn 𝖳ei=ed−i\mathsf T e_i=e_{d-i}.

rN,σ(wN(α))=∑i=0N−1qσiEi,N(α).(3) r_{N,\sigma}(w_N(\alpha)) =\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}q^{\sigma i}E_{i,N}(\alpha). \qquad\text{(3)} Use the entire endpoint functions of [K]:

Theorem 1. The series and functions

D(α)=1+∑m≥1[𝖤m(α)−𝖧m(α)],Fσ(α)=D(α)+σic(α)(5) D(\alpha)=1+\sum_{m\ge1} [\mathsf E_m(\alpha)-\mathsf H_m(\alpha)],\qquad F_\sigma(\alpha)=D(\alpha)+\sigma i c(\alpha) \qquad\text{(5)} are entire and even. For every fixed A<∞A<\infty and integer k≥0k\ge0,

sup|α|≤A|∂αk[rN,σ(wN(α))−Fσ(α)]|≤CA,k/N.(6) \sup_{|\alpha|\le A} \left|\partial_\alpha^k [r_{N,\sigma}(w_N(\alpha))-F_\sigma(\alpha)]\right| \le C_{A,k}/N. \qquad\text{(6)} The limit is through both parities. On the real axis DD is real and

|Fσ(α)|2=D(α)2+c(α)2≥c(α)2≥1.(7) |F_\sigma(\alpha)|^2=D(\alpha)^2+c(\alpha)^2 \ge c(\alpha)^2\ge1. \qquad\text{(7)} In particular the original microscopic crossing is uniformly nonzero on each bounded real parameter interval for all sufficiently large NN. The known unit scalar κσ\kappa^\sigma has not been discarded from the original crossing; (6) states its normalization explicitly.

2. A common bulk unit for the two Newton ends

Write sinc⁡z=sin⁡z/z\operatorname{sinc}z=\sin z/z and sinhc⁡z=sinh⁡z/z\operatorname{sinhc}z=\sinh z/z, with removable values one. The exact product and complementary identities [K] are

Em,N=𝖤mUm,N,Um,N=∏r=0m−1sinhc⁡((α+i(2r+1)π)/(2N))sinhc⁡((α−i(2r+1)π)/(2N))sinc⁡(π(r+1)/N)2, E_{m,N}=\mathsf E_m U_{m,N},\qquad U_{m,N}=\prod_{r=0}^{m-1} \frac{\operatorname{sinhc}((\alpha+i(2r+1)\pi)/(2N)) \operatorname{sinhc}((\alpha-i(2r+1)\pi)/(2N))} {\operatorname{sinc}(\pi(r+1)/N)^2}, EN−m,N=𝖧msinc⁡(πm/N)−2Um,N−1.(8) E_{N-m,N}=\mathsf H_m \operatorname{sinc}(\pi m/N)^{-2}U_{m,N}^{-1}. \qquad\text{(8)} They hold through all apparent zeros. For 1≤m≤N/21\le m\le N/2, all displayed unit factors are nonzero on a fixed bounded α\alpha-disk once NN is sufficiently large. We prove the stronger comparison

Vm,N:=Um,Nsinc⁡(πm/N),Vm,N±1=1+OA(m/N2).(9) V_{m,N}:=U_{m,N}\operatorname{sinc}(\pi m/N),\qquad V_{m,N}^{\ \pm1}=1+O_A(m/N^2). \qquad\text{(9)}

3. Summation with the oscillating bulk retained

Uniformly on compact parameter disks, as m→∞m\to\infty,

𝖤m=Cm(1−ab/m+OA(m−2)),𝖧m=Cm(1+ab/m+OA(m−2)).(12) \mathsf E_m=\frac C m (1-ab/m+O_A(m^{-2})),\qquad \mathsf H_m=\frac C m (1+ab/m+O_A(m^{-2})). \qquad\text{(12)} Here is also a direct way to check the opposite first corrections. The Gamma product gives 𝖤m=CΓ(m+a)Γ(m+b)/Γ(m+1)2\mathsf E_m=C\Gamma(m+a)\Gamma(m+b)/\Gamma(m+1)^2, using 1/(Γ(a)Γ(b))=sin⁡(πa)/π=C1/(\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b))=\sin(\pi a)/\pi=C. The normalized ratio um=mΓ(m+a)Γ(m+b)/Γ(m+1)2u_m=m\Gamma(m+a)\Gamma(m+b)/\Gamma(m+1)^2 (equivalently m𝖤m/Cm\mathsf E_m/C, by removable continuation) tends to one and obeys um+1/um=1+ab/[m(m+1)]u_{m+1}/u_m=1+ab/[m(m+1)]. The normalization follows, for example, by scaling the beta integral for Gamma ratios and then shifting its parameters; it is uniform on fixed compact sets. For sufficiently large mm, take logarithms of its tail product: log⁡um=−∑j≥mlog⁡(1+ab/[j(j+1)])=−ab/m+OA(m−3)\log u_m=-\sum_{j\ge m}\log(1+ab/[j(j+1)]) =-ab/m+O_A(m^{-3}). This proves the first expansion; the exact identity 𝖧m=C2/(m2𝖤m)\mathsf H_m=C^2/(m^2\mathsf E_m) proves the second. The Gamma expressions extend through the finitely many exceptional parameter values, so the estimates are uniform there as well. Thus

𝖤m−𝖧m=OA(m−2),𝖤m+𝖧m=2C/m+OA(m−3).(13) \mathsf E_m-\mathsf H_m=O_A(m^{-2}),\qquad \mathsf E_m+\mathsf H_m=2C/m+O_A(m^{-3}). \qquad\text{(13)} The first bound proves normal convergence and entire evenness in (5).

For the positive sign, pair indices m,N−mm,N-m in (3). Set τm=πm/N\tau_m=\pi m/N and M=⌊(N−1)/2⌋M=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor. By (8)–(9), their exact sum is

τmcot⁡τm(𝖤mVm,N−𝖧mVm,N−1)+iτm(𝖤mVm,N+𝖧mVm,N−1).(14) \tau_m\cot\tau_m (\mathsf E_m V_{m,N}-\mathsf H_m V_{m,N}^{-1}) +i\tau_m(\mathsf E_m V_{m,N} +\mathsf H_m V_{m,N}^{-1}). \qquad\text{(14)} Replacing both VV’s by one costs OA(N−2)O_A(N^{-2}) per pair, since 𝖤m,𝖧m=OA(1/m)\mathsf E_m,\mathsf H_m=O_A(1/m), and the two displayed weights are bounded. The real-weight error also costs OA(N−2)O_A(N^{-2}) per pair, because |τcot⁡τ−1|≤Cτ2|\tau\cot\tau-1|\le C\tau^2 for 0≤τ≤π/20\le\tau\le\pi/2 and the difference in (13) is OA(m−2)O_A(m^{-2}). Its omitted infinite tail is OA(1/N)O_A(1/N).

The imaginary part of (14), after this replacement, sums to i∑m=1M[2πC/N+OA(1/(Nm2))]=ic+OA(N−1)i\sum_{m=1}^M[2\pi C/N+O_A(1/(Nm^2))] =ic+O_A(N^{-1}). The index zero contributes one. At even NN the additional midpoint is iEN/2,N=OA(N−1)iE_{N/2,N}=O_A(N^{-1}), by (8)–(9) and (12); at odd NN there is no unpaired index. This proves (6) for the positive sign and zero derivatives. The negative sign reverses the imaginary part in (14) with no other change. Applying the result on a larger parameter disk and Cauchy’s formula proves every derivative in (6). The endpoint functions are real for real parameters, proving (7).

4. The original physical first normals

For a fixed integer j≥0j\ge0, put αj=iπ(2j+1)\alpha_j=i\pi(2j+1) and νj=(−1)jqj(j+1)\nu_j=(-1)^j q^{j(j+1)}. At all sufficiently large levels this is a double physical node. The actual crossing data [W, equation (8)] give exactly

rN,σ(wN(αj))=νjσ,∂αrN,σ(wN(α))|αj=−σd−2j2Nνjσ.(15) r_{N,\sigma}(w_N(\alpha_j))=\nu_j^\sigma,\qquad \left.\partial_\alpha r_{N,\sigma}(w_N(\alpha)) \right|_{\alpha_j} =-\sigma\frac{d-2j}{2N}\nu_j^\sigma. \qquad\text{(15)} Consequently (6) retains, with their original sign,

Fσ(αj)=(−1)j,Fσ′(αj)=−σ2(−1)j,D(αj)=(−1)j,D′(αj)=0, F_\sigma(\alpha_j)=(-1)^j,\qquad F_\sigma'(\alpha_j)=-\frac\sigma2(-1)^j,\qquad D(\alpha_j)=(-1)^j,\quad D'(\alpha_j)=0, rN,σ′(cj)N2=σi(−1)j4π(2j+1)+Oj(N−1).(16) \frac{r_{N,\sigma}'(c_j)}{N^2} =\frac{\sigma i(-1)^j}{4\pi(2j+1)}+O_j(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(16)} The last derivative is the ordinary fixed-root Casimir derivative; it follows from wN′(αj)=2isin⁡((2j+1)π/N)/Nw_N'(\alpha_j)=2i\sin((2j+1)\pi/N)/N. It is not a genuine-qq normal or a changed-meridian derivative. The estimates concern fixed jj, not indices proportional to NN.

5. The surviving inverse-pair carry

The exact inverse identity (2) defines an ordinary polynomial

QN(z)=rN,+(z)rN,−(z)−1PN(z).(17) Q_N(z)=\frac{r_{N,+}(z)r_{N,-}(z)-1}{P_N(z)}. \qquad\text{(17)} Since PN(wN(α))=4c(α)2=:P(α)P_N(w_N(\alpha))=4c(\alpha)^2=:P(\alpha) exactly, Theorem 1 implies

QN(wN(α))→Q∞(α)=D(α)2+c(α)2−14c(α)2,(18) Q_N(w_N(\alpha))\longrightarrow Q_\infty(\alpha)=\frac{D(\alpha)^2+c(\alpha)^2-1} {4c(\alpha)^2}, \qquad\text{(18)} with OA(N−1)O_A(N^{-1}) error and every fixed holomorphic derivative. The quotient is entire and even: (16) makes its numerator vanish to order at least two at every zero of cc. For the convergence through those zeros, choose a larger circle avoiding them. Division on that circle gives the O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) bound, the maximum principle carries it inside, and Cauchy’s formula gives the derivatives. For real α≠0\alpha\ne0, (18) is strictly positive, because DD is real and c2−1>0c^2-1>0. Thus the carry is not zero.

In particular F+F−≠1F_+F_-\ne1 as ordinary functions, whereas their classes modulo PP are inverse. Pointwise multiplication of limiting crossing values would miss this explicit order-one source correction.

6. The actual crossed Frobenius kernel

Put η=(−1)d\eta=(-1)^d. The original reverse Newton kernel satisfies

BN(z,w)=∑i=0dei(z)ed−i(w)=ηN2PN(z)−PN(w)z−w.(19) B_N(z,w)=\sum_{i=0}^d e_i(z)e_{d-i}(w) =\frac\eta{N^2}\frac{P_N(z)-P_N(w)}{z-w}. \qquad\text{(19)} For completeness, use zei=ciei−si+12ei+1z e_i=c_i e_i-s_{i+1}^2 e_{i+1} for i<di<d, the top identity (z−cd)ed=ηPN/N2(z-c_d)e_d=\eta P_N/N^2, and cd−i=cic_{d-i}=c_i, sN−i=sis_{N-i}=s_i. All interior terms in (z−w)BN(z-w)B_N telescope. The original Frobenius identity in section 1 also gives ϕN,z[BN(z,w)f(z)]=f(w)\phi_{N,z}[B_N(z,w)f(z)]=f(w) for canonical ff.

For r=rN,σr=r_{N,\sigma}, its canonical multiplication kernel is

Cr,N(z,w)=can⁡z[r(z)BN(z,w)]=ηN2PN(z)r(w)−PN(w)r(z)z−w.(20) C_{r,N}(z,w)=\operatorname{can}_z[r(z)B_N(z,w)] =\frac\eta{N^2} \frac{P_N(z)r(w)-P_N(w)r(z)}{z-w}. \qquad\text{(20)} The expression is canonical in both variables. Subtracting it from r(z)BNr(z)B_N leaves a multiple of PN(z)P_N(z), which proves (20), and ϕN,z[Cr,N(z,w)f(z)]=can⁡(rf)(w)\phi_{N,z}[C_{r,N}(z,w)f(z)] =\operatorname{can}(rf)(w). Its full microscopic limit is

ηCrN,σ,N(wN(α),wN(β))→P(α)Fσ(β)−P(β)Fσ(α)α2−β2.(21) \eta C_{r_{N,\sigma},N}(w_N(\alpha),w_N(\beta)) \longrightarrow \frac{P(\alpha)F_\sigma(\beta)-P(\beta)F_\sigma(\alpha)} {\alpha^2-\beta^2}. \qquad\text{(21)} The error is OA(N−1)O_A(N^{-1}), including every fixed mixed holomorphic derivative on bounded bidisks. Indeed N2(wN(α)−wN(β))/(α2−β2)=sinhc⁡((α+β)/(2N))sinhc⁡((α−β)/(2N))=1+OA(N−2)N^2(w_N(\alpha)-w_N(\beta))/(\alpha^2-\beta^2) =\operatorname{sinhc}((\alpha+\beta)/(2N)) \operatorname{sinhc}((\alpha-\beta)/(2N))=1+O_A(N^{-2}). Every numerator is even in each variable and antisymmetric under their exchange, so division by α2−β2\alpha^2-\beta^2 is removable. Buffered Cauchy estimates preserve the error in (6): write each even function as an entire function of u=α2u=\alpha^2, and use its divided difference at u,v=β2u,v=\beta^2. This is the integral of its first derivative on the segment from uu to vv; a larger disk controls that derivative. Another buffered Cauchy estimate gives all mixed derivatives.

These are original atomic multiplication and inverse-pair gluing data. No estimate for iterated canonical reductions, whole-kernel contractions, escaping Newton mass, or the original closing after an arbitrary fixed short component follows. Such estimates are required before interchanging this limit with arbitrary Conway compositions or turns.
