Combining the first normals before estimating the trace

Research proof, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

Splitting a trace at the triangular support of a Newton polynomial separates terms that can cancel. There is an exact way to put them back together. Interpolate the values of the other factor at the distinct physical roots, and subtract the derivative of this interpolant from its actual first normal. The trace then involves only this normal difference and the nonzero values of the Newton polynomial. No first normal is discarded.

At even levels the resulting crossing operator has a positive Hermitian form. Its positivity follows from an explicit integral representation of the original quadratic crossing phase. This gives a positive observation with a specified adjoint cap; it does not make the original same-sign pair contraction positive.

4. What this does and does not prove for the coefficient

The second is the actual trace, and multiplies observations at the complementary times tt and n−tn-t without conjugation. A lower bound for that integral needs control of those observations together. Neither a lower bound for the positive energy alone nor the invertibility of the finite crossing form supplies it.

There is a useful exact timescale for observing every channel. It is proportional to the level, not a fixed crossing power.

This supplies a polynomially coercive observation of the original value space over the full crossing period, with its appropriate odd weighted interpretation. It does not supply the corresponding bound on a fixed time interval: the small-eigenvalue theorem gives exponentially small directions for every fixed positive power as NN grows. Taking n=Nn=N or n=2Nn=2N changes the crossing power with the quantum level and therefore is not a volume-conjecture statement for a fixed knot. Nor does a positive energy replace the bilinear observation in (38). The averaging in (41) is the continuous crossing flow (35).
