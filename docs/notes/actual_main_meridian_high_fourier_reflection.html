A reflected trace bound for one high Newton index

The finite tangle algebra is written in a Newton polynomial basis e0,…,eN−1e_0,\ldots,e_{N-1}. In this note an index is high when it exceeds ⌊(N−1)/2⌋\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor. This is an internal basis index, not an auxiliary crossing-circle color. Its distinction from a low index matters because the high polynomial has signed Fourier coefficients and vanishes at the complete spectral nodes, while its derivatives at repeated roots need not vanish.

When one high basis polynomial is multiplied by several low ones, moving the main meridian can make the trace exponentially larger than its complete value. The useful comparison concerns the difference of the two traces. Its first Fourier alias cancels exactly. The remaining difference is bounded by a positive complete trace in which the high index hh is replaced by N−1−hN-1-h. In a regular low-index window there is a further strict exponential loss.

The proof below retains both level parities, all Fourier aliases, and the coefficient trace at repeated roots. Section 5 explains how the reflected index is used in a whole ordered tree through the component-pressure comparison. The general component theorem combines this bound with the other index sectors. The comparison does not identify the two diagrams produced by deleting a turn, and it does not assert a bound by the same-index complete trace.

5. Several reflected interfaces and the strict old window

The finite estimates above now have a precise whole-tree interface. The required positive-pressure comparison is proved separately in the component-pressure theorem. It applies to an original ordered tree of whole bare junctions, all of valence at least three, whose internal edges are physical quarter-turns and whose external profiles are the original rigid functions g+cjg+c_j. The constants are retained. Write F(𝒢)F(\mathcal G) for its complete positive maximum.

5.1. Reflection removes a turn in the positive comparison

The exact complete metric and turn are ϕ0(eiej)=δi+j,d\phi_0(e_i e_j)=\delta_{i+j,d} and T0(ei)=ed−iT_0(e_i)=e_{d-i}. Consequently ∑i=0dϕ0(Aei)ϕ0(ed−iB)=ϕ0(AB). \sum_{i=0}^d\phi_0(Ae_i)\phi_0(e_{d-i}B)=\phi_0(AB). \tag{29} Reflecting exactly one endpoint of an equal-label physical-turn edge therefore changes it into the complementary pairing (29). Removing that turn and fusing the two ordered old products is an exact positive tensor operation, with the induced boundary order. It is not an assertion that the physical links before and after deletion are isotopic.

The pressure comparison retains the neighboring profiles. At a cut physical edge they are unturned product profiles fA,fBf_A,f_B, and the original and deleted pressures are FT=maxb{fA(b)+fB(b)},FI=maxb{fA(b)+fB(1−b)}. F_T=\max_b\{f_A(b)+f_B(b)\},\qquad F_I=\max_b\{f_A(b)+f_B(1-b)\}. \tag{30} The product-response theorem gives fX′≥f2′f_X'\ge f_2', and f2f_2 is symmetric. Hence fX(b)≥fX(1−b)f_X(b)\ge f_X(1-b) for b≥1/2b\ge1/2. Applying this to the smaller of the two arguments in the deleted summand gives FI≤FTF_I\le F_T. After fusion the new hub still has valence at least three, and its original ordered positive resolution has a unique strict maximizing state. The same argument can therefore be applied successively to the current fused tree. For any finite set SS of deleted edges, F(𝒢/S)≤F(𝒢). F(\mathcal G/S)\le F(\mathcal G). \tag{31} This does not hold the old multi-boundary responses fixed while changing their couplings. Each stage uses the actual profiles of that fused tree. The cited geometric proof establishes the induced cyclic order, exact fusion and strictness hypotheses.

For moving-source applications, expand each one-high tensor as its complete tensor plus its difference. Replace a selected difference by (21). Actual typing makes moving equal-color vertices nonadjacent. A selected internal high edge meets a constant old tensor, whose complete support permits at most one high incidence. Thus the selected internal edges form a matching. Each selected moving endpoint has all other indices low, and its old neighbor also has all other indices low. Every fused hub therefore has an all-low exterior in that band sector. The other physical edges are retained.

After positive fusion, summing all complementary hidden labels gives the old tensor of 𝒢/S\mathcal G/S. The original band restrictions can be kept in its positive resolution or discarded for an upper bound. They do not change its lattice spacing: reflection is the exact integer substitution h↦d−hh\mapsto d-h.

5.2. The strict fused optimizer supplies the Fourier window

First note an elementary degree restriction that needs no old-support assumption. A cubic tensor with one high index and two low indices has degree at most d+2m≤2N−2d+2m\le2N-2. Its trace difference vanishes, because the first possible changing alias has degree 2N−12N-1. Therefore every nonzero selected one-high difference has valence at least four. It need not have valence at least five: off the complete support a valence-four difference can be nonzero.

Fix a nonempty set of selected internal differences, and keep the complementary labels of the deleted edges as hidden variables in a fixed old positive resolution of 𝒢/S\mathcal G/S. Let wSw_S be its unique strict complete optimizer. An internal fusion of a valence-at-least-four selected vertex with a valence-at-least-three neighbor creates valence at least five. In particular the fused tree cannot be the exceptional tree consisting of two trivalent hubs, each carrying two rigid external leaves.

The old physical-edge half theorem now puts every remaining physical internal label of wSw_S strictly inside (0,1/2)(0,1/2); the external rigid labels also lie strictly in that interval. Hidden unturned labels remain in their original strict interval (0,1)(0,1). For a selected difference, each of its other incidences is a remaining physical edge or an original rigid leaf: the matching has removed only its unique high edge. Thus all the low factors entering that difference lie in a fixed compact low-index window near wSw_S.

Let rr be the hidden complementary index of that selected edge, and let xrx_r be its normalized value at wSw_S. Strictness gives xr>0x_r>0. If xr>1/2x_r>1/2, the closed limiting sector r/N≤1/2r/N\le1/2 excludes wSw_S, and the whole sector has a compact old-action gap. Otherwise r/Nr/N is bounded below by a fixed positive constant in a sufficiently small neighborhood of wSw_S. The value xr=1/2x_r=1/2 is allowed: (23) requires a positive lower bound on r/Nr/N, not separation of that particular probe from half. All other factors are strictly below half. Hence the precise hypotheses of (28) hold near every fused optimizer that can contribute to the reflected sector. The finite restriction r≤mr\le m can only reduce this sum.

This argument uses the unconstrained strict optimizer of the same fused old action, followed by restriction to the band sector. It does not assume that an arbitrary band-constrained optimizer is strict. Values rr near zero are in the compact complement of this optimizer, rather than being inserted into a false uniform interior Fourier estimate.

5.3. Combining the local Fourier loss and compact separation

Use the coarse inequality (21) on all selected differences. Resolve every resulting complete tensor positively. This gives a polynomial multiple of the original positive state sum of 𝒢/S\mathcal G/S, with its exact rigid boundary profiles and constants. Its closed factorial errors and the number of states are subexponential uniformly up to support faces.

Outside a fixed neighborhood of wSw_S, uniqueness and strictness give a rate at most F(𝒢/S)−ηSF(\mathcal G/S)-\eta_S, with ηS>0\eta_S>0. Inside that neighborhood, apply (28) at one selected difference and use (21) at the others. The resulting bound has a further fixed loss κS>0\kappa_S>0. If the band sector excludes wSw_S, only the first case is needed. Thus the sum of this selected correction sector has rate F(𝒢/S)−min⁡(ηS,κS)+o(1)≤F(𝒢)−min⁡(ηS,κS)+o(1), F(\mathcal G/S)-\min(\eta_S,\kappa_S)+o(1) \le F(\mathcal G)-\min(\eta_S,\kappa_S)+o(1), \tag{32} with the irrelevant minimum omitted when only the compact-complement case occurs. One may shrink either positive constant to absorb polynomial factors. The structural loss is fixed before the parameter disk; RR affects constants and the eventual level threshold. There are only finitely many selected sets for a fixed component.

No division by a complete tensor is used when that tensor vanishes. Equation (21) already forces the corresponding exact difference to be zero. Similarly, the argument never assigns a relative asymptotic to an old-support boundary tensor. It uses its positive closed upper envelope and the compact separation of the whole old action.

5.4. A reflected original external index

If the unique high incidence is an original external leaf, there is no internal turn to delete. Its actual high coefficient cone contributes the rate cjc_j. After reflection to r=d−hr=d-h, the old comparison leaf contributes g(r/N)+cjg(r/N)+c_j. Thus the missing quantity g(r/N)≥0 g(r/N)\ge0 \tag{33} is a loss, and must be retained rather than silently supplied to the original source. At the strict old optimizer the reflected leaf is inside (0,1/2)(0,1/2), where this loss is strictly positive. Away from a neighborhood of that optimizer, old compact separation applies. This handles the two endpoints without pretending that gg is uniformly positive on the closed interval.

If internal corrections are present as well, first perform their fusions and retain (33) in that same positive tree. If there are only external corrections, the nonzero selected vertex already has valence at least four by the cubic degree argument, so the exceptional two-trivalent geometry cannot arise. One external loss or one internal Fourier loss is enough; the other differences may retain their coarse reflected bounds. The boundary constants and all unchanged input profiles are counted once.

5.5. What this interface supplies

Equations (29)–(33), with the separately proved old fusion theorem, close the strict-window requirement for a finite family of one-high trace differences.
