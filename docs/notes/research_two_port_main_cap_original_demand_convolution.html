An exact cap/CROSS convolution with the original MAIN-face demand

The proposed inequality (8) remains open. This note keeps the even-level, original-demand convolution; an unconstrained positive envelope is a different problem.

Later status, 31 October 2026. The all-demand contraction theorem proves the subexponential version of (8), uniformly over every original remaining degree, and supplies the actual odd-level regularized comparison. The polynomial factor proposed in (8) remains open. The two-spectrum continuation also controls complex neighborhoods of both endpoints, with a rate excess tending to zero with their radius. The original internal demand and incidence hypothesis remain essential to this paired-reference comparison; the counterexample in section 4 still rules out arbitrary exterior face demands.

1. The actual local source and the demand that must be extracted first

Put N=2m≥4N=2m\ge4, d=N−1d=N-1, M=m−1M=m-1, and θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N. Let HN(σ)H_N(\sigma) denote the original raw cleared numerator and put PN=(−1)dHN(−2)P_N=(-1)^dH_N(-2), its positive endpoint polynomial.

Consider two actual adjacent ports whose specialized ordered quartets are E=(A,0,C,Z),F=(0,A,0,C). E=(A,0,C,Z),\qquad F=(0,A,0,C). The two zeros in FF are the original distinct protected faces, specialized to zero. The output triangle (A,C,Z)(A,C,Z) of EE and input triangle of FF meet on the ordinary MAIN edge ACAC. Restrict EE to its genuine CROSS support and FF to its genuine RIGHT support. These projections are parts of the original homogeneous polynomials; they are not asserted to be covariant Casimir coupons.

Assume the ordinary face AA has no incidence outside these two ports. Its original demand is then exactly dd, and must be extracted before taking a local bound. The unchanged exterior tensor may depend on C,ZC,Z and other faces, but not on AA.

This is a local incidence hypothesis at arbitrary fixed width. For an explicit check of its original-source typing, it occurs in the certified seven-port word [1,3,0,2,4,1,3][1,3,0,2,4,1,3] with the first cap marked. The protected faces are 0,60,6; take E=port 0=(1,0,4,2),F=port 2=(0,1,6,4),(A,C,Z)=(L1,L4,L2). E=\text{port }0=(1,0,4,2),\qquad F=\text{port }2=(0,1,6,4), \qquad (A,C,Z)=(L_1,L_4,L_2). The original seven-port dictionary shows that face 11 belongs only to these ports. No other original face or target changes.

3. The exact original-demand convolution

Define BN(C,Z)=[Ad]HF,RIGHT(2)HE,CROSS(−2),BN0(C,Z)=[Ad]PF,RIGHTPE,CROSS. \begin{aligned} B_N(C,Z) &=[A^d]\,H_{F,\mathrm{RIGHT}}(2) H_{E,\mathrm{CROSS}}(-2),\\ B_N^0(C,Z) &=[A^d]\,P_{F,\mathrm{RIGHT}}P_{E,\mathrm{CROSS}}. \end{aligned} \tag{5} Both have degree dd in C,ZC,Z. Neither has a CdC^d term, because the two original middle degrees are each at most MM. For 0≤n≤d−10\le n\le d-1, put Jn={j:max⁡(0,n−M)≤j≤min⁡(M,n)}. J_n=\{j:\max(0,n-M)\le j\le\min(M,n)\}. Then [CnZd−n]BN=(−1)d∑j∈Jn(−1)jUjCj,n−j, [C^nZ^{d-n}]B_N =(-1)^d\sum_{j\in J_n}(-1)^jU_jC_{j,n-j}, \tag{6} [CnZd−n]BN0=∑j∈JnVjCj,n−j. Both remaining face degrees stay in the polynomial. Multiplying BNB_N by the untouched exterior tensor and extracting every remaining original target reproduces the chosen two-port contribution exactly.

The concrete possible elimination lemma is the existence of constants C0,κ<∞C_0,\kappa<\infty, independent of N,nN,n, such that |BN|coeff≤C0NκBN0coefficientwise. \boxed{\quad \lvert B_N\rvert_{\mathrm{coeff}} \le C_0N^\kappa B_N^0 \quad\text{coefficientwise.}\quad} \tag{8} Equation (8) is not proved. Even a proof would address this particular incidence and support pattern, not every odd MAIN cycle. It would not permit arbitrary other spectral coupons to be replaced by endpoints.

The two extreme coefficients do admit direct polynomial comparisons. For n=0n=0, only j=h=0j=h=0 occurs and the absolute ratio in (6)–(7) is cot⁡2(θ/2)≤N2\cot^2(\theta/2)\le N^2. These endpoint checks do not control the interior alternating sums.

4. Relaxing the original face demand gives a false inequality

A stronger comparison on every coefficient of the uncontracted product fails. Extract ZdZ^d from EE: homogeneity forces its unique monomial N2ZdN^2Z^d, leaving FF alone on A,CA,C. Thus the ratio of the absolute coefficient at Ad−jCjZdA^{d-j}C^jZ^d to the paired endpoint coefficient is exactly UjVj=∏r=0jcot⁡2((r+12)θ). \frac{U_j}{V_j} =\prod_{r=0}^{j}\cot^2((r+\tfrac12)\theta). \tag{9} For NN divisible by 88, N≥16N\ge16, choose j=N/8j=N/8. Every angle in (9) is at most π/6\pi/6, so this ratio is at least 3N/8+13^{N/8+1}. No fixed polynomial comparison holds for all coefficients of the uncontracted pair.

But this exponent has AA-degree d−j<dd-j<d, and the actual exterior has no AA incidence. It is absent from the original full coefficient. Hence (9) is not a counterexample to (8), to an original shared-target inequality, or to the volume conjecture.

5. Exact remaining finite problem

A proof of (8) must control the signed finite convolution (6), including both summation endpoints. A terminating orthogonality or continuant identity would have to be derived with the original cutoff and unused products intact. No such identity is currently established here.

The two-anchor minimax estimate and blockwise source transport concern unit-factor anchor cores with other clocks fixed. They do not identify these conditional two-port values with the complete volume. The cycle-packing estimate provides loss relative to an envelope and does not prove (8).

A private 80-digit probe evaluated (1), (4), (6), and (7) at five even levels through 128128. It is a diagnostic, not an audit or proof, and is not part of this note’s supporting evidence. No rate, universal polynomial comparison, or theorem for the remaining skeletons is inferred from those values.

Any proof of (8) must control the original finite constrained sum, not replace it by an unconstrained envelope.
