The marked edge cover with some boundary angles fixed at zero

Research note, 15 November 2026.

The original circle-tree face construction has one square root for each tree edge. When some auxiliary boundary angles are fixed at zero, different edges can acquire the same radicand. The restricted cover can then be reducible. This note identifies its component through the marked complete structure, proves that its regular complex charts are connected, and checks that the original face maps are rational on this component.

The conclusion concerns an explicit family of representations of the original augmented exterior. It does not assert that every hyperbolic short partial filling occurs in that family. A path in this family need not preserve filling slopes, positive tetrahedra, logarithmic periods, or the coefficient of a cycle in the quantum source.

1. The restricted cover and its components

Let 𝒯=(V,E)\mathcal T=(V,E) be an original ordered circle tree with at least one edge. Retain its page orders, disk frames, half-twist carriers, and primitive peripheral curves, as in Sections 2–3 of the circle-tree geometry manuscript. Choose a nonempty subset S⊆VS\subseteq V, and write U=V\SU=V\setminus S. The selected variables are av=tan⁡(tv/2)a_v=\tan(t_v/2), for v∈Sv\in S; set au=0a_u=0 for u∈Uu\in U. The equations are qe2=1+av2+aw2,e=vw∈E.(1) q_e^2=1+a_v^2+a_w^2,\qquad e=vw\in E. \qquad\text{(1)} At the marked complete point oo, every av=0a_v=0 and every qe=1q_e=1. Fixing aU=0a_U=0 will be called the complete-parameter slice. Away from the positive metric domain, this terminology specifies parameters and peripheral multipliers, not a complete hyperbolic metric.

Partition the edges into ESE_S, with both endpoints selected; EUE_U, with neither endpoint selected; and boundary edges, with one endpoint in each set. For v∈Sv\in S, let kvk_v be its number of boundary edges, and put B={v∈S:kv>0}B=\{v\in S:k_v>0\}. Choose one reference boundary edge eve_v at each v∈Bv\in B. Set fe=1+av2+aw2(e=vw∈ES),gv=1+av2(v∈S).(2) f_e=1+a_v^2+a_w^2\quad(e=vw\in E_S), \qquad g_v=1+a_v^2\quad(v\in S). \qquad\text{(2)}

Theorem 1. The reduced affine cover (1) has 2|EU|+∑v∈B(kv−1)(3) 2^{\,|E_U|+\sum_{v\in B}(k_v-1)} \qquad\text{(3)} irreducible components. Each has degree 2|ES|+|B|(4) 2^{\,|E_S|+|B|} \qquad\text{(4)} over the selected parameter space. On the open set where every qe≠0q_e\ne0, they are disjoint, and their labels are exactly qe=σe(e∈EU),qe=ϵeqev(e≠ev a boundary edge at v),(5) q_e=\sigma_e\quad(e\in E_U),\qquad q_e=\epsilon_e q_{e_v} \quad(e\ne e_v\text{ a boundary edge at }v), \qquad\text{(5)} where every displayed sign is independently 11 or −1-1. The marked component XSX_S, through oo, has every sign in (5) equal to +1+1. Thus (5) with these signs is an exact sheet test on this open set. The absolute signs of the remaining independent roots are not additional component labels.

Proof. Work over R=ℂ[av:v∈S]R=\mathbb C[a_v:v\in S]. Each internal polynomial fef_e is irreducible: its homogenization is the rank-three quadratic form z2+av2+aw2z^2+a_v^2+a_w^2, whereas a product of two linear forms has rank at most two. Distinct tree edges give distinct primes. For each v∈Bv\in B, the prime av−ia_v-i divides gvg_v to order one, divides no other gwg_w, and divides no internal fef_e. Consequently the classes {fe:e∈ES}∪{gv:v∈B}(6) \{f_e:e\in E_S\}\cup\{g_v:v\in B\} \qquad\text{(6)} are independent in Frac⁡(R)*/Frac⁡(R)*2\operatorname{Frac}(R)^*/ \operatorname{Frac}(R)^{*2}: take valuations first at the internal primes and then at av−ia_v-i.

Independent square classes give a field extension of degree 2|ES|+|B|2^{|E_S|+|B|}. One elementary justification is induction: in a multiquadratic extension, an element whose square belongs to the base field is an eigenvector for every sign automorphism. Expanding it in the root-monomial basis shows that it is a base-field multiple of one root monomial. Thus a new base-field radicand becomes a square only if its class is generated by the preceding ones.

It follows that R[re,sv:e∈ES,v∈B]/(re2−fe,sv2−gv)(7) R[r_e,s_v:e\in E_S,\ v\in B]/ (r_e^2-f_e,\ s_v^2-g_v) \qquad\text{(7)} is a domain. Indeed it is free over RR in the root monomials, and these monomials are independent in that extension field. On qe≠0q_e\ne0, the untouched roots have the constant signs in (5), and each quotient of two boundary roots at the same vertex has square one. Splitting by these signs leaves exactly (7), with the corresponding units inverted. This proves the disjoint decomposition and both counts.

For completeness, the full algebra in (1) is free over RR by successively adjoining monic quadratics. Its generic algebra is a product of the fields just described. Hence it is reduced: a nilpotent would vanish generically and so would be RR-torsion, contrary to freeness. Its minimal components all dominate the base: a nonzero element of RR is a non-zero-divisor in this reduced finite algebra and cannot lie in a minimal prime. The closures of the components above are therefore all the components. Their degrees sum to 2|E|2^{|E|}. Different closures can meet when a shared boundary root vanishes, so the disjointness assertion was restricted to qe≠0q_e\ne0. At oo, all signs are +1+1, as claimed. ▫\square

2. A regular open set and its connectedness

On XSX_S use the following convenient open set: ℛS={∏v∈S(1+av2)∏e∈Eqe(qe2+1)≠0}.(8) \mathcal R_S= \left\{\prod_{v\in S}(1+a_v^2) \prod_{e\in E}q_e(q_e^2+1)\ne0\right\}. \qquad\text{(8)} This deliberately excludes the ramification locus qe=0q_e=0, even where an individual face expression might extend. It contains oo. Its base is the complement of the hypersurfaces gv=0(v∈S),fe=0,fe+1=0(e∈ES),gv+1=0(v∈B).(9) g_v=0\ (v\in S),\quad f_e=0,\ f_e+1=0\ (e\in E_S),\quad g_v+1=0\ (v\in B). \qquad\text{(9)} The map to that base is an unramified cover with all the independent sign changes in (6) as deck transformations.

Proposition 2. The complex manifold ℛS\mathcal R_S is path connected. Removing any further proper algebraic divisor from it leaves a path-connected set. In particular any two surviving regular points can be joined without crossing that divisor.

Proof. The base in (9) is path connected. On the complex line through any two allowed points, none of its defining polynomials is identically zero, since it is nonzero at those points. There are only finitely many forbidden values on that line, and a path can detour around them in its complex parameter plane.

A small loop about a generic smooth point of fe=0f_e=0 changes the sign of rer_e alone. Such a point can be chosen away from all the other hypersurfaces in (9). Likewise a loop about a generic point of av=ia_v=i changes the sign of svs_v alone. If an internal edge touches vv, its radicand there is aw2a_w^2, so it is nonzero at a generic point of this divisor. The preceding loops generate every deck sign. Conjugating them by base paths permits all these sign changes at any one base point. Thus a lifted path between two base points can be followed by loops that correct its endpoint sheet. This proves connectedness of ℛS\mathcal R_S.

Finally cover a path in ℛS\mathcal R_S by finitely many complex coordinate balls. A proper algebraic divisor has empty interior in this connected manifold: otherwise a local defining holomorphic function, and hence the divisor, would contain the whole component. Choose intermediate points outside the divisor in the successive ball overlaps. In each ball, the complex line between its two chosen points is not contained in the divisor. Its intersections with the divisor are discrete; only finitely many lie near the compact line segment under consideration. Detour around them inside the ball. The resulting concatenated path proves the last assertion. ▫\square

This argument also proves that the independent absolute root signs are connected by complex continuation. It does not prove that they can be changed within the positive tetrahedron domain.

3. The original face maps, not only their multipliers

Fix an edge with a=ava=a_v, b=awb=a_w, and q=qeq=q_e. The coordinates in Section 3 of the geometry manuscript become E=q+iq−i,Hv=q−aq+a,Hw=q−bq+b,R=1+b2q2+1,R1=1−R=1+a2q2+1,Zv=q+aq−i,Zw=q+bq−i,Qv=1+ia1−ia.(10) \begin{gathered} E=\frac{q+i}{q-i},\qquad H_v=\frac{q-a}{q+a},\qquad H_w=\frac{q-b}{q+b},\\ R=\frac{1+b^2}{q^2+1},\qquad R_1=1-R=\frac{1+a^2}{q^2+1},\\ Z_v=\frac{q+a}{q-i},\qquad Z_w=\frac{q+b}{q-i},\qquad Q_v=\frac{1+ia}{1-ia}. \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(10)} Here RR is a face-coordinate ratio; it is unrelated to the ring in the proof of Theorem 1. No square root in addition to those in (1) has been introduced.

All denominators in (10) are units on (8). Across pages, the doubled disk-side triangle ratios are (−Qv−1,−Qv,−Qv−1)(-Q_v^{-1},-Q_v,-Q_v^{-1}) on the incoming boundary and (−Qv,−Qv−1,−Qv)(-Q_v,-Q_v^{-1},-Q_v) on the outgoing boundary. They depend only on the shared vertex parameter. Normalizing one ordered triangle then matches the full other triangle. The map between two ordered distinct triples is rational in their coordinates. Consequently every page identification and every finite product or inverse used to develop the original cut is rational in (a,q)(a,q). Apparent poles caused by an affine vertex moving to infinity can be handled by homogeneous coordinates; they do not require a new field extension.

Proposition 3. On ℛS\mathcal R_S, these maps give a rational PGL2\mathrm{PGL}_2 representation family of the original marked augmented exterior. Its image has the same irreducible Zariski closure as the marked complete germ with aU=0a_U=0.

Proof. Near oo, the original cut construction, the full page ratio table, and the pure and odd edge-return identities establish the representation of that exterior. This includes the original peripheral paths, rather than just their eigenvalues. Every group relation is a rational identity in the face matrices on the integral component XSX_S. An identity holding on the complete open analytic germ therefore holds in its function field. One elementary way to check this last step is to take the field norm of a putatively nonzero rational difference to ℂ(aS)\mathbb C(a_S). Its norm is nonzero, but would vanish on an open base polydisk, a contradiction. Regularity of the face maps extends the identity throughout (8).

The same argument applies to any polynomial relation among the resulting representation coordinates. Hence a relation holds on the complete germ if and only if it holds on the whole rational image. The closure of a rational image of an integral variety is irreducible, which proves the assertion. It also applies after passing to conjugacy invariants. It does not identify this closure with every component of the full representation or character variety. ▫\square

All assertions here are projective. Choosing determinant-one matrix representatives over the function field could require square roots of their determinants; none is needed for Proposition 3.

4. Avoiding critical points of the primitive-period map

There is also a useful regularity divisor that can be removed without disconnecting the marked component. Fix arbitrary integers nvn_v, v∈Sv\in S. It is therefore nonsingular for every integer choice of nn.

Corollary 4. Every point of ℛS\mathcal R_S with det⁡Jn≠0\det J_n\ne0 can be connected to oo through points having that same property. Along such a path the actual primitive multipliers admit continued logarithms, and the selected map (17) has a local inverse at every point.

Proof. The determinant is a nonzero rational function, regular on (8), since its value at oo is nonzero. Apply Proposition 2 after deleting its zero divisor. The factors in (16) are nonzero on (8), so they have logarithms along any parameterized interval. The holomorphic inverse function theorem applies to (19) locally. ▫\square

This path does not in general hold FvF_v constant. In particular it is not a path of solutions of the fixed primitive filling equations Fv=−πiF_v=-\pi i. Its endpoint logarithms can differ from independently prescribed endpoint logarithms by integral periods. Corollary 4 does not discard that difference.

5. What this resolves for short partial fillings

For a proposed short partial filling, the first question is whether its marked discrete faithful representation has a lift to the original coordinates (1). If such a lift is supplied, (5) tests whether it lies on the marked complete component. If it also satisfies (8) and the Jacobian condition, the preceding results provide a path of regular marked representation and period charts from completeness to that lift. The complex path can pass through tetrahedra with negative or zero imaginary shape, provided the shapes themselves stay nondegenerate.

There is no proof here that every hyperbolic short partial filling has such a lift. There is also no assertion that a regular path preserves the original integration cycle or prevents cancellation of its geometric contribution. Those are separate from the algebraic component and period-map regularity proved above. On the positive domain, the stronger metric and full-period conclusions already follow from the marked completion theorem; this note adds complex continuation outside that domain without extending those metric conclusions.

The independent-square-class argument for unrestricted edge parameters already appears in Section 7 of the all-circle characteristic note. The additional issue here is the repeated roots created by fixing aU=0a_U=0, their marked component, and the regular continuation of the literal original face construction. No new general claim about multiquadratic extensions is intended.
