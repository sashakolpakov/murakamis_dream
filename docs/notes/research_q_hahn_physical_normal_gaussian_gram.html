The full physical q-Hahn value and normal form a Gaussian–Hermite pair

Written proof, 24 November 2026.

The original ordinary column and its actual confluent partner have a nondegenerate two-column Hermitian Gram after an explicit centering and scaling. The ordinary column is Gaussian in its full physical magnetic norm. Orthogonal projection removes the scalar part of the normal and leaves its first Hermite mode. Both conclusions include every physical magnetic coordinate, rather than a truncated window.

The result concerns one complete double channel at a real physical degree separated from the two degree endpoints. It does not turn the original bilinear gluing into a positive pairing. The proof also computes the entrywise value-column Gram across a larger channel window, retaining the original center phases.

7. What the Gram does and does not change

The magnetic energy calculation [E] already gives exact Fourier norm comparisons and its original center action. A separate initialized-meridian-row calculation in the research ledger constructs fixed-order Gaussian jet Grams for a different source. Here the vectors are the ordinary and actual generic-confluent q-Hahn columns (3), and (12) controls their complete physical tails. Their particular normal direction and the cross-channel scale in (25) are computed from these original source equations.

The Hermitian Gram above is not the bilinear complete Christoffel–Darboux gluing matrix. Original inverse rows, its measure and phase, the marked closing, turns, and external normalizations must be transported through any basis change. No cap is asserted to be the Hermitian adjoint of a cup. The statement also excludes the physical endpoint t=N−1t=N-1 as a uniform degree family and excludes the isolated simple channel.

This supplies full-packet norm and transverse-normal information. It does not estimate the sum over distinct Casimir nodes, match geometric packets across an arbitrary short component, or prove a nonzero original scalar after closing. No new knot-volume case or finite-exception theorem follows without those further estimates.
