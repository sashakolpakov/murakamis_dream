A modulus-only switch on the independently derived 6_1 longitude curve

28 October 2026. This note tests the remaining classical inequality in the macroscopic actual-source spectral upper bound. It proves that the largest-modulus longitude root switches between two distinct analytic branches already on the 6_1 two-bridge curve. The switch is exact and transverse. Consequently, a theorem that simply identifies the pointwise largest-modulus root with one distinguished geometric branch cannot hold for this curve.

This does not invalidate the spectral upper bound, which makes no such identification. An actual all-level quantum annihilator with this classical companion has not been verified here. The integral values below are labelled numerical diagnostics; no rigorous volume calibration or general sharp upper bound is claimed.

1. The curve from the two meridians, without a polynomial table

Use the p=9,q=5 two-bridge presentation, with alternating exponents (+,-,-,+,+,-,-,+), and matrices

A=(M10M−1),B=(M0−zM−1),W=(AB−1A−1B)2. A=\begin{pmatrix}M&1\\0&M^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\-z&M^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad W=(AB^{-1}A^{-1}B)^2.

The relation is WA=BWWA=BW. Setting t=M2+M−2t=M^2+M^{-2}, direct matrix multiplication gives

WA−BW=(0RzR0),R=z4+(5−2t)z3+(t2−6t+11)z2+(t2−7t+12)z+5−2t. WA-BW=\begin{pmatrix}0&R\\zR&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad R=z^4+(5-2t)z^3+(t^2-6t+11)z^2+(t^2-7t+12)z+5-2t. \tag{1}

Let $W^{\rm rev}=(BA^{-1}B^{-1}A)^2$. The exponent sum is zero, so the usual peripheral word has no meridian-power correction: $\Lambda=WW^{\rm rev}$. It commutes with B modulo R. Its chosen eigenvalue is L=Λ11L=\Lambda_{11}. An explicitly checked simplification is

L=−W12(M,z)W12(M−1,z),W12=−(−M2+z+1)(−M4z+M2z2+2M2z+2M2−z)M3. L=-\frac{W_{12}(M,z)}{W_{12}(M^{-1},z)},\qquad W_{12}=-\frac{(-M^2+z+1)(-M^4z+M^2z^2+2M^2z+2M^2-z)}{M^3}. \tag{2}

Equation (2) holds on the regular open set and its cleared numerator vanishes modulo R. Eliminating z gives, up to a nonzero meridian factor, the following normalized longitude polynomial. Put Y=M2Y=M^2:

F(Y,L)=L4+a(Y)L3+b(Y)L2+c(Y)L+1, F(Y,L)=L^4+a(Y)L^3+b(Y)L^2+c(Y)L+1, \tag{3}

a=−Y4+Y3+3+3Y−1−2Y−2,b=Y4−3Y3−Y2+3Y+6+3Y−1−Y−2−3Y−3+Y−4,c=−2Y2+3Y+3+Y−3−Y−4. \begin{aligned} a&=-Y^4+Y^3+3+3Y^{-1}-2Y^{-2},\\ b&=Y^4-3Y^3-Y^2+3Y+6+3Y^{-1}-Y^{-2}-3Y^{-3}+Y^{-4},\\ c&=-2Y^2+3Y+3+Y^{-3}-Y^{-4}. \end{aligned}

For |Y|=1|Y|=1, b is real and c=a‾c=\bar a, so F is self-inversive. The defining matrices, cleared relation, peripheral commutation, rational longitude, and resultant are checked symbolically by the independent audit. Swapping A and B gives the equivalent reversed convention independently checked by the root agent; no table identity is used in the proof.

2. Exact transverse switch, without numerical branch tracking

F=L4+5L3+15L2+5L+1. F=L^4+5L^3+15L^2+5L+1.

Writing w=L+L−1w=L+L^{-1} reduces it to

w2+5w+13=0,w=−5±3i32. w^2+5w+13=0,\qquad w=\frac{-5\pm3i\sqrt3}{2}. \tag{4}

The four roots are distinct. The two outside the unit circle are conjugates, approximately

L±=−2.325158539755156±2.809324795549955i,|L±|=3.646733886903201. L_\pm=-2.325158539755156\ \pm2.809324795549955i, \qquad |L_\pm|=3.646733886903201.

They therefore define two different analytic eigenvalue branches near x*x_*. Implicit differentiation gives the exact radical expression

ddxlog⁡|L|=ℜ(−2πiYFYLFL). \frac{d}{dx}\log|L| =\Re\left(-2\pi i\,\frac{Y F_Y}{L F_L}\right). \tag{5}

Substitution of (4) into (5), evaluated using certified 192-bit Arb balls, gives

The root modulus ordering thus reverses across x*x_*. On a sufficiently small left interval |L−|>|L+|>1|L_-|>|L_+|>1; on the right |L+|>|L−|>1|L_+|>|L_-|>1. This is a modulus crossing, not an eigenvalue collision. No root-selection numerical algorithm is needed for (6). In particular, the pointwise spectral envelope is locally a splice of two distinct analytic branches. Its integral across any small interval containing the switch strictly exceeds the integral of either one of these two branches across the same interval.

The rigorous result of this note is the local transverse switch (6), not a certified comparison of the last row with hyperbolic volume. The next analytic question is therefore phase-aware transport of the actual recurrence. Since the expanding eigenvalues remain distinct at the modulus switch, a product of slowly varying companion matrices need not follow the pointwise larger modulus by arbitrary switching. This note proves no adiabatic estimate and no lower bound for the selected original source.
