A triangle upper bound for every one-sided relative-energy coefficient

31 October 2026. local theorem. Every actual LEFT or RIGHT coefficient has relative spectral energy bounded by one complete-triangle entropy, uniformly through both parities and all closed degree boundaries. The original middle degrees remain unchanged. A shared global entropy comparison is a separate problem.

Use the actual coefficient and reference conventions in the endpoint-conditioned source theorem. For a nonnegative triangle load of total one half, put

C(α1,α2,α3)=∑j=13f(αj),f(t)=Λ(πt)+Λ(π/2−πt)π,Λ(a)=−∫0alog⁡|2sin⁡u|du. C(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)=\sum_{j=1}^3f(\alpha_j),\qquad f(t)=\frac{\Lambda(\pi t)+\Lambda(\pi/2-\pi t)}\pi, \quad \Lambda(a)=-\int_0^a\log|2\sin u|\,du.

If an actual one-sided exponent is v=(vx,vb,vc,vy)v=(v_x,v_b,v_c,v_y), write q=v/(N−1)q=v/(N-1). The result is

supv one-sidedσ∈[−2,2][1Nlog|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)−C(qx,qy,12−qx−qy)]≤o(1). \boxed{\displaystyle \sup_{\substack{v\text{ one-sided}\\\sigma\in[-2,2]}} \left[ \frac1N\log\frac{|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)} -C(q_x,q_y,\tfrac12-q_x-q_y)\right]\le o(1).} \tag{T}

Zero coefficients have logarithm minus infinity. The error is uniform; the outer sum is at most one half on either original one-sided support. Protected incidences retain their exact zero targets.

1. Positive dependence on a single spectral parameter

Write θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N, sj=2sin⁡(jθ)s_j=2\sin(j\theta), and normalize the original tail by

qi,j=Ki,j/∏r=i+1jsr2. q_{i,j}=K_{i,j}/\prod_{r=i+1}^j s_r^2.

It satisfies qi,i=1q_{i,i}=1, qi,i+1=x+yq_{i,i+1}=x+y, and

qi,j=(x+y)qi,j−1+(ηj−1)xyqi,j−2,ηj=λdj,dj=cos⁡θ−cos⁡((2j−1)θ)>0,λ=cos⁡θ+cos⁡(2πδ). q_{i,j}=(x+y)q_{i,j-1}+(\eta_j-1)xyq_{i,j-2}, \quad \eta_j=\frac{\lambda}{d_j}, \quad d_j=\cos\theta-\cos((2j-1)\theta)>0, \quad \lambda=\cos\theta+\cos(2\pi\delta). \tag{1}

The actual start has j≥i+2j\geq i+2 in the last term, so no zero sine denominator occurs. On the entire interval 0≤δ≤1/20\leq\delta\leq1/2, one has |λ|≤λ0=1+cos⁡θ|\lambda|\leq\lambda_0=1+\cos\theta for N≥3N\geq3. The parameter λ\lambda may be negative near the last spectral endpoint.

For an interval of length r=j−ir=j-i, expand the continuant in the parameters η\eta. A chosen set of disjoint edges contributes (xy)|S|∏e∈Sηe(xy)^{|S|}\prod_{e\in S}\eta_e. The remaining vertex intervals contribute their baseline continuants

hℓ(x,y)=∑k=0ℓxkyℓ−k, h_\ell(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}x^ky^{\ell-k},

which solve (1) with η=0\eta=0. Thus every outer coefficient qi,j,k(λ)=[xkyr−k]qi,jq_{i,j,k}(\lambda)=[x^ky^{r-k}]q_{i,j} is a polynomial in λ\lambda with nonnegative coefficients. This expansion follows by grouping the selected η\eta terms in the usual matching expansion of a tridiagonal determinant; the deleted-edge complements are disjoint intervals.

Let λ0=1+cos⁡θ\lambda_0=1+\cos\theta. Cauchy applied to these nonnegative polynomial coefficients gives the exact finite inequality for every real λ\lambda

|qi,j,k(λ)|2qi,j,k(λ0)≤qi,j,k(λ2λ0). \frac{|q_{i,j,k}(\lambda)|^2}{q_{i,j,k}(\lambda_0)} \leq q_{i,j,k}\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{\lambda_0}\right). \tag{2}

Indeed, if qi,j,k(λ)=∑rcrλrq_{i,j,k}(\lambda)=\sum_r c_r\lambda^r with cr≥0c_r\geq0, use the vectors crλr/λ0r/2\sqrt{c_r}\lambda^r/\lambda_0^{r/2} and crλ0r/2\sqrt{c_r}\lambda_0^{r/2}. The first may have signed entries; Cauchy still applies. It is a coefficient inequality for the actual tail, not a positivity assertion for the original tail on the final spectral cap. It does not assume real negative roots or a coefficient central-limit theorem.

2. The original prefix and suffix normalization

For the actual LEFT exponent v=(k,i,d−j,r−k)v=(k,i,d-j,r-k), write, up to a common unit sign, aN(v;σ)=BN(δ)qi,j,k(λ)a_N(v;\sigma)=B_N(\delta)q_{i,j,k}(\lambda) with σ=−2cos⁡(2πδ)\sigma=-2\cos(2\pi\delta). Here BNB_N contains the exact omitted-half scalar, all unused lower prefix and suffix diagonals, and ∏r=i+1jsr2\prod_{r=i+1}^j s_r^2. The original endpoint reference is pN(v)=BNrefqi,j,k(λ0)p_N(v)=B_N^{\mathrm{ref}}q_{i,j,k}(\lambda_0), BNref>0B_N^{\mathrm{ref}}>0. At even levels the reference is −VN(−2)-V_N(-2); at odd levels it is VN(−2)−N−2∂σVN(−2)V_N(-2)-N^{-2}\partial_\sigma V_N(-2). For a one-sided odd path the central zero is unused, so the derivative removes precisely that zero; its remaining normalization has only polynomial size. Bare odd VN(−2)V_N(-2) is not the reference.

Consequently (2) implies

∑k=0r|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)xkyr−k≤|BN(δ)|2BNrefqi,j(x,y;λ2/λ0). \sum_{k=0}^{r}\frac{|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)}x^ky^{r-k} \leq\frac{|B_N(\delta)|^2}{B_N^{\mathrm{ref}}} q_{i,j}\left(x,y;\lambda^2/\lambda_0\right). \tag{3}

All unused local rows remain in BNB_N; the original external character and marked source targets are unchanged. We will estimate (3) by the uniform tail upper bound and logarithmic prefix/suffix estimates in the global CROSS proof, also used for arbitrary tail intervals in the LEFT theorem.

3. The effective weighted kernel

Put μ=i/N\mu=i/N, ν=j/N\nu=j/N, w=ν−μw=\nu-\mu, and, for 0<t<1/20<t<1/2,

s=sin⁡(πt),c=cos⁡(πt),z=sin⁡2(πδ),kδ2=1−z,A=x+y,ρ=4xy/A2. s=\sin(\pi t),\quad c=\cos(\pi t),\quad z=\sin^2(\pi\delta),\quad k_\delta^2=1-z, \quad A=x+y,\quad \rho=4xy/A^2.

cos⁡(2πδeff)=(cos⁡θ+cos⁡(2πδ))21+cos⁡θ−cos⁡θ. \cos(2\pi\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}) =\frac{(\cos\theta+\cos(2\pi\delta))^2}{1+\cos\theta} -\cos\theta.

Thus |λ|≤λ0|\lambda|\le\lambda_0 gives 0≤λ2/λ0≤λ00\le\lambda^2/\lambda_0\le\lambda_0, in the original positive-tail range. Its coefficients βj\beta_j may be negative; no positive-matrix or separated-eigenvalue hypothesis is added. Define

5. The coefficient consequence and its current scope

Write ξ=k/N\xi=k/N. Weighted coefficient extraction in (3), (9), and the original complete-triangle Legendre formula give

limsup⁡1Nlog⁡supσ∈[−2,2]|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)≤C(ξ,w−ξ,1/2−w). \limsup\frac1N\log\frac{\sup_{\sigma\in[-2,2]}|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)} \leq C(\xi,w-\xi,1/2-w). \tag{10}

The statement is uniform on the entire closed one-sided simplex. For this, the terminal weighted action minus the outer linear weights has infimum C(ξ,w−ξ,1/2−w)C(\xi,w-\xi,1/2-w) by the proved terminal-triangle Legendre formula. At each limiting degree choose a fixed finite positive tilt within any prescribed error of this infimum. Continuity of the terminal integral in ww, of the linear degree weights, and of CC gives a neighborhood on which that same tilt works. Compactness supplies finitely many such tilts. Apply the uniform weighted estimates only to this finite set. The original d=N−1d=N-1 normalization differs by O(1/N)O(1/N); uniform continuity of CC absorbs it into the o(1)o(1) error. This includes outer degree zero and collapsing intervals. The finite signed-parameter Cauchy inequality already covers the entire real spectral interval, including its final O(N−2)O(N^{-2}) cap; no cap extension is needed here. Both parity reference normalizations are kept. RIGHT follows by the original row reflection. If a protected outer is zero, only its exponent-zero coefficients survive, and the sharp protected-outer formula attains (10).

The middle degrees remain μ\mu and 1−ν1-\nu in the original shared flow. Although the entropy bound depends only on the two outer degrees, it is not permission to reassign middle loads independently. The global energy comparison still requires a proof using the original shared targets.

6. Validation and scope

The focused audit and results check exact finite identities and selected original coproduct coefficients. They do not use sampled large levels as a proof of the uniform theorem.

The theorem bounds local coefficients before their original shared extraction. It does not move middle degrees to new faces, identify the bound with a representation volume, or prove the global inequality. The joint shared-flow criterion records the precise conditional consequence.
