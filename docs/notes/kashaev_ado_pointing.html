A literature quantum A-family with the Kashaev evaluation fixed

Research note, 25 October 2026. The preceding crossing calculation independently identifies the normalized enhanced Kashaev period with the reduced highest-weight quantum-group period. Here we give its meridian family, its exact normalization lattice, and its implications for the norm torsor.

Definitions are the unrolled quantum-group R, pivotal and modified-dimension formulas in Brown–Dimofte–Garoufalidis–Geer, 2005.08176, Section 2. Their holonomicity and recursion-ideal comparison theorems are not assumed. The calculations below use the displayed definitions directly.

1. The zero-framed reduced family is rational in its color

Fix r>=2, q=exp(pi*i/r), and x=q^alpha. On V_alpha use

H v_j=(alpha-2j)v_j, K v_j=x q^(-2j)v_j,
E v_j=[j]_q[alpha-j+1]_q v_(j-1),
F v_j=v_(j+1),       0<=j<r.

Use the finite universal R from the preceding note with k<r, pivotal matrix K^(1-r), and twist

theta_alpha=q^((alpha^2+2(1-r)alpha)/2).

Let F_r(alpha) be the reduced (1,1)-tangle scalar for a zero-framed knot, with unknot value one. It is the usual modified-trace ADO invariant divided by the modified dimension, not a new local weight.

Rationality lemma. F_r(alpha) is a Laurent polynomial in x with complex coefficients at fixed q.

Proof: remove the common factor q(alpha2/2) from each positive R and its inverse from each negative R. The remaining entries are Laurent polynomials in x, since

q^((alpha-2c)(alpha-2d)/2)
       =q^(alpha^2/2) x^(-c-d) q^(2cd),
[alpha-j+1]_q
       =(x q^(-j+1)-x^(-1)q^(j-1))/(q-q^(-1)).

All denominators [k]_q!, k<r, are nonzero constants. For a braid word of writhe w, the total removed factor is q^(w alpha^2/2). It cancels with theta_alpha^(-w); the remainder is x^(-w(1-r)). The pivotal entries are also Laurent monomials in x. Finite products, sums and partial traces prove the assertion. Scalarity on generic typical colors and the resulting identity extend through the finite exceptional set.

In particular F_r(alpha+2r)=F_r(alpha). This has a direct algebraic proof from the defining matrices; it is not a consequence imported from a holonomicity theorem.

2. Exact specialization to the original Kashaev period

At alpha=r-1 the matrices are the r-dimensional highest-weight matrices of the crossing note. The two pivotal conventions differ by

K^(1-r)=(-1)^(r-1) K.

The corresponding twists differ by the same sign. For a b-strand braid closing to a knot, w has parity b-1. Hence the resulting factor (-1)^((r-1)(b-1-w)) is one. The dual convention in the crossing note is equivalent to the original highest-weight color: an explicit intertwiner with its dual is

B v_j=(-1)^j q^(j(j-r)) v_(r-1-j).

For example, with Delta E=E tensor K+1 tensor E and Delta F=F tensor 1+K^(-1) tensor F, the antipodes are S(E)=-E K^(-1) and S(F)=-K F. Direct substitution verifies B on E,F,K. The same substitution in the finite R gives R^T=(B tensor B)R(B^(-1) tensor B^(-1)).

Consequently the already specified enhanced period satisfies

F_r(r-1)=<K>_r.                                   (2.1)

This is a uniform matrix and pairing comparison for knots, with the zero framing and unknot normalization fixed.

The subsequent root-orbit pointing proof extends this calculation to every integer color 1<=j<=r: F_r(M=-q^j)=C_j(q), where C_j is the reduced j-dimensional period. Its proof uses the invariant quotient of the color and a filtered cut-trace argument for a one-component braid closure. In particular F_r(-q)=1 supplies a nonzero reference anchor on the same central fiber as M=1. For the full state this is a residue anchor, as the modified dimension has a pole at M=-q.

The all-level period theorem now uses these identities and polynomial rigidity to recover the exact Kashaev value from any specified actual all-level annihilator and finitely many fixed small colors. Whole q-germs handle singular steps with a uniform jet bound. Root-level rank one is not required.

3. The generic peripheral factor is an actual cyclic lattice gauge

Write N_r(alpha)=d_r(alpha)F_r(alpha) for the modified-trace ADO invariant. Its defining modified dimension is

d_r(alpha)=-q^(r(1-r)/2)
      (q^(alpha+1)-q^(-alpha-1))/(q^(r alpha)-q^(-r alpha)).

Introduce the marked meridian coordinate M=-q x, so that M=1 at alpha=r-1. Then, exactly,

d_r(M)=eta_r (M-M^(-1))/(M^r-M^(-r))
       =eta_r M^(r-1)/P_r(M^2),
eta_r=(-1)^(r+1)q^(r(1-r)/2)=i^(r+3),
P_r(t)=1+t+...+t^(r-1).                           (3.1)

Thus the modified-dimension factor is the cyclic stable-lattice generator 1/P_r(t) from Section 3.1 of the intrinsic norm-torsor note, with t=M^2, together with its explicit monomial and phase. This is an instance in the literature state itself, not just a model.

At M=1 the whole rational germ is regular and evaluates to

d_r(1)=eta_r/r,
<K>_r=r eta_r^(-1) N_r(r-1).                      (3.2)

Setting numerator and denominator separately to zero would discard this value. The exact comparison keeps the peripheral polynomial P_r and its specialization P_r(1)=r. No topological or norm conclusion uses a bare numerical multiplier in its place.

There is also a level-independent difference equation. Since the color shift sends M to q M and q^r=-1,

d_r(qM)/d_r(M)=g(q,M),
g(q,M)=-q^(-1)(q^2 M^2-1)/(M^2-1).               (3.3)

If f(M)=M-M^(-1), then g=-f(qM)/f(M). Thus this normalization line is a fixed rational gauge tensored with the sign character of the shift. Its root-level realization is the explicit peripheral divisor in (3.1). It is not a collection of arbitrary independent finite-level scalars.

The subsequent relative octahedral calculation also derives 1/P_r(M^2) as the missing cyclic transition of the finite factorial actually appearing in these R entries. Its polynomial recurrence retains the source M^(2r)e_partial, and its cyclic system is nonsingular even on the completion’s polar divisor. Thus this normalization has an explicit local relative origin as well as the meridian gauge calculation above. The global source/regulator comparison is not inferred from it.

4. The literature annihilator ideal and the pointed conjecture

The all-level ADO annihilator ideal is defined in the literature by

I_ADO={P(q,x,T): P(q_r,x,T)N_r(x)=0
                     for all but finitely many r},
T x=q x T, q_r=exp(pi*i/r).

See equation (1.5) of the cited definition source. Under x=-q^(-1)M, the exact change from N_r to F_r induces the automorphism of the localized Ore algebra C(q,M)[T^(+/-1);sigma]

M -> M,          T -> g(q,M) T.                  (4.1)

Indeed d_r^(-1) T d_r=g T. Therefore existence of a nonzero all-level annihilator is equivalent in the two normalizations, and their localized ideals correspond by (4.1). No unverified equality with the colored-Jones recursion ideal or AJ specialization is needed for this statement. The subsequent finite cut-state proof now supplies a nonzero actual all-level meridian annihilator for every knot. The cited paper’s holonomicity theorem remains outside the proof premises; the stronger multicomponent dimension statement is not needed for this conclusion. At M=1 the comparison uses the lattices and regular whole period germs in (3.1)–(3.2), since the localized coefficient g is meromorphic there. Localization alone does not specify this specialization.

The subsequent actual peripheral-fusion proof derives this gauge from the pivotal partial traces of the two adjacent-color projectors, without a modified-trace comparison theorem. The full state has elementary boundary actions M+M^(-1) and T+T^(-1). The fusion wall at M=1 has a nonzero square-zero Casimir residue with exactly normalized partial trace; its actual knot coefficient and the normal Kashaev functional are retained as distinct meridian derivatives. Peripheral skein relations thus yield actual annihilators, without identifying the entire ideals or their geometric components.

For this literature family with its prescribed pointing, the desired quantum-A volume statement is

lim_(r->infinity) (2*pi/r) log|F_r(M=1)|=Vol(S^3 minus K).
                                                           (4.2)

Equations (2.1) and (3.2) identify (4.2) with Kashaev’s original conjecture. The same rate for N_r at the regular germ M=1 is equivalent, since log r/r tends to zero and |eta_r|=1. This is a precise formulation with a fixed literature state, not an arbitrary solution of an operator. Equation (4.2) is not proved here.

5. What a root-of-unity central torsor does and does not select

There is a useful general constraint. Let a nonzero rational function h(x) solve a specialized finite-order q-difference equation with q a primitive n-th root. Let H=T^n be its companion monodromy as in the intrinsic norm-torsor note. Then

det(I-H)=0.                                       (5.1)

Proof: evaluation at h is a nonzero module map from the cyclic operator module to (C(x),sigma). On the target sigma^n=1. Hence the map kills H-I, so H-I cannot be invertible. This proof uses the solution pairing, not an identification of column vectors with solutions.

Both F_r and N_r are rational in x at zero framing. Thus any specified all-level operator annihilating them has a central v=1 sector at its regular root levels n=2r. This finite-level sector must not be identified automatically with the geometric component of the classical q->1 A-curve. The quantum family and its distinguished pointing remain essential. No assertion is made that the literal classical A-curve is the central spectral curve of each root-level state.

The required torsor comparison is therefore a comparison of the all-level pointed period with the marked geometric regulator, retaining the normalization lattice. The cyclic norm torsor alone does not select the volume-dominant geometric sector.
