Uniform full-dilogarithm ray moments and finite transfer inverses

Fix the full function and its marked negative ray from [F]. The ray moments have size comparable to N−1N^{-1}, uniformly over every physical degree, for all sufficiently large levels. The top degree has an additional endpoint contribution, which is retained below. The resulting finite nilpotent transfer and its inverse have subexponential operator bounds. No lower bound for a closed scalar observation is inferred.

3. Actual atom scope

The original unit-color transfers in [F, Section 5] have precisely ∥T∥≤1\|T\|\le1, TN=0T^N=0. Their unit geometric phases are retained by replacing TT by the corresponding unit multiple. In particular the original uu-ray is 𝒥−,N(Q−1T)\mathcal J_{-,N}(Q^{-1}T), while the vv-ray is 𝒥+,N(T)\mathcal J_{+,N}(T). Formula (15) keeps the additional (−1)a(-1)^a phase explicitly rather than dropping it.

The original reciprocal-factorial ray diagonal has entries Q−aj−,aQ^{-a}j_{-,a}, and the reflected factorial ray diagonal has entries NQd−aj+,d−aNQ^{d-a}j_{+,d-a}. By (4), for all sufficiently large levels their norms and inverse norms satisfy respectively ∥Drec∥≤C/N,∥Drec−1∥≤CN,∥Dfac∥+∥Dfac−1∥≤C.(19) \|D_{\mathrm{ rec}}\|\le C/N,\quad \|D_{\mathrm{ rec}}^{-1}\|\le CN, \qquad \|D_{\mathrm{ fac}}\|+\|D_{\mathrm{ fac}}^{-1}\|\le C. \qquad\text{(19)} These are the literal factors and index reflection of [F,(15)]. Any fixed composable product of these ray maps and fixed unitary maps therefore has norm and inverse norm at most NMeCNN^M e^{C\sqrt N}, with constants depending on the fixed product. The assertion is about those invertible atomic maps. Original cups, projections, marked covectors and subsequent contractions remain their specified maps; (16)–(19) give no lower bound for their scalar observation. Mixed circle/ray patterns may still retain exponential source factors.

The estimates concern the full function’s selected moments at radius one and the original nilpotent finite atoms. They do not identify a negative-ray chain with a geometric thimble, prove an off-root identity, or extend an odd-level tetrahedral state to even levels.
