A source-sensitive polar bound for Conway correction errors

Private written proof, 23 November 2026.

The backward closing row in the normalized Conway recursion determines a positive seminorm for each bilinear error term. This gives a computable error disk which is never worse than the earlier local full-norm disk. At the original cylindrical closing it removes an exponentially large multiplication cost. Every genuine turn changes the transported carrier explicitly; no boundary direction is removed permanently, and no positive lower rate is inferred.

1. The original normalized recursion and Frobenius carriers

Fix N≥2N\ge2, and use the complete normalized source of [C] and R. The actual Newton space is HN=ℂ[X]/(PN),PN=2TN(X/2)+2,d=N−1,q=eπi/N,cj=q2j+1+q−2j−1, H_N=\mathbb C[X]/(P_N),\quad P_N=2T_N(X/2)+2,\quad d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad c_j=q^{2j+1}+q^{-2j-1}, sr=∏a=1r2sin⁡(πa/N),er(X)=sr−2∏j<r(cj−X),e0=1.(1) s_r=\prod_{a=1}^r2\sin(\pi a/N),\qquad e_r(X)=s_r^{-2}\prod_{j<r}(c_j-X),\qquad e_0=1. \qquad\text{(1)} Empty products are one. Coefficient vectors have the Euclidean Hermitian norm. With Jer=ed−rJ e_r=e_{d-r}, [F] gives the actual quarter-turn and original Frobenius functional: J=J*=J−1,ϕ(f)=fd=(−1)dN2[Xd]rem⁡PNf,ϕ(fg)=f𝖳Jg.(2) J=J^*=J^{-1},\qquad \phi(f)=f_d=(-1)^dN^2[X^d]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}f, \qquad \phi(fg)=f^{\mathsf T}Jg. \qquad\text{(2)} Thus the full values and first normals at double roots, and the odd simple-root value, remain in this algebra. The two original closing rows are λd=ϕ\lambda_d=\phi and λ0(f)=f(c0)\lambda_0(f)=f(c_0).

Let bvb_v be the recorded normalization budget of each original tensor. At a product the normalized map is Φv(u,w)=uw/bv\Phi_v(u,w)=uw/b_v; at a turn it is Ju/bvJu/b_v. All normalized tensor norms are at most σ=1/16\sigma=1/16. Keep the original normalized closing LL, budget product BtotB_{\mathrm{ tot}}, and true packets aa: av=Φv((ac)c),k:=KN/Btot=Latop,∥av∥≤σ.(3) a_v=\Phi_v((a_c)_c),\qquad k:=K_N/B_{\mathrm{ tot}}=La_{\mathrm{ top}},\qquad \|a_v\|\le\sigma. \qquad\text{(3)} All original framing or other prescribed scalars stay in these data. Any separately recorded exterior scalar multiplies the final disk.

For a feasible trial xx, set e=a−x,rv=Φv((xc)c)−xv,A=DΦ(x),z=Lxtop,d=c(I−A)−1,(4) e=a-x,\quad r_v=\Phi_v((x_c)_c)-x_v,\quad A=D\Phi(x),\quad z=Lx_{\mathrm{ top}},\quad d=c(I-A)^{-1}, \qquad\text{(4)} where cc is LL on the top packet and zero elsewhere. The matrix AA is strictly triangular, so the displayed inverse of I−AI-A is a finite sum. These are the source-preserving rows of R. At each proper vertex define the full algebra carrier hv=Jdv𝖳.(5) h_v=Jd_v^{\mathsf T}. \qquad\text{(5)} There is no conjugation in (5): (2) gives dv(f)=ϕ(hvf)d_v(f)=\phi(h_vf). The backward recursion becomes hc=hvxc′/bvat a product, with sibling c′,hc=Jhv/bvat a genuine turn.(6) \begin{split} h_c&=h_vx_{c'}/b_v &&\text{at a product, with sibling }c',\\ h_c&=Jh_v/b_v&&\text{at a genuine turn}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(6)} The product rule follows from associativity inside ϕ\phi. For the turn, dc=dvJ/bvd_c=d_vJ/b_v and (5) give the second rule. It uses the literal turn, not an algebra automorphism. Hence these carriers retain every later return through the specified exterior.

2. A sharp polar estimate at a product

Write MhM_h for multiplication by hh in the full algebra. At a product define Bv=JMhv/bv,Sv=(Bv*Bv)1/2=|Mhv|/bv≽0.(7) B_v=JM_{h_v}/b_v,\qquad S_v=(B_v^*B_v)^{1/2}=|M_{h_v}|/b_v\succeq0. \qquad\text{(7)} These are known matrices once xx and its backward rows are known. Commutativity and (2) imply u𝖳Bvw=dvΦv(u,w),Bv𝖳=Bv.(8) u^{\mathsf T}B_vw=d_v\Phi_v(u,w),\qquad B_v^{\mathsf T}=B_v. \qquad\text{(8)} For every u,w∈HNu,w\in H_N, |dvΦv(u,w)|≤(u*Svu)1/2(w*Svw)1/2.(9) \boxed{\quad |d_v\Phi_v(u,w)| \le (u^*S_vu)^{1/2}(w^*S_vw)^{1/2}. \quad} \qquad\text{(9)} Here is a finite-dimensional proof including the complex conjugations. For any matrix BB, singular-value decomposition gives |⟨f,Bg⟩|≤⟨f,|B*|f⟩1/2⟨g,|B|g⟩1/2. |\langle f,Bg\rangle| \le\langle f,|B^*|f\rangle^{1/2} \langle g,|B|g\rangle^{1/2}. If B𝖳=BB^{\mathsf T}=B, then |B*|=|B|𝖳|B^*|=|B|^{\mathsf T}. Apply this with f=u‾f=\bar u, g=wg=w; its first quadratic form is u*|B|uu^*|B|u. This proves (9), also when the matrices are singular. Singular vectors show that its constant one is sharp whenever Bv≠0B_v\ne0. No claim of a Loewner-smallest majorizing metric is made.

Let βv=sup⁡∥u∥=∥w∥=1∥Φv(u,w)∥\beta_v=\sup_{\|u\|=\|w\|=1}\|\Phi_v(u,w)\| be the bilinear operator norm. It is at most the normalized full tensor norm, hence at most σ\sigma. From (8), ∥Sv∥=∥Bv∥≤∥dv∥βv,ker⁡Sv=Ann⁡(hv).(10) \|S_v\|=\|B_v\|\le\|d_v\|\beta_v,\qquad \ker S_v=\operatorname{Ann}(h_v). \qquad\text{(10)} The first equality and bound use decomposable inputs u⊗wu\otimes w. They do not identify a bilinear norm with the norm of a row on the whole tensor product. The kernel statement is the exact equality ker⁡Mhv={u:hvu=0}\ker M_{h_v}=\{u:h_vu=0\}, including nilpotent directions. It concerns this particular contraction, not a quotient which can be transported unchanged through another turn.

These Grams also assemble on the literal tree. Each packet is a child of at most one product, so distinct sibling pairs are disjoint. Let ℬ\mathcal B have blocks (0BvBv0)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&B_v\\B_v&0\end{smallmatrix}\right) on those pairs and zero on unused packets. Then ∑vproducteL𝖳BveR=12e𝖳ℬe,|ℬ|=𝒮=⨁vproduct(Sv⊕Sv)⊕0,|12e𝖳ℬe|≤12e*𝒮e.(10a) \sum_{v\ {\mathrm{ product}}}e_L^{\mathsf T}B_ve_R =\tfrac12e^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Be,\qquad |\mathcal B|=\mathcal S =\bigoplus_{v\ {\mathrm{ product}}}(S_v\oplus S_v)\oplus0, \qquad \left|\tfrac12e^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Be\right| \le\tfrac12e^*\mathcal Se. \qquad\text{(10a)} This is a positive majorant for the error at the fixed trial field. It makes no positivity assertion for the original complex source.

3. A computable disk for the original scalar

The exact error identity in R is (I−A)e=r+Q(e),k=z+dr+∑vproductdvΦv(eL,eR),(11) (I-A)e=r+Q(e),\qquad k=z+dr+\sum_{v\ {\mathrm{ product}}}d_v\Phi_v(e_L,e_R), \qquad\text{(11)} where Qv(e)=Φv(eL,eR)Q_v(e)=\Phi_v(e_L,e_R) at products and zero otherwise. Only the last sum requires an estimate. Compute the known correction and nonnegative scalar majorants upward through the actual tree: δ=(I−A)−1r,Δv=∥rv∥+σ∑cΔc,(12) \delta=(I-A)^{-1}r,\qquad \Delta_v=\|r_v\|+\sigma\sum_{c}\Delta_c, \qquad\text{(12)} ωv=∑c∥Avc∥ωc+𝟏vproductβvΔLΔR.(13) \omega_v=\sum_c\|A_{vc}\|\omega_c+ \mathbf1_{v\ {\mathrm{ product}}}\beta_v\Delta_L\Delta_R. \qquad\text{(13)} Empty sums are zero. The argument in R, equations (16)–(18) gives ∥ev∥≤Δv\|e_v\|\le\Delta_v: at a product use the ordered difference Φ(eL,aR)+Φ(xL,eR)\Phi(e_L,a_R)+\Phi(x_L,e_R), with the true and trial packets in their prescribed balls. Subtracting δ\delta from (11) gives (I−A)(e−δ)=Q(e)(I-A)(e-\delta)=Q(e). Its triangular equations and (13) therefore prove ∥ev−δv∥≤ωv.(14) \|e_v-\delta_v\|\le\omega_v. \qquad\text{(14)} Thus both majorants are explicit finite calculations, with no unknown closed invariant used as input.

At a product put sv=∥Sv∥s_v=\|S_v\|, and, for either child cc, set tvc=min⁡{svΔc,(δc*Svδc)1/2+svωc},Rpol(x)=∑vproducttvLtvR.(15) t_{vc}=\min\left\{\sqrt{s_v}\,\Delta_c, (\delta_c^*S_v\delta_c)^{1/2} +\sqrt{s_v}\,\omega_c\right\}, \qquad R_{\mathrm{ pol}}(x)=\sum_{v\ {\mathrm{ product}}}t_{vL}t_{vR}. \qquad\text{(15)} Triangle inequality for the seminorm ∥Sv1/2⋅∥\|S_v^{1/2}\cdot\|, (12), and (14) bound ∥Sv1/2ec∥\|S_v^{1/2}e_c\| by tvct_{vc}. Equations (9)–(11) consequently give |k−(z+dr)|≤Rpol(x)≤∑vproduct∥dv∥βvΔLΔR=Rloc(x).(16) |k-(z+dr)|\le R_{\mathrm{ pol}}(x) \le\sum_{v\ {\mathrm{ product}}} \|d_v\|\beta_v\Delta_L\Delta_R =R_{\mathrm{ loc}}(x). \qquad\text{(16)} The last expression is the earlier local radius of R, with its actual local bilinear norms. For evaluation, βv\beta_v may everywhere be replaced by its recorded full tensor upper bound; no bilinear maximization is required, and the same comparison remains valid.

Keep also R’s global quadratic radius. The fully computable result is k̂=z+dr,R(x)=min⁡{Rpol(x),∥r∥2/343},(17) \widehat k=z+dr,\qquad R(x)=\min\{R_{\mathrm{ pol}}(x),\ \|r\|^2/343\}, \qquad\text{(17)} Btot(|k̂|−R)+≤|KN|≤Btot(|k̂|+R).(18) \boxed{\quad B_{\mathrm{ tot}}(|\widehat k|-R)_+ \le |K_N|\le B_{\mathrm{ tot}}(|\widehat k|+R). \quad} \qquad\text{(18)} It is never a worse disk than the earlier minimum of the local and global radii. Vanishing carriers and singular polar matrices require no inversion or exceptional case. The center remains the original coherent correction, before taking an absolute value.

4. An exponential saving at the actual cylindrical closing

Suppose the top proper gate is a product with budget bvb_v, and the original normalized closing is L=χϕ/bρL=\chi\phi/b_\rho. The prescribed scalar χ\chi and its actual closing budget bρb_\rho are retained. Then hv=χ/bρ,Bv=χbρbvJ,Sv=|χ|bρbvI.(19) h_v=\chi/b_\rho,\qquad B_v=\frac{\chi}{b_\rho b_v}J,\qquad S_v=\frac{|\chi|}{b_\rho b_v}I. \qquad\text{(19)} Thus the top contribution to (16) is at most |χ|ΔLΔR/(bρbv)|\chi|\Delta_L\Delta_R/(b_\rho b_v). The old local bound instead charges |χ|∥M∥bilΔLΔR/(bρbv)|\chi|\|M\|_{\mathrm{ bil}}\Delta_L\Delta_R/(b_\rho b_v), where MM is actual multiplication. This is a bilinear saving: |ϕ(uw)|≤∥u∥∥w∥|\phi(uw)|\le\|u\|\|w\|, whereas the full tensor row ϕM\phi M has Euclidean norm N\sqrt N.

The difference is exponential. For k=⌊N/4⌋k=\lfloor N/4\rfloor and all sufficiently large levels, the exact formula in M, equation (2) gives ∥M∥bil≥μk,k,2k=s2k2/sk4,log⁡μk,k,2k=2GCatπN+O(log⁡N).(20) \|M\|_{\mathrm{ bil}}\ge\mu_{k,k,2k} =s_{2k}^{\,2}/s_k^{\,4},\qquad \log\mu_{k,k,2k}=\frac{2G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}}\pi N+O(\log N). \qquad\text{(20)} For completeness, let J0(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dtJ_0(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi t)dt. Subtracting log⁡t\log t leaves a smooth function on every interval [0,b][0,b], b<1b<1; its Riemann-sum error is bounded. Integral comparison for ∑log⁡(a/N)\sum\log(a/N) gives log⁡sn=NJ0(n/N)+O(log⁡N)\log s_n=NJ_0(n/N)+O(\log N) in the ranges used here. The elementary sine double-angle identity gives J0(1/2)=0J_0(1/2)=0 and J0(1/4)=−GCat/(2π)J_0(1/4)=-G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/(2\pi), where GCat=∫0π/4log⁡(cot⁡t)dtG_{\mathrm{ Cat}}=\int_0^{\pi/4}\log(\cot t)dt. Indeed the integrals of log⁡(2sin⁡t)\log(2\sin t) and log⁡(2cos⁡t)\log(2\cos t) on [0,π/4][0,\pi/4] sum to zero; the cosine integral minus the sine integral is GCatG_{\mathrm{ Cat}}. These facts prove (20), with the rounding of kk changing only the stated error.

The same normalization budgets occur on both sides of this comparison. Restoring BtotB_{\mathrm{ tot}} preserves the ratio. The saving comes from composing multiplication with the original closing before estimating it. Other products can still control the radius of a larger expression.

5. Turns, marked caps, and scope

For the other original closure L=χλ0/bρL=\chi\lambda_0/b_\rho, (2) gives hv=χed/bρh_v=\chi e_d/b_\rho. Since Medu=u0edM_{e_d}u=u_0e_d, its polar matrix at a product is Sv=|χ|bρbv|e0⟩⟨e0|.(21) S_v=\frac{|\chi|}{b_\rho b_v} |e_0\rangle\langle e_0|. \qquad\text{(21)} Only the two value errors at c0c_0 enter that contraction. But the next genuine turn sends this carrier to a scalar multiple of 11, by (6), and the full-rank matrix (19) returns. Thus neither normal directions nor a future return have been discarded.

The matrices and radii above apply to arbitrary feasible trial fields, all levels and both parities, with the literal original closing. They do not reduce the physical dimension, assert positivity of the complex error, or supply an asymptotic estimate for k̂\widehat k. A lower bound still needs that corrected center to exceed its proved radius at the desired rate. No volume identity or finite-exception theorem is inferred. The new quantitative assertions are the source-sensitive no-worse disk (15)–(18) and the exponential local saving (19)–(20), rather than a claim of novelty for polar decomposition or adjoint-weighted error estimation.
