Independent audit of bounded-jet cyclotomic Mellin uniqueness

27 October 2026. The proof of the whole-function uniqueness theorem passes independently. Its finite X-window, uniform quantum-jet budget, and cyclotomic numerator condition are sufficient as written. No semisimplicity, minimal operator, or scalar-ratio hypothesis is needed.

1. The finite window really is closed

In the exact Fourier ordering, a monomial X−jSℓX^{-j}S^\ell takes the coefficient wm+jw_{m+j} to power XmX^m, with factor QℓmQ^{\ell m} after the retained Q−ℓjQ^{-\ell j}. Since 0≤j≤d0\le j\le d and m≤N−dm\le N-d, only coefficients through wNw_N enter. Hence a truncation at XNX^N is enough for the entire indicated window. Negative S-powers cause no change to this statement: they rescale the same X exponent.

Subtracting the actual truncation therefore gives precisely ∑j=0daj(Q,Qm)em+j=0mod⁡ϵB+1. \sum_{j=0}^d a_j(Q,Q^m)e_{m+j}=0 \quad\bmod\epsilon^{B+1}. The starting lattice sets every ere_r below n0n_0 to zero. There is no unmentioned assumption on the X-degree of higher quantum-normal coefficients.

2. The singular-step budget and truncated valuation argument

After one fixed coherent denominator clearing, ada_d is Laurent polynomial in Q,YQ,Y. Its Q-span AA implies ad(Q,Qm)≠0a_d(Q,Q^m)\ne0 for m>Am>A: equality of two collected exponents u+mvu+mv with different vv’s would force m≤Am\le A. Its at most ss remaining monomials have nonzero-root multiplicity at most s−1s-1, by the logarithmic-derivative Vandermonde determinant.

For all sufficiently large levels, ad(QN,Y)≠0a_d(Q_N,Y)\ne0 as a Laurent polynomial in YY. The visited Y-values are distinct; there are at most DD roots on them. Thus the sum of all leading vanishing orders through coefficient NN is at most B=D(s−1)B=D(s-1). The excluded levels include any zeros or poles of fixed Q-scalar clearing factors and any specialization where all Y-coefficients of ada_d vanish. There are only finitely many such root values. No uniform lower bound on their nonzero numerical magnitudes is needed.

The truncated-ring step is valid despite zero divisors. If k=B+1k=B+1, h=ϵμuh=\epsilon^\mu u with uu a unit, and he∈ϵtRkhe\in\epsilon^tR_k, t≤kt\le k, then e∈ϵt−μRk(t≥μ). e\in\epsilon^{t-\mu}R_k\qquad(t\ge\mu). The additional annihilator ϵk−μRk\epsilon^{k-\mu}R_k is already contained in that asserted ideal. Applying this at each step gives er∈ϵk−MrRke_r\in\epsilon^{k-M_r}R_k, so every root coefficient through XNX^N agrees. In particular no singular step was divided at its root value.

For the finitely many fixed initial colors the leading trajectory polynomials are units at all sufficiently large roots, as suggested in the outline. The written proof is stronger: the total budget would handle their vanishing as well, whenever its other hypotheses hold.

3. The whole pole lattice is the necessary last step

Subtracting the two cyclotomic conditions gives H(X)=(1−XN)(WN−ZN)∈ℂ[X],deg⁡H≤N. H(X)=(1-X^N)(W_N-Z_N)\in\mathbb C[X],\qquad \deg H\le N . Both expansions have the same starting lattice and agree through power NN. Thus every coefficient of HH through its maximal degree vanishes, proving WN=ZNW_N=Z_N.

Simple cyclotomic poles alone would not suffice: an arbitrary polynomial part of higher degree could remain invisible to the finite window. The stated degree bound and prescribed low prefix are therefore substantive source-lattice data. The residue at infinity is then fixed, including the case that the actual Kashaev value vanishes.

4. The exact actual-unknot sharpness example checks

Direct Ore multiplication gives the displayed actual operator (T−Q)[(1−QY)T−(1−Y)]. (T-Q)[(1-QY)T-(1-Y)]. The inner bracket applied to the actual color 11 is (1−Q)Y(1-Q)Y, which the outer factor kills. Its exact Fourier transform is X−2(1−X)(1−QX)(1−S)X^{-2}(1-X)(1-QX)(1-S). Applying this to Xn0/(1−X)X^{n_0}/(1-X) gives exactly the stated finite source. The leading trajectory is 1−Qr1-Q^r; only r=Nr=N vanishes in the reconstruction range, and it has multiplicity one.

The alternative root section λXN/(1−XN)\lambda X^N/(1-X^N) is shift-invariant and preserves the affine root source and all the stated root pole/prefix data. Its residue contribution is +λ+\lambda. Its first normal would require (1−S0)g1=NUf′(U),f(U)=λU/(1−U). (1-S_0)g_1=NUf'(U),\qquad f(U)=\lambda U/(1-U). The cyclic average kills the left side and gives N2Uf′(U)≠0N^2Uf'(U)\ne0 on the right for λ≠0\lambda\ne0. This works in the formal Laurent field as well as the rational field. The two required jet levels in this actual example are therefore sharp.

The proof and the saved exact diagnostics support the stated uniqueness theorem. They do not construct a regulator candidate in the same pole and source lattice, establish its bounded quantum lift, or estimate the selected residue. Those remain the geometric comparison and volume questions.
