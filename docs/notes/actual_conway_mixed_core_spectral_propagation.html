Spectral and physical-color propagation through mixed Conway cores

31 October 2026. source theorem. This proves the sharp canonical spectral envelope and hereditary physical-color gap for an unflagged component, at its actual closed-probe action. Together with the proved decorated-character estimate, it gives a mutual induction through fixed mixed flagged/unflagged reduced source trees. All original full Hermite coordinates and finite targets are retained. No filled-volume statement or change of marked geometry is inferred here.

The finite inputs are the full-Hermite Newton and interpolation bounds, the exact root-character Fourier theorem, and the decorated-character coefficient duality. The separately proved positive coefficient-cone theorem supplies the original-action upper bound and strict high-sector gap. The reduced-hub adapter and rigid-profile calculation supply the actual component typing and additive classical constants. The intrinsic component-probe geometry identifies the same pressure and the proper effective-leaf parameter ranges.

1. Exact finite norms and the regular-index extension

Let d=N−1d=N-1, M=⌊d/2⌋M=\lfloor d/2\rfloor, and use the original complete algebra, normalized Newton polynomials Br=B̂rB_r=\widehat B_r, marked functional ϕ\phi, and turn TT. Write J(u)=∫0ulog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dtJ(u)=\int_0^u\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt and g(u)=J(2u)−4J(u)g(u)=J(2u)-4J(u). The norm ∥⋅∥𝒥\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal J} records all nodal values and first normals, with only the value at the odd simple node. Thus

TBr=Bd−r,ϕ(BrBs)=𝟏r+s=d,Djϵ[E]=T(RϵLjE),[Br]Djϵ[E]=ϕ(BrRϵLjE). T B_r=B_{d-r},\qquad \phi(B_rB_s)=\mathbf1_{r+s=d},\qquad D_j^\epsilon[E]=T(R^\epsilon L_jE),\qquad [B_r]D_j^\epsilon[E]=\phi(B_rR^\epsilon L_jE). \tag{1}

Write wN,r=Br(−2)w_{N,r}=B_r(-2) for r≤Mr\le M, and wN,r=1w_{N,r}=1 for r>Mr>M. All these weights are strictly positive. The existing finite estimates are uniform at both parities and all colors:

∥Br∥𝒥≤NCwN,r,log⁡wN,r=Ng(r/N)+O(log⁡N)(r≤M),|[Br]Djϵ[E]|≤NC∥E∥𝒥wN,r. \|B_r\|_{\mathcal J}\le N^Cw_{N,r},\qquad \log w_{N,r}=Ng(r/N)+O(\log N)\ (r\le M),\qquad |[B_r]D_j^\epsilon[E]|\le N^C\|E\|_{\mathcal J}w_{N,r}. \tag{2}

Here the last wN,rw_{N,r} is only the coefficient envelope: it is not a spectral weight on the unreduced input. The canonical degree-<N<N representative HH obeys

|H(2cos⁡(θ+iy))|≤NCeN|y|∥H∥𝒥. |H(2\cos(\theta+iy))|\le N^C e^{N|y|}\|H\|_{\mathcal J}. \tag{3}

The regular-index far-color estimate of the decorated-character theorem holds on every fixed compact interval I⊂(0,1/2)I\subset(0,1/2), including intervals away from 1/41/4. Precisely, suppose an actual representative ENE_N has

|EN(2cos⁡(θ+iy))|≤exp⁡N{c−cE(θ−π)2+CEy2+eN},0≤θ≤2π,|y|≤yE,cE≥0,eN→0. |E_N(2\cos(\theta+iy))| \le \exp N\{c-c_E(\theta-\pi)^2+C_Ey^2+e_N\}, \quad 0\le\theta\le2\pi,\ |y|\le y_E, \quad c_E\ge0,\quad e_N\to0. \tag{4}

The empty input 11 is allowed. For any fixed II and δ>0\delta>0, there is η>0\eta>0 such that, uniformly in physical colors and ϵ∈{0,±1}\epsilon\in\{0,\pm1\},

r/N∈I,N−j≥δN⇒|[Br]Djϵ[EN]|≤exp⁡N{c+g(r/N)−η}. r/N\in I,\quad N-j\ge\delta N \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |[B_r]D_j^\epsilon[E_N]| \le \exp N\{c+g(r/N)-\eta\}. \tag{5}

Proof. The original low Newton polynomial has logarithmic strip action

b(u,θ,y)=∫0ulog⁡|2cos⁡(2πv)−2cos⁡(θ+iy)|dv−2J(u). b(u,\theta,y)=\int_0^u \log|2\cos(2\pi v)-2\cos(\theta+iy)|\,dv-2J(u). \tag{6}

For u∈Iu\in I, circular rearrangement gives b(u,θ,0)<g(u)b(u,\theta,0)<g(u) away from θ=π\theta=\pi, uniformly on a closed angular complement. Near π\pi, with z=θ−π+iyz=\theta-\pi+iy, its holomorphic action is

ℬ(u,z)=−J(1/2−u+z/(2π))−J(1/2−u−z/(2π))−2J(u),ℬzz(u,0)=−tan⁡(πu)/(2π)<0. \mathcal B(u,z)=-J(1/2-u+z/(2\pi))-J(1/2-u-z/(2\pi))-2J(u), \qquad \mathcal B_{zz}(u,0)=-\tan(\pi u)/(2\pi)<0. \tag{7}

The compact interval stays away from both index endpoints. The uniform logarithmic-product upper estimate therefore gives constants cI>0c_I>0, CI,yI>0C_I,y_I>0 and eN′→0e'_N\to0 such that

|Br(2cos⁡(θ+iy))|≤wN,rexp⁡N{−cI(θ−π)2+CIy2+eN′}. |B_r(2\cos(\theta+iy))| \le w_{N,r}\exp N\{-c_I(\theta-\pi)^2+C_Iy^2+e'_N\}. \tag{8}

This uses truncated-log Riemann upper bounds, so moving numerator zeros are retained. Multiplying (8) by (4) supplies the exact root-character Fourier theorem with input BrENB_rE_N and reference c+g(r/N)c+g(r/N). Its missing folded winding, both joining currents, and the physical R normal are unchanged. That theorem proves (5). Fixed derivatives required there follow by Cauchy on radius 1/N1/N in a smaller fixed strip, with polynomial cost. For a fixed source the actual product representatives have degree O(N)O(N); no continuation of an arbitrary physical-color array is used.

A preexisting norm loss in ENE_N is even simpler: (2) propagates it uniformly at every output index, both low and high.

2. The proved coefficient-cone input

Let AA be one fixed original unflagged component, with effective type-LL inputs FiF_i, and set Q=T(A)Q=T(A). The inputs retain their actual Newton coefficient arrays, which may depend on arbitrarily many original physical colors. Assume their uniform envelopes are

|[Br]Fi|≤exp⁡N(ci+eN){wN,r,r≤M,1,r>M. |[B_r]F_i|\le \exp N(c_i+e_N) \begin{cases}w_{N,r},&r\le M,\\1,&r>M.\end{cases} \tag{9}

The original Newton multiplication constants are nonnegative; a turn is exact index reversal. Taking coefficientwise absolute values therefore supplies a positive state majorant without deleting any finite state or normal. For the canonical output Q=∑rqrBrQ=\sum_rq_rB_r, use the exact nonnegative probe

WN=∑r≤MwN,rBr+∑r>MBr,∑rwN,r|qr|≤ϕ(WNAabs). W_N=\sum_{r\le M}w_{N,r}B_r+\sum_{r>M}B_r, \qquad \sum_rw_{N,r}|q_r|\le\phi(W_N A^{\mathrm{abs}}). \tag{10}

Here AabsA^{\mathrm{abs}} means the positive circuit with absolute input arrays; it is not the absolute value of the output polynomial at a point. Equality holds with the nonnegative majorant output coefficients. In particular the original output reversal d−rd-r is retained in the probe extraction.

The independently proved cone theorem gives the following input. Set

Ω=∑ici+ΦC(hA), \Omega=\sum_i c_i+\Phi_{C(hA)}, \tag{11}

Here Φ\Phi is the original complete action with unit type-LL profiles gg; thus Ω\Omega is the intrinsic action ΩQ\Omega_Q. In (10):

  • the sum of every sector with a high input index OR a high probe index is at most exp⁡N(Ω−γ)\exp N(\Omega-\gamma), for some γ>0\gamma>0;
  • the all-low sector has the original positive action as a uniform closed upper envelope on its compact finite state polytope;
  • that upper-envelope action has one strict interior maximizer, of value Ω\Omega, whose probe index β\beta and all effective input indices αi\alpha_i belong to (0,1/2)(0,1/2). Every fixed closed complement of that maximizer has a strict gap.

The fixed finite number of sectors and polynomial number of original states are included in the uniform o(N)o(N) estimates. The effective modules need no independent physical alias identity: the cone proof uses (9), the exact positive gate and the earlier scalar supporting-plane certificates. It proves all these statements without assuming a signed mixed-tree source bound. In particular the upper-envelope action may have a strict maximizer even when the given input arrays vanish identically.

3. Canonical Gaussian localization at the actual pressure

Using the theorem in §2, uniformly over all original physical colors,

∥QN∥𝒥≤exp⁡N(Ω+o(1)), \|Q_N\|_{\mathcal J}\le\exp N(\Omega+o(1)), \tag{12}

and its canonical representative satisfies

|QN(2cos⁡(θ+iy))|≤exp⁡N{Ω−c(θ−π)2+Cy2+o(1)}(|y|≤y0) |Q_N(2\cos(\theta+iy))| \le\exp N\{\Omega-c(\theta-\pi)^2+Cy^2+o(1)\} \quad (|y|\le y_0) \tag{13}

with fixed c,C,y0>0c,C,y_0>0. This is the sharp canonical spectral envelope at ΩQ\Omega_Q. It does not add the uniform bare-turn cost 2G02G_0.

Proof. Equation (12) is (2) and (10). Choose a fixed compact interval II about β\beta strictly inside (0,1/2)(0,1/2). Decompose the actual state expansion of the canonical output as Q=Qgood+QbadQ=Q_{\mathrm{good}}+Q_{\mathrm{bad}}, where QgoodQ_{\mathrm{good}} contains only all-low states with r/N∈Ir/N\in I. The cone theorem and strict compactness bound the weighted coefficient mass of QbadQ_{\mathrm{bad}} by exp⁡N(Ω−γ1)\exp N(\Omega-\gamma_1). Hence its full-jet norm has that same gap. It is itself a canonical polynomial, including its high-index part, so (3) gives

|Qbad(2cos⁡(θ+iy))|≤exp⁡N{Ω−γ1+|y|+o(1)}. |Q_{\mathrm{bad}}(2\cos(\theta+iy))| \le\exp N\{\Omega-\gamma_1+|y|+o(1)\}. \tag{14}

For the good part apply (8) to every output BrB_r. The original positive probe already contains exactly its factor wN,rw_{N,r}, so summing costs no additional exponential weight:

|Qgood(2cos⁡(θ+iy))|≤exp⁡N{Ω−cI(θ−π)2+CIy2+o(1)}. |Q_{\mathrm{good}}(2\cos(\theta+iy))| \le\exp N\{\Omega-c_I(\theta-\pi)^2+C_Iy^2+o(1)\}. \tag{15}

Choose y0<γ1/4y_0<\gamma_1/4 and c≤cIc\le c_I with cπ2<γ1/4c\pi^2<\gamma_1/4. Then (14) fits below (13) with a fixed residual gap; (15) proves the remaining part. Only canonical output polynomials enter (3), while input products may remain unreduced. Thus no representative-dependent ellipse estimate has been applied to an arbitrary quotient element.

4. Physical-color deficits of effective L modules

Suppose an original physical color jj lies in input module ii. It suffices that for every fixed compact interval IiI_i about its actual clean index αi\alpha_i, and every fixed δ>0\delta>0, the module has a local coefficient loss

N−j≥δN,r/N∈Ii⇒|[Br]Fi|≤exp⁡N{ci+g(r/N)−ηi,δ},ηi,δ>0. N-j\ge\delta N,\quad r/N\in I_i \ \Longrightarrow\ |[B_r]F_i|\le \exp N\{c_i+g(r/N)-\eta_{i,\delta}\}, \qquad \eta_{i,\delta}>0. \tag{16}

The unconditional envelope (9) remains in force at other indices. Then the actual unflagged output has a full-jet deficit

N−j≥δN⇒∥QN∥𝒥≤exp⁡N(Ω−ηi,δ′),ηi,δ′>0. N-j\ge\delta N \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|Q_N\|_{\mathcal J}\le\exp N(\Omega-\eta'_{i,\delta}), \qquad \eta'_{i,\delta}>0. \tag{17}

Indeed contaminated sectors already have a fixed cone gap. In the clean sector outside the αi\alpha_i window, the unique original maximizer gives another fixed gap. Inside that window, replacing just the one input coefficient envelope by (16) lowers every positive state weight by its fixed exponential factor. Equations (2),(10) finish the proof. No bound on the variance of an arbitrary spectator, no pointwise sign control, and no independent alias provenance for the effective module is used. There are finitely many original colors in a fixed component, so their positive margins can be minimized.

For a genuine decorated module Fi=Djϵ[E]F_i=D_j^\epsilon[E], (9) follows from (2). Its own character satisfies (16) by the regular-index theorem (5). A physical-color deficit in EE giving a full-jet loss yields (16), indeed a uniform all-index loss, directly by (2). Thus descendant losses pass through a complete unflagged component at its actual intrinsic action. The local index αi\alpha_i can be above 1/41/4; no quarter-only estimate is used.

5. Mutual induction through the actual reduced source

The finite reduced-hub decomposition expresses each fixed mixed strict source, rooted at a flagged character, as alternating decorated type-LL modules and unflagged components of the kind covered by §2. The entirely unflagged case already has the completed source estimates. Start with the empty input 11. At a decorated module, multiply the actual child canonical representatives; their full-jet rates add, their angular Gaussian envelopes multiply, and a child deficit supplies a norm loss. Equations (2),(5) give its low/high coefficient envelopes and all regular-index physical-color losses. At an unflagged component, (12),(13),(17) give the output full-jet rate, fresh Gaussian localization, and every descendant deficit. Iterate over the fixed finite source. The classical rigid-spine identity identifies its decorated constant with the sum of its satellites’ actual Ω\Omega, so this induction never inserts an artificial character or rate budget.

For a closing original character, the existing exact Fourier theorem now supplies its own far-color loss and the norm bounds supply all other color losses. The complementary-color expansion can then restore the original all-core packet, as in the assembly. Those final source and marked-filling adapters are not re-proved here. The analytic induction uses the separately proved coefficient-cone theorem and the original reduced-hub typing; it does not assume a prior full mixed-tree source theorem.

This note does not promote a new global volume statement. All sources, physical signs, levels and finite targets remain original. The input and output constants may depend on the fixed finite component, and no claim is uniform over trees whose size grows with NN.

scope and preservation

The mutual induction uses exact normal-preserving probe weights and the canonical good/bad decomposition. It does not assume the signed mixed-source bound that it proves.

An arbitrary input can vanish. The propagation argument does not give a lower coefficient rate.
