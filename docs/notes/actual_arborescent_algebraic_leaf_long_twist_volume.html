Long twists in Conway tangles with an algebraic leaf certificate

31 October 2026. This research theorem enlarges the previously proved three-or-more-leaf seed grammar to include two-leaf seeds. Its additional hypothesis is a finite inequality on the selected complete geometric solution. The conclusion concerns the original invariant and the hyperbolic volume of the knot complement. It is not a theorem for all arborescent knots.

1. The precise class and conclusion

Let ℛ\mathcal R denote the literal quarter-turn of an ordered tangle, and let h=ℛ(LN)h=\mathcal R(L_N) be the original complete crossing-circle coupon. Use distinct leaves in each seed and form a fixed finite expression

As=hs,s≥2,E::=As∣ℛ(E)∣E1E2. A_s=h^s,\quad s\ge2,\qquad E::=A_s\mid\mathcal R(E)\mid E_1E_2. \tag{1}

Stacking and turning retain the original strand order, cups, caps, disk framings and scalar normalization. Close FGFG by the original cylindrical closure C(FG)C(FG), where both children belong to (1). The isolated closure C(A2)C(A_2) is excluded.

At the unique strict complete optimizer, let aℓa_\ell be the original leaf coordinate, and put Xℓ=cot⁡(πaℓ)>0X_\ell=\cot(\pi a_\ell)>0. Assume

Xℓ≥23at every leaf. X_\ell\ge\frac23 \quad\text{at every leaf}. \tag{2}

Choose a fixed retained half-twist pattern ϵℓ∈{0,1,−1}\epsilon_\ell\in\{0,1,-1\} whose endpoint pairings give one main component without internal circles. At each auxiliary crossing circle insert the independently chosen disk-framed full twist R−2pℓR^{-2p_\ell}. Thus, after all auxiliary circles have been filled, the actual leaf is

ℛ(Rϵℓ−2pℓ). \mathcal R(R^{\epsilon_\ell-2p_\ell}). \tag{3}

Theorem. For each fixed expression and such half-twist pattern there is a finite threshold T(E,ϵ)T(E,\epsilon) such that, whenever the fixed signed integers pℓp_\ell all satisfy |pℓ|>T(E,ϵ)|p_\ell|>T(E,\epsilon), the resulting knot KE,ϵ(p)K_{E,\epsilon}(p) is hyperbolic and

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(KE,ϵ(p))|=Vol⁡(S3\KE,ϵ(p)). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N} \log\left|K_N\bigl(K_{E,\epsilon}(p)\bigr)\right| =\operatorname{Vol}\bigl(S^3\setminus K_{E,\epsilon}(p)\bigr). \tag{4}

Here KNK_N is the original marked one-cut invariant with its recorded enhancement. The reduced enhanced Kashaev normalization has an outer factor of unit modulus; the full modified one-cut normalization differs by the recorded factor of modulus 1/N1/N. Neither changes (4). This is the comparison proved in the original normalization theorem and crossing calculation, not a new normalization chosen to force a volume limit.

The diagram and slopes are fixed before NN tends to infinity. Both parities and composite levels are included. Slopes may have different signs and unrelated magnitudes. There is no bound on the number of leaves, branching or turn depth, but the threshold is allowed to depend on the fixed expression. The knot cusp is never filled: the fillings in this construction remove only the auxiliary crossing-circle cusps.

2. Why the condition is finite and algebraic

The geometry selects real BB and positive X,YX,Y, with B=cot⁡(πb)B=\cot(\pi b) and X2+Y2−B2=1X^2+Y^2-B^2=1. For a two-leaf seed,

B=X−12X,Y=12X. B=X-\frac1{2X},\qquad Y=\frac1{2X}. \tag{5}

For a seed of width ss, the corresponding formulas are Bs=Ts(X)/Us−1(X)B_s=T_s(X)/U_{s-1}(X) and Ys=1/Us−1(X)Y_s=1/U_{s-1}(X), on the branch with the original positive product denominators. Products use the original rational port rules; turns send (B,X,Y)(B,X,Y) to (−B,Y,X)(-B,Y,X); the two root ports match as (B,X,Y)(B,X,Y) and (−B,X,Y)(-B,X,Y). Thus (2) is checked on the selected complete gluing branch by the polynomial inequalities

Xℓ>0,9Xℓ2≥4. X_\ell>0,\qquad 9X_\ell^2\ge4. \tag{6}

The branch signs and nonzero denominators must be retained. Squaring equations and testing an arbitrary root of their elimination ideal is not the criterion. No quantum limit must be computed to test (6). More precisely, keep intermediate seed ports and all branch inequalities when clearing the positive denominators. These are equations and inequalities over ℚ\mathbb Q, using rational tangent coordinates to record the strict angle branches if needed. The local equivalence with the critical-wire equations and their nonsingular Hessian makes the selected point isolated. An isolated point of a semialgebraic set over ℚ\mathbb Q has real-algebraic coordinates. Rational isolating boxes and the retained branch signs therefore turn (6) into an exact algebraic sign test. This does not require the entire complex solution variety to be zero-dimensional.

The complete geometry proves, for every proper seed of width ss,

cos⁡πs+1<Xℓ<1. \cos\frac{\pi}{s+1}<X_\ell<1. \tag{7}

Consequently every old proper seed with s≥3s\ge3 passes (2) automatically. Only the new two-leaf seeds require a test; by (5) it is equivalently Y2≤3/4Y_2\le3/4. The previously proved isolated closures C(As)C(A_s), s≥4s\ge4, also retain their original theorem.

There are actual passing expressions with the new seeds. For C(A2ℛ(A2))C(A_2\mathcal R(A_2)), both seed ports are (0,1/2,1/2)(0,1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2), which are fixed by the turn and match at the root. Their leaves have a=arctan⁡(2)/πa=\arctan(\sqrt2)/\pi, all retained gates are strict, and X2=1/2>4/9X^2=1/2>4/9. Strict concavity identifies this stationary solution with the complete optimizer. This is a genuine addition to the literal grammar; no topological novelty claim about its resulting knots is needed.

3. Source and geometric proof

The new issue in admitting A2A_2 is real: at even level its Newton support stops at N−2N-2, not N−1N-1. The proof does not restore that missing coefficient by changing the source. For any prescribed interior seed output bb, choose

b/2<a<min⁡(b,1/2). b/2<a<\min(b,1/2). \tag{8}

The two leaves and their product are then strict. This construction propagates through actual turns and products. At the closed root the only deleted gate has exact coefficient one and inputs summing to d=N−1d=N-1. Contract turns and solve that root relation integrally; all remaining wire coordinates form the original primitive lattice. Rounding a strict continuous state in these free coordinates gives admissible states at every sufficiently large level, including even levels. No relation 2r=d2r=d is introduced. The original uniform factorial estimates and polynomial state count therefore give the complete variational limit.

The boundary barrier and strict concavity give a unique interior optimizer and a negative definite reduced Hessian. The complete port inequality −Y<B<X-Y<B<X starts already at (5), propagates under the same operations, and proves (7). Every complete leaf lies in (1/4,1/2)(1/4,1/2). The unchanged local ideal cells, ordered face maps, closed bowtie cut, edge words and cusp decorations construct the actual complete augmented link complement. The same local shaded pairings handle every retained half-twist pattern. These arguments are written in the source extension and geometric extension.

What is new analytically is the quantitative alias estimate. Define

J(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt,g(a)=J(2a)−4J(a),k(u)=−2J(u/2). \begin{split} J(x)&=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt,\\ g(a)&=J(2a)-4J(a),\qquad k(u)=-2J(u/2). \end{split} \tag{9}

The exact leaf certificate proves, whenever 1/4≤a≤arctan⁡(3/2)/π1/4\le a\le\arctan(3/2)/\pi,

k(u)≤g(a)+g′(a)(u−a)−12500,0≤u≤1. k(u)\le g(a)+g'(a)(u-a)-\frac1{2500}, \qquad 0\le u\le1. \tag{10}

The core envelope has the same supporting plane without this loss, and equality only at the complete leaf. Add the original product supporting planes. All linear terms cancel by the actual closed-wire stationarity equations. Hence every nonempty alias subset has a strict loss, uniformly over its full physical range. The all-core majorant has the same unique complete maximum, giving the remaining physical-color complement gap. All original normal terms, endpoint offsets and both level parities are retained.

Inside a fixed strict complex neighborhood the old local coefficient interpolation applies, because its hypotheses are 1/4<a<1/21/4<a<1/2 and strict factorial arguments, not a seed-width condition. With HH leaves the primitive wire lattice has dimension 2H−32H-3. The leaf and gate powers together with its Gaussian sum give the same finite analytic packet

CNϵ(χ)=N3/2aϵ(χ)eNΦϵ(χ)(1+O(N−1)),aϵ(0)≠0. C_N^\epsilon(\chi) =N^{3/2}a_\epsilon(\chi)e^{N\Phi_\epsilon(\chi)} \bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr), \qquad a_\epsilon(0)\ne0. \tag{11}

The original integer shifts and normalization remain in this formula. Restoring the original source also restores its explicitly stripped unit phase ηN=exp⁡[(iπ(N−1)/4)∑ℓϵℓ]\eta_N=\exp[(i\pi(N-1)/4)\sum_\ell\epsilon_\ell]. Its color Hessian is diagonal at completeness, with entries −2πXℓ/(1−ϵℓiXℓ)-2\pi X_\ell/(1-\epsilon_\ell iX_\ell), whose real parts are negative. Equations (10)–(11), together with the whole complement gap, verify the hypotheses of the existing original-source filling transfer. It proves a nonzero full-sequence asymptotic for the original finite invariant, not only for a selected saddle contribution.

For completeness, the action selected by that transfer is

Sϵ,p(χ)=Φϵ(χ)+πi2∑ℓ(χℓ−1)2pℓ. S_{\epsilon,p}(\chi)=\Phi_\epsilon(\chi) +\frac{\pi i}{2}\sum_\ell\frac{(\chi_\ell-1)^2}{p_\ell}. \tag{12}

The marked geometric extension retains Uℓ=−2πiχℓU_\ell=-2\pi i\chi_\ell, Vℓ=−2∂ℓΦϵV_\ell=-2\partial_\ell\Phi_\epsilon, and the absolutely calibrated primitive G=2πiΦϵG=2\pi i\Phi_\epsilon. At its selected small saddle, (12) imposes the original primitive filling equations Uℓ+pℓVℓ=−2πiU_\ell+p_\ell V_\ell=-2\pi i. The complete-volume anchor and the original representation-volume differential give

2πℜSϵ,p(χ*)=Vol⁡(S3\KE,ϵ(p)). 2\pi\Re S_{\epsilon,p}(\chi_*) =\operatorname{Vol}\bigl(S^3\setminus K_{E,\epsilon}(p)\bigr). \tag{13}

The bounded marked branch completes to the intended smooth hyperbolic knot complement. Combining the full invariant asymptotic with (13) proves (4).

4. Arborescent scope and the remaining general problem

Each all-filled leaf (3) is a rational tangle. Actual stacking and quarter-turns preserve Conway algebraicity, so their prescribed one-component closure is an arborescent knot. The old loop-free endpoint induction also extends: a two-leaf seed realizes the two types I,UI,U, turns permute types, and products retain at least two feasible choices while avoiding the circle-producing pair. Thus every expression has a constructible knot parity pattern. Full twists preserve that endpoint pattern.

The strongest tangent version replaces (2) by aℓ<acrita_\ell<a_{\mathrm{crit}} at every leaf, where acrita_{\mathrm{crit}} is the unique root in (arctan⁡(3/2)/π,1/3)(\arctan(3/2)/\pi,1/3) of

g(a)+(1−a)g′(a)=0. g(a)+(1-a)g'(a)=0. \tag{14}

Condition (2) is the simpler algebraic sufficient test, with an explicit uniform margin. It is not automatic for every two-leaf tree. For example, the strict complete optimizer of C(A2ℛ(A4))C(A_2\mathcal R(A_4)) has two leaves with X=1/3X=1/\sqrt3, or a=1/3a=1/3; their tangent at the genuine alias endpoint u=1u=1 has negative deficit. This disproves an automatic application of this supporting-plane argument. It does not disprove the full source gap or the volume conjecture for that expression.

The next general problem is therefore concrete: control the original closed-source aliases when local tangent deficits are not individually positive, allowing compensation through the shared tangle constraints, or find a different estimate preserved under the actual contractions. The theorem above allows any finite number of suitably certified tangles and independently long twists; it does not remove the geometric certificate or cover every arborescent template. Nor does it construct a generic main-meridian quantum A-operator. The selected algebraic boundary branch, its logarithmic lifts and its calibrated period remain essential data beyond an elimination polynomial.
