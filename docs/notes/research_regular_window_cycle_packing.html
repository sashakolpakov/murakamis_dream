Fractional odd-cycle packing for the original regular-window source

This regular cycle-packing lemma has local hypotheses. It gives neither a covering statement nor equality of volume and source rates.

1. Original face phases and certified local losses

Keep one positive radius LfL_f and one phase ϑf\vartheta_f per original ordinary face, with the unchanged target exponent tf(N)t_f(N). For the original homogeneous source this is d=N−1d=N-1. Protected variables are specialized to zero as in that source.

Let JJ consist of selected original ports whose literal start/stop windows satisfy either the four-positive regular-window theorem or the one-protected-outer theorem. In the latter case exactly one outer weight is zero and the other three incident weights are positive. Write We,NW_{e,N} for the actual window polynomial and Ee,N(L)E_{e,N}(L) for its positive envelope. Every unused row, scalar zero and raw sign stays in these definitions. At other ports use certified phase-uniform majorants Me,N(L)M_{e,N}(L) for their actual factors.

Index the supported incidences at a port separately, even if they meet the same face. A protected zero incidence is omitted and has no phase variable. The required alignment has the form ϑf(a)=φe+πϵa(mod⁡2π),a incident to e,ϵa∈𝔽2, \vartheta_{f(a)}=\varphi_e+\pi\epsilon_a\pmod{2\pi}, \qquad a\text{ incident to }e,\quad \epsilon_a\in\mathbb F_2, \tag{1} with a free common phase φe\varphi_e. A straddling LEFT window has labels (0,0,1,0)(0,0,1,0) in the order (x,b,c,y)(x,b,c,y); full-row windows use the actual signs at their two endpoints. In a one-protected-outer window delete the zero incidence from this list; the remaining three labels are exactly those in its scalar-active theorem.

Let |⋅|𝕋|\cdot|_{\mathbb T} be circular distance modulo 2π2\pi, and set de(ϑ)=minφ∈𝕋maxa at e|ϑf(a)−φ−πϵa|𝕋. d_e(\vartheta)=\min_{\varphi\in\mathbb T} \max_{a\text{ at }e} |\vartheta_{f(a)}-\varphi-\pi\epsilon_a|_{\mathbb T}. \tag{2} The minimum exists by compactness. This is precisely distance from the local alignment in the product supremum metric. To compare with the relative-phase version of the local theorem, fix one incidence a0a_0 and put De=maxa at e|(ϑf(a)−πϵa)−(ϑf(a0)−πϵa0)|𝕋. D_e=\max_{a\text{ at }e} |(\vartheta_{f(a)}-\pi\epsilon_a) -(\vartheta_{f(a_0)}-\pi\epsilon_{a_0})|_{\mathbb T}. The circular triangle inequality and the choice of a0a_0 give de≤De≤2ded_e\le D_e\le2d_e. Hence, for each fixed η>0\eta>0, the local theorem supplies ce>0c_e>0, Ce≥1C_e\ge1, and NeN_e such that |We,N|≤Cee−ceNEe,N(L)if de≥η,|We,N|≤Ee,N(L)for every phase. \begin{aligned} |W_{e,N}|&\le C_e e^{-c_eN}E_{e,N}(L) &&\text{if }d_e\ge\eta,\\ |W_{e,N}|&\le E_{e,N}(L)&&\text{for every phase}. \end{aligned}\tag{3} The second inequality is the original positive-envelope bound. A zero common scalar makes both sides zero exactly; no local value is divided out.

2. Odd cycles force a collection of mismatched ports

Form the bipartite incidence multigraph with ordinary-face vertices, selected-port vertices, and the labeled incidences (1). Repeated edges remain distinct. A simple cycle here has no repeated vertices except its initial/final vertex; two distinct parallel edges are allowed to form a length-two cycle. Protected zero incidences are absent.

Choose a nonempty finite collection 𝒞\mathcal C of cycles for which the sum of the incidence labels is odd. Let rmaxr_{\max} be the largest number of port vertices on one of these cycles. Fix η=π4rmax,B(ϑ)={e∈J:de(ϑ)≥η}. \eta=\frac{\pi}{4r_{\max}},\qquad B(\vartheta)=\{e\in J:d_e(\vartheta)\ge\eta\}. \tag{4} Then B(ϑ)B(\vartheta) meets every cycle in 𝒞\mathcal C.

Indeed, choose a minimizing φe\varphi_e at each port on the cycle and subtract its two incidence equations. Upon summing around the cycle, the face phases telescope, while the odd label sum leaves π\pi modulo 2π2\pi. Applying the circular triangle inequality gives π≤2∑e on Cde(ϑ). \pi\le2\sum_{e\text{ on }C}d_e(\vartheta). \tag{5} If every port of CC were outside BB, the right side would be strictly below 2rmaxη=π/22r_{\max}\eta=\pi/2, a contradiction. This uses only the original incidence multigraph; no white-sphere topology is needed.

3. Fractional cycle capacities give an additive loss

Take every cec_e in (3) at the same threshold (4). Define κ𝒞=maxλC≥0{∑C∈𝒞λC:∑C∋eλC≤ce for all e∈J}. \kappa_{\mathcal C}=\max_{\lambda_C\ge0} \left\{\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}\lambda_C: \sum_{C\ni e}\lambda_C\le c_e \text{ for all }e\in J\right\}. \tag{6} This finite linear program has a finite attained positive value. Every cycle contains a port, so its variable is bounded by that port’s finite capacity. The feasible set is therefore compact. A sufficiently small positive weight on any single odd cycle proves positivity.

For any feasible packing, (5) implies ∑e∈B(ϑ)ce≥∑C∈𝒞λC|B(ϑ)∩C|≥∑C∈𝒞λC. \begin{aligned} \sum_{e\in B(\vartheta)}c_e &\ge\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}\lambda_C |B(\vartheta)\cap C|\\ &\ge\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}\lambda_C. \end{aligned}\tag{7} Consequently every phase point has total certified loss at least κ𝒞\kappa_{\mathcal C}. For a port-disjoint collection one obtains the simple lower bound κ𝒞≥∑C∈𝒞mine∈Cce. \kappa_{\mathcal C}\ge \sum_{C\in\mathcal C}\min_{e\in C}c_e. \tag{8} Overlapping cycles can improve the packing value; their phases are not assumed independent.

The capacities are analytic inputs, not automatically known numerical constants. If a different cycle collection changes rmaxr_{\max}, the threshold and its certified cec_e’s must be checked again. Alternatively one may fix the smaller threshold π/(4|J|)\pi/(4|J|) once and then compare cycle collections using that common set of capacities.

4. The unchanged Cauchy coefficient

At selected ports set Pe,N=We,NP_{e,N}=W_{e,N}. Multiply (3) over the bad ports, use the envelope bounds elsewhere, and then apply (7). Uniformly on the one original phase torus, for N≥max⁡eNeN\ge\max_eN_e, |∏ePe,N|≤Ce−κ𝒞N∏e∈JEe,N(L)∏e∉JMe,N(L),C=∏e∈JCe. \left|\prod_eP_{e,N}\right| \le C e^{-\kappa_{\mathcal C}N} \prod_{e\in J}E_{e,N}(L) \prod_{e\notin J}M_{e,N}(L), \qquad C=\prod_{e\in J}C_e. \tag{9} Use formal face variables zfz_f, distinct from their radii LfL_f. The original Cauchy formula gives |[∏fzftf(N)]∏ePe,N(z)|≤Ce−κ𝒞N∏e∈JEe,N(L)∏e∉JMe,N(L)∏fLftf(N). \boxed{\displaystyle \left|\left[\prod_f z_f^{t_f(N)}\right]\prod_eP_{e,N}(z)\right| \le C e^{-\kappa_{\mathcal C}N} \frac{\prod_{e\in J}E_{e,N}(L) \prod_{e\notin J}M_{e,N}(L)} {\prod_fL_f^{t_f(N)}}.} \tag{10} All protected specializations, source target exponents and scalar modes remain unchanged. There is one radius per original face, not one per incidence. Any external source character or prefactor retains its actual modulus separately; no such factor is silently discarded in (10).

5. A conditional strict source-rate certificate

Let BL=limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡∏e∈JEe,N(L)∏e∉JMe,N(L)∏fLftf(N), B_L=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log \frac{\prod_{e\in J}E_{e,N}(L) \prod_{e\notin J}M_{e,N}(L)} {\prod_fL_f^{t_f(N)}}, \tag{11} with log⁡0=−∞\log0=-\infty. Then (10) bounds the actual window coefficient’s normalized logarithmic limsup by BL−κ𝒞B_L-\kappa_{\mathcal C}. For a diagram with certified complete rate ΦD=Vol⁡(YD)/(2π)\Phi_D=\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)/(2\pi), BL−κ𝒞<ΦD, B_L-\kappa_{\mathcal C}<\Phi_D,\tag{12} is a sufficient strict-rate certificate for this original window piece. It strengthens merely recording that some positive phase loss exists. It does not establish (12) without quantitative envelope and local-loss inputs.

A polynomial-size family of pieces can be summed only with uniform estimates. Precisely, it suffices that the positive quotient for each piece pp be at most exp⁡{N(Bp+ωN)}\exp\{N(B_p+\omega_N)\}, where ωN→0\omega_N\to0 uniformly, that the multiplicative constants have uniformly subexponential growth, that the bounds apply from one common level onward, and that Bp−κp≤ΦD−εB_p-\kappa_p\le\Phi_D-\varepsilon with one ε>0\varepsilon>0. The polynomial number of pieces then costs only o(N)o(N) in the logarithm. Uncovered source configurations require their own estimates.

No claim is made that compatible phase systems are negligible, that arbitrary boundary windows satisfy (3), or that the packing capacities overcome every diagram’s envelope excess. This is a general fixed-graph criterion available at arbitrary fixed width, not universal diagram coverage or the volume conjecture.
