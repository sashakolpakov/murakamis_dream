Formal coefficients along the marked positive source component

Private written proof, 22 November 2026.

The geometric formal coefficients of the initialized contour source are finite rational Gaussian expressions. Their continuation needs less logarithmic data than the action. For any supplied positive unit-charge primitive target, the original positive tangent domain supplies a marked regular path and a one-complex-parameter neighborhood on which every coefficient continues. The leading coefficient is nonzero at the target. In this class the polynomial upper-bound criterion therefore has no additional formal-coefficient or leading-unit hypothesis to discharge.

No global upper estimate, quantum-cycle coefficient, or new filling class is proved. A positive lift of an arbitrary short hyperbolic filling is not assumed to exist.

2. Identification with the actual initialized coefficients

The initial identification retains the original polynomial source. At each discrete index the canonical halfwire satisfies BN(j/N,t)=ej(−2cos⁡t)(11) B_N(j/N,t)=e_j(-2\cos t) \qquad\text{(11)} as an ordinary polynomial identity. The original local finite-sum argument encloses the retained indices with the cotangent residue kernel. Its constant mode has coefficient exactly +N+N times the wire integral; its nonconstant modes, endpoint connectors and omitted index tails have a strict exponential gap. This is the finite-sum calculation in the regular low-source proof. Only that local calculation is used here, not its zero-power subtree theorem: at completeness the wire is x=1/4x=1/4, all normalized factorial arguments are 1/41/4, and the wire second derivative is −8π-8\pi. They retain their regularity and strict local gaps near the actual small-charge seed. The all-order factorial expansion therefore applies to that same finite-index calculation. The actual small-charge theorem estimates the remaining original rows and all mixed angular currents. The pattern-adapted all-plus family retains these estimates and removes just the factor 2−m2^{-m} from the central horizontal cosine.

Consequently its actual all-order seed coefficients come from (12) followed by the angular Gaussian expansion. Formal Gaussian integration in successive variable groups equals the full rule (10). One elementary verification is to test the quadratic Gaussian on elTξe^{l^T\xi}: completing the wire square gives the same inverse quadratic form and determinant as the block Schur formula. Expansion in ll proves equality for every polynomial moment, hence at every fixed Taylor order.

The powers are N2mN^{2m} from the angular traces, N−eN^{-e} from the paired halfwire factors and index prefactors, and N−d/2N^{-d/2} from the full Gaussian. Their product is NβN^\beta, where β=3(m−e)2.(13) \beta=\frac{3(m-e)}2. \qquad\text{(13)} This proves (5) with its actual constant and marks, not only up to a nonzero function. Both level parities have the same formal calculation; their exact rational trace denominators differ only in terms already exponentially small at the seed.

No global factorial substitution for the off-endpoint contour family has been made. Its ordinary polynomial numerator, frozen heights, removed rows and vertical currents remain the prescribed ones. Only the known seed coefficients have been identified and continued.

3. The positive tangent domain and its original marks

4. A one-parameter marked full critical curve

Choose the actual small positive-charge seed for these same fixed powers, and let w*w^* be any supplied target as just specified. Both points have all selected coordinates nonzero. Contract each along its radial arm into a sufficiently small product polydisk contained in 𝒫\mathcal P, stopping before zero. Connect the two contracted points coordinate by coordinate inside the corresponding punctured disks. The two radial arms and this connector give a compact continuous path in 𝒫∩(ℂ*)m\mathcal P\cap(\mathbb C^*)^m. Approximate it uniformly by a fixed Bernstein polynomial vector p(s)p(s) preserving its endpoints. Such approximation follows directly from uniform continuity: in the Bernstein average, the mass at distance at least δ\delta from ss is at most 1/(4Mδ2)1/(4M\delta^2), while the remaining oscillation is bounded by the path’s continuity modulus at δ\delta. Taking the fixed degree MM large keeps the image inside 𝒫∩(ℂ*)m\mathcal P\cap(\mathbb C^*)^m, since the compact path has positive distance from its complement. This degree is unrelated to the level NN.

By compactness a complex stadium DD around [0,1][0,1] can be chosen so that p(D)⊂𝒫∩(ℂ*)mp(D)\subset\mathcal P\cap(\mathbb C^*)^m. The adopted polynomial upper-bound criterion now applies if supK|N−βe−NS(s)𝒢N(s)|≤CKNBK(K⋐D),(23) \sup_K\left|N^{-\beta}e^{-NS(s)}\mathscr G_N(s)\right| \le C_KN^{B_K}\qquad(K\Subset D), \qquad\text{(23)} or if its local complex-area L2L^2 version holds. Estimate (23) has not been proved. The source in it is the same frozen ordinary-polynomial contour representative, with every original current retained.

No global one-phase asymptotic for individual exact factorials is inferred from their formal coefficients. No free cardinal end or reversed-index edge is assigned the phase (2). Outside data remain complete; a general incoming-boundary initializer is not supplied. Nor is a positive lift of every hyperbolic short filling established. For a knot conclusion the original source identification, peripheral marking and equality 2πℜS(1)=Vol2\pi\Re S(1)=\operatorname{Vol} must still be retained or proved.
