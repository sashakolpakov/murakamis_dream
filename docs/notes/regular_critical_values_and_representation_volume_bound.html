A sharp bound for every regular full critical value

28 October 2026. The regular full critical values of the actual marked knot phase, including its interior integer cut-wall marks, have imaginary part at most the hyperbolic volume in absolute value. The representation-volume estimate below is proved directly by averaging finite regular tetrahedra. It does not assume a volume conjecture, a positive triangulation for the other representation, or an efficient triangulation of the knot exterior.

This bounds critical VALUES, not yet the whole original period. The unresolved upper-bound issue is whether all remaining endpoint and escaping stationary strata of the selected relative cycle have such values, or cancel/group away by exact source identities.

1. A boundary-parabolic representation has an integrable Lipschitz development

Let M=Gamma^3 be an oriented complete finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold, and let rho:Gamma->PSL_2(C) be boundary-parabolic. This means each peripheral image consists of parabolic translations or the identity and has a common fixed point on the ideal sphere. No discreteness or irreducibility of rho is assumed.

There exists a rho-equivariant globally Lipschitz map

f:H^3 -> H^3

which is conical on each lifted cusp. To construct it, put a domain cusp in coordinates (w,y) in C times (Y,infinity), with peripheral translations w->w+lambda. Put its target fixed point at infinity. The target translations tau(rho(gamma)) determine a real-linear map A:C->C by A(lambda_gamma)=tau(rho(gamma)). Set

f(w,y)=(Aw+b,c y), c>0.                         (1)

This is peripheral-equivariant. In the metric (|dw|2+dy2)/y^2 its Lipschitz constant is bounded by max(1,||A||/c). Rank-one or zero A is allowed. Define these maps on disjoint lifted horoballs and extend rho-equivariantly over the compact truncated core. The target is contractible; a finite triangulation and geodesic interpolation extend the boundary map. A collar joins it to (1). Compactness of the quotient core gives a finite derivative bound there, so the resulting piecewise smooth map has one global Lipschitz constant L.

The signed integral

V(rho)=integral_M f^*(vol_H3)                   (2)

is absolutely convergent, since |f*vol|<=L3 vol_M. It is independent of these choices. On the truncated core use a rho-equivariant geodesic homotopy and Stokes. On a height-Y cusp torus, two horizontal derivatives of a conical homotopy are O(1/Y), and its homotopy derivative is bounded. The side volume is therefore O(Y^(-2)) and tends to zero. If the peripheral image is trivial, the affine cusp maps may be chosen independent of w. Changing their ideal fixed point then gives a side homotopy independent of both torus directions, whose three-dimensional volume is zero. These observations also cover different choices of affine cusp data.

The usual ideal straightened sum computes exactly (2). One direct verification is to truncate the finite ideal fundamental chain, straighten its developed tetrahedra, and use the same face-homotopy Stokes argument. Internal faces cancel. All tails point to the decorated peripheral fixed points and have volume tending to zero in cusp horospheres. A cusp-equivariant horospherical map differs from A w by a bounded periodic function; its conical homotopy to (1) has the same vanishing side estimate. Thus

V(rho)=sum_Delta oriented Vol(straight_rho Delta)
      =sum_Delta D(z_Delta),                   (3)

with repeated developed vertices contributing zero. Auxiliary interior vertices may also be idealized: approximate their chosen ideal flags by finite vertices, use the finite chain identity, and take the limit in its finitely many bounded tetrahedron volumes. This is the normalization used by the actual crossing fundamental cycle, not an independently chosen regulator constant.

2. An elementary universal tetrahedron bound

Every geodesic tetrahedron has absolute oriented volume at most v_3, the volume of the regular ideal tetrahedron. For a finite convex tetrahedron, put an interior point at the origin of the Klein ball and extend its four vertex rays to the ideal sphere. The origin is still in the convex hull of the four ideal endpoints: rescale its positive barycentric coefficients by the original vertex radii. Every original vertex consequently lies in the new ideal convex hull. The finite tetrahedron is contained in that ideal tetrahedron.

For an ideal tetrahedron with positive angles alpha,beta,gamma adding to pi, its volume is Lambda(alpha)+Lambda(beta)+Lambda(gamma). The cotangent/Cauchy inequality already proved in the charged-simplex calculation makes this strictly concave, so its maximum is the balanced regular value v_3. Degenerate and negatively oriented tetrahedra satisfy the same absolute bound by continuity and orientation reversal.

3. Average FINITE regular tetrahedra; the face errors cancel

Let G=Isom+(H3). The finite-volume quotient Gammaarries its normalized right-Haar probability measure mu. Its projection under g->g p, for any fixed p in H^3, is vol_M/Vol(M).

Choose a finite regular tetrahedron Delta_R. For example in the Klein ball take the four regular ideal vertex directions, each at radius tanh R. Let v_R=Vol(Delta_R). These tetrahedra increase to the regular ideal tetrahedron, so v_R increases to v_3.

First, Fubini and invariance give

integral_(Gamma\G) integral_(g Delta_R) f^*vol dmu(g)
              =[v_R/Vol(M)] V(rho).             (4)

This holds for any integrable 3-form: write it as h vol_M, average h(g p) for each p in Delta_R, and then integrate in p. The Lipschitz bound above justifies absolute Fubini.

Next replace f composed with g Delta_R by the straight tetrahedron whose four vertices are f(g v_i). Use a symmetric, face-compatible, isometry-equivariant straightening: in the hyperboloid model normalize the positive Lorentz-barycentric sum of the vertex vectors. Its Klein projection parametrizes their convex hull. For each face take the geodesic homotopy between its image under f and this straightening. Stokes gives

integral_(g Delta_R) f^*vol - c_rho(g)
      =sum_(i=0)^3 (-1)^i b_rho(g,face_i),       (5)

up to the one fixed homotopy orientation; the sign will not matter. Here c_rho(g) is the oriented volume of the straight tetrahedron.

The face prisms are uniformly bounded as g varies. The domain face has fixed diameter D_R and fixed parametrization. Its f-image and the convex hull of its three f-vertices lie in a ball of radius L D_R. The geodesic homotopy has length at most 2 L D_R and derivative bounds depending only on R,L. Its volume is at most a finite constant C(R,L). Thus every b_rho in (5) is Haar-integrable. No uniform bound as R tends to infinity is needed.

All FOUR ordered regular faces are congruent by orientation-preserving isometries of H^3. Naturality of the chosen parametrization and homotopy, followed by right-Haar invariance, therefore makes their averages equal. The alternating sum in (5) has two plus and two minus signs and averages to zero. Consequently

[v_R/Vol(M)] V(rho)=integral c_rho(g) dmu(g).     (6)

By Section 2, |c_rho(g)|<=v_3. Therefore

|V(rho)|<=Vol(M) v_3/v_R.

Letting R tend to infinity proves the SHARP bound

|V(rho)|<=Vol(M).                              (7)

The proof did not use an unproved equality between an ordinary measure-homology cycle and a fundamental class. Equations (4)-(6) are direct integrals; finite regular face congruence cancels the only straightening error. It also did not need a controlled norm for an exponentially conditioned quantum face frame.

4. Apply it to all regular FULL critical values of the actual period

Use the actual cotangent/fusion and coupled-cap equations from the full scalar period, and the exact regulator identity in the critical-wall calculation. At a regular full critical point Z, the actual crossing/cap equations give a framed representation rho of the knot group. At the complete meridian its peripheral image is boundary-parabolic. Indeed a noncentral parabolic meridian has parabolic centralizer; if the meridian is central in PSL_2, normal generation makes the entire representation trivial.

The actual crossing certificate is representation-independent: its finite oriented-chain proof uses the Wirtinger relations, unipotent meridian action, and generic auxiliary region flags, not the geometric embedding. Degenerate developed configurations are normalized to zero, as specified in the fundamental-cycle proof. Together with (3) it gives

sum_j epsilon_j D(A_j)=V(rho).                  (8)

For an interior ray/cut mark, the action has the retained integer form W_m=W+2pii sum_j m_j Log A_j. The proved identity is

Im W_m -sum_j epsilon_j D(A_j)
                    =Re Z dot Im(gradient W_m). (9)

At a FULL critical point the right side vanishes. The original real phase constants and scalar line are retained in the cited calibration. Equations (7)-(9) therefore prove

|Im W_m(Z_critical)|<=Vol(M)                    (10)

for every such regular full critical point, including nongeneric, nongeometric and integer-marked embeddings on that regular chart. No positivity of the individual developed tetrahedra is required. The oversized weak alias-face example is not a counterexample: a maximum on a constrained angle face need not solve the full critical equations of this actual knot representation.

5. Regular cap-endpoint stationary values are exactly zero

There is a further class of non-full source strata to which the same argument DOES extend. Use the exact coupled interval sources, and write their logarithmic variables as

p_c=2*pi*i*s_c.

Consider any stationary point of a regular stratum where one or more s_c are fixed at 0 or 1. Require stationarity in ALL remaining variables, including head, Gaussian and interior cap variables. The phase, its logarithmic marks and its physical canonical/Hurwitz maps must remain regular on this stratum. No assertion below applies to an infinite position or an undefined logarithmic shape chart. All ACTION coordinates, including auxiliary fusion positions, must be finite and regular; bounded physical meridian matrices alone do not supply this hypothesis.

In (9) the only potentially nonzero gradient entries are now those of endpoint p_c. But Re p_c=0 both at p_c=0 and at p_c=2pii. Their weighted contribution vanishes EXACTLY. Therefore

Im W_m=sum_j epsilon_j D(A_j)                  (11)

even though stationarity in these endpoint variables was not imposed. The missing equation partial_(p_c)W=0 would have required the paired position ratio to be one. It is not needed for the following argument.

Differentiate the actual source phase in its two adjacent head coordinates, using the input or covector orientation as appropriate. At complete color the two PHYSICAL momenta are

p_1=p_c, p_2=2*pi*i-p_c,
B_1=exp(p_c), B_2=exp(-p_c).                   (12)

This formula follows from the retained degree d=N-1, whose leading affine charge is one. It uses neither partial_(p_c)W=0 nor the paired position relation z_2=-z_1. At either endpoint, B_1=B_2=1. For finite nonzero physical positions, the actual complete generators

K=-B^(-1), f=(1-B)z, e=z^(-1)(B^(-1)-1)

give K=-1, e=f=0 and hence G=-I on both incident heads. Their based meridians are also central, so their paired cap product is I regardless of the position ratio.

The other stationary equations give the actual Hurwitz transport; the remaining interior caps give their ordinary inverse-meridian relations. Thus these data still define the knot representation. For a ONE-component knot, all oriented meridians are conjugate up to inverse. One central meridian makes every PSL_2 meridian the identity; the Wirtinger generators then make the entire PSL_2 representation trivial. Its volume is zero, as is its marked fundamental Bloch regulator. This last assertion can also be checked without choosing eigenflags of a central matrix: every physical Gauss meridian then has clocks A=B=1 and e=f=0, so C_+=C_-=1 in the original five-argument coefficient symbol. Every one of its Bloch–Wigner arguments is 1 and contributes zero, by the continuous normalization D(1)=0. The exact nine-to-five regulator identity gives the same zero in every regular continuation. Equations (8) and (11) prove

Im W_m=0                                       (13)

on every such regular cap-endpoint stationary stratum. The proof allows simultaneous endpoints and any number of remaining interior caps. In charts where centrality forces an atomic degeneration, there is no regular solution; no singular extension is being claimed. The ordinary-cap integrated source can still cancel exactly, as in K_N(Q^(-1))=0. This is a bound on stationary VALUES, not a claim that the corresponding integrated source coefficient is nonzero.

6. Exact critical degenerations are safe; arbitrary boundary strata are not covered

An EXACT sequence of full regular critical points obeys (10) pointwise even if its coordinates, frames or matrices escape. Every finite limit of its imaginary critical values still lies in [-Vol(M),Vol(M)]. The bounded continuous Bloch–Wigner function also extends to the degenerate shape values 0,1,infinity by zero, so exact critical degenerations do not create a larger regulator value. No compactness of the representation matrices is needed for this statement.

An approximate logarithmic gradient tending to zero at infinity is weaker. Equation (9) has the weight Re Z; its error need not vanish unless the weighted residual does. Moreover approximate primitive gluing equations at infinity need not approach the saturated closure of their exact representation scheme. Neither claim follows just from polynomiality or local Zariski rigidity.

A singular or escaping cap endpoint, a ray endpoint, or another critical point of a boundary restriction may have fewer equations than a full critical point. Outside Section 5 it cannot be declared a knot representation using (10). The Markov ray normalization example further shows why some equal-exponent boundary pieces must first be grouped by exact operator identities rather than assigned a strict gap individually.

Thus (10) closes the regular critical-VALUE part of the desired sharp upper bound. To conclude limsup N^(-1)log|K_N|<=Vol(M)/(2*pi), one still needs a source-preserving global stationary/compactification theorem placing every unpruned contributing asymptotic stratum under this bound, or cancelling/eliminating the remaining strata. The present proof does not assert that last theorem.

7. A whole-source quantitative cutoff at very deep zero ends

There is a separate global tail estimate directly from the EXACT scalar flow cycle. It applies to every fixed original word, before or after a valid normalization-cell pruning. Retain its external line chi_(beta,N) explicitly. Let T_N(delta) denote the sum of the ABSOLUTE integrals over all circle/ray components on which at least one radial coordinate lies in 0<r_j<delta. There are constants C,B,L depending on the fixed word and fixed compact cap paths, but not on N or delta, such that for 0<delta<=1/2,

T_N(delta)<=|chi_(beta,N)| C N^B exp(CN)
                             delta(1+|log delta|)^L. (14)

Here is the proof. The source-faithful LP radius bound is N^B exp(CN(1+sum|xi|)) product_radial r_j^(-d). The EXACT shifted full-Psi ray measure has modulus sqrt(1+r_j) r_j^(N-1)dr_j/(2*pi). Since d=N-1, the powers cancel exactly. With interval cap sources only a fixed power of 1+sum|log r_j|+sum|xi| remains. In particular this cancellation retains the affine +1; replacing d by N would lose the conclusion.

The full-Psi factors on the finitely many fixed outer circles have a coarse uniform bound exp(CN), with a possible harmless fixed power of N. One elementary justification uses the compact finite-product estimate (7b)-(7c) on two vertical logarithmic lines Re z=+/-L away from zero. It extends along those lines through the ends of a full turn, because their nonzero real parts keep the regularized summand away from its other poles. The exact real-axis moduli (10) and top-turn monodromy bound the two horizontal sides. The maximum principle on the intervening rectangle then gives the same exponential bound on every required circle. Lower-half lifts introduce only the retained factor 1-t^N, again at most exp(CN) on a fixed circle. Thus this is a bound for the actual full functions, not an assumed endpoint-uniform small-disk asymptotic.

The normalized Gaussian integral of exp(CN sum|xi|-N||xi||^2/2), with a fixed polynomial in |xi|, is bounded by exp(C’N) times a fixed polynomial in N. Finally

integral_0^delta(1+|log r|)^L dr
                       <=C_L delta(1+|log delta|)^L.

Integrate the other radial variables on (0,1], whose corresponding logarithmic moments are finite; any fixed exterior segments are absorbed in the exp(CN) constant using their retained radial powers. Take the finite union over j. This proves (14), including all original coupled sources and all matched relative components.

In particular, with delta_N=exp(-aN),

limsup N^(-1)log[T_N(delta_N)/|chi_(beta,N)|]
                                            <=C-a. (15)

Taking a sufficiently large fixed a makes this tail exponentially small at any prescribed finite scale. This does not prove the sharp upper bound: the retained region r_j>=exp(-aN) still permits logarithmic head coordinates of order N, and layers such as r~1/N remain. The same-volume normalization rays are compatible with (14). Neither fixed-domain compactness nor domination by the regular critical values follows from this cutoff alone.

8. Literature coverage only

The representation-volume inequality and stronger rigidity statements are established territory; see the abstract of Francaviglia, Hyperbolic volume of representations of fundamental groups of cusped 3-manifolds. The independently supplied argument above is not a novelty claim for that inequality. Its role here is to justify its application to the actual nine-atom period and its retained integer cut marks without assuming a published proof. No proof technique from that paper was used as an input to this derivation.
