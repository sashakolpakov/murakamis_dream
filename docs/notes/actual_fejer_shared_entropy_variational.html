The actual Fejér source: a general shared entropy theorem

Status update, 31 October 2026: the separate full endpoint theorem now proves the geometric upper bound (14), and the unique-maximizer theorem proves concentration at the complete configuration. The pattern-specific support theorem below is preserved; full-kernel support no longer requires pattern selection. The filled-knot quantum limit remains open.

31 October 2026. This concerns the positive endpoint numerator in the original homogeneous compiler, at even levels. It does not assert a quantum filling asymptotic or identify the new maximum with hyperbolic volume. All faces, incidences and marked targets are the actual ones. No independently optimized local norm is substituted.

Let N=2m, d=N-1, M=m-1. Fix a connected original flat plat D with R ports, R ordinary faces and the two original protected faces u,v. Write the ordered local faces at port e as (x_e,b_e,c_e,y_e), allowing repeated global labels through the literal incidence map. Let

P_e=-H_(V_N(-2)), V_N(sigma)=chi_N(sigma)(sigma-Omega)^(-1),
Z_(D,N)=[prod_(f ordinary)L_f^d] prod_e P_e |_(L_u=L_v=0).

The sign has made each local polynomial positive. The original homogeneous theorem makes this the same marked endpoint-numerator source, up to its recorded common sign, with no new powers of N. All statements below are about this source; it is not being renamed Kashaev’s invariant.

1. The local entropies, including boundary degrees

Define on 0<=mu<=nu<=1/2, 0<=xi<=nu-mu

E(mu,nu,xi)=inf_(l in R){integral_mu^nu g(t,l)dt-xi*l},
g(t,l)=l/2+log(tan(pi*t))
                 +asinh(tan(pi*t)*cosh(l/2)).          (1)

The integral is improper at 0 and 1/2, with integrable logarithmic singularities. If mu=nu then xi=0 and E=0. The closed-coefficient theorem proves continuity of E on this compact domain and uniform convergence of the logarithms of ALL actual half-block coefficients divided by N to E, including zero outer degrees and endpoint rows. Its proof uses the original continuant, negative-root coefficient factorization in strict interiors, and explicit monomer bounds at the endpoints. It does not infer coefficient asymptotics by differentiating a weighted limit. The multiplier 4/a_i has logarithm O(log N) uniformly.

The left type has normalized exponent vector and entropy

q_left=(xi,mu,1-nu,nu-mu-xi),   E_left=E(mu,nu,xi), (2)

in the order (x,b,c,y). Right paths are the actual row reflection, so

q_right=(xi,1-nu,mu,nu-mu-xi), E_right=E(mu,nu,xi). (3)

For a triangular maximal complete block, put

C(alpha_x,alpha_b,alpha_y)=E(alpha_b,1/2,alpha_x),
alpha_x+alpha_b+alpha_y=1/2, alpha_j>=0.            (4)

This is the ACTUAL triangular coefficient entropy. Indeed its path polynomial D_b=R_M^* is coefficientwise between R_M/sqrt(C_N) and R_M, with 1<=C_N<2, while the j=M term of P_left is exactly 2c^m R_M. The leading-coefficient normalization C_front is only polynomial in N. Thus (4) follows from the same closed half-block lemma, with its absolute normalization. Equivalently, using the already fixed triangle potential,

C(alpha)=V(2pi*alpha)/(2pi),
V(theta)=2sum_j[Lambda(theta_j/2)+Lambda((pi-theta_j)/2)],
sum_j theta_j=pi.                                 (5)

One can verify (5) by the source triangle gradient formula and its already normalized positive-radius Legendre duality. Formula (4) alone suffices for the theorem below.

A cross type uses two triangular vectors alpha,beta, each of sum1/2:

q_cross=(alpha_x+beta_x,alpha_b,beta_c,alpha_y+beta_y),
E_cross=C(alpha)+C(beta).                         (6)

When a local exponent vector q is fixed, maximize the right side of (6) over its allowed outer split. The exact identity B=4(x+y)D_b D_c and the coefficientwise comparison (N/2)H_M<=P_cross<=(3N/2)H_M prove this local coefficient limit. The forced x+y has degree1 and must be retained at finite N; its contribution to the limiting degree vector is zero. There are only O(N) outer splits. Boundary splits are included by the continuous closed lemma, not omitted.

2. Retain eventual feasibility before taking the scaled limit

At a finite even level the local supports are EXACTLY:

cross: nonnegative (k,i,t,l), sum=d, i,t<=m-1;
left:  nonnegative (k,i,t,l), sum=d, t>=m;
right: nonnegative (k,i,t,l), sum=d, i>=m.          (7)

Every allowed coefficient is positive. For left/right a path consisting entirely of one-steps supplies every outer monomial. For cross the two positive triangular polynomials and their forced x+y supply every vector in (7); their coefficientwise comparison transfers positivity to P_cross.

Fix one assignment s in {cross,left,right}^R. Its shared exponent arrays are bounded bipartite flows: a port supplies d, an ordinary face demands d, a protected face demands0, and each literal incidence is an arc. Impose the middle-arc bounds in (7), with all other bounds0<=q<=d. Multiple incidences remain separate arcs. All supplies, demands and bounds are integers affine in m. Subtract lower bounds and apply the elementary augmenting-path max-flow argument. Feasibility is equivalent to a finite set of cut inequalities, each of the form a*m+b>=0, together with the nonnegative residual supplies/demands. Therefore each assignment is either feasible at EVERY sufficiently large integer m, or infeasible at every sufficiently large m. There is no unproved arithmetic subsequence.

There is also a uniform explicit test level. Each residual port supply has constant term -1; each residual ordinary-face demand has constant term -1 and each protected demand has constant term0. Every residual incidence capacity has constant term -1. In a cut inequality

sum_(f in B) demand_f + sum_(e in A,f notin B) capacity_(e,f)
                                  -sum_(e in A) supply_e >=0,

the constant term therefore lies between -5R and R, since there are R ordinary faces and 4R incidence arcs. The other nonnegativity conditions have constant terms -1 or0. Every slope is an integer. Hence at m_=5R+1 all positive-slope inequalities hold and all negative-slope inequalities fail; the zero-slope conditions are unchanged. Consequently an assignment is eventually feasible IF AND ONLY IF its bounded integer flow is feasible at this ONE level m_. The same verdict then holds at every m>=m_*. This is a finite source-incidence test, not a numerical asymptotic heuristic; no entropy convexity is used.

Let A_D consist of the eventually feasible assignments. This is a finite, level-independent combinatorial test. It is essential to make this test before replacing m/N by1/2: a limiting inequality can be an equality while its original finite inequality is violated by1. In particular two overloads on one ordinary face are impossible because 2m>d, even though their limiting loads would sum to1. No protected face can be overloaded. The original gap-graph theorem then makes every noncross assignment in A_D a rooted forest, with its prescribed orientation. No arbitrary graph or re-marking is introduced.

An exact example shows why a terminal equality does not permit deleting one forest label. For the certified width-three word [1,3,0,2,4,1,3], the protected faces are 0,8 and the ordered port quartets are

(1,0,4,2), (2,0,5,3), (0,1,6,4), (4,2,7,5),
(5,3,8,0), (6,4,0,7), (7,5,0,8).

Take right types at ports4,6 (zero-based) and cross types elsewhere. For every m>=2, the following rows give an actual feasible integer flow:

(m,0,1,m-2), (m,0,0,m-1), (0,m-1,m-1,1),
(2m-3,1,1,0), (m-1,m,0,0), (m,0,0,m-1),
(m-1,m,0,0).

All ordinary targets equal 2m-1. Its scaled terminal point has nu_4=1/2, where the local right entropy and degree vector admit a cross description. Nevertheless changing ONLY port4 to cross is infeasible at every m: its protected c8,y0 force x5>=m since b3<=m-1, while the retained right port6 forces b5>=m. Face5 would receive at least 2m>2m-1. This is an obstruction to that relabeling step, not to the volume bound.

For s in A_D, let P_s be the closed limiting flow polytope: port supply1, ordinary demand1, protected demand0, cross middle upper bounds1/2, and one-sided middle lower bound1/2. On cross ports retain the triangular split in (6), or equivalently maximize over that split after fixing q.

3. Every limiting flow is approximated by actual integer source degrees

Here is an elementary argument avoiding any convexity hypothesis on E. Fix q in P_s. Clamp its entries to the finite-N bounds after division by N. This changes each entry by O(1/N), so the node-balance defects are O(1/N). Choose ANY feasible real finite-N flow, which exists by eventual feasibility. Its signed difference from the clamped flow is a directed residual flow. Decompose it into simple defect-to-defect paths and cycles. Discard cycles. Each retained edge adjustment is in the direction of the chosen feasible flow and has no larger magnitude, so it respects the finite bounds. The path part has the required divergences. Its total mass is O(1/N), and each simple path has length bounded by the FIXED incidence graph. Thus it repairs q by O(1/N) to a feasible finite-N flow. The estimate is uniform in q.

Multiply by N. Supplies, demands and bounds are integers. Round inside the floor/ceiling interval of each entry by cancelling fractional cycles in the bipartite graph. A node incident to a fractional edge cannot have fractional degree1 because its balance is integer. Hence a fractional cycle exists until all entries are integral. Alternating increments on that cycle preserve every balance; move until some entry is integral. Each step respects the original integer bounds. The final rounding error is at most1 in each entry. This constructs an actual source degree vector whose scaled value tends to q.

For cross ports choose the two triangular outer allocations and the one forced x/y factor after the incidence rounding. All local allocations are intervals with integer endpoints. Their rescaled endpoints converge to the endpoints of the limiting allocation interval, so any prescribed limiting split has an integer approximation, still respecting the SAME total q. This retains the finite forced factor even at zero outer degrees; the finite support has at least one outer unit.

Conversely every sequence of scaled feasible integer flows has a subsequence converging to P_s. These two statements supply precisely the lattice approximation needed below.

4. General shared variational theorem

Define the finite classical optimization problem

W_D=max_(s in A_D) max_(q in P_s)
               sum_e E_(s_e)(q_e),                (8)

with the triangular splitting variables of (6) retained. All domains are compact and all local entropies are continuous. Then, provided A_D is nonempty,

lim_(even N->infinity) N^(-1) log Z_(D,N)=W_D.      (9)

Proof. Expand the original positive product by the three disjoint local supports in (7), then by its incidence degrees. At cross ports the exact coefficientwise bounds B<=P_cross<=3B permit replacing P_cross by B=4(x+y)D_b D_c for this rate calculation: after the other positive factors and the SAME target extraction, the whole assignment is trapped between the replaced coefficient and 3^(number of cross ports) times it. Only this replaced coefficient is expanded into triangular splits; an exact factorization of P_cross itself is not asserted. The number of terms is polynomial in N because D is fixed. Every nonzero term has its logarithmic rate given by the uniform closed local coefficient theorems in section1. Compactness therefore bounds the limsup by (8). For the liminf choose a maximizer, use section3 to realize its actual integer degrees and splits at every sufficiently large even N, and retain that single positive term. Its rate tends to (8). There is no cancellation and no independent optimization of repeated faces.

If A_D is empty, the original positive coefficient is eventually zero; one can use W_D=-infinity. Under the existing labeled right-angled geometric certificate the all-cross term is nonzero and has rate

Phi_D=Vol(Y_D)/(2pi),

so W_D>=Phi_D. The theorem does NOT prove the reverse inequality.

5. The central forest corridor already has the geometric rate

Under the same labeled right-angled certificate, there is a genuine shared coefficient estimate near the central row. For a left path put k=M-j, so its overloaded exponent is m+k; for a right path use the reflected k=i-m. If all such k are at most delta*N, then the source formed by these terms together with the all-cross term obeys

Phi_D <= liminf_(even N) N^-1 log Z_(D,N,delta)
       <=limsup_(even N) N^-1 log Z_(D,N,delta)
       <=Phi_D+C_D*delta*(1+log(1/delta)).          (10)

In particular if every noncross excess is restricted to K_N=o(N), the restricted source INCLUDING all-cross has limit Phi_D. The bound is valid at arbitrary fixed width and keeps both protected targets.

Here is the exact transfer estimate behind this assertion. Put A_j=2+c_j, delta_N=A_M, and let R_j be the lower-block path numerator starting at j with all b powers retained. A positive path injection gives

R_(j+1)>=A_j*b*R_j, 0<=j<M.                        (11)

To prove it, raise every visited row by1. Both allowed sine step weights increase on this half interval. If U is the old unused set, the new unused set is {0} union(U+1), and its spectral-factor ratio is

A_0*product_(r in U)(A_(r+1)/A_r)>=A_j,

since all omitted ratios are at most1 and the full product telescopes. The outer exponents are unchanged and the b power increases by1. Iterating in the positive LAURENT coefficient order and using the literal half-block expansion gives

P_left<=2c^m R_M sum_(j=0)^M (delta_N/A_j)(c/b)^(M-j).

The paired-root comparison supplies R_M<=sqrt(C_N)D_b and D_c>=C_front*c^M, where

C_N=4(4-delta_N)/(delta_N*N^2),
C_front=N/sqrt(delta_N*(4-delta_N)),
H_M=8(x+y)D_bD_c/N.

Consequently the individual excess-k piece is bounded coefficientwise by

P_(left,k)<=a_(N,k)*[H_M/(x+y)]*c^(k+1)*b^(-k),
a_(N,k)=(4-delta_N)/(2N)*delta_N/A_(M-k)<=2/N.     (12)

The reflected formula interchanges b,c. This is an inequality for the original polynomials inside a finite Laurent ring; it does not replace a one-sided port by an identity coupon. In particular the remaining coefficient target changes by

gamma_e=(k_e+1)e_overloaded-k_e e_parent.          (13)

A protected parent may therefore acquire a POSITIVE remaining target. It must not be set to zero before extracting that shifted coefficient.

To obtain (10), take the original complete geometric curvature vector and replace its two zero marked entries by one epsilon>0, leaving all ordinary entries unchanged. This is ONE shared radius vector, fixed before N. At a certified port the outer matching pair cannot be the marked MAIN pair, so x+y stays bounded below; a fixed power of epsilon would also suffice here. The Laurent factors in (12) cost at most exp[C_DdeltaN*(1+log(1/epsilon))], with fixed extra constants. There are polynomially many k choices and finitely many assignments. Positive Cauchy applied to the original target thus bounds the entire restricted coefficient by the complete product at that shared vector times this exponential cost and a polynomial in N. No shifted marked target is discarded.

The complete capacity at these regularized radii is at most Phi_D+C_D*sqrt(epsilon). Indeed its log-radius derivative is the actual center angle divided by2pi. A single regularized zero corner has angle O(epsilon); two simultaneously regularized zero corners in a triangle have angles O(sqrt(epsilon)). Integrating from0 to epsilon and summing the finitely many triangles gives this modulus. The original target logs involve only the unchanged ordinary faces. All beta, forced-factor and other retained scalar logarithms are o(N).

Choose epsilon=delta^2, FIXED before N. This proves the upper estimate in (10); all-cross gives its lower estimate. For K_N=o(N), first let N tend to infinity at each fixed delta, then delta tend to zero. This argument does not use a downhill-radius condition. It shows that the unresolved positive-source maximum requires a genuinely macroscopic one-sided excess, rather than an omitted central endpoint window.

6. The geometric comparison and the remaining quantum assertion

The geometric problem isolated by this variational theorem was the inequality

W_D<=Vol(Y_D)/(2pi).                               (14)

This is a problem about the actual compact constrained entropy maximum, including every admissible forest and boundary stratum. It is not the unjustified interchange of inf over shared radii and max over local weighted branches. Indeed the fixed-degree coefficient entropy can be nonconvex in a terminal row, so that shortcut is unavailable.

At full interior critical points, the half-block equations have the parabolic Casimir energy sigma=-2 and the actual local action F(x,b,y)-F(x,c,y)+log c (or its reflected version). Developing these critical points and their boundary strata into actual augmented-group representations with the correct regulator proves (14) in the separate endpoint theorem; it is not assumed in (9). The positive endpoint identity (9) still does not select the original Gaussian filling cycle or its nonzero geometric coefficient. The fixed signed long-filled knot volume conjecture remains a further phase-sensitive assertion.

7. Verification and scope

The focused shared-source audit and results compare 228 original numerator coefficients, 4590 actual marked support patterns against bounded integer flows, 748302 affine-cut conditions, 118 explicit one-level certificates, and 236 eventual-flow checks. The width-three chart has 39 feasible path assignments at N=4 and 40 at N=6; the finite support distinction is retained. These checks support the exact source/flow interface, not a numerical proof of the limit.

The separate transfer audit and results check the exact path injection and Laurent coefficient comparison used in section5: 3045 injection coefficients, 679 paired-root comparisons, 39599 Laurent-transfer coefficients, 42 scalar identities and 42 conditional radius checks at 14 even levels and 90 digits. The conditional radius checks are not substituted for the shared regularization argument.

The full asymptotic theorem follows from sections 1–4 and the uniform local theorem. The later endpoint theorem evaluates the geometric maximum. This note alone does not settle the phase-sensitive filled-knot limit.

The finite source checks include the all-m terminal-relabel countercertificate and the width-three support distinction of 39 versus 40. They do not establish a geometric maximum or a quantum-cycle estimate.
