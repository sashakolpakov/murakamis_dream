Boundary orders of the actual charged gain arrangement

28 October 2026. The fixed-diagram charged polynomial has an additional restriction absent from arbitrary unit-root coefficient examples: it has a bounded number of root-INTERVAL blocks. Each block has distinct roots, even though its length grows with N. This bounds the order of every noncoordinate collision at a generic linear flat independently of N. A zero logarithmic residue at such a boundary can therefore involve only a bounded coordinate cut deficit.

The following statements concern actual fixed-clock binary products, before or after unit-factor-preserving fusions. They do not identify the full coupled clock phase or establish its volume bound.

1. Exact classification of the linear flats

Write the source, with extracted scalars retained in gamma, as

P=gamma product_e product_(r=0)^(L_e-1)
                   (X_(u_e)-a_e Q^r X_(v_e)),
f=P/product_i X_i^(q_i), sum_i q_i=sum_e L_e.

There are B block slots, bounded by the fixed original diagram, with L_e<=N and a_e in the actual 2Nth roots of unity. Loops are first evaluated as their exact scalar monomial and their source charge adjusted by the proved source identity. A zero scalar kills the state. Thus genuine remaining factors have two distinct endpoints. The coordinate hyperplanes X_i=0 are included in the arrangement.

Every intersection flat F has the following form. Some coordinates are identically zero; call their set Z. The others are partitioned into classes, with relations X_i=zeta_i T_class and |zeta_i|=1, where the T_class are independent. Indeed follow the gain equations X_i=g X_j along a connected component. A nontrivial cycle gain or one coordinate-zero equation forces that whole component to zero; otherwise path gains give precisely these proportional coordinates.

Put

m_Z=number of factors with both endpoints in Z,
e_Z=q(Z)-m_Z,
c_F=number of OTHER factor hyperplanes containing F.

Every one of these c_F factors joins a pair in the SAME nonzero class and has its particular matching gain zeta_i/zeta_j. Each root-interval block contributes AT MOST ONE such factor, since its Q-roots are distinct. Consequently

0<=c_F<=B.                                      (1)

If F has codimension c, then

c=|Z|+sum_nonzero_classes (class_size-1).          (2)

The common origin is omitted after the usual common-scale quotient; it does not supply an additional projective boundary divisor.

2. Its masterfunction and source-form orders are explicit

Blow up the linear flat F and take a generic point of its exceptional divisor, away from smaller intersections. Every linear factor whose hyperplane contains F has order one in the normal scale t; the other factors are units. A coordinate X_i vanishes there exactly when i is in Z. Therefore the EXACT logarithmic residue/order of f is

a_F=ord_F(f)=m_Z+c_F-q(Z)=c_F-e_Z.             (3)

In particular a_F=0 forces e_Z<=B. A macroscopic coordinate deficit cannot be neutralized by bounded noncoordinate multiplicity. If Z is empty, a proper arrangement flat has a_F=c_F>0, and f tends to zero rather than a finite nonzero value.

The actual coefficient form is

Omega=P dX_1...dX_k/product_i X_i^(q_i+1).

The normal blow-up Jacobian has order c-1. Its exact normal order is

ord_F(Omega)=a_F+c-|Z|-1.                      (4)

This counts the extra one in each coordinate pole; it must not be confused with only the phase order (3). At ZERO phase order, a noncoordinate flat c>|Z| produces NO pole of the original form. The only possible logarithmic source poles with zero phase order are pure coordinate flats: c=|Z|, c_F=0 and e_Z=0. These are the already proved exact tight-cut factorizations. Equation (4) does not declare stationary values of a holomorphic boundary extension irrelevant; it distinguishes those from an original residue pole.

For example (4) follows in explicit local coordinates: choose a basis of F and c normal vectors, put the normal displacement equal to t times a nonzero normal direction, and use its remaining c-1 direction ratios. The wedge Jacobian contributes t^(c-1), numerator factors contribute t^(m_Z+c_F), and coordinate denominators contribute t^(q(Z)+|Z|). This proves the formula with no quantum asymptotic.

3. Linear chart changes have polynomial conditioning

Normalize the factor and coordinate rows to

e_i-g e_j, g in mu_(2N), or e_i.

Every nonzero s-by-s minor A of such a matrix obeys

(2/N)^s <= |det A| <=2^s.                     (5)

Here s is bounded by the fixed number of anchors. To prove this, restrict each chosen row to the chosen columns. A two-entry row is a gain edge; a one-entry row is a unit ground row. Permuting connected components splits the minor. A nonzero square block must have equal numbers of rows and vertices. Each connected block is either a tree with one ground row, or an ungrounded unicycle. Leaf elimination gives a unit determinant in the first case and a unit times (1-holonomy) in the second. An unbalanced count or trivial cycle holonomy gives zero. All gains and holonomies are 2Nth roots of unity, whose nonzero differences from one have modulus at least 2 sin(pi/(2N))>=2/N. Multiplying component factors proves (5).

Thus inverse normal-coordinate matrices obtained from independent rows have entries at most a fixed-dimensional polynomial in N by Cramer’s rule. No uncontrolled exponential condition number is hidden in THIS linear arrangement-coordinate step. This is not yet a bound for a global Morse-flow Jacobian.

4. Mixed-sign monomial corners create no new finite critical value

Blowing up all linear intersection flats in increasing dimension gives local normal-crossing charts whose boundary divisors are labelled by the original flats. One can see this directly by induction: the intersection of two incomparable flat transforms has already been blown up, so only nested flat labels can meet; a basis adapted to that flag gives the successive normal ratios. Each arrangement factor is a monomial in these normal coordinates times a unit.

Accordingly a local masterfunction is

f=u(t,y) product_i t_i^(a_i),                  (6)

where u is a nonvanishing holomorphic unit and the a_i are the orders (3). If at least one VANISHING normal coordinate t_j has a_j!=0, choose a local holomorphic a_j-th root of u and change

t'_j=t_j u^(1/a_j).

The Jacobian at the boundary is nonzero and the coordinate divisors are preserved. This absorbs the unit EXACTLY: (6) becomes a monomial, not merely its first-order approximation.

Resolve its mixed-sign indeterminacy by the toric subdivision of the normal cone along a dot w=0. On an orbit mapping to a finite nonzero value, its cone sigma lies in this kernel. The nonzero character a therefore descends to a NONCONSTANT character of that orbit’s torus: it annihilates sigma but is not the zero character. Its derivative in a toric-ratio direction is nonzero. Thus a new zero-order exceptional divisor from cancellation of positive and negative weights is NOT a critical stratum over C*. No assumption that the weights have one sign is needed.

Possible finite nonzero boundary critical strata must instead meet a normal-crossing stratum where EVERY vanishing original-flat order is zero. By (1)–(3), every associated nonempty coordinate cut then has bounded deficit e_Z<=B. In particular the sufficient condition

e_Z>B for all nonempty proper coordinate subsets Z

excludes ALL nonzero finite boundary critical values of this fixed anchor masterfunction. This is a qualitative boundary-tameness statement. It still requires a separately controlled cycle construction to give a uniform quantum upper estimate.

5. What is left for the FULL phase

The existing cut-jet source theorem controls the normal coefficient cost of the bounded deficits isolated here. It retains the lower dimensional periods and does not bound them by a regulator. Similarly the gain-minor estimate controls linear charts, not arbitrary nonlinear transport. The polynomial logarithmic-mass lemma specifies what a controlled sublevel representative would accomplish.

There is also an N-dependent saturation issue: an O(1) normal order is O(1/N) in the normalized log action. Quantum critical points may approach such a divisor in a 1/N boundary layer even when the limiting interior critical equations lose a normal constraint. They cannot be declared points of the SATURATED classical critical closure without an additional actual source or connection argument. The known sharp critical-regulator bound covers that closure, not every restricted boundary stationary equation.

Finally all the preceding critical statements fix the integer clocks. Complex clock deformation changes the unit-gain interpretation and must retain the exact feedback and factorial interpolants. No bound for the complete knot scalar or its nonzero geometric coefficient is claimed from these anchor results alone.

Audit: actual source flats, complete gain-class/zero-class bookkeeping, bounded noncoordinate multiplicities, exact normal orders, and root-of-unity minor/conditioning bounds.
