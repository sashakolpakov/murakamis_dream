Polynomial structural costs in the uniform physical Conway metric

22 November 2026. Research note. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The full physical pair algebra has a positive metric in which the actual quarter-turn is unitary, multiplication has polynomial tensor norm, and both original closing rows have polynomial dual cost. Consequently the synthesis covariance of every fixed unfilled Conway tree has polynomial condition number. This uses the uniform balanced physical metric, rather than the normalized Newton coefficient metric or the binomial head metric.

Recompiling the known convex quartic in this metric permits constant structural budgets. Its leaf norms and its numerical minimum change. This is not a bound on the surviving projection of the actual leaf data or on its overlap with the original closing.

1. The full physical algebra and the chosen positive metric

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, and q=eπi/N,Q=q2,x=qd=−q−1.(1) q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad Q=q^2,\qquad x=q^d=-q^{-1}. \qquad\text{(1)} Declare the recorded balanced physical basis w0,…,wdw_0,\ldots,w_d orthonormal. This is the uniform head metric: ∥wa∥=1\|w_a\|=1, not (da)−1/2\binom da^{-1/2}. Give VN⊗VNV_N\otimes V_N its product metric and its endomorphisms the unnormalized Hilbert–Schmidt inner product ⟨A,B⟩ph=Tr⁡(A*B).(2) \langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{ph}}=\operatorname{Tr}(A^*B). \qquad\text{(2)} In particular the identity on the physical pair has norm NN.

The original balanced generators and the actual pair Casimir are Ewa=(Q−a−1)wa−1,Fwa=(1−Qa+1)wa+1,Kwa=xQ−awa, Ew_a=(Q^{-a}-1)w_{a-1},\quad Fw_a=(1-Q^{a+1})w_{a+1},\quad Kw_a=xQ^{-a}w_a, EΣ=E1K2+E2,FΣ=F1+K1−1F2,Ω=FΣEΣ+qK1K2+q−1K1−1K2−1.(3) E_\Sigma=E_1K_2+E_2,\quad F_\Sigma=F_1+K_1^{-1}F_2,\quad \Omega=F_\Sigma E_\Sigma+qK_1K_2+q^{-1}K_1^{-1}K_2^{-1}. \qquad\text{(3)} Missing extreme arrows are zero. The complete physical commutant theorem identifies 𝒜N=ℂ[Ω]≃ℂ[z]/PN(z),𝒜N⊂End⁡(VN⊗VN),PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2.(4) \begin{gathered} \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[\Omega]\simeq\mathbb C[z]/P_N(z), \qquad\mathcal A_N\subset\operatorname{End}(V_N\otimes V_N),\\ P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2 . \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(4)} faithfully, and dim⁡ℂ𝒜N=N\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal A_N=N. Here TNT_N is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. All first derivatives at double Casimir roots remain independent directions of this space, including at even and composite levels. Restriction of (2) to 𝒜N\mathcal A_N is positive definite. We do not replace (4) by its distinct-root value quotient.

All physical coefficients below are known before evaluating a closed source. One can choose a concrete Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal basis F1,…,FNF_1,\ldots,F_N of 𝒜N\mathcal A_N by Gram–Schmidt applied to I,Ω,…,ΩdI,\Omega,\ldots,\Omega^d, with positive normalizing square roots. These powers are independent by the full minimal polynomial (4). Their finite entries, Gram matrix, and resulting basis are algebraic at each level. No source value or chosen leaf packet enters this choice. No numerical conditioning of that basis construction is asserted.

2. Actual turns, products, and both original closures

The actual cup, ordinary cap, and unrestricted marked cap in the recorded all-primal chart are pab=Nx−b𝟏a+b=d,ℓab=xa𝟏a+b=d/N,mab=xb/N2.(5) p_{ab}=Nx^{-b}\mathbf1_{a+b=d},\qquad \ell_{ab}=x^a\mathbf1_{a+b=d}/N,\qquad m_{ab}=x^b/N^2. \qquad\text{(5)} Their fixed duality conventions and snakes are those of the source directs. In particular mp=1mp=1. The marked cap is not substituted for the ordinary cap inside a turn.

Let 𝒯\mathcal T be the genuine unscaled quarter-turn. On a whole physical Hopf box B∈𝒜NB\in\mathcal A_N, its exact formula is (𝒯B)(a,b);(c,e)=xa−eB(d−a,c);(b,d−e).(6) (\mathcal TB)_{(a,b);(c,e)} =x^{a-e}B_{(d-a,c);(b,d-e)}. \qquad\text{(6)} This formula uses the symmetry of actual Hopf boxes in the recorded chart; no all-array identification of different oriented bends is needed here. It is the original cup/cap contraction on 𝒜N\mathcal A_N, with their NN and 1/N1/N factors canceled exactly, not an additional crossing or a rescaling of the turn. It preserves 𝒜N\mathcal A_N and obeys 𝒯2=I\mathcal T^2=I.

The index map on the right of (6) permutes the entire four-index array, and |xa−e|=1|x^{a-e}|=1. Summing the absolute squares of all entries therefore proves ∥𝒯B∥ph=∥B∥ph,∥𝒯∥op=1,∥𝒯∥HS(𝒜N)=N.(7) \boxed{\quad \|\mathcal TB\|_{\mathrm{ph}}=\|B\|_{\mathrm{ph}},\qquad \|\mathcal T\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1,\qquad \|\mathcal T\|_{\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal A_N)}=\sqrt N . \quad} \qquad\text{(7)} The last norm is the coefficient-tensor norm of this linear map on the NN-dimensional positive space, not a norm of one box.

Retain the original two normalized closing rows λd(B)=ϕ(B)=1NTr⁡[(I⊗g)B],g=K1−N=(−1)dK,λ0(B)=mBp.(10) \lambda_d(B)=\phi(B) =\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}[(I\otimes g)B], \quad g=K^{1-N}=(-1)^dK, \qquad \lambda_0(B)=mBp . \qquad\text{(10)} The exact sewing identity, including the unrestricted mark, is λ0=ϕ∘𝒯on 𝒜N.(11) \lambda_0=\phi\circ\mathcal T \quad\text{on }\mathcal A_N . \qquad\text{(11)} It retains every Hermite channel. Since gg is diagonal unitary, the ambient functional ϕ\phi has Hilbert–Schmidt dual norm ∥I⊗g∥HS/N=1\|I\otimes g\|_{\mathrm{HS}}/N=1. Off this physical algebra the marked functional B↦mBpB\mapsto mBp has ambient dual norm N\sqrt N, since ∥p∥=N3/2\|p\|=N^{3/2} and ∥m∥*=N−1\|m\|_*=N^{-1}. The stronger bound in (12) uses the exact restricted identity (11). No ordinary cap has replaced the original mark on arbitrary arrays.

3. Polynomial covariance for every unfilled Conway tree

Let EE be a fixed finite expression whose leaves are independent copies of the entire space 𝒜N\mathcal A_N, with binary products and genuine turns. If there are ll leaf occurrences and pp products, then p=l−1p=l-1. The actual synthesis map is BE:𝒜N⊗l→𝒜N,Bleaf=I,B𝒯E=𝒯BE,BEF=M(BE⊗BF).(13) B_E:\mathcal A_N^{\otimes l}\longrightarrow\mathcal A_N, \quad B_{\mathrm{leaf}}=I,\quad B_{\mathcal TE}=\mathcal TB_E,\quad B_{EF}=M(B_E\otimes B_F). \qquad\text{(13)} All tensor products use the physical Hilbert metrics. The map depends on the unfilled expression, not on the eventual filling leaf values.

Theorem. There is an explicit linear right inverse SES_E, using only (13), the physical identity box, and turns, with BESE=I,∥BE∥op≤Np,∥SE∥op≤Np.(14) B_ES_E=I,\qquad \|B_E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le N^p,\qquad \|S_E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le N^p . \qquad\text{(14)} Consequently, for CE=BEBE*C_E=B_EB_E^*, N−2pI≤CE≤N2pI,cond⁡2(CE)≤N4p.(15) \boxed{\qquad N^{-2p}I\le C_E\le N^{2p}I,\qquad \operatorname{cond}_2(C_E)\le N^{4p}. \qquad} \qquad\text{(15)} For either original normalized row in (10), its squared dual cost in the synthesis quotient norm is aλ(E)=λCEλ*,N−2p−2≤aλ(E)≤N2p.(16) a_\lambda(E)=\lambda C_E\lambda^*,\qquad N^{-2p-2}\le a_\lambda(E)\le N^{2p}. \qquad\text{(16)} These bounds are uniform over the filling assignments at the leaves.

Proof. Equations (7) and (9) give ∥BE∥≤Np\|B_E\|\le N^p by induction. Define Sleaf=I,S𝒯E=SE𝒯,SEF=(SE⊗SF)ι,ι(y)=y⊗I.(17) S_{\mathrm{leaf}}=I,\qquad S_{\mathcal TE}=S_E\mathcal T,\qquad S_{EF}=(S_E\otimes S_F)\iota,\quad \iota(y)=y\otimes I . \qquad\text{(17)} The last II is the known physical identity box, not an identity depending on an unknown tangle value. Since M(y,I)=yM(y,I)=y and 𝒯2=I\mathcal T^2=I, induction gives BESE=IB_ES_E=I. Also ∥ι∥=∥I∥ph=N\|\iota\|=\|I\|_{\mathrm{ph}}=N, proving the right-inverse bound in (14).

For any yy, the identity BESE=IB_ES_E=I gives ∥y∥2=|⟨BE*y,SEy⟩|≤Np∥BE*y∥∥y∥. \|y\|^2 =|\langle B_E^*y,S_Ey\rangle| \le N^p\|B_E^*y\|\|y\|. Thus ∥BE*y∥≥N−p∥y∥\|B_E^*y\|\ge N^{-p}\|y\|. Together with the upper bound in (14), this proves (15). Applying (15) to the Riesz vector of λ\lambda and using (12) proves (16). ▫\square

For example the least-norm expanded lift is BE*CE−1yB_E^*C_E^{-1}y, and its squared norm is y*CE−1yy^*C_E^{-1}y. This follows directly by splitting any other lift into that vector and a vector in ker⁡BE\ker B_E. The latter is orthogonal to ran⁡BE*\operatorname{ran}B_E^*. Equation (15) therefore makes this quotient norm polynomially equivalent to the fixed physical norm. It does not identify a prescribed leaf tensor with its least-norm lift.

The exponent pp is fixed by the template. No bound independent of a growing tree size, and no filling-dependent cancellation estimate, is being deduced from (15).

Every leaf in (13)–(17) is still an independent copy of the full algebra. The right inverse is allowed to insert the identity into those slots. It need not respect a prescribed short filling at a leaf. Thus (15) does not give a lower singular-value bound for a partially specialized boundary channel after some incoming packets have been fixed. Such a channel can have a kernel or poor conditioning. For the filling application, keep all crossing blocks as variable leaf slots when constructing BEB_E, and only then insert their actual fixed or free filling packets as the input tensor vv. Their surviving projection and closing angle remain separate data.

5. What has and has not changed

The earlier fixed Newton coefficient metric makes the turn unitary and the two closing rows unit vectors, but its multiplication and unfilled synthesis covariance can have exponential cost. The present theorem changes the positive metric to the inherited uniform physical Hilbert–Schmidt metric. It proves polynomial structural costs in that metric, rather than contradicting those Newton-metric lower bounds.

In particular BphB_{\mathrm{ph}}, the normalized trial coordinates, and the raw minimum (20) are new. They are not asserted to agree with their counterparts in the earlier quartic. Both constructions retain the same original source and have their stated constant-factor comparisons to it. No uniformly conditioned change to Newton coordinates is needed to evaluate (20) from full physical matrices.

If a full physical matrix approximation to a true box is supplied, orthogonal projection onto 𝒜N\mathcal A_N cannot increase its Hilbert–Schmidt error. This retains all NN physical Hermite coordinates. It does not turn a one-coherent-input estimate into a whole-matrix estimate.

For actual leaf tensors v=⨂jAjv=\bigotimes_jA_j, there remains the projection onto (ker⁡BE)⟂(\ker B_E)^\perp and then the angle with the prescribed closing row. Neither (15) nor (16) bounds these quantities below. In particular a polynomially conditioned synthesis covariance does not make an arbitrary signed closed period a positive norm.

The physical metric here also differs from the binomial metric used in the small-activity full-norm Euler packet estimates. No estimate is transferred between those metrics without its actual cost. There is no new original-source lower bound, geometric trial field, or arbitrary-short-filling volume theorem in this note.
