The finite cut average: exact resonance, a source obstruction, and a leading criterion

Independent calculation, 27 October 2026. The Euler–Maclaurin operator is exact in its proper domain. Constancy of the geometric action and its nonzero principal density nevertheless do not, by themselves, determine the leading actual finite cut. This note gives a counterexample using the same canonical finite source, not a change of period functional, and an analytic criterion that would suffice to obtain its nonzero leading coefficient.

1. The exact averaging operator and its resonances

Write A=euA=e^u, D=∂u=A∂AD=\partial_u=A\partial_A, H=2πi/NH=2\pi i/N, and ℰN(D)=1N∑j=0N−1ejHD. \mathcal E_N(D)=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{jHD}. As an entire function of a scalar spectral variable λ\lambda, ℰN(λ)=e2πiλ−1N(eHλ−1), \mathcal E_N(\lambda) =\frac{e^{2\pi i\lambda}-1}{N(e^{H\lambda}-1)}, \tag{1.1} with all removable values retained. On polynomials in uu, DD is nilpotent, and the exact finite Taylor identity is ℰN(D)=HDeHD−1e2πiD−12πiD. \mathcal E_N(D)= \frac{HD}{e^{HD}-1}\, \frac{e^{2\pi iD}-1}{2\pi iD}. \tag{1.2} The second factor is the continuous logarithmic average.

On Laurent powers the multipliers are instead ℰN(Ak)={Ak,N∣k,0,N∤k,𝒞(Ak)=∫01(e2πitA)kdt={1,k=0,0,k≠0. \mathcal E_N(A^k)=\begin{cases}A^k,&N\mid k,\\0,&N\nmid k,\end{cases} \quad \mathcal C(A^k)=\int_0^1(e^{2\pi it}A)^kdt =\begin{cases}1,&k=0,\\0,&k\ne0.\end{cases} \tag{1.3} The 11 in the second display denotes its constant-function action. In particular 𝒞(AN)=0\mathcal C(A^N)=0 but ℰN(AN)=AN\mathcal E_N(A^N)=A^N. Therefore no linear operator BB on all Laurent polynomials satisfies ℰN=B𝒞\mathcal E_N=B\mathcal C. The meromorphic factors in (1.2) are not separately composable there: at kN≠0kN\ne0, one has the essential zero/pole cancellation in (1.1).

On the canonical band 1,A,…,AN−11,A,\ldots,A^{N-1}, the finite average at A=1A=1 and the logarithmic residue coincide exactly: ϕN(f)=1N∑a=0N−1f(QNa)=[A0]f=Res⁡A=0f(A)dAA. \boxed{\phi_N(f)=\frac1N\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}f(Q_N^a) =[A^0]f=\operatorname{Res}_{A=0}f(A)\frac{dA}{A}.} \tag{1.4} No asymptotic Euler correction is needed on this band. It does not follow that the Bernoulli factor is uniformly 1+O(H)1+O(H): at frequency N−1N-1, its modulus is of order NN.

In the whole normal order AN=UA^N=U, an unreduced ANA^N gives the canonical constant UU. Formula (1.4) must be applied after this carry. It cannot be applied to the unreduced ordinary Laurent expression and then used to delete UU.

2. A canonical, pole-free counterexample with the actual cut

For N≥3N\ge3, set fN(A)=1−AN−1−(1−A)N−1. \boxed{f_N(A)=1-A^{N-1}-(1-A)^{N-1}.} \tag{2.1} It is already a canonical polynomial of degree at most N−1N-1. It has fN(0)=fN(1)=0f_N(0)=f_N(1)=0. Consequently ϕN(fN)=0,ϕS,N(fN)=NϕN(fN)−fN(1)N−1=0, \boxed{\phi_N(f_N)=0,\qquad \phi_{S,N}(f_N)= \frac{N\phi_N(f_N)-f_N(1)}{N-1}=0,} \tag{2.2} for both the original finite source and the repaired source which deletes A=1A=1.

On every compact lens |A|≤r<1,|1−A|≤r<1, |A|\le r<1,\qquad |1-A|\le r<1, one has, for each fixed derivative order kk, ∂Ak(fN−1)=Ok(NkrN−1−k). \partial_A^k(f_N-1)=O_k(N^k r^{N-1-k}). Thus fNf_N has the normalized local asymptotic series identically 11, to every order in 1/N1/N, on a nonempty open chart. Its local action is zero and its principal amplitude is the nonzero constant 11. There are no poles.

At completeness the actual density is, up to its nonzero frame-independent torsion/base line, Ωcut=dt∧dlog⁡A. \Omega_{\mathrm{cut}}=dt\wedge d\log A. Multiplying it by fN(A)f_N(A) and taking the already marked relative interval t=0,1t=0,1, with zero endpoint co-sources, still gives the same nonzero local principal density to all orders. The relative transgression is exactly fNdlog⁡Af_N\,d\log A. Both finite cut pairings of it are nevertheless zero by (2.2).

The example also has formally closed local Ward jets. On a slightly smaller lens, QNAQ_NA stays in the same larger lens, so fN(QNA)−fN(A)f_N(Q_NA)-f_N(A) is exponentially small with every fixed derivative. Diagonal KK-commutation is exact, and the adjacent E,FE,F Ward differences are flat there. They are not exact global Ward identities.

This is not an actual knot state or a second coherent rational-QQ unfolding. It proves that canonical degree, absence of poles, exact source normalization, all local asymptotic jets, and formal residual Ward closure do not imply the desired source survival. It does not contradict scalarity of the actual complete cut, nor an exact global Ward/source chain map if one is constructed. Its failure is concrete: outside the local lens its norm can be exponential, and the global source sees the resulting cancellation.

3. A functional leading-source theorem

The following is a sufficient globalization statement whose hypotheses can be checked on functions rather than at individual levels.

Let 𝒟⊂ℂ×\mathcal D\subset\mathbb C^\times be connected and contain a nonempty geometric chart 𝒰\mathcal U and a circle Γρ={|A|=ρ}\Gamma_\rho=\{|A|=\rho\} around zero. Suppose:

  1. FNF_N is a canonical polynomial of degree <N<N.
  2. FNF_N is locally polynomially bounded on 𝒟\mathcal D: for each compact K⊂𝒟K\subset\mathcal D, sup⁡K|FN|≤CKNdK\sup_K|F_N|\le C_KN^{d_K}, with dKd_K independent of NN.
  3. On 𝒰\mathcal U, it has a complete uniform asymptotic expansion FN∼∑j≥0N−jFjF_N\sim\sum_{j\ge0}N^{-j}F_j, where every FjF_j is holomorphic on the same 𝒟\mathcal D.
  4. F0=c≠0F_0=c\ne0 is the geometric constant after the retained action, torsion and base-only lines have been factored out.

Then the expansion propagates to compact subsets of 𝒟\mathcal D, and ϕN(FN)=c+O(N−1). \boxed{\phi_N(F_N)=c+O(N^{-1}).} \tag{3.1}

Here is a direct proof of the propagation. On three concentric disks with radii r<ρ<Rr<\rho<R, the maximum principle on an annulus gives M(ρ)≤M(r)θM(R)1−θ,θ=log⁡(R/ρ)log⁡(R/r)>0. M(\rho)\le M(r)^\theta M(R)^{1-\theta},\quad \theta=\frac{\log(R/\rho)}{\log(R/r)}>0. It follows by applying the maximum principle to log⁡|f|\log|f| minus the radial harmonic interpolation of its two boundary bounds. A finite chain of overlapping disks therefore propagates an O(N−m)O(N^{-m}) local error to O(N−mθ*+d*)O(N^{-m\theta_*+d_*}), for fixed θ*>0,d*\theta_*>0,d_*.

Apply this to FN−∑j<mN−jFjF_N-\sum_{j<m}N^{-j}F_j. Its global bound has a polynomial exponent independent of mm, since each fixed truncation is bounded on a compact set. Choosing mm large proves any desired target order. Compactness gives uniformity on Γρ\Gamma_\rho. Equation (1.4) and the residue integral then give (3.1). This proof uses no asymptotic expansion of the Bernoulli operator.

The theorem also applies to a relative density with coefficients depending on tt, if these bounds are uniform on the marked interval and its endpoint co-sources and the coefficient functions extend on a common domain. The relative source then remains the full integral-minus-endpoints functional. For the root-deleted source, its additional term FN(1)/(N−1)F_N(1)/(N-1) must also be controlled or compensated by its retained Ward homotopy; deletion alone is not that estimate.

Alternatively, an exponentially small local error and a subexponential global bound suffice by the same disk argument. Local formal expansions without either global bound do not.

4. The deleted-root boundary layer has an explicit size test

Deleting A=1A=1 removes the point, not its neighboring sampled values. For a fixed meromorphic coefficient with pole order pp at 11, assume |r(A)|≤C|1−A|−p |r(A)|\le C|1-A|^{-p} on the sampled circle away from that point. The elementary bound |1−QNa|≥4min⁡(a,N−a)/N|1-Q_N^a|\ge4\min(a,N-a)/N gives 1N−1∑a=1N−1|r(QNa)|={O(1),p=0,O(log⁡N),p=1,O(Np−1),p>1. \frac1{N-1}\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}|r(Q_N^a)| =\begin{cases} O(1),&p=0,\\ O(\log N),&p=1,\\ O(N^{p-1}),&p>1. \end{cases} \tag{4.1} Thus an Hjr(A)H^j r(A) loop term need not be small after averaging if p≥j+1p\ge j+1. A proof of suitable pole-order bounds and a global remainder estimate could establish leading source control. Merely knowing that each loop coefficient is rational on the geometric chart does not give either bound.

5. Consequence for the proposed geometric source map

The constant action on the B1B_1-torsor and Ωgeom=nonzero constant⋅Ωcut\Omega_{\mathrm{geom}}=\text{nonzero constant}\cdot\Omega_{\mathrm{cut}} correctly identify the local candidate coefficient. The specified relative geometric interval would pair it to that nonzero constant. The exact finite averaging identity does not show that the actual canonical source is that relative class after transverse elimination.

The missing comparison must preserve the resonant/carry data and exclude the concrete cancellation in section 2. A global tempered function theorem of the form in section 3, or an exact source chain map with a nonzero leading projection, would do so. Neither follows from the present local principal-density identity or the formal residual Ward operators alone.
