The elevated wall source is not supplied by the finite divided-Ward orbit

Research proof and two exact tests, 27 October 2026. The binomial wall co-source has a literal realization on the degree-N symmetric power of the already proved divided-source standard module. That realization does not automatically carry the true divided Hopf coproduct. A finite-head weight bound rules out a full SL2-equivariant embedding at fixed bridge number and large N. The most immediate q-binomial elevation also fails a bounded diagonal comparison.

These are precise restrictions on proposed constructions, not a claim that a linear relative B1 current map is impossible. An explicit weighted stabilization shows why its second transfer coordinate needs an actual network map in addition to a cut orbit.

The later actual high-color construction evades the fixed-head dimension restriction explicitly. Integral color N(N+1) carries the required Sym^N divided multiplicity, its coherent row is exactly B_N, and its root knot scalar equals K_N with all braiding/peripheral signs retained. Its normal block arrows are actual regularized entries of E,F. This is a source enlargement, not a contradiction to the fixed-head nilpotence bound. Its full divided coproduct/current map and the geometric source projection remain separate requirements.

1. The actual wall row and its literal tensor-source realization

The cut-current theorem gives

B_N(J)=[1/(N2^N)]sum_(j=0)^N binom(N,j)J_j.             (1)

J here has the matrix normalization of equation (14a) in the cut-current note. Original raw Green arrays use N B_N instead; that coefficient is retained.

Use the actual two-dimensional divided-source subspace

L=span(Ubar,W), Ubar=(sum E_(j,j+1))/(N-1),
W=E_(N-1,0), Phi(Ubar)=Phi(W)=1/N.

On L the explicitly proved normalized divided Hopf adjoints are the standard SL2 matrices e,f,h. Let M_j be the sum of the distinct tensors with j copies of W and N-j copies of Ubar. Then the literal coupon row

ell=((N/2)Phi)^(tensor N)

satisfies ell(M_j)=binom(N,j)/2^N. Hence

B_N(J)=(1/N)ell(sum_j J_j M_j).                       (2)

This is an exact source pairing. The primitive tensor SL2 action induced from L makes their span Sym^N L, with a highest string of length N+1.

That primitive action is not automatically the true global divided Hopf adjoint on the tensor-source network. Its coproduct contains the mixed ordinary divided powers already computed in the actual fusion theorem. Equation (2) does not discard those terms, nor prove a monoidal Ward intertwiner.

2. A fixed-head equivariance obstruction

On H=V_N^(tensor b) retain the ordinary integer position grading. For a matrix unit indexed by an output tuple r and input tuple s, its matrix weight is sum r_i-sum s_i, in

-b(N-1)<=weight<=b(N-1).

The true E Hopf adjoint decreases this weight by one. Therefore its degree-N divided-normal operator J_E decreases it by N; normal division changes the coefficient, not the support degree. It follows exactly that

(J_E)^(2b)=0 on End(H).                               (3)

On Sym^N L, by contrast, e^(2b) is nonzero when N>=2b. Consequently there is no nonzero full-SL2-equivariant map of Sym^N L into this finite-head adjoint space intertwining its e with the actual normalized J_E when N>=2b. Indeed such a map is injective: the standard highest-weight polynomial module Sym^N L is irreducible in characteristic zero, as is seen by raising a nonzero highest weight term and then applying the lowering operator. Equation (3) contradicts injectivity.

The same argument applies to a proposed nonzero full-SL2 intertwiner into any subquotient with this same weight bound. It does not rule out a non-equivariant linear current map, a Borel-only quotient, an enlarged normal/tail complex, or a source involving a number of additional factors growing with N. It identifies the additional sector a full equivariant construction would have to supply.

3. The first divided q-binomial row has a different whole-head lattice

At Q=Q_N, all middle coefficients of {N choose j}_Q vanish. Their exact first divided-normal values are

d_(N,j)=[{N choose j}_Q/(1-Q^N)]_(Q=Q_N)
  =N/[(Q_N;Q_N)_j(Q_N;Q_N)_(N-j)]
  =(-1)^(j-1)Q_N^[-j(j-1)/2]/(1-Q_N^j), 0<j<N.        (4)

The proof cancels the single numerator factor 1-Q^N before specialization and then pairs the complementary root factors. In particular |d_(N,j)|=1/[2sin(pi j/N)].

If the degree-zero endpoint coefficients 1 and the degree-one middle coefficients (4) are put in the same trial source row, the result is (1,d_1,...,d_(N-1),1). This statement specifies the trial Rees identification: it does not identify its two normal degrees by an unproved map.

A diagonal adapter to the ordinary row (binom(N,0),...,binom(N,N)) has endpoint entry one and middle entries binom(N,j)/d_(N,j). Its condition number in every common diagonal metric is at least

2^(N+1)/(N+1), N even,
sqrt(3)2^N/(N+1), N odd>=3.                           (5)

Choose a central coefficient, which is at least 2^N/(N+1), and use 2sin(pi j/N)>=sqrt(3) (equal to 2 at even N). The endpoint-to-center ratio proves (5). Overall row normalization does not remove this condition number.

Thus the most immediate q-binomial/Frobenius elevation is not the bounded full-head Gamma unit already proved at source degree N-1. A higher normal extension or a different linear current map might still repair it. Simply replacing classical binomial weights by the first divided q-binomial weights would change the actual co-source.

4. An actual weighted stabilization does not give a second cut Weyl identity

There is a small universal test using the literal crossing, rather than an arbitrary array. Set N=2, q=i, Q=-1, M=1, x=i. Take a positive Markov stabilization, whose new crossing transfer is k, and attach a weight w^k. Its full root framing and pivot normalization has kappa_D=0. The two cut diagonal entries, computed directly from the actual finite coefficients, are

C_0(w)=1, C_1(w)=2w-1.                               (6)

For the second entry the routes are (i,j,k)=(1,0,1) and (1,1,0), with their complete normalized weights 2w and -1. The zeroth entry has the single route (0,0,0) with weight 1. Thus the original normalized cut gives exactly w; at w=1 this is the correct identity tangle after the twist correction.

For a preceding scalar knot cut F I, this local operation gives F times (6). Therefore the transfer distribution after the normalized cut is (0,F). Its unnormalized transfer sum is F. Encoding that sum by a two-dimensional auxiliary diagonal source means the matrix diag(0,2F). The two hemisphere co-sources of that transfer source are then

lambda_2^+=F/4, lambda_2^-=3F/4.                     (7)

They are not the two equal cut values F/2. This is an exact universal local stabilization test, not a knot-by-knot argument. It shows that applying a second auxiliary Bernstein reflection to a transfer index is not already the actual cut Weyl identity. The original unweighted invariant remains unchanged.

This does not rule out an adapted second framing coordinate on a hyperbolic geometric chart: the new stabilization transfer need not be etale on that critical framing torsor. It proves that such an identification must use its actual network and current equations; it cannot follow from the one-cut source orbit or its two Weyl charts alone.

The concrete next map must therefore transport the degree-N wall row (1), the two actual cut endpoints, and the second network framing current together, possibly in a normal/tail extension. The finite cut-current chain map is already explicit. No selected geometric cycle, global microstalk or volume limit is asserted here.

Verification is recorded in audit_degree_elevated_wall_source.py and its results. The coupon and stabilization tests are exact rational-complex matrix calculations. The divided q-row formula and conditioning bound are checked at 80-digit precision for N=2 through 32; their general proof is the finite product cancellation (4). No kernel of the full knot network or selected volume limit is inferred from these tests.
