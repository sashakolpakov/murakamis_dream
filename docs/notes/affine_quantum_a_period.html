The inhomogeneous quantum-A period is an affine difference torsor

Research note, 26 October 2026. A homogeneous norm line alone does not record how a knot period is sourced. A literature-defined inhomogeneous A-equation gives a more structured object: an affine extension of its geometric difference module by a trivial line. Its class survives finite algebraic covers unless it was already split. At a root, a finite cyclic resolvent reconstructs its period. These statements do not prove that every knot has the required inhomogeneous factor or identify its extension class with the classical regulator.

Inhomogeneous noncommutative A-polynomials are existing definitions; see Section 1.5, equation (2), of Garoufalidis–Sun, 0802.4074v2. No creative-telescoping proof or knot operator from that paper is being assumed. The homogeneous operator and actual state remain those of the all-level formulation. No minimal-order claim is made for a pair extracted from a given homogeneous operator. Its source is fixed by the actual finite color pointing, rather than selected to change the period.

1. The affine extension class

Over F=C(q,M), sigma(M)=qM, take a specified pair

A y=b, A=sum_(i=0)^d a_i(q,M)T^i, b in F,
T y=sigma(y), a_0*a_d!=0.                       (1.1)

Its column companion equation is

sigma(v)=C v+c,
c=e_d*b/a_d,
sigma((v,1)^t)=U*(v,1)^t,
U=[C c; 0 1].                                  (1.2)

C here is the forward solution companion (superdiagonal one and last row -a_i/a_d). This is the transpose-dual convention to the cyclic-module matrix used in the intrinsic norm-torsor note.

Translation v -> v+t changes the source by

c -> c+sigma(t)-C t.

Thus the source defines the additive class

[c] in F^d/(sigma-C)F^d.                        (1.3)

It is zero exactly when (1.2) has a rational particular solution. The inhomogeneous solution set, when nonempty, is an affine torsor under the vector space of homogeneous solutions. This linear and affine structure is proved by subtraction of equations, not by a nonlinear test on scalar sections.

More precisely, (1.3) measures a horizontal point of a trivial affine bundle with semilinear transport. The underlying affine space has ordinary F-points; nonsplitting concerns the horizontal equation. This qualification is essential when comparing the difference extension to the ordinary algebraic norm torsor at a fixed root.

The class is intrinsic under changes v -> Gv+t with G invertible: the augmented matrices change by the usual difference gauge U -> G_aug(qM) U G_aug(M)^(-1). One must transport the period functional and its actual meridian lattice along with that gauge.

2. Finite algebraic covers do not kill a nonzero affine class

Let L/F be finite in characteristic zero, with sigma extending to an automorphism of L. If t in L^d solves sigma(t)-C t=c, then

t_0=Tr_(L/F)(t)/[L:F]

belongs to F^d and solves the same equation. Indeed trace is semilinearly natural, Tr(sigma(t))=sigma(Tr(t)), and C,c are over F. Consequently pullback on the classes (1.3) is injective.

This is the relevant finite-cover descent statement. It differs from multiplicative Hilbert 90 on a fixed finite cyclic extension. A nonsplit generic source cannot be made into a rational particular solution merely by replacing F with a finite sigma-stable algebraic cover. The qualification sigma-stable is necessary.

The subsequent common period-space note, Section 6 proves an additional injectivity statement for the rational Frobenius enlargement. If sigma(w)=-w, extraction of the Laurent w^0 coefficient commutes with sigma-C and retracts the enlarged source quotient onto the original one. Thus a nonsplit source defined over F remains nonsplit over F(w), also after finite stable algebraic covers. This does not concern unrestricted formal or analytic period extensions. Its Section 7 also bounds the presentation degrees needed by exact separate root splittings.

Formal splitting is a different operation

Put q=exp(h), delta=M*d/dM, and assume C,c are formal meromorphic in h with coefficients in C(M), with C regular in h. If det(I-C_0)!=0 in C(M), the source has a unique formal particular solution in C(M)((h)). At order h^k, its coefficient is determined by

(I-C_0)v_k=c_k-sum_(ell>=1)
               [(delta^ell/ell!)-C_ell]v_(k-ell). (2.1)

Only previously computed coefficients occur on the right. The sum is finite at each order, starting at the lowest h-order of c. This proves the assertion by recursion. In scalar companion form, the condition is A_0(M,1)!=0 generically; it does not require nonvanishing at a special boundary point.

Thus an extension can be generically nonsplit, remain nonsplit on every finite stable algebraic cover, and nevertheless split after formal completion. That completion is not a finite cover. Its formal splitting is not automatically an actual selected period or a convergent section. For the nonsplit example in Section 6, its coefficients begin

v_0=1/(1-M), v_1=-M/(1-M)^3,
v_2=M*(1+4M+M^2)/(2*(1-M)^5).

They have boundary poles at M=1, while the exact root period there is the regular finite expression (6.3). Evaluating the coefficients separately at that point would lose the period. This locates the algebraization and boundary comparison that remains after formal branch lifting; it does not identify it with the Bloch regulator.

3. Exact affine descent at a root of unity

Now let q have order n, E=C(M), K=C(u), u=M^n. Put C_j=C(q^j M), c_j=c(q^j M), and form the forward monodromy

H=C_(n-1)...C_0,
s=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) C_(n-1)...C_(j+1)c_j.

Empty matrix products are identity. The augmented monodromy is

U_(n-1)...U_0=[H s; 0 1].                       (3.1)

A rational solution necessarily obeys

(I-H)v=s.                                      (3.2)

Conversely consistency of this ordinary E-linear equation suffices. Define the affine semilinear map

Phi(v)=sigma^(-1)(C v+c).

Its n-th iterate is H v+s. On the nonempty affine solution space W of (3.2), Phi^n is identity and Phi preserves W. For any v_0 in W the affine average

v=(1/n)sum_(j=0)^(n-1) Phi^j(v_0)               (3.3)

is fixed by Phi and hence solves (1.2). Affine averaging is valid because its coefficients sum to one. This is an explicit additive cyclic descent argument for any level, including even and composite.

In the nonresonant case det(I-H)!=0, there is a unique rational particular solution and no average is needed:

v_N=(I-H)^(-1)s.                               (3.4)

Each component is a Cramer quotient. Apparent zeros of its denominator must be canceled in the whole meridian germ. The solution transfer monodromy H is the transpose of the corresponding cyclic-module monodromy, so this uses the same central spectrum as the norm-torsor formulation, with the source retained.

If (3.2) is inconsistent there is no rational period. If it is consistent and resonant, (3.3) supplies one; the others form an affine torsor under the homogeneous rational solutions. One must keep their actual lattice and pointing.

There is no conflict between a nonsplit generic extension and rational splittings at individual roots. Specialization changes the order of sigma and its constant field. The finite inverses (3.4) can exist at every level without forming a fixed algebraic section in (q,M); the adequacy theorem excludes just such a section for the actual traces. This is information forgotten by performing Hilbert 90 separately at unrelated levels.

The subsequent actual threaded-character calculation gives another exact selection criterion. A null-homologous 2N-threaded loop acts on the actual period and its polar subtraction by two. If an actual extension carries a compatible observable X=[G d_gamma;0 2] with 2-G invertible, its unique source-one eigenvector is ((2-G)^(-1)d_gamma,1); commutation with difference transport makes this vector horizontal. A complete hyperbolic commutator supplies a classical scalar gap g_gamma!=2. The actual relative extension, observable and off-diagonal coefficient are still unconstructed. For a nonsplit generic source no such fixed generic separating endomorphism can exist, since it would split the class; level-dependent degree-2N observables are different data.

4. A simple algebraic criterion for nonresonance

There is no need to compute a growing monodromy determinant when the operator has a unique extremal coefficient. Suppose, as Laurent functions of M, exactly one a_i has the strictly smallest order at M=0. The same criterion works with the strictly largest degree at infinity. Generic uniqueness persists at q_N for all but finitely many levels.

For a nonzero rational homogeneous solution y with order k at zero, each sigma^i(y) has the same order k. The terms a_i sigma^i(y) would have a unique least order and could not sum to zero. Thus there is no rational homogeneous solution at these levels.

This implies det(I-H)!=0. Here is a direct descent justification: if ker(I-H) were nonzero, the homogeneous semilinear Phi above has Phi^n=1 on that kernel. For nonzero w in it, some average sum_j Phi^j(fw) with f in E is nonzero. Otherwise the distinct field automorphisms sigma^(-j), applied to all f, would satisfy a nontrivial E-linear relation. Their independence follows by taking a relation with fewest terms and subtracting its value at af from one coefficient times its value at f. A choice of a distinguishing two automorphisms shortens the relation, a contradiction. The nonzero average would give a homogeneous rational companion solution, contradicting the valuation argument.

This criterion is uniform in n and concerns the actual quantum coefficients. A classical extremal pattern alone does not prove it: coefficients vanishing at q=1 can change the M-extremum at every nonzero quantum parameter.

5. Returning to the actual all-level period

Suppose a specified actual all-level reduced annihilator factors as

P=(T-sigma(b)/b)A, b!=0,                        (5.1)

and its generic color traces satisfy A C=b. More generally, after such a factorization, A C_j/b(q,-q^j) is independent of j. Its value kappa(q) is computed from finitely many fixed colors. If kappa!=0, replace b by the exact source kappa(q)b; the first factor is unchanged. No root-dependent source normalization is chosen.

At a root, (5.1) implies

A F_N=c_N(u)b, c_N in C(u).                     (5.2)

Choose a fixed integer color J where b and the coefficients are regular and b(q,-q^J) is not identically zero; a sufficiently large fixed J avoids the finitely many exceptional trace identities. For all large N, the quotient-color identity and A C=b give c_N(1)=1. Its germ is regular there, since b is nonzero at that reference color and A F_N is regular there.

If the geometric block A is nonresonant, (3.4) is the unique inhomogeneous root solution h_N. Equation (5.2) gives

F_N=c_N(u)h_N, <K>_N=[h_N(M)]_(M=1).            (5.3)

The reference pointing has fixed the central unit exactly on this fiber. Its unit property also proves that h_N has a regular whole germ there. Formula (3.4), or its Cramer quotients, is therefore an exact source-normalized A-period formula. A rational homogeneous left factor and the nonresonance criterion are extra properties to prove for the chosen literature operator, not new definitions of a favorable quantization.

The full ADO factor remains eta_N*(M-M-1)*MN/(u-1), with its residue and first jet as in the meridian note. It is not erased from (5.3) or replaced by its numerical value before the whole-germ comparison.

6. Adequacy forces this extension to be nonsplit

Under the actual-source hypotheses of Section 5, suppose A is nonresonant at infinitely many levels (the extremal criterion is a sufficient condition). If [c]=0 generically, a fixed rational particular solution h(q,M) exists. At every nonresonant level uniqueness gives h_N=h(q_N,M), so for every sufficiently large fixed j,

C_j(q_N)=h(q_N,-q_N^j)

at infinitely many distinct q_N. Polynomial rigidity makes the identity hold rationally in q. But for any nontrivial adequate knot the degree-span theorem excludes even a fixed algebraic meridian trace, and in particular a rational one. Therefore

[c]!=0.                                        (6.1)

By Section 2 it remains nonzero under every finite sigma-stable algebraic cover. This supplies a broad knot-class constraint on the extension, including all the hyperbolic alternating knots in the user’s proposed class, whenever the explicit source and nonresonance properties have been established. It is not a proof that all those knot operators possess those properties.

A formal test that does not require cyclotomic nonresonance

There is a second, often simpler route to (6.1). Put q=exp(h), M=-exp(Xh), with X an independent color variable, and expand the trace operator over C(X)((h)):

a_i(exp(h),-exp(Xh))=h^nu*(alpha_i(X)+O(h)),      (6.2)

where nu is the least h-order among the coefficients; some alpha_i may be zero. Let R be their maximum degree at X=infinity. If the sum of their degree-R leading coefficients is nonzero, the leading trace operator

A_*=sum_i alpha_i(X)T^i, T r(X)=r(X+1),

has no nonzero rational solution. Indeed, for r(X)=a X^k(1+O(1/X)), the coefficient of X^(R+k) in A_*r is a times that nonzero sum.

Suppose a rational splitting g(q,M) of the source exists. Its trace expansion is in C(X)((h)); the lower h-order is finite, and all its coefficients are rational in X. Subtract it from the actual color period’s formal expansion. By the independently proved color polynomiality, this difference is also in C(X)((h)) and is annihilated by A. Its first nonzero h-coefficient would be a nonzero rational solution of A_*, a contradiction. Thus the difference is zero formally. For every sufficiently large fixed integer j the two trace functions are rational in q; all their Laurent coefficients at q=1 agree, so they agree identically. This contradicts the adequacy degree span. Therefore (6.1) follows from this formal test without any hypothesis on root-level kernel dimensions.

Only finitely many fixed j must be excluded when expanding a rational trace: the first nonzero denominator coefficient is a fixed nonzero polynomial in X, and subsequent coefficients only introduce powers of it. If the coefficients are regular at (q,M)=(1,-1) and A_0(-1,1)!=0, the test reduces to that one nonvanishing value. When they share an apparent peripheral zero, (6.2) keeps the actual leading polynomial in X instead of substituting into a zero-over-zero expression.

Nonsplitting is not itself a positive volume theorem

The equation sigma(y)-My=1 is a simple nonsplit generic example. A rational particular solution cannot have a pole in C^: a finite q-orbit chain of poles would leave an unmatched endpoint. It must therefore be Laurent polynomial. Its order at zero must be nonnegative, whereas comparing degrees at infinity forces its degree to be at most -1, a contradiction. The argument uses generic q, so its nonzero powers have infinite orbits.

At a root of even order n its exact particular solution is

y_n(M)=[sum_(r=0)^(n-1) q^(-r(r+1)/2) M^r]
                     /(1+M^n).                 (6.3)

The cyclic norm of M is -M^n and (6.3) follows directly from (3.4), or by translating the finite sum. At M=1 the denominator is exactly two, and |y_n(1)|<=n/2. Thus this nonsplit source has no positive exponential growth. It is not a knot model. It demonstrates why the regulator identification, rather than nonsplitting alone, is the missing norm theorem.

7. Why the classical A-equation alone cannot select volume

The source class is essential. Given any ordered operator A with nonzero b=A(1), form P=(T-sigma(b)/b)A. The coherent period y=1 solves A y=b and P y=0 at every level. Its affine extension is split, with the constant companion vector as a splitting. If the classical b is nonzero, the classical symbol of P is

(L-1)A_0(M,L).

Thus one may use the geometric factor of a hyperbolic knot in A_0 and still obtain a split model with zero exponential growth. This is not the knot’s literature quantization or state. It shows why the source extension, actual quantum operator, and finite pointing cannot be inferred solely from the classical A-polynomial.

There is a corresponding classical warning about matrix norms. For the figure-eight geometric curve

L^2-t(M)L+1=0,
t(M)=M^4-M^2-2-M^-2+M^-4,

the log spectral radius on M=exp(i theta), 0<=theta<=pi, is positive on two reflected intervals (0,pi/3) and (2pi/3,pi). It has equal integrals on them. Hence choosing the largest modulus at each point counts two equal contributions, whereas a relative period from a zero-volume real place to one complete place uses one contribution. Numerical integration gives the full integral 2.0298832128193, twice the required action Vol/2 in the recorded square-holonomy convention. The diagnostics label this a numerical classical check; it is not a quantum volume proof.

The normalized curve matters for the zero-volume reference. Under the conjugation/Weyl involution (M,L)->(1/conj(M),1/conj(L)), a conjugation-odd volume primitive vanishes at a fixed normalized place. A smooth torus point fixed in these coordinates has one place and therefore value zero. At a node, the two normalized places can be exchanged, as at the complete figure-eight point; its coordinate values on the torus do not force volume zero. This is an intrinsic algebraic way to locate a possible classical reference, subject to the actual marked regulator’s conjugation compatibility. It does not yet transport the quantum source to it.

8. Status and checks

The affine class (1.3), finite-cover trace descent, cyclic averaging, resolvent, extremal-coefficient criterion, and formal nonsplitting test are proved above. The standard inhomogeneous-operator coverage was checked to avoid presenting a new definition. The independent diagnostics check nonresonant and resonant affine descent, an inconsistent source, the scalar source factorization, a whole-germ rank drop, the formal trace criterion, and the nonsplit Gaussian-source example. Results are supplementary exact checks, not proofs of a broad knot operator class.

The necessary next comparison is between the actual nonsplit source class and the marked regulator extension, with their peripheral pointing and norms. It is not supplied by Hilbert 90 at one level, by nonzero extension alone, or by maximizing classical eigenvalues. The volume conjecture remains unproved.

The subsequent regular-period theorem supplies a canonical rootwise affine source for any specified actual all-level operator, without Section 5’s rational left-factor hypothesis. Its construction subtracts the full state’s proper polar function, fixed by every ordinary-color value. Positive trace bounds make all those data polynomial in N. After a fixed gauge and denominator clearing, the source lies in a fixed Laurent space with polynomially bounded coefficients. The selected complete regular value is exactly a weighted cyclic field trace. Under a separate rational rank-one hypothesis, regularity at the nonzero pole anchor and at completeness fixes this affine value uniquely. A single generic rational source, its nonsplit class, geometric coupling, and the regulator norm do not follow from this rootwise construction.
