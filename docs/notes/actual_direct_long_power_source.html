Direct crossing powers and a limitation of phase-blind spectral bounds

, 30 October 2026. This is an exact root-source statement for the original connected fixed flat-plat compiler. It retains every cap, mark, level coset and crossing scalar. It gives a direct formulation for fixed integer twist powers and rules out one particular positive spectral upper bound as a route to a strict filled-volume rate. It does not bound the phase-preserving source or prove a filling asymptotic.

Write N>=3, d=N-1, q=exp(pi i/N), S_j=prod_(v=1)^j 2sin(pi v/N), and lambda0=q(d2/2), with the original exponential lift. All Casimir derivatives below are taken at this fixed root, not in a generic-q germ.

1. Direct powers in the original coefficient source

The homogeneous source theorem assigns a degree-d polynomial H_F(x,b,c,y) to a polynomial F in the actual pair Casimir Omega. For two coupons on the SAME pair,

H_(FG)(x,b,c,y)=[z^d] H_F(x,b,z,y) H_G(x,z,c,y).       (1)

A left term has intermediate exponent d-j, while a right term has output exponent i. Extraction imposes i=j, exactly matrix composition. The outer x,y powers add; there is no extra N or pivot factor. The identity is H_1=sum_(j=0)^d b^j c^(d-j).

For every nonzero integer n, therefore,

H_(R^n)(x,b,c,y)
  =[prod_(r=1)^(|n|-1) z_r^d]
     prod_(r=1)^|n| H_(R^sign(n))(x,z_(r-1),z_r,y),
z_0=b, z_|n|=c.                                     (2)

Each layer retains its original scalar lambda0^sign(n). Thus the right side contains lambda0^n and the full repeated-node data. A filled box with exponent n_e=epsilon_e-2p_e may be compiled directly by (2), with all other physical tensors and marked face targets unchanged. This exact identity needs no external-color or alias split. The exponents p_e are fixed before N; no uniform asymptotic in growing word length is asserted. Casimirs at different ports need not commute.

For reference, the normalized one-layer coefficient is

[x^k b^i y^l c^t] H_(r_N^epsilon)
   =(-1)^(k+l+1) q^[epsilon(kl-(i+1)(t+1))]
                          N/(S_i S_k S_l S_t),
i+k+l+t=d, epsilon=+/-1.                            (3)

The full layer is lambda0^epsilon times (3). For scaled simplex coordinates I,K,L,T its principal phase includes

E_simplex + I Log b+K Log x+L Log y+T Log c
    +pi i(K+L)+epsilon pi i(KL-IT)+epsilon pi i/2.    (4)

Here E_simplex=sum Lambda(pi I_j)/pi. The finite d=N-1 offsets and order-one signs stay in (3). Each intermediate extraction subtracts Log z_r in (4). The crossing Casimir transport theorem derives the signed local characteristic, its punctured norm torus and its distinct endpoint schemes. None of these local statements selects the original global coefficient contour.

2. A general obstruction for the Hermite triangle bound

The paired nodes are c_a=qh+q(-h), h=2a+1<N. Let P_a,Q_a denote the canonical confluent Hermite polynomials modulo chi(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2: P_a has value1 at c_a and all other node data0; Q_a has derivative1 there and all other node data0. At odd N retain the single c_=-2 node with its value projector P_.

The ACTUAL normalized crossing data are

r_N(c_a)=nu_a=(-1)^a q^[a(a+1)],
gamma_a=r_N'(c_a)/r_N(c_a)
         =-(N-h)/[2(q^h-q^(-h))].                  (5)

Thus r_N^n has data nu_a^n and n gamma_a nu_a^n for every integer n; negative powers are legitimate because the values are units. These are full Hermite data, not an eigenvalue-only interpolation.

For a polynomial H let |H|_coef replace each coefficient by its absolute value. Define the positive polynomial

E_n=sum_a (|H_(P_a)|_coef+|n gamma_a| |H_(Q_a)|_coef)
                   +[odd N: |H_(P_*)|_coef].       (6)

For n!=0, |H_(R^n)|coef<=E_n. The unit lambda0^n does not affect this inequality. The complete coupon is L_N=(-1)^(N-1)U(N-1)(Omega/2). Its paired values vanish, while

|L_N'(c_a)|=N/[2sin^2(pi h/N)],
|L_N'(c_a)|/|gamma_a|
      =2N/[(N-h)sin(pi h/N)] <= N^2.              (7)

Indeed sin(pi h/N)>=2min(h,N-h)/N. If h<=N/2 the ratio is at most N2/[h(N-h)]<=N2; if h>=N/2 it is at most N2/(N-h)2<=N^2. At the odd simple node L_N(-2)=N. The triangle inequality, |n|>=1, and the independently proved nonnegative coefficients of H_(L_N) give the EXACT coefficientwise comparison

                     H_(L_N) <= N^2 E_n.            (8)

This comparison is local, but its consequence retains the full shared source constraint. On any certified original connected fixed flat plat with R boxes and nonzero integers n_e, multiply (8) and extract the original marked target:

[target] prod_e E_(n_e)
   >=N^(-2R) [target] prod_e H_(L_N).                (9)

The common original prefactor is polynomial in N and every crossing scalar has modulus1. The right side has the augmented complete-volume rate, by the complete augmented-source theorem with zero half twists. Every positive-radius Cauchy bound for the left side is at least that coefficient, so optimizing the radii cannot remove this obstruction. In particular this specific phase-blind Hermite strategy cannot yield any upper exponential rate strictly below the augmented volume, even for arbitrarily large fixed powers.

When a hyperbolic nontrivial filling with smaller volume is under consideration, the representation-volume strictness theorem identifies the needed strict geometric decrease. Inequality (9) shows why a successful direct spectral proof must retain cancellation beyond (6). It does not contradict a phase-preserving bound, the volume conjecture, or a source estimate using different structure. The case n=0 is excluded: its nilpotent derivative weight vanishes.

3. The exact Gaussian retains the missing phases

The existing Gaussian filling theorem, section1, proves even powers using full Hermite functional calculus. The same value-and-derivative argument gives every integer n:

hbar=2pi i/N, K(z)=arccosh(-z/2)^2,
R^n=q^(n d^2/4) exp[n K(Omega)/(2hbar)].              (10)

K is analytic on C minus[2,infinity), with its removable value at -2. The fractional q power uses the literal original Log q. At a paired node set phi=pi(2a+1)/N and s=d-2a. Then

K(c_a)=-pi^2s^2/N^2,
q^[(d^2+s^2)/4]=lambda0(-1)^a q^[a(a+1)],
K'(c_a)=-pi s/[Nsin(phi)],
[n/(2hbar)]K'(c_a)=-ns/[4i sin(phi)]=n gamma_a.

Thus (10) matches every derivative as well as every value. The odd simple node needs only its value. The unwrapped sign in the value formula is (-1)^a=exp(-pi i a); resetting it to the opposite analytic character would change the derivative. Full Hermite uniqueness proves (10), including n<0. This is not a generic-q identity or a unitary diagonalization. The cut at2, the nodes approaching it, all source punctures and the noncommuting neighboring Casimirs remain relevant to a global estimate.

Validation and remaining scope

Results record the finite checks; they do not serve as a proof of an asymptotic.
