Ordinary-zero barriers for the sharp spectral interval entropy

The zero barrier is at fixed real spectra and retains the positive unused intervals. Feasible activation and the stated regularity assumptions are essential; it does not prove regularity or finite multipliers at every global maximum.

1. Exact functional and source qualification

Fix delta in a compact subset of (0,1/2), and put k=cos(pi delta)>0,

alpha(t)=4[k^2-sin^2(pi t)], beta(t)=4sin^2(pi t),
eta(t)=k^2/sin^2(pi t),
rho(t;x)=[1+x+sqrt((1-x)^2+4 eta(t)x)]/2,
Lambda(t;x,1)=beta(t) rho(t;x).

For an original LEFT degree vector

q=(xi,mu,1-nu,zeta), zeta=nu-mu-xi,
0<=mu<nu<1/2, xi,zeta>=0,

write

h_delta(mu,nu,xi)=B_delta(mu,nu)+S(mu,nu)+J^*(mu,nu,xi), (1)
B_delta=int_0^mu log|alpha|+int_nu^(1/2) log|alpha|,
S=int_mu^nu log beta,
J^*=inf_(x>0){J(mu,nu,x)-xi log x},
J(mu,nu,x)=int_mu^nu log rho(t;x) dt.

At xi=0 or zeta=0, use the continuous monomial value of J^*, which is zero. Formula (1) is the sharp interval Legendre functional, not t C(alpha). Its coefficient input is the exact normalized word

Q_(i,j)=sum_(w in{x,y}^{j-i}) x^#x y^#y
           product_(r=i+2)^j eta_r^[w_(r-1)=y,w_r=x],
eta_r=[cos(pi/N)+cos(2pi delta)]/
           [cos(pi/N)-cos((2r-1)pi/N)].             (2)

For the fixed delta compact these eta_r are positive at all sufficiently large levels. The usual tail is K_(i,j)=product_(r=i+1)^j s_r^2 Q_(i,j). All unused alpha rows and the omitted-half scalar still multiply it.

The positive word’s coefficient entropy equals J^*: for mu>0 and strict outer proportions this is the positive-transfer Perron/coefficient theorem. The same rate identification extends to mu=0 by endpoint truncation. For precision, cut the first aN letters, with a>0 fixed and small. Their total coefficient mass at any bounded positive tilt has normalized logarithm O(a log(1/a)), since

eta_r<=C N^2/r^2,
sum_(r<=aN) log max(1,eta_r)
                  <=C aN log(1/a)+O(log N).        (3)

For an upper bound sum over the at most aN+1 prefix degrees, use (3), and apply the strict coefficient theorem to the remaining regular interval. For a lower bound choose an all-y prefix and retain the desired x count in the remaining interval; for a strict limiting outer proportion this is possible for all small a. The joining transition is bounded below by a positive constant at the cut. The limiting tilted integrals converge as a decreases to zero, because log rho has only an integrable logarithm at t=0. This proves the rate for each strict outer proportion at mu=0, without assuming a relative packet at its initial row. At the outer endpoints the word coefficient is exactly one. The same finite-tilt upper argument and the all-word lower bound using min(1,eta_r)>=c>0 give continuity at those endpoints.

This identifies the positive tail, not a nonzero lower bound through a sampled omitted-half zero. For any fixed sign chamber of varying rows, the common unused factors can be factored EXACTLY before comparing the remaining positive coefficients. If a common factor vanishes, the whole corresponding source piece vanishes. Formula (1) and the arguments below are also valid as calculus statements for the sharp variational upper functional; they do not identify the absolute rate of a signed sum of central CROSS paths. No full-source entropy theorem is being assumed.

2. Complete boundary expansion on a nonterminal interval

Fix a source point as in (1), with nu strictly between zero and 1/2, positive interval length, and neither positive endpoint at the moving pole t_c=1/2-delta. Allow mu=0. Keep delta fixed. Along any fixed admissible affine direction q(eps)=q+eps dot_q, write dots for the corresponding affine coordinates. Then

h_delta(q(eps))-h_delta(q)
       =b(q,dot_q) eps log(1/eps)+O(eps),          (4)

with the following coefficients:

mu,xi,zeta>0:               b=0;
mu=0, xi,zeta>0:            b=dot_mu;
mu>0, xi=0:                b=dot_xi;
mu>0, zeta=0:              b=dot_zeta;
mu=xi=0:                   b=2(dot_mu+dot_xi);
mu=zeta=0:                 b=2(dot_mu+dot_zeta).    (5)

Vanishing-coordinate slopes are nonnegative. In particular every b is nonnegative; it is positive whenever the direction activates one of the listed zero coordinates. These cases exhaust the positive-length domain. The constants are uniform for delta in a fixed compact and for the remaining strict coordinates in fixed compact subsets. A single fixed direction suffices for the shared exclusion argument below; no moving regularization or diagram-uniform constant is asserted.

Here is a proof using the actual positive root rather than the endpoint triangle-difference formula. If mu>0 and the outer loads are strict, strict tilt convexity gives a finite smooth minimizing x. At xi=0, rho=1+eta x+O(x^2), uniformly on the interval, so

J=x int_mu^nu eta(t)dt+O(x^2),
partial_(log x)J=x int_mu^nu eta(t)dt+O(x^2).

When xi=eps X with X>0, the minimizer satisfies x asymptotic to a positive constant times eps, and J^*=-X eps log eps+O(eps). If X=0, the affine path remains on the exact monomial boundary. The symmetry of the original continuant in x,y gives the zeta-reflected statement. The remaining terms in (1) are smooth here, including row variations.

If mu=0 and both outer loads are strict, the minimizing tilt stays in a positive compact. Uniformly there,

Lambda(t;x,1)=4 pi k sqrt(x) t [1+O(t)], t->0.

Removing the interval [0,eps dot_mu] therefore contributes -dot_mu eps log eps+O(eps) to the tail integral. Its tilt derivative has a finite limit, so the tilt changes by O(eps); the other row and degree variations contribute O(eps). The unused prefix is smooth at zero because alpha(0)=4k^2>0. This proves the second case of (5).

For the simultaneous corner mu=xi=0 put mu=eps B, xi=eps X. First separate the monomer part S. Since beta(t)=4pi^2 t2[1+O(t2)], its removed prefix contributes

S(eps B,nu+O(eps))-S(0,nu)
                   =-2B eps log eps+O(eps).        (6)

If X=0, J^*=0 exactly and this finishes the proof. Suppose X>0. For x sufficiently small, put D=sqrt((1-x)^2+4eta(t)x). Direct differentiation gives the exact identity

partial_(log x)log rho
  =1/2+(x-1)/(2D)=2eta(t)x/[D(D+1-x)].

For x<=1/2 its denominator is comparable to 1+eta(t)x. Since eta(t) is comparable to t^(-2) throughout the fixed interval ending at nu<1, this proves the uniform comparisons

partial_(log x)log rho(t;x) asymp min(1,x/t^2).

Integrating min(1,x/t^2), splitting at sqrt(x) when that point lies inside the interval, gives, for mu small and nu bounded below,

partial_(log x)J(mu,nu,x) asymp x/(mu+sqrt(x)).     (7)

At xi=eps X the unique tilt solving this equation therefore satisfies x asymp eps^2, with constants depending on the fixed B,X and compact parameters. Also

0<=J(mu,nu,x)<=C sqrt(x)=O(eps).                  (8)

To see (8), split at t=sqrt(x): below that point log rho is bounded by C+log(sqrt(x)/t), and above it by Cx/t^2, plus a smooth O(x) tail. Thus J^*=-2X eps log eps+O(eps). Adding (6) and the smooth unused factor changes proves b=2(B+X). Reflection gives the final case. If both B and X vanish the affine path stays on that corner, and only the regular nu dependence changes. No constant hidden in (8) divides by a vanishing source coefficient or omitted-half scalar.

The same calculation applies to any full-row strict interval contained in [0,1), with its upper endpoint bounded away from 1 and the two poles, after replacing the constant upper integration endpoint in B. The singular behavior at the lower endpoint is identical. Exact finite row reflection supplies the corresponding upper-endpoint-only statement. This observation does not claim the simultaneous two-terminal corner.

3. A width-independent feasible-direction lemma

Let P be the actual selected source’s bounded incidence-flow fiber: the original unit port supplies (or separate half-unit triangle rows when those are genuinely the specified local variables), unchanged ordinary face demands, original lower/upper incidence bounds, and protected demands zero. In a reflected RIGHT row use its actual exchanged parent/child coordinates. Let q be a local maximum of the sum of sharp interval functionals. Suppose:

  1. Each affected sharp row has positive interval length, strictly nonterminal upper row and endpoints away from the spectral poles, as in section 2, or satisfies its stated exact reflection.
  2. All other objective terms have a directional expansion whose eps log(1/eps) coefficient is nonnegative and whose remaining change is O(eps). Smooth terms qualify. Actual complete-triangle entropies also qualify by their elementary closed-corner expansion, when those triangles are genuinely the specified source functional.
  3. There is a point q^+ in P which activates at least one of the zero ordinary incidences appearing in (5). Protected zeros remain zero.

Then q is not a maximum. Indeed the EXACT common-target segment q(eps)=(1-eps)q+eps q^+ stays in P. The strict interval and pole margins survive for small eps. Every term in (5) is nonnegative, and at least one is strictly positive. Hence the sum increases by

b_total eps log(1/eps)+O(eps)>0, b_total>0.        (9)

No concavity of h_delta in the row or face variables is used. No fractional atomization, cover, or redistribution of triangle mass is invoked. All balances are maintained by the original feasible segment. The conclusion applies at every fixed width whenever these hypotheses are verified, not to every boundary point of every diagram.

In particular, if every nonstructural zero ordinary incidence has some feasible activation, average finitely many such witnesses to obtain one q^+ activating all of them. A maximum on this regular stratum then has only the ordinary zeros FORCED by P and the prescribed protected zeros. This differs from asserting that every ordinary incidence is positive or that the complete geometric flow lies in the selected spectral-sector fiber.

4. The finite source test for a removable zero

The feasibility input is a finite transport question, not a geometric assumption. Let l_a<=q_a<=u_a be the original arc bounds. Subtract lower bounds to obtain residual row supplies r’_e, residual face demands d’_f, and arc capacities u_a-l_a. Feasibility is equivalent to their nonnegativity, equality of total supply and demand, and all the finite cut inequalities

sum_(e in I) r'_e - sum_(f in J) d'_f
      <=sum_(a:e in I,f notin J)(u_a-l_a).         (10)

They are the cuts of the ordinary source-row-face-sink maxflow network. For a zero arc a with positive upper capacity, replace its lower bound by eps>0 and test (10), updating both incident margins. That arc is removable exactly when the modified network is feasible for some eps>0. Protected zero arcs have upper capacity zero and remain absent. Repeated incidences are retained as separate arcs.

Equivalently, from a given feasible q draw a directed row-to-face arc when q_a<u_a and a directed face-to-row arc when q_a>l_a. A zero incidence a=(e,f) can increase iff its forward capacity is positive and this residual graph has a directed path from f back to e. The cycle augmentation proves sufficiency. Necessity follows by decomposing the difference of two feasible bounded flows into residual circulations. Thus tight Hall cuts characterize precisely the structural zeros on this fixed fiber; an abstract middle-edge forest does not remove them.

Any finite-level support offsets and eventual-feasibility qualifications belong to the original definition of P and must already have been checked if P is obtained as a limiting sector polytope. This lemma does not replace that source check by a larger relaxed network.

5. Why the excluded strata cannot be hidden in O(eps)

There are exact negative singular terms outside section 2. At the row-zero diagonal take the actual monomer direction

mu=0, nu=eps W, xi=eps W, zeta=0, W>0.

Its sharp entropy difference is

int_0^(eps W) log[beta(t)/|alpha(t)|]dt
                    =2W eps log eps+O(eps)<0.     (11)

Thus a zero-length port at the initial row can offset another port’s positive activation barrier. A shared regularity theorem cannot ignore such terms merely because they are continuous.

Similarly, if a monomer interval begins at mu=t_c, increasing its start by eps B>0 changes the entropy by

int_(t_c)^(t_c+eps B) log[|alpha(t)|/beta(t)]dt
                      =B eps log eps+O(eps).      (12)

This is another negative singular coefficient. It explains the explicit pole exclusion. The opposite endpoint and full-row reflections require the same care. Neither example is asserted to extend to a globally feasible maximizing source flow.

The previous fractional-forest and cover-star obstructions remain unaffected: they concern t C(alpha), while (1) is the true sharp interval functional. They warn against assuming a lossless same-sector activation or a supplied global concavity theorem. Here feasibility is tested in P and only the exact directional singularities are used. The result supplies no automatic finite positive multipliers, no control of all diagonal/pole/terminal strata, and no complete-volume inequality.

6. Terminal-row calculus, with the KKT boundary retained

The six ordinary-zero coefficients in the barrier theorem remain valid at nu=1/2 when 0<=mu<nu and delta lies in a fixed compact subset of (0,1/2). Only the affine directions allowed by the original LEFT bound are used: dot_nu<=0 at this endpoint.

Indeed beta(1/2)=4, eta(1/2)=cos^2(pi delta)>0, and alpha_delta(1/2)=-4sin^2(pi delta) is bounded away from zero. Thus the upper-row changes in B, S and the finite-tilt word integral are O(eps), exactly as in the original proof. The positive-length condition still prevents mu=nu. At a joint mu=xi=0 corner, the estimate eta(t) comparable to t^(-2) remains uniform on [0,1/2], so the same x comparable to eps^2 calculation applies unchanged. No coefficient in the six-case table depends on a singularity at nu=1/2; its former strict inequality was only a convenient stratum restriction. Exact RIGHT reflection gives the corresponding terminal constraint on the reflected interval.

Consequently the feasible-direction exclusion of removable ordinary zeros extends to these terminal rows, provided the SAME original fiber supplies a direction, the stated pole exclusions hold at the other endpoint, and all other affected terms have no negative singular coefficient (or are diagonals controlled by free spectral retuning).

This is a directional boundary calculation, not terminal stationarity. The active inequality nu<=1/2 remains in the variational problem. There is no conclusion partial_nu h + lambda=0; an inequality or additional KKT multiplier can remain. No terminal retagging, complete triangle substitution, or all-interior spectral classification is licensed by this extension.

Dependencies

The original one-sided source retains both unused row products and the omitted-half scalar. The exact positive word supplies the coefficient input used above. This note proves its own directional boundary estimates and bounded-flow activation criterion; it does not borrow concavity or atomization from a different entropy.

The active terminal inequality is essential. Sections 1, 3 and 5 concern the specified open strata.
