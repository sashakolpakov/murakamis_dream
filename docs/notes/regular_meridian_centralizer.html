The regular meridian centralizer and the actual normalization line

Research note, 26 October 2026. There is a smaller commutative group relevant to the framed geometric period: the centralizer of the regular marked meridian. It is multiplicative away from the parabolic fiber and additive on that fiber. Its framing torsor, invariant differential and power adapters can all be computed exactly. The actual ADO modified dimension is the inverse Lie factor of one such adapter, with its original phase.

The group model G^(lambda) is an existing definition, already recorded in the uniform source-base note, from Mezard–Romagny–Tossici, Section 1.2. Here its parameter is the meridian eigenvalue gap, rather than the quantum gap rho. The matrix calculations below supply the geometric application without importing a classification proof.

This identifies the peripheral normalization line, not the selected global quantum period or its Bloch-regulator norm.

1. The centralizer is a smooth commutative group model

On the marked meridian eigenvalue cover put

kappa=M-M^(-1),
mu_M=[M 1;0 M^(-1)].

This meridian is regular even at M=1, where it is a nontrivial unipotent. Commutation of a general invertible matrix with mu_M forces its lower-left entry to zero and a-d=kappa*b. Normalize the lower diagonal entry to one in PGL_2. The projective image of this GL_2 centralizer is

J_M={C_b=[1+kappa*b  b;0 1]:1+kappa*b!=0},
b*b'=b+b'+kappa*b*b',
b^(-1)=-b/(1+kappa*b).                       (1.1)

It is commutative. Its generic multiplicative coordinate is y=1+kappa*b; its kappa=0 fiber is the additive b-line. Its invariant relative differential is

omega_J=db/(1+kappa*b).                      (1.2)

These facts are identities of the smooth open affine-line group scheme. A limit of diagonalizing matrices is unnecessary. The PGL convention is explicit; fixed SL_2 sign characters remain separate marked data.

On the open set M+M^(-1)!=0 this is also the full PGL_2 centralizer: a projectively commuting lift can only commute with mu_M, since conjugation preserves its nonzero trace. At trace zero there is an additional anticommuting component. For example, at M=i the invertible matrix [1 0;-2i -1] conjugates mu_i to -mu_i. All statements here use the commutative regular centralizer (1.1); near the complete meridian M=1 the distinction disappears.

This geometric meridian centralizer is distinct from the large quantum commutant End_(U_q)(V^(tensor b)) computed in the intrinsic cut atlas. Its commutativity does not contradict the genuine SL_2 sector of the full actual braid transport.

2. The framing torsor has an explicit quotient by this group

Use the same two framing coordinates as in the actual critical correspondence, with

F_M(r,s)=[r 1-r;s -kappa-s],
Delta=s+kappa*r !=0,
F_M(1,1)^t=(1,-kappa)^t.

The latter vector is the M^(-1) eigenvector of mu_M. Direct conjugation gives

F_M^(-1) mu_M F_M
   =[M+s -kappa-s;s M^(-1)-s]=mu_s.         (2.1)

In particular this boundary meridian is independent of r. Left multiplication by C_b acts on the frame by

r -> r+b Delta, s -> s,
Delta -> Delta(1+kappa*b).                   (2.2)

Where s!=0, r=0 is a section and b=r/s is the torsor coordinate. Thus r is the residual centralizer direction, while s records the boundary coadjoint coordinate. At completeness the action is r->r+bs. These statements hold for every representation in the marked chart, independently of its knot presentation.

The initial position coordinate on the classical critical chart of the actual balanced state is

A_cut=M/(M+s),                               (2.3)

because x=-M and the raw K is -(M+s). At fixed M an invariant framing two-form adapted to this original cut coordinate is

Omega_cut=(dr/Delta) wedge dlog A_cut
         =-dr wedge ds/[Delta(M+s)].         (2.4)

Its fundamental centralizer vector is V_J=Delta partial_r. Direct differentiation gives

Lie_(V_J) Omega_cut=0,
contraction_(V_J) Omega_cut=dlog A_cut.       (2.5)

At M=1 this is -dr wedge ds/[s(1+s)]. It is not an arbitrary choice of Haar measure on the two-dimensional framing group: it uses its one-dimensional centralizer and the specified cut coordinate. If t=r/s, then it is -dt wedge ds/(1+s).

Equation (2.5) is a canonical algebraic reduction of this form. It does not yet prove that the actual selected finite period implements that contraction or divides out a group volume.

3. The marked A-curve supplies a second section of J_M

Let ell be the centered longitude eigenvalue, with ell(1)=1; retain the original preferred-longitude sign separately. Any SL_2 longitude commuting with mu_M has the form

lambda=[ell (ell-ell^(-1))/kappa;0 ell^(-1)]. (3.1)

Normalize its lower diagonal entry in PGL_2. Its section of the same group (1.1) is

b_lambda=(ell^2-1)/kappa,
b_mu=M,
1+kappa*b_lambda=l=ell^2,
1+kappa*b_mu=m=M^2.                         (3.2)

On the normalized smooth geometric branch,

b_lambda|_(M=1)=dlog ell/dlog M=tau_cusp.     (3.3)

This is an ordinary value of a regular function: both numerator and denominator in (3.2) have simple first meridian terms. The positive cusp shape selects the geometric direction.

The geometric A-equation in these coordinates is exactly

A_geo(M^2,1+kappa*b_lambda)=0.                (3.4)

Its strict transform removes the common kappa power; the exceptional polynomial is its tangent-cone equation in b_lambda. Distinct slopes separate there. Normalization still retains the branch mark if several branches have the same tangent. The group model alone does not supply the marked central regulator constant or its volume.

4. Powers give the actual ADO normalization, including its phase

Define the Laurent polynomial

u_n(M)=(M^n-M^(-n))/(M-M^(-1)),
u_0=0, u_1=1,
u_(n+1)=(M+M^(-1))u_n-u_(n-1).

Matrix multiplication gives exactly

mu_M^n=[M^n u_n(M);0 M^(-n)].                (4.1)

At M=1 the polynomial has value n, and at M=-1 it has value (-1)^(n-1)n. The original full ADO factor is therefore

d_N(M)=eta_N/u_N(M), eta_N=i^(N+3).          (4.2)

This is precisely the previously fixed formula, since u_N=M(1-N)(1+M2+…+M^(2N-2)). It is an exact algebraic evaluation, with its whole meridian germ and phase retained.

For a power degree D, let H_D=diag(u_D(M),1). Wherever u_D!=0, it is the canonical meridian normalization adapter:

H_D^(-1) mu_M^D H_D=mu_(M^D),
H_D^(-1) C_b^(kappa) H_D
             =C_(b/u_D)^(kappa_D),
kappa_D=M^D-M^(-D)=kappa*u_D(M).             (4.3)

Its map on the centralizer Lie coordinate is multiplication by 1/u_D. Thus the actual dimension line is eta_N times this determinant line for D=N. At the parabolic fiber the adapter is diag(D,1), so the original value eta_N/N follows without separately evaluating two zero factors.

The same adapter is a determinant and residue map

There is an intrinsic spectral-algebra version. Put

B_M=R[T]/(T^2-(M+M^(-1))T+1).

The regular matrix mu_M is cyclic: the vectors e_2,mu_M e_2 form a basis of determinant -1 at every M. An operator commuting with mu_M is therefore uniquely a+b mu_M. This identifies its GL_2 centralizer with B_M^times, including the nonreduced fiber B_1=R[epsilon]/(epsilon^2), epsilon=T-1. Its norm is

Norm(a+bT)=a^2+(M+M^(-1))ab+b^2.

The PGL normalization of (1.1) is the class of (1-b/M)+bT; its norm is 1+kappa*b. Adjoining the marked square root of that norm gives its SL_2 lift. This is a spectral unit torsor, with its quadratic root retained.

For a degree D power define

phi_D:B_(M^D)->B_M, T'->T^D.

The Cayley–Hamilton relation gives, integrally,

T^D=u_D(M)T-u_(D-1)(M),
Mat(phi_D)=[1 -u_(D-1);0 u_D],
det(phi_D)=u_D(M).                           (4.4)

Use the canonical algebraic residue Res_M(a+bT)=b. Its Gram matrix in 1,T is [[0,1],[1,M+M^(-1)]], with determinant -1 even at the dual-number fiber. Equation (4.4) gives the exact comparison of these functionals:

Res_M(phi_D(f))=u_D(M) Res_(M^D)(f),
(d_D(M)/eta_D) Res_M(phi_D(f))
                              =Res_(M^D)(f). (4.5)

The same map also preserves the spectral norm exactly:

Norm_(B_M)(phi_D(a+bT'))
   =a^2+(M^D+M^(-D))ab+b^2
   =Norm_(B_(M^D))(a+bT').                  (4.6)

Indeed mu_M^D has determinant one and the indicated trace, so the determinant of aI+b mu_M^D is that polynomial. The norm torsor and the residue co-source therefore have compatible cover maps, including at the dual-number fiber.

Thus the normalization line and its residue co-source are compared together. At M=1, phi_D(epsilon’)=D epsilon; the original 1/D factor is its exact dual correction. This does not identify B_M with the knot’s entire quantum-A spectral algebra or its two-Casimir source: it proves the specified peripheral determinant and residue-line identity.

The adapter becomes singular at a nontrivial torsion root where mu_M^D is scalar. The regularized lattices in the resonant-source calculation must be retained there; a regular matrix conjugation through that point is not asserted.

The actual root-central augmentation is still the identity representation in the earlier threaded-character test. We have not identified it with the nontrivial geometric parabolic mu_1 or with mu_M^N. Equations (4.2)–(4.3) identify the peripheral scalar line and its adapter only.

5. The source normalization composes exactly across cover degrees

Taking powers in two orders gives

u_(DN)(M)=u_D(M)u_N(M^D)
         =u_N(M)u_D(M^N).                   (5.1)

The original phases satisfy

eta_(DN)/(eta_D eta_N)=i^((D-1)(N-1)).

Consequently the exact dimension tower is

d_(DN)(M)=i^((D-1)(N-1)) d_D(M)d_N(M^D).    (5.2)

Its phase factors obey the associative three-degree cocycle identity, by expanding their integer exponents. This includes even and composite degrees. For example

d_(2N)(M)/d_N(M)
             =i^N M^N/(1+M^(2N)).          (5.3)

The peripheral factor becomes i^N/2 only after M=1. This is a verified power-composition factor; identifying a move’s multiplier still requires its actual source adapter.

The paired actual source comparison has a simpler strict law. Its previously proved scalar is

Gamma_N=gamma_N(M)gamma_N(M^(-1))
       =d_N(M)/(N eta_N)=1/[N u_N(M)].

It therefore satisfies the exact identity

Gamma_(DN)(M)=Gamma_D(M) Gamma_N(M^D).       (5.4)

This supplies a coherent peripheral paired line across power covers. It is not an identification of the tensor spaces or selected knot periods at levels D,N,DN.

The actual root meridian step is compatible as well:

d_N(q_N M)/d_N(M)
          =-kappa(q_N M)/kappa(M),          (5.5)

because (q_N M)N=-MN. Thus this line has exactly the original ADO difference cocycle, not a newly chosen gauge.

6. Covers also transform the framing coordinate

For the connected meridional D-cover, the longitude is unchanged while M’=M^D. Equations (3.2) and (4.3) give

b'_lambda=b_lambda/u_D,
tau'_cusp=tau_cusp/D.

The actual boundary coadjoint coordinate changes differently. The Cayley–Hamilton power formula in (2.1) gives

mu_s^D=mu_(s'), evaluated at M'=M^D,
s'=u_D(M)s.

The normalized cover frame is

F_(M^D)(r,u_D s)=u_D H_D^(-1) F_M(r,s).      (6.1)

In particular

A'_cut=M^D/(M^D+u_D s),                     (6.2)

which is generally different from A_cut^D. This distinguishes the internal position coordinate from the peripheral eigenvalue m, whose cover map really is m’=m^D.

Pulling back the two-form (2.4) yields exactly

Omega'_cut/Omega_cut=(M+s)/(M^D+u_D s).      (6.3)

Thus a cover comparison must keep both the Lie adapter and the specified cut-coordinate Jacobian. The regulator’s degree-D law from the A-curve cover note does not replace this local source data.

7. Relation with the actual normal density

The exact figure-eight calculation on the full two-variable framing chart now gives

Omega_actual^2=-tau_cusp/[6 s^2(1+s)^2]
                                  (dr wedge ds)^2.

It has precisely the dependence of Omega_cut^2 at M=1, with a representation-dependent scalar -tau_cusp/6. The old tests against dr ds/s and dr ds/s^2 did not test the centralizer/cut form (2.4). The two-variable audit retains both free variables, the actual coefficient amplitude, the two Hessian null vectors and its transverse determinant in Q(sqrt(-3))(s,r). The exact identity proves independence of r; no interpolation or finite-field inference is used. Results. The displayed density law is not asserted for every knot.

The uniform conclusions proved here are the geometric centralizer, its framing quotient, its marked longitude section, and the exact original normalization and cover composition laws. Establishing that the actual source implements the reduction (2.5), and then transporting the selected period to its geometric regulator line, remains necessary for the original volume conjecture.

8. At the additive fiber the relative boundary is essential

The quotient form specifies a sharper requirement on the source. On the chart s!=0 put t=r/s. At M=1 one has

Omega_cut=dt wedge dlog A_cut
         =d(t dlog A_cut), A_cut=1/(1+s).    (8.1)

Thus its ordinary closed two-periods vanish on this chart. Commutativity of the centralizer does not give a nonzero absolute period by itself. Its relative boundary data cannot be discarded during the proposed reduction.

For a concrete relative cycle let gamma be a closed curve in the s-plane avoiding 0,-1, and take the oriented chain

Gamma=[t_0,t_1] times gamma.

Stokes’ theorem gives exactly

integral_Gamma Omega_cut
   =(t_1-t_0) integral_gamma dlog A_cut.     (8.2)

For a positive small circle around s=-1 the last integral is -2 pi i; around s=0 it is zero. The endpoint separation is part of the relative source, rather than a consequence of the invariant two-form. Setting it to one specifies a normalization, which must be compared with the actual cut. The rectangular cycle here is a model relative class; it has not been identified with that selected quantum cut.

Off the parabolic fiber the same chart has

Omega_cut=db/(1+kappa*b) wedge dlog A_cut,
b=r/s,
kappa Omega_cut=dlog(1+kappa*b)
                                  wedge dlog A_cut. (8.3)

The multiplicative fiber has an absolute logarithmic circle; the additive fiber does not. This elementary change is why one must transport a relative source or its regularized lattice, not just a class of ordinary closed cycles. It agrees with the earlier need to retain resonant normal jets, without identifying the different source constructions.

The subsequent actual cut-residue calculation now identifies beta=dlog A_cut in the original finite cut pairing, with its canonical representative, pole packet and normal carry retained. It also derives the principal crossing determinant and pivotal-volume factors uniformly. The remaining relative pushforward is not supplied by this exact boundary co-source identification.

The audit checks the group, frame, longitude, invariant-density and cover identities, including the full normalization phases. Results.
