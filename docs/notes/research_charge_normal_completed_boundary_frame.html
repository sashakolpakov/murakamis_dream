A full boundary frame from charge-normal values and genuine turns

Written proof, 23 November 2026. Values of an actual boundary packet and its genuine turn recover every Newton coefficient with polynomial inverse cost. The observation weights come from the actual charge-normal trace. An explicit odd-level completion retains the simple-node direction. The resulting positive metric preserves the turn and both original closing rows. This is a reconstruction and error estimate, not a lower bound for the original knot scalar.

3. Reconstruction at both parities

Let f=∑αkekf=\sum\alpha_ke_k. Values at the double nodes only see α0,…,αr−1\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{r-1}, because all later ek(cj)e_k(c_j) vanish. Their vector is A(α0,…,αr−1)𝖳A(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{r-1})^{\mathsf T}. Values of JfJf similarly recover αd,…,αd−r+1\alpha_d,\ldots,\alpha_{d-r+1}. Equations (13)–(14) give ∑k<r|αk|2+∑k>d−r|αk|2≤N464π{GD(f)+GD(Jf)}.(15) \sum_{k<r}|\alpha_k|^2+ \sum_{k>d-r}|\alpha_k|^2 \le\frac{N^4}{64\pi}\{G_D(f)+G_D(Jf)\}. \qquad\text{(15)} At even N=2rN=2r, these are all the coefficients. This proves (7) in that case.

4. Positive resolution and original-source error bounds

Let SNfS_Nf be the observation vector in (6), including its square-root weights and both odd simple rows. Then HNobs=SN*SN≽N−4I,ℛN=(SN*SN)−1SN*,ℛNSN=I,∥ℛN∥2→N≤N2.(20) H_N^{\mathrm{ obs}}=S_N^*S_N\succeq N^{-4}I,\qquad \mathcal R_N=(S_N^*S_N)^{-1}S_N^*,\qquad \mathcal R_NS_N=I,\qquad \|\mathcal R_N\|_{2\to{\mathrm{ N}}}\le N^2. \qquad\text{(20)} The last assertion follows because SNℛNS_N\mathcal R_N is the ordinary orthogonal projection onto the observation range, and (7) bounds its inverse there. Thus redundant observation data cause no ambiguity. Equivalently, if sℓs_\ell are the observation rows and ψℓ=(HNobs)−1sℓ*\psi_\ell=(H_N^{\mathrm{ obs}})^{-1}s_\ell^*, then f=∑ℓψℓ(sℓf),∥f∥D2=∑ℓ|sℓf|2.(21) f=\sum_\ell\psi_\ell(s_\ell f),\qquad \|f\|_{\mathrm{ D}}^2=\sum_\ell|s_\ell f|^2. \qquad\text{(21)} This is an exact positive frame in the completed metric. Indeed ⟨ψℓ,f⟩D=sℓf\langle\psi_\ell,f\rangle_{\mathrm{ D}}=s_\ell f, so its rank-one resolution operators are positive for that inner product.

Equation (6) contains w0|f(c0)|2w_0|f(c_0)|^2 and w0|(Jf)(c0)|2w_0|(Jf)(c_0)|^2. Identities (2) and (14) therefore prove the closing bounds in (8). Its bilinear bound follows from |α𝖳Jβ|≤∥α∥2∥β∥2|\alpha^{\mathsf T}J\beta|\le\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2 and (7). It concerns two separate inputs; no unit bound on a full tensor-product row is asserted.

In particular the entire boundary error is controlled by ∥f−f̃∥N≤N2∥f−f̃∥D.(22) \|f-\widetilde f\|_{\mathrm{ N}} \le N^2\|f-\widetilde f\|_{\mathrm{ D}}. \qquad\text{(22)} The right side requires both actual value observations and those of the genuine turned error. It is not supplied by one coherent input, one physical row, or a positive scalar sum alone.

No upper polynomial bound for SNS_N, its Gram condition number, or the full multiplication operator in this metric is claimed. Every original gate and closing must use the same chosen metric when its budget is estimated. The theorem does not transfer an earlier budget or source lower bound from a different metric.

These are auxiliary pair-Casimir samples. They have not been identified with ordinary main-meridian colors of an assembled knot. Nor does the charge-normal initializer of [D] provide both sets of observations or a lower bound for their particular original closing coordinate. Reversed edges and the simple completion remain explicit. No unit-coupon continuation, geometric rate, or finite-exception theorem follows.
