Controlled cyclic completion of the actual Euler source

Research note, 26 October 2026. The nilpotent Euler polynomial in the actual R-matrix has a canonical completion by the existing cyclic dilogarithm. We compute its entire coefficient normalization, derive its boundary pentagon through the mixed central jet, and prove polynomial bounds for its coefficient change on marked compact central charts, uniformly in N. This treats a genuine N-dependent algebraic normalization, not a fixed-degree substitute.

This is local source transport. It does not identify the global meridian source extension, select a geometric period of a knot, or prove the volume conjecture.

1. The Euler polynomial is an actual quantum-group atom

Fix N>=2, q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2 and rho=q-q^(-1). Define

c_j=(-1)^j Q^(j(j-1)/2)/(Q;Q)_j,
P_N(X)=sum_(j=0)^(N-1)c_j X^j.                (1.1)

Every denominator is nonzero. In the normalized actual uncheck crossing of the semicyclic construction, the Euler part is precisely

Theta=P_N(q rho^2 E_1 F_2).                   (1.2)

Indeed [j]_q!=q(-j(j-1)/2)(Q;Q)_j/(1-Q)j and rho(1-Q)=-q rho^2. Substituting into its original coefficient q(j(j-1)/2)rhoj/[j]_q! gives (1.2). The original Cartan factor, flip and full source wrap are retained. For the uncheck holonomy map the second F wrap is phi_2/K_1^N, exactly as in that note; for the checked map its input F wrap is phi_2. No new invariant or replacement crossing is defined.

An exact polynomial recurrence already displays the boundary:

(1-X)P_N(QX)=P_N(X)-X^N/N.                  (1.3)

The coefficients below X^N cancel by their defining recurrence. The top one is -Q^(N-1)c_(N-1)=-1/N, since

(Q;Q)_(N-1)=N, c_(N-1)=Q/N.

Thus the familiar 1/N source is forced by the actual Euler atom. When X^N=0, (1.3) is its ordinary nilpotent shift identity.

2. The uncompleted pentagon has noncentral boundary terms

Let XY=QYX, U=X^N, V=Y^N. Normal order means X before Y. For the unmodified truncated pentagon put

D=P_N(X)P_N(-YX)P_N(Y)-P_N(Y)P_N(X).

The coefficient of X^p Y^s on the first product is exactly

sum_k c_(p-k)c_(s-k)Q^(-k)/(Q;Q)_k,          (2.1)

where 0<=k,p-k,s-k<N. This follows by reordering (-YX)k=(-1)k Q(-k(k+1)/2)XkY^k. For p,s<N it equals c_p c_s Q^(-ps): the generic pointed compact pentagon’s coefficient uses only indices below N and can therefore be specialized without a vanishing denominator. Consequently D lies in the central ideal (U,V), with the exact three-block decomposition

D=U B_X+V B_Y+UV B_XY, deg_X,deg_Y B_*<N.    (2.2)

The coefficients are (2.1) with the indicated wrapped exponents. Two useful nonzero coefficients are

[X^N Y]D=1/[N(1-Q)],
[X^(2N-2)Y^(2N-2)]D=Q^3/N^3.               (2.3)

For N=2 this is exactly

D=U Y/4+V X/4-UV/8.

A central scalar cannot repair this specific unmodified operator identity. Its U constant coefficient would have to vanish, while the nonzero UY coefficient in (2.3) would remain. This comparison retains arbitrary central scalar functions, not only their values at a complete point. It does not refute a differently normalized or source-completed move, or a particular contracted scalar identity.

3. Use the literature cyclic completion, with its marked root

The alternate cyclic dilogarithm already defined in the four-atom note is

d_N(X)=(1-X^N)^((N-1)/(2N))
          product_(r=1)^(N-1)(1-Q^rX)^(-r/N). (3.1)

It is normalized by d_N(0)=1, with its roots continued from that point. Write nu=(1-U)^(1/N), nu(0)=1. Direct expansion gives

log d_N(X)=-sum_(k>=1,N does not divide k)
                           X^k/[k(1-Q^k)].  (3.2)

The terms with k divisible by N cancel between the factors of (3.1). In particular d_N(X)=P_N(X) when X^N=0. Finite product cancellation, or (3.2), gives

(1-X)d_N(QX)=nu d_N(X),
product_(a=0)^(N-1)d_N(Q^a X)=1.            (3.3)

This is the existing cyclic function, including its determinant normalization. It supplies a completion of the actual atom (1.2). It is not a claim that each separate compact Verma factor can be evaluated at a root without the whole compression used there.

4. An explicit norm-torsor formula for every coefficient

Reduce d_N(X) in the free algebra with X^N=U:

d_N(X)=sum_(j=0)^(N-1)a_j(U)X^j.

The coefficient equations of (3.3) give

a_j=a_0 product_(r=1)^j Q^(r-1)/(Q^r-nu).

Equivalently, with the c_j of (1.1),

a_j/c_j=a_0 product_(r=1)^j
                             (Q^r-1)/(Q^r-nu). (4.1)

The entire scalar is fixed by the following product, with a_0(0)=1:

a_0(U)^N=nu^(-N(N-1)/2)
              product_(r=1)^(N-1)
                [(nu-Q^r)/(1-Q^r)]^(N-r).      (4.2)

Here is an independent determinant proof. Put

T_nu(X)=sum_j t_j X^j,
t_j=product_(r=1)^j Q^(r-1)/(Q^r-nu), t_0=1,
X e_j=e_(j+1), X e_(N-1)=U e_0,
S e_j=Q^j e_j.

The relation is (1-X)S T_nu=nu T_nu S. The cyclic bidiagonal matrix (1-X)S has characteristic polynomial lambdaN-nuN, by its diagonal and full cyclic determinant terms. An eigenvector of eigenvalue lambda normalized to have zeroth coordinate one has

v_j(lambda)=Q^(j(j-1)/2)/product_(r=1)^j(Q^r-lambda).

To compute the determinant of these columns at lambda=nu Q^a, multiply each column by product_(r=1)(N-1)(Qr-lambda). The resulting row polynomials have degrees N-1-j. Their determinant is a fixed nonzero Q constant times the ordinary Vandermonde. The original denominator product is U^(N-1), and the zeroth coordinates of T_nu’s columns have product U^(N-1)product_j t_j. Those factors cancel. Scaling the Vandermonde by nu then proves

det T_nu=nu^(N(N-1)/2)
                product_(r=1)^(N-1)
                     [(1-Q^r)/(nu-Q^r)]^(N-r). (4.3)

The remaining Q constant is fixed by nu=1,U=0: T_nu is then P_N of a nilpotent matrix and has determinant one. Equations (3.3) and det(a_0 T_nu)=1 give (4.2).

This also proves uniqueness in the marked formal neighborhood. Two unit solutions of (3.3) differ by a central function f(U). Their norms imply f(U)^N=1; f(0)=1 forces f=1 in characteristic zero. The root is not independently reset at each occurrence.

5. The first central jet is explicit and nonscalar

Write a_j(U)=c_j+U r_j+O(U^2), and R_N(X)=sum_j r_j X^j. Differentiating (4.1)–(4.2) gives

r_0=(1/N^2)sum_(k=1)^(N-1) k/(1-Q^k),
r_j/c_j=r_0+(1/N)sum_(k=1)^j 1/(1-Q^k).     (5.1)

For the first formula use sum_k(1-Qk)(-1)=(N-1)/2, obtained by pairing k with N-k, including its even-level middle term. The increments in the second formula are nonzero. Thus R_N is not a scalar multiple of P_N; changing only a scalar source line cannot replace this operator jet.

For the actual complex roots, (5.1) also proves directly

max_j |r_j/c_j|=O(1+log N).                  (5.2)

Indeed |1-Q^k|>=4 min(k,N-k)/N, and the resulting harmonic sum is O(log N). This is a coefficient estimate in the specified Euler frame, not a condition-number estimate for every tensor operator or a lower bound for a contracted knot period.

6. The pentagon extends through the nilpotent central order

Let R=K[[U,V]], with the roots of 1-U,1-V,1-U+UV marked by their value one. The algebra

A_R=R<X,Y>/(XY-QYX,X^N-U,Y^N-V)

is free over R with basis XiYj, 0<=i,j<N. Formula (3.1) defines units there: its reduction is P_N of a nilpotent element. The strict cyclic pentagon proved in the root pentagon note is

d_N(Y)d_N(bX)
  =d_N(X)d_N(-YX/a)d_N(Y/h),                 (6.1)

a^N=1-U, b^N=1-V, h^N=1-U+UV.

Its anchored branches are regular in this order. Localizing the free order to its generic central field gives the proved identity; torsion freeness therefore extends it to A_R itself. This includes its nilpotent source, rather than replacing the source by a generic unpointed splitting module.

Here is its complete first mixed jet, modulo (U2,V2), which retains UV. Put T=-YX, P_Z=P_N(Z), R_Z=R_N(Z), and partial f(Z)=Z f’(Z). The three transported factors are

B_X=P_X+U R_X-(V/N)partial P_X
                  -UV(R_X+partial R_X/N),
B_T=P_T+(U/N)partial P_T-UV R_T,
B_Y=P_Y+(U/N)partial P_Y+V R_Y
                +UV(-partial P_Y/N+R_Y+partial R_Y/N).

Then exactly in this quotient,

(P_Y+V R_Y)B_X=(P_X+U R_X)B_T B_Y.           (6.2)

These formulas follow by expanding the actual transported norm roots and arguments in (6.1). In particular (bX)^N=U-UV, (-YX/a)^N=-UV, and (Y/h)^N=V+UV in this quotient. Thus the corner term -UV R_T is derived, not a freely chosen correction. Omitting it gives a nonzero operator at every level: R_N is nonzero by (5.1), the powers T^j are independent in the nilpotent PBW basis, and the remaining P_X,P_Y factors are units.

Any specified external vectors and covectors inherit (6.2), with their full source and co-source transported. A nonzero omitted corner is detectable by some exterior functional: coefficient extraction in the PBW basis is perfect, and pairing with the complementary monomial detects a chosen coefficient. This does not prove that a particular knot’s selected cut has nonzero geometric coupling.

The central variables here are primitive quantum-plane centers. They are not the earlier meridian wall coordinate or the point source of the scalarized A-curve. Nor is this free primitive order a common stable lattice for the unscaled adjacent meridian projectors; their double-pole obstruction remains unchanged.

7. Uniform polynomial bounds beyond fixed algebraic degree

Let D be a simply connected domain containing 0 but not 1, and let L(U)=Log(1-U) on D with L(0)=0. Take

nu_N(U)=exp(L(U)/N), Q_N=exp(2pi*i/N).

For every compact K in D there are constants B,C, independent of N>=2 and 0<=j<N, such that

C^(-1)N^(-B)<=|a_j(U)/c_j|<=C N^B,
U in K.                                      (7.1)

Proof. L is bounded on K, so |nu_N-1|<=C_K/N. For roots with min(r,N-r) larger than a fixed constant, the ratios

(nu_N-Q_N^r)/(1-Q_N^r)

are 1+O_K(1/min(r,N-r)). Their logarithmic moduli have the same bound, whose sum is O_K(log N). There are only finitely many remaining endpoint indices. Their ratios converge uniformly on K to (L(U)-2piir)/(-2piir), or its negative-index version. These limits never vanish: L(U)=2piir, r!=0, would imply U=0, where the chosen L is zero. Hence their moduli and inverse moduli are bounded. Finitely many small N satisfy the same bound by compactness and the identical nonvanishing argument.

Finally log|nu_N|=Re L/N. In (4.2), all product weights (N-r)/N are bounded by one. Thus |log|a_0||=O_K(1+log N); (4.1) gives the same bound for every coefficient ratio. This proves (7.1).

Both ratios and their inverses are holomorphic on D with the marked roots. Cauchy estimates on a fixed small circle about zero therefore bound every fixed central Taylor jet by a power of N. The sharper first-jet estimate is (5.2).

The bounded logarithm lift is essential. Arbitrary cut indices growing with N can make nu stay away from one and are not covered by this proof. The theorem also does not compare sums of complex terms from different source channels: polynomial entrywise factors alone do not exclude global cancellation.

8. An exact comparison with the original factorial source and cut

This completion also identifies a specified source vector, not only an operator normalization. Put

p_a(nu)=(Q nu;Q)_a, S_N(nu)=p_(N-1)(nu),
J_(j,a)=Q^(-j) delta_(a,N-1-j),
gamma_N(U)=a_0(U)/S_N(nu).

Complementing the finite product gives exactly

t_j(nu)=Q^(-j)p_(N-1-j)(nu)/S_N(nu),
vector(a_j)=gamma_N J vector(p_a).            (8.1)

For the product identity, the omitted factors are product_(k=1)j(1-Q(-k)nu); dividing their reciprocals by Q^j gives the factors Q(k-1)/(Qk-nu) of t_j. No endpoint factor is deleted. At U=0, nu=1 and gamma_N=1/N, so this is the literal reflection identity c_j=Q^(-j)(Q;Q)_(N-1-j)/N.

The full affine source equation is transported too. Let

(L_p v)_a=v_(a+1)-(1-Q^(a+1)nu)v_a,
(L_a v)_j=(Q^j-nu)v_j-Q^(j-1)v_(j-1),

with cyclic indices, and let R_0 be the reversal without its Q^(-j) phase. Direct substitution proves the matrix identity

L_a (gamma_N J)=-gamma_N R_0 L_p.             (8.2)

The actual factorial source is L_p p=(1-U)e_(N-1). Its image is therefore L_a a=-gamma_N(1-U)e_0. Thus (8.1) identifies these pointed finite cyclic source presentations with their entire boundary coefficient. It does not merely compare their kernels.

The auxiliary argument shift delta(nu)=Qnu is transported as well. On an overlap of its specified lifted charts it gives

delta(a_j)=[delta(gamma_N)/gamma_N]
              [(1-Q^(-j)nu)/(1-Qnu)] a_j.     (8.2a)

This follows from the actual factorial identity p_a(Qnu)/p_a(nu)=(1-Q^(a+1)nu)/(1-Qnu). The ratio of gamma_N is fixed by (4.2) and the retained endpoint, with its root continued on the lifted cover. A cut change can leave the principal source chart and meet its polar lattice; (8.2a) does not assert regularity of each separate ratio there. For the physical argument nu=M^2, this is the squared-meridian shift. The full local two-direction source transport is thus obtained by conjugation, while its lattice arrows are retained.

For any specified co-source lambda_p, set

lambda_a=lambda_p J^(-1)/gamma_N.

Then lambda_a vector(a)=lambda_p vector(p) exactly. Over C, J is unitary; the full source change is a scalar times a unitary matrix, with no hidden matrix condition number.

On the compact marked charts of Section 7,

C^(-1)N<=|S_N(nu_N(U))|<=C N.                (8.3)

Indeed S_N(nu)=U/(1-nu), with its whole value N at U=0; S_N/N converges uniformly to -U/L(U), whose value at zero is one and which is nonzero on the chart. This and Section 7 give polynomial upper and lower bounds for gamma_N and its inverse. Thus the actual vector and co-source transport has uniform normalization control, despite its growing algebraic degree. This is stronger than an entrywise estimate alone.

For the physical meridian argument nu=M^2 at a root, the exact parameter relation is U=1-M^(2N). With the original phase eta_N=i^(N+3), the full ADO dimension remains

d_N(M)=eta_N M^(N-1)/S_N(M^2),
gamma_N/d_N=a_0/(eta_N M^(N-1)).              (8.4)

The coherent source branch M=(1-U)^(1/(2N)) makes the second factor polynomially bounded as well. At completeness gamma_N=1/N while d_N(1)=eta_N/N; the phase has not been identified with one. The U here is the primitive center complementary to the meridian center, not the variable called U=M^(2N) in several earlier notes.

Equations (8.1)–(8.4) give a local, source-compatible comparison between actual factorial data and the coefficients of the existing cyclic dilogarithm. They apply at each fixed root and on its marked central chart. They do not identify the knot’s global meridian difference extension or its selected geometric period. Passing to spectral values of X at U=0 would still collapse these coefficient directions; the coefficient lattice and co-source are retained.

The subsequent Rivin flow and paired-source calculation also computes the exact involutive norm of this comparison:

gamma_N(M)gamma_N(M^(-1))=d_N(M)/(N eta_N).

The marked roots at M=1 fix its phase. Transforming both actual dual source frames therefore gives the full Gaussian cap scalar N eta_N/(Q d_N), with its reflected operator retained. This calibrates opposite source orientations and supplies a local norm-one edge variable for a proposed global flow lift. It does not identify that lift or the selected knot period.

The subsequent source-connection calculation expresses gamma_N itself as a cyclic dilogarithm times the exact monomial and S_N factors, with a fixed phase. Its rational connection gives transport beyond the compact central chart. At the other norm-root lifts z=Q^r of U=0, its regularized value is a specified phase times (Q;Q)_r/N. The corresponding primitive operator has image X^r V and rank N-r. The opposite source weights and actual caps are regularized together on their common cover. This retains the kernel that an invertible-frame assumption would miss, without inferring the knot’s geometric coupling from it.

9. Existing coverage, checks and the remaining global theorem

Definition (3.1) is the alternate cyclic dilogarithm recorded by Ip–Yamazaki, 1412.5777, not a new quantum definition. Cyclic Fourier analysis and coefficient-mutation coverage also occur in Ishibashi, 2501.02316. Its 16 October correction, 2610.23572 explicitly adds lifting data on a mu_N-cover and an extra phase in general pentagons. Only the stated coverage was consulted; none of those papers’ pentagon, Fourier or strict-lift proofs is used. Our strict identity is restricted to the already proved compatible anchored cover. Unrelated root choices are not equated.

No novelty is claimed for cyclic completion, cyclic Fourier normalization or the compact pentagon. The contribution needed here is their explicit actual-R source calibration, mixed central boundary terms and uniform coefficient control in that frame. The determinant, jet and bound proofs above are direct.

The exact audit checks the full noncommutative defect, mixed-source pentagon, cyclic normalization determinant, literal finite-product jet, and agreement with the actual crossing at both colors and wraps. Complex-number bound samples are labeled diagnostics; the uniform bound is proved in Section 7. Results.
