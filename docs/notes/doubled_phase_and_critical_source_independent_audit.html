Independent audit: doubled Rivin phases and critical source traces

27 October 2026. The current proofs in the doubled-phase optimizer and the irreducible critical-source theorem require that a density on a reducible etale algebra be a unit on every selected component.

1. The phase map and the optimizer

The fixed-meridian shape Jacobian is invertible by the stated positive Lagrangian pairing. Its peripheral slopes satisfy τ−=τ+¯\tau_-=\overline{\tau_+}, Im⁡τ+>0\operatorname{Im}\tau_+>0. Thus the doubled logarithmic phase Jacobian (1−1τ+−τ−) \begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\\tau_+&-\tau_-\end{pmatrix} has nonzero determinant τ+−τ−\tau_+-\tau_-. The selected normalized shape germ is genuinely etale over the two phase ratios. The conclusion is local at the chosen place and is not an assertion about the raw singular plane A-polynomial fiber.

On unit phases, the conjugation involution and local uniqueness force w=z‾w=\bar z. Positivity persists. Stationarity on the affine fixed-two-phase angle slice supplies the real edge equations; the imaginary edge equations were already imposed. Strict concavity then makes this point the unique global maximizer of that entire affine angle fiber and its closure. This does not assert such a positive optimizer at arbitrary remote phase values.

Writing u=x+ia,v=y+ibu=x+ia,v=y+ib, the derivative is dΦ=xdb−ydad\Phi=x\,db-y\,da. Solving the phase Jacobian gives Hess⁡Φ=1Im⁡τ+(|τ+|2−Re⁡τ+−Re⁡τ+1). \operatorname{Hess}\Phi =\frac1{\operatorname{Im}\tau_+} \begin{pmatrix}|\tau_+|^2&-\operatorname{Re}\tau_+\\ -\operatorname{Re}\tau_+&1\end{pmatrix}. It is positive definite with determinant one. The stated complex algebraic formula is exactly the same calculation before the real restriction. The rational cotangent formulas also prove the claimed algebraicity of the constrained covariance. Neither covariance is thereby the actual quantum Gaussian density.

The centered complete meridian and longitude signs are explicitly retained as external characters. They must accompany any later SL2 or quantum comparison.

The added global real corollary also passes. Within ONE fixed affine edge-angle and logarithmic marking, equal unwrapped boundary phases place two positive edge solutions in the same strictly concave affine fiber. They coincide; the sine rule then identifies their shapes. The phase map is a local diffeomorphism at every positive point, so this global injection is a diffeomorphism onto its open image, of absolute degree one. Fixed edge winding numbers and unwrapped phases are essential. This proves neither image convexity or surjectivity nor any degree statement for the periodic phase torus or quantum cycle.

3. Irreducible trace descent and finite moments

The trace pairing proof is exact: its Gram matrix is VtVV^tV for the Vandermonde embedding matrix. Hence the density a=η(ψ)a=\eta(\psi) is uniquely reconstructed from the stated dd moments by G−1mG^{-1}m. In a rank-one line over a FIELD, nonzero source and nonzero co-source imply a≠0a\ne0. Every embedding coefficient is then σi(a)≠0\sigma_i(a)\ne0. The normalization λ(ψ)=1\lambda(\psi)=1 is a convenient sufficient certificate, not a necessary one.

For higher-rank lines the normalization still certifies a≠0a\ne0, but mere individual nonvanishing does not, as stated. For a reducible etale algebra a faithful normalized matrix trace sees every primitive idempotent with weight rank/dimension.

The density multiplying that faithful trace must be a UNIT on every selected etale component. Mere nonzero density is insufficient: (1,0)(1,0) in F×FF\times F would kill the second component. The current note now includes this correction.

Neither critical-field irreducibility nor normalized trace descent has been proved for an actual global geometric quotient of the quantum state. The explicit component, splitting-field and already-closed-scalar qualifications are necessary.

4. The whole arc and height estimates

Distinct exponentials with polynomial coefficients are linearly independent: the stated differential operators isolate one exponential with an invertible operator on its polynomial factor. Their order-mm constant-coefficient equation bounds the zero order at the origin by m−1m-1. Hence every fixed nonzero rational coefficient on the physical source slice has finite vanishing order. Applied to a polynomial relation with nonzero constant and leading terms, Cauchy bounds and their reciprocals give polynomial upper and lower bounds for ALL specialized conjugates. This argument does not need bounded absolute cyclotomic degree.

The degree-independent naive-height inequality (1+H)−1≤|α|≤1+H(1+H)^{-1}\le|\alpha|\le1+H is correct after removing zero constant powers and reversing the polynomial for the lower bound. The bounded-Weil-height counterexample is also correct: the displayed monic polynomial is Eisenstein at two, and all roots of modulus at least one lie in the disk centered at 2N2^N with radius two. The fixed-point map there is a strict contraction for every N≥2N\ge2, so only its one real root lies outside the unit disk. Its Weil height stays bounded while its chosen archimedean value grows exponentially.

The resulting finite-sum rate theorem correctly requires a strict TOTAL dominance gap or a smaller-rate bound on any total remainder. Rivin concavity alone supplies neither global period descent nor that gap over all complex sectors.

These are substantive sufficient theorems for a proposed whole source comparison. They do not prove its existence or the selected volume limit.

5. Precise scope of the optimizer algebraicity

The current proofs already establish the claimed classical algebraicity under their stated positive gluing/rank inputs: geometric rigidity Section1, doubled phase optimization Sections3–5, and whole comparison rigidity Section1. No algebraicity correction is needed in those statements. It is not a new conclusion obtained from quantum jets.

Here is an independent short proof. Let F be the peripheral coefficient field and K=F(z_1,…,z_t) the selected shape field. Differentiate the normalized polynomial gluing equations over F. The invertible shape Jacobian gives J(dz_j)=0, hence every dz_j=0 in Omega_(K/F). If K/F had positive transcendence degree, choose a transcendence basis and its coordinate derivations. In characteristic zero each extends uniquely over the remaining finite separable field extension, producing a nonzero derivation of K. But all dz_j=0 makes every such derivation zero, a contradiction. Thus K/F is finite algebraic. The complete normalized isolated point has F=Q with its recorded algebraic lift characters, and therefore has algebraic shape coordinates.

For the ordinary one-cusped geometric branch the correct field is the function field of its marked peripheral CURVE, Frac(Q[M+/-1,L+/-1]/(A_geo)), after choosing the component and normalized place. M and L are dependent. The result is already finite algebraic over the meridian field Q(M) locally. It is not a claim that arbitrary independent complex M,L give a geometric shape solution, or that all shape coordinates are integral over the unlocalized full polynomial ring Q[M,L].

At the complete parabolic lift, M=+1 or -1 and the selected longitude character is retained. This gives algebraic numbers for the UNFRAMED ideal shapes. The raw actual braid critical scheme still has its two framing directions: its other shape/ position coordinates become algebraic numbers only after those framing marks are fixed algebraically as well. Fixing a meridian alone does not isolate that entire framed critical scheme.

Strict concavity identifies the unique positive real stationary point on the stated affine angle fiber. Complex transversality and polynomial gluing, rather than uniqueness alone, supply the algebraicity. With two independent boundary PHASES the doubled curve is the appropriate algebraic cover; those phase variables are not two independent full peripheral holonomies.

Algebraic shapes do not imply algebraic logarithms or regulator values. The regulator is a transcendental FUNCTION/period of its algebraic connection; this wording makes no theorem that the particular hyperbolic-volume NUMBER is transcendental. For definition/coverage only, see Zagier, The Dilogarithm Function, Sections I.3–4. No proof from that source is imported here. The actual selected wall coefficient2^-N supplies a concrete reminder that even nonzero algebraic source values can have exponential size: all weight-N characters must be retained before applying a bounded whole-algebraic coefficient argument.

6. The whole selected-line polar criterion

The whole selected-line theorem also passes independently. On the EXACT marked ray s_N=kappa N^(-1/e), kappa^e=pi i, a meromorphic rank-one connection integrates to C s^rho exp(B(s)+H(s)), with finite B=sum b_r s^-r and convergent H holomorphic. Its normalized log modulus is exactly the displayed finite sum of distinct powers N^(r/e-1), plus O(logN/N). The r=e contribution is 2Im(b_e); terms r>e can contribute zero only when each real coefficient vanishes on that ray. A fixed meromorphic frame changes dlog only by a logarithmic pole and preserves B.

The logarithmic quotient by exp(S_geo/h) gives the stated volume rate and O(logN/N) error. This is a genuinely useful sufficient GLOBAL target, not an assumption that a local Gaussian block is the actual selected invariant. For a perturbed physical ray the polar primitive must first be pulled back to that exact ray; imaginary leading high poles can otherwise produce real lower terms. The theorem uses the exact ray and does not make that mistake.

The accumulating zeros of a sum of two different exponential characters correctly obstruct a single meromorphic selected line. The amplified whole-unit example has primitive -2i pi^2/h and normalized rate -4pi^2 as printed. Existence of the ACTUAL selected line, its original co-source and its marked leading regulator character is still the new theorem to prove.
