What the short-filling results imply about finite exceptions

Research scope note, updated 3 November 2026. The target continues to count the original integer filling assignments. It has not been replaced by a statement about distinct knot types, essential parameters, or density. The manuscripts remain frozen.

The new boundary theorems allow many more partial fillings. They do not yet prove that every fixed template has only finitely many untreated assignments. There are two distinct issues already on small paths: adjacent short vertices require a composition estimate or a marked reduction to an already treated family, and a zero vertex can give infinitely many assignments of the same unresolved knot. The joint estimate is now proved for the pendant pair of powers 2,22,2. An exact marked reduction now covers every same-sign pendant chain of powers 22 or −2-2, and boundary multiplication gives its canonical boundary asymptotic. The mixed pair (2,−2)(2,-2) now also has a complete analytic boundary theorem whose complete rate is the volume of the specified augmented complement. Its original peripheral marking and geometric deformation are now proved as well, giving the volume identity for every sufficiently large fixed odd signed final twist in its literal closure. Every isolated pair with d+|n|>2d+|n|>2 now has a complete boundary theorem. The finite remainder of 6,369 pairs passed the common interval certificate and a separate archive-coverage check. Consequently arbitrary nonadjacent short fillings in that domain can precede sufficiently long remaining fillings. Same-sign internal 22-crossing chains are also estimated at every fixed length. The mixed pair and its mirror have now been proved eligible as one-boundary pendant pieces in the hybrid tree theorem. Arbitrary adjacent short assignments remain untreated.

1. Adjacent short vertices and the two-crossing reduction

Use the original crossing RR, physical quarter-turn TT, and cylindrical closure CC. The three-vertex path has the expression

K3(a,b,c)=C(RaT(RbT(Rc))).(1) K_3(a,b,c)=C\bigl(R^aT(R^bT(R^c))\bigr). \qquad\text{(1)}

Fix a=b=2a=b=2, and let cc vary through the odd integers. The endpoint matching gives one component: an even integral braid has the through pairing, an odd braid has the crossed pairing, and the quarter-turn preserves the crossed pairing. Thus (1) has the crossed pairing before cylindrical closure, which closes to one component. As in the finite-exception target, restrict to assignments whose resulting knot is hyperbolic. No assertion about hyperbolicity is being inferred from the quantum expression below.

The fixed vertex aa is an original leaf of power two. The fixed vertex bb has original degree two and power two. Each has its own proved whole-boundary theorem. However, they are adjacent. If uu is the angle at the remaining vertex, their exact joint polynomial is

ZN(u)=𝒟2,N(t)[H2,N(t)KN(t,u)],H2,N(t)=𝒟2,N(s)KN(s,t).(2) Z_N(u)=\mathcal D_{2,N}^{(t)} \bigl[H_{2,N}(t)K_N(t,u)\bigr], \qquad H_{2,N}(t)=\mathcal D_{2,N}^{(s)}K_N(s,t). \qquad\text{(2)}

Here KNK_N is the original complete edge polynomial and 𝒟n,Nf=ϕN(Rnf)\mathcal D_{n,N}f=\phi_N(R^nf) is the complete closing functional, including its first derivatives at repeated roots.

Expression (2) is neither an original leaf polynomial nor the degree-two singleton polynomial 𝒟2,N[KN(t,u)KN(t,v)]\mathcal D_{2,N}[K_N(t,u)K_N(t,v)]. Replacing the first kernel by the already traced function H2,NH_{2,N} changes the phase and its global estimates. The nonadjacent assembly therefore does not apply. The connected-cluster criterion also does not apply: the selected original degrees are 1,21,2, and both powers are 22.

The earlier degree-two marked simplifications concern powers 0,±10,\pm1, so they do not remove this pair. This is the smallest tree on which adjacent selected vertices can leave an unfilled boundary. Separate singleton theorems alone do not estimate (2).

A direct joint theorem for (2) is proved in the adjacent-pair boundary calculation. It uses the exact double sum, its calibrated positive geometry, the horizontal maximum, and a new full-period contour. The added lattice terms outside the original triangle are exactly zero. The lower first-variable contours consequently form complete circles; their main integrals cancel after an explicit normalization, with exponentially small errors. Combining the two vertical sides before taking absolute values retains the peripheral multiplier and removes the former diagonal-corner obstruction. The written endpoint bounds and the interval certificate account for every remaining contour term.

There is now a second proof of the closed volume conclusion. The repeated-two-crossing reduction replaces the pendant pair by a terminal power −3-3 and shifts its parent power by −1-1. The terminal theorem then applies when the remaining power is sufficiently long. Thus a joint estimate of the unreduced polynomial was not logically necessary for that closed conclusion.

More generally, a pendant chain of kk powers 2σ2\sigma, σ=±1\sigma=\pm1, becomes a terminal power −σ(k+1)-\sigma(k+1) and a parent shift by −σ-\sigma, with the actual branch rotations retained. The boundary multiplication theorem also upgrades this quotient identity to a full asymptotic for the canonical unreduced boundary polynomial. These arguments treat all same-sign chains, but they do not validate an arbitrary iteration of singleton theorems.

The mixed pair now has a separate complete analytic theorem. It estimates the original polynomial T(R−2T(R2))T(R^{-2}T(R^2)) on a fixed complex neighborhood, proves a nonzero leading coefficient, and controls the whole boundary period. The proof accounts for all terms in an exact contour identity; it does not infer this estimate from the two singleton results. The positive ideal subdivision also proves its complete volume normalization, by exact gluing and dilogarithm identities. The original word and collar proof now identifies the full marked geometric germ and proves the volume theorem for its literal long odd final closure, of either sign. The proof uses the exact original diagram meridian and disk longitude, not just a comparison of complete cusp traces. Other adjacent short powers still need a composition proof.

For comparison, neighboring zero-power vertices of original degrees at least three are now covered by their joint Newton-coordinate theorem and literal strict Conway geometry. That proof treats the combined polynomial and applies to connected sets of any size. The nonzero pair in (2) uses the separate full-period argument just described.

2. An infinite fiber on a four-vertex path

For the four-vertex path, write

K4(a,v,b,c)=C(RaT(RvT(RbT(Rc)))).(3) K_4(a,v,b,c) =C\bigl(R^aT(R^vT(R^bT(R^c)))\bigr). \qquad\text{(3)}

All powers refer to their original local crossing frames. Let τ=T2\tau=T^2 be the actual physical half-turn. The ordered rotation and closure isotopies give

τ(FG)=τ(G)τ(F),τ(TF)=Tτ(F),τ(Rr)=Rr,C(FG)≃C(GF).(4) \tau(FG)=\tau(G)\tau(F),\qquad \tau(TF)=T\tau(F),\qquad \tau(R^r)=R^r, \qquad C(FG)\simeq C(GF). \qquad\text{(4)}

In particular, the reversal of the product by τ\tau must be retained. At v=0v=0, equations (3)–(4) give an ambient isotopy

K4(a,0,b,c)=C(Raτ(RbT(Rc)))=C(RaT(Rc)Rb)≃C(Ra+bT(Rc))=K2(a+b,c).(5) \begin{aligned} K_4(a,0,b,c) &=C\bigl(R^a\tau(R^bT(R^c))\bigr)\\ &=C\bigl(R^aT(R^c)R^b\bigr)\\ &\simeq C\bigl(R^{a+b}T(R^c)\bigr) =K_2(a+b,c). \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(5)}

The first equality uses the through identity R0R^0, before applying the two turns. The last isotopy uses closed cyclicity and then concatenation of integral braids. No open tangles have been commuted, and no half-turn has been erased. These are the same physical moves used in the marked degree-two zero reduction.

The isotopy carries the main knot, its marked arcs, orientations, and any supplied ribbon. Consequently it identifies the filled knot complements and their preferred meridian and longitude, after the same orientation convention, and identifies the reduced zero-framed Kashaev invariant. A raw diagram evaluation can retain a framing factor of modulus one. Equation (5) is not a claimed homeomorphism between exteriors having two unfilled auxiliary tori and an exterior having only one: all the displayed powers are evaluated.

Proposition. Suppose the finite-assignment conclusion holds for every fixed parity pattern of the four-vertex path (3). Then every hyperbolic two-circle knot K2(s,c)K_2(s,c), at every fixed pair of integer powers s,cs,c, satisfies the original Kashaev volume identity.

Proof. Fix such a hyperbolic knot and choose a parity ϵ∈{0,1}\epsilon\in\{0,1\}. For every integer rr, take

(a,v,b,c)=(ϵ+2r,0,s−ϵ−2r,c).(6) (a,v,b,c)=(\epsilon+2r,\ 0,\ s-\epsilon-2r,\ c). \qquad\text{(6)}

This is one fixed parity pattern: the parities are ϵ,0,s−ϵ,c\epsilon,0,s-\epsilon,c modulo two. The carrier at the zero vertex is 00. Choose the original other carriers with these parities. Since nj=ηj−2pjn_j=\eta_j-2p_j, every tuple (6) is an allowed integer surgery assignment; as rr increases by one, the first surgery coefficient decreases by one and the third increases by one. Thus the assignments are distinct.

Equation (5) identifies all of them with the same hyperbolic knot K2(s,c)K_2(s,c). In particular their fixed matching is a knot matching. If that knot failed the volume identity, infinitely many assignments of this one marked four-vertex template would fail it. This contradicts the assumed finite-exception conclusion. Hence the knot satisfies the identity. The fixed pair s,cs,c was arbitrary. QED.

This proposition is an implication, not a proof of its conclusion. The proved two-circle result leaves a finite set of pairs untreated. Any one hyperbolic pair in that untreated set has infinitely many preimages in (6). It therefore cannot be discarded as finitely many assignments of the larger path. This gives a small explicit instance of the zero-cap padding issue.

The reflected even knot theorem now settles the pairs (s,c)=(−2k,2k)(s,c)=(-2k,2k) for every integer k≥2k\ge2, and their mirrors. Therefore every assignment (m,0,−2k−m,2k)(m,0,-2k-m,2k) satisfies the original volume identity. With fixed parity m=ϵ+2rm=\epsilon+2r, its original filling coefficients are (−r,0,k+ϵ+r,−k)(-r,0,k+\epsilon+r,-k). All of these infinite fibers are covered. The argument does not extend the same conclusion to an arbitrary unequal pair (s,c)(s,c).

3. What exact simplification can transfer safely

There is a useful elementary criterion. Suppose a fixed partial assignment is transformed by proved marked isotopies into a family with a cofinite theorem, and let ff be the induced map from its remaining original assignments to the new assignments. If every fiber of ff is finite, the inverse image of the finite untreated set is finite. This transfers the cofinite conclusion without any new source estimate. Alternatively, an infinite fiber is harmless when the volume identity is already proved for its knot.

A fixed degree-two power σ=±1\sigma=\pm1 has precisely the first behavior: it deletes that vertex and shifts both neighboring powers by −σ-\sigma, with the prescribed physical rotations. On the remaining parameter lattice the map is injective. A finite sequence of such proved moves can therefore transfer the two-circle cofinite theorem to a template with those extra powers already fixed. It does not prove the theorem when those powers are also arbitrary free parameters.

For example, on the three-vertex path, each fixed slice b=±1b=\pm1 reduces to K2(a∓1,c∓1)K_2(a\mp1,c\mp1). A fixed endpoint power a=σa=\sigma reduces, by closed turn transfer, to K2(b−σ,c)K_2(b-\sigma,c); the other endpoint is the same calculation from the opposite root. Thus the union of these six fixed slices has only finitely many untreated hyperbolic assignments. At a zero power the cap rules or the degree-two zero move leave an integral braid closure or an unknot, so there are no hyperbolic assignments on those zero slices. Those particular reductions do not treat the adjacent pair in Section 1; the separate two-crossing chain identity does.

A zero merger (a,b)↦a+b(a,b)\mapsto a+b has infinite fibers when both powers remain free. If one is already fixed, that particular merger is injective on the other power; otherwise finite exceptions cannot be pulled back without resolving the affected knots. The same test must be applied to deleted parameters in a cap splitting. Merely having finitely many resulting diagrams does not make the parameter fibers finite.

4. Recommendation for the next theorem

The joint polynomial (2) is now controlled, as are the same-sign chains described above. The next missing composition estimate concerns general short connected regions, including mixed-sign chains beyond the now treated pair. For that pair, both the full boundary asymptotic and its original marked geometric identification are proved, so the stated long-filling volume conclusion follows. Multiplication and fixed boundary crossings preserve the proved concentrated boundary asymptotics. Quarter-turns do not preserve that class in general: T(Hp)=RpT(H_p)=R^p for Hp=T(Rp)H_p=T(R^p), and RpR^p has unit-modulus values throughout the physical spectrum. The missing theorem must therefore use the geometry of the actual compositions, or include intermediate sources without strict concentration. More isolated estimates do not by themselves establish such a theorem.

The new fixed-domain turn theorem does give a uniform composition result when the subsequent powers are long. One threshold works for chains of arbitrary fixed length, with either sign at each step and any initial terminal power of magnitude at least two. It preserves the complete complex boundary domain and global estimates. The proof therefore removes an actual iteration problem, but does not establish the required stability under arbitrary fixed short turns. A noninitial power can still remain short in infinitely many assignments.

For the general target, the required statement is a boundary theorem stable under the actual connected partially filled sources, together with exact treatment of their degenerations. The finite-branching lemma then applies to the original assignments. Zero mergers and cap padding require additional care: the four-path proposition shows that even an unresolved finite list of two-circle knots cannot simply be left behind. Neither the independent-set assembly nor the current high-degree connected-cluster theorem supplies this general result.
