What a regular original shared stationary point actually implies

Unpublished workspace research reduction, 31 October 2026. This audits the hypotheses of the conditional signed-circle/group/fan theorem. No strong duality, concavity in the two rows, or separate weighted local maximality is assumed. The result is a first-order reduction on specified smooth strata of the original shared incidence problem, at every fixed width.

1. The exact functional being differentiated

For a LEFT port write its original limiting degrees, in order (x,b,c,y),

q=(xi,mu,1-nu,nu-mu-xi),
0<=mu<=nu<=1/2, 0<=xi<=nu-mu.                 (1)

Let alpha_delta(t)=4[cos^2(pi delta)-sin^2(pi t)] and Lambda_delta(t;x,y) be the positive root of the original cleared tail recurrence. Define

B_delta(mu,nu)=int_0^mu log|alpha_delta| dt
                        +int_nu^(1/2) log|alpha_delta| dt,
Phi_delta(mu,nu,l)=int_mu^nu log Lambda_delta(t;exp(l),1) dt,
h_delta=B_delta+inf_l[Phi_delta-xi l].          (2)

This keeps BOTH unused intervals. The omitted-half scalar has zero exponential upper modulus, but its actual two phases are not discarded when reconstructing the source. Formula (2) is the sharp interval Legendre functional, not the fractional comparison t C(alpha). The fractional obstruction specifically prevents replacing (2) by that latter concave perspective and borrowing its duality.

The global closed-domain coefficient-majorant theorem now proves a uniform coefficient LIMSUP upper bound for the original full-interval functional at both parities, including all closed degree boundaries, the final spectral cap and sampled zeros. The regular theorem identifies (2) and its CROSS split with that full-interval functional. Thus the reduction applies to regular extrema of a proved compact original-source upper problem. An every-level remote coefficient limit or nonzero prefactor is NOT needed for this upper-bound route. No lower asymptotic or equality of a signed central sum with its upper rate is asserted. Uniform coefficient coverage does not imply regularity or geometric realizability of a boundary maximizer.

On a strict ordinary chart,

0<mu<nu<1/2, 0<xi<nu-mu, 0<delta<1/2,

and neither row equals t_c=1/2-delta. The minimizing tilt in (2) is unique and finite. Indeed, put eta=cos^2(pi delta)/sin^2(pi t)>0. Apart from a t-only constant,

log Lambda(t;exp(l),1)
  =l/2+log[cosh(l/2)+sqrt(sinh^2(l/2)+eta)],
partial_l^2 log Lambda
  =eta cosh(l/2)/[4(sinh^2(l/2)+eta)^(3/2)]>0. (3)

Its first derivative ranges from 0 to 1. Thus Phi_ll>0 and its first derivative ranges from 0 to nu-mu, proving existence, uniqueness and smoothness of the tilt. The moving alpha zero has an integrable log; as long as neither endpoint meets it, B is smooth by its explicit logarithmic antiderivative. No convexity in mu or nu follows from (3).

2. Linear-target stationarity, without strong duality

Use one original row supply1 and each original ordinary face target1; protected incidences have target0 and are removed, not regularized. For CROSS retain its two half-row allocations as separate local variables. Fix a smooth branch stratum in which every remaining local inequality is strict, apart from the explicitly forced protected zeros.

At a relative-interior constrained local extremum of sum_e h_e, its differential annihilates the kernel of the LINEAR target map. Elementary finite-dimensional row-space orthogonality therefore gives face multipliers lambda_f, regardless of redundant target equations. All these multipliers are finite real numbers on this smooth finite- dimensional stratum, so L_f=exp(-lambda_f)>0 automatically. Then EACH local Lagrangian

h_e+sum_(f at e) q_(e,f) log L_f               (4)

is STATIONARY in all its free local variables, with the same L_f at every occurrence of f. This is not an assertion that (4) is maximal over its full local polytope. A finite positive multiplier vector also does not imply that every local inequality is inactive; the preceding relative-interior hypothesis is substantive.

At an ordinary LEFT port, xi stationarity and (3) give l=log(x/y), where x,y,b,c now denote these common positive weights. After this equality, (4) is exactly

F=mu log b+(1-nu)log c+int_mu^nu log L_delta(t;x,y) dt,
L_delta=Lambda_delta/|alpha_delta|.            (5)

The identity int_0^(1/2)log|alpha_delta|=0 is used only here, after retaining the actual outside products. The free row equations are

L_delta(mu)=b, L_delta(nu)=c.                 (6)

Since delta_e is independent of every shared target constraint, its first derivative at an interior spectral extremum is zero. This derivative is taken at FIXED (mu,nu,xi) in (2), before applying the tilt/envelope cancellation. At a solution of (6) it equals the partial_delta of (5). It is not differentiation along an already imposed equation c=b+(x+y)/sin^2(pi delta).

3. Classification of strict LEFT/RIGHT critical rows

Write A=x+y, P=xy, z=sin^2(pi delta), D=A^2-4Pz. The proved L_delta is increasing before t_c and decreasing after t_c, from infinity down to L_end=(A+sqrt D)/(2z). For a pre-pole stationary row q>0 use

E_q=A+2qz+2sqrt(z)*sqrt(qA+P+q^2z),
Q_q'=-log(E_q/sqrt D).

For a strict post-pole stationary row q>L_end use

H_q=2qz-A+2sqrt(z)*sqrt(q^2z-qA+P),
J_q'=-log(H_q/sqrt D).                        (7)

These are the absolutely normalized primitives of the original cofactor. Both derivatives follow from the retained outside integrals; no maximum property of the chosen row is used to differentiate its stationary branch.

There are three possibilities for mu<nu:

  • Pre/pre: (6) gives b<c. The weighted stationary action is Q_b-Q_c+log c, so its spectral derivative is log(E_c/E_b)>0.

  • Post/post: (6) gives b>c>L_end. The action is J_b-J_c+log c, and its spectral derivative is log(H_c/H_b)<0.

  • Pre/post: the action is Q_b-J_c+log c. Spectral stationarity is H_c=E_b. The two numbers are larger roots with the SAME product D, and their half-sums are A+2bz and 2cz-A. Strict monotonicity of that larger root gives exactly

    (c-b)z=A, R0=-E_b/H_c=-1.                 (8)

Thus a strict interior free-spectral LEFT critical point MUST be the calibrated straddling branch. At this point the second row derivatives of (5) are -partial_t log L(mu)<0 and partial_t log L(nu)<0; the endpoint dependence is separable. The strict start/stop maxima at FIXED delta therefore follow from (6)–(8), rather than being assumed from the shared maximum. This checks the endpoint hypothesis of the regular switching-window theorem on compact subcharts.

It does NOT make (5) a local maximum in delta: after following both stationary rows with fixed weights, this branch has a spectral minimum. Indeed sign(log(H_c/E_b))=sign((c-b)z-A) by the same larger-root comparison, and c>b at (8); as delta increases this changes from negative to positive. That row-following variation generally changes the original shared face loads and is not an admissible variation of the constrained problem. It cannot exclude a shared maximum. RIGHT is the exact original row reversal, exchanging b and c and the overloaded end.

4. CROSS and the exact protected exceptions

A regular CROSS allocation has two actual half-cofactor rows, ending at 1/2, and their retained outer split. Its split stationarity gives the same shared tilt; each free starting row solves L_delta(mu)=q. Without assuming either start to be a weighted maximum, its branch is Q_q if pre-pole and J_q if post-pole. Every one of QQ,QJ,JQ,JJ therefore has strictly NEGATIVE free spectral derivative when all displayed weights are positive, by (7). A regular CROSS port cannot have 0<delta<1/2 at a free spectral critical point. Its remaining regular possibility is the complete boundary delta=0; delta=1/2 and terminal/pole rows remain separate boundary strata.

Protected cases are specialized in the literal source BEFORE a tilt:

  • A protected LEFT overloaded c makes the whole finite sector zero.
  • If x=0 or y=0, the tail is its exact one-monomer product. Its outer degree is forced, so no finite log(x/y) tilt is used. Equations (5)–(8) still follow for the FREE rows by that monomer action, with P=0. These are the one-outer-line fan cases.
  • If b=0 with x,y>0, the start is exactly mu=0. Its sole-start primitive is Q_0, with E_0=A+2sqrt(zP). A pre-pole free stop c>0 has log(E_c/E_0)>0. A post-pole free stop is critical precisely when H_c=E_0, giving c=A/z. This is the middle-line case.
  • If b=0 and one outer is also protected, then P=0 and E_0=A=sqrt D. A strict pre-pole stop has E_c>A, and a strict post-pole stop has H_c>A. Its spectral derivative is positive in either case. Consequently there is NO free 0<delta<1/2 Green critical point with this double zero and nu<1/2. Equality forces the terminal nu=1/2, which is not covered by the regular argument.

For a CROSS half with protected middle, use Q_0. Its derivative is negative if both outers are positive; it is zero only if the half is a genuine one-positive-weight monomial. In the actual simple-white plat an outer MATCHING pair cannot be the two protected MAIN faces. There are only two protected faces, and the four port labels are distinct. Thus BOTH CROSS halves cannot be monomial simultaneously: if one outer is protected, both would additionally need both middles protected. At least one half has strict loss. Hence the preceding CROSS exclusion survives every allowed protected pattern, provided its other local rows/splits are regular. No finite cross endpoint with a forbidden zero outer unit is silently inserted.

At delta=0 a centralizer eigenratio tending to1 does NOT imply C=I: the outer product is parabolic and the collar’s removable divided difference retains a nonzero limit. No kernel or identity assertion is made from that ratio. The existing endpoint entropy/chain theory is the relevant separate input there.

4a. A one-sided nonterminal maximum cannot have delta=0

There is a stronger statement for MAXIMA, without any local weighted maximum assertion. Fix any 0<=mu<nu<1/2 and any allowed closed outer degree 0<=xi<=nu-mu. Put z=sin^2(pi delta) and restrict 0<z<cos^2(pi nu), so the entire fixed interval remains pre-pole. At any positive outer weights, q=L_delta(t) is the positive solution of sin^2(pi t)=(1-z)q2/(q2+Aq+P). Differentiating gives

partial_z log L_delta(t)
  =(q^2+Aq+P)/[(1-z)(Aq+2P)]
  >=1/[2(1-z)].                              (8a)

The inequality is exactly 2q^2+Aq>=0. It is UNIFORM in the outer tilt, not a claim restricted to the minimizing tilt. Integrating first in z and then in t yields

int_mu^nu log L_delta(t;exp(l),1)dt
  >=int_mu^nu log L_0(t;exp(l),1)dt
                   -(nu-mu)/2 log(1-z).

Since the total half-alpha integral is zero, (2) is the infimum of these integrals minus xi l. Taking that infimum preserves the same uniform additive gain:

h_delta(mu,nu,xi)
  >=h_0(mu,nu,xi)-(nu-mu)/2 log(1-z)>h_0.      (8b)

At mu=0 the logarithmic endpoint is integrable and (8a) is used on (0,nu] before taking its integral limit. At xi=0 or nu-mu the same tilt-uniform inequality applies to the limiting infimum. Alternatively the literal one-outer monomer has L=A sin^2(pi t)/(cos^2(pi t)-z), whose logarithmic z derivative is 1/(cos^2(pi t)-z)>0. Thus neither protected starts nor a closed outer-degree endpoint invalidate (8b).

Changing only this port’s delta leaves EVERY original face degree and target fixed. Consequently a maximum of the stated sharp shared functional cannot have a positive-length, nonterminal LEFT/RIGHT port at delta=0. Diagonal length0 and terminal nu=1/2 are deliberately excluded. For a fully regular maximum away from delta=1/2, every one-sided port is therefore a strict positive-spectral straddler, whereas every regular CROSS port is complete. Mixed endpoint Green ports are needed for general stationary data, but not for this regular MAXIMUM reduction.

5. Original face angles and the integer overload

At any stationary local branch just classified, the ordinary weight derivatives of its stationary action are its original degrees q. This is the ordinary envelope rule at a stationary branch, not a strong-duality assertion. The physical triangle derivative gives alpha_(t,f)=theta_(t,f)/(2pi), with each triangle load sum1/2. The signed continuation retains the original output character:

CROSS: q=alpha_input+alpha_output,
LEFT:  q=alpha_input-alpha_output+e_c,
RIGHT: q=-alpha_input+alpha_output+e_b.         (9)

The signs and integer terms come from the actual local primitive, including Log c (respectively Log b), and its prescribed continued root. They are not the fractional t alpha loads of an envelope. At a protected line its alpha corner is exactly zero, by the literal line limit, so its zero target remains unchanged.

Summing (9) at each original ordinary face yields exactly

sum_(t incident f) sigma_t theta_(t,f)
                         =2pi(1-o_f),         (10)

where o_f is the INTEGER number of incident LEFT/RIGHT overloaded middles. Every strict one-sided overload has q_f>1/2, so the target1 allows at most one, while a protected target0 allows none. Thus o_f in {0,1} on this regular stratum. Equation (10) is an automatic consequence of the original targets and first-order equations, with no independent maximization or source factorization.

6. Dependency audit: what is proved, what still is not

For the smooth stratum described here, first-order shared stationarity automatically supplies common finite positive weights, all row and tilt equations, the straddling signed primitive and R0=-1 at every positive-spectral one-sided port, complete spectral parameters at every regular CROSS port, the literal allowed single-line adapters, and the integer face-angle equations (10).

Global uniform coefficient-majorant coverage is now proved separately on the entire closed domain; neither that upper theorem nor an unnecessary remote coefficient LIMIT is a remaining hypothesis here. The following issues remain:

  1. Relative-interior regularity: finite positive ordinary multipliers do not remove ordinary zero incidences, diagonal/terminal rows, spectral endpoints, or active structural flow facets. The separate pole-endpoint exclusion removes positive-length endpoint/pole contacts from genuine maxima, without asserting that every other boundary is regular.
  2. The shared Z/2 face-character solution. The actual incidence signs must still satisfy epsilon_(e,f)=gamma_f+kappa_e on the original allowed incidence graph. The real target equations (10) do not impose these mod2 cycle conditions.
  3. A proof that inconsistent-phase cancellation beats Phi. Its positive envelope may exceed Phi, so a relative exponential loss is not by itself the desired absolute comparison.

Once the shared sign cocycle in2 is supplied on the stated regular real branches, the signed developing-disk/group dictionary uses (10) and the literal original marks to construct the comparison representation. Its original cup/cap/prefix and raw-line checks are still REQUIRED, but are supplied by that lemma rather than by an unproved new contributing-mode assertion. The new direct-fan theorem then supplies canonical volume and a uniform fixed peripheral-word gap on its stated regular branch list. Neither theorem needs local weighted strong duality. If some remaining ports instead lie at noncross delta=0, their correct endpoint tangent adapters must also be retained; one must not replace their collars by identity.

Here is the explicit mixed endpoint adapter. At a tangent delta=0 Green port the frozen original reflection calculation gives

C=I+i r(P+I),
r=(eta_c T_c-eta_b T_b)/(2(x+y)) real,

including its one-line and two-line formulas. Normalize the common tangent point to infinity and its two outer white planes to Re w=0,1. Their original raw product is P=-[[1,2],[0,1]]. Therefore C acts by w->w-2ir, preserving BOTH outer planes. The proved simultaneous head conjugations then carry the middle plane and all three ordered contacts. This establishes the same within-copy shaded pairing as the positive-spectral half-turn, with no inference from eigenratio1. Its entire peripheral image is parabolic and preserves the common horosphere. The old tangent signed local fan identity and new intersecting fan identity may therefore be summed in ONE developed family and the SAME original two-ball fundamental chain. At least one positive-spectral strict Green port still gives the fixed trace-zero meridian and uniform gap. If all spectral coordinates are0, use the existing endpoint noncross-mass gap, not a new trace-zero claim.

The retained omitted-half modes give U0=+/-pi i at (8), not zero. Their corresponding central eigenvalue choices +/-i give the SAME order-two PSL collar. Reversing its selected eigenline also leaves that PSL collar unchanged. The compact-peripheral canonical volume is independent of which fixed endpoint is used, by the proved horosphere-to-axis contraction. Thus the real comparison does not require selecting a complex contributing mode or proving a nonzero intersection with the original Fourier cycle. Those questions belong to a lower-bound/contribution theorem, not this conditional upper bound. No retained finite scalar or original framing is being deleted; their convention is distinguished from a claimed contributing saddle.

No arbitrary KKT datum is asserted to be a limit of these coherent regular solutions. This first-order classification removes a genuine local-maximality assumption but does not close the remaining global phase and compact-boundary problems.

Dependencies and verification scope

The original finite rows and retained scalars are in the four-state source identity. The weighted primitives and their derivative constants are in the LEFT envelope proof. The fractional obstruction is why no perspective concavity or local strong-duality claim is imported. The remaining face-sign criterion, regular group sewing, and direct-fan fundamental chain retain the original source and canonical volume conventions. The tangent endpoint matrix is the literal formula in marked signed development.

The global coefficient-majorant theorem supplies only its stated upper bound. These reductions do not supply a lower coefficient limit, an incompatible-phase comparison with complete volume, or regularity and geometric control of every compact-boundary maximum.
