Positive Ward measure and the coherent Cauchy tail of an original turn

Private written proof, 17 November 2026.

The source-exact Ward representative has a positive Stieltjes density on its actual spectral cut. The double physical rows are exactly orthogonal for the resulting finite-moment measure. This identifies the jump of a general turned source by an exact Plancherel formula.

More particularly, the difference between its canonical polynomial and its Ward representative is one Cauchy transform. Orthogonality turns its estimate into a sum of inverse physical rows. The estimate applies to every actual input, including a product of received sources followed by any original integer multiplication crossing. No input first normal is estimated or discarded in this step.

This controls the coherent null correction, not the Ward representative itself or a subsequent prescribed scalar.

2. The positive cut measure

For x>2x>2, set h(x)=arcosh⁡(x/2)>0h(x)=\operatorname{arcosh}(x/2)>0 and ρN(x)=N4πsin⁡θtanh⁡Nh(x)2,dμN(x)=N2ρN(x)PN(x)dx.(10) \rho_N(x)=\frac{N}{4\pi\sin\theta} \tanh\frac{Nh(x)}2,\qquad d\mu_N(x)=\frac{N^2\rho_N(x)}{P_N(x)}\,dx. \qquad\text{(10)} This is a positive measure on (2,∞)(2,\infty): PN(2cosh⁡h)=eNh(1+e−Nh)2>0. Theorem 1. On the entire slit plane, Q̃N(C)=∫2∞ρN(x)(1x−C−1x−c0)dx.(12) \boxed{\quad \widetilde Q_N(C)=\int_2^\infty \rho_N(x) \left(\frac1{x-C}-\frac1{x-c_0}\right)dx . \quad} \qquad\text{(12)} In particular Q̃N\widetilde Q_N has positive imaginary part in the upper half-plane. For completeness the normalization at infinity does not leave an unproved entire term. Use the branch with |ℜt|≤π|\Re t|\le\pi, ℑt>0\Im t>0 at large |C||C|. Since log⁡(−C)=−it+o(1)\log(-C)=-it+o(1) on this slit branch, Q̃N(C)=−bNlog⁡(−C)+ON(1)\widetilde Q_N(C)=-b_N\log(-C)+O_N(1). 3. Exact physical-row orthogonality and jump Plancherel

Theorem 2. The rows F0,…,Fj−1F_0,\ldots,F_{j-1} span all polynomials of degree below jj. Thus FjF_j is orthogonal to that whole space for a strictly positive measure on (2,∞)(2,\infty). 4. The coherent null tail and its inverse-row estimate

Theorem 3. For every actual A∈𝒜NA\in\mathcal A_N, ĤA(C)−HA(C)PN(C)=1N2∫2∞LA(x)x−CdμN(x).(21) \boxed{\quad \frac{\widehat H_A(C)-H_A(C)}{P_N(C)} =\frac1{N^2}\int_2^\infty \frac{L_A(x)}{x-C}\,d\mu_N(x). \quad} \qquad\text{(21)} The left side is interpreted by holomorphic continuation at the physical nodes. For a double row put Sj(C)=∫2∞Fj(x)x−CdμN(x). S_j(C)=\int_2^\infty\frac{F_j(x)}{x-C}\,d\mu_N(x). The polynomial (Fj(x)−Fj(C))/(x−C)(F_j(x)-F_j(C))/(x-C) has degree below jj. The estimate is for a coherent Cauchy tail. It does not estimate YAY_A and Q̃NLA\widetilde Q_NL_A separately. In particular (18) is compatible with those two channels having large compensating values away from the grid.

5. Original composition and the retained closing

Let H1,…,Hs∈𝒜NH_1,\ldots,H_s\in\mathcal A_N be any actual received sources, with their full canonical jets, and let p∈ℤp\in\mathbb Z.
