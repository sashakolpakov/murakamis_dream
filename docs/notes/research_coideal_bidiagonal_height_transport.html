Bidiagonal transport of full neighboring-height frames

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

The neighboring-height frame in P has an exact first-order update on each parity of the physical height grid. The update retains all NN coordinates. A binomial estimate controls its accumulated full Gram, including every coherent cross term, at subexponential cost across any o(N)o(N) height steps. This is a simplification of the actual source coefficients, not an evaluation of the global quartic minimum.

5. Relation to the fixed-degree problem

Equations (4) are linear in the full packet coefficients. They compare frame columns, or UsxU_sx and Us+2xU_{s+2}x for one common vector xx; they impose no relation between independently summed physical height sectors. They may be retained as auxiliary linear equations when these frames are used; they introduce quadratic squared residuals rather than higher-degree products. Eliminating these constant-coefficient frame equations is a linear substitution and preserves polynomial degree. This does not identify the simple coideal module with the confluent Casimir algebra of the original Conway quartic compiler or remove its bilinear multiplication gates.

The initialized marked angle in (9) is polynomially small. After an arbitrary original short tangle, the direction and observed scalar can change. The comparison retains the source, cap, phases and full Gram; it does not bound that later marked angle below or compare the norm rate with hyperbolic volume. No fixed GhtG_{\mathrm{ ht}} metric bound, universal braid unitarity, or finite-exception theorem follows from this note.
