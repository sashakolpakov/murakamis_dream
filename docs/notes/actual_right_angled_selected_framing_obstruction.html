The right-angled cut obstructs an all-central selected frame

Subsequent full-domain result: the full strict physical packet extends the actual ordinary amplitude and the stated small/near-fold complex bounds to fixed physical compacts, with the necessary finite-level boundary correction. The selected-factor corollary then selects every ordinary factor outside one prescribed matching-edge block. Global contraction and period estimates remain open. The argument below keeps its original domain and proof scope.

30 October 2026.

The shaded-face circle packing does give common exteriors. It does not supply a frame in which every original ordinary column is both in the proved central domain and on its selected endpoint sheet. For the literal projection Morse prefixes of a positively certified right-angled augmented plat, the following argument instead gives an obstruction to that simultaneous choice. The unfolded geometric critical germ, the actual quantum source, and the volume conjecture are not contradicted. The obstruction concerns this particular all-column physical-support/selected-sheet route.

The decisive dictionary is section 3. It retains the actual projection basepaths and the positive normalizations of the reflection lifts; an abstract circle packing without this dictionary would not prove the statement.

1. Geometric input and statement

Let Q be the labeled convex ideal right-angled polyhedron supplied by the actual matching-graph certificate. Its shaded faces are triangles; its white faces are the actual projection regions. Every edge separates the two colors, and every vertex has four alternating incident faces. The shaded triangles are paired by the actual crossing disks. Half twists retain the face quotient and primitive marks recorded in that construction.

At the horizontal cuts outside the crossing boxes use the original ordered Morse meridians H_1,…,H_m and prefixes Q_i=H_i…H_1. The exterior projection region is F_0. The two conditions in question are:

  • Every input and output triple has canonical wire logarithms in the CENTRAL domain of the framing criterion and the joint kernel theorem.
  • Every input and output lies on the source-selected endpoint sheet in the selected-local certificate, with the positive geometric orientation.

Subject to the literal prefix identification proved in section 3, NO common rank-one frame has both properties. The argument uses the weaker necessary strict-support test, not a failure peculiar to the central wedge boundary.

2. Disks of nonadjacent facets and the white-normal polyhedron

Use Lorentz signature (-,+,+,+). Choose an interior point of Q as the Klein origin, and write every outward unit facet normal as n=(n_0,n_vec). Then n_0>0. Adjacent normals have Lorentz product zero.

Distinct facets which do not share an edge have disjoint supporting planes in hyperbolic space. Here is a direct proof for this ideal right-angled setting. If two such facets share an ideal vertex, their planes are the opposite vertical planes of its rectangular horosphere link, hence are parallel. Otherwise their closed ideal boundaries are disjoint. A bounded- distance minimizing sequence between the two facets cannot escape: two bounded-distance sequences escaping to infinity have the same ideal limit, which would be a common ideal boundary point. Thus the distance is achieved at finite points. A minimizing point in a facet interior has a perpendicular joining geodesic. If it is on an edge, the neighboring facet plane is orthogonal. The tangential direction toward the other closest point lies in the outward normal cone of that edge, by minimality; but its scalar product with the same edge normal is nonpositive because the other point belongs to Q. Orthogonality makes these two statements force the tangential component to vanish. There are no finite vertices. The minimizing segment is therefore perpendicular to both supporting planes. Intersecting planes have no positive-length common perpendicular. The planes are disjoint.

The orientations face the common interior of Q, so their outward halfspaces are disjoint. Indeed, for disjoint planes the alternative orientations would put one plane strictly in the outward halfspace of the other, making its supporting inequality redundant or removing that facet of Q. Their unit normals consequently have product at most -1. In particular same-colored outward ideal disks have disjoint interiors, with tangencies only at shared ideal vertices. A finite set of these disks has a nonempty open common exterior: take a point on one boundary away from all finitely many tangencies and then move a little to its exterior.

For each WHITE face f put

v_f=n_f,vec/n_f,0,      C=conv{v_f}.                         (1)

For each SHADED face b with outward normal m_b, exactly its three neighboring white normals satisfy

m_b,vec . v_f=m_b,0.                                      (2)

Every other white normal satisfies the strict inequality < in (2), by the preceding disjoint-plane argument. Those three v_f are not collinear: their three unit normals have Gram matrix with diagonal 1 and off-diagonal -1, of determinant -4. They are linearly independent, and their positive affine normalizations are therefore not collinear. Thus they give an exposed triangular facet of C.

These triangles have exactly the dual incidence of the original checkerboard sphere: two white vertices are joined at each ideal vertex of Q, and the two shaded triangles there are its two incident facets. The cyclic links at a white vertex are the original white-face boundary cycles. Consequently the known triangles form an embedded closed connected two-manifold in the boundary of the three-dimensional convex hull C. Along every known edge both convex-hull sides are already present; at every vertex the known cyclic link is the whole link. There is no room for an extra facet. Equivalently this closed triangulated sphere is both open and closed in the boundary of C. Hence

C={ y in R^3 : m_b,vec . y <= m_b,0 for EVERY shaded b }.    (3)

This is an equality of finite Euclidean convex polyhedra. It is stronger than merely saying that C meets the unit ball. It identifies C with the intersection of the shaded inward halfspaces, extended in Klein coordinates. Its vertices are generally outside the unit ball and its edges are tangent to that ball.

Let K be the open common exterior of all shaded ideal disks. A flag in K, viewed as a unit vector on the Klein sphere, is a STRICT interior point of C. The distinction matters: an arbitrary hyperbolic geodesic need not meet C. Only the selected flags will supply its required endpoints in C.

3. The literal white-reflection prefixes

Let r_f be reflection in the white face plane f. At a horizontal Morse cut outside all crossing boxes, label the white intervals encountered from the exterior left spine by F_0,F_1,…,F_m. The original prefix loop surrounding the first i punctures is the two-white-face bigon: it crosses the projection cut in F_0 and F_i, with its upper and lower paths lying in the two opened projection balls. The horizontal cut misses every crossing-disk half sheet; the exterior spine misses them as well. Thus these paths need no unrecorded shaded-face deck transformation. Reversing the literal cut identifies its projective holonomy as

Q_i=r_(F_i) r_(F_0),
H_i=r_(F_i) r_(F_(i-1)).                              (4)

The order is the original convention Q_i=H_i…H_1, not its inverse. It can be checked in the canonical incoming disk coordinate eta, in which its punctures are 0 and 1 and its auxiliary flag is infinity. The consecutive white reflections are

r_0(eta)=-conjugate(eta),
r_mid(eta)=conjugate(eta)/(2conjugate(eta)-1),
r_1(eta)=2-conjugate(eta).

They give A=r_mid r_0, B=r_1 r_mid and P=r_1 r_0, exactly the original canonical puncture pair after eta=1/(2s+1). A cup or cap has equal first and last white regions, so (4) gives the required inverse pair. A retained half twist changes the shaded pairing and the auxiliary primitive meridian; it does not change these outside two-white-face loops. The input and output before/after a box have the same two outer white faces. Both are adjacent to BOTH shaded faces meeting at that box’s auxiliary ideal vertex.

The lift signs are also fixed. In the Hermitian Lorentz model use

M(n)=[[n_0+n_3,n_1-i n_2],[n_1+i n_2,n_0-n_3]],
H(n)=adj M(n), E=[[0,-1],[1,0]], J(n)=E conjugate(H(n)).

For an outward UNIT spacelike normal, J(n) conjugate(J(n))=I, and J(n) followed by conjugation is its plane reflection. Direct multiplication gives

tr[J(n_g) conjugate(J(n_f))]=2<n_g,n_f>.             (5)

The consecutive white planes at a main segment are tangent and face the common interior, so their product is the required trace -2 lift. The actual negative-parabolic heads therefore telescope without independent signs:

Q_i=J(n_(F_i)) conjugate(J(n_(F_0))).                (6)

Changing from a unit normal to the normalized vertex (1,v_f) rescales by n_f,0>0. Thus the linear coefficients used below have only POSITIVE relative normal factors. Arbitrary sign choices for reflections would lose this fact.

4. Selected sheets put both global flags in K

Write the common projector as Pi=v ell, ell(v)=1, and let w=ker ell. Put

w_0=r_(F_0)(v).

For a coupon whose left prefix interval is F, equation (4) gives

W_prefix v=r_F(w_0).                              (7)

Its two shaded disk circles bound the region Omega of the selected-local certificate. This is precisely the common exterior of those two outward shaded disks: at their common auxiliary vertex they become the parallel sides of the original cusp rectangle. The primitive orientation gives the positive strip between them, not either exterior complementary half-plane. Both planes are orthogonal to the adjacent white plane F. Thus r_F preserves EACH shaded halfspace and preserves Omega.

On the CENTRAL domain the proved selected input/output tests require w and W_prefix v in Omega. By (7) this is equivalent to w,w_0 in Omega. Every shaded face occurs in exactly one original disk pair, so all the tests together imply

w in K,                 w_0 in K.                 (8)

This proves the separated strip intersections are nonempty in this geometry; it does NOT yet give the coupled physical-support inequalities. The latter are what fail below.

5. Polarization forces a failed strict-support triangle

For every original prefix, its diagonal is

a_f=ell[ J(n_f) conjugate(J(n_(F_0))) v ]
   =ell[ J(n_f) conjugate(w_0) ].

Since ell has kernel w, all a_f are one common nonzero scalar times the polarized circle forms

L_n(w,w_0)=det(w,J(n)conjugate(w_0)).               (9)

These are real-linear in the Lorentz normal n. Up to complex conjugation, (9) is the Hermitian polarization w^* H(n) w_0. For a null normal with M(n)=y y^*, direct factorization gives

w^* H(n) w_0=conjugate(det(y,w)) det(y,w_0).        (10)

If w!=w_0, the simultaneous real and imaginary kernel of (9) is therefore the Lorentz two-plane spanned by the two null lifts of w and w_0. To verify that it has exactly dimension two, send these two distinct flags to the coordinate axes: the condition is the vanishing of one off-diagonal Hermitian entry. If w=w_0, the kernel is a real hyperplane containing its null lift; the following argument still applies.

By (8) and (3), those null lifts, normalized to time coordinate 1, give points of the STRICT interior of C. Thus the affine kernel of (9) meets int C. A real line in that kernel, through such a point, meets the boundary of the bounded polyhedron C. Let a boundary point lie in a shaded facet with neighboring white vertices v_0,v_1,v_2. It has barycentric coordinates lambda_i>=0, sum lambda_i=1. Linearity gives

sum_i lambda_i L_((1,v_i))(w,w_0)=0.               (11)

The unit normal n_i is n_i,0(1,v_i), with n_i,0>0. By (9), equation (11) is a nontrivial NONNEGATIVE relation among that facet’s three actual prefix diagonals a_i, after dividing each weight by n_i,0 and renormalizing their sum. Therefore

0 in conv_R{a_0,a_1,a_2}.                         (12)

But this shaded facet is one original input or output factor. Its strict physical-support criterion requires exactly the opposite of (12). If the boundary point lies on an edge or vertex, zero weights are allowed in (11), and the same contradiction follows. A vanishing diagonal would additionally violate the regular chart rather than evade the argument.

Thus the geometric oriented-strip conditions and simultaneous strict physical support cannot both hold for this literal right-angled factorization. In particular the proved CENTRAL-domain source-sheet certificate has no common frame here.

6. A generic pair has exactly two failed physical triples

The obstruction has bounded combinatorial size. For a generic pair of DISTINCT flags w,w_0 in K, its affine polarization kernel is a line through int C which meets the boundary in the interiors of exactly TWO facets. A facet’s diagonal triangle contains zero if and only if this line meets that facet: both directions follow from (9), the positive normal scalings, and barycentric coordinates. Therefore exactly two original input/output triples fail the convex-hull test, independently of the number of bridges, boxes, or white faces.

Such generic pairs exist, and one may also avoid every total-degree seam and regular-chart divisor. Indeed distinct endpoint pairs in the open set K times K parameterize an open set of hyperbolic geodesics, equivalently Klein affine lines meeting the open ball with these endpoints. Meeting any one fixed edge line of C imposes a nonzero real algebraic coplanarity condition on this four-real-dimensional line space. It cannot contain an open subset. Avoiding the finitely many such conditions makes the two boundary intersections interior to facets. A bounded convex polyhedron and an interior-crossing line have exactly two boundary points.

For a disk, put its two outer white circles at Re eta=0 and Re eta=1. If eta(w)=a+ib and eta(w_0)=c+id, its actual right flag has coordinate -c+id under the left white reflection. Up to a common scalar the two outer prefix coefficients are

a_0=-(a+c)+i(d-b),
a_2=(a+c-2)-i(d-b).

Thus Im(a_0 conjugate(a_2))=-2(d-b). The seam condition is exactly Im eta(w_0)=Im eta(w), a proper real analytic condition on ANY open subset of K times K. The strip coordinate is finite there and its imaginary part is nonconstant. A finite union of these seam loci also has empty interior. At cups and caps the two outer faces coincide, but one may additionally avoid a real ratio for the two DISTINCT adjacent white coefficients. This too is a proper algebraic condition: for any two distinct white normals, real dependence of their complex polarizations means that the kernel Lorentz plane meets their real span. In the affine chart it is the coplanarity condition for the kernel line and the line through those two white vertices, including parallel intersection at infinity. A fixed such condition has empty interior in the open space of secants. Avoiding it is stronger than, and implies, the required cup/cap canonical-log test. Finally (w,w_0) maps to the common frame by v=r_(F_0)(w_0), ell(v)=1, ker ell=w. Away from v=w this is a real analytic diffeomorphism onto an open part of the frame quotient. The finitely many proper algebraic regular-chart divisors cannot contain that open part. All the exclusions can be made simultaneously.

At every remaining facet the framing criterion therefore gives STRICT PHYSICAL support, with its canonical low or high adapter. Exactly two facets still fail that criterion. The next corollary shows that their identities can in fact be prescribed.

This is a geometric localization statement. It does not show that the other factors lie in the smaller central domain, extend the proved source-sheet equivalence to the whole physical strip, or estimate the two exceptional factors. Those distinctions prevent this corollary from being mistaken for a global amplitude theorem.

The two exceptional facets can be prescribed

Choose ANY two distinct shaded facets b_1,b_2 of C. Each supporting hyperbolic plane lies in the inward halfspace of every other shaded plane. Its whole unit-sphere circle therefore belongs to its C facet; away from the finitely many tangency points it lies in that facet’s relative interior. Equivalently, the triangular facet circumscribes this circle and its edges touch at the original ideal vertices.

Choose distinct non-tangency points r_i on the two circles. Their open chord is in int C, since its endpoints are relative-interior points of different facets, and it lies in the open unit ball. Perturb each r_i a little within its own facet, away from the circle’s center, to a point p_i OUTSIDE the closed unit ball and still in the relative facet interior. A fixed midpoint of the chord remains in the open ball by continuity. Thus the line through p_1,p_2 has two distinct sphere intersections, both strictly between the p_i and hence in int C. These sphere flags w,w_0 belong to K. The full intersection of their line with C is precisely [p_1,p_2], so its two failed facets are exactly b_1,b_2.

All these properties persist on an open set of endpoint pairs. The finite edge, seam, cup/cap, projector and regular-chart exclusions from the preceding argument can therefore be imposed as well. For an algebraic positive certificate the flags may also be chosen algebraic: the strict feasible set is open in a rational chart of the sphere-pair space, and algebraic points are dense there.

In particular take the two shaded triangles incident to the original marked physical main edge of the matching graph. They are distinct and meet at that edge’s ideal vertex in Q. The two exceptional ordinary column factors can be localized at those two vertices, without moving the mark or changing the source. One can instead choose the two halves of any designated crossing-circle matching edge. This is a geometric choice of frame; it neither deletes those factors nor proves an estimate for their exact contraction. The other factors are strictly physical, not automatically in the smaller central domain.

7. Scope and consequence

The obstruction is to a proposed sufficient condition for applying the ordinary local theorem everywhere at a geometric critical point. It does not contradict the joint kernel theorem, whose domain remains correct; the selected-local certificate remains a conditional theorem. Nor does it contradict the unfolded geometric critical scheme or its volume regulator. A geometric critical point can lie outside the strict physical chamber of some factors, and its relevant contour can require additional continuations, branch changes, normal terms, or a coherent resummation before local estimates are applied.

The generic-frame theorem avoids algebraic poles. It never supplied strict physical support or selected sheets. The present obstruction explains why those additional real inequalities cannot be obtained from the right-angled circle packing by choosing a sufficiently generic common frame.

Audit: exact outward lifts and convex polarization. The formal reflection and null-factorization identities pass. The regular ideal octahedron checks 72 exact prefix products, 78 selected endpoint pairs with exact failed-facet convex relations, 67 transverse two-facet exit checks, and 936 reflected selected halfspace checks. This finite fixture does not replace section 3’s actual Morse-basepath proof or section 2’s general facet-incidence argument.

The source conclusion is restricted to an ALL-CENTRAL selected frame; the larger physical-domain statement is geometric with the oriented strip sides imposed. No volume conjecture, unfolded critical germ, or contour outside that central route is refuted by this result.

The original marked main edge is selectable without transporting its mark. The exact fixture checks all six distinct facet pairs of the regular ideal octahedron. Central support and source estimates for the other factors and the exceptional block remain additional restrictions.
