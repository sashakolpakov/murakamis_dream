A uniform Gaussian integral for the normalized Conway minimum

23 November 2026. Research note. The manuscripts and earlier final notes remain unchanged.

The source minimum is a polynomial of degree four, although its number of variables grows with the quantum level. This permits a uniform comparison with a positive integral. In particular the inverse temperature can be chosen as N4N^4, simultaneously with the quantum level NN. The comparison keeps the Gaussian determinant, whose dimension-independent bound was proved previously.

This closes the simultaneous-limit qualification in the earlier Gaussian calculation. It does not evaluate the minimum. An absolute error of polynomial size can still exceed an exponentially small minimum, so the result does not prove a volume lower bound.

1. The original normalized source

Use the full Newton boundary spaces and the Hilbert–Schmidt budgets of the normalized source. If the fixed literal Conway expression has ss proper vertices, put D=NsD=Ns, and identify its packets with ℂD\mathbb C^D. Here N≥2N\ge2 is the quantum level. All repeated-root derivatives remain in these spaces.

Write the original normalized maps as Φv\Phi_v, and put Gv(x)=Φv((xc)c)−xv,E(x)=∥G(x)∥2+|cx|2,σ=116.(1) G_v(x)=\Phi_v((x_c)_c)-x_v,\qquad E(x)=\|G(x)\|^2+|cx|^2,\qquad \sigma=\frac1{16}. \qquad\text{(1)} The row cc is the original marked closing row at the top packet, divided by its recorded budget. No cap, phase, or framing factor is changed. Each normalized local tensor has Hilbert–Schmidt norm at most σ\sigma. The feasible set 𝒟\mathcal D is the product of the packet unit balls.

The complete source proof and the residual-Jacobian calculation give the following facts. 3. Simultaneous quantum level and temperature

Fix the literal expression, hence ss. Take τN=N4\tau_N=N^4. For all sufficiently large NN, the radius condition in (6) holds. Its error satisfies δN≤928((Ns+1)log⁡τN)3/2τN+116((Ns+1)log⁡τN)2τN=Os((log⁡N)3/2N).(15) \delta_N \le\frac{9\sqrt2}{8} \frac{((Ns+1)\log\tau_N)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\tau_N}} +\frac1{16} \frac{((Ns+1)\log\tau_N)^2}{\tau_N} =O_s\left(\frac{(\log N)^{3/2}}{\sqrt N}\right). \qquad\text{(15)} In particular, with the same eNe_N and gNg_N as in (2)–(3), ZN(N4)=e−N4eNgN(1+o(1)).(16) \boxed{\qquad Z_N(N^4)=\frac{e^{-N^4e_N}}{g_N}\,(1+o(1)). \qquad} \qquad\text{(16)} The error tends to zero as NN and τN\tau_N grow together. More generally (7) applies to any temperature sequence satisfying its radius condition, with a relative error tending to zero whenever (D+1)3(log⁡τ)3/τ→0(D+1)^3(\log\tau)^3/\tau\to0.

If BNB_N is the unchanged product of all source budgets, the original comparison remains BN2eN≤|KN|2≤γBN2eN.(17) B_N^2e_N\le |K_N|^2\le\gamma B_N^2e_N. \qquad\text{(17)} Thus (16) gives a controlled positive integral for this particular minimum. It does not give its value from the geometry. For example, (8) estimates eNe_N with an absolute error (δN+4ϵN)/N4(\delta_N+4\epsilon_N)/N^4 after the determinant is retained. Such an error need not be small relative to eNe_N. If eNe_N is exponentially small, the determinant term and this absolute error cannot be omitted when extracting its rate. No nonzero geometric coefficient, arbitrary-short-filling lower bound, or finite-exception volume theorem follows from (16).
