Twist-knot identification and exact peripheral certificates

Research note, 25 October 2026. This supplements the uniform geometric and matrix proof in twist_family_audit.md. The ordered face data come from Ben Aribi–Guéritaud–Piguet-Nakazawa, arXiv:1903.09480v5, Figures 7–14 and 17–23. We reconstruct the local topological operations below. We do not use their identification of a preferred longitude, geometricity theorem, or quantum asymptotic theorem as premises.

There are two different kinds of evidence here. The local constructions and the formulas with arbitrary p give the arguments for the whole family. The exact script and Regina recognition at the end are independent finite diagnostics of the transcribed data. Finite recognition alone would not establish an infinite-family knot identification.

1. Ordered triangulations and their marked extensions

Use p>=0, f_0=e_1, and the face tuples and signs from Section 1 of the geometric note. In particular,

T_j=(e_j,e_(j+1),f_j,f_(j-1)),
U=(r,v,s,g),
odd:  V=(g,s,f_p,u), W=(u,r,v,e_(p+1)),
even: V=(s,g,f_p,u), W=(u,v,r,e_(p+1)).

Every face pairing maps the three vertices in increasing order. The ideal triangulation is denoted X_p. Its compact marked extension Y_p is obtained by adding

Z=(m,m,s,s'),

changing V’s face 1 from s to s’ in the odd case, or U’s face 2 from s to s’ in the even case. The marked knot edge is Z_23. Pairing the two m faces uses the full vertex permutation (01); it fixes 2 and 3. These conventions also cover p=0, without any chain tetrahedron.

1.1 Reconstruction from the twist diagram

Let K_n be the twist knot with a two-crossing clasp and an alternating n-crossing twist region, with the mirror convention fixed by the oriented diagram in Figures 7 and 17 of the source. Thus

odd: n=2p+3,       even: n=2p+2.

In particular K_2 is 4_1, K_3 is 5_2, and K_4 is 6_1. The parameter n is not the total crossing number of this reduced diagram: that number is n+2.

Here is a reconstruction of the diagram-to-face-data argument. It is useful to distinguish actual local changes of a decomposition from any quantum move identity; only topology is involved in this subsection.

  1. Put vertices at the middle of the diagram arcs, with one additional vertex on the clasp arc, and draw a quadrilateral around each crossing. The two knot arcs at that crossing are opposite edges of an embedded crossing tetrahedron. Replace this block by an interval: the two knot edges become its ends, while its four lateral edges become the interval. The local quotient has the same regular neighborhood as the original block, with the four exterior sectors in the same cyclic order. One can see this by shrinking transverse disk sections inside a collar and expanding the adjacent collar by the same amount. Reversing the operation expands the interval back to the crossing block and restores the two knot arcs. Thus the resulting quotient and its marked curve describe the same pair (S^3,K_n). The crossing information is retained in the cyclic attachment of the exterior sectors.

  2. After these disjoint local replacements, the regions above and below the projection are two balls. Their boundary face pairs are D,E,m,r,s. The arrow identifications are read by following one sector of each crossing: successive arrows in the long twist region alternate. Glue the two balls along the disk E. The result is one ball with paired faces D,m,r,s and one surviving marked knot edge. This is a disk gluing of two balls, rather than an assumption about a face-pairing quotient.

  3. The repeated reduction in the twist region is a disk cut and regluing. Split a paired face F into a residual face and a triangle; isolate the adjacent ball with two triangular faces and one bigon; rotate this ball and reglue it along the triangular disk; then merge the residual face with the bigon. Each operation has its inverse, and the rotating ball misses the marked knot edge. This proves preservation of the pair. The effect on the boundary is to replace two successive twist edges by one long edge and move the triangle u upward by one level. These are exactly the labeled attachments in source Figure 10. Repeat p times.

  4. In the odd case the residual pair of (p+2)-gons G is triangulated by a fan. The successive layers have boundary face tuples (e_j,e_(j+1),f_j,f_(j-1)). The clasp region has four tetrahedra; reading the faces opposite 0,1,2,3 gives

    U=(r,v,s,g), V=(g,s',f_p,u),
W=(u,r,v,e_(p+1)), Z=(m,m,s,s').

    This reads the common triangular face at each layer, so it is unchanged when an additional level is added to the fan. For p=0 the fan is absent and the last two boundary faces have the common name f_0=e_1.

  5. In the even case the same p disk reductions leave the opposite clasp attachment. Separate off the ball along the quadrilateral formed by the two adjacent G faces and change its diagonal. Both diagonals triangulate the same ball relative to its exterior boundary. The fan still has the preceding T_j tuples. Reading the residual clasp gives

    U=(r,v,s',g), V=(s,g,f_p,u),
W=(u,v,r,e_(p+1)), Z=(m,m,s,s').

    This is the diagonal change visible in source Figure 21. For p=0 the intervening bigon is collapsed in its collar, so the same tuples with f_0=e_1 apply. No negative or zero number of layers is used.

All the face maps in this reading preserve the increasing order of their three vertices. The signs in the geometric note follow by orienting the ball and each tetrahedron. This gives a construction of Y_p as the marked pair (S^3,K_n), rather than an inference from its homology or from a finite list of recognized manifolds.

1.2 Why removing Z gives the knot complement

The quotient of Z by its m pairing is a ball. Its unpaired faces 2 and 3 are two disks meeting along their common equator 01: in each disk the two radial sides have been identified. Their centers are 3 and 2 respectively. The edge 23 is the unknotted proper arc joining these centers. Removing a small neighborhood of that arc gives the product between the two punctured disks. Absorbing this product into a collar identifies the punctured disks.

The equator vertices map 0->0, 1->1 and the centers map 3->2. Composing with the two external face maps gives precisely the increasing vertex map between the two s faces in X_p, in both parities. Thus drilling the marked edge and deflating this product gives X_p, with no change to the remaining tetrahedra. In particular X_p triangulates S^3 minus K_n.

The same local description explains the topology of the cusp. The vertex link in Y_p is a sphere; deleting the two marked-edge germs and adjoining the tube around the open edge makes a torus. The computation in Section 3 uses this description to identify the meridian without a linking-number guess.

2. A separate uniform homology check

Orient each dual face edge from its first to its second occurrence in the ordered tetrahedron list. A spanning tree in the dual graph is

e_2,...,e_(p+1),r,s.

After contracting it, the generators are e_1,f_1,…,f_p,v,g,u. All edge relations below are abelianized boundaries of the actual dual edge disks; each is determined by crossing the consecutive faces incident to the edge. For p>=1 put f_0=e_1. The relations, up to reversing individual disk orientations, are

f_j-f_(j-1)=0                         (1<=j<p),
f_p-f_(p-1)-g+v=0,
g-v-f_p=0,
-v-g=0,
sum_(j=1)^p f_j+u+2e_1=0.                              (2.1)

These formulas hold in both parities. The first two relations involving f_p add to -f_(p-1)=0. Back substitution then gives

e_1=f_1=...=f_(p-1)=0,
v=-g, f_p=2g, u=-2g.

Every elimination has a unit integral pivot. There is no remaining relation on g and no torsion; hence H_1(X_p;Z)=Z. For p=0 the three relations are

-v-g=0, v-2e_1+u-g=0, v-g-e_1=0,

and give v=-g,e_1=-2g,u=-2g, again with no relation on g.

The single dual 1-chain g-s before tree contraction will be the meridian. Thus its class is the displayed generator, not a multiple of it. Homology by itself would not identify this class with the meridian of the knot; that requires the next local argument.

The chain T_1,…,T_p by itself is also a useful diagnostic. For p>=1 it is a layered solid torus with top faces 1 and 2. If A is its boundary edge 01/23 and B is 02/13, their images in its H_1 are a and (p+1)a; edge 03 has image (p+2)a. For p=1 the two face relations give B=2A and 03=3A. Adding a layer leaves A unchanged, sends the new B to the previous 03, and sends the new 03 to A plus that previous 03. This proves the formulas inductively. Its meridian slope is (p+1)A-B and its three unoriented meridional cuts are (1,p+1,p+2). This solid-torus description does not assert that the fixed three-tetrahedron clasp piece is a two-cusped link exterior.

3. The actual meridian, from the marked edge

A cusp normal walk is listed as (tetrahedron, vertex, exit face). The entry face is determined by the preceding step. Define

odd:  mu=((U,1,3),(V,2,1)),
even: mu=((U,1,3),(V,2,0)).                             (3.1)

Thus U_1 enters through s and leaves through g; V_2 returns through s. This is a simple two-arc curve. Its dual 1-chain is g-s.

To verify that it is a meridian, lift it to the spherical vertex link of Y_p before drilling. Insert one arc in Z_1 at the s crossing. The resulting closed three-arc walks are

odd:  ((U,1,3),(V,2,1),(Z,1,2)),
even: ((U,1,3),(V,2,0),(Z,1,3)).                       (3.2)

They separate the two germs of the marked edge Z_23. Here is a constant-size certificate that can be checked directly from the face tuples. One side contains the three whole link triangles

odd:  U_3,W_3,Z_3,       even: U_3,W_3,Z_2,

and, in both parities, the following corner regions of the three cut triangles:

U_1: corner 0;
V_2: the region containing corners 0 and 1;
Z_1: corner 0.

There are thirteen local corners in this region: nine in whole triangles and four in the cut pieces. Each non-cut edge is matched within the region; its only boundary is (3.2). In the odd case it contains germ (Z,3;2) but not (Z,2;3); in the even case it contains (Z,2;3) but not (Z,3;2). All these triangles are in the fixed clasp block; no chain tetrahedron enters the certificate. On the sphere, the simple curve (3.2) therefore bounds a disk containing just one marked-edge germ. After the two germs are removed and the tube is attached, it is precisely a meridian. Deflation gives (3.1).

This proves the meridian claim independently of the source cusp drawing.

4. A preferred longitude without a diagrammatic linking count

The following auxiliary simple normal walks beta stay in the fixed clasp block. Each row is (tetrahedron, vertex, exit face), read cyclically:

Step Odd beta Even beta
1 (U,0,1) (U,0,1)
2 (W,0,1) (W,0,2)
3 (U,1,2) (U,1,2)
4 (V,2,3) (V,2,3)
5 (W,3,1) (W,3,1)
6 (U,3,2) (U,3,2)
7 (V,3,0) (V,3,1)
8 (U,2,0) (U,2,0)
9 (W,2,0) (W,1,0)
10 (V,1,0) (V,0,1)

The cusp is oriented by the oriented complement. Direct normal-arc intersection gives

mu . beta = +1 (odd), -1 (even).                       (4.1)

For an entirely rational check, place the vertices of each cusp triangle at (0,0),(1,0),(0,1) in increasing order. Put mu’s endpoints one-third of the way along each side and beta’s endpoints two-thirds of the way along it. The increasing face maps match these positions across triangles. Count the transverse interior segment intersections with triangle orientation -epsilon_t(-1)^v. This evaluates (4.1) using only the two triangles visited by mu. It also avoids coincident-arc conventions.

Their dual face chains are

odd:  [beta]=-r+v+2s-2g,
even: [beta]=2s-2g.

Using (2.1), beta has homology class -3[mu] in the odd case and -2[mu] in the even case. Define on the peripheral torus

odd:  lambda=beta+3mu,
even: lambda=beta+2mu.                                 (4.2)

There is a direct integral boundary certificate, with no division or specialization. The dual disk at U_01 has oriented face word

s u v^(-1) r u^(-1) g^(-1)

in the odd case. Its abelian boundary is s-v+r-g. Consequently the odd lambda chain -r+v-s+g is minus this boundary. In the even case lambda’s dual chain is already zero. Thus both classes in (4.2) are null-homologous in the complement. Equation (4.1) makes each lambda primitive. Since mu is the actual meridian, these are preferred longitudes, with the orientation specified by (4.1). This argument replaces a crossing-sign linking count.

5. Angular branches and the exact longitude rows

Let a,b,c be the local edge angles, in radians, assigned to opposite edge pairs 01/23,02/13,03/12. For a normal arc at (t,v) entering face i and leaving face j, let k be the unused vertex. Its signed corner-angle contribution is

epsilon_t (-1)^v sign((i,j,k) relative to the sorted other vertices)
    times the angle at edge vk.                       (5.1)

This follows by orienting the vertex link triangle and following its oriented incoming and outgoing sides. It fixes the signs rather than assigning an unsigned sum to a normal walk. Applying it gives

h_mu = a_U-a_V                    (odd),
h_mu = a_V-a_U                    (even),

h_beta = -b_U-c_U-a_V-b_V+c_V+2a_W+c_W   (odd),
h_beta = a_V-b_V+c_V+a_W-b_W-c_W         (even).

Put T_t=a_t+b_t+c_t and let

omega_s=2a_U+b_V+c_V+a_W+b_W.

The target longitude formulas are

h_lambda=2(a_U-a_V+c_V-b_W)        (odd),
h_lambda=2(-a_U+a_V-b_V+a_W)       (even).               (5.2)

These follow from (4.2) with exact constraint certificates:

odd:  (5.2)-(h_beta+3h_mu)=T_U+2T_V-T_W-omega_s,
even: (5.2)-(h_beta+2h_mu)=-T_V+T_W.

On the balanced angle space T_t=pi and omega_s=2pi, both right sides are exactly zero. In particular no unnoticed multiple of 2pi is needed to match the source formula. This is stronger than equality modulo angular holonomy.

For the shape convention in the geometric note, a,b,c correspond to z,z’‘,z’ at positive tetrahedra and z,z’,z’’ at negative ones. Using T_W=pi in the odd case and T_V=pi in the even case converts (5.2) to

h_lambda=a_l Arg z+b_l Arg z''-2pi,

with chain entries zero and the U,V,W rows

odd:  a_l=(2,-2,2),  b_l=(0,2,2),
even: a_l=(-2,4,2),  b_l=(0,2,0).                      (5.3)

Thus the longitude rows and the branch constant nu_l=2pi used in the geometric and Gaussian certificates are now independently identified. The natural spectral boundary coordinate x=y_V-y_U has angular meridian a_U-a_V in both parities. It reverses mu in the even case; its intersection with the chosen preferred longitude is consequently +1 in both cases.

6. Consequence and the remaining scope

For every fixed p>=0 in both parities, the uniform topological identification, the independently identified peripheral rows, and the geometric certificates in the companion note apply to the same literature integral. Therefore the state defined by FAMED equation (4.5), with the explicit spectral rescaling and b-shift convention in general_annihilator_and_volume.md, has a primitive minimal boundary annihilator and satisfies

lim_(b->0+) -2pi hbar log|J_(X_p)(hbar,0)|
      = Vol(S^3 minus K_n),
hbar=b^2/(1+b^2)^2.                                    (6.1)

Its classical symbol contains the geometric peripheral component in the recorded eigenvalue-ratio/lift convention. The limiting state normalization is fixed by the literature integral. This is a statement for these explicit literature realizations. The identification of all triangulations or all purported standard knot states requires the global quantum transformations with their generic peripheral factors and contours. It does not follow from the topological disk operations used in Section 1. No equality of the entire classical symbol with the full A-polynomial is inferred here.

The all-alternating-knot extension also remains open in this program; the positive geometric and matrix certificates have been supplied here for the twist families. A Menasco decomposition alone does not supply those certificates or an exact quantum move identity.

Run the exact cellular and normal-curve checks with

python3 experiments/twist_topology_audit.py

and the optional independent 3-manifold recognition with

.venv/bin/python experiments/twist_topology_audit.py --regina

The latter uses Regina 7.4.1 and SymPy 1.14. It recognizes Y_p as S^3, checks X_p has one ideal torus cusp and H_1=Z, recognizes Z as a ball, and checks the layered-solid-torus cuts for p=1,…,8. Both modes check the eighteen cases p=0,…,8 in both parities against the uniform homology, marked-germ, intersection, integral-boundary and zero-branch certificates. The source figures specify the input drawings; the local reconstruction and the all-p certificates, rather than these finite checks, justify the family statement.
