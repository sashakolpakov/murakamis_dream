Positive boundary transport gives the cube rate on an anchored grid

22 November 2026. Research note. Earlier final notes and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The original four-coherent scalar has a noncancelling geometric asymptotic on a continuous family of half-grid boundary paths. An exact three-input source initializes a positive recurrence. Its growth exponent equals the action of the actual ideal cube. This is a source estimate, with its original closing and phase, rather than an estimate for a norm or for a formal critical value.

The result requires the displayed anchor and stops a fixed distance before the boundary fold. Generic grid offsets and pair-wall starts are not included.

1. Source, geometric domain and theorem

Use the full algebra of the four-boundary source theorem. Put N≥2,d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,κ=eπid2/(2N),Ua=∑ℓ=0daℓfℓ,(1) N\ge2,\quad d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad \kappa=e^{\pi i d^2/(2N)},\quad U_a=\sum_{\ell=0}^d a^\ell f_\ell , \qquad\text{(1)} where the centered basis is orthonormal in the positive Newton coefficient norm. All repeated-root Hermite coordinates, original products, turns and Frobenius closings remain present. Define ΦN(y,z;s,t)=ϕ(UyUzUsUt).(2) \Phi_N(y,z;s,t)=\phi(U_yU_zU_sU_t). \qquad\text{(2)}

Write S=b+cS=b+c. Our parameter domain is b,c>0,S<1,1−S<R<1−max⁡(b,c),0<h<R/2.(3) \begin{gathered} b,c>0,\qquad S<1,\qquad 1-S<R<1-\max(b,c),\\ 0<h<R/2 . \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(3)} For fixed b,c,Rb,c,R, put y=−e−2πib,z=−e−2πic,r=e−2πiR,hj=(j+12)/N,kj=R−hj,sj=−q−1Q−j,tj=−qrQj.(4) \begin{gathered} y=-e^{-2\pi ib},\quad z=-e^{-2\pi ic},\quad r=e^{-2\pi iR},\\ h_j=(j+\tfrac12)/N,\quad k_j=R-h_j,\quad s_j=-q^{-1}Q^{-j},\quad t_j=-qrQ^j . \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(4)} Thus sj=−e−2πihjs_j=-e^{-2\pi ih_j}, tj=−e−2πikjt_j=-e^{-2\pi ik_j}, and sjtj=rs_jt_j=r. The entries j=−1,0j=-1,0 will initialize transport.

Let L(x)=∫0xlog⁡|2sin⁡πv|dvL(x)=\int_0^x\log|2\sin\pi v|\,dv, with its odd, one-periodic extension. On its strict multiplication domain set m(x,y,u)=L(x+y)+L(u)−L(x)−L(y)−L(u−x)−L(u−y)−L(x+y−u).(5) m(x,y,u)=L(x+y)+L(u)-L(x)-L(y) -L(u-x)-L(u-y)-L(x+y-u). \qquad\text{(5)} For k=R−hk=R-h, define Ih=(max⁡(b,c,1−R),min⁡(S,1−h,1−k)),ℱ(b,c,R,h)=maxu∈Ih{m(b,c,u)+m(h,k,1−u)}.(6) \begin{split} I_h&=\bigl(\max(b,c,1-R),\ \min(S,1-h,1-k)\bigr),\\ \mathcal F(b,c,R,h)&= \max_{u\in I_h}\{m(b,c,u)+m(h,k,1-u)\}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(6)} Every quadruple in (3) has all six pair sums below one and total S+R>1S+R>1. The positive cube theorem therefore gives a unique strict interior maximizer and an actual convex ideal cube of volume πℱ\pi\mathcal F. Its usual second action m(u,h,1−k)m(u,h,1-k) equals m(h,k,1−u)m(h,k,1-u) by L(1−x)=−L(x)L(1-x)=-L(x).

Theorem. For every compact 𝒦\mathcal K in (3), there are constants C𝒦,N𝒦C_{\mathcal K},N_{\mathcal K} such that, whenever N≥N𝒦N\ge N_{\mathcal K}, j≥0j\ge0, and (b,c,R,hj)∈𝒦(b,c,R,h_j)\in\mathcal K, ΦN(y,z;sj,tj)=e−πid(S+R)PN,j,PN,j>0,|log⁡PN,j−Nℱ(b,c,R,hj)|≤C𝒦log⁡N.(7) \boxed{\quad \Phi_N(y,z;s_j,t_j)= e^{-\pi i d(S+R)}\,P_{N,j},\qquad P_{N,j}>0,\qquad |\log P_{N,j}-N\mathcal F(b,c,R,h_j)| \le C_{\mathcal K}\log N . \quad} \qquad\text{(7)} The assertion includes both parities and composite levels. The original normalized closing of the two turned products is ΦN/N2\Phi_N/N^2, since all four coherent inputs have norm N\sqrt N. It has the same exponential rate. In particular, on convergent shape sequences in this compact domain, πN−1log⁡|ΦN/N2|\pi N^{-1}\log|\Phi_N/N^2| tends to the volume of this cube. No knot-volume conclusion is asserted.

2. The exact carrier and the two source initial values

The common phase in (7) is an exact source property, not a choice of a positive replacement. Here is a verification from the full coefficients in the four-boundary theorem. For a unit pair of angles v,wv,w, write U−e−2πivU−e−2πiw=∑ℓBℓeℓU_{-e^{-2\pi iv}}U_{-e^{-2\pi iw}}=\sum_\ell B_\ell e_\ell. The original four-input contraction is ∑ℓBℓ(y,z)Bd−ℓ(s,t)\sum_\ell B_\ell(y,z)B_{d-\ell}(s,t). The exact unit and socle readouts give ΦN(y,z;s0,t0)=κN𝒞N(y,z,t0),ΦN(y,z;s−1,t−1)=κ2NFN(y)FN(z)FN(t−1),FN(a)=∑ℓ=0d(−qa)ℓ.(11) \begin{split} \Phi_N(y,z;s_0,t_0) &=\kappa\sqrt N\,\mathcal C_N(y,z,t_0),\\ \Phi_N(y,z;s_{-1},t_{-1}) &=\frac{\kappa^2}{N}F_N(y)F_N(z)F_N(t_{-1}),\qquad F_N(a)=\sum_{\ell=0}^d(-qa)^\ell . \end{split} \qquad\text{(11)} Here t0t_0 has angle RN=R−1/(2N)R_N=R-1/(2N), whereas t−1t_{-1} has angle R+1/(2N)R+1/(2N). The triple b,c,RNb,c,R_N ranges in a compact strict prism family. The initialized three-input theorem therefore applies with all radii equal to one.

For completeness, its calibrated phase fixes the sign of the initial value. Let AN=S+RNA_N=S+R_N, and denote its action and amplitude by Ψ3,B3\Psi_3,B_3, with the canonical branches of that theorem. The elementary unit-circle dilogarithm formulas give ℜΨ3=π(1−AN),arg⁡B3=−3π/4+πAN,−ℑΨ3=M(b,c,RN),M(b,c,R)=m(b,c,1−R)>0.(12) \begin{split} \Re\Psi_3&=\pi(1-A_N),\qquad \arg B_3=-3\pi/4+\pi A_N,\\ -\Im\Psi_3&=M(b,c,R_N),\qquad M(b,c,R)=m(b,c,1-R)>0 . \end{split} \qquad\text{(12)} Indeed ℑLi⁡2(e−2πix)/(2π)=L(x)\Im\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-2\pi ix})/(2\pi)=L(x). Including both κ\kappa’s, the complete initial phase is κ2eiπ/4−πi/(2N)ei(−3π/4+πAN)eiNπ(1−AN)=ωN.(13) \kappa^2e^{\,i\pi/4-\pi i/(2N)} e^{\,i(-3\pi/4+\pi A_N)}e^{\,iN\pi(1-A_N)} =\omega_N . \qquad\text{(13)} The initialized relative error is O𝒦(N−1)O_{\mathcal K}(N^{-1}). Its nonzero amplitude is uniformly bounded above and below. Because H0H_0 is exactly real, (11)–(13) imply, uniformly, H0>0,log⁡H0=NM(b,c,R)+O𝒦(log⁡N),|H−1|≤C𝒦/N.(14) H_0>0,\qquad \log H_0=N M(b,c,R)+O_{\mathcal K}(\log N),\qquad |H_{-1}|\le C_{\mathcal K}/N . \qquad\text{(14)} The last bound follows from the geometric sums in (11): their denominators stay away from zero on this compact angle family. 3. Positive transfer before the fold

The exact source equation, with σ(s,t)=(Qs,Q−1t)\sigma(s,t)=(Qs,Q^{-1}t), is B(σΦN−ΦN)+D(σ−1ΦN−ΦN)=λNΦN,λN=(Q−1)(1−Qyz),(15) B(\sigma\Phi_N-\Phi_N)+D(\sigma^{-1}\Phi_N-\Phi_N) =\lambda_N\Phi_N,\qquad \lambda_N=(Q-1)(1-Qyz), \qquad\text{(15)} B=(t+q)2(1−Qys)(1−Qzs)(t−s)(t−Qs),D=(s+q)2(1−Qyt)(1−Qzt)(Qt−s)(t−s).(16) B=\frac{(t+q)^2(1-Qys)(1-Qzs)}{(t-s)(t-Qs)},\qquad D=\frac{(s+q)^2(1-Qyt)(1-Qzt)}{(Qt-s)(t-s)}. \qquad\text{(16)} On (4), σ\sigma replaces jj by j−1j-1. Put ρj=𝖻j/𝖽j\rho_j=\mathsf b_j/\mathsf d_j. 4. The rate integral and the actual cube action

Define the classical ratio ρ0(h)=sin⁡2π(R−h)sin⁡π(b+h)sin⁡π(c+h)sin⁡2πhsin⁡π(b+R−h)sin⁡π(c+R−h).(25) \rho_0(h)= \frac{\sin^2\pi(R-h)\sin\pi(b+h)\sin\pi(c+h)} {\sin^2\pi h\sin\pi(b+R-h)\sin\pi(c+R-h)}. \qquad\text{(25)} In the product of (19), its last factors telescope to sin⁡π(R−2j/N)/sin⁡πR\sin\pi(R-2j/N)/\sin\pi R, bounded above and below. All remaining factors are smooth log-sine Riemann sums except the explicit denominator sin⁡(π(m+1)/N)\sin(\pi(m+1)/N) at zero. On this interval, log⁡sin⁡πv=log⁡v+\log\sin\pi v=\log v+ a smooth function. Integral comparison for log⁡(j!)\log(j!) bounds its Riemann error by O(log⁡N)O(\log N). The bounded shifts of the other arguments cost O𝒦(1)O_{\mathcal K}(1). Moving the upper integration limit from j/Nj/N to hjh_j costs at most O(log⁡N)O(\log N) after multiplication by NN. Hence ∑m=0j−1log⁡ρm=N∫0hjlog⁡ρ0(v)dv+O𝒦(log⁡N).(26) \sum_{m=0}^{j-1}\log\rho_m =N\int_0^{h_j}\log\rho_0(v)\,dv +O_{\mathcal K}(\log N). \qquad\text{(26)}

We next identify this primitive with the cube, rather than only with a formal boundary characteristic. Let uu be the unique critical point in (6), and set k=R−hk=R-h. 5. Scope and complete proof sources

Only sjs_j, not a generic boundary parameter, lies on the anchored half-grid. Fixed stst shifts preserve a fractional grid offset. This proof does not control arbitrary offsets, a boundary layer connecting them to the anchor, or pair-wall initializations when R≥1−max⁡(b,c)R\ge1-\max(b,c). It does not cross the fold h=R/2h=R/2 or estimate its pole-cancelled normal readout. Symmetry does not automatically cover h>R/2h>R/2: the smaller parameter need not lie on this anchored grid after the swap.

The theorem proves a nonzero original source coefficient and its geometric rate on the stated universal cell family. It does not assert a full output approximation, arbitrary Conway composition, or a finite-exception knot-volume theorem. All coefficients and carriers come from the original full-Hermite scalar.
