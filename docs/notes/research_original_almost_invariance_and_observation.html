Almost-invariant original vectors and their marked observations

An almost-invariant vector can give a lower bound for a specified source if its observation at the original closing row is also controlled. The finite Newton representation makes that requirement quite explicit. It has genuine geometric Casimir quasimodes whose overlaps with both original cap directions are exponentially small. For the forced three-crossing operator there is an exact adjoint quasimode whose endpoint overlap loses precisely the entire gain from its small residual.

There is also a natural self-adjoint form of the adjacent three-crossing problem. Its inertia is known exactly, and the closing scalar is an off-diagonal Green entry. Thus this change of form retains the original source without turning it into a positive norm.

All statements below concern the full original finite algebra, including first normals at double roots. They do not contradict unitarity of the different ordinary-color representations.

1. Original operators and the unitarization obstruction

Fix N≥2N\ge2, D=N−1D=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, Q=q2Q=q^2. Use the original normalized Newton basis of 𝒜N=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2). {\mathcal{ A}}_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2). Write CC for multiplication by zz, MM for the original positive crossing, and JJ for index reversal. Their exact relations include (Cv)j=cjvj−djvj−1,cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N),dj=4sin⁡2(πj/N),J2=I,C†=JCJ,M†JM=J.(1) \begin{split} (Cv)_j&=c_jv_j-d_jv_{j-1},\\ c_j&=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N),\qquad d_j=4\sin^2(\pi j/N),\\ J^2&=I,\qquad C^\dagger=JCJ,\qquad M^\dagger JM=J . \end{split} \qquad\text{(1)} The missing lower-end term is zero. These are identities on the entire original quotient. The positive Newton inner product is the ordinary coefficient inner product. The two original cap observations are e0†𝒇=f(c0),eD†𝒇=ϕN(f).(2) e_0^\dagger\mathbf f=f(c_0),\qquad e_D^\dagger\mathbf f=\phi_N(f). \qquad\text{(2)} Both rows have norm one. The first is the original turned cap, equivalently evaluation at the first physical Casimir node; the second is the unturned closing functional.

The crossing is not unitary for a different positive inner product. At a double root cjc_j, its restriction to the original two-dimensional local algebra is Mj=λj(I+Kj),Kj2=0,Kj≠0,|λj|=1.(3) M_j=\lambda_j(I+K_j),\qquad K_j^2=0,\quad K_j\ne0, \qquad |\lambda_j|=1 . \qquad\text{(3)} For example, in the basis 1,ϵ1,\epsilon, with ϵ=z−cj\epsilon=z-c_j, its nonzero nilpotent entry is R′(cj)R(cj)=i(D−2j)4sin⁡((2j+1)π/N).(4) \frac{R'(c_j)}{R(c_j)} =\frac{i(D-2j)}{4\sin((2j+1)\pi/N)} . \qquad\text{(4)} The pair j=0,Dj=0,D is double and has nonzero entry at every N≥2N\ge2. Formula (4), including its original normalization, is proved in the arbitrary-power compilation, Section 6.

If H>0H>0 satisfied M†HM=HM^\dagger HM=H, then H1/2MH−1/2H^{1/2}MH^{-1/2} would be unitary and hence diagonalizable. This contradicts (3). The same argument excludes making CC self-adjoint while retaining its double-root Jordan blocks.

There is a more precise conclusion for an actual positive average of crossing transports.

Proposition 1. Let G1,…,GrG_1,\ldots,G_r be original invertible word matrices with |det⁡Gi|=1|\det G_i|=1, with positive weights summing to one. Suppose the list contains MM. The completely positive average 𝒫(ρ)=∑ipiGiρGi†(5) {\mathcal{ P}}(\rho)=\sum_i p_iG_i\rho G_i^\dagger \qquad\text{(5)} has no positive definite stationary trace metric: there is no H>0H>0 satisfying 𝒫†(H)=H{\mathcal{ P}}^\dagger(H)=H. In particular this applies to the symmetric average of M,M−1,JM,M^{-1},J.

Proof. Put Ai=H−1/2Gi†HGiH−1/2A_i=H^{-1/2}G_i^\dagger HG_iH^{-1/2}. Each Ai>0A_i>0, det⁡Ai=1\det A_i=1, and ∑piAi=I\sum p_iA_i=I. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for its eigenvalues gives tr⁡Ai≥N\operatorname{tr}A_i\ge N, with equality only at Ai=IA_i=I. Taking traces of the average forces equality for every ii. In particular M†HM=HM^\dagger HM=H, which (3) excludes. ▫\square

This does not exclude a Perron rescaling, a degenerate stationary form, or a different source-adapted unitary operator. Such a change must retain its scaling and the original test row; (5) is not already a trace-preserving quantum channel in a faithful positive metric.

Even the simplest symmetric crossing average retains the normal obstruction explicitly. On the invariant local plane (3), use any positive inner product and its adjoint. Its restriction to positive matrices is 12{MjρMj†+Mj−1ρMj−†}=ρ+KjρKj†.(6) \tfrac12\{M_j\rho M_j^\dagger+ M_j^{-1}\rho M_j^{-\dagger}\} =\rho+K_j\rho K_j^\dagger. \qquad\text{(6)} The added map is nonzero and square-zero. Its mm-th iterate is ρ+mKjρKj†\rho+mK_j\rho K_j^\dagger. Thus this actual positive average has a Jordan direction at eigenvalue one, rather than a mixing gap to a stationary projector. Passing to the semisimple quotient would remove precisely that original normal direction.

2. A geometric quasimode invisible to the original caps

Fix k≥2k\ge2, and let αN=M2ke0,aN=αN/∥αN∥2.(7) \alpha_N=M^{2k}e_0,\qquad a_N=\alpha_N/\|\alpha_N\|_2. \qquad\text{(7)} The reflected volume theorem and the fixed-insertion theorem give ∥(C−zk)aN∥2→0,limN→∞1Nlog⁡∥αN∥2=Vk4π>0,(8) \begin{split} \|(C-z_k)a_N\|_2&\longrightarrow0,\\ \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\|\alpha_N\|_2 &=\frac{V_k}{4\pi}>0, \end{split} \qquad\text{(8)} where zk=−2cos⁡tkz_k=-2\cos t_k is the actual geometric core value and VkV_k is the volume of the original closed reflected knot. See the Casimir insertion theorem and the reflected volume theorem.

This is an original source vector and an original geometric approximate eigenvalue. The small overlaps are not obtained by choosing an artificial orthogonal test row. In particular any fixed collection of Casimir observations at the first cap has the exact observation Gram matrix ∑ℓ=0m(C†)ℓe0e0†Cℓ=(∑ℓ=0m|c0|2ℓ)e0e0†.(15) \sum_{\ell=0}^{m}(C^\dagger)^\ell e_0e_0^\dagger C^\ell =\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}|c_0|^{2\ell}\right)e_0e_0^\dagger. \qquad\text{(15)} It cannot provide full-space coercivity.

The same example already exists on the doubled positive space. Put ρN=aNaN†\rho_N=a_Na_N^\dagger, and use the actual Casimir transport 𝒞zk(ρ)=|zk|−2CρC†.(16) {\mathcal{ C}}_{z_k}(\rho) =|z_k|^{-2}C\rho C^\dagger. \qquad\text{(16)} If ϵN=∥(C−zk)aN∥\epsilon_N=\|(C-z_k)a_N\|, then ∥𝒞zk(ρN)−ρN∥1≤2ϵN|zk|+ϵN2|zk|2→0.(17) \|{\mathcal{ C}}_{z_k}(\rho_N)-\rho_N\|_1 \le\frac{2\epsilon_N}{|z_k|} +\frac{\epsilon_N^2}{|z_k|^2} \longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(17)} This follows by expanding the two rank-one products; ∥xy†∥1=∥x∥∥y∥\|xy^\dagger\|_1=\|x\|\|y\|. Nevertheless tr⁡(e0e0†ρN)=exp⁡{−NVk/(2π)+o(N)}.(18) \operatorname{tr}(e_0e_0^\dagger\rho_N) =\exp\{-NV_k/(2\pi)+o(N)\}. \qquad\text{(18)} Map (16) is completely positive; it has not been asserted to preserve trace or to possess a mixing gap. Doubling preserves positivity but does not repair the specified observation.

3. The exact adjoint test for a forced boundary source

For an invertible original operator ANA_N and its specified source ANfN=ζNeD,|ζN|=1,(19) A_Nf_N=\zeta_N e_D,\qquad |\zeta_N|=1, \qquad\text{(19)} every trial vector vv gives ∥fN∥≥|vD|∥AN†v∥.(20) \boxed{\quad \|f_N\|\ge \frac{|v_D|}{\|A_N^\dagger v\|}. \quad} \qquad\text{(20)} Indeed ζNvD¯=⟨AN†v,fN⟩\zeta_N\overline{v_D} =\langle A_N^\dagger v,f_N\rangle. For normalized trials, an exponentially small adjoint residual is useful only after comparing its rate with that of vDv_D.

The original free polynomial is FN(w)=𝒆(w)TfNF_N(w)=\mathbf e(w)^Tf_N, where 𝒆(w)=(e0(w),…,eD(w))T\mathbf e(w)=(e_0(w),\ldots,e_D(w))^T is the full Newton evaluation column. If AN†v=𝒆(w)¯+rN, A_N^\dagger v=\overline{\mathbf e(w)}+r_N, then the exact identity and its useful lower bound are FN(w)=ζNvD¯−⟨rN,fN⟩,|FN(w)|≥|vD|−∥rN∥∥fN∥.(21) \begin{split} F_N(w)&=\zeta_N\overline{v_D}-\langle r_N,f_N\rangle,\\ |F_N(w)|&\ge |v_D|-\|r_N\|\,\|f_N\|. \end{split} \qquad\text{(21)} Thus a dual approximate solution can prove the specified lower bound without positive unitarization. It must retain the actual endpoint observation and make the residual smaller than that observation after multiplication by a proved upper bound for ∥fN∥\|f_N\|.

The following exact original operator shows why the residual alone is insufficient. Put T=C−q𝖷,𝖷=diag⁡(Qj),hj=qj(j+2)Sj2.(22) T=C-q\mathsf X,\qquad \mathsf X=\operatorname{diag}(Q^j),\qquad h_j=q^{j(j+2)}S_j^2. \qquad\text{(22)} The exact three-crossing equation gives Th=q−1e0,T†=JTJ.(23) Th=q^{-1}e_0,\qquad T^\dagger=JTJ. \qquad\text{(23)} For v=Jh/∥h∥v=Jh/\|h\|, therefore, ∥T†v∥=1∥h∥,|vD|=1∥h∥.(24) \|T^\dagger v\|=\frac1{\|h\|},\qquad |v_D|=\frac1{\|h\|}. \qquad\text{(24)} These are exponentially small: the uniform sine-factorial estimate, with 𝒥(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πs)ds\mathcal J(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi s)\,ds, gives limN→∞1Nlog⁡∥h∥=2max0≤x≤1𝒥(x)=2𝒥(5/6)>0.(25) \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\|h\| =2\max_{0\le x\le1}\mathcal J(x) =2\mathcal J(5/6)>0 . \qquad\text{(25)} The bound (20) for the endpoint-forced equation Tx=eDTx=e_D is exactly one. That is sharp: x=q−1eD,∥x∥=1.(26) x=q^{-1}e_D,\qquad \|x\|=1. \qquad\text{(26)} Indeed TT is lower bidiagonal and TDD=qT_{DD}=q. This does not replace the original adjacent-pair operator by TT; it demonstrates, within its actual original factor, the exact loss from the wrong endpoint overlap.

4. The natural self-adjoint form of the adjacent pair

For the actual pendant JM3JM3e0JM^3JM^3e_0, retain the exact factorization from the boundary-current theorem: AN=BSTS−1,S=JM3J,ANfN=ζNeD,det⁡AN=−(−1)D.(27) A_N=B S T S^{-1},\qquad S=JM^3J,\qquad A_Nf_N=\zeta_Ne_D,\qquad \det A_N=-(-1)^D. \qquad\text{(27)} Here fN=JM3JM3e0f_N=JM^3JM^3e_0, κ=exp⁡(πiD2/(2N))\kappa=\exp(\pi iD^2/(2N)), and ζN=(−1)Dκ2q−1\zeta_N=(-1)^D\kappa^2q^{-1}. Here TT is (22), and the original upper bidiagonal matrix BB has Bjj=−qQj,Bj,j+1=1−Qj+1.(28) B_{jj}=-qQ^j,\qquad B_{j,j+1}=1-Q^{j+1}. \qquad\text{(28)} All absent entries are zero. In particular B†=JBJ,S†=JS−1J.(29) B^\dagger=JBJ,\qquad S^\dagger=JS^{-1}J . \qquad\text{(29)}

The original matrix HN=ANBJ(30) H_N=A_NBJ \qquad\text{(30)} is Hermitian and invertible, with inertia (n+,n−,n0)=(⌊N/2⌋,⌈N/2⌉,0).(31) (n_+,n_-,n_0) =(\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\lceil N/2\rceil,0). \qquad\text{(31)} The unturned original closing scalar is exactly ϕN(FN)=(fN)D=−ζNq−1e0†HN−1eD.(32) \phi_N(F_N)=(f_N)_D =-\zeta_Nq^{-1}e_0^\dagger H_N^{-1}e_D. \qquad\text{(32)} The full free-boundary source is FN(w)=ζN𝒆(w)TBJHN−1eD.(32a) F_N(w)=\zeta_N\mathbf e(w)^T BJH_N^{-1}e_D . \qquad\text{(32a)}

Proof. Relations (23), (27), and (29) give the exact congruence HN=(BS)(TJ)(BS)†.(33) H_N=(BS)(TJ)(BS)^\dagger. \qquad\text{(33)} Since T†=JTJT^\dagger=JTJ, the middle matrix is Hermitian. At λ=0\lambda=0, paired indices j,D−jj,D-j give Hermitian blocks with zero diagonal and conjugate off-diagonal entries of modulus one. Each has eigenvalues 1,−11,-1. If NN is odd, the unpaired central entry is q−1Q−(N−1)/2=q−N=−1q^{-1}Q^{-(N-1)/2}=q^{-N}=-1. This proves the inertia of TJTJ, and (33) gives the same inertia for HNH_N.

Finally AN−1=BJHN−1A_N^{-1}=BJH_N^{-1}, and the last row of BB is −q−1eD†-q^{-1}e_D^\dagger. Applying this to (19) proves (32); applying the complete Newton evaluation row gives (32a). ▫\square

The Hermitian matrix in (30) is built from the original finite source operators. No geometric projector has been substituted. Its fixed inertia precludes a positive-energy interpretation of its inverse. Formula (32) is an off-diagonal Green entry; it can involve cancellations between positive and negative spectral subspaces. For this turned pendant, its particular unturned cap is already simple: (fN)D=κ3(M3e0)D. (f_N)_D=\kappa^3(M^3e_0)_D. This follows from the first row of M3M^3. Its endpoint asymptotic is known. It must not be confused with the unresolved retained-boundary value FN(−2)F_N(-2), whose actual Green formula is (32a). That formula has a boundary-dependent left observation, not a matching positive quadratic observation. For the negative pendant, conjugate the original matrices and source, as in the boundary-current theorem. The congruence, inertia and conjugated closing identity hold with that same convention.

5. What remains to obtain a lower bound

The rational geometric curve of the adjacent (−3,−3)(-3,-3) pendant is now identified with its original peripheral germ in the geometric branch theorem. That identifies a candidate phase for a local adjoint construction. It does not supply an adjoint vector with exponentially accurate residual and a controlled overlap at row DD. The end rows, the repeated-root directions and the phase through the intervening indices still have to be matched.

Equations (20)–(21) state a precise sufficient target. A local frozen-symbol root or a first-order bulk approximation does not yet verify it. The sharp counterexamples (9) and (24) show that the overlap condition cannot be omitted even for actual geometric or exponentially accurate original quasimodes.

The reflected Stein equation remains a useful positive special case: its observation Gram matrix has the proved polynomial lower bound in the original Stein-energy theorem. There the source itself is the matching nonnegative observation sum. The retained adjacent-pair boundary value (32a) has a different observation, which that theorem does not estimate.

6. Focused finite replay

The original-matrix checker constructs ANA_N from its band entries and independently constructs the literal source from M,JM,J. At levels 2,…,102,\ldots,10, for both crossing signs, it compares the Hermitian congruence, inertia, endpoint equations, and the complete Green formula (32a), including first normals. The boundary tests include −2-2, a nonreal point and the repeated physical node c0c_0. It also checks the exact residual/overlap equality in (24) and the norm-one solution (26).

The saved 100-digit record retains the script, note and original-matrix dependency hashes. An independent output can be selected with –𝚘𝚞𝚝𝚙𝚞𝚝\texttt{--output}. The finite eigenvalue checks are diagnostics; the all-level inertia is the nonsingular Hermitian-path proof in Theorem 3. No source-asymptotic rate or uniform spectral gap is inferred from these computations.
