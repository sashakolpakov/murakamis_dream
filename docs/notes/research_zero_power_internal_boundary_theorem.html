A zero crossing power with at least three neighboring tangles

Research proof, 2 November 2026. This note proves a whole-boundary asymptotic at every original vertex of degree d≥3d\ge3 whose crossing power is zero. The degree is fixed before the quantum level tends to infinity. The argument uses the full Fourier circle. Its vertical endpoint currents vanish exactly at zero crossing power, which removes the need for an endpoint estimate on a short angular interval. The frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

1. The original polynomial and its selected action

Use the complete Newton polynomials and trace

ek(z)=Sk−2∏j=0k−1{2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N)−z},Sk=∏j=1k2sin⁡(πj/N), e_k(z)=S_k^{-2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1} \{2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N)-z\},\qquad S_k=\prod_{j=1}^k2\sin(\pi j/N),

3. A strict gap for the omitted wire indices

First suppose d≥4d\ge4, and put Δd=dM∞−Md\Delta_d=dM_\infty-M_d. The action-gap lemma proves

0<Δd<M∞(d≥4). 0<\Delta_d<M_\infty\qquad(d\ge4).

For clarity, this uses the explicit zero-power branch hd(λ)=2arsinh⁡tan⁡(πλ/d)h_d(\lambda)=2\operatorname{arsinh}\tan(\pi\lambda/d). Its integrated action loss decreases strictly with dd, and the exact degree-three loss is M∞M_\infty. Thus

ηd:=Md−(d−1)M∞=M∞−Δd>0.(12) \eta_d:=M_d-(d-1)M_\infty=M_\infty-\Delta_d>0. \qquad\text{(12)}

Choose rational cutoffs 0<α<β<1/20<\alpha<\beta<1/2, sufficiently close to zero and one half, that xd∈(α,β)x_d\in(\alpha,\beta) and

supx∈[0,α]∪[β,1/2]2g(x)<ηd/2,g(x)=J(2x)−4J(x). \sup_{x\in[0,\alpha]\cup[\beta,1/2]}2g(x)<\eta_d/2, \qquad g(x)=J(2x)-4J(x).

Such cutoffs exist because gg is continuous and zero at both endpoints. Retain exactly the wire indices k/N∈I=[α,β]k/N\in I=[\alpha,\beta] in every edge kernel. The complete Hermite norm estimate applies before the trace and gives, for all omitted assignments together,

|omitted contribution|≤CNAexp⁡N(Md−ηd/2),(13) |\text{omitted contribution}| \le CN^A\exp N(M_d-\eta_d/2), \qquad\text{(13)}

uniformly for all real parent angles. One omitted low wire has rate at most ηd/2\eta_d/2; a high wire has rate zero; and every other wire has rate at most M∞M_\infty. There are polynomially many assignments for fixed dd. This includes the first normal derivatives and does not presume positivity of multiplication in the quotient algebra.

At d=3d=3, the coarse gap in (12) is zero, so a different argument is necessary. The degree-three discarded-wire proof establishes the following stronger statement: for every fixed interval I⊂(0,1/2)I\subset(0,1/2) containing x3=arctan⁡(2)/πx_3=\arctan(2)/\pi in its interior, all omitted assignments together have a uniform real-boundary bound

|omitted contribution|≤CNAeN(M3−δI)for some δI>0,M3=2M∞.(13a) |\text{omitted contribution}|\le CN^A e^{N(M_3-\delta_I)} \qquad\text{for some }\delta_I>0, \qquad M_3=2M_\infty. \qquad\text{(13a)}

Its two ingredients are exact finite identities. Two high indices give zero in the full quotient algebra. For a term with one high index, the positive Newton multiplication law couples its coefficient to the two low parent factors; their continuous joint rate is strictly below 2M∞2M_\infty on its entire compact support. For an all-low triple, write the product of its three Newton polynomials as ∑mameimt\sum_m a_me^{imt}. All ama_m are nonnegative, and the full trace is exactly N2(aN−1−aN+1)N^2(a_{N-1}-a_{N+1}). Positivity of the Newton multiplication then gives the upper bound N2e−(N−1)h3∑mamemh3N^2e^{-(N-1)h_3}\sum_ma_me^{mh_3}. Strict concavity of the three resulting real wire phases supplies the gap whenever one index is outside II. This retains the first normal derivatives and all real parent angles. The cited proof includes the uniform endpoint estimates and the compactness argument.

Thus for every fixed d≥3d\ge3 we have an interval II with xdx_d in its interior and a strict whole-source omitted-wire gap. These are the only properties of the discarded terms used below.

7. Global boundary bounds and geometric normalization

Equation (19) proves a real-parent angular loss for the retained trace. The omitted contribution from Section 3 has a fixed gap independently of the real parents. Since ∑j(1−cos⁡uj)≤2d\sum_j(1-\cos u_j)\le2d, decreasing the angular constant absorbs that contribution as well. This proves (5) first for real parents.

Separate evenness gives ∂td/∂uj(0)=0\partial t_d/\partial u_j(0)=0. The stationary boundary Hessian is consequently the explicit parent Hessian of the edge phase, which is (6). The local holomorphic expansion therefore gives a quadratic bound on a small complex parent neighborhood. Away from that neighborhood, the real angular gap persists on a sufficiently thin complex strip by the polynomial Bernstein–Walsh estimate on a finite cover by products of real spectral intervals. Each boundary degree is at most N−1N-1. Combining those two estimates proves (5).

The selected action is exactly (3): the physical trace has no crossing chirp and CN,0=1C_{N,0}=1, and the unit-charge factor in (17) is e−iNte^{-iNt}. The isolated-vertex geometric proof identifies this same stationary action along the original positive primitive cone path, starting with 2d2d regular ideal octahedra. Its Schläfli derivative is 2π2\pi times the real action derivative. Thus the complete partially filled star contributes volume 2πMd2\pi M_d, with its original peripheral curves and absolute normalization. No additional phase or additive volume constant occurs.

Accordingly these degree-d≥3d\ge3 zero-power vertices may be used as singleton selected components in the further-filling theorem of the connected-cluster note. The resulting statement still requires all remaining powers to be independently sufficiently long, unless their own joint boundary polynomials have also been established.

8. The degree-three value and the remaining scope

The additional omitted-wire argument is essential at degree three; the elementary Hermite estimate alone has no strict gap there. With (13a), however, the same full-circle proof applies. The explicit normalization (21) and the action-gap identity give

M3=2M∞,A3(0)=116,P3,0,N(0,0,0)=e2NM∞16N3(1+O(N−1)). M_3=2M_\infty,\qquad A_3(0)=\frac1{16},\qquad P_{3,0,N}(0,0,0) =\frac{e^{2NM_\infty}}{16N^3}(1+O(N^{-1})).

The theorem proves the entire three-boundary polynomial near zero and its global boundary bound, not only this value at complete parents. It makes no assertion that the finite polynomial is exactly the square of an edge kernel.

At degree two and zero power, the selected cone branch degenerates at unit charge; its exact algebraic and marked tangle reduction is a separate result. Connected sets of adjacent low-degree short fillings also require their joint boundary estimates. The present theorem supplies one zero-power vertex of any degree at least three, with every neighboring boundary left free.
