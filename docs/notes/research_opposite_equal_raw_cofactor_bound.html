A uniform opposite-equal-face bound for the original cleared cofactor

31 October 2026. analytical research theorem. This concerns the original raw cleared cofactor R_j, not only its complete determinant. It is a signed evaluation at x=-C,b=C,y=Z>=0, uniform over every real spectral value. No original shared-source Phi_D bound or filled-invariant conclusion is asserted.

1. Statement and exact source

Put theta=pi/N, J=floor((N-1)/2), and fix a nonnegative integer k. Set j=J-k, restricting to N large enough that j>=0. Use the original

ar=2+2cos⁡((2r+1)θ),sr=2sin⁡(rθ),u=−(σ+2). a_r=2+2\cos((2r+1)\theta),\qquad s_r=2\sin(r\theta), \qquad u=-(\sigma+2).

The cleared cofactor R_j is defined by R_0=1 and

Rj=bj∏r<j(ar+u)+sj2(x+y)Rj−1+sjsj−1(aj−1+u)xyRj−2. R_j=b^j\prod_{r<j}(a_r+u) +s_j^2(x+y)R_{j-1} +s_js_{j-1}(a_{j-1}+u)xyR_{j-2}. \tag{1}

with the last term absent at j=1. This is exactly the finite source in original cleared cofactor source section 1. Every original inhomogeneous term is retained.

For C in a fixed compact subinterval of (0,infinity) and Z in a fixed compact subinterval of [0,infinity), the claim is

limsupN→∞supC,Z−4≤u≤0{1Nlog|RJ−k(−C,C,Z;u)|−12logmax(C,Z)}≤0. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\ \sup_{\substack{C,Z\\-4\le u\le0}} \left\{\frac1N\log|R_{J-k}(-C,C,Z;u)| -\frac12\log\max(C,Z)\right\}\le0. \tag{2}

The logarithm at a zero is -infinity. Both level parities are included. Equivalently there is a uniform o(N) upper error in (2). A fixed finite set of k can be included uniformly.

On the spectral fold u(delta)=-4sin^2(pi delta), polynomial Bernstein also gives

|RJ−k(−C,C,Z;u(δ))|≤exp⁡[N(12logmax(C,Z)+o(1))+2πj|Imδ|]. |R_{J-k}(-C,C,Z;u(\delta))| \le \exp\left[N\left(\frac12\log\max(C,Z)+o(1)\right) +2\pi j|\operatorname{Im}\delta|\right]. \tag{3}

The zero third face is included; C stays bounded away from zero. The real estimate is the mathematical point; (3) is only its exact degree-j spectral extension.

2. Exact nodal adapter and a uniform positive tail estimate

At the LOWER physical nodes u_a=-a_a, 0<=a<=j, the original source adapter gives

Rj(−C,C,Z;ua)=S2aDa(−C,C,Z)Ka,j(−C,Z;ua),Sr=∏v=1rsv. R_j(-C,C,Z;u_a)=S_{2a}D_a(-C,C,Z)K_{a,j}(-C,Z;u_a), \qquad S_r=\prod_{v=1}^r s_v. \tag{4}

This is original nodal cofactor adapter section 1, equations (3)-(4), specialized without taking coefficientwise absolute values. Here K_(a,a)=1, K_(a,a-1)=0 and the tail satisfies the original homogeneous two-step recurrence.

Write B=|Z-C| and H=CZ. On the actual half-range a<r<=j<=J, a_a-a_(r-1)>=0. A fixed sign change by sign(Z-C)^(r-a), when Z!=C, therefore turns K into the nonnegative continuant

La=1,La−1=0,Lr=prLr−1+qrLr−2,pr=sr2B,qr=srsr−1(aa−ar−1)H. \begin{aligned} L_a&=1,\qquad L_{a-1}=0,\\ L_r&=p_rL_{r-1}+q_rL_{r-2},\\ p_r&=s_r^2B,\qquad q_r=s_rs_{r-1}(a_a-a_{r-1})H. \end{aligned} \tag{5}

Thus |K|=L. When Z=C the same statement follows directly: odd-length terms are zero, and even-length terms are nonnegative. In particular no absolute value has been inserted into an oscillatory sum of two different signs.

Both p_r and q_r are nondecreasing in r on this ACTUAL range: s_r,s_(r-1) increase before the fold, and a_(r-1) decreases. The first q_(a+1) is zero. Add a FIXED epsilon>0 to B in p_r; this only increases every L_r. Let p_repsilon=s_r2(B+epsilon) and define its local Perron root

λrϵ=prϵ+(prϵ)2+4qr2>0. \lambda_r^\epsilon =\frac{p_r^\epsilon+\sqrt{(p_r^\epsilon)^2+4q_r}}2>0.

These roots increase with r. For T_r=[[p_r^epsilon,q_r],[1,0]], the vector v_r=(lambda_repsilon,1)T is a positive right eigenvector. Since v_(r-1)<=v_r componentwise, induction from (1,0)^T gives

Lj≤λjϵλa+1ϵ∏r=a+1jλrϵ≤CϵN2∏r=a+1jλrϵ. L_j\le \frac{\lambda_j^\epsilon}{\lambda_{a+1}^\epsilon} \prod_{r=a+1}^j\lambda_r^\epsilon \le C_\epsilon N^2\prod_{r=a+1}^j\lambda_r^\epsilon. \tag{6}

For the empty interval K_(a,a)=1, no prefactor is needed. The bound uses lambda_(a+1)^epsilon =s_(a+1)^2(B+epsilon)>=c epsilon/N^2, while lambda_j^epsilon is bounded above on the parameter compact. It therefore includes a=0, a near the fold, and B=0. There is no hypothesis of distinct tail eigenvalue moduli or a nonzero asymptotic seed.

For alpha in [0,.5] define the continuum root

Λϵ(t;α)=2sin⁡(πt)[(B+ϵ)sin⁡(πt)+(B+ϵ)2sin⁡2(πt)+4CZ(sin⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πα))],α≤t≤12. \begin{aligned} \Lambda_\epsilon(t;\alpha) =2\sin(\pi t)\Bigl[ &(B+\epsilon)\sin(\pi t)\\ &+\sqrt{(B+\epsilon)^2\sin^2(\pi t) +4CZ\bigl(\sin^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\alpha)\bigr)} \Bigr], \qquad \alpha\le t\le\tfrac12. \end{aligned} \tag{7}

In fact the exact finite roots are bounded DIRECTLY by (7) at t=r/N, alpha=a/N. The p terms agree, while

qr=16CZsin⁡(rθ)sin⁡((r−1)θ)[sin⁡2((r−12)θ)−sin⁡2((a+12)θ)]≤16CZsin⁡2(rθ)[sin⁡2(rθ)−sin⁡2(aθ)]. \begin{aligned} q_r &=16CZ\sin(r\theta)\sin((r-1)\theta) \left[\sin^2((r-\tfrac12)\theta) -\sin^2((a+\tfrac12)\theta)\right]\\ &\le16CZ\sin^2(r\theta) \left[\sin^2(r\theta)-\sin^2(a\theta)\right]. \end{aligned}

Every sine argument is in the increasing half-range; the exact bracket is nonnegative for r>=a+1. Increasing the nonnegative q increases the Perron root, proving the asserted upper bound. The positive addition epsilon also supplies the uniform lower bound Lambda_epsilon(t;alpha)>=4epsilon sin^2(pi t). Consequently monotone Riemann upper sums in (6) give

limsupN→∞supa,C,Z{1Nlog|Ka,J−k(ua)|−∫a/N1/2logΛϵ(t;a/N)dt}≤0. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\ \sup_{a,C,Z} \left\{ \frac1N\log|K_{a,J-k}(u_a)| -\int_{a/N}^{1/2}\log\Lambda_\epsilon(t;a/N)\,dt \right\}\le0. \tag{8}

Here and below an empty tail has logarithm zero. For completeness, log Lambda_epsilon is increasing in t. Its right-Riemann sum at r/N differs from the integral by O_epsilon(log N), uniformly in a, before division by N: the first cell is controlled by 4epsilon sin^2(pi t)<=Lambda_epsilon<=C_epsilon, and all remaining cell discrepancies telescope. The fixed terminal defect k omits only O(1) fold cells, with the same bound. Thus no half-step or uncontrolled moving-alpha replacement is needed in (8).

As epsilon decreases to zero, these tail integrals decrease to

Itail(α;C,Z)=∫α1/2log⁡Λ(t;α)dt,Λ=Λ0. I_{\mathrm{tail}}(\alpha;C,Z) =\int_\alpha^{1/2}\log\Lambda(t;\alpha)\,dt, \qquad \Lambda=\Lambda_0. \tag{9}

Their limit is finite and continuous jointly on the compact parameter set and alpha in [0,.5], including Z=0. To see a uniform integrable lower bound, if B>=C/2 then Lambda>=2C sin^2(pi t); otherwise CZ>=C^2/2. These two cases give

Λ(t;α)≥csin⁡(πt)sin⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πα). \Lambda(t;\alpha)\ge c\sin(\pi t)\sqrt{\sin^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\alpha)}.

with c>0 uniform on the stated compact. In particular B=0 has

Λ(t;α)=4CZsin⁡(πt)sin⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πα). \Lambda(t;\alpha) =4\sqrt{CZ}\sin(\pi t) \sqrt{\sin^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\alpha)}.

whose moving endpoint logarithm is integrable; the identity sin^2(pi t)-sin^2(pi alpha) =sin(pi(t-alpha))sin(pi(t+alpha)) gives uniform integrable logarithmic bounds, including alpha=0,.5. Together with a uniform upper bound, these logarithmic bounds prove continuity also as B tends to zero or Z tends to zero. Compact monotone convergence of the continuous integrals therefore makes epsilon->0 UNIFORM. Equations (6)-(8) prove (8) with epsilon=0. This is an upper estimate only; a zero odd-length tail causes no difficulty.

3. The exact integral cancellation

For C>0 and Z>=0 set

S=C+Z,ρ=4CZS2,β=|Z−C|S=1−ρ,α∈[0,12]. S=C+Z,\qquad \rho=\frac{4CZ}{S^2},\qquad \beta=\frac{|Z-C|}{S}=\sqrt{1-\rho}, \qquad \alpha\in[0,\tfrac12].

Define, with integrable endpoint values,

I(α)=∫02αlog⁡(2sin⁡(πt))dt+∫0αlog⁡|Z+Ce2πit|4cos⁡2(πt)dt+∫α1/2log⁡Λ(t;α)dt. \begin{aligned} I(\alpha) ={}&\int_0^{2\alpha}\log(2\sin(\pi t))\,dt\\ &+\int_0^\alpha \log\frac{|Z+Ce^{2\pi it}|}{4\cos^2(\pi t)}\,dt +\int_\alpha^{1/2}\log\Lambda(t;\alpha)\,dt. \end{aligned} \tag{10}

Then EXACTLY

I(α)=12log⁡max⁡(C,Z),0≤α≤12. I(\alpha)=\frac12\log\max(C,Z), \qquad 0\le\alpha\le\frac12. \tag{11}

Here Lambda is (7) with epsilon=0 and its ACTUAL scale; no undetermined normalization constant remains.

Proof first suppose 0<rho<1 and 0<alpha<.5. Put v=sin^2(pi alpha), h=sqrt(1-rho v), beta=sqrt(1-rho)>0. The derivatives of the first two integrals and the moving lower endpoint of the third add to

log⁡(h/β). \log(h/\beta).

Indeed Lambda(alpha;alpha)=4|Z-C|v, and |Z+C exp(2pi i alpha)|=S h. For the remaining derivative, first differentiate with respect to v and then put u=cot(pi t):

∫α1/2∂vlog⁡Λ(t;α)dt=−ρ2π∫0cot⁡(πα)duA(u)(β+A(u)),A(u)=1−ρv−ρvu2. \begin{aligned} \int_\alpha^{1/2}\partial_v\log\Lambda(t;\alpha)\,dt &=-\frac{\rho}{2\pi} \int_0^{\cot(\pi\alpha)} \frac{du}{A(u)(\beta+A(u))},\\ A(u)&=\sqrt{1-\rho v-\rho v u^2}. \end{aligned} \tag{12}

Substitute u=sqrt((1-rho v)/(rho v)) sin theta. At the upper endpoint cos theta_*=beta/h. The elementary tangent-half-angle integral is

∫0θ*dθβ+hcos⁡θ=log⁡(h/β)ρ(1−v). \int_0^{\theta_*}\frac{d\theta}{\beta+h\cos\theta} =\frac{\log(h/\beta)}{\sqrt{\rho(1-v)}}.

Thus (12), multiplied by dv/dalpha=2pi sqrt(v(1-v)), equals -log(h/beta). This cancels the moving-endpoint contribution exactly, so I’(alpha)=0.

At alpha=0 the first two integrals vanish and

Λ(t;0)=4max⁡(C,Z)sin⁡2(πt). \Lambda(t;0)=4\max(C,Z)\sin^2(\pi t).

Since integral_0(.5)log(4sin2(pi t))dt=0, this gives (11). The same uniform integrable logarithmic bounds as in section 2 justify alpha->0,.5, beta->0 and Z->0. In particular C=Z is included by continuity; the derivation does not divide beta at that limit. At Z=0 one may instead use Lambda=4C sin^2(pi t) directly.

4. Uniform nodal bound for the ACTUAL cofactor

The separately proved original determinant prefix bound is

1Nlog⁡|Da(−C,C,Z)|≤∫0a/Nlog⁡|Z+Ce2πit|4cos⁡2(πt)dt+o(1). \frac1N\log|D_a(-C,C,Z)| \le \int_0^{a/N}\log\frac{|Z+Ce^{2\pi it}|} {4\cos^2(\pi t)}\,dt+o(1). \tag{13}

UNIFORMLY for all 0<=a<=J and the stated parameter compact. Its exact source and proof are in opposite-equal determinant prefix theorem (5a): the original tridiagonal determinant roots are (Z+C exp(+-2pi i t))/(4cos^2(pi t)), both moduli retained. This input is a genuine determinant upper, not an inference from a coefficientwise positive comparison.

The elementary sine-product estimate gives, also uniformly in a,

1Nlog⁡S2a=∫02a/Nlog⁡(2sin⁡(πt))dt+O(log⁡NN). \frac1N\log S_{2a} =\int_0^{2a/N}\log(2\sin(\pi t))\,dt +O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right). \tag{14}

The products have no zero since 2a<=N-1. Splitting at the midpoint and bounding the endpoint harmonic sums proves (14).

Combine the exact source (4), tail upper (8)-(9), determinant upper (13), and sine product (14). Identity (11) proves

max0≤a≤j|Rj(−C,C,Z;ua)|≤exp⁡[N(12logmax(C,Z)+o(1))]. \max_{0\le a\le j}|R_j(-C,C,Z;u_a)| \le \exp\left[N\left(\frac12\log\max(C,Z)+o(1)\right)\right]. \tag{15}

The o(1) is uniform in a,C,Z and both parities. No coefficient lower asymptotic or nodal nonvanishing is used.

5. Interpolation with the omitted nodes retained correctly

The polynomial R_j has degree at most j in u. The full physical distinct-node grid has J+1 points u_a,0<=a<=J. We know (15) only for a<=j=J-k. Define

Q(u)=Rj(u)∏a=j+1J(u−ua). Q(u)=R_j(u)\prod_{a=j+1}^J(u-u_a).

Then deg Q<=J; at each omitted node Q=0 exactly. Interpolation on the original Chebyshev/Radau full grid has Lebesgue norm O(log N). At retained nodes, the added product is bounded by 4^k. Therefore

sup[−4,0]|Q|≤Cklog⁡(N+1)exp⁡[N(12logmax(C,Z)+o(1))]. \sup_{[-4,0]}|Q| \le C_k\log(N+1) \exp\left[N\left(\frac12\log\max(C,Z)+o(1)\right)\right].

Recovering R_j costs only a fixed polynomial. If q(t0)=0 for t0 in [-4,0], then q(t)/(t-t0)=integral_0^1 q’(t0+s(t-t0))ds, so its interval supremum is at most sup|q’|. The ordinary polynomial derivative bound costs O(J^2) per removed factor (O(J^3) would also suffice). Repeat k times. This proves (2). It also shows why no uncontrolled value at an omitted node or division by a sampled zero enters the argument.

Finally apply the degree-j cosine Bernstein bound to obtain (3). The nodal factorization, all scalar sine products and the odd central endpoint remain literal original-source quantities.

6. Scope and useful consequence

The same actual positive cofactor has weighted rate Q(C,C,Z) at u=0, by the original complete half-triangle theorem. Thus (2) gives the explicit strictly positive asymptotic loss

Q(C,C,Z)−12log⁡max⁡(C,Z)>0. Q(C,C,Z)-\frac12\log\max(C,Z)>0.

This is uniform on the stated compacts, including Z=0. The strict inequality follows by a small positive triangle-corner perturbation of the largest pure weight, retaining its entropy gain. At the protected third face Z=0 this loss is exactly

2f(14)>0,f(t)=−∫0tlog⁡tan⁡(πs)ds. 2f(\tfrac14)>0,\qquad f(t)=-\int_0^t\log\tan(\pi s)\,ds.

This upgrades the determinant-only result to the raw cleared cofactor for ALL real spectra and a sufficiently thin fixed spectral strip. It does NOT automatically give a coefficientwise bound, an all-face-phase bound, a Green-star endpoint comparison, or an absolute bound by the original complete source volume. Other shared face targets and other coupons remain to be controlled.

This raw-cofactor bound is for the specified real-face specialization, including Z=0Z=0. It does not cover an arbitrary face-phase torus or prove a complete-volume comparison.
