Spectral control and the tail-free boundary of Racah normal transport

29 October 2026. The new estimate here is a polynomial spectral gap for the ACTUAL folded root polynomial and bounds for every fixed SPECTRAL derivative. It also records a general sufficient criterion for full generic-q partner-normal transport: the actual factorial range must stop before its [2N] current. A one-edge partner always has that property, so its normal bound is a corollary of the existing positive-core theorem, not a bound on a nonzero discarded current. The first-normal spectral identity below is exact, but the general nonempty-tail normal problem and a physical confluent-frame norm bound remain open.

1. Exact source convention and the restricted transport theorem

Put D=N-1, M=floor(D/2), q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, and theta=pi/N. All six spins below are integers. Let a,b,c,d,e,f<=M and assume

a+b+e,c+d+e,a+d+f,b+c+f >= D.                 (1)

Use the SAME generic seven-factorial sum S(zeta), face factors Delta_F^2, and normalized squared scalar

K_e(zeta)=[N]^2 S(zeta)^2 product_F Delta_F^2 (2)

as in the common-phase/normal theorem. This is the scalar diagonal gauge of the ACTUAL generic four-port source proved in the full source theorem, not a substituted root 6j value. Let e=D-e and form K_e by changing ONLY e in the same generic formula. Both germs are analytic at q.

More generally let B be ANY of the sixty-four partner branches and let Q_B be its three opposite sums. If

min Q_B <= 2D,                              (3a)

then, for every fixed r, in t=log(zeta/q),

|D_t^r K_B(q)| <= C_r N^(c_r) W_N^2.         (3b)

The constants are independent of labels and N. Indeed its actual z range ends at min Q_B. Every term therefore contains exactly one [N], and no [2N]. After [N] cancellation, the generic expression has a positive common-phase root core and only unit denominator factors. The uniform positive-core germ theorem applies to all its fixed jets; its normalized root value is W_N^2 by the all-partner value theorem. This proves (3b) without discarding any term.

For the single partner e*=D-e, the untouched opposite sum a+b+c+d<=2D automatically proves (3a). In particular there is an absolute C such that

K_e(q)=K_e*(q)=W_N^2>0,
|dot K_e*(q)| <= C N^8 W_N^2.                (3)

The value equality follows from the stronger all-sixty-four VALUE reflection theorem. The proof below only needs its single-internal-edge case. By tetrahedral relabeling, (3) applies to ANY ONE partner of a folded sextuple. Its first-normal estimate is rederived in section4 to identify exactly the spectral term which higher-order transport would require. A local square root with positive root value also has polynomial fixed-jet bounds under criterion (3a).

Criterion (3a) can fail already for TWO adjacent partners: N=5, folded labels (1,1,2,2,2,2), partners (3,3,2,2,2,2), give the true range z=8,9, so z=9 contains [2N]. Here the exact regularized sum is C+[2N]T with C(q)=(3+sqrt(5))/2 and T(q)=-1: the current is really nonzero, not merely allowed by an index bound. The spectral theorem below does not control it. No assertion for such branches is made.

2. The actual root polynomial and its degree recurrence

Set

p=min(a+d,b+c), s=a+b+c+d, t0=s-2p,
h=max(|a-b|,|c-d|), rA=a+b-h, rC=c+d-h,
L=2p-D, n=p-f, t=p+f-D.                     (4)

The supports imply 0<=t<=n, n+t=L, and rA,rC>=L. In particular L>=0. If t=0 (possibly with L>0) the root polynomial below is 1, all its positive-order spectral derivatives vanish, and no killed matrix is needed. Let alpha=q^(2h+1), lambda=z+z^-1. The actual generic source polynomial is

G_n(z;zeta)=sum_(k=0)^n
   (Q^-n,P Q^(n-1),alpha z,alpha/z;Q)_k Q^k
                     /(Q,Q^(t0+1),Q^-rA,Q^-rC;Q)_k,
P=Q^(-2p),                                  (5)

where in the GENERIC formula Q=zeta^2 and alpha=zeta^(2h+1). The denominators are root units. At the root P=Q^(1-L), the second upper parameter is Q^-t, and (5) becomes the degree-t polynomial

R_t(lambda)=sum_(k=0)^t A_k
                product_(i<k)(c_(h+i)-lambda),
c_j=2cos((2j+1)theta),
A_k= [s_n/s_(n-k)] [s_t/s_(t-k)]
      [s_(rA-k)/s_rA] [s_(rC-k)/s_rC]
                     s_t0/(s_k s_(t0+k)) >0. (6)

Here s_j=product_(u=1)^j 2sin(u theta). This is an identity of root polynomials, not a deletion of the GENERIC k>t terms. The phase in (6) cancels exactly: the residual exponent is Nk and the remaining sign is (-1)^k, so (-1)^k q^(Nk)=1. It also follows directly by converting each finite Pochhammer to sine factors.

For completeness the following recurrence is an OWN finite-product calculation. For arbitrary generic alpha,beta,gamma,delta, put P=alpha beta gamma delta, and define R_j by the finite sum (5) with upper parameters Q-j,PQ(j-1) and denominators Q,alpha beta,alpha gamma,alpha delta. Then

lambda R_j=A_j R_(j+1)
      +(alpha+alpha^-1-A_j-C_j)R_j+C_j R_(j-1),
A_j=product_(u=beta,gamma,delta)(1-alpha u Q^j)
      (1-PQ^(j-1))
       /[alpha(1-PQ^(2j-1))(1-PQ^(2j))],
C_j=alpha(1-Q^j)
      product_(uv=beta gamma,beta delta,gamma delta)(1-uvQ^(j-1))
       /[(1-PQ^(2j-2))(1-PQ^(2j-1))].        (7)

To verify it without invoking a polynomial-family theorem, use M_k=(alpha z,alpha/z;Q)_k and

lambda M_k=(alpha Q^k+alpha^-1 Q^-k)M_k
                                  -alpha^-1 Q^-k M_(k+1).

If h_jk is its coefficient in R_j, the three required ratios are

h_(j+1),k/h_jk=(1-Q^(-j-1))(1-PQ^(j+k-1))
                   /[(1-Q^(k-j-1))(1-PQ^(j-1))],
h_(j-1),k/h_jk=(1-Q^(k-j))(1-PQ^(j-2))
                   /[(1-Q^-j)(1-PQ^(j+k-2))],
h_j,k-1/h_jk=(1-Q^k) product_u(1-alpha u Q^(k-1))
                /[Q(1-Q^(k-j-1))(1-PQ^(j+k-2))].

Substitution proves (7) after one common-denominator multiplication; the k=0 and terminating endpoints are obtained before division, or by the same cleared polynomial identity. Thus it remains valid at our root whenever j<t. Here C_0 is identically zero and is set to zero BEFORE root specialization; after omitting it, the remaining recurrence denominators are units.

3. A killed birth-death operator with a polynomial spectral gap

At the parameters in (4), A_j=-a_j and C_j=-b_j, where b_0=0 and for 0<=j<t

a_j=4 sin((t0+1+j)theta) sin((rA-j)theta)
         sin((rC-j)theta) sin((L-j)theta)
       /[sin((L-2j)theta) sin((L-2j-1)theta)],
b_j=4 sin(j theta) sin((rA-L+j)theta)
         sin((rC-L+j)theta) sin((s+2-N-j)theta)
       /[sin((L-2j+1)theta) sin((L-2j)theta)]. (8)

Every numerator sine index of a_j is in 1,…,D, and hence

a_j>=64/N^4,  a_j>=b_j>=0.                   (9)

Here is a proof of the second inequality, which is important for the uniformity. First t0<=2h and rA,rC>=L follow from the four choices of the largest difference in h and a,b,c,d<=D/2. For example, when h=a-b>=|c-d|, p=b+c, rA-L=D-2c>=0 and rC-L=D-a-b-c+d>=0 because c-d<=a-b. The other cases follow by exchanging the two pairs and their ends. For j>=1 put v=L-2j, x=t0+1+j, y=rA-L+j, z=rC-L+j, and

Delta_v(u)=log sin((u+v)theta)-log sin(u theta).

All indices used are strictly between 0 and N. The function Delta_v is decreasing, and Delta_v(N-v-u)=-Delta_v(u). Moreover y+z=t0+D-2h-v<=D-v<N-v, so Delta_v(y)+Delta_v(z)>=0. Since x>=j+1, (8) gives

log(a_j/b_j)>=Delta_v(j)-Delta_v(j+1)
                       +log[sin((v+1)theta)/sin((v-1)theta)]>=0.

For the last step clear its positive denominators and use EXACTLY

sin((v+j)theta)sin((j+1)theta)sin((v+1)theta)
   -sin(j theta)sin((v+j+1)theta)sin((v-1)theta)
  =sin(theta)[cos(theta)-cos(v theta)cos((v+2j+1)theta)]>=0.

The final inequality uses 1<=v<=D. This proves the drift inequality; at j=0 it is immediate. The lower bound in (9) uses sin(u theta)>=sin(theta)>=2/N and denominators at most 1.

For t>=1 let B_t be the t by t tridiagonal matrix with diagonal c_h+a_j+b_j, upper diagonal -a_j and lower diagonal -b_j. Its characteristic roots are EXACTLY those of R_t, by (7), R_0=1, and the nonzero leading coefficients. It is diagonally similar to a real symmetric matrix, since a_j b_(j+1)>0. Put M_t=B_t-c_h I. The maximum principle with a Dirichlet boundary at t and reflection at 0 proves M_t^-1 is entrywise nonnegative. To bound its row sums, solve M_t u=1, u_t=0, and set d_j=u_j-u_(j+1). Then

a_j d_j-b_j d_(j-1)=1,
0<d_j<=(j+1)/a_min,
||M_t^-1||_infinity=max_j u_j
                   <=t(t+1)/(2a_min)<=N^6/64. (10)

The real eigenvalues of M_t are positive (also by the same maximum principle), so every zero rho of R_t satisfies

rho>=c_h+64/N^6.                            (11)

This proof does not need the stronger observed but unproved claim that all these zeros exceed 2.

Every supported folded e has lambda_e=c_e<=c_h. Factoring R_t over its real roots and using (11) now proves, for each fixed r,

|R_t^(r)(lambda_e)|/R_t(lambda_e)
                        <=(N^7/64)^r.        (12)

The same argument bounds fixed derivatives of 1/R_t by a polynomial times 1/R_t(lambda_e). This is a root SPECTRAL statement, not an assertion about the coefficients of the disappearing generic tail.

4. Convert spectral control to the actual first partner normal

The proved full-source formula is S=S0(E)(V_n/C_n)G_n(E). The factor V_n/C_n is independent of E and is kept generically. Write

K_e=J_e G_n(lambda_e;zeta)^2,
R_J=J_e*/J_e
   =[product_F D_F(e*)/product_F D_F(e)]
                                  [S0(e*)/S0(e)]^2,             (13)

where D_F=(-1)(F-D)[N]Delta_F2 is analytic and nonzero at q. The total face sign is even. All numerator [N] zeros in (13) cancel before evaluation; every remaining factor is a root unit. The exact root value symmetry, or its elementary factorial reflection s_(D-r)=N/s_r, gives R_J(q)=1. Each fixed logarithmic derivative of R_J is polynomially bounded uniformly in the labels: it is a quotient of O(N) unit quantum-integer factors with index O(N). In particular |dot R_J(q)|<=C N^3.

Set z_e=zeta^(2e+1), z_e*=zeta^(2N)/z_e. Because G_n is symmetric in its spectral argument, its partner evaluation is EXACTLY G_n(zeta^(-2N)z_e;zeta). The two spectral coordinates satisfy

lambda_e*-lambda_e
   =(zeta^(2N)-1)(zeta^(2N)-z_e^2)/(zeta^(2N)z_e),
|dot lambda_e*-dot lambda_e|<=4N.             (14)

At q they coincide. Differentiating the FULL generic polynomial only ONCE therefore cancels its common coefficient normal and gives the exact identity

(dot K_e*-dot K_e)/W_N^2
  =dot R_J+2(dot lambda_e*-dot lambda_e)
                                  R_t'(lambda_e)/R_t(lambda_e). (15)

Equation (15) differentiates the full source identity (13), rather than its value alone. In THIS one-edge case its [2N] tail is empty by (3a); thus (15) must not be presented as a new bound for a nonzero T. Equation (12), the root-unit derivative bound in (13), and the uniform folded-germ bound from the common-phase theorem imply (3).

At SECOND order the common coefficient derivative no longer cancels: mixed spectral/generic-q derivatives of G_n appear. Its generic degree is n, whereas its root degree is t, and the vanished k>t coefficients can contribute to precisely these mixed derivatives. Consequently (12) ALONE cannot be iterated to higher generic-q jets. The already proved tail-free criterion still handles every fixed order in the one-edge case, by its separate positive-core proof. Neither argument yet bounds a nonempty [2N] tail or the relevant mixed coefficient/spectral derivatives after external partners.

5. An exact central normal check by a finite product derivative

For odd N=2m+1 and a=b=c=d=f=m, R_m is the actual turned Steinberg projector h_N. Its endpoint c_0 obeys h_N(c_0)=1 and

h_N'(c_0)=-[2sin(theta)]^-1 sum_(u=1)^m cot(u theta). (16)

This is an exact identity, not a fitted asymptotic. To prove it, specialize the OWN balanced Sears identity by canceling its parameter c=d. The transformed sum has an upper parameter 1 and only its k=0 term survives, giving the finite product

sum_(k=0)^m (Q^-m,Q^-m,A;Q)_k Q^k
                 /(Q,Q,AQ^-2m;Q)_k
    =(Q^(m+1),Q/A;Q)_m/(Q,Q^(m+1)/A;Q)_m.   (17)

At Q^N=1, Q^-2m=Q and (A)_k/(AQ)_k=(1-A)/(1-AQ^k). Differentiate (17) at A=1. Pairing u with N-u on its right gives

sum_(k=1)^m (Q^-m;Q)_k^2 Q^k
                      /[F_k^2(1-Q^k)]
                     =-i sum_(u=1)^m cot(u theta).

Differentiating the finite Newton expression (6) at c_0 gives (16), with its factor -i/[2sin(theta)]. In particular |h_N’(c_0)|<=C N^2(1+log N), consistent with the general polynomial bound. This identity tests a genuine normal direction and does not replace the source germ by its value.

Audit: degree recurrence, positive drift, spectral derivatives and exact first normals. The finite tests supplement the recurrence, drift and generic source proofs above. They do not certify transport for a nonempty [2N] tail or an all-partner higher-jet theorem.

The one-flip case has an empty tail. The nonempty-tail boundary remains unestimated.

The theorem is restricted to the tail-free case; it does not extend to the nonempty [2N] current.
