The original one-cap coefficient reduces to two negative-axis suprema

31 October 2026. exact-source and analytic reduction. Unpublished. The original diagram, mark, and every ordinary degree target remain fixed. All noncap coupons are at their actual CROSS endpoint sigma=-2; the cap is at +2. No spectral-neighborhood, arbitrary-spectator, or unconditional absolute volume estimate is asserted. The final two polynomial suprema are the remaining problem.

1. Extract the original exterior first

Use a simple matched source with protected MAIN faces u,v, cap F=(u,A,v,C), and R-1 other original ports. At level N put d=N-1 and M=floor(N/2)-1. Remove the fixed common raw unit signs from each CROSS coupon. Define the genuine binary residual polynomial

PN(A,C)=[∏fordinaryf≠A,CLfd]∏e≠FPe,CROSS(−2;L),PN(A,C)=∑j=0dpN,jAjCd−j. \begin{aligned} P_N(A,C)&=\left[\prod_{\substack{f\ \mathrm{ordinary}\\f\ne A,C}}L_f^d\right] \prod_{e\ne F}P_{e,\mathrm{CROSS}}(-2;L),\\ P_N(A,C)&=\sum_{j=0}^{d}p_{N,j}A^jC^{d-j}. \end{aligned}\tag{1}

Protected variables are zero BEFORE this extraction. Every remaining ordinary variable is extracted to its original d target. Homogeneity gives degree d exactly: (R-1)d-(R-2)d=d. At both parities the actual endpoint CROSS polynomials have nonnegative coefficients, so pN,j≥0p_{N,j}\ge0. This is the entire exterior, not only the ports adjacent to A.

Set theta=pi/N and

sj=2sin⁡(jθ),cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)θ),Uj=(∏r=1jsr2)∏r=j+1d(2−cr). s_j=2\sin(j\theta),\qquad c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\theta),\qquad U_j=\left(\prod_{r=1}^{j}s_r^2\right)\prod_{r=j+1}^{d}(2-c_r).

The actual protected cap at +2 has all-index polynomial sum_(j=0)^d (-1)^j U_j A(d-j)Cj; its original RIGHT piece has exactly j<=M. Thus the original cap/CROSS sector coefficient, up to its fixed unit scalar, is

SN=∑j=0M(−1)jUjpN,j. S_N=\sum_{j=0}^{M}(-1)^jU_jp_{N,j}.\tag{2}

Both original A^d and C^d demands have been imposed in (2). No sign has been assigned independently to a face occurrence.

2. Complete the finite cap sum, with a strictly smaller omitted tail

Let R(s) be the original residual complete-triangle capacity with A load s, C load 1-s, all other ordinary loads one, and the two original protected loads zero. It is upper semicontinuous on its compact feasible interval, with value minus infinity when the fiber is empty. The same complete coefficient entropy gives

ΦD=max1/2≤s≤1[R(s)+2f(s−12)]. \Phi_D=\max_{1/2\le s\le1}\bigl[R(s)+2f(s-\tfrac12)\bigr].\tag{3}

There is a strict constant gamma_D>0, depending only on the fixed diagram and mark, such that

max1/2≤s≤1R(s)≤ΦD−γD. \max_{1/2\le s\le1}R(s)\le\Phi_D-\gamma_D.\tag{4}

Indeed, if 1/2<s<1 and R(s) is finite, the added cap entropy in (3) is strictly positive. At s=1/2 or s=1 it vanishes, but the cap’s complete half-triangle is a pure vertex. The original complete optimizer is unique and positive on every ordinary incidence. Neither endpoint can attain its value. Compactness and upper semicontinuity now give (4). This uses the original complete-flow theorem, not any full signed-source volume conclusion.

The proved uniform CLOSED local CROSS coefficient upper, followed by the actual finite shared-array sum, implies

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡pN,jN≤R(s)whenever jN/d→s. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log p_{N,j_N}\le R(s) \qquad\text{whenever }j_N/d\longrightarrow s.\tag{5}

The number of local-degree arrays is polynomial because the diagram is fixed. Their limits satisfy exactly the residual targets defining R(s). This argument needs only the upper coefficient theorem; no rounding or lower estimate is needed. It includes zero coefficients.

For all 0<=j<=d,

Uj+1Uj=sin⁡2((j+1)θ)sin⁡2((j+3/2)θ)(0≤j<d),U0=csc⁡2(θ/2)≤N2,Ud=N2,0<Uj≤N2. \begin{aligned} \frac{U_{j+1}}{U_j}&=\frac{\sin^2((j+1)\theta)}{\sin^2((j+3/2)\theta)} \quad(0\le j<d),\\ U_0&=\csc^2(\theta/2)\le N^2,\qquad U_d=N^2,\qquad 0<U_j\le N^2. \end{aligned}\tag{6}

The ratio crosses one only once, from below to above, so the maximum is at an endpoint. Consequently the omitted actual tail satisfies

|TN|:=|∑j=M+1d(−1)jUjpN,j|≤eN(ΦD−γD/2). |T_N|:=\left|\sum_{j=M+1}^{d}(-1)^jU_jp_{N,j}\right| \le e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma_D/2)}.\tag{7}

for all sufficiently large N. At odd N=2m+1, its smallest j=m gives j/d=1/2 exactly; at even N=2m it gives m/(2m-1)>1/2. Thus no parity seam is lost in the compact upper argument.

3. Exact positive moment reduction of the completed sum

The original positive cap moment law supplies X_N>0 with

𝔼XNj=UjU0(0≤j≤d),md:=𝔼XNd=N2sin⁡2π2N≤π24. \mathbb E X_N^j=\frac{U_j}{U_0}\quad(0\le j\le d),\qquad m_d:=\mathbb E X_N^d=N^2\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2N}\le\frac{\pi^2}{4}.\tag{8}

Only these finite moments are used. In particular its unbounded support is retained; no integration of degree greater than d occurs. The completed finite sum is exactly

SN+TN=U0𝔼PN(−XN,1). S_N+T_N=U_0\mathbb E P_N(-X_N,1).\tag{9}

Define two actual negative-axis suprema

BN−=sup0≤t≤1|PN(−t,1)|,BN+=sup0≤t≤1|PN(−1,t)|. B_N^- =\sup_{0\le t\le1}|P_N(-t,1)|,\qquad B_N^+ =\sup_{0\le t\le1}|P_N(-1,t)|.\tag{10}

At t=0 these use the actual polynomial endpoint values. For X>=1, homogeneity gives P_N(-X,1)=X^d P_N(-1,1/X). Split the expectation at X=1 and use (8). The following FINITE inequality is therefore exact:

|SN|≤U0[BN−+mdBN+]+|TN|. |S_N|\le U_0\bigl[B_N^-+m_dB_N^+\bigr]+|T_N|.\tag{11}

In particular

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡|SN|≤max⁡{limsupN→∞1Nlogmax(BN−,BN+),ΦD−γD2}. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|S_N| \le\max\left\{\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\max(B_N^-,B_N^+), \Phi_D-\frac{\gamma_D}{2}\right\}.\tag{12}

The weak bound B_N^pm<=exp[N(Phi_D+o(1))] would prove the weak absolute cap estimate at these fixed spectra. A strict bound for both suprema would prove strict cap suppression. The exterior cannot be replaced by its independent positive torus majorant in this reduction: the coefficient extraction in (1) has already retained all its phases and all original demands.

4. Genuine odd-level real stability

At odd N=2m+1 the actual sigma=-2 CROSS coupon is exactly

N2Dm(x,b,y)Dm(x,c,y),Dm(x,b,y)=det⁡[bI+x2H−+y2H+]. N^2D_m(x,b,y)D_m(x,c,y),\qquad D_m(x,b,y)=\det\left[bI+\frac{x}{2}H_-+\frac{y}{2}H_+\right].\tag{13}

with the original positive-definite Hermitian H_-,H_+. If x,b,y all have positive imaginary parts, the matrix’s imaginary part is positive definite. Its determinant is nonzero. Thus each factor is real stable. Identifying shared face variables, multiplying, taking the indicated derivatives for the ordinary coefficient extractions, and specializing those variables and the two protected variables to zero preserve real stability or give the zero polynomial. These closure statements also follow directly from the one-variable Gauss–Lucas/Hurwitz argument applied successively to the variables.

The actual P_N is nonzero whenever its original complete coefficient is nonzero, as holds for every sufficiently large level under the retained complete-source hypotheses. Hence P_N is a homogeneous binary real-stable polynomial with nonnegative coefficients. Equivalently, including possible roots at zero or infinity,

PN(A,C)=PN(1,1)∏r=1d[(1−λN,r)C+λN,rA],0≤λN,r≤1. P_N(A,C)=P_N(1,1)\prod_{r=1}^{d} \bigl[(1-\lambda_{N,r})C+\lambda_{N,r}A\bigr], \qquad 0\le\lambda_{N,r}\le1.\tag{14}

This applies to the original globally extracted polynomial, not to a degree projection of an isolated coupon. We make no corresponding even-level stability assertion here; the exact reduction (1)-(12) holds at both parities.

5. The exact remaining negative-axis variational problem

For odd levels (14) turns (10) into

log⁡BN−−log⁡PN(1,1)=max0≤t≤1∑rlog⁡|1−(1+t)λN,r|,log⁡BN+−log⁡PN(1,1)=max0≤t≤1∑rlog⁡|t−(1+t)λN,r|. \begin{aligned} \log B_N^- -\log P_N(1,1) &=\max_{0\le t\le1}\sum_r\log|1-(1+t)\lambda_{N,r}|,\\ \log B_N^+ -\log P_N(1,1) &=\max_{0\le t\le1}\sum_r\log|t-(1+t)\lambda_{N,r}|. \end{aligned}\tag{15}

Zeros have logarithm minus infinity. These are genuine signed boundary potentials, with the SAME root parameters for both branches. There is no modulus replacement before the original face extraction.

A mean condition N−1∑rλN,r→νN^{-1}\sum_r\lambda_{N,r}\to\nu alone cannot bound these potentials by a universal positive loss. Products approaching A^(nu N) C^((1-nu)N) have that mean and almost no loss at t=1. Such abstract examples are NOT asserted to arise from an original stationary triangulated exterior. The original R(s), genuine circle packing and fixed exterior targets are additional necessary data.

For a strict one-cap stationary exterior, the original-chain theorem supplies M_cap-Phi_D<=W(nu)/pi-g/(2pi). A sufficient next statement is a bound on both actual quantities in (15) that pays the actual positive normalization’s excess over Phi_D; the whole-star phase gap alone does not quantify it. No such bound is proved here. In particular neither real stability, nor the formal identity of the mean with nu, is being used as a substitute for an original-disk variational inequality.

6. A finite global variance budget at odd levels

The genuine factorization (14), unlike an arbitrary star majorant, also supplies an explicit finite loss. Set

LN=−∑rlog⁡max⁡(λN,r,1−λN,r),VN=∑rλN,r(1−λN,r)=∂z2logPN(ez,1)|z=0. \begin{aligned} L_N&=-\sum_r\log\max(\lambda_{N,r},1-\lambda_{N,r}),\\ V_N&=\sum_r\lambda_{N,r}(1-\lambda_{N,r}) =\left.\partial_z^2\log P_N(e^z,1)\right|_{z=0}. \end{aligned}\tag{16}

For every p,t in [0,1], the two affine functions in (15) attain their largest absolute values at an endpoint of the t interval. The endpoint values are 1-p, |1-2p| and p, all at most max(p,1-p). Moreover

−log⁡max⁡(p,1−p)≥min⁡(p,1−p)≥p(1−p). -\log\max(p,1-p)\ge\min(p,1-p)\ge p(1-p).

Thus the following inequalities are exact, with no asymptotic coefficient or root-distribution assertion:

max⁡(BN−,BN+)≤PN(1,1)e−LN≤PN(1,1)e−VN,|SN|≤N2(1+π24)PN(1,1)e−LN+|TN|. \begin{aligned} \max(B_N^-,B_N^+)&\le P_N(1,1)e^{-L_N}\le P_N(1,1)e^{-V_N},\\ |S_N|&\le N^2\left(1+\frac{\pi^2}{4}\right)P_N(1,1)e^{-L_N}+|T_N|. \end{aligned}\tag{17}

Equivalently the first term uses the genuine logarithmic Mahler measure

Mahler⁡(PN)=exp⁡[12π∫02πlog|PN(eiθ,1)|dθ]=PN(1,1)e−LN. \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Mahler}(P_N) &=\exp\left[\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|P_N(e^{i\theta},1)|\,d\theta\right]\\ &=P_N(1,1)e^{-L_N}. \end{aligned}\tag{17a}

The equality follows factor by factor from the circle logarithmic mean; roots on the circle are integrable. This is a property of the exact globally extracted polynomial. It is not the Mahler measure of separate local coupons or an exchange of a coefficient and a norm.

There is a simple additional sufficient condition. Let k_N be the number of factors in (14) with λN,r≥1/2\lambda_{N,r}\ge1/2, counting all ties. At a tied factor the two terms are equal, and here the A term is selected. The original positive coefficient pN,kNp_{N,k_N} contains the product obtained by selecting the larger term from every factor. Therefore

Mahler⁡(PN)≤pN,kN. \operatorname{Mahler}(P_N)\le p_{N,k_N}.\tag{17b}

If liminf_(N odd) k_N/d>=1/2, (4)-(5) imply a strict gap for (17a) and hence for the actual cap sector. This is a root-count condition on the original extracted polynomial; it is not implied by its mean.

In a genuine strict one-cap stationary configuration, finite common weights L_A=L_C give the SAME valid residual dual upper bound for every s, and attain that bound at the stationary nu. Therefore R(nu)=M_cap is its unrestricted maximum, including s>1/2. The same upper coefficient argument (5), summed over j, gives

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡PN(1,1)≤Mcap. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log P_N(1,1)\le M_{\mathrm{cap}}.\tag{18}

No lower coefficient rate or convergence of the second derivative is needed here. Consequently a sufficient, explicitly quantitative condition for this fixed-spectrum odd subsequence is

liminfN→∞NoddLNN>Mcap−ΦD. \liminf_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\ \mathrm{odd}}}\frac{L_N}{N} >M_{\mathrm{cap}}-\Phi_D.\tag{19}

The same sufficient condition with V_N in place of L_N is weaker but uses only the finite positive residual variance. If either liminf is at least M_cap-Phi_D, the weak absolute rate bound follows. If it exceeds that value by delta>0, (7), (17), (18) give a strict rate loss at least min(delta,gamma_D/2), after an arbitrarily small limiting error. These are sufficient criteria, not conclusions about all original exteriors.

The geometric W budget is one possible way to upper-bound the right side of (19), but it has NOT been compared with L_N or V_N. In fact the factorwise estimate itself has a definite limitation:

0≤LN≤2log⁡2∑rλN,r,0≤VN≤dλ‾N(1−λ‾N),λ‾N=1d∑rλN,r. \begin{aligned} 0\le L_N&\le2\log2\sum_r\lambda_{N,r},\\ 0\le V_N&\le d\,\bar\lambda_N(1-\bar\lambda_N),\qquad \bar\lambda_N=\frac1d\sum_r\lambda_{N,r}. \end{aligned}\tag{20}

For the first inequality, -log(1-p)<=2p log2 on 0<=p<=1/2 by convexity and the endpoint chord, while for p>=1/2 one has -log p<=log2<=2p log2. The second is Jensen’s inequality for p(1-p). Thus this factorwise method cannot automatically pay a larger proposed nu-only budget. Equation (15), which retains the common optimizing t across ALL factors, is potentially sharper.

This section is a general exact-source extraction criterion. It does not iterate a local cancellation estimate, claim a stationary phase for the signed polynomial, or infer an inequality from the mean alone.

Inputs and scope

Exact cap support and the all-index formula are in the original cap source interface, section 6. The positive cap moment law supplies the finite moments, including the last moment used here. The original residual capacity fixes every target and the complete-cap entropy in (3). The closed CROSS coefficient uppers are in the even half-block theorem and the odd original-source theorem, with the literal original shared-array dictionary. The odd determinant factorization, section 3, and positive physical pencils give (13). The original one-cap chain budget provides the geometric sufficient comparison discussed after (19).

Common weights give the same upper bound for every boundary split and attain it at the stationary split ν\nu. This is a sufficient criterion, not a universal absolute-volume comparison.

This supplies no near-spectrum extension and no complete fixed-plat or filled-period theorem. No numerical scan or source lower asymptotic is used in (1)–(12). The inequality comparing the actual negative-axis or Mahler growth with Phi remains open without a sufficient condition such as (19) or the stated root-count test.
