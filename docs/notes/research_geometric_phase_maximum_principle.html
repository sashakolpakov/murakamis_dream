A maximum principle for the geometric cosine phases

Research note, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts and running certificate inputs are unchanged.

The cosine phases of a positive path satisfy a simple maximum principle. It gives a short proof of the spectral half-plane bound and, more usefully for pair activation, an upper bound for each real angle in terms of its own charge. The latter removes a large part of the parameter region considered in the existing support tests.

The argument uses the already proved positive charge family and height bound. It does not use the spectral half-plane theorem as an input. Thus it provides an alternative proof of that theorem without changing its current file or the inputs of any running verification.

1. The geometric equations

Use the finite positive paths of the fractional-charge theorem: all selected integer powers satisfy nv≥3n_v\ge3, all charges satisfy 0≤χv≤10\le\chi_v\le1, and at least one outside endpoint is complete. At a positive-charge vertex write tv=av−iHv,0<av<π,0<Hv<H*:=2320.(1) t_v=a_v-iH_v,\qquad 0<a_v<\pi,\qquad 0<H_v<H_*:=\frac{23}{20}. \qquad\text{(1)} Zero-charge vertices and complete outside endpoints have tv=0t_v=0. For the affine height estimate, put χmax(H)=min⁡{1,4037(H−37200)}.(24) \chi_{\max}(H)= \min\left\{1,\frac{40}{37}\left(H-\frac{37}{200}\right)\right\}. \qquad\text{(24)} A failure of that estimate implies χ≤χmax(H)\chi\le\chi_{\max}(H). The neighboring cosine domains can also be reduced: writing c=x+iy=cos⁡(tw/2)c=x+iy=\cos(t_w/2), Theorem 1 and Corollary 3 give 0≤y≤1011x,x2−y2>116.(26) 0\le y\le\frac{10}{11}x,\qquad x^2-y^2>\frac1{16}. \qquad\text{(26)} The value c=1c=1 at a complete neighbor satisfies these conditions. No running certificate has been changed, and neither the full pair-height estimate nor the integrated pair-energy gap follows until the remaining support bounds are proved.

7. A sharper bound for the maximum cosine phase

The negative return phases at a maximizing vertex give more than their sign. Retaining their sizes improves (7) to a bound that removes some of the largest possible neighboring returns.

8. A single arc suffices for the smaller cosine domain

For a height cap C>0C>0, let DCD_C be the quarter ellipse from the pair-activation note, and put DC♯=DC∩{x+iy:0≤y≤x/2},ϑC=arctan⁡(12coth(C/2)).(49) D_C^\sharp=D_C\cap\{x+iy:0\le y\le x/\sqrt2\},\qquad \vartheta_C=\arctan\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}\coth(C/2)\right). \qquad\text{(49)} Theorem 5 puts every neighboring geometric cosine in this domain. The support function on the ray has no strict interior maximum; its maximum is at zero or at the outer endpoint.

Thus both support terms for pair activation can be bounded using one arc and the value zero, even in the affine regime. The old three-piece sector boundary need not be retained if this smaller phase domain is used instead. This observation gives a simpler candidate for a new verification; it does not certify its support inequalities by itself.
