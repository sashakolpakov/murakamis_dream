The actual cyclic sewing has a canonical regularized cut cycle

28 October 2026. The exact two-head cyclic sewing comparison introduces additional poles which do survive the original source. This note carries those terms to the original cyclic-dilogarithm cuts without dropping them. At a ramified endpoint only two Taylor subtraction terms are needed; equivalently, at most two ordinary derivatives make its radial integral absolutely convergent. Unramified cyclic poles contribute their specified residues. The prescription applies entry by entry and through the whole original ordered word.

This is an exact comparison of selected periods, including their boundary terms. It is not a positivity theorem or a geometric lower bound. In particular the positive radial symbol of the unsplit resolvent does not automatically apply separately to these differentiated sewing terms.

1. The actual additional divisor

On a total-index residue fibre, the actual two-head transfer is a cyclic shift with one or two edges removed. Write its cyclic completion as A=a S, where S^N=I and a=x^-1, or a=Q^-1 x^-1 for the opposite-root realization of an original u factor. Its cyclic resolvent is

R_A(t)=(tI-A)^-1.

The exact two-cut Woodbury comparison has a sewing matrix

C_h(t)=[[1,-(a/t)^(N-h)],[-(a/t)^h,1]], 1<=h<=N.

The h=N case uses the same cut column twice and reduces to the one-cut identity. The pure cyclic and the two diagonal sewing terms are analytic at zero and have zero original small-circle functional-calculus period. The remaining two cross terms contain two copies of R_A and negative powers of t. Thus their only new nonzero finite poles lie at

t^N=a^N,                                      (1)

with order at most two in each integration variable. They decay at infinity. The original nilpotent resolvent has no such poles: their cancellation belongs to the complete sewing identity.

At completeness x=-q^-1, so both realizations retain EXACTLY

a^N=(-1)^(N+1).                               (2)

For odd N, (1) meets every finite dilogarithm branch point, with the additional unramified point t=1. For even N its points are interlaced with the Nth roots. Away from completeness the full factor a^N=(-1)^(N+1) M^-N is retained. No replacement by a numerical scalar before taking the source is justified. The theorem below uses the unit circle case, in particular the complete point; small-circle sewing itself is an identity in the full meridian parameter.

2. One-variable regularized Hankel theorem

Fix Q=exp(2 pi i/N), epsilon in {1,-1}, and the original marked branch

A_epsilon(t)=product_(k=1)^(N-1)
                          (1-Q^(epsilon k)t)^(-k/N),
A_epsilon(0)=1.

Let B(t) be a scalar or matrix rational function with poles only at zero and finitely many points of the unit circle, each nonzero pole of order at most two, and with B(t)=O(t^-1) at infinity. Let Gamma_0 be a sufficiently small positive circle, lifted to the marked sheet. Put

alpha_j=Q^(-epsilon j), u_j=j/N,
g_j(r)=product_(k!=j,1<=k<N)
                       (1-r Q^[epsilon(k-j)])^(-k/N).

Every logarithm has the radial continuation already specified for the original cut. Let Z be the unramified poles of B, excluding zero. Then

(1/(2 pi i)) integral_(Gamma_0) A_epsilon(t) B(t) dt
   =sum_(j=1)^(N-1) sin(pi u_j)/pi
       FP integral_1^infinity
            alpha_j (r-1)^(-u_j) g_j(r) B(alpha_j r) dr
     -sum_(beta in Z) Res_(t=beta) A_epsilon(t) B(t) dt. (3)

If B is regular at a branch endpoint, FP is the ordinary convergent integral. At a pole alpha_j, write x=r-1 and the Laurent germ

f_j(x)=alpha_j g_j(1+x) B(alpha_j(1+x))
      =b_(-2)x^-2+b_(-1)x^-1+O(1),           (4)

with a missing coefficient set to zero. Its finite part means EXACTLY

integral_0^1 x^-u [f_j(x)-b_(-2)x^-2-b_(-1)x^-1] dx
  +integral_1^infinity x^-u f_j(x) dx
  -b_(-2)/(u+1)-b_(-1)/u.                   (5)

Both integrals in (5) converge absolutely. This formula fixes the subtraction convention; there is no adjustable finite constant. The numbers 1/u and 1/(u+1) are retained. In particular the former can grow with N at small cut labels.

Proof and signs

Deform the original small circle within the marked cut plane. The outer arcs tend to zero because A_epsilon=O(|t|^(-(N-1)/2)) and B=O(t^-1). As in the original Hankel proof, this is a sectorwise deformation and does not assume that the entire outer circle has a closed lift at even N. The two radial banks have difference

2i sin(pi u_j) (r-1)^(-u_j) g_j(r).

The factor dt contributes alpha_j dr, giving the first term in (3). Ordinary positively oriented circles about additional unramified poles are moved to the other side of the contour identity, giving the MINUS sign in its residue term.

Near a ramified endpoint, integrate each term b_n x^(n-u) of (4) on the two banks and the joining small arc before sending its radius to zero. The arc cancels exactly the divergent powers from n=-2,-1. There is no constant logarithmic term because u is strictly between zero and one. The remaining finite part is (5). Equivalently, on the normalization put t-alpha=s^(N/d), d=gcd(N,j). A possible local residue would require (n+1)N=j for an integer n, which cannot hold for 1<=j<N. These endpoint poles are of second kind; they do not supply an independently choosable residue cycle. This proves (3) entry by entry, with the same marked endpoint continuation as the original small circle.

3. At most two derivatives give a convergent flow integral

Let p in {0,1,2} be the pole order in (4), and put

H_j(x)=x^p f_j(x).

This is analytic at zero. Integration by parts with the endpoint finite parts retained gives, for p>=1,

FP integral_0^infinity x^(-u-p) H_j(x) dx
   =1/[u(u+1)...(u+p-1)]
                     integral_0^infinity x^-u H_j^(p)(x) dx. (6)

For p=0 use the original integral. Each derivative in the integration by parts removes one negative integer power. Its factor is positive 1/(u+p-1) at the first step and so on. The possible finite boundary term at zero is absent because all the exponents are nonintegral; the divergent terms are exactly those subtracted in (5).

At infinity, g_j(1+x)=O(x^(-(N-1)/2+u)), so H_j=O(x^(p-1-(N-1)/2+u)). Every intermediate boundary term tends to zero. The final integrand in (6) is integrable both at zero and infinity. Rationality and the finite product expression also give these derivative estimates directly. Equation (6) is particularly convenient for computing the marked period without subtracting two numerically divergent endpoint quantities.

These derivatives act on the COMPLETE H_j, including the cyclic resolvents, power characters and source entries. Applying them only to the dilogarithm factor would change the original period. Neither H_j^(p) nor the subtraction terms need be positive.

4. The extra terms survive an original crossing cap

The actual two-head calculation at N=2, M=1, Q=-1, x=i uses the opposite-root u transfer with a=i, the original fusion C, D=I and the original averaged cap with pivots (-i,i). After those contractions the unsplit transfer and the sum of its two cyclic cross terms are respectively

B_nil(t)=2i/t^2,
B_cross(t)=-2i(t^2-1)/[t^2(t^2+1)^2].         (7)

Here A_epsilon(t)=(1+t)^(-1/2) for either orientation. Their original small-circle periods are both -i. However B_cross has unramified double poles at beta=+i,-i, off the negative real dilogarithm cut. Its coefficients there are

at +i: b_(-2)=i, b_(-1)=-2,
at -i: b_(-2)=i, b_(-1)= 2.

Consequently the residue sum which MUST be subtracted in (3) is

i A_epsilon'(+i)-2 A_epsilon(+i)
  +i A_epsilon'(-i)+2 A_epsilon(-i),          (8)

and is nonzero. Thus even a whole original cap does not delete the new cyclic-pole correction. The original crossing’s external common Cartan line multiplies both representations equally and is unchanged.

5. Tensoring the prescription preserves the entire source

Apply (3) to each Euler variable of the exact cyclic sewing network, and use the ordinary original Hankel rule on its factorial variables. Each variable has at most two cyclic-resolvent factors, irrespective of level or braid length. The subtraction order is therefore at most two PER EULER VARIABLE. The number of variables and of cross-sewing types depends on the fixed word; the number of root-labelled cuts and unramified cyclic poles still grows with N.

All pole loci in a variable depend only on its own coordinate and the retained meridian, not on the other integration variables. The ordered matrix contraction is finite. Its products of endpoint Laurent expansions have independent variables; taking their finite constant parts and ordinary residues commutes. Equivalently apply the proved one-variable identity repeatedly to the original compact product circles, where every integral is initially convergent. This defines a tensor prescription even when an unregularized multiple radial integral would fail to be absolutely convergent.

The outcome is an exact selected cyclic period with explicit endpoint jets and pole residues, not a bare cyclic state. It retains the full original boundary source and every peripheral factor. The required future geometric comparison must carry this specified relative cycle. The finite subtraction order makes that target more concrete, but does not identify its leading hyperbolic component or prove that it survives the final sum.
