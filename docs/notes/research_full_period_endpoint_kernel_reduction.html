Coherent full-period endpoint bounds for the original edge kernel

Private research note, 16 November 2026. Written proof only; frozen manuscripts and previously adopted notes are unchanged.

The original kernel at the angular endpoint t=πt=\pi, hence spectral color w=2w=2, has polynomial bounds in the other real color, including all fixed mixed spectral derivatives. The proof sums the alternating low Newton sector by exact physical-row interpolation and retains the entire high radical. It therefore applies to the original first-normal closing, not just to its value part. A separate continuation estimate has exponential rate tending to zero as both angular heights tend to zero. No full-source lower bound or contour deformation is asserted.

1. Original data and direct sources

Put N≥2,D=N−1,m=⌊N/2⌋,J=⌊(N−1)/2⌋=D−m,θ=π/N,η=(−1)D, N\ge2,\quad D=N-1,\quad m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\quad J=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor=D-m,\quad \theta=\pi/N, \qquad \eta=(-1)^D, sk=2sin⁡(kθ),Sk=∏a=1ksa,S0=1,cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)θ), s_k=2\sin(k\theta),\quad S_k=\prod_{a=1}^k s_a,\quad S_0=1, \qquad c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\theta), ek(z)=Sk−2∏a=0k−1(ca−z),KN(w,z)=∑k=0Dek(w)ek(z).(1) e_k(z)=S_k^{-2}\prod_{a=0}^{k-1}(c_a-z),\qquad K_N(w,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{D}e_k(w)e_k(z). \qquad\text{(1)} The distinct physical nodes are c0,…,cJc_0,\ldots,c_J. They are double except for cJ=−2c_J=-2 at odd levels, where this last node is simple. All statements below concern these ordinary polynomials before any physical quotient is taken.

The directly used sources, read in full at the displayed versions, are:

  • Actual matching-edge root location, SHA256 f84b82d7924a0c92ca803bc4834bcb0f18f63cedebf2bd072d41d0545a9f5793. Its exact polynomial (1), Jacobi realization and positive matrix shift prove that every nonconstant folded row has all roots strictly above 22.
  • Full high Newton radical, SHA256 1faa5d2e02edf0a1462cc43b243e0ab4403bf56e9f1af0befb6b492441d9dd03. Equations (17), (34)–(36) give the exact low/high split and the ordinary radical’s real-period and complex-height bounds; (2), (10) give the original full-normal closing.
  • Last physical-row transport, SHA256 a6a79106b81b94f5d163022b056c9c6a51e0cdb4f602f090c9f3401436f87c7d. Theorem 1 and (23) bound the actual last row at each fixed real color below 22, at both parities. This source is used only in Section 5.

The first source proves roots greater than 22, not merely greater than c0c_0. That stronger conclusion is essential here.

Define the physical rows Fj,N(w)=KN(cj,w)F_{j,N}(w)=K_N(c_j,w), 0≤j≤J0\le j\le J. For k≤jk\le j, the difference-of-cosines product gives ∏a<k(ca−cj)=Sj+kSj−k,ek(cj)=Sj+kSj−kSk2. \prod_{a<k}(c_a-c_j)=\frac{S_{j+k}}{S_{j-k}},\qquad e_k(c_j)=\frac{S_{j+k}}{S_{j-k}S_k^2}. Write the exact ordinary split KN=KNlow+HNrad,KNlow(w,z)=∑k=0Jek(w)ek(z),HNrad(w,z)=∑r=0m−1eD−r(w)eD−r(z).(5) K_N=K_N^{\mathrm{ low}}+H_N^{\mathrm{ rad}},\qquad K_N^{\mathrm{ low}}(w,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{J}e_k(w)e_k(z),\qquad H_N^{\mathrm{ rad}}(w,z)=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}e_{D-r}(w)e_{D-r}(z). \qquad\text{(5)} Every radical polynomial vanishes at every distinct physical node. If Lj,NL_{j,N} are the cardinal polynomials of degree at most JJ on c0,…,cJc_0,\ldots,c_J, ordinary interpolation gives KNlow(w,z)=∑j=0JFj,N(w)Lj,N(z).(6) K_N^{\mathrm{ low}}(w,z)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}F_{j,N}(w)L_{j,N}(z). \qquad\text{(6)} Equation (6) is an exact polynomial identity in both variables. Here is a uniform bound on this interpolation at both parities. Put ϑℓ=(2ℓ+1)π/N\vartheta_\ell=(2\ell+1)\pi/N, 0≤ℓ<N0\le\ell<N. The numbers 2cos⁡ϑℓ2\cos\vartheta_\ell fold onto the distinct physical nodes, twice each except for the odd node −2-2, which occurs once. Hence |a0|≤M|a_0|\le M, |ak|≤2M,sup−2≤z≤2|P(z)|≤(2J+1)M≤NM.(8) |a_k|\le2M,\qquad \sup_{-2\le z\le2}|P(z)|\le(2J+1)M\le NM. \qquad\text{(8)} This also proves the interpolation estimate without a separately assumed Lebesgue bound. In particular (4) and (6) imply supc0≤w≤2,−2≤z≤2|KNlow(w,z)|≤N.(9) \sup_{c_0\le w\le2,\ -2\le z\le2} |K_N^{\mathrm{ low}}(w,z)|\le N. \qquad\text{(9)}

3. Full endpoint bounds, including mixed normals

Theorem 1. For every pair of fixed nonnegative integers a,ba,b, there is Ca,bC_{a,b}, independent of NN, such that sup−2≤z≤2|∂wa∂zbKN(2,z)|≤Ca,bN2+2a+2b.(10) \sup_{-2\le z\le2} |\partial_w^a\partial_z^b K_N(2,z)| \le C_{a,b}N^{2+2a+2b}. \qquad\text{(10)} More precisely, the low sector in (10) is bounded by Ca,bN1+2a+2b,(11) C_{a,b}N^{1+2a+2b}, \qquad\text{(11)} and the high sector by Ca,bN2+2a+2bC_{a,b}N^{2+2a+2b}.

Proof. The same root product gives a bound on a shrinking complex disk, not just on a real interval. If |w−2|≤2−c0|w-2|\le2-c_0, then |rj,ℓ−w|≤rj,ℓ−c0|r_{j,\ell}-w|\le r_{j,\ell}-c_0, so |Fj,N(w)|≤1|F_{j,N}(w)|\le1. Cauchy’s formula therefore gives |Fj,N(a)(2)|≤a!(2−c0)−a≤CaN2a,(12) |F_{j,N}^{(a)}(2)|\le a!(2-c_0)^{-a}\le C_aN^{2a}, \qquad\text{(12)} because 2−c0=4sin⁡2(π/(2N))≥4/N22-c_0=4\sin^2(\pi/(2N))\ge4/N^2. Apply (8) to (∂waKNlow)(2,z)(\partial_w^aK_N^{\mathrm{ low}})(2,z), whose physical values are Fj,N(a)(2)F_{j,N}^{(a)}(2). The elementary polynomial Markov inequality on an interval of length LL bounds the first derivative of a degree-dd polynomial by 2d2/L2d^2/L times its sup norm. For complex polynomials the same bound follows by rotating the derivative at the point being estimated and taking the real part. Iteration bounds fixed higher derivatives by the corresponding power. Applying this on [−2,2][-2,2] in zz proves (11). No lower bound on rj,ℓ−2r_{j,\ell}-2 is required.

The full radical source proves supw,z∈[−2,2]|HNrad(w,z)|≤m(N−1).(13) \sup_{w,z\in[-2,2]}|H_N^{\mathrm{ rad}}(w,z)|\le m(N-1). \qquad\text{(13)} Its degree in each variable is at most DD. Markov on each real interval therefore gives the high-sector bound. Add (11) to obtain (10). The cited radical proof is stated for N≥3N\ge3; for N=2N=2, e0=1,e1(z)=−z/4,K2(w,z)=1+wz/16, e_0=1,\quad e_1(z)=-z/4,\quad K_2(w,z)=1+wz/16, which verifies all the assertions directly. ▫\square

Thus a physical first normal in the second color costs only a fixed power of NN; so does a first normal at the endpoint w=2w=2, or any fixed mixed derivative. This includes the odd simple node by evaluation. The estimate was obtained after the alternating low sum was contracted. Taking absolute values of its individual Newton summands is a different estimate and need not have this zero exponential rate.

4. Application to the prescribed full-normal trace

Let AA be an arbitrary ordinary spectator polynomial, or its class in 𝒜N=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2)\mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2). Define its physical seminorm ∥A∥H=max⁡{maxa<m(|A(ca)|+|A′(ca)|),|A(−2)| if N is odd}.(14) \|A\|_{\mathrm{ H}}= \max\left\{\max_{a<m}(|A(c_a)|+|A'(c_a)|), \ |A(-2)|\text{ if }N\text{ is odd}\right\}. \qquad\text{(14)} The original closing is ϕN(f)=η∑a=0m−1{(4−ca2)f′(ca)−caf(ca)}+𝟏N oddf(−2).(15) \phi_N(f)=\eta\sum_{a=0}^{m-1} \{(4-c_a^2)f'(c_a)-c_af(c_a)\} +\mathbf1_{N\text{ odd}}f(-2). \qquad\text{(15)} The double-node formula is the original source residue. At the odd simple node, PN′(−2)=N2P_N'(-2)=N^2 and η=1\eta=1, which gives its displayed coefficient from ϕN=ηN2[zD]rem⁡PN\phi_N=\eta N^2[z^D]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}. Apply (10) to the product rule in (15). For every fixed aa, |ϕN((∂waKN)(2,z)A(z))|≤CaN5+2a∥A∥H.(16) \left|\phi_N\big((\partial_w^aK_N)(2,z)A(z)\big)\right| \le C_aN^{5+2a}\|A\|_{\mathrm{ H}}. \qquad\text{(16)} This is a bound for the literal original trace, including both its value and normal terms. It is coefficientwise in additional free variables, and their derivatives commute with the identity. For several independently traced endpoint incidences it can be iterated using the corresponding full mixed Hermite seminorm of the shared spectator. The spectator is retained: (16) does not estimate it or assert that its whole network contribution is small.

5. A uniform small-height continuation

For r>0r>0 set ρr=2cosh⁡r−2,xr=2−ρr=4−2cosh⁡r<2, \rho_r=2\cosh r-2,\qquad x_r=2-\rho_r=4-2\cosh r<2, Ψ(x)=2π∫0π/2asinh⁡(2−x2coty)dy(x<2).(17) \Psi(x)=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} \operatorname{asinh}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{2-x}}2\cot y\right)dy \quad(x<2). \qquad\text{(17)} Corollary 2. For every fixed r>0r>0, there are Cr,ArC_r,A_r such that, for every N≥2N\ge2, every |w−2|≤ρr|w-2|\le\rho_r, and every complex angle uu, |KNlow(w,−2cos⁡u)|≤CrNAreNΨ(xr)+J|ℑu|.(18) |K_N^{\mathrm{ low}}(w,-2\cos u)| \le C_rN^{A_r}e^{N\Psi(x_r)+J|\Im u|}. \qquad\text{(18)} For the actual full-period endpoint displacement w=2cosh⁡hw=2\cosh h, 0≤h≤r0\le h\le r, this gives the full-kernel bound |KN(2cosh⁡h,−2cos⁡u)|≤CrNAreN[Ψ(xr)+|ℑu|/2]+m(N−1)eD[h+|ℑu|].(19) |K_N(2\cosh h,-2\cos u)| \le C_rN^{A_r}e^{N[\Psi(x_r)+|\Im u|/2]} +m(N-1)e^{D[h+|\Im u|]}. \qquad\text{(19)} In particular the upper exponential rate tends to zero as r→0r\to0 and |ℑu|→0|\Im u|\to0. Constants in (18)–(19) may depend on fixed rr; uniformity as rr shrinks with NN is not asserted. At h=0h=0, the stronger polynomial endpoint theorem already applies.

Proof. The exact root product (3) gives |Fj,N(w)|≤Fj,N(2−|w−2|)≤Fj,N(xr).(20) |F_{j,N}(w)|\le F_{j,N}(2-|w-2|)\le F_{j,N}(x_r). \qquad\text{(20)} For sufficiently large NN, xr<c0x_r<c_0. The actual row recurrence in the last-row source has positive coefficients and starts with Fj+1,N(xr)Fj,N(xr)>1. \frac{F_{j+1,N}(x_r)}{F_{j,N}(x_r)}>1. Indeed its displayed recurrence (2) inducts this inequality from its first ratio. Thus all the right sides of (20) are at most FJ,N(xr)F_{J,N}(x_r). Theorem 1 of that source gives FJ,N(xr)≤CrNAreNΨ(xr).(21) F_{J,N}(x_r)\le C_rN^{A_r}e^{N\Psi(x_r)}. \qquad\text{(21)} Here the omitted interval from J/NJ/N to 1/21/2 changes the exponent by O(N−1)O(N^{-1}), at either parity. Only a fixed color below 22 is used. The finitely many remaining levels can be absorbed in the constants.

Apply the exact coefficient formula (7) to the polynomial in (6). For a complex angle, |Tk(−cos⁡u)|=|cos⁡(ku)|≤ek|ℑu||T_k(-\cos u)|=|\cos(ku)|\le e^{k|\Im u|}. Finally Ψ(xr)→0\Psi(x_r)\to0. For an explicit bound put c=sinh⁡(r/2)c=\sinh(r/2) and assume 0<c≤10<c\le1. Split the integral in (17) at y=cy=c, use asinh⁡(ccot⁡y)≤log⁡(1+2c/y)(0<y≤c),asinh⁡(ccot⁡y)≤ccot⁡y(y≥c). \operatorname{asinh}(c\cot y)\le\log(1+2c/y)\quad(0<y\le c), \qquad \operatorname{asinh}(c\cot y)\le c\cot y\quad(y\ge c). Since sin⁡c≥2c/π\sin c\ge2c/\pi, direct integration yields Ψ(xr)≤2cπ(3log3−2log2+logπ2c)=O(rlog⁡(1/r)).(22) \Psi(x_r)\le\frac{2c}{\pi} \left(3\log3-2\log2+\log\frac{\pi}{2c}\right) =O(r\log(1/r)). \qquad\text{(22)} This establishes the stated limiting rate. ▫\square

Fixed derivatives in ww on a strictly smaller disk than the one in (18) follow by Cauchy’s formula with the larger fixed rr. Fixed spectral derivatives in the other color follow by applying Markov before (7), at an additional polynomial cost. These observations do not enlarge the endpoint or small-height domain to a full semicircular boundary at fixed nonzero height.

The coherent endpoint cancellation and (16) supply an actual boundary kernel estimate. They do not discard the Laurent primitive, angular vertical current, alias completion, or any other side term in a full relative contour identity. A filled-source asymptotic still requires those original terms to be combined and controlled.
