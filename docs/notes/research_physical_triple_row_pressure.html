Uniform pressure and a nonzero amplitude for the original triple-row trace

Private written proof, 17 November 2026.

The positive original scalar Γjkℓ=ϕN(FjFkFℓ)\Gamma_{jk\ell}=\phi_N(F_jF_kF_\ell) has a uniform variational rate on its entire nonzero physical support. On compact subsets of strict macroscopic support, its exact normalization is N−3N^{-3} times a positive analytic amplitude and its exponential action. The optimization is a strictly concave sum of nine ideal-tetrahedron actions.

This controls the actual normal coefficients in the full F/GF/G product basis. An inserted GG row is a different derivative observable with a retained alternating tail; no estimate of that tail or of a subsequent signed closing is inferred by differentiating a continuous degree label.

2. The variational action on the whole physical support

3. Nine positive atoms and the unique strict optimizer

For a+b+c=1a+b+c=1, let V(a,b,c)=−π{𝒥(a)+𝒥(b)+𝒥(c)}.(10) V(a,b,c)=-\pi\{\mathcal J(a)+\mathcal J(b) +\mathcal J(c)\}. \qquad\text{(10)} This is the usual ideal-tetrahedron action on the closed angle simplex. Using 𝒥(1−x)=−𝒥(x)\mathcal J(1-x)=-\mathcal J(x), one has exactly πf(x,t)=V(x−t,t,1−x)+V(x,t,1−x−t).(11) \pi f(x,t) =V(x-t,t,1-x)+V(x,t,1-x-t). \qquad\text{(11)} All angles are nonnegative on 0≤t≤x≤1/20\le t\le x\le1/2. Its three-atom expression is π𝔱(a,b,c)=V(b,a,1−a−b)+V(1−c−a,1−c−b,a+b+2c−1)+V(2−a−b−2c,a+b+c−1,c).(12) \begin{split} \pi\mathfrak t(a,b,c)={}&V(b,a,1-a-b)\\ &+V(1-c-a,1-c-b,a+b+2c-1)\\ &+V(2-a-b-2c,a+b+c-1,c). \end{split} \qquad\text{(12)} These are exactly the three atoms of the original gate, with its output set to 1−c1-c. Write 𝒳∘={𝒙:0<xi<1/2,∑ixi>1}.(13) \mathcal X^\circ =\{\mathbf x:\ 0<x_i<1/2,\ \sum_i x_i>1\}. \qquad\text{(13)} For every 𝒙\mathbf x in (13), the maximum has 0<ti*(𝒙)<xi,∑iti*(𝒙)>1.(14) 0<t_i^*(\mathbf x)<x_i,\qquad \sum_i t_i^*(\mathbf x)>1. \qquad\text{(14)} To prove this, choose a strict feasible state, which exists because ∑ixi>1\sum_i x_i>1, and join any boundary state to it. A coordinate ti=0t_i=0 is impossible on this polytope: the other two are bounded by numbers whose sum is less than one. If ti=xit_i=x_i, the first atom of (11) is V(0,xi,1−xi)V(0,x_i,1-x_i), a nonvertex-flat atom. Its inward directional derivative is +∞+\infty. If ∑iti=1\sum_i t_i=1, the last atom of (12) is V(1−c,0,c)V(1-c,0,c), also nonvertex-flat. Every other atom has either finite or positive infinite inward derivative, never negative infinite derivative. This argument includes simultaneous intersections of these faces. No boundary point can maximize.

Strictness and nondegeneracy can also be checked directly. 4. Uniform nonzero amplitude on strict macroscopic support

For the amplitude use the exact parameters 𝒙N=(j1/N,j2/N,j3/N)\mathbf x_N=(j_1/N,j_2/N,j_3/N). This differs from the convention in the uniform rate (7): the D=N−1D=N-1 shift must now be retained in the amplitude. For a strict state set d=t1+t2+t3−1>0d=t_1+t_2+t_3-1>0 and ℛ(𝒙,𝒕)=∏isin⁡π(xi+ti)∏i<hsin⁡π(ti+th)∏isin⁡π(xi−ti)∏isin⁡3πtisin⁡πd.(18) \mathcal R(\mathbf x,\mathbf t)= \frac{\displaystyle \prod_i\sin\pi(x_i+t_i) \prod_{i<h}\sin\pi(t_i+t_h)} {\displaystyle \prod_i\sin\pi(x_i-t_i) \prod_i\sin^3\pi t_i\ \sin\pi d}. \qquad\text{(18)} All displayed factors are strictly positive.

Theorem 2. For any compact K⊂𝒳∘K\subset\mathcal X^\circ, uniformly over physical triples with 𝒙N∈K\mathbf x_N\in K, Γ𝒋=N−3𝒜(𝒙N)eN𝒢(𝒙N)(1+OK(N−1)),(19) \boxed{\quad \Gamma_{\mathbf j} =N^{-3}\mathcal A(\mathbf x_N) e^{N\mathcal G(\mathbf x_N)} \bigl(1+O_K(N^{-1})\bigr), \quad} \qquad\text{(19)} where the positive real analytic amplitude is 𝒜(𝒙)=π3/28sin⁡(πd*)ℛ(𝒙,𝒕*)det⁡ℋ(𝒙),d*=∑iti*−1.(20) \mathcal A(\mathbf x)= \frac{\pi^{3/2}}{8\sin(\pi d^*)}\, \frac{\sqrt{\mathcal R(\mathbf x,\mathbf t^*)}} {\sqrt{\det\mathcal H(\mathbf x)}}, \qquad d^*=\sum_i t_i^*-1. \qquad\text{(20)} In particular it is bounded above and below by positive constants on KK.

On them the bulk sine-factorial expansion is SNα+c=2Nsin⁡παeN𝒥(α)(2sin⁡πα)c(1+O(N−1)),(21) S_{N\alpha+c} =\sqrt{2N\sin\pi\alpha}\ e^{N\mathcal J(\alpha)} (2\sin\pi\alpha)^c\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr), \qquad\text{(21)} with uniform analytic derivatives on smaller bulk domains. To apply this to the exact finite sum, its possible strip ∑iri/N∈[1−N−1,1)\sum_i r_i/N\in[1-N^{-1},1) can be moved into the limiting polytope by increasing coordinates by a total at most N−1N^{-1}, without leaving their upper box. The remaining lattice has three independent integer coordinates, with no congruence or parity restriction. Its Gaussian factor is N3/2(2π)3/2det⁡ℋ.(24) N^{3/2}\frac{(2\pi)^{3/2}} {\sqrt{\det\mathcal H}}. \qquad\text{(24)} Combining (23) and (24) yields N−3N^{-3} and exactly (20). 5. Actual normal coefficients and the remaining signed observable

The result applies directly to the normal sector of the original full product. For a double node cℓc_\ell, the exact coefficient of GℓG_\ell in FjFkF_jF_k is Bjk;ℓ=η(4−cℓ2)Γjkℓ.(25) B_{jk;\ell} =\eta(4-c_\ell^2)\Gamma_{jk\ell}. \qquad\text{(25)} Therefore (7) controls its entire nonzero macroscopic rate, with only polynomial endpoint factors. On every strict compact of Theorem 2, its full asymptotic is Bjk;ℓ=η(4−cℓ2)N−3𝒜(𝒙N)eN𝒢(𝒙N)(1+OK(N−1)).(26) B_{jk;\ell} =\eta(4-c_\ell^2)N^{-3}\mathcal A(\mathbf x_N) e^{N\mathcal G(\mathbf x_N)} (1+O_K(N^{-1})). \qquad\text{(26)} Here the factor 4−cℓ24-c_\ell^2 is left exact. At the odd simple node there is no normal coefficient; its value coefficient is the same exact triple trace. Theorem 1 covers that row, while the bulk amplitude theorem does not assert a uniform fold or support-boundary expansion.

The inserted first normal ϕN(GjFkFℓ)\phi_N(G_jF_kF_\ell), with cjc_j double, is a separate observable. For precision, if T(FkFℓ)=∑m=0Dhmem,hm≥0,(27) T(F_kF_\ell)=\sum_{m=0}^D h_m e_m,\qquad h_m\ge0, \qquad\text{(27)} with the explicit factorial coefficients of the full-product source, then the original identity is ϕN(GjFkFℓ)=∑m=0Dhmem′(cj).(28) \phi_N(G_jF_kF_\ell) =\sum_{m=0}^D h_m e_m'(c_j). \qquad\text{(28)} For m≤jm\le j, logarithmic differentiation gives em′(cj)=−fj,m∑a=0m−1(ca−cj)−1, e_m'(c_j)=-f_{j,m}\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}(c_a-c_j)^{-1}, with the empty sum zero. For j<m≤D−jj<m\le D-j they instead equal em′(cj)=(−1)m−jSj+mSm−j−1s2j+1Sm2.(29) e_m'(c_j)=(-1)^{m-j} \frac{S_{j+m}S_{m-j-1}}{s_{2j+1}S_m^2}. \qquad\text{(29)} For m>D−jm>D-j they vanish. The alternating tail (29) is retained; it is not present in the positive sum (4). No bound for it follows from Theorem 2 by differentiating (17). The same caution applies to mixed original first normals.

Thus the new estimate gives the actual size and nonzero amplitude of the signed normal coefficient (25), not a positivity replacement for the complete F/GF/G packet. Its subsequent VnV_n action retains the original phase and the value-channel shear (Aℓ,Bℓ)↦λn,ℓ(Aℓ−nαℓBℓ,Bℓ)(A_\ell,B_\ell)\mapsto \lambda_{n,\ell}(A_\ell-n\alpha_\ell B_\ell,B_\ell). Here λn,ℓ=(−1)nℓe−iπnℓ(ℓ+1)/N\lambda_{n,\ell}=(-1)^{n\ell}e^{-i\pi n\ell(\ell+1)/N} and αℓ=i(D−2ℓ)/(4sin⁡((2ℓ+1)π/N))\alpha_\ell=i(D-2\ell)/(4\sin((2\ell+1)\pi/N)) are the original coefficients from the full-product source. The prescribed crossed closing still includes both channels and their coherent quadratic phases. No lower bound for that sum is asserted.

The nine-atom expression provides a concrete positive variational problem with original integer labels. Identifying its external derivatives or internal stationary equations with a supplied parent triangulation requires its actual charge and gluing equations; no geometric volume equality is inferred from the atom decomposition alone.
