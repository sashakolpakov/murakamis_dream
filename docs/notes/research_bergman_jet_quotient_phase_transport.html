Holomorphic phase transport on the original Hermite jet quotient

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,χN=qd2/4,η=(−1)d.(1) P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\qquad \chi_N=q^{d^2/4},\qquad \eta=(-1)^d . \qquad\text{(1)} Fractional powers of qq always mean their displayed exponential, not a newly chosen root of its numerical value. The finite algebra, Newton normalization, original trace, and signed crossing data are those of [C]. We construct norms on this same full algebra, retaining its repeated-root derivatives.

1. The domain and the full jet map

Choose R>2R>2 and the fixed slit disk D={z:|z|<R}\[2,R).(2) D=\{z:|z|<R\}\setminus[2,R). \qquad\text{(2)} Its physical double nodes are ca=2cos⁡((2a+1)π/N)c_a=2\cos((2a+1)\pi/N), 0≤a<⌊N/2⌋0\le a<\lfloor N/2\rfloor. For odd NN, there is also the simple node −2-2. Let JNfJ_N f list f(ca),f′(ca)f(c_a),f'(c_a) at every double node and the odd simple value. Its target is the NN-dimensional algebra 𝒬N=∏aℂ[ϵa]/(ϵa2)with an additional ℂ if N is odd.(3) \mathcal Q_N=\prod_a\mathbb C[\epsilon_a]/(\epsilon_a^2) \quad\text{with an additional \(\mathbb C\) if \(N\) is odd}. \qquad\text{(3)} This is precisely ℂ[z]/(PN)\mathbb C[z]/(P_N), by Hermite interpolation. In particular, the product on (3) retains the derivative product rule.

All nodes lie in DD, and their distances to its boundary satisfy dist⁡(ca,∂D)≥min⁡{R−2,4sin⁡2(π/(2N))}≥cRN−2.(4) \operatorname{dist}(c_a,\partial D) \ge \min\{R-2,\,4\sin^2(\pi/(2N))\}\ge c_R N^{-2}. \qquad\text{(4)} The same bound holds for the odd simple node. An arbitrary bounded domain containing the nodes need not satisfy (4); the explicit choice (2) is used for the polynomial estimates below.

For ss traced variables use DsD^s and the full tensor jet map JN(s)J_N^{(s)}. At a product of double nodes it retains every mixed derivative ∂α\partial^\alpha, αv∈{0,1}\alpha_v\in\{0,1\}; a simple-node slot has only αv=0\alpha_v=0. The target has dimension NsN^s. Tensor products of the one-variable Hermite polynomials prove surjectivity on holomorphic functions.

2. Minimal weighted representatives

Let Φ\Phi be any holomorphic function on DsD^s. Define ℋΦ,N={f∈𝒪(Ds):∥f∥Φ,N2:=∫Ds|f|2e−2NRe⁡ΦdV<∞}.(5) \mathcal H_{\Phi,N} =\left\{f\in\mathcal O(D^s): \|f\|_{\Phi,N}^2 :=\int_{D^s}|f|^2e^{-2N\operatorname{Re}\Phi}\,dV<\infty \right\}. \qquad\text{(5)} Multiplication by e−NΦe^{-N\Phi} is an onto isometry from this space to the unweighted Bergman space ℋ0=A2(Ds)\mathcal H_0=A^2(D^s). Indeed its inverse is multiplication by eNΦe^{N\Phi}. The latter assertion does not require the weight to be bounded. The unweighted space is complete: local mean-value bounds turn an L2L^2-Cauchy sequence of holomorphic functions into a locally uniformly convergent sequence with the same L2L^2 limit.

The holomorphic functions with zero full jets form a holomorphic ideal ℐN⊂𝒪(Ds)\mathcal I_N\subset\mathcal O(D^s): the mixed Leibniz rule proves this assertion. Its intersection IΦ,N=ℐN∩ℋΦ,N=ker⁡JN(s)(6) I_{\Phi,N}=\mathcal I_N\cap\mathcal H_{\Phi,N} =\ker J_N^{(s)} \qquad\text{(6)} is a closed Hilbert subspace. We do not treat ℋΦ,N\mathcal H_{\Phi,N} as an algebra or assert that an unbounded product stays in it. Evaluations and the indicated derivatives are continuous by local mean-value and Cauchy estimates after the gauge in (5).

The restricted jet map in (6) is onto. Given a target jet uu, multiply it in the finite jet algebra by J(e−NΦ)J(e^{-N\Phi}), choose its Hermite polynomial pp, and take eNΦpe^{N\Phi}p. This belongs to (5), since DsD^s is bounded and p∈A2(Ds)p\in A^2(D^s). It has jets uu. Consequently the quotient norm |u|Φ,N=inf⁡{∥f∥Φ,N:Jf=u}(7) |u|_{\Phi,N} =\inf\{\|f\|_{\Phi,N}:Jf=u\} \qquad\text{(7)} is a positive Hilbert norm on the entire original algebra 𝒬N⊗s\mathcal Q_N^{\otimes s}.

Here and below J=JN(s)J=J_N^{(s)}. Let G0=JJ*,S0=J*G0−1,P0=S0J(8) G_0=JJ^*,\qquad S_0=J^*G_0^{-1},\qquad P_0=S_0J \qquad\text{(8)} in the unweighted space. Surjectivity makes G0G_0 positive definite. Orthogonal decomposition by ker⁡J\ker J proves that S0uS_0u is the unique least-norm lift and that P0P_0 is its orthogonal projection. Put ΓΦ=MJ(eNΦ)\Gamma_\Phi=M_{J(e^{N\Phi})}, multiplication in the jet algebra. It is invertible, including all mixed normal entries. The weighted section and projection are exactly SΦu=eNΦS0ΓΦ−1u,PΦf=eNΦP0(e−NΦf),GΦ=ΓΦG0ΓΦ*,|u|Φ,N2=u*GΦ−1u.(9) \begin{split} S_\Phi u&=e^{N\Phi}S_0\Gamma_\Phi^{-1}u,\\ P_\Phi f&=e^{N\Phi}P_0(e^{-N\Phi}f),\\ G_\Phi&=\Gamma_\Phi G_0\Gamma_\Phi^*,\qquad |u|_{\Phi,N}^2=u^*G_\Phi^{-1}u . \end{split} \qquad\text{(9)} These formulas prove existence, uniqueness, jet preservation, and projection norm one, rather than selecting a new polynomial source.

If Φ(z,λ)\Phi(z,\lambda) and u(λ)u(\lambda) depend holomorphically on ordinary exterior parameters, the first formula (9) is holomorphic in (z,λ)(z,\lambda). It uses the fixed finite section S0S_0 and the holomorphic matrix ΓΦ−1\Gamma_\Phi^{-1}. Thus exterior derivatives are retained without differentiating a varying orthogonal basis. The exterior variables are not themselves quotiented.

3. Exact crossing transport

The function τ(z)=arccos⁡(−z/2)2=4arcsin⁡((z+2)/4)2(10) \tau(z)=\arccos(-z/2)^2 =4\arcsin\!\left(\sqrt{(z+2)/4}\right)^2 \qquad\text{(10)} is the single holomorphic continuation on ℂ\[2,∞)\mathbb C\setminus[2,\infty) that is real nonnegative on [−2,2)[-2,2). The square removes the apparent branch at −2-2: the last expression has a power series in z+2z+2 there. Away from that point, if t=arccos⁡(−z/2)t=\arccos(-z/2) in its local branch, τ′=t/sin⁡t\tau'=t/\sin t; this also extends at −2-2.

For any signed integer nn, set θn(z)=in4πτ(z),mn,N(z)=χNneNθn(z).(11) \theta_n(z)=\frac{in}{4\pi}\tau(z),\qquad m_{n,N}(z)=\chi_N^n e^{N\theta_n(z)} . \qquad\text{(11)} This is a nowhere-zero holomorphic function. It represents the original RnR^n in the full algebra. To check that claim directly, at the listed double nodes let ta=π(d−2a)/Nt_a=\pi(d-2a)/N. Then mn,N(ca)=κn(−1)naqna(a+1),κ=qd2/2,mn,N′(ca)mn,N(ca)=in(d−2a)4sin⁡((2a+1)π/N).(12) \begin{split} m_{n,N}(c_a) &=\kappa^n(-1)^{na}q^{na(a+1)},\qquad \kappa=q^{d^2/2},\\ \frac{m_{n,N}'(c_a)}{m_{n,N}(c_a)} &=\frac{in(d-2a)}{4\sin((2a+1)\pi/N)} . \end{split} \qquad\text{(12)} For the first equality, use [d2+(d−2a)2]/4=d2/2+a(a+1)−Na[d^2+(d-2a)^2]/4=d^2/2+a(a+1)-Na. For the second use dt/dz=1/(2sin⁡t)dt/dz=1/(2\sin t). These are exactly [C, (5), (21)]. For odd NN, t=0t=0 at the simple node and (12)’s value formula is χNn\chi_N^n; there is no derivative condition at that node.

It follows pointwise from (5) that multiplication by mn,Nm_{n,N} is an onto isometry ℋΦ,N→ℋΦ+θn,N,∥mn,Nf∥Φ+θn,N=∥f∥Φ,N.(13) \mathcal H_{\Phi,N}\longrightarrow \mathcal H_{\Phi+\theta_n,N},\qquad \|m_{n,N}f\|_{\Phi+\theta_n,N}=\|f\|_{\Phi,N}. \qquad\text{(13)} It maps the zero-jet subspaces onto each other, with inverse m−n,Nm_{-n,N}. Hence the actual finite operator MRnM_{R^n} is an exact isometry between the quotient norms in (7), and PΦ+θn(mn,Nf)=mn,NPΦf.(14) P_{\Phi+\theta_n}(m_{n,N}f)=m_{n,N}P_\Phi f . \qquad\text{(14)} For ss variables this holds with any signed powers nvn_v, the multiplier ∏vmnv,N(zv)\prod_v m_{n_v,N}(z_v), and phase increment ∑vθnv(zv)\sum_v\theta_{n_v}(z_v), even when Φ\Phi is not separable. The original Jordan blocks have been retained. Equation (13) changes the norm and does not make them unitary in one fixed positive metric. Nor does (11) claim equality to the canonical polynomial off its jets.

More generally, if b=eNΘab=e^{N\Theta}a is holomorphic and ∥a∥∞≤B\|a\|_\infty\le B, multiplication gives |J(b)u|Φ+Θ,N≤B|u|Φ,N.(15) |J(b)u|_{\Phi+\Theta,N}\le B|u|_{\Phi,N}. \qquad\text{(15)} Apply the ambient bound to the least-norm representative. An inverse bound follows when a−1a^{-1} is holomorphic and bounded. This states the needed product hypothesis; arbitrary holomorphic spectators need not be bounded multipliers.

4. Evaluation bounds and the original closing

For g=e−NΦfg=e^{-N\Phi}f, product disks of radius comparable to N−2N^{-2} at the nodes, the mean-value inequality and Cauchy’s formula give, for each fixed multi-index α\alpha, |∂αg(c)|≤Cα,s,RN2|α|+2s∥f∥Φ,N.(16) |\partial^\alpha g(c)|\le C_{\alpha,s,R}N^{2|\alpha|+2s}\|f\|_{\Phi,N}. \qquad\text{(16)} Thus the gauge-covariant evaluations have polynomial cost, including the first mixed normals and the endpoint nodes. Ordinary evaluations extract eNΦ(c)e^{N\Phi(c)}; ordinary derivative bounds also contain the explicit derivatives of that gauge. For arbitrary holomorphic Φ\Phi those derivatives are not asserted polynomial. For (11) they are polynomial at the nodes for fixed derivative order and fixed nn: near 22, τ(k)=O(|2−z|1/2−k)\tau^{(k)}=O(|2-z|^{1/2-k}), and away from 22 they are bounded. In particular Nθn(k)(c)=On(N2k)N\theta_n^{(k)}(c)=O_n(N^{2k}) for k≥1k\ge1.

Here is the actual original trace, not an arbitrary evaluation. For a holomorphic germ ff at all nodes, its full Hermite class has ϕN(f)=η∑a{(4−ca2)f′(ca)−caf(ca)}+𝟏Noddf(−2).(17) \phi_N(f)=\eta\sum_a\{(4-c_a^2)f'(c_a)-c_af(c_a)\} +\mathbf1_{N\ {\mathrm{ odd}}}f(-2). \qquad\text{(17)} Indeed [C, (1)–(2)] imply that ϕN\phi_N extracts the ede_d coefficient. The polynomial ede_d has leading coefficient η/N2\eta/N^2, since ∏j=1N−12sin⁡(πj/N)=N\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}2\sin(\pi j/N)=N. The leading coefficient of the Hermite interpolant is the sum of residues of f/PNf/P_N. The Chebyshev differential equation gives, at a double node, PN″(ca)/2=N2/(4−ca2),PN‴(ca)/(3PN″(ca))=ca/(4−ca2). P_N''(c_a)/2=N^2/(4-c_a^2),\qquad P_N'''(c_a)/(3P_N''(c_a))=c_a/(4-c_a^2). At the odd simple node PN′(−2)=N2P_N'(-2)=N^2. These identities give (17), and also the exact Cauchy expression ϕN(f)=ηN22πi∫ΓNf(z)PN(z)dz,(18) \phi_N(f)=\frac{\eta N^2}{2\pi i} \int_{\Gamma_N}\frac{f(z)}{P_N(z)}\,dz, \qquad\text{(18)} where ΓN⊂D\Gamma_N\subset D is the positive union of small circles around every distinct physical node. Products with attachments and the Gaussian (11) retain their full derivatives in this formula. Tensor products give the corresponding original multi-trace.

Write any specified original closing, with its attachment and powers, as a row ℓ\ell on these full jets. The exact norm of that row is ∥ℓ∥Φ,N,*2=ℓGΦℓ*=ℓΓΦG0ΓΦ*ℓ*.(19) \boxed{\ \|\ell\|_{\Phi,N,*}^2=\ell G_\Phi\ell^* =\ell\Gamma_\Phi G_0\Gamma_\Phi^*\ell^*.\ } \qquad\text{(19)} The entries of G0G_0 are the appropriate holomorphic and antiholomorphic derivatives of the unweighted Bergman kernel at the physical nodes. Formula (19) follows either from (9) or by applying the weighted adjoint of JJ to ℓ*\ell^*. It retains cross terms between all physical rows and all their normal components. Under (13), the inverse-dual row ℓnew=ℓMRn−1\ell_{\mathrm{ new}}=\ell M_{R^n}^{-1} has exactly the old dual norm and pairs with the transported source to give the same scalar. This is bilinear source/co-source transport, not replacement of the original closing by a Hermitian positive expectation.

The finite source compiler [C] can therefore be represented exactly on these least-norm ranges: an original finite operation AA becomes SΨAJS_\Psi A J, and a closing remains ℓJ\ell J. Composition is exact because JSΦ=IJS_\Phi=I at each intermediate stage; no estimate for that composition is being assumed. At a fully traced variable any replacement with the same full jets preserves the scalar, including arbitrary original attachment products and all mixed normals. A free exterior variable remains ordinary. Bounds for turns or general compositions do not follow from the crossing isometry alone.

In particular, (19) does not estimate the fixed original row in a geometric phase norm, prove nonzero overlap, or assemble independently repaired spectral windows into a global holomorphic representative with controlled norm. The original exterior and seam terms in (18) must still be transported or estimated. All statements here concern an exact phase-adapted representation of the same finite invariant.
