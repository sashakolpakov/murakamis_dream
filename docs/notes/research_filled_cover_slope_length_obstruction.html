A filled cover cannot make its short meridian geometrically long

25 November 2026. Research proof.

The proposed cover shortcut has a necessary filling-compatibility condition. It prevents using an unbranched cover to turn a fixed short filling into a long one while retaining multiplication of the completed hyperbolic volume by the covering degree. This is a check on that route, not a quantum descent statement or a restriction of the finite-exception target.

Let XX be a complete hyperbolic drilled exterior, and let Y=X(s)Y=X(s) denote some prescribed fillings along primitive slopes sis_i. Suppose p:Y′→Yp:Y'\to Y is a finite unbranched cover of degree DD. Drill every preimage of each filling core to obtain X′→XX'\to X. Equip X′X' with the pullback of the complete drilled metric on XX. For a horotorus TiT_i, let Tij′→TiT'_{ij}\to T_i have degree dijd_{ij}, and define normalized slope length by ℒT(s)=length⁡T(s)area⁡(T). \mathcal L_T(s)=\frac{\operatorname{length}_T(s)} {\sqrt{\operatorname{area}(T)}}. Changing the size of the horotorus does not change this ratio.

The primitive filling meridian sij′s'_{ij} upstairs maps to ±si\pm s_i, rather than to a higher multiple. Indeed each component of the inverse image of the filled solid torus is an unbranched solid- torus cover. A meridian disk downstairs lifts to disks mapped homeomorphically to it, since the disk is simply connected. Their boundaries are the primitive upstairs meridians. Equivalently, the boundary subgroup contains sis_i, the kernel of the map from the boundary fundamental group to that of the solid torus.

The lifted flat metric gives area⁡(Tij′)=dijarea⁡(Ti),length⁡(sij′)=length⁡(si),ℒ(sij′)=ℒ(si)/dij. \operatorname{area}(T'_{ij})=d_{ij}\operatorname{area}(T_i), \qquad \operatorname{length}(s'_{ij})=\operatorname{length}(s_i), \qquad \boxed{\mathcal L(s'_{ij})=\mathcal L(s_i)/\sqrt{d_{ij}}.} In particular the normalized length does not increase. If all the preimage filling cores are retained, ∑jdij=D\sum_jd_{ij}=D for each ii. For the simultaneous parameter ℒtot−2=∑iℒ(si)−2\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tot}}^{-2}=\sum_i\mathcal L(s_i)^{-2}, this gives ℒtot(X′)=ℒtot(X)/D. \mathcal L_{\mathrm{tot}}(X') =\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tot}}(X)/\sqrt D.

A cover of the unfilled exterior can instead have primitive lifted slope mapping to ksiks_i, k>1k>1. Its normalized length is kℒ(si)/dijk\mathcal L(s_i)/\sqrt{d_{ij}}, but that filling map cannot extend unbranched across the core: its meridian has degree kk. A branched completion does not supply the unbranched completed-volume law used by the proposed descent. This distinction does not rule out a different drilling construction or a separately proved branched quantum identity.

The earlier cover analysis gives peripheral isogenies, the marked regulator degree and quantum parameter changes. It does not impose the filled-meridian condition above. The proof here is disk lifting and flat area scaling; no invariant identity is imported. The original source lower bound remains unproved.
