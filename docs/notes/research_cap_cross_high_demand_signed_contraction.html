Exact signed cap/CROSS contraction in a macroscopic high-demand range

31 October 2026. , unpublished analytical theorem for the exact two-port incidence in the original-demand convolution. The original internal face demand, both remaining degrees, and both genuine source cutoffs are retained. This proves a nontrivial range of that note’s proposed comparison. It is not a bound for its remaining degrees, arbitrary spectra, or the whole invariant. The same exact degree window is controlled at every level N>=3: the comparison factor is one at even levels and 4N at odd levels, using the actual odd regularized reference specified in section 7.

1. Statement with the original finite normalization

Let N=2m>=4, d=N-1, M=m-1 and theta=pi/N. Keep the original ports

E=(A,0,C,Z), F=(0,A,0,C),

where A occurs only at these two ports. E is its genuine CROSS polynomial at -2; F is its genuine RIGHT polynomial at +2. Extract the actual A-degree d first. In the notation of the original reduction,

S_(N,n)=sum_(j in J_n) (-1)^j U_j C_(j,n-j),
D_(N,n)=sum_(j in J_n) V_j C_(j,n-j)>0,
J_n=[max(0,n-M),min(M,n)] intersect Z,
0<=n<=2M.                                      (1)

The raw B_N coefficient is (-1)^d S_(N,n), and the positive endpoint coefficient B_N^0 is D_(N,n). No extra N factor occurs.

Theorem. For every even N>=4 and every original remaining degree

ceil(3N/4-3/2)<=n<=N-2,

one has the exact inequality

|S_(N,n)|<=U_M C_(M,n-M)
           =V_M C_(M,n-M)<=D_(N,n).            (2)

Thus the polynomial comparison holds in this entire range with constant one, not just with a polynomial loss. In terms of the other remaining degree k=d-n, the range is

1<=k<=floor(N/4+1/2).

In particular it contains every macroscopic limit n/N->rho>3/4, and includes the stated exact threshold at rho=3/4. The comparison is at the fixed spectra of (1) and at even levels only.

2. A positive injection with every unused factor retained

Recall the literal source recurrence and coefficients

s_r=2 sin(r theta), a_r=4 cos^2((r+1/2)theta),
K_0=1, K_1=s_1^2(A+Z),
K_r=s_r^2(A+Z)K_(r-1)
      +s_r s_(r-1)a_(r-1)AZ K_(r-2),
C_(j,h)=[A^j Z^(d-h-j)]K_(d-h)
                      product_(r=d-h+1)^d a_r. (3)

All these coefficients are positive on the indices below. Let h=n-j and r=d-h. For j in J_n with j<M, append the A-monomer at the next row of K. Its contribution to the coefficient of A^(j+1) Z^(d-n) is s_(r+1)^2 times the preceding coefficient; every other contribution is nonnegative. The unused suffix loses exactly the factor a_(r+1). Therefore

C_(j+1,h-1)>=s_(r+1)^2/a_(r+1) C_(j,h)
              =s_h^2/a_(h-1) C_(j,h).          (4)

The last equality uses r+1=N-h, hence s_(r+1)=s_h and a_(r+1)=a_(h-1). The factor in (4) is not an approximation; it is the exact weight of this subset of original paths, divided by the single unused row removed from the suffix.

In the range of the theorem n>M, so J_n=[L,M] with L=n-M. For j=L,…,M-1, both j and h remain within the actual cutoffs; h>=n-M+1>=1. No full-sum or endpoint extension is being used.

3. Monotonicity of the actual alternating summands

The literal cap ratio is

U_(j+1)/U_j
   =sin^2((j+1)theta)/sin^2((j+3/2)theta).

Put T_j=U_j C_(j,n-j)>0, a=(j+1)theta, b=h theta, and epsilon=theta/2. Combining this ratio with (4) gives

T_(j+1)/T_j
  >=[sin(a) sin(b)/(sin(a+epsilon)cos(b-epsilon))]^2. (5)

The finite threshold in (2) is exactly

n-M+1>=N/4+1/2.

Consequently for every adjacent pair in J_n,

pi/4+epsilon<=a,b<=pi/2-theta.

All denominators in (5) are positive. On the relevant intervals,

sin(a)/sin(a+epsilon)
   and sin(b)/cos(b-epsilon)

are increasing functions of their arguments: their logarithmic derivatives are cot(a)-cot(a+epsilon)>0 and cot(b)+tan(b-epsilon)>0. Comparing the first ratio at a=pi/4 and the second at b=pi/4+epsilon gives

[sin(pi/4)/sin(pi/4+epsilon)]
   [sin(pi/4+epsilon)/cos(pi/4)]=1.

Thus (5) is at least one, and

T_L<=T_(L+1)<=...<=T_M.                         (6)

For a singleton J_n this assertion is vacuous and the next step holds directly.

4. Alternation uses the original cutoff endpoint

For any nonnegative increasing finite sequence, its alternating sum has absolute value at most the final entry. This follows by pairing consecutive terms from the final end for nonnegativity and from the opposite end for the upper bound. Applying it to (6) gives

|sum_(j=L)^M (-1)^j T_j|<=T_M.                 (7)

The original cutoff is crucial: its final cap weight obeys

U_M/V_M
   =product_(r=0)^M cot^2((r+1/2)theta)=1,      (8)

by pairing angles with their complements. In fact U_M=V_M=N: the product over integer sine angles 1,…,m-1 has square N/2^(N-1), while the product over half-integer angles 1/2,…,m-1/2 has square 2^(1-N); the prefactors in U_M and V_M cancel their respective omitted first angles.

The term V_M C_(M,n-M) is present in the original positive sum D_(N,n). Equations (7)–(8) prove (2), with all raw and endpoint normalizations unchanged.

5. Exact scope and remaining issue

Equivalently, restrict the two degree-d polynomials B_N(C,Z) and B_N^0(C,Z), after extracting A^d, to the C degrees in the theorem. The coefficientwise modulus of the restricted B_N is bounded by the same restriction of B_N^0. Every exterior face target and the remaining C,Z degrees are retained. No exterior coupon has been changed, and a signed exterior may only be bounded after its own absolute coefficients are explicitly retained.

This argument uses cancellation in the original alternating sequence. It does not pass through the disproved bare cap-Cauchy norm or absolute tensor coefficient mass. It gives no estimate for lower macroscopic n, and does not identify the projected coupons with unprojected Casimir functions. The full uniform inequality in the original reduction and the general strictly overloaded source bound remain open. No numerical sampling is used in this proof.

6. At low remaining demand the two projections are redundant

There is a separate exact source simplification for 0<=n<=M. This section gives an identity, not another estimate.

The FULL original cap at F, before RIGHT projection, has

H_F(A,C;2)=sum_(j=0)^d (-1)^j U_j A^(d-j)C^j.   (9)

To verify the full range, set gamma_r=2-2cos((2r+1)theta)>0. The protected output middle forces the actual stop to be d. For the start i=d-j, the original raw sign and magnitude are

(-1)^d (-1)^(d-j)=(-1)^j,
product_(r=0)^(d-j-1) gamma_r
                     product_(r=d-j+1)^d s_r^2.

Reflection gives gamma_(d-r)=gamma_r and s_(N-r)=s_r. Thus the magnitude equals product_(r=j+1)^d gamma_r times product_(r=1)^j s_r^2. The exact full product product_(r=0)^d gamma_r=4 identifies it with U_j, including both j=0 and j=d. No upper-half state, derivative datum, or unused row has been discarded.

Likewise the FULL original E coupon at -2, before CROSS projection, is

H_E(A,C,Z;-2)=(-1)^d sum_(h=0)^d sum_(a=0)^(d-h)
                C_(a,h) A^a C^h Z^(d-h-a),     (10)

where the same literal coefficient formula (3) defines C_(a,h) for all these h. The protected input middle forces start zero, so (10) retains every possible actual stop and its unused suffix.

In the product of (9)–(10), extracting the original A^d forces a=j. The remaining C-degree n then forces j+h=n. All exponents are nonnegative. Hence if n<=M, necessarily j<=M and h<=M. These are exactly the genuine RIGHT and CROSS restrictions already imposed in (1). Consequently

[A^d C^n Z^(d-n)] H_F(full;2) H_E(full;-2)
                        =(-1)^d S_(N,n),
0<=n<=M.                                       (11)

This permits using a valid identity of the actual UNPROJECTED Casimir coupons before taking this low-degree coefficient. It does not make the projected coupons covariant operators and does not turn their overlapping pair Casimirs into functions of one commuting operator. Any further tensor identity must keep the original pair order and cup/turn frames. Equation (11) alone supplies no polynomial bound in the missing low- or intermediate-demand ranges.

7. Odd levels retain the same degree window

The reference is the actual spectral regularization at the fixed original root, not the raw odd endpoint. The even statement in sections 1–5 and the even low-demand identity in section 6 remain unchanged.

Let N=2m+1>=3, d=2m, M=m-1, theta=pi/N and keep the original quartets E=(A,0,C,Z), F=(0,A,0,C), with A internal to these two ports. The raw E coupon is V_N(-2), while the raw F coupon is V_N(2), restricted to RIGHT. The positive reference at each port is the ACTUAL polynomial coupon

F_N=V_N(-2)-N^(-2) V_N'(-2),

where the derivative is spectral at the fixed root. Use its CROSS part at E and RIGHT part at F. These are the original odd supports: E has C-middle degree h<=m, and F has A-middle degree d-j>=m+1, or j<=M.

The same literal continuant K_r and suffix coefficients C_(j,h) as in the even theorem describe raw E, with a_m=0. The polynomial has no terms h>m, since those paths miss the central row. The full cap formula U_j is unchanged at every j=0,…,d. Consequently the original signed coefficient after extracting A^d and then C^n Z^(d-n) is

S_(N,n)=sum_(j in J_n) (-1)^j U_j C_(j,n-j),
J_n=[max(0,n-m),min(M,n)] intersect Z, 0<=n<=N-2.

There is no extra raw sign here because d is even. Write V_j^o for the positive F-reference cap coefficient. Its path lies entirely ABOVE the central row when j<=M. Its raw endpoint coefficient therefore vanishes; the spectral derivative removes its one unused zero factor. The exact positive coefficient is

V_j^o=N^(-2)
   product_(r=0,...,d-j-1; r!=m) a_r
   product_(r=d-j+1,...,d) s_r^2.                     (12)

The minus sign in F_N converts the negative spectral derivative to the displayed positive product. Equivalently this follows from the positive u-derivative in H_(V_N(-2-u)), with its coefficient N^(-2).

Let Cbar_(j,h) be the genuine CROSS coefficient of H_(F_N) at E. The odd positive path expansion proves Cbar_(j,h)>=C_(j,h)>=0. Define the actual positive comparator after the same demands by

D^o_(N,n)=sum_(j in J_n) V_j^o Cbar_(j,n-j).

For every odd N>=3 and

ceil(3N/4-3/2)<=n<=N-2,

we claim

|S_(N,n)|<=4N D^o_(N,n).                            (13)

For a singleton J_n the argument below reduces to its endpoint term. Otherwise n>=m and J_n=[L,M], L=n-m. The exact monomer injection is unchanged: for j=L,…,M-1 and h=n-j,

C_(j+1,h-1)>=s_h^2/a_(h-1) C_(j,h).

Its denominator is positive because h<=m and h-1<m; the unused row removed from the suffix is N-h>m. Thus the central zero is never divided out. With a=(j+1)theta, b=h theta, epsilon=theta/2 the original summands T_j=U_j C_(j,n-j) satisfy

T_(j+1)/T_j
   >=[sin(a)sin(b)/(sin(a+epsilon)cos(b-epsilon))]^2.

The stated threshold is n-m+1>=N/4. Hence a>=pi/4 and b>=pi/4+theta, which is stronger than b>=pi/4+epsilon. All denominator angles stay in the positive sine/cosine range, since j+1<=M and h<=m. The two increasing sine ratios, evaluated at a=pi/4 and b=pi/4+epsilon, have product one. Therefore T_j is increasing on the original J_n, and its alternating sum has modulus at most T_M.

The endpoint normalization is exact, not merely asymptotic. The standard full sine products and reflection give

product_(r=0)^(m-1) a_r=N,
product_(r=1)^m s_r^2=N,
s_m^2=4cos^2(theta/2).

The first identity follows because the two noncentral halves have equal products and their total product is N^2. Formula (12) at j=M gives

V_M^o=N^(-2) N [N/s_m^2]=1/[4cos^2(theta/2)].

For U_M, its numerator and denominator products are respectively

product_(r=1)^(m-1) sin^2(r theta)
     =N/[2^(N-1) cos^2(theta/2)],
product_(r=0)^(m-1) sin^2((r+1/2)theta)=2^(1-N).

Thus U_M=N/cos^2(theta/2)=4N V_M^o. Combining monotone alternation with the original endpoint term yields

|S_(N,n)|<=U_M C_(M,n-M)
   =4N V_M^o C_(M,n-M)
   <=4N D^o_(N,n),

which is (13). For N=3 only the singleton case occurs, and the same exact products give U_0/V_0^o=12. No small-level exception is omitted.

The original even theorem and (13) consequently cover the SAME degree range at all N>=3, with constant one at even levels and factor4N at odd levels. The odd reference and its different CROSS cutoff remain explicit. This is a signed two-port coefficient estimate at the stated spectra, not a whole-source bound or a general volume-conjecture conclusion.

The source input is the original odd regularized numerator, sections 1–2: the literal derivative sign, noncentral product N^2, genuine supports, and coefficientwise P0<=H_(F_N). The all-index cap formula follows by the same unused-row reflection as in section 6; its full gamma product is 4 at both parities. No new finite audit or numerical asymptotic experiment is used.

8. Low demand still detects the actual Casimir Jordan data

This final section concerns an EVEN-level source obstruction to a coupon-uniform semisimple adapter. It does not obstruct the particular fixed-resolvent signed inequality or a non-intertwining positive realization.

Use even N=2m>=4, d=N-1, M=m-1 and the original two-port incidence E=(A,0,C,Z), F=(0,A,0,C). For arbitrary polynomial Casimir coupons f,g define the actual low-demand functional

Lambda_n(f,g)=[A^d C^n Z^(d-n)]
    H_f(0,A,0,C) H_g(A,0,C,Z),  0<=n<=M.       (14)

The full homogeneous source fixes every raw normalization in (14). It is legitimate to study unprojected coupons here. In the fixed pair f=V(2), g=V(-2), the original RIGHT/CROSS projections are automatically satisfied after these degrees are extracted, as section 6’s low-demand identity proves.

The following proposed stronger realization is impossible: a semisimple realization of the ACTUAL E Casimir on a space, satisfying its original annihilating polynomial chi_N, which computes (14) uniformly for polynomial g by replacing g(Omega_E) with that same polynomial of the semisimple operator. Boundary vectors/functionals and the other coupon may be arbitrary. A finite-dimensional self-adjoint restriction or quotient intertwining the actual Casimir and preserving all these polynomial-coupon observables would be a special case. No assumption about a particular choice of positive inner product is needed for the obstruction.

A literal nonzero nilpotent coupon

The actual annihilating polynomial at even levels is

chi_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2=4T_m(z/2)^2.

The original complete-circle polynomial is

L_N(z)=(-1)^d U_d(z/2)
       =(-1)^d 2T_m(z/2) U_(m-1)(z/2).        (15)

Here T and U are the ordinary Chebyshev polynomials with their displayed arguments; U_d(z/2) is monic. Thus L_N vanishes at every distinct root of chi_N. Also L_N^2 is a multiple of chi_N, so its value on the actual pair Casimir is square-zero. It need not be zero because the actual source retains confluent data.

For any semisimple operator X with chi_N(X)=0, one has L_N(X)=0. Therefore a coupon-uniform realization of the type in the preceding definition would force Lambda_n(1,L_N)=0. We now check the opposite statement in the original source at every low remaining degree.

Every original low degree detects this coupon

The identity at F specializes to

H_1(0,A,0,C)=A^d.

Consequently (14) becomes exactly

Lambda_n(1,L_N)=[C^n Z^(d-n)]H_(L_N)(0,0,C,Z). (16)

The original complete-channel identity is

H_(L_N)(x,b,c,y)
   =N sum_(a=0)^M k_a beta_a^2
      D_a(x,b,y)D_a(x,c,y) Ptilde_(d-2a)(x,y), (17)

where k_a beta_a^2>0. Each D_a is the actual positive-definite determinant polynomial and every homogeneous degree-a coefficient in its three variables is strictly positive. The retained polynomial Ptilde_s has strictly positive coefficients; at a=M, s=1 and Ptilde_1(x,y)=x+y. The positive determinant theorem proves these exact finite source identities, not coefficient-entropy assertions.

After the specialization in (16), the a=M summand in (17) contains

N k_M beta_M^2 D_M(0,0,Z) D_M(0,C,Z) Z.

The first determinant is a strictly positive multiple of Z^M, and the second has a strictly positive coefficient at every C^n Z^(M-n), 0<=n<=M. Their product therefore contributes positively to C^n Z^(d-n), since 2M+1=d. All other summands in (17) are nonnegative. It follows that

Lambda_n(1,L_N)>0 for EVERY 0<=n<=M.           (18)

As a normalization check, Lambda_0(1,L_N)=N^2. The highest-degree term of L_N is (-1)^d z^d; producing the pure outer monomial Z^d from the actual face operator requires all d lowering steps and gives another (-1)^d times product_(r=1)^d s_r2=N2. Lower powers cannot produce that outer degree. The two signs cancel.

Equations (15) and (18) contradict the proposed coupon-uniform semisimple realization. In particular the low original coefficient filtration has not annihilated the complete-circle nilpotent. This is an all-even-level/all-low-degree source statement, not a numerical example or an unconditioned coefficient obstruction.

Scope of the obstruction

The actual signed target uses the particular pair V(2),V(-2). The proof does not exclude an identity specialized to this pair, a positive representation that does not intertwine arbitrary Casimir polynomials, or a bound that retains explicit Jordan corrections. It does exclude calling such a construction an actual semisimple Casimir restriction while also claiming preservation of every polynomial-coupon observable in (14).

No estimate for the signed coefficient, no hyperbolic-volume statement, and no obstruction to the original volume conjecture is inferred. The only inputs are the actual homogeneous source, its full complete polynomial with confluent data, and the exact positive determinant formula, all already .

Inputs

The exact normalization and local incidence are those of the original two-port source reduction. The full-interval source used in section 6 is the original four-state source identity; its homogeneous physical adapter keeps the original pair order and all matrix data.

The obstruction is to a coupon-uniform semisimple adapter. It does not rule out a special identity for the fixed coupon or an explicit treatment of confluent terms.
