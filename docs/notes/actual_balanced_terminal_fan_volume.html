Balanced terminal decorations and volume limits beyond tangle-operation trees

A crossing circle surrounds two strands and bounds their twice-punctured disk. A terminal decoration inserts another such two-strand piece in an actual product collar on one side of an original crossing disk. The original diagram may have arbitrary cycles; it is not required to be built from a tree of two-strand products.

The starting condition is combinatorial: the actual connected augmented diagram has a simple triangulated white sphere, with its recorded crossing-disk matching and cyclic orders. The proof constructs its positive right-angled geometry, then adds the terminal pieces so that one shared complete circle packing controls every sector of the original quantum source. All data are fixed before the level tends to infinity.

1. The theorem and its quantifiers

Let DD be a fixed connected augmented link diagram with the following data.

  • Its white triangulation is a simple triangulated sphere, with the original perfect matching of dual triangles and four distinct white vertices at each matched quadrilateral.
  • Its simple white sphere has at least four vertices; the positive geometric certificate is constructed below, not imposed separately.
  • A strand edge, meaning a nonmatching side in the white triangulation and called MAIN in the source formulas, is marked. The associated two protected white curvatures are zero in the actual marked complete packing.
  • The cyclic order, original half-twist frame and disk-framed meridian/longitude at every crossing circle are fixed.
  • A parity vector for the original boxes makes their filled main strands a knot, with no internally closed extra component.

There is a finite literal terminal decoration D̂\widehat D of this diagram with even numbers of new terminal pieces on both sides of each original box such that:

  1. The original endpoint pairing and knot condition are preserved when each new terminal power is odd.
  2. The refined white sphere is simple, has its actual positive cut certificate, and its one marked complete packing satisfies the strict complete-radius balance inequalities at every old and new crossing circle.
  3. There is a threshold T(D̂)T(\widehat D) such that, for every fixed integer filling vector pp with all |pe|≥T(D̂)|p_e|\ge T(\widehat D), independently in sign and size, and the recorded retained half twists ϵe∈{−1,0,1}\epsilon_e\in\{-1,0,1\}, the actual powers ne=ϵe−2pen_e=\epsilon_e-2p_e produce a hyperbolic knot KpK_p and limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(Kp)|=Vol⁡(S3\Kp). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K_p)| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K_p). \tag{T1} Here KNK_N is the original zero-framed reduced complete-color invariant at qN=eπi/Nq_N=e^{\pi i/N}, with its recorded enhanced component convention. Both parities of NN occur. The same logarithmic conclusion holds in the specified full one-cut modified convention after retaining its known polynomial-level conversion.

The diagram, decorations and signed powers are fixed before NN. The threshold may depend on the whole decorated diagram; there is no uniform bound over all cores or a growing-diagram limit. The core’s original powers are also filled sufficiently long in this theorem. The result is not an assertion for arbitrary short fillings of the core. The decorated knot generally differs from the core knot.

2. A simple sphere supplies the actual metric

A combinatorial starting hypothesis

It is enough to start with an actual connected marked augmented diagram whose white cellulation is a simple triangulated sphere with at least four vertices. The matching pairs, cyclic orders and marked MAIN edge are those of its projection/crossing-disk cut. A positive geometric certificate need not be supplied independently.

3. The original link and a knot parity

From a matched sphere to an actual knot diagram

Conversely begin with any finite oriented simple triangulated sphere with at least four vertices and a chosen perfect matching of its cubic dual. Two matched triangles share one edge, have distinct third vertices, and form an embedded quadrilateral with four distinct boundary vertices. Choose disjoint small neighborhoods of the matched dual edges and their endpoints. Contracting these disks gives a connected planar four-valent graph. At each disk use the original ordered untwisted pairing (0,1),(2,3)(0,1),(2,3); join the outside terminals by the embedded nonmatching dual edges. Put the standard perpendicular crossing circle in a disjoint three-ball neighborhood of each disk. This constructs an augmented link in S3S^3, with exactly the prescribed cut graph and disk frames.

Choose a regular height after straightening each local box in its orientation-preserving frame. The outside arcs give finitely many ordinary cups and caps and no new crossing. This is the literal general Morse diagram, not an assumed flat-plat presentation. A MAIN edge may be chosen for the original reduced mark.

The graph whose vertices are the untwisted main circles and whose edges are the crossing boxes is connected. Otherwise the union of boxes and outside arcs would split the connected planar graph. Choose a spanning tree of this graph and give exactly those boxes odd parity. Switching one tree edge to the odd pairing joins two different current circles. Induction along any order of the tree edges gives one main component, with no internal extra component. The odd signs are arbitrary. Subsequent full twists preserve this pairing, and the even terminal fans preserve it as well. This proves the combinatorial corollary without asserting that every arbitrary tensor graph is a knot diagram.
