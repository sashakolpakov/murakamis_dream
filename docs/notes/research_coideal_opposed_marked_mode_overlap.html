A signed lower bound for the opposed marked mode

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

The original marked boundary has explicit left and right modes in the coideal height frame. With the same crossing sign their pairing is an elementary cap slide. With the opposite sign it is a quadratic oscillatory sum. This note proves that the latter pairing has a positive real part after one specified, label-independent phase is removed, at every height and all sufficiently large levels. The lower bound is polynomial.

The estimate also solves the opposite-sign normal equation in the gauge specified by the original marked row. It does not give a lower bound for contraction against an arbitrary coherent exterior or for the original closed knot scalar.

9. Source scope and coverage

The new assertion is a signed scalar lower bound for the actual opposed marked modes, uniformly over all their height channels eventually, and the resulting local normal equation with the original marked gauge. Its bulk leading amplitude is explicit. The Gaussian, Wallis, Abel and Fresnel tools themselves are classical. The proofs above retain their phases and endpoint normalizations.

The normal residual in (51) is transverse to a vector line. It is not a pair-Casimir derivative or a moving-level first normal. The full physical height frames retain those original normal channels. At larger fixed width the original earlier ordinary caps, their Kronecker constraints and their source factors remain exactly [C,H]’s. The factor βN\beta_N in (7), and any original χN\chi_N, must be restored when reading (6) as a boundary coefficient.

The row is observed at the literal marked boundary. As in [N], rl=rlPlr_l=r_lP_l permits selecting its support only at that final observation. The path IlI_l is not declared invariant under an arbitrary preceding crossing word. Neither (8) nor (51) assigns nonnegative coefficients to an arbitrary coherent exterior. The remaining marked amplitude after general transport is not controlled by a normal residual alone. No geometric gluing cycle, original whole-knot lower bound or finite-exception volume theorem follows from this local estimate.
