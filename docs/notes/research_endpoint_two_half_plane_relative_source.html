Two half-plane representatives and the exact endpoint-product interface

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

Every fixed product of full microscopic endpoint kernels, at an original traced vertex, has an exact pair of representatives with polynomial bounds on their respective half-planes. Each preserves all angular Hermite jets. Using the lower representative below the real axis and the upper representative above it leaves an explicit holomorphic interface current. The original Gaussian, all spectators, and every vertical side remain.

The projection and Fourier folding are archived identities. The new assembly applies them to the actual full shared endpoint product, with a polynomial coefficient bound and the complete relative contour. No intermediate Newton cutoff is introduced.

4. Shared graph placement and scope

All identities are coefficientwise in uncontracted variables. At every traced vertex multiply its full endpoint coupons first, take the full-Hermite remainder, and then apply (9)–(17). Operations in distinct variables commute. Their tensor product gives the finite sum of exterior-half and interface integrals for a fixed graph, retaining every shared edge and all remaining original traces. Outside mixed derivatives commute with the identities. The spectator itself is not replaced by a value-only interpolant.

There is no canonical reduction in an ordinary free variable. If the unreduced product QNQ_N is retained on separate contour sides, its difference from CNC_N has an additional holomorphic current after multiplication by WNW_N; that current must remain. In (16), full quotient reduction has already been performed inside the exact closed original trace.

The construction uses only full endpoint products. It is independent of a low/high truncation or a central physical window. It avoids a globally even moderate representative by using two angular representatives with the explicit interface. It supplies no bound for their Gaussian-spectator product at the filled action and no general inserted-descendant estimate.
