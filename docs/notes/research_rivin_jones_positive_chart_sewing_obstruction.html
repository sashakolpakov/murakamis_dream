Two local positive crossing charts cannot be sewn across a two-crossing twist

Private written deduction, 14 November 2026. No frozen manuscript, external code, numerical calculation, or test is used here.

The exact Rivin-to-Newton adapter identifies an arbitrary signed block with its original physical block. It does not supply a new positive complex contour. In fact the existing exact physical Fourier kernel already gives nonzero local densities and a positive octahedral chart. Two source-specific calculations below show why applying that chart independently at every original crossing cannot prove general short filling. The obstruction occurs in the actual two-crossing block R+2R_+^2, before any arbitrary replacement exterior is chosen.

1. The actual local positive chart

Use the exact four-function Fourier kernel from the balanced crossing theorem, including its original Cartan scalar, Gaussian, and logarithmic marks. For its input ports z0,z1z_0,z_1 and output ports z6,z7z_6,z_7, the classical shapes in its signed regulator are u=−z1/z0,v=−z6/z7,w=z7/z1,D(u)+D(v)+D(w)−D(uvw).(1) u=-z_1/z_0,\qquad v=-z_6/z_7,\qquad w=z_7/z_1, \qquad D(u)+D(v)+D(w)-D(uvw). \qquad\text{(1)} The positive chart uses the four positively oriented shapes u,v,w,(uvw)−1u,v,w,(uvw)^{-1}. On its unit-angle locus their angles lie in (0,π)(0,\pi) and sum to 2π2\pi. Its kernel has the nonzero source-normalized density proved in the cited note.

Now compose two positive crossings on the same adjacent heads, using the coherently propagated Fourier frames. Those frames make the interior adapters exactly the identity, as proved in the propagated-frame theorem. The first crossing’s output ports are literally the second crossing’s input ports: z0′=z6,z1′=z7. z'_0=z_6,\qquad z'_1=z_7. Consequently their shapes obey the exact algebraic relation u′=1v.(2) u'=\frac1v. \qquad\text{(2)}

Lemma 1. The product of these two local positive octahedral charts has empty intersection with the actual interior sewing equations.

Proof. For every nonreal vv, Im⁡(1/v)=−Im⁡v|v|2. \operatorname{Im}(1/v) =-\frac{\operatorname{Im}v}{|v|^2}. Thus Im⁡v>0\operatorname{Im}v>0 and Im⁡u′>0\operatorname{Im}u'>0 cannot both hold under (2). ▫\square

This concerns the original signed shapes in (1). Cyclic positive quad rotations do not repair it: the maps zz, 1/(1−z)1/(1-z), and 1−1/z1-1/z each preserve the sign of the imaginary part. Declaring the reciprocal tetrahedron positive would change its orientation and its signed action unless an additional source identity is supplied.

At finite level the full functions have their recorded qq-shifts. These do not permit both crossings to lie in fixed compact subsets of their strict positive classical charts as N→∞N\to\infty. The limiting relation remains (2). Any apparent repair confined to angles of order 1/N1/N approaches a degenerate boundary rather than a strict positive geometric chart.

The lemma is independent of the external boundary parameters. In particular, requiring those parameters to arise from a positive hyperbolic geometric representation does not make these two original local charts compatible. It does not exclude a positive triangulation of the component after fusing or changing its internal decomposition. Such a change needs its own exact source comparison.

2. The same two-crossing block has a nontrivial sewn deck character

There is a separate exact obstruction to treating the finite Fourier polynomial as the raw four-function continuation on a whole complex torus. Put s=(−1)N+1s=(-1)^{N+1}. Use logarithmic ports (a,b;c,d)(a,b;c,d) for the first crossing and (c,d;e,f)(c,d;e,f) for the second. Their central coordinates are A=eNa,B=eNb,C=eNc,D=eNd,E=eNe,F=eNf. A=e^{Na},\ B=e^{Nb},\ C=e^{Nc},\ D=e^{Nd},\ E=e^{Ne},\ F=e^{Nf}. Write MN(a,b,c,d)M_N(a,b,c,d) for the specific raw full-function continuation fixed in the deck-character calculation, and set ℳN=MN(a,b,c,d)MN(c,d,e,f).(3) \mathscr M_N=M_N(a,b,c,d)M_N(c,d,e,f). \qquad\text{(3)} No contour transformation is implicit in (3).

The exact local full-turn characters needed here are μa=sC(A−sB)B(A−C),μb=s(D−B)A−sB,μc=s(A−C)D−sC,μd=sB(D−sC)C(D−B).(4) \mu_a=\frac{sC(A-sB)}{B(A-C)},\quad \mu_b=\frac{s(D-B)}{A-sB},\quad \mu_c=\frac{s(A-C)}{D-sC},\quad \mu_d=\frac{sB(D-sC)}{C(D-B)}. \qquad\text{(4)} The sewn characters in the two internal variables are their actual products. Cancellation of the displayed factors gives νc=−sE(A−C)D(C−E),νd=−sB(F−D)C(D−B).(5) \nu_c=-\frac{sE(A-C)}{D(C-E)},\qquad \nu_d=-\frac{sB(F-D)}{C(D-B)}. \qquad\text{(5)} These identities hold wherever the written ratios are regular and extend meromorphically across removable factors.

The propagated two-crossing central frames have C=sB,D=sA,E=A,F=B.(6) C=sB,\quad D=sA,\quad E=A,\quad F=B. \qquad\text{(6)} Keep the external central values E=A,F=BE=A,F=B, but allow the internal ports to move. Then (5) simplifies to νc=sAD,νd=sBC.(7) \nu_c=\frac{sA}{D},\qquad \nu_d=\frac{sB}{C}. \qquad\text{(7)} Thus identity interior frame adapters do not make this raw continued product periodic away from the original balanced sampling multisection.

3. A microscopic movement already forces a same-order correction

Choose regular nonzero A,BA,B with B≠sAB\ne sA, and fix a nonzero constant λ≠1\lambda\ne1 sufficiently close to one to avoid the other displayed divisors. Starting at (6), move only c↦c+log⁡λN.(8) c\longmapsto c+\frac{\log\lambda}{N}. \qquad\text{(8)} This changes CC to λsB\lambda sB, while D=sAD=sA remains. It is precisely the microscopic scale of a change between bounded Fourier-radius frames. Equations (7) now give νc=1,νd=λ−1.(9) \nu_c=1,\qquad \nu_d=\lambda^{-1}. \qquad\text{(9)} The defect is independent of NN, including its parity.

Let 𝒫N\mathscr P_N be the product of the actual two finite Fourier Laurent-polynomial crossing interpolants, with the same shared ports. It is fixed by the original finite matrices. In particular it is 2πi2\pi i-periodic in dd. It agrees with (3) on the original sampling grids, but no off-grid equality is assumed. Let pp be the point after (8), and let p+p^+ be the same point with dd replaced by d+2πid+2\pi i.

Lemma 2. Wherever the raw product is regular, max⁡{|𝒫N(p)−ℳN(p)|,|𝒫N(p+)−ℳN(p+)|}≥|1−λ−1|2|ℳN(p)|.(10) \max\bigl\{ |\mathscr P_N(p)-\mathscr M_N(p)|, |\mathscr P_N(p^+)-\mathscr M_N(p^+)| \bigr\} \ \ge\ \frac{|1-\lambda^{-1}|}{2}|\mathscr M_N(p)|. \qquad\text{(10)}

Proof. Periodicity gives 𝒫N(p+)=𝒫N(p)\mathscr P_N(p^+)=\mathscr P_N(p), whereas the exact character (9) gives ℳN(p+)=λ−1ℳN(p)\mathscr M_N(p^+)=\lambda^{-1}\mathscr M_N(p). Subtract the two errors and apply the triangle inequality. ▫\square

In particular there is no relative o(1)o(1) identification of the actual finite polynomial with the raw full-function continuation on a domain containing both lips, even after a displacement of order 1/N1/N. If the raw product has a given exponential rate along the chosen points, at least one correction in (10) has that rate or larger. This is a statement about density values. It does not give a lower bound for an integrated seam period, whose terms may themselves cancel.

The exact one-port and paired-port interpolation theorems in the physical port-polynomial note remain valid. Their fixed opposite grids and retained central factors are exactly what distinguish them from the simultaneous raw continuation ruled out by (10).

4. Consequence for the short-filling route

These are two concrete gaps in a proposed universal argument from nonzero local Fourier kernels to a nonzero geometric coefficient. The original two-crossing twist cannot be placed in the product of the known strict positive local charts. Its raw continued Fourier product also needs a correction of nonnegligible relative size when microscopic frame movements are made across a full logarithmic cycle.

Thus a general proof must do more than complete each crossing into its known nonzero full-function block, reflect the negative crossings, and apply positive-volume concavity. It must transform or fuse the actual block before imposing the positive chart, and preserve the finite boundary and seam source in that transformation. Neither lemma rules out such a transformation, or the original volume conjecture. They identify precisely why the new local matrix adapter alone does not provide it.
