Exact coherent inner elimination for the original pretzel polynomial

29 October 2026. Reversing the TWO ACTUAL finite sums in the complete pretzel box gives a balanced terminating finite transformation. It retains the half shift and proves a nonzero, uniformly tame inner packet throughout the geometric range r>=N/4, at BOTH parities. An explicit nonzero leading amplitude follows on compact subintervals of 1/4<r/N<1/2. No stationary-cycle assumption is used for this inner sum. The remaining outer sum and the all-r upper bound are separate.

The exact all-r recurrence below retains the same original source, including the part below N/4 which has NOT been discarded.

1. The unchanged source and a generic finite identity

Use q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, d=N-1, M=floor(d/2), F_a=(Q;Q)_a. In formula (11a) of the original complete box, interchanging the FINITE v,r sums gives exactly

kappa z_N(z)/N^2
  =sum_(r=0)^M (-1)^r Q^(r(r+1)/2) P_r(z) A_r/F_r^3,
A_r=sum_(v=r)^M Q^(-vr)F_(2v)/(F_v^3 F_(v-r)),
P_r(z)=(qw;Q)_r(q/w;Q)_r, z=w+w^-1.              (1)

This retains every original coefficient, all half-twist and Cartan phases, and the full polynomial BEFORE its marked Hermite period. There is no removal of boundary r=v. Put n=M-r and

D_r=Q^(-r^2)F_(2r)/F_r^3, U_r=A_r/D_r.

Then

U_r=sum_(k=0)^n
   (qQ^r;Q)_k(-qQ^r;Q)_k(-Q^(r+1);Q)_k Q^(-rk)
                     /[F_k(Q^(r+1);Q)_k^2].     (2)

Exactly one of the three numerator parameters is Q^(-n): -qQ^r at odd N, and -Q^(r+1) at even N. All denominators are nonzero throughout the TRUE finite ranges.

Here is the finite transformation used in both cases. For generic parameters with nonzero displayed denominators,

sum_(k=0)^n (Q^-n;Q)_k(a;Q)_k(b;Q)_k
              (cdQ^n/(ab))^k/[F_k(c;Q)_k(d;Q)_k]
  =(c/a;Q)_n/(c;Q)_n
    *sum_(j=0)^n (Q^-n;Q)_j(a;Q)_j(d/b;Q)_j Q^j
                /[F_j(d;Q)_j(aQ^(1-n)/c;Q)_j].   (3)

This is proved here by finite summation, not assumed from a named transformation theorem. The terminating identity

sum_(j=0)^k (Q^-k;Q)_j(t;Q)_j Q^j/[F_j(d;Q)_j]
              =t^k(d/t;Q)_k/(d;Q)_k             (4)

was proved by two finite binomial expansions in the elementary q-Chu proof. Its balanced version is

sum_(j=0)^k (Q^-k;Q)_j(a;Q)_j(cQ^k/a)^j
                  /[F_j(c;Q)_j]=(c/a;Q)_k/(c;Q)_k. (5)

For completeness, reverse the summation in (5). After its last term is factored out, the remaining sum is (4) with parameters t=Q^(1-k)/c and d=Q^(1-k)/a. The factorial reversal (Q(1-k)/a;Q)_k=(-a)(-k)Q^(-k(k-1)/2)(a;Q)_k cancels that last-term factor and gives (5).

To prove (3), expand

(b;Q)_k/(d;Q)_k
  =(b/d)^k sum_(j=0)^k
    (Q^-k;Q)_j(d/b;Q)_j Q^j/[F_j(d;Q)_j]

using (4), interchange the finite k,j sums, and put k=j+l. The l sum is (5) with parameters Q(j-n),aQj,cQ^j and argument cQ^(n-j)/a. It equals (c/a;Q)(n-j)/(cQ^j;Q)(n-j). Cancel (c;Q)j(cQ^j;Q)(n-j)=(c;Q)_n and use

(c/a;Q)_(n-j)/(c/a;Q)_n
  =(-c/a)^(-j)Q^(-nj+j(j+1)/2)
                       /(aQ^(1-n)/c;Q)_j.

All remaining powers combine to Q^j. This proves (3) exactly, and rational continuation gives every root specialization used here.

2. Odd levels: a coherent quarter-phase series

Let N=2M+1, n=M-r. Substitution in (3) gives

U_r=(q;Q)_n/(Q^(r+1);Q)_n
  *sum_(j=0)^n
   (Q^(2r+1);Q^2)_j(-1;Q)_j Q^j
                        /[(Q^(2r+2);Q^2)_j F_j]. (6)

Every summand on the second line is EXACTLY i^j h_j, h_j>0. Its consecutive positive magnitudes obey, with delta=pi/N,

h_(j+1)/h_j
  =sin((2r+1+2j)delta)/sin((2r+2+2j)delta)
                              *cos(j delta)/sin((j+1)delta).

The prefactor has phase q^(-n(r+1/2)), so its product with i^n has the EXACT phase q(n2), using N=2M+1. Reverse the series. Direct cancellation of its positive products gives

U_r=q^(n^2) B_(N,n) sum_(l=0)^n (-i)^l g_l,       (7)

B_(N,n)=product_(k=0)^(n-1)
   cos(k delta)cos((k+1)delta)/cos((k+1/2)delta)^2,

g_l=product_(k=1)^l
   [sin((2k-1)delta)/sin(2k delta)]
                [sin((n-k+1)delta)/cos((n-k)delta)], g_0=1.

Equation (7) holds for EVERY 0<=r<=M, not just the geometric range. All its factors are retained. In particular a half-shift amplitude has not been replaced by one before an exponentially canceling sum.

Now suppose r>=ceil(N/4), hence n delta<=pi/4. Then h_j is strictly increasing: its sine ratio is at least one on the descending half of the sine interval, and cos(j delta)>sin((j+1)delta). Thus g_l decreases. The real part of the reversed alternating parity sum is at least 1-g_2 (or exactly one for n=0,1). For n>=2,

g_2 <= tan(n delta)^2/[2cos(delta)]
     <=1/[2cos(pi/9)]<1-5/12,

since then N>=9. Concavity and |(log cos)’’|<=2 on [0,pi/4] give

exp(-pi^2/(8N))<=B_(N,n)<=1.

These elementary bounds, with the n=0,1 cases separately, prove

1/3<=|U_r|<=N,        r>=ceil(N/4), N odd.        (8)

Moreover g_l<=tan(n delta)^l. On any compact subinterval of 1/4<r/N<1/2, put beta=n delta. The product formula gives

g_l=((1/2)_l/l!)tan(beta)^l[1+O(l^2/N)]

for l in a fixed small linear fraction of N; the exact geometric bound controls the remaining tail. Choosing that fraction small enough that tan(beta)exp(C epsilon)<1, summing the errors proves UNIFORMLY

U_r=q^(n^2)[(1+i tan beta)^(-1/2)+O(1/N)].         (9)

The square root is the branch with positive real part. Its modulus is sqrt(cos beta), so the amplitude is nonzero, with no unknown cycle coefficient. The error statement uses the actual discrete beta=n pi/N, not a reset parity-independent angle.

3. Even levels: an alternating series with a retained small chirp

Let N=2M+2, n=M-r. A different permissible ordering in (3) gives

U_r=(-q;Q)_n/(Q^(r+1);Q)_n
   *sum_(j=0)^n
    (-Q^(r+1);Q)_j(-qQ^r;Q)_j(q;Q)_j Q^j
             /[F_j(Q^(r+1);Q)_j(q^3Q^r;Q)_j].   (10)

The j-th term is EXACTLY (-q(1/2))j h_j, h_j>0. The principal q^(1/2)=exp(pi i/(2N)) is retained; the series is not declared real. Its positive ratio is

h_(j+1)/h_j
  =cos((r+1+j)delta)cos((r+1/2+j)delta)sin((j+1/2)delta)
    /[sin((r+1+j)delta)sin((r+3/2+j)delta)sin((j+1)delta)].

For r>=ceil(N/4), every ratio is below one and in fact h_j<=cot(r delta)^(2j). Pair consecutive even/odd terms. Each pair’s real part is nonnegative because h_j decreases and the cosine of its retained phase decreases on the range 0<=j delta/2<=pi/8. The first pair alone gives Re(sum)>=1-h_1>1/3; n=0 gives one. Indeed h_1<=1/[2cos(delta/2)], with N>=8 when n>=1.

The prefactor’s positive magnitude is

R_(N,n)=product_(k=0)^(n-1)
          cos((k+1/2)delta)/cos((k+1)delta),
1<=R_(N,n)<=1/cos(n delta)<=sqrt(2).

Its EXACT phase is sigma_r=i^n q(-n(r+1/2))=q(n(n+1/2)). Therefore

1/3<=|U_r|<=2N,       r>=ceil(N/4), N even.       (11)

On compact subintervals 1/4<r/N<1/2 the geometric domination and the fixed-j sine products prove, by the same summable estimate,

sum in (10)=sin(r delta)+O(1/N),
R_(N,n)=(cos(n delta))^(-1/2)[1+O(1/N)].

Consequently, uniformly on those compact sets,

U_r=q^(n(n+1/2))[sqrt(sin(r delta))+O(1/N)].        (12)

Equations (8),(11) prove the genuine all-level exponential equivalence |A_r|=|D_r| exp(O(log N)) throughout the geometric range. Equations (9),(12) sharpen it to an explicit nonzero amplitude and phase.

4. Newton phases and the precise remaining lower-index problem

For odd N, use P_r(z)=q(r2)product_(a<r)(c_a-z), c_a=qQa+q(-1)Q^(-a). The factorial phases in (1),(7) cancel:

kappa z_N(z)/N^2
  =q^(M^2) sum_(r=0)^M [s_(2r)/s_r^6]
         B_(N,M-r) Srev_r product_(a<r)(c_a-z),
Srev_r=sum_(l=0)^(M-r)(-i)^l g_l.                (13)

For r>=ceil(N/4), Re Srev_r>0 and Im Srev_r<=0, with the uniform quantitative lower bound above. Thus the high-index Newton packet has a common quadrant at a real z<-2, with no macroscopic phase cancellation. This is an EXACT coherent packet of the original box, not a sum of separate absolute-valued expanded states.

For r<N/4 the transformed identities STILL HOLD but the monotonicity and geometric domination used in (8)–(12) fail. No polynomial bound for all such Srev_r, or a global common quadrant, is inferred here. In particular these lower indices remain in the original outer sum. Even (10) is decreasing only in the displayed high-index range. No sharp global circle bound, original outer-cycle survival, or full pretzel volume theorem has been proved by this inner calculation.

5. An exact all-r source Ward equation

There is nevertheless a low-order equation controlling the ENTIRE remaining sequence, including the lower-index range. With Y=Q^r,

QY^2(1-Q^2Y) A_(r+2)
  +Y(2Q^3Y^2+Q+1) A_(r+1)
  -Q(QY+1)(QY^2-1) A_r=0,                      (14)

A_M=Q^(-M^2)F_(2M)/F_M^3, A_(M+1)=0.

Here A_r=0 for r>M. The backwards coefficient in (14) is nonzero for r<M at both parities, so these are the LITERAL selected endpoint conditions, not an unspecified solution of an annihilating equation.

Proof. For t(v,r)=Q(-vr)F_(2v)/(F_v3F_(v-r)), z=Q^v,

t(v+1,r)/t(v,r)
  =Y^(-1)(1-Qz^2)(1+Qz)/[(1-Qz)^2(1-Qz/Y)].

The rational certificate g(z)=(1-z)2(1-z/Y)/z2 obeys

g(Qz)t(v+1,r)/t(v,r)-g(z)
  =(1/(YQ^2)-1)z^-2
    +(1/(YQ)+2+1/Y)z^-1-1/(YQ)-1-2/Y.

The sum telescopes: g(Q^r)=0 and t(M+1,r)=0 by its literal root factor. Finally t(v,r+1)/t(v,r)=z-1-Y-1, so its first two moments are A_(r+1)+Y^-1 A_r and A_(r+2)+(1+Q-1)Y-1A_(r+1)+Y^-2A_r. Substitution yields (14).

The classical characteristic ratios of (14) are (Y+1)^2/[Y(Y-1)] and (Y-1)/Y; their moduli cross at r/N=1/4. Their equality has no finite nonzero solution. This makes the source-calibrated continuation a concrete remaining analytic problem, not a reason to substitute a uniform norm or an arbitrary branch.

Audit checks (3) at independent generic parameters, both literal root sums for EVERY r, their exact phases, the all-input source Ward endpoints, the uniform high-index inequalities, and the explicit amplitude limits.

6. Exact parity alignment and the high-packet Gaussian

This section concerns the ACTUAL high packet r>=ceil(N/4) of (1); the lower indices are retained separately, not declared zero. Write z_N^hi for that partial polynomial. Its Newton coefficient before the outside N^2 is

b_(N,r)=kappa^(-1)(-1)^r q^(2r^2+r) A_r/F_r^3.

For odd N, factorial phases and (7) give exactly

b_(N,r)=kappa^(-1)q^(M^2)
                [s_(2r)/s_r^6] B_(N,n) Srev_r.

The leading Srev_r in (9) is sqrt(cos beta)exp(-i beta/2), where beta=n pi/N=pi/2-pi/(2N)-u and u=pi r/N. Since kappa=q(d2/2), M=d/2, its total leading phase is EXACTLY

kappa^(-1)q^(M^2)exp(-i beta/2)
                     =exp(-i pi d/4)exp(iu/2).

At even N the prefactor in (10) has exact phase q^(n(n+1/2)); the remaining factorial phases give kappa(-1)q(M2+M/2)q(r/2). The first two factors are EXACTLY exp(-i pi d/4), since d=2M+1. Together with (12) this proves, uniformly on every compact 1/4<r/N<1/2 at BOTH parities,

b_(N,r)=exp(-i pi d/4)[s_(2r)/s_r^6]
               sqrt(sin u)exp(iu/2)[1+O(1/N)].   (15)

No parity-dependent sign or unknown root phase remains in (15).

Fix a real T>2, eta=arcosh(T/2), R=exp eta, and define

J_T(u)=[-Lambda(2u)+6Lambda(u)]/pi
         +(1/(2pi)) integral_0^(2u)log(T+2cos v)dv,
u_*=atan(sqrt(T+2)/2).

Its derivative is pi(-1)log[(T+2cos(2u))cos(u)2/(4sin(u)^4)]. The difference of the numerator and denominator in this logarithm has the sign of T+2-(T+6)sin(u)^2. Hence u_* is the unique strict maximum on 0<=u<=pi/2, and u_*>pi/4. Direct differentiation gives

J_T''(u_*)=-(T+6)^2/[pi(T+2)^(3/2)].

The original sine-factorial and smooth midpoint products give uniformly near this maximum

s_(2r)/s_r^6=(2N)^(-5/2)
   sqrt(sin(2u))/sin(u)^3
      *exp{N[-Lambda(2u)+6Lambda(u)]/pi}[1+O(1/N)],

product_(a<r)(c_a+T)
   =exp{N/(2pi) integral_0^(2u)log(T+2cos v)dv}
                                                *[1+O(1/N)].

All other high indices have a strict profile gap, using (8),(11) and the uniform logarithmic endpoint factorial estimate. The real discrete Laplace sum has step du=pi/N. Inserting (15), its Gaussian prefactor is

2^(-5/2)pi^(-1)sqrt(2pi/|J_T''(u_*)|)
                     *sqrt(sin(2u_*))/sin(u_*)^(5/2)
   =1/[2((T+2)(T+6))^(1/4)].

Thus this ENTIRE coherent high packet has the asymptotic

z_N^hi(-T)
  =exp(-i pi d/4) exp(iu_*/2)
    /[2((T+2)(T+6))^(1/4)]
      *exp[N J_T(u_*)][1+O(1/N)].                 (16)

It also has a uniform central-window extension without deforming its index contour. Let j(eta)=J_(2cosh eta)(u_(eta)), and let tau be bounded real. Taylor-expand the analytic midpoint logarithm at eta+i tau/sqrt N and the REAL index u=u_+x/sqrt N. Its quadratic part is

Nj+i sqrt N j' tau
  +(Psi_uu/2)x^2+i Psi_(u eta)x tau
                          -(Psi_(eta eta)/2)tau^2.

The real index Gaussian dominates all x tails uniformly for bounded tau, and the remaining high indices retain their fixed profile gap. Dominated Riemann sums therefore evaluate the mixed term by the exact Fourier transform of this real Gaussian. The Schur complement is j’’=Psi_(eta eta)-Psi_(u eta)^2/Psi_uu, giving

z_N^hi(-exp(eta+i tau/sqrt N)-exp(-eta-i tau/sqrt N))
   =exp(-i pi d/4) a(eta)exp[Nj(eta)]
      *exp[i sqrt N j'(eta)tau-j''(eta)tau^2/2][1+o(1)],
a(eta)=exp(iu_*/2)/[2((T+2)(T+6))^(1/4)].        (17)

The error is uniform for bounded tau. The displayed limiting Gaussian and a(eta) are nonzero, so the error is relative as written. This is an actual finite-sum calculation with every coherent inner factor; it does not assert an unknown r-cycle incidence. To replace z_N^hi by the FULL z_N requires the independent all-index upper envelope and its strict lower-range profile gap. The whole-period assembly states that additional gate explicitly before applying (17) to the original marked residue.

Fresh audit replay: PASS eight independent generic finite transforms, 39 complete root families N=2,…,40 (EVERY r, both exact transformed phases and the source Ward), six complex-amplitude profiles through N=256, and four literal high-packet central-window families at N=63,64,127,128. The latter compare tau=-1,0,1 with (17), retaining the full kappa and both parity phases. These tests supplement the finite and analytic proofs; convergence samples are not their premise.
