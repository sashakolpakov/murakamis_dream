The even Fejér source: complete crossing paths and a forest remainder

Status update, 31 October 2026: the full endpoint theorem now controls the entire positive source, and the unique-maximizer theorem gives a strict exponential gap for the one-sided remainder under the same labeled certificate. The exact decomposition and the warnings about inferring bounds from forest topology alone remain valid.

, 31 October 2026. This is an exact coefficient reduction at every even level N>=4 for the original connected fixed flat plat. No estimate of the remaining forest contribution is asserted. The shared original marked targets are unchanged. The geometric rate corollary assumes the same labeled right-angled certificate as the complete-source theorem.

Let N=2m>=4,d=N-1, c_j=2cos((2j+1)pi/N), s_j=2sin(jpi/N). In the actual face monomial basis,

Omega z^j=c_j z^j-s_j^2(x+y)z^(j-1)-s_js_(j-1)xy z^(j-2).

Use the original homogeneous compiler. Let chi_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2 and V_N(z)=chi_N(z)(z-Omega)^(-1), its polynomial resolvent numerator. Let P=-H_(V_N(-2)) be the positive Fejer homogeneous polynomial. The exact fixed-root resolvent convention is that of the spectral derivative theorem. Every path goes from its input j to its output i using steps1 or2; its middle exponents are b^i c^(d-j). A k-step path contributes its positive edge product times product_(unvisited r)(2+c_r), with the usual binomial expansion of its one-step factors (x+y).

Split P=P_cross+P_left+P_right by

cross: i<=m-1 and j>=m;
left: j<=m-1;
right: i>=m.

These three classes partition the actual path set. Equivalently the cross part consists of monomials whose b and c exponents are both<=m-1. The two other classes have respectively c exponent>=m or b exponent>=m. Thus the decomposition is also by disjoint monomial supports, without any cancellation or selection of a new physical state. The three polynomials are parts of the original homogeneous coupon; they are not asserted separately to be covariant Casimir coupons or physical-head projections. The identity between the original source and the coefficient of their SUM is the frozen compiler identity.

1. Exact coefficientwise comparison for the entire cross part

Put c_=-2cos(pi/N), delta=2+c_=4sin^2(pi/(2N)). The two minimum diagonal entries are c_(m-1)=c_m=c_. At spectral value c_ the polynomial numerator has a nonzero path exactly when it visits BOTH indices m-1,m: otherwise its unused-factor product contains a zero. Call the resulting positive polynomial B=|H_(V_N(c_*))|_coef.

For a path visiting both central indices, its coefficient at -2 divided by its coefficient in B is a subproduct of

(2+c_r)/(c_r-c_*), r notin{m-1,m}.

Every ratio is>=1, and the product of ALL such ratios is exactly

C_N=[4/delta^2]/[chi''(c_*)/2]
   =4(4-delta)/(delta N^2)<2.                       (1)

Here chi(-2)=4 and chi’’(c_*)/2=N^2/[delta(4-delta)]. The bound delta N^2>=8 follows from the chord bound for sin on[0,pi/4]. Consequently B<=P_both<=C_N B coefficientwise.

Every cross path that misses one of the central indices has exactly one crossing two-step: either m->m-2 or m+1->m-1. Split that step into two consecutive one-steps. This produces a path visiting both central indices, with the SAME input/output and total face degree. Its other edges and unused factors are unchanged; only the missed central factor delta is removed. If the original two-step starts at j0, its edge is s_(j0)s_(j0-1)xy and the repaired pair is s_(j0)2s_(j0-1)2(x+y)^2. Since xy<= (x+y)^2/2 coefficientwise,

original path <= delta/[2s_(j0)s_(j0-1)] * repaired path
              =delta/[8cos(pi/N)] * repaired path.   (2)

A repaired path has at most TWO original preimages (merging either of those two pairs). Hence

B <= P_cross
  <= C_N[1+delta/(4cos(pi/N))] B <3B.               (3)

The last bound uses N>=4, delta<=2-sqrt2 and cos(pi/N)>=sqrt2/2. All statements are coefficientwise, so they remain true at zero protected faces and after every original shared coefficient extraction.

Let H_M be the actual maximal complete-channel contribution to H_(L_N), including N k_M beta_M^2 D_M(x,b,y)D_M(x,c,y)Ptilde_1(x,y), M=m-1. The exact paired derivative formula gives H_M=2B/N. Thus

             (N/2)H_M <= P_cross <= (3N/2)H_M.      (4)

Here beta and D are the literal factors of the complete-circle polynomial theorem. This includes the forced factor x+y and the actual multiplicity k_M=2. It is a comparison within the original source coefficient, not after choosing independent local radii. For a diagram with the stated geometric certificate, the all-cross term of the entire marked Fejer source has exactly the known complete-volume exponential rate, with only fixed-diagram polynomial factors between its two bounds. At odd N the full positive endpoint numerator is already N times the simple maximal complete coupon, so its whole coefficient has that rate directly. The forest remainder treated here is specific to even levels.

2. Exact shared degree restriction on the remainder

Expand the whole positive product by choosing cross,left,right at each original port. A noncross port puts at least m units into exactly one of its two middle faces: c for left, b for right. Draw its middle-graph edge pointing to that overloaded face. Each ordinary face has target d=2m-1, so it cannot receive TWO such arrows. Each protected face has target0, so it receives none.

The original flat-plat middle graph has the frozen gap description: each odd gap is a path of distinct bounded faces; each internal even gap is a cycle based at the SAME exterior face u, with its other faces unique to that gap. Therefore every selected-edge cycle contains u. An orientation with indegree<=1 at each vertex and indegree(u)=0 cannot exist on such a cycle: its equal numbers of edges and vertices would force indegree1 everywhere. This includes the length-two multicycles.

Thus every nonzero remainder term is a ROOTED FOREST of noncross middle edges. Each component has exactly one indegree-zero root; any protected face in that component must be that root. Geometry independently checked this use of the frozen gap lemma. No free re-marking, smoothing order, or graph-to-plat adapter is used.

This is the precise remaining positive source problem: bound the shared marked coefficient for these one-sided forest components, with the cross coupons on their complement. The cross comparison(4) does not estimate their weights. Local positive-radius estimates can be too large and are not substituted for this shared problem.

3. Exact half-size factorization, without an asymptotic conclusion

For a left path all visited rows are in0,…,m-1, so every upper-half diagonal factor is unused. Their product is exactly2, since the two halves have equal products and chi(-2)=4. Moreover c(d-j)=cm c^(m-1-j). Consequently P_left=2c^m times the degree-(m-1) positive polynomial resolvent numerator of the literal lower m-row triangular block, at spectral value -2. The analogous upper block gives P_right with the factor b^m. All original sine coefficients are still evaluated at level N, not at level m. Replacing them by a level-m source would be a new, unproved step.

The factorization explains the overloaded-face condition but does not yet identify an entropy or an actual smoothed representation for the forest. In particular the already proved L_1 smoothing gap cannot be applied merely because the selected middle graph is a forest.

Validation and remaining scope

The focused audit checks the complete positive path sum, the central coefficient comparison, the repair and half-block identities, and actual original gap charts. Results include nonzero marked-target support terms with noncross ports: the forest contribution does not vanish merely by this graph argument. The source interface of the path representation is separately audited against the original coproduct matrices; these new checks do not relabel that earlier test as a new independent physical-matrix construction.

The positive path partition is not a covariant complete-colored image or a half-colored tensor. The scalar identity does not justify an L1L^1 smoothing argument for the half-block forest.
