The edge Hessian and the response of a composed phase

Research note, 3 November 2026.

This note recalls the determinant-one edge Hessian and its exact positivity criterion from the edge-Hessian note. The equivalent tangent-coordinate criterion is also part of the positive-edge theorem and Section 2 of marked completion. Those earlier results already give a graph comparison on convex domains and global uniqueness throughout the positive geometric domain, respectively.

The additional conclusions here are an explicit quadrant strip and horizontal rectangles that can be used for contour bounds, a fractional-linear curvature response with a quantitative contraction estimate, and exact accounting for a peripheral shear. Their algebra does not require a large-power approximation.

Section 7 locates the actual selected geometry of every fixed positive path with powers at least eight in one common complex box, independently of its length. The exact kernel asymptotic and the remaining original contour estimates on this larger domain are not proved here. Arbitrary smaller or mixed short powers also remain outside this conclusion.

1. Branches and the exact edge matrix

5. What an invariant-domain proof would still require

The finite-current compiler gives an exact way to state those remaining estimates. Positive curvature of a geometric branch does not replace them.

6. Joint concavity for a finite graph of edges

Theorem 3 of the earlier edge-Hessian note already controls arbitrary connected sets on convex domains. The result below supplies particular horizontal rectangles on which its edge hypotheses hold automatically, together with a degree-weighted bound. It does not require successive elimination of the vertices.

Theorem 3 supplies a global comparison on the principal horizontal rectangle once the selected critical point lies there. 7. A common complex box for paths with powers at least eight
