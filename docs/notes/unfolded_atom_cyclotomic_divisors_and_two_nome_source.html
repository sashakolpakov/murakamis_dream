The actual unfolded atom requires its cyclotomic divisors and second nome

Research proof, 27 October 2026. The original unfolded Ward factorial is not horizontal in a rank-one meromorphic line at the physical parameter origin. Its cyclotomic singularities already accumulate there. A two-nome difference-period object cancels the local root pole and has an exact cyclic/source factorization, but it also does not become an ordinary meromorphic line at the origin. It has explicit accumulating zeros on another parameter ray.

The positive result is a whole exceptional-slope evaluation of the actual Ward atom, including its Kummer norm character, and an exact two-nome root formula retaining the original cyclic gauge and Heisenberg period. No published analytic-continuation or quantum dilogarithm proof is a premise.

Use H=Log Q throughout. The physical root is H_N=2pi i/N. The earlier ADO parameter h=Log q is H/2. A fixed-root normal epsilon=H-H_N is a different parameter from the varying H_N.

1. The function is fixed by the actual Ward jets

The literal finite source has f_N(z)=sum_(j=0)^(N-1) z^j/(Q_N;Q_N)_j. Its all-level rational-coefficient unfolding was proved uniquely to be

F(Q,z)=∑j≥0zj(Q;Q)j,F(Q,Qz)=(1−z)F(Q,z),F(Q,0)=1. F(Q,z)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{z^j}{(Q;Q)_j},\qquad F(Q,Qz)=(1-z)F(Q,z),\quad F(Q,0)=1.

For |Q|<1, |z|<1, coefficient comparison or iteration gives

F(Q,z)=1(z;Q)∞,log⁡F(Q,z)=∑m≥1zmm(1−Qm). F(Q,z)=\frac1{(z;Q)_\infty},\qquad \log F(Q,z)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{z^m}{m(1-Q^m)}. \tag{1}

These are the actual unfolded source functions of the Ward construction, not an unrelated function chosen for its growth. The finite nilpotent head itself only uses the first N coefficients; scalar root values with nonnilpotent z require the retained central/source data below.

2. Cyclotomic singularities exclude the strict meromorphic-line target

Fix a primitive n-th root zeta and Q=zeta exp(epsilon). On a closed sector around the negative real epsilon ray, uniformly for |z|<=r<1 with n FIXED,

log⁡F(ζeϵ,z)=−Li⁡2(zn)n2ϵ+log⁡Dn(z)−12Log⁡(1−zn)+O(ϵ),Dn(z)=∏j=1n−1(1−ζjz)j/n. \log F(\zeta e^\epsilon,z) =-\frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(z^n)}{n^2\epsilon} +\log D_n(z)-\frac12\operatorname{Log}(1-z^n)+O(\epsilon), \quad D_n(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1-\zeta^jz)^{j/n}. \tag{2}

All marks start at z=0. Here is an analytic justification of the coefficient calculation already recorded in the Ward note. Separate m divisible by n in (1). Nonresonant denominators are uniformly bounded away from zero on the stated sector: for m|epsilon| small, use the distance of the finitely many nontrivial n-th roots from one; otherwise their radial modulus gives a uniform bound. Their termwise difference from epsilon=0 is O(m|epsilon|), so the z-series remainder is O(epsilon). For the resonant terms use

11−eu=−1u+12+O(u) \frac1{1-e^u}=-\frac1u+\frac12+O(u)

uniformly on the same negative sector. For large u the error bound O(|u|) still holds; summing against r^(kn) justifies the uniform remainder. The weighted finite-root sum gives the displayed D_n constant. Thus (2) is a genuine sectorial estimate, not merely a formal Laurent calculation.

For fixed 0<z<1, every H_(m,n)=2pi i m/n, gcd(m,n)=1, has the nonzero polar logarithm in (2). Along epsilon=-t, log|F|=Li_2(zn)/(n2t)+O(1). These singularities accumulate at H=0. A nonzero horizontal frame of a meromorphic rank-one H-line would be holomorphic and nonzero at every sufficiently small H_(m,n)!=0, a contradiction. Finite ramification does not help. A fixed algebraic/Puiseux gauge has only finite-order behavior there and cannot cancel this exponential singularity. Removing only exp(-Li_2(z)/H) also leaves every nonzero cyclotomic point unchanged as a singularity.

This disproves automatic applicability of the strict one-variable meromorphic-line criterion to the original UNFOLDED ATOM. It does not prove that singularities cannot cancel in a completed whole word or selected period; that cancellation would need its actual source theorem.

3. The actual norm is an exceptional normal-slope evaluation

Retain U independently and form the WHOLE ratio

R(Q,z,U)=F(Q,zU)/F(Q,z). R(Q,z,U)=F(Q,zU)/F(Q,z). \tag{3}

The bare generic restriction U=1 is one. Instead put Q=zeta_n exp(epsilon), U=exp(t n epsilon). For fixed n, t in a compact subset of C, and |z|<=r<1, the same sectorial argument gives

log⁡R(Q,z,U)=tLog⁡(1−zn)+O(ϵ),R→(1−zn)t. \log R(Q,z,U)=t\operatorname{Log}(1-z^n)+O(\epsilon), \qquad R\longrightarrow(1-z^n)^t. \tag{4}

Proof. Nonresonant terms in the difference of (1) tend to zero uniformly. For m=kn the term is z(kn)(exp(tkn2 epsilon)-1)/[kn(1-exp(kn epsilon))], whose limit is -t z^(kn)/k. Uniform Taylor bounds near zero and exponential bounds away from zero, absorbed into a slightly larger r<1, justify the summed O(epsilon). This proves (4).

At the ACTUAL slope t=1, U=Q^n and Ward iteration gives EXACTLY

R(Q,z,Qn)=(z;Q)n,R(Qn,z,1)slope=1=1−zn. R(Q,z,Q^n)=(z;Q)_n,\qquad R(Q_n,z,1)_{slope=1}=1-z^n. \tag{5}

Thus the original finite cyclic norm is an exceptional normal-slope value, not the result of first imposing U=1 generically. It retains factors such as 1+M^n when they arise from the actual z substitution.

For rational t=a/b, b>0, retain the telescope

∏j=0b−1R(Q,zUj,U)=F(Q,zUb)/F(Q,z). \prod_{j=0}^{b-1}R(Q,zU^j,U)=F(Q,zU^b)/F(Q,z). \tag{6}

Here Ub=Q(an), so for a>=0 the right side is the finite product (z;Q)_(an), and negative a gives its specified reciprocal product. Its root value is (1-zn)a. The marked individual exceptional period nu therefore satisfies nub=(1-zn)^a, with nu=1 at z=0. Equation (6) does NOT assert R^b equals that finite product away from the exceptional restriction: its arguments are different.

This is a fixed-n sectorial exceptional period and Kummer norm statement. It is not a holomorphic regular extension across an entire complex blowup neighborhood, where other cyclotomic divisors still accumulate. No fixed-n limit is interchanged with a growing-n limit.

4. A two-nome flat difference object made from exact products

Let z=e^x and define, for Re H<0,

Q=eH,Q̃=e4π2/H,y=e2πix/H,w=Q̃y, Q=e^H,\quad \widetilde Q=e^{4\pi^2/H},\quad y=e^{2\pi i x/H},\quad w=\widetilde Q y, Ψ(H,x)=(w;Q̃)∞(z;Q)∞=F(Q,z)F(Q̃,w). \Psi(H,x)=\frac{(w;\widetilde Q)_\infty}{(z;Q)_\infty} =\frac{F(Q,z)}{F(\widetilde Q,w)}. \tag{7}

Both nomes have modulus<1. The numerator product is entire in w; the log-series quotient is used on the smaller common chart |w|<1. Elementary product cancellation proves the exact two transports

Ψ(H,x+H)=(1−z)Ψ(H,x),Ψ(H,x+2πi)=(1−y)Ψ(H,x). \Psi(H,x+H)=(1-z)\Psi(H,x),\qquad \Psi(H,x+2\pi i)=(1-y)\Psi(H,x). \tag{8}

The two shifts commute, since each multiplier is fixed by the other shift. In whole independent coordinates (Q,Qtilde,z,y), the automorphisms are z->Qz and y->Qtilde^(-1)y, respectively. Equations (8) are a rank-one flat algebraic DIFFERENCE presentation over that fixed finite-type base. The product (7) supplies one marked horizontal period. It is not thereby an algebraic section or an ordinary meromorphic differential line in H.

The second factor is invariant under x->x+H. It is a genuine difference-constant for the original Ward equation. On the left upper H sector, x->-infinity along the real axis makes both z,w tend to zero, specifying the product mark Psi->1. That extra period/source datum must not be inferred from the first equation alone.

5. Exact physical-root value and the full source factor

Now fix N and H=H_N+epsilon, H_N=2pi i/N. Write X=z^N and mark V=Log(1-X), nu=(1-X)^(1/N). The exact normal identities are

H̃=4π2/H+2πiN,1H̃=1N2ϵ+12πiN,w=XeγH̃,γ=1−x/(2πi). \widetilde H=4\pi^2/H+2\pi iN, \quad\frac1{\widetilde H} =\frac1{N^2\epsilon}+\frac1{2\pi iN}, \quad w=Xe^{\gamma\widetilde H},\quad \gamma=1-x/(2\pi i). \tag{9}

For x in a compact marked subset of Re x<0, use (2) on the numerator atom and its n=1 case on the dual atom. The entire negative Laurent part of the two logarithms is the same simple pole -Li_2(X)/(N^2 epsilon), and cancels. Their exact constant difference gives the sectorial root value

ΨN(x)=DN(z)exp⁡(Li⁡2(X)2πiN)(1−X)x/(2πi)−1. \boxed{\ \Psi_N(x)=D_N(z) \exp\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(X)}{2\pi iN}\right) (1-X)^{x/(2\pi i)-1}.\ } \tag{10}

For clarity the dual constant is -Li_2(X)/(2pi iN)+(gamma-1/2)V; subtracting it from log D_N- V/2 proves (10), including its sign. The estimate is O(epsilon) on the stated sector. This establishes the finite marked root value, not an unproved holomorphic H-extension around the whole root.

Use the ACTUAL determinant-normalized cyclic source gauge

gD,N=DN/ν(N−1)/2,S(X)=Li⁡2(X)+(Nx)V. g_{D,N}=D_N/\nu^{(N-1)/2},\quad S(X)=\operatorname{Li}_2(X)+(Nx)V.

Then the COMPLETE factorization is

ΨN=gD,Nexp⁡(S(X)/(2πiN))ν−(N+1)/2. \boxed{\ \Psi_N=g_{D,N}\, \exp(S(X)/(2\pi iN))\,\nu^{-(N+1)/2}.\ } \tag{11}

Every determinant root, its half-power mark, and the logarithmic period is retained. In the actual cyclic Ward substitution z=-t e one has X=-kappa_N t^N, so 1-X=1+kappa_N t^N is exactly the original norm, not an arbitrarily dropped central factor.

The source/transport check is decisive. Under x->x+H_N, g_D multiplies by (1-z)/nu, while S changes by 2pi i V. Their factors in (11) combine to 1-z, exactly (8). Under x->x+2pi i, g_D returns to itself and S changes by 2pi iN V, giving 1-X. Thus the n-step quotient is precisely the finite product (5), with its central norm and branch marks still present.

The raw finite polynomial also retains its affine source:

fN(QNz)−(1−z)fN(z)=zN/N. f_N(Q_Nz)-(1-z)f_N(z)=z^N/N. \tag{12}

On the nilpotent quotient z^N=0, (11) has central factor congruent to one and g_D reduces to f_N. This is a canonical projection in the logarithmic z-jet ring: the Log z terms first occur with z^N. It is not a literal evaluation of Log of a nilpotent matrix. For moving tensor sources or nonzero central norm, (11)–(12) must be retained. Nilpotent-jet agreement does not prove that the two-nome completion preserves the actual whole normal source.

The formula has the established rational quantum-dilogarithm coverage described in the existing finite/continuous torsor note. Its proof here is the exact original Ward product and the two normal identities (9), without adopting that literature’s proof.

6. The completion still is not an ordinary meromorphic H-line

Fix real x<0. The function (7) has exact SIMPLE zeros

Hk=(x−2πi)/k→0. H_k=(x-2\pi i)/k\longrightarrow0. \tag{13}

Indeed Re H_k<0, both nomes have modulus<1, and w(H_k)=exp(2pi i k)=1. Only the numerator’s first factor 1-w vanishes; the remaining product and the denominator are nonzero, since |Q|,|Qtilde|,|z|<1. Moreover w’(H_k)!=0. This proves simplicity directly. A nonzero horizontal frame of a rank-one meromorphic line at zero cannot have these accumulating interior zeros. A fixed algebraic Puiseux gauge cannot remove them. Thus the completion does NOT repair the strict one-parameter criterion globally either.

This does not prevent a finite polar ASYMPTOTIC along the physical positive-imaginary sequence. On a fixed marked compact annulus 0<rho<=|z|<=r<1, the actual finite sum for log D_N and elementary trapezoid bounds give

log⁡DN(z)=−Li⁡2(z)/HN−12Log⁡(1−z)+O(1/N). \log D_N(z)=-\operatorname{Li}_2(z)/H_N -\tfrac12\operatorname{Log}(1-z)+O(1/N).

The central factor in (10) is exponentially small in its logarithm on this chart, because X=z^N. Therefore

log⁡ΨN(x)=−Li⁡2(z)/HN−12Log⁡(1−z)+O(1/N). \log\Psi_N(x)=-\operatorname{Li}_2(z)/H_N -\tfrac12\operatorname{Log}(1-z)+O(1/N). \tag{14}

For a direct proof of the leading integral, expand integral_0^1 t Log(1-z exp(2pi it))dt=-Li_2(z)/(2pi i); the endpoint correction is the displayed -Log(1-z)/2. Uniform derivative bounds on |z|<=r give the error. This is a statement about the specified root sequence and chart, not an ordinary meromorphic H-germ or a global geometric-knot norm.

7. The usable target for the whole-period program

The strict selected meromorphic-line polar-character theorem remains a valid sufficient condition. It cannot be inferred from the actual local q-factorial atom: both its original and completed forms give explicit obstructions. The natural exact local target instead keeps the cyclotomic divisors, the independent power/normal direction of (3)–(6), the two-nome difference presentation (8), and the central Heisenberg/norm-root period (11).

The completion is not permission to replace the already selected Kashaev source by a convenient periodic factor. It has the same finite nilpotent jets but additional difference-constant period data. A knot comparison must carry those data, every peripheral factor, the actual internal projectors and its selected contraction. No topological invariant, global source descent, or volume conjecture is proved by this local construction.

Exact source identities and high-precision root/zero audit.

The audit passes at 80 decimal digits: 21 fixed-root expansions, 24 exceptional-slope limits, nine exact rational-slope telescopes, four generic two-nome shift pairs, 14 physical-root constants with both marked source transports, 12 exact finite-field affine-source polynomials, six accumulating simple zeros, and 12 independently bounded physical-root sequence cases. The two normal-coordinate identities in (9) also pass symbolic verification. The saved results record every case. These finite checks support the proofs above; they do not assert uniformity in growing n for the fixed-n normal limits.
