Exact radial compactification and sublevel transport of strict-Hall source cores

28 October 2026. For the original fixed-clock unit-factor source, every strict-Hall core admits an explicit radial retraction which never increases the modulus of its masterfunction. The retraction is piecewise monomial, has uniformly bounded logarithmic differential, and puts every sublevel cycle in a logarithmic box of radius O(log N). In addition, the complete logarithmic metric gives a global horizontal-lift proof that ordinary critical values are the ONLY nonzero finite bifurcation values of this product masterfunction.

Consequently the original coefficient torus can actually be homotoped into every sublevel above all its regular critical moduli. This is not yet the polynomial-mass theorem: the horizontal flow in the bounded box need not have a proved uniform complexity or Jacobian bound. The clocks are fixed throughout. Their full coupled critical equations and the original volume bound do not follow from this result.

1. The actual source and its strict cores

Let k>=2 and

f(X)=gamma product_(e=1)^M(alpha_e X_(u_e)-beta_e X_(v_e))
                     / product_i X_i^(q_i),       (1)

where u_e!=v_e, |alpha_e|=|beta_e|=1, gamma!=0, q_i are integers, and sum_i q_i=M. All original scalar, pivot and feedback factors which have already been extracted remain in gamma or in the separate clock weight. Loops are evaluated as their exact scalar monomials before (1).

For a nonempty proper anchor set I, let m_I count the factors internal to I, C_I its crossing factors, and e_I=q(I)-m_I. Assume

1<=e_I<=C_I-1 for EVERY such I.                 (2)

The source cut theorem gives exactly these strict inequalities in each nontrivial residual SCC after tight cuts and forced factors are removed. They imply C_I>=2 and M>=2. They concern endpoint support, not the nonvanishing of the selected complex coefficient.

The function (1) is invariant under common complex scaling. We work on the quotient torus T=(C)^k/C, using X_k=1 when a concrete coordinate system is needed. Its original source is the positive generator

I=(2*pi*i)^(-(k-1)) integral_(|X_1|=...=|X_(k-1)|=1)
                 f(X) dlog X_1 ... dlog X_(k-1).  (3)

The divisor f=0 is allowed in this coefficient cycle: the form in (3) is holomorphic there. It is removed only when discussing horizontal lifts over the nonzero base.

2. An exact OFF-critical cut derivative

Put rho_i=log|X_i|, r_i=|X_i|, and delta_i=S_i-2q_i, where S_i counts the incident linear factors. At any point with f!=0,

partial_(rho_i) log|f|
  =delta_i/2 + (1/2) sum_(e incident i)
             (r_i^2-r_j^2)/|alpha_e X_i-beta_e X_j|^2.  (4)

The sign in the numerator always uses i as the first endpoint, irrespective of the displayed orientation of the factor. This follows from

Re[a/(a-b)]=(1+(|a|^2-|b|^2)/|a-b|^2)/2.

Suppose every radius in I is at least exp(g) times every radius outside I, where g>0. Internal edge terms cancel after summing (4). Also

(r_i^2-r_j^2)/|alpha_e X_i-beta_e X_j|^2
                  >=(r_i-r_j)/(r_i+r_j)>=tanh(g/2).

Since delta(I)=C_I-2e_I, the exact high-cut direction V_I=sum_(i in I) X_i partial_(X_i) satisfies

Re[(V_I f)/f]
  >=(C_I-2e_I+C_I tanh(g/2))/2.               (5)

In particular, for L=log M, (2) gives the uniform quantitative bound

g>=L  ==>  Re[(V_I f)/f]>=1/(M+1).           (6)

Indeed tanh(L/2)=(M-1)/(M+1), so the right side of (5) is C_I M/(M+1)-e_I >=1-C_I/(M+1)>=1/(M+1). This is an identity and inequality away from the zero divisor, not an evaluation of the critical equations alone.

3. Explicit simultaneous gap clipping

Order the logarithmic radii rho_(s1)<=…<=rho_(sk), and let g_j=rho_(s(j+1))-rho_(sj). Replace each g_j by min(g_j,L), preserving all arguments. Reconstruct the radii from these new adjacent gaps and then divide all coordinates by their kth coordinate to restore X_k=1. Call the resulting map C:T->T. The reconstruction is independent of the choices among equal radii, so C is continuous across order walls.

A semialgebraic homotopy is obtained by interpolating the RADIUS RATIOS, not by using arbitrary real powers. For R_j=exp(g_j), set

R_j(s)=(1-s)R_j+s min(R_j,M), 0<=s<=1.        (7)

Reconstruct as above to obtain C_s, with C_0=identity and C_1=C. Every changing gap stays at least L. Its derivative is nonpositive, and changing gap j is exactly translation in the high-cut direction I_j={s(j+1),…,sk}, modulo common scaling. Therefore (6) implies

d/ds log|f(C_s X)|<=0,
|f(C_s X)|<=|f(X)|.                          (8)

At a zero of f, a zero factor has equal endpoint radii and equal phased arguments. Its endpoints remain at equal radii throughout (7), so that factor stays zero. Thus (8) extends across f=0 by continuity. Conversely no new zero factor is created: an originally positive radius gap remains positive because L>0, and arguments never change. The same homotopy therefore preserves the arrangement complement as well.

Every output gap is at most L. Hence

max_i log|C(X)_i|-min_i log|C(X)_i|
                           <=(k-1)log M.     (9)

The map fixes this clipped region pointwise. It is thus a genuine strong deformation retraction of EVERY sublevel {|f|<=R} onto its intersection with the compact projective box (9). In the X_k=1 gauge, all log radii then have absolute value at most (k-1)log M.

The endpoint map C is piecewise monomial in the positive radii. The pieces are indexed only by the k! orders and 2^(k-1) clipped-gap choices. On each piece, its logarithmic radial differential has integer entries bounded by 2; the argument map is the identity. Thus, in the standard Euclidean metric on (rho_1,…,rho_(k-1),theta_1,…,theta_(k-1)), its Lipschitz constant is at most 4k. The same bound holds throughout (7): the derivative of log R_j(s) with respect to log R_j lies in [0,1].

In particular, clipping increases Euclidean logarithmic (k-1)-mass by at most (4k)^(k-1). This statement uses the full real logarithmic metric; it does NOT claim such a comparison with only the absolute holomorphic wedge |dlog X_1 wedge … wedge dlog X_(k-1)|, which can vanish on a real tangent plane before a nonholomorphic map is applied.

Using auxiliary positive r_i with r_i^2=X_i conjugate(X_i), the maps C and C_s have semialgebraic graphs of degree bounded solely in k on each piece (the coefficient M is retained). Images of chains whose algebraic support degrees and multiplicities are polynomial in N therefore still have polynomial support complexity after clipping. No such complexity claim for the INTERIOR horizontal flow is inferred here.

4. Complete horizontal lifts: no hidden finite escape

Give T its complete flat logarithmic metric. Let J be a compact subset of C* disjoint from all regular critical values of f. Write

l_i=(X_i partial_(X_i) f)/f.

Their sum is zero by homogeneity, so they define the logarithmic cotangent vector on the quotient. Outside the box (9), there is an adjacent gap greater than L. Equation (6) implies

||dlog f|| >=c_k/(M+1).                       (10)

Here one may take c_k=1/(2sqrt(k)), a conservative quotient-coordinate constant. Inside the box, the set f^(-1)(J) is compact. A factor cannot approach zero there while f stays in J: the denominator is bounded away from zero, all other factors are bounded, and J stays away from zero. There is also no ordinary critical point there by the choice of J. Thus ||dlog f|| has a positive minimum on this compact set.

For a piecewise C^1 base path w(t) in J, the standard logarithmic Hermitian dual gives the explicit horizontal vector field

dZ/dt=(w'(t)/w(t)) conjugate(l(Z))/||l(Z)||^2,  (11)

in any orthonormal logarithmic quotient coordinates. Its defining identity is dlog f/dt=w’/w. Its norm is bounded on f^(-1)(J), by (10) outside and the compact positive minimum inside. Local uniqueness and completeness of the logarithmic metric therefore extend its flow for the whole finite path time. Indeed a bounded-speed finite-time path is Cauchy at a hypothetical endpoint; its limit remains in T, and f stays in J, so the ordinary local ODE existence theorem extends it.

The same argument applies to every initial point, with smooth dependence on it and on the base path. Lifting a short base contraction and its reverse gives a local smooth trivialization over J. No finite nonzero bifurcation values occur outside the ordinary critical-value set. The outside bound (10) is uniform in N; the compact INSIDE minimum is only proved positive at each fixed N and has no polynomial lower bound here.

5. An actual homologous sublevel cycle for the original source

There are finitely many regular critical VALUES. The critical locus is algebraic with finitely many irreducible components, and f is constant on each connected component of its regular critical locus. Put

Mcrit=max_regular_critical |f|,

using zero if that set is empty. Fix R>Mcrit. Over |w|>=R the horizontal lift (11) is complete on every finite closed radial base interval. Choose a smooth radial base homotopy which fixes |w|<=R and moves each point with |w|>R toward modulus R (or toward R+epsilon, followed by a limit). Lifting it wherever |f|>R and using identity elsewhere yields a deformation of T into {|f|<=R+epsilon}; the cutoff can be chosen so that it is the identity at the joining level. Only the compact range of |f| on the original coefficient torus is needed for its transported cycle, so there is no assertion of a uniform infinite-time lift.

This produces a cycle Gamma_(R,epsilon) homologous in T to the original torus (3), with |f|<=R+epsilon. Compose it with the explicit clipping map C to put it in (9), preserving that sublevel and its homology class. The holomorphic coefficient form is closed on all of T, so its integral over the resulting cycle is exactly I, including its original scalar.

This is a global existence theorem for the required sublevel homotopy, not a selection theorem for any individual critical point. It improves the earlier sufficient macrodeficit condition e_Z>B: strict Hall support alone gives the needed complete logarithmic lift for these PRODUCT masterfunctions. It does not assert that every critical stratum of an arbitrary compactification is regular; a topological horizontal lift on T is the exact conclusion needed here.

6. The quantitative step which this proof does NOT supply

The endpoint clipping has fixed-dimensional semialgebraic complexity and bounded logarithmic mass distortion. The preceding interior horizontal flow need not be semialgebraic, and no uniform bound for its Jacobian or for the multiplicities of a replacement algebraic chain has been proved. Thus it is not yet valid to combine cycle existence with the earlier polynomial logarithmic-mass lemma and announce

|I|<=poly(N) Mcrit.

Effective triangulation of the sublevel SET alone would not prove that bound either: one must control a representative of the particular original integral homology class, including its coefficients. The present proof isolates that remaining mass/complexity issue inside an explicit compact logarithmic box, rather than leaving radius escape or asymptotic bifurcation as additional hypotheses.

For the knot problem, every claim above still fixes the integer clocks. Their exact feedback, factorial weights and original finite source must remain when constructing the full phase cycle. Neither full critical Hurwitz support nor a general-knot exponential upper bound follows by taking absolute values over these fixed-clock cores.

Audit: actual source residual cores, exact off-critical cut derivatives, strict-Hall bounds, modulus-monotone simultaneous gap clipping, and horizontal right-inverse identities. The audit supplements the uniform proof; it does not estimate the unresolved interior flow mass.
