The actual primitive Hilbert-90 gauge has a dilogarithm action and exponential norm

27 October 2026. The explicit cyclic source gauge in the actual Fourier twisted-trace theorem has a rigorously computable exponential scale, even in the flat right-regular metric. This is an ACTUAL gauge from the original source construction, not an arbitrary central rescaling of a state. It proves why solving its norm-one transport equation does not by itself give a subexponential comparison to a geometric period.

The result also identifies a concrete dilogarithm action of this source gauge on a regular chart. It does not identify that chart with the marked geometric A-component or its Bloch regulator.

1. The specified actual gauge

Let q=exp(pii/N), Q=q^2, h=2pii/N and z^N=0. The primitive k=1 source sector has the unique scalar-polynomial gauge with constant term one

g_N(z)=v_N(z)v_N(Qz)
      =[u_N(z)u_N(Qz)]^-1,                   (1)
u_N(z)=sum_(a=0)^(N-1) z^a/(Q;Q)_a,
v_N=u_N^-1 in the nilpotent algebra.

It solves g_N(Qz)/g_N(z)=1/[(1-z)(1-Qz)], eliminating the corresponding inner twist of the source automorphism. The constant-term and transport equation make it unique: a quotient of two gauges would be Q-invariant, hence constant in degree<N. The full matrix comparison can have other choices; no uniqueness claim for all matrix gauges is made.

The CANONICAL polynomial representative of (1), of degree<N, is exactly the Taylor polynomial through degree N-1 of exp(L_N(z)), where

L_N(z)=sum_(a=1)^(N-1)
                (1+Q^a) z^a/[a(Q^a-1)]
      =-i sum_(a=1)^(N-1) cot(pi*a/N) z^a/a.   (2)

Indeed the logarithm of v_N in the nilpotent algebra is sum za/[a(Qa-1)]. Adding its shifted logarithm gives (2). This establishes the relation to the ACTUAL finite gauge before using analytic estimates. It is not an unproved resummation of quantum jets.

2. Uniform asymptotics of the actual polynomial on a fixed disk

For |z|<=1/4 one has, uniformly as N grows,

g_N(z)=exp[(2/h) Li_2(z)+O(1/N)].            (3)

The same statement with the opposite sign holds for the canonical degree-<N representative of g_N^-1. In particular these finite polynomials have no zero on that disk for all sufficiently large N.

Here is a proof that retains the truncation error. First, on every fixed disk |z|<=rho<1,

L_N(z)=(2/h)Li_2(z)+O(1/N).                 (4)

For a<=N/2 use the uniform elementary estimate cot(t)=1/t+O(t) on 0<t<=pi/2. Dividing its error by a gives O(1/N) per coefficient, whose sum against rho^a remains O(1/N). For a>N/2, the inequality

|cot(pi*a/N)| <= N/[2 min(a,N-a)]

gives a tail O(rho^(N/2) log N), and the omitted tail of Li_2 is smaller. This proves (4) with both root endpoints accounted for.

Put G_N=exp(L_N) as an ordinary analytic function. On |z|=1/2, the coarser |cot(pi*a/N)|<=N/2 gives

|L_N(z)| <= (N/2)log 2,
|G_N(z)| <= exp[(N/2)log 2].

Cauchy’s coefficient bound and the degree-N Taylor tail therefore give, throughout |z|<=1/4,

|G_N(z)-g_N(z)| <= 2 exp[-(N/2)log 2].       (5)

Meanwhile (4) gives

|G_N(z)| >= exp[-N Li_2(1/4)/pi-O(1/N)].

For example Li_2(1/4)<=13/48, so Li_2(1/4)/pi is strictly smaller than log(2)/2. Thus (5) is exponentially small RELATIVE to G_N on the entire disk. Combining it with (4) proves (3) for the actual canonical polynomial. Replacing L_N by -L_N proves the inverse statement by the identical estimate.

The Taylor-tail estimate is essential. Nilpotent equality alone would not justify evaluating its analytic exponential away from zero, as the nonzero endpoint carry in the meridian half-step theorem explicitly demonstrates.

3. A positive exponential lower bound for this comparison gauge

Take w=i/4 and set

c=Im Li_2(i/4)/pi > 0.

The alternating power series proves

Im Li_2(i/4) >= 1/4-(1/4)^3/9=143/576.

Equation (3) gives the precise scalar-gauge rate

log|g_N(i/4)|=c N+O(1/N).                    (6)

To pass from this polynomial value to an ACTUAL finite operator, use the flat right-regular basis f^a e_j. The operator R_f is a truncated shift with unit coefficients. For |x|=1, its argument xR_f has norm one, and the powers applied to a degree-zero basis vector are orthogonal. If g_N(z)=sum c_a z^a, this gives

||g_N(xR_f)|| >= (sum |c_a|^2)^(1/2)
                >= sqrt(15/16)|g_N(i/4)|.    (7)

The second inequality is just Cauchy–Schwarz for evaluation at |w|=1/4. Multiplication by x^a changes no coefficient norm. Thus this specified comparison gauge has exponential operator growth, with liminf N^-1 log||g_N(xR_f)||>=c. Applying the inverse version of (3) at -i/4 proves the same lower bound for its inverse. Its condition number therefore has liminf logarithmic rate at least 2c.

All these operators are unipotent: their diagonal entries and determinants are one. The source transport is cyclic and obeys its exact norm-one equation. Those algebraic facts coexist with the exponential norm in (7). This result concerns the flat coefficient metric. A nonunitary physical fusion change requires its own metric comparison.

4. Implication for the intended torsor proof

The primitive source transport is explicitly SOLVED at each level, and its gauge action is the specified dilogarithm in (3). Its comparison is not tempered. A proof may carry this action into the marked regulator rather than try to discard the gauge as a normalization. Which combination of actions and source sectors is selected by the original knot word remains to be proved.

This establishes neither a counterexample to the volume conjecture nor an exponential estimate for the knot invariant. The number c above is a gauge-chart value, not a knot’s hyperbolic volume. It does not rule out a different whole comparison with controlled norm. It does rule out inferring such control for this actual primitive gauge solely from Hilbert 90, finite cyclicity, unipotence or determinant one.

The rigorous uniform normal-form estimate (3) is the new local period/action comparison here. The nonzero selected geometric image and global sector/cancellation estimates remain open.

Subsequent direct source calculations now distinguish that operator norm from selected local periods. The canonical resonant endpoint is an explicit Gauss period of size N^-1/2. The normalized top-coefficient period selects the algebraic point 2 on fixed polynomial observables and intertwines fixed ordered gauge meridian steps. Thus this specified source has an evaluated local leading period, not just an exponential operator estimate. Its full knot-word coupling and selected hyperbolic action remain unproved.

The high-precision diagnostic and saved output compare the literal finite v_N(z)v_N(Qz) polynomial with exp(L_N) and its dilogarithm action. The theorem rests on the uniform proof above; numerical rate samples are not used as a proof. Another agent independently checked its source-gauge orientation, the Cauchy remainder, both coefficient tails and the operator lower bound.
