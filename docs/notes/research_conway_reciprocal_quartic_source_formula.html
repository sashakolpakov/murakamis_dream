A positive reciprocal integral for the original Conway quartic

23 November 2026. Research proof. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

Supersedes the initial version.

The actual normalized source has a positive integral which approximates its reciprocal squared modulus with a uniform relative error. A known polynomial damping of the original closing row suffices. Neither a minimizer, its Hessian determinant, nor a chosen temperature is input. The proof uses the triangular residual map and a fixed residual ball. It does not evaluate the integral from hyperbolic geometry.

1. The original physical source and the integral

Use the complete physical compiler of [P]. At level N≥2N\ge2, every proper packet belongs to its full NN-dimensional complex physical algebra, with the uniform Hilbert–Schmidt metric. This retains the first derivatives at double Casimir roots. Fix a literal expression with s≥1s\ge1 proper vertices, and put D=NsD=Ns, m=D+1m=D+1. Choose physical orthonormal coordinates on their direct sum ℂD\mathbb C^D; dxdx means real Lebesgue measure in these coordinates.

Keep [P]’s known budgets and their positive product BB. Each normalized leaf has norm at most σ=1/16\sigma=1/16; turns and the original closing row have operator norm at most σ\sigma; products have bilinear norm at most σ\sigma. No small coefficient-tensor Hilbert–Schmidt norm for constant-budget physical multiplication is assumed. Write cc for the normalized original row, extended by zero off the top packet, and Gv(x)=Φv((xj)j child of v)−xv,G(a)=0,k=ca=KN/B,|k|≤σ2.(1) G_v(x)=\Phi_v((x_j)_{j\text{ child of }v})-x_v,\qquad G(a)=0,\qquad k=ca=K_N/B,\qquad |k|\le\sigma^2. \qquad\text{(1)} Here aa is the exact forward field. It is used in the proof, not supplied to define the integral. All original phases, turns, markings, and prescribed short-filling packets remain in the known coefficients.

For 0<ε≤10<\varepsilon\le1, damp only the closing row and record the compensating budget: Eε(x)=∥G(x)∥2+ε2|cx|2,Bε=B/ε,k′=εk.(2) E_\varepsilon(x)=\|G(x)\|^2+\varepsilon^2|cx|^2,\qquad B_\varepsilon=B/\varepsilon,\qquad k'=\varepsilon k. \qquad\text{(2)} The base EεE_\varepsilon has degree at most four. Define the extended nonnegative integral 𝒫ε=D!πD∫ℂDEε(x)−D−1dx.(3) \mathcal P_\varepsilon= \frac{D!}{\pi^D}\int_{\mathbb C^D} E_\varepsilon(x)^{-D-1}\,dx . \qquad\text{(3)} The exponent grows with DD: the rational integrand does not have bounded total degree. For the rational damping used below, its base coefficients are algebraic at each level for the original finite source. The normalization π−D\pi^{-D} is explicit; no assertion about an algebraic period class is needed.

Theorem. Take the known damping ε=m−3\varepsilon=m^{-3}, and put δ=m−2\delta=m^{-2}. If KN≠0K_N\ne0, the integral is finite and positive, and ||k′|2𝒫ε−1|≤δ,(1−δ)Bε2𝒫ε≤|KN|2≤(1+δ)Bε2𝒫ε.(4) \left||k'|^2\mathcal P_\varepsilon-1\right|\le\delta,\qquad (1-\delta)\frac{B_\varepsilon^2}{\mathcal P_\varepsilon} \le |K_N|^2\le (1+\delta)\frac{B_\varepsilon^2}{\mathcal P_\varepsilon}. \qquad\text{(4)} If KN=0K_N=0, then 𝒫ε=+∞\mathcal P_\varepsilon=+\infty; with 1/(+∞)=01/(+\infty)=0, the two source inequalities remain exact at zero. There is no parity or nonvanishing assumption in this convention.

2. Source-exact residual coordinates and a local bound

Order children before parents. The inverse of GG is the finite polynomial recursion xv=Φv((xj)j)−rv,detℂDG=(−1)D,|detℝDG|=1.(5) x_v=\Phi_v((x_j)_j)-r_v,\qquad \det_{\mathbb C}DG=(-1)^D,\qquad |\det_{\mathbb R}DG|=1. \qquad\text{(5)} Thus GG is a global polynomial automorphism. This is the residual change already used in [R], now in the physical coordinates of [P]. Let H=G−1H=G^{-1} and fε(r)=εcH(r)f_\varepsilon(r)=\varepsilon cH(r). Then (3) is exactly the same integral with denominator ∥r∥2+|fε(r)|2\|r\|^2+|f_\varepsilon(r)|^2.

Fix ρ=1/4\rho=1/4. For ∥r∥≤ρ\|r\|\le\rho, upward induction in (5) gives ∥H(r)v∥≤σ+ρ=5/16<1\|H(r)_v\|\le\sigma+\rho=5/16<1. Indeed a leaf is bounded by σ+ρ\sigma+\rho; a turn or product of previously bounded inputs has norm at most σ\sigma, before subtracting rvr_v. This also covers prescribed constant leaves.

On these packet balls write DG=−I+ADG=-I+A. Each child enters one parent row and each row has at most two children, so ∥A∥≤2σ≤1/8\|A\|\le\sqrt2\sigma\le1/8 and ∥DH∥≤8/7\|DH\|\le8/7. Only products contribute to D2GD^2G. At one product the two terms are Φ(hL,kR)+Φ(kL,hR)\Phi(h_L,k_R)+\Phi(k_L,h_R). Cauchy–Schwarz on that pair, followed by disjointness of sibling pairs, proves ∥D2G[h,k]∥≤σ∥h∥∥k∥,∥D2(cH)∥≤σ2(8/7)3<1.(6) \|D^2G[h,k]\|\le\sigma\|h\|\|k\|,\qquad \|D^2(cH)\|\le\sigma^2(8/7)^3<1. \qquad\text{(6)} The second inequality follows by twice differentiating G(H(r))=rG(H(r))=r; there are three factors of DHDH and one of D2GD^2G. No dimension factor or unknown source value occurs.

Taylor’s formula on the residual ball consequently gives fε(r)=k′+d′r+p(r),d′=Dfε(0),|p(r)|≤ε2∥r∥2.(7) f_\varepsilon(r)=k'+d'r+p(r),\qquad d'=Df_\varepsilon(0),\qquad |p(r)|\le\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|r\|^2 . \qquad\text{(7)} These are holomorphic derivatives, equivalently their real variations.

3. The affine reciprocal and both tails

Suppose first that k′≠0k'\ne0, and put Eaff(r)=∥r∥2+|k′+d′r|2E_{\mathrm{ aff}}(r)=\|r\|^2+|k'+d'r|^2. Completing the Hermitian square uses A0=I+d′*d′A_0=I+d'^*d', whose determinant is 1+∥d′∥21+\|d'\|^2. The remaining constant is e0=|k′|2/(1+∥d′∥2)e_0=|k'|^2/(1+\|d'\|^2). Radial integration after this linear change gives D!πD∫ℂDEaff−D−1dr=1e0det⁡A0=1|k′|2.(8) \frac{D!}{\pi^D}\int_{\mathbb C^D}E_{\mathrm{ aff}}^{-D-1}\,dr =\frac1{e_0\det A_0}=\frac1{|k'|^2}. \qquad\text{(8)} For example, the radial substitution reduces the scalar integral to ∫0∞tD−1(t+e0)−D−1dt=1/(De0)\int_0^\infty t^{D-1}(t+e_0)^{-D-1}dt=1/(De_0). The last equality follows by u=t/(t+e0)u=t/(t+e_0). Thus the affine slope cancels exactly, not approximately.

Both residual energies dominate ∥r∥2\|r\|^2. For either one, the normalized integral outside the fixed ball is bounded by D!πD∫∥r∥>ρ∥r∥−2D−2dr=Dρ2=16D.(9) \frac{D!}{\pi^D}\int_{\|r\|>\rho}\|r\|^{-2D-2}\,dr =\frac D{\rho^2}=16D. \qquad\text{(9)} This controls the entire nonlinear exterior without a large-field estimate for HH. If k′≠0k'\ne0, the energy has no zero: a zero would require r=0r=0 and fε(0)=k′=0f_\varepsilon(0)=k'=0. Continuity on the remaining compact ball proves finiteness of (3).

4. Uniform relative error without a source lower bound

On ∥r∥≤ρ\|r\|\le\rho, (7) gives |p(r)|≤(ε/8)Eaff(r),(1−ε/8)2Eaff≤∥r∥2+|fε(r)|2≤(1+ε/8)2Eaff.(10) |p(r)|\le(\varepsilon/8)\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{ aff}}(r)},\qquad (1-\varepsilon/8)^2E_{\mathrm{ aff}} \le\|r\|^2+|f_\varepsilon(r)|^2 \le(1+\varepsilon/8)^2E_{\mathrm{ aff}}. \qquad\text{(10)} This follows by applying the triangle and reverse triangle inequalities to the augmented vector (r,k′+d′r)(r,k'+d'r). Since −log⁡(1−t)≤t/(1−t)-\log(1-t)\le t/(1-t), the relative integrand error is at most exp⁡[(2/7)mε]−1\exp[(2/7)m\varepsilon]-1. Indeed t=ε/8≤1/8t=\varepsilon/8\le1/8 and the exponent of each square is −m-m. Comparing the two inside integrals, then using both tails (9), proves ||k′|2𝒫ε−1|≤exp⁡[(2/7)mε]−1+32D|k′|2.(11) \left||k'|^2\mathcal P_\varepsilon-1\right| \le \exp[(2/7)m\varepsilon]-1+32D|k'|^2. \qquad\text{(11)} No lower bound on |k′||k'| has been used.

For ε=m−3\varepsilon=m^{-3}, one has |k′|≤ε/256|k'|\le\varepsilon/256. Writing a=(2/7)m−2a=(2/7)m^{-2}, the elementary series bound ea−1≤a/(1−a)e^a-1\le a/(1-a) gives ea−1≤25m−2,32D|k′|2≤D2048m6≤12048m−2.(12) e^a-1\le\frac25m^{-2},\qquad 32D|k'|^2\le\frac{D}{2048m^6}\le\frac1{2048}m^{-2}. \qquad\text{(12)} Their sum is less than δ=m−2\delta=m^{-2}. If k′=0k'=0, holomorphy gives fε(r)=O(∥r∥)f_\varepsilon(r)=O(\|r\|) near zero. The energy is therefore at most C∥r∥2C\|r\|^2 there, and its integral dominates a positive constant times ∫0ρt−3dt\int_0^\rho t^{-3}dt. It diverges, proving the stated zero convention.

5. Temperatures, normalization, and the remaining geometric task

For t>0t>0, define Zε(t)=(t/π)D∫e−tEε(x)dxZ_\varepsilon(t)=(t/\pi)^D\int e^{-tE_\varepsilon(x)}dx. Residual coordinates show 0<Zε(t)≤10<Z_\varepsilon(t)\le1. Tonelli and the elementary scalar Gamma integral give the exact identity 𝒫ε=∫0∞Zε(t)dt.(13) \mathcal P_\varepsilon =\int_0^\infty Z_\varepsilon(t)\,dt . \qquad\text{(13)} This also holds as an extended nonnegative equality at a source zero. All temperatures are integrated; the intrinsic small-source scale has not disappeared from the value of the integral.

For a fixed expression D=NsD=Ns, (4) has relative error O(N−2)O(N^{-2}). The extra budget m3m^3 is polynomial and explicitly restored. This improves the previous constant-factor minimum comparison to a relative reciprocal-integral comparison, including arbitrarily small nonzero sources. Unlike [L], no selected temperature, minimum or Gaussian determinant is needed. The argument uses only the bilinear physical bounds; [R]’s coefficient-Hilbert–Schmidt determinant theorem is not transferred to a different metric.

The original signed closing is still inside EεE_\varepsilon. Neither positivity nor (13) evaluates the integral at a hyperbolic-volume rate. No geometric source coefficient, arbitrary-short-filling volume theorem, or finite-exception result follows.
