A sharp projector barrier for the original repeated-crossing blocks

28 October 2026. The actual physical factorial frame gives a sharper uniform bound when its spectral-projector interval products are estimated together. Write v_3 for the regular ideal tetrahedron volume and v_8 for the regular ideal octahedron volume. There are absolute constants C,A such that, for every N>=2 and every integer ell,

||B_N^ell||_op
   <= C N^A(1+|ell|) exp[N(2v_3+v_8)/pi].           (1)

B_N is the original complete crossing in the balanced physical metric, with its original unit Cartan and framing factors. The HS estimate costs one further factor N. No spectrum-only or semisimplicity assumption is used. The proof retains the actual nilpotent blocks by the meridian Cauchy argument in the factorial-frame theorem.

This improves that theorem’s volume constant 12v_3 to 4v_3+2v_8. It is a coarse bound for the whole original knot source at arbitrary braid width, not the desired sharp hyperbolic-volume estimate.

1. The entire projector product, with its physical domain

Keep the actual degree-T bidiagonal Casimir and interval-projector formula from Sections 3–5 of the factorial-frame theorem. Let integer indices 0<=r<=k<=s<N obey 0<=T-s<=T-r<N. Its projector entry is

(E_k)_(r,s)=product_(l=r+1)^s b_l(M)
             /product_(l=r,l!=k)^s(c_k(M^2)-c_l(M^2)).

Put J(u)=integral_0^u log(2sin(pi t))dt on [0,1], and extend J periodically. This is consistent because J(1)=0. In the following formula T,r,s,k denote the integer indices DIVIDED by N. The exact root-factorial identities give the leading projector exponent

p(T,r,s,k)=J(s)-J(r)+J(T-r)-J(T-s)
           -J(k-r)-J(s-k)-J(k+s)+J(k+r).           (2)

The last two terms use the periodic extension. The physical domain is

0<=r<=k<=s<=1,  0<=T-s<=T-r<=1.                  (3)

The numerator consists of two ordinary interval products. The first denominator is |F_(k-r)F_(s-k)|. The second is a circular root interval, with one factor omitted and at most one complete-meridian zero. Thus the endpoint-uniform factorial estimate gives (2) with error O(log N) after removing that possible zero. The +1 in k+l+1 shifts endpoints by 1/N; the logarithmic modulus of continuity of J bounds its effect by O(log N), uniformly even at the endpoints. This does not assume uniform separation from a root.

On the specified circle

|M-1|=delta=1/[8N^3(1+|ell|)],

all noncolliding products change by a uniformly bounded factor, using the harmonic sum of their relative changes. The one possible zero costs at most C delta^(-1). The prefactors which are not exactly unit off the complete fiber have modulus exp(O(N delta)). Consequently

|(E_k)_(r,s)|
   <=C_0 N^A0(1+|ell|) exp[N p(T/N,r/N,s/N,k/N)].  (4)

Here the notation on the right restores integer indices. The constants are independent of the degree block and of the chosen spectral node.

2. Exact maximization of the projector exponent

Let G(u)=log|2sin(pi u)|, periodically extended. For 0<=l<=1, every interval of length l has its G-integral between

B(l)=2J(l/2),
A(l)=J((1+l)/2)-J((1-l)/2)=-2J((1-l)/2).           (5)

Indeed G is symmetric and increases strictly with distance from its integer zero until that distance reaches 1/2. The minimum interval is centered at an integer and the maximum at a half-integer. Alternatively, differentiate J(t+l)-J(t): its regular stationary intervals have exactly these two centers. Moving through a singular endpoint gives the same ordering by continuity and monotonicity. The cases l=0,1 follow by limits.

Set l=s-r. Both numerator differences in (2) are integrals over an interval of length l, so each is at most A(l). The last difference, J(k+s)-J(k+r), is at least B(l). The remaining split denominator obeys

J(k-r)+J(s-k)>=2J(l/2).                           (6)

For 0<l<1 this follows directly from strict convexity:

d^2/dx^2 [J(x)+J(l-x)]
   =pi sin(pi l)/[sin(pi x)sin(pi(l-x))]>0.

Symmetry places the minimum at x=l/2; the endpoints follow by continuity. Combining (5)–(6) gives

p<=2A(l)-4J(l/2)
  =-4[J((1-l)/2)+J(l/2)]
  <=-8J(1/4)=v_8/pi.                              (7)

For the last inequality the two arguments have sum 1/2, and the same convexity calculation places their minimum at 1/4 each. Equality in ALL these inequalities holds at

T=1, r=1/4, s=3/4, k=1/2.

These values satisfy (3). Thus v_8/pi is the exact maximum of the specified projector profile, not an estimate obtained by separately maximizing its factorials. The identity -8J(1/4)=v_8/pi uses v_8=8 Lambda(pi/4) and J(u)=-Lambda(pi u)/pi.

3. Restore both physical frames, eigenvalues and the complete source

The explicit physical fusion and its inverse each have norm exp[2 gamma_* N+O(log N)], where gamma_*=v_3/(2pi). Both are necessary. The spectral eigenvalue powers are bounded on the delta circle; the full original Cartan obeys

kappa(M)/kappa(1)
   =M^(N-1) exp[(Log M)^2/(2Log q)],

whose ell-th power is bounded there too. Summing projectors costs only a polynomial. Equations (4)–(7) therefore bound the full matrix power on the circle by

C N^A(1+|ell|) exp[N(4gamma_*+v_8/pi)].

The original matrix power is holomorphic across M=1 for either sign of ell. Cauchy’s formula transfers this estimate to its center without another inverse delta. Since 4gamma_*+v_8/pi=(2v_3+v_8)/pi, this proves (1), including the complete nilpotent normal contribution.

For a fixed b-strand braid closing to a knot, the original reduced cut is

K_N I=theta_N^(-w) Tr_env[W_N(I tensor g_N^(tensor(b-1)))],

where the retained complete pivot g_N is unitary and |theta_N|=1. Expanding that partial trace in an orthonormal environmental basis gives

|K_N|<=N^(b-1)||W_N||_op.                          (8)

This is a norm bound for the actual complex pivotal trace, not a claim that it is positive. Partition the specified word into consecutive repeated-generator blocks with nonzero exponents ell_t. The matrices are coherently multiplied inside each block BEFORE estimating. From (1), (8), and the independently proved one-crossing exponent,

limsup_N (2pi/N)log|K_N|
   <=sum_t min(|ell_t|v_8, 4v_3+2v_8).             (9)

This applies to odd or even braid width and either sign of each block. It implies the corresponding full Fourier-energy upper bound by the audited polynomial ordinary-color remainder. The complete full-state modified-dimension factor is a specified root phase divided by N, so its exponential rate obeys the same estimate. None of those factors has been reset at an intermediate meridian.

Every block of at least four crossings improves its separate-crossing bound, since 4v_3+2v_8<4v_8. The block must be a repeated generator of the specified braid; arbitrary diagram twist regions would require a proved tangle conversion. Formula (9) has not been shown to be bounded by Vol(S^3 minus K). It proves neither the sharp upper bound nor geometric survival.

4. Verification
