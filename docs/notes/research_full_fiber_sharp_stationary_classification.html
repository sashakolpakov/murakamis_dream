Finite shared multipliers and the stationary branches after full-fiber activation

The stationary classification gives neither separate weighted local maximality nor strong duality. Phase compatibility, cap geometry and volume comparison remain separate.

1. The original maximum and its remaining degrees

Use precisely the original simple-white certified diagram, protected pair, full transport fiber and independent real spectra of full-fiber boundary activation theorem. Each original port has supply one; each ordinary face has demand one; the two protected faces have demand zero. No LEFT/CROSS/RIGHT label inequality is imposed. For one port write

q=(ξ,μ,1−ν,ν−μ−ξ)in the order (x,b,c,y). q=(\xi,\mu,1-\nu,\nu-\mu-\xi) \quad\text{in the order }(x,b,c,y). \tag{1}

The theorem applies to an unrestricted global maximum. It also applies to a competing maximum of value at least ΦD\Phi_D in the closed non-CROSS union, provided at least one middle load is strictly greater than 1/21/2. That strict margin persists in a neighborhood, so every sufficiently small variation within the full transport fiber is still admissible for this restricted problem. No seam-only NC maximum is included.

The boundary reduction has already proved:

  • all ordinary incidences have positive load and every port has positive interval length;
  • at 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/2, neither interval endpoint is an alpha zero;
  • every δ=0\delta=0 port is strict CROSS;
  • half-terminal monomial intervals are absent at every spectrum;
  • a remaining cap δ=1/2\delta=1/2 is a monomial interval [0,ν][0,\nu], 0<ν<1/20<\nu<1/2, or its reflection, with its parent middle and one outer incidence both protected. There are at most two, adjacent to the original marked MAIN edge.

Keep these protected coordinates fixed at zero. The maximum is in the relative interior of the resulting original transport polytope. The relative-interior assertion is about actual incidence coordinates, not an enlarged triangle or fractional-cover flow.

2. Fixed-spectrum smoothness and finite common weights

Put αδ(t)=4[cos⁡2(πδ)−sin⁡2(πt)],β(t)=4sin⁡2(πt),ηδ(t)=cos⁡2(πδ)sin⁡2(πt),Λδ(t;x,y)=β(t)2[x+y+(x−y)2+4ηδ(t)xy]. \begin{aligned} \alpha_\delta(t)&=4\bigl[\cos^2(\pi\delta)-\sin^2(\pi t)\bigr],\\ \beta(t)&=4\sin^2(\pi t),\qquad \eta_\delta(t)=\frac{\cos^2(\pi\delta)}{\sin^2(\pi t)},\\ \Lambda_\delta(t;x,y)&= \frac{\beta(t)}2 \left[x+y+\sqrt{(x-y)^2+4\eta_\delta(t)xy}\right]. \end{aligned} \tag{2} The original sharp functional is hδ(q)=∫0μlog⁡|αδ(t)|dt+∫ν1log⁡|αδ(t)|dt+infℓ∈ℝ{∫μνlogΛδ(t;eℓ,1)dt−ξℓ}. h_\delta(q)= \int_0^\mu\log|\alpha_\delta(t)|\,dt+ \int_\nu^1\log|\alpha_\delta(t)|\,dt+ \inf_{\ell\in\mathbb R} \left\{\int_\mu^\nu\log\Lambda_\delta(t;e^\ell,1)\,dt -\xi\ell\right\}. \tag{3} Both unused integrals and their normalization remain present.

If both outer incidences are ordinary, their loads are positive. Thus 0<ξ<ν−μ0<\xi<\nu-\mu. For δ<1/2\delta<1/2 the integrated logarithmic root has a unique finite minimizing tilt. Indeed ∂ℓ2log⁡Λδ(t;eℓ,1)=ηδ(t)cosh⁡(ℓ/2)4(sinh⁡2(ℓ/2)+ηδ(t))3/2>0, \partial_\ell^2\log\Lambda_\delta(t;e^\ell,1) =\frac{\eta_\delta(t)\cosh(\ell/2)} {4\bigl(\sinh^2(\ell/2)+\eta_\delta(t)\bigr)^{3/2}}>0, \tag{4} and its first derivative ranges from zero to one. The integrated first derivative therefore ranges from zero to ν−μ\nu-\mu. At a protected start zero or stop one, the first derivative is bounded by one and the second derivative is integrable; on compact tilt sets it is O(t)O(t) or O(1−t)O(1-t) at the corresponding endpoint. The implicit function theorem consequently gives a smooth finite tilt in the free degree coordinates.

Every movable row is strictly inside (0,1)(0,1). At strict spectrum it also avoids the two alpha zeros, so differentiating the outside integrals in that row is regular. Their possible interior logarithmic zeros do not affect this degree smoothness. At δ=0\delta=0, the strict CROSS inequalities keep both movable endpoints away from the central zero. A protected endpoint is fixed and is not differentiated. Thus (3) is C1C^1 on the relevant relative open face.

If an outer incidence is protected, specialize the exact monomer first: its forced outer degree leaves Λδ(t;0,y)=β(t)y\Lambda_\delta(t;0,y)=\beta(t)y, or its reflection. The same row argument proves smoothness on this protected face without taking a finite tilt of a zero weight. Both outer incidences cannot be protected, because their MATCHING edge differs from the marked MAIN edge. Finally, at the cap the full sharp functional is identically zero for every degree vector. It is smooth on its protected-coordinate face as well.

Let AA be the original finite row/face constraint matrix after protected incidences are removed. The differential of the total fixed-spectrum entropy annihilates ker⁡A\ker A: every sufficiently small positive and negative tangent displacement is admissible. Elementary row-space orthogonality gives finite real row and face multipliers. Redundant balance equations cause no difficulty. Choose the sign of the ordinary face multiplier λf\lambda_f so that Lf=e−λf>0,ℱe(qe)=hδe(qe)+∑f at eqeflog⁡Lf. L_f=e^{-\lambda_f}>0,\qquad \mathcal F_e(q_e)=h_{\delta_e}(q_e) +\sum_{f\text{ at }e}q_{ef}\log L_f. \tag{5} Here ℱe\mathcal F_e is stationary on the local row simplex. The row multiplier drops out because the local supply is fixed. The same finite LfL_f occurs at every original incidence of ff. At protected faces use the literal value zero only after specializing their zero coordinates; no expression 0log⁡00\log0 or infinite multiplier is used.

This is equality stationarity in degree variables. It is not local maximality of an individual weighted coupon. In particular no independent optimization of local rows or interchange of a shared infimum and maximum has been used.

3. The exact row equations and branch derivatives

At a port let x,b,c,yx,b,c,y now denote its common face weights. A protected one is zero in the literal specialized formula. If both outer weights are positive, outer stationarity in (5) sets the minimizing tilt to ℓ=log⁡(x/y)\ell=\log(x/y). Therefore ℒδ(μ)=b(b>0),ℒδ(ν)=c(c>0). \mathcal L_\delta(\mu)=b\quad(b>0),\qquad \mathcal L_\delta(\nu)=c\quad(c>0). \tag{7} A protected input has μ=0\mu=0, and a protected output has ν=1\nu=1, with no corresponding derivative equation.

4. Strict spectral CROSS and half seams cannot remain

First suppose μ<1/2<ν\mu<1/2<\nu. Reflect the upper half by t↦1−tt\mapsto1-t. The full interval (3) has exactly its original two half-cofactor Legendre expressions with one common outer tilt. At that tilt their actual outer allocations sum to the original ξ\xi; when both outer weights are positive, each positive-length half has a strict outer split. No new independent angle flow is introduced.

At (7) the weighted action is the sum of one branch from {Qb,Jb}\{Q_b,J_b\} and one from {Qc,Jc}\{Q_c,J_c\}, with a protected middle interpreted as Q0Q_0. Its free spectral derivative is their sum. Every summand is nonpositive by (11). Equality of a summand is possible only for a monomial half with its middle and one outer weight zero. Both halves cannot be monomial: both outer weights cannot vanish, and otherwise it would require one outer and both distinct middles to be protected, although there are only two protected faces. Hence the sum is strictly negative. This contradicts interior spectral stationarity for every strict-spectrum CROSS port, including all allowed protected patterns.

Now suppose ν=1/2\nu=1/2 and μ<1/2\mu<1/2. The row ν\nu is ordinary and is a free coordinate in the full local simplex. Its row equation is c=qendc=q_{\mathrm{end}}. The local action is Ub(δ)+12log⁡c,Ub∈{Qb,Jb}. U_b(\delta)+\tfrac12\log c, \qquad U_b\in\{Q_b,J_b\}. \tag{12} Taking the spectral derivative at the stationary rows gives Ub′U_b'. It is strictly negative unless b=P=0b=P=0, in which case the input start is zero and this is exactly the double-protected half-terminal monomial. That case has already been excluded by the cost-free δ=0\delta=0 retuning in the boundary theorem. Thus no strict-spectrum half seam remains. Exact original row reflection proves the same for μ=1/2<ν\mu=1/2<\nu.

This uses an equality row equation, not a one-sided terminal KKT test: the problem is the full original fiber, and 1/21/2 is not one of its local row boundaries. The only fixed row boundaries are those forced by protected zero middles.

6. The residual cap equation and the remaining scope

For a surviving cap corner, orient it as x=b=0x=b=0, q=(0,0,1−ν,ν)q=(0,0,1-\nu,\nu), 0<ν<1/20<\nu<1/2. Both remaining faces are ordinary. The fixed-cap entropy is identically zero, and its local stationarity equation in the genuinely free degree ν\nu is 0=∂ν[(1−ν)log⁡c+νlog⁡y]=log⁡(y/c),c=y>0. 0=\partial_\nu\bigl[(1-\nu)\log c+\nu\log y\bigr] =\log(y/c),\qquad c=y>0. \tag{16} Use outer exchange or exact row reflection for the other orientation. There are still at most two such ports, at the original marked pair. Equation (16) is not a spectral boundary derivative equality and constructs no finite cap collar or geometric pairing.

Thus every maximum in section 1 has finite common positive ordinary face weights. Every noncap port is either complete strict CROSS at δ=0\delta=0, or a strict positive-spectral straddling LEFT/RIGHT branch, including its allowed protected single-line specialization. No other positive-length half seam survives. The only cap configurations not removed by this addendum are the already classified double-protected monomial corners, now with equal remaining weights.

These conclusions concern the proved original-source upper functional; the global closed-domain coefficient-limsup theorem is a separate input. No lower coefficient asymptotic, finite-level relabeling, loss of an unused scalar, shared sign compatibility, geometric cap continuation, absolute bound by ΦD\Phi_D, or filling-period conclusion is inferred.
