Nonvanishing of actual inverse rows and signed q-Hahn node blocks

Written proof, 24 November 2026.

Replaces the first author-final; only index terminology is changed.

The complete inverse Clebsch frame contains a coherent subtraction of the derivative column and a multiple of its ordinary partner. The calibrated normal amplitude makes this subtraction nonzero on real strict physical compacts and fixed complex magnetic collars. Both original inverse rows then have the ordinary exponential action with their known Christoffel–Darboux measure retained. The same imaginary-part gap controls both terms of a signed mixed double-node Jordan contribution, uniformly over every physical auxiliary color, including its exact color zeros.

The pair degree remains an original physical integer. No continuation of the finite-dimensional inverse measure in that degree is asserted. No scalar closing or full contracted coupon lower bound follows.

7. Scope and complete inputs

The complete frame still includes every other double block and every unpaired simple block, including the odd self-paired node when present. No additional normal is assigned to a simple block; its inverse row remains its original inverse simple measure times its ordinary column. The result estimates selected double-block rows, not a quotient obtained by removing the other channels.

[H,M] already supply the exact full inverse and local coupon formulas. [O] already supplies strict real half-prefix action and spectral derivatives. The added conclusion here is the nonzero complex magnetic packet for the actual dual value-row subtraction and for an individual signed double-node contribution, at every color. It is not a new sector estimate or a general assertion about positivity of the inverse frame.

There is no noninteger-tt version of PtP_t, σt,j\sigma_{t,j}, or its inverse Gram being asserted. Subsequent turns, wire sums, source restrictions, caps and exterior factors remain literal. Nonzero inverse entries and individual node contributions do not prevent cancellation in the sum over nodes, a complete sewn coupon, or the original scalar. Nor do they bound the complete tangential error of a geometric trial for the quartic minimum. No knot-volume or finite-exception conclusion is claimed.
