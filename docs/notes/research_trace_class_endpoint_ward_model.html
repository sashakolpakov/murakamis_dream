A trace-class endpoint model for the actual cut Ward current

Written proof, 24 November 2026.

The original binomial cut has a controlled endpoint limit, including its entire rescaled Ward current and every fixed micro-meridian jet. The current, both products with the limiting unbounded lowering operator, and their commutator converge in trace norm at rate O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). This is a statement about the actual boundary operators before pairing with an exterior; it is not a large-level estimate for the exterior or its geometric framing period.

1. The original finite source and the endpoint scaling

Use the orthonormal factorial head basis of [I], with N≥2,d=N−1,QN=e2πi/N,M=eu/N,θ=λ/N,|u|<π.(1) N\ge2,\quad d=N-1,\quad Q_N=e^{2\pi i/N},\qquad M=e^{u/N},\quad \theta=\lambda/N,\quad |u|<\pi . \qquad\text{(1)} The parameters u,λu,\lambda are complex. For real λ≥0\lambda\ge0 and sufficiently large NN, the cut weights below are probabilities; elsewhere their formulas mean holomorphic coefficients. The original lowering matrix is ℰN|a⟩=ϵN,a−1(u)|a−1⟩(1≤a<N),ℰN|0⟩=0,ϵN,r=eu/NQN−r−1−e−u/N.(2) \mathcal E_N|a\rangle =\epsilon_{N,a-1}(u)|a-1\rangle\quad(1\le a<N),\qquad \mathcal E_N|0\rangle=0,\qquad \epsilon_{N,r}=e^{u/N}Q_N^{-r-1}-e^{-u/N}. \qquad\text{(2)} No outward endpoint arrow is added.

Embed this basis into ℋ=ℓ2(ℕ0)\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0), and extend finite matrices by zero. Define the coefficients and tails by pN,a=(N−1a)(λ/N)a(1−λ/N)N−1−a(0≤a<N),pN,a=0(a≥N), p_{N,a}=\binom{N-1}{a}(\lambda/N)^a(1-\lambda/N)^{N-1-a} \quad(0\le a<N),\qquad p_{N,a}=0\quad(a\ge N), TN,r=∑a>rpN,a,PN=∑a≥0pN,a|a⟩⟨a|,P0=|0⟩⟨0|.(3) T_{N,r}=\sum_{a>r}p_{N,a},\qquad P_N=\sum_{a\ge0}p_{N,a}|a\rangle\langle a|,\qquad P_0=|0\rangle\langle0|. \qquad\text{(3)} The notation P0P_0 always means this fixed vacuum projection. In particular TN,r=0T_{N,r}=0 for r≥N−1r\ge N-1, and Tr⁡PN=1\operatorname{Tr}P_N=1. Put DN=NℰN,BN=−∑r=0N−2TN,rNϵN,r(u)|r+1⟩⟨r|.(4) D_N=N\mathcal E_N,\qquad B_N=-\sum_{r=0}^{N-2} \frac{T_{N,r}}{N\epsilon_{N,r}(u)} |r+1\rangle\langle r|. \qquad\text{(4)} These are precisely [I,S]’s actual cut current and lowering matrix with opposite scalar rescalings. Every finite denominator is a unit on |u|<π|u|<\pi, as also proved below.

Multiplication cancels each denominator exactly: DNBN=−diag⁡(TN,0,TN,1,…),BNDN=−diag⁡(0,TN,0,TN,1,…),[DN,BN]=PN−P0.(5) \begin{split} D_NB_N&=-\operatorname{diag}(T_{N,0},T_{N,1},\ldots),\\ B_ND_N&=-\operatorname{diag}(0,T_{N,0},T_{N,1},\ldots),\\ [D_N,B_N]&=P_N-P_0 . \end{split} \qquad\text{(5)} Thus for every actual finite marked cut matrix CC, Tr⁡((PN−P0)C)=Tr⁡(BN[C,DN]).(6) \operatorname{Tr}((P_N-P_0)C) =\operatorname{Tr}(B_N[C,D_N]). \qquad\text{(6)} All original crossings, tail pivots, marks and scalar lines remain in CC. For the original unmarked knot cut, [I] gives CN(M)=FN(M)IC_N(M)=F_N(M)I, so either trace-one probe computes the same original scalar. Equation (6), rather than equality of arbitrary marked probes, is the source-preserving statement used here. The rescaling in (4) changes neither (5) nor (6).

2. The whole limiting operators

Let pa(λ)=e−λλa/a!,τr(λ)=∑a>rpa(λ),P(λ)=diag⁡(p0,p1,…), p_a(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^a/a!,\quad \tau_r(\lambda)=\sum_{a>r}p_a(\lambda),\quad P(\lambda)=\operatorname{diag}(p_0,p_1,\ldots), B(u,λ)=∑r≥0τr(λ)2(πi(r+1)−u)|r+1⟩⟨r|.(7) B(u,\lambda)= \sum_{r\ge0}\frac{\tau_r(\lambda)} {2(\pi i(r+1)-u)} |r+1\rangle\langle r|. \qquad\text{(7)} Every tail is entire in λ\lambda. The series defines a trace-class operator, holomorphic for |u|<π|u|<\pi and entire in λ\lambda, in the trace-class norm.

The limiting lowering operator is the closed weighted shift Du|0⟩=0,Du|a⟩=(2u−2πia)|a−1⟩(a≥1), D_u|0\rangle=0,\qquad D_u|a\rangle=(2u-2\pi ia)|a-1\rangle\quad(a\ge1), Dom⁡Du={v∈ℋ:∑a≥1a2|va|2<∞}.(8) \operatorname{Dom}D_u= \left\{v\in\mathcal H:\sum_{a\ge1}a^2|v_a|^2<\infty\right\}. \qquad\text{(8)} The displayed domain is independent of uu in the stated disk. Closedness follows directly by taking coordinates in a convergent graph sequence. The tail estimates below show that BB maps ℋ\mathcal H into this domain. They also give DuB=−diag⁡(τ0,τ1,…),BDu=−diag⁡(0,τ0,τ1,…).(9) D_uB=-\operatorname{diag}(\tau_0,\tau_1,\ldots),\qquad BD_u=-\operatorname{diag}(0,\tau_0,\tau_1,\ldots). \qquad\text{(9)} The second identity first holds on Dom⁡Du\operatorname{Dom}D_u; its right side is its unique bounded extension to ℋ\mathcal H. Both operators in (9) are trace class. In particular the commutator below means the difference of these specified trace-class products: [Du,B]=P(λ)−P0.(10) [D_u,B]=P(\lambda)-P_0 . \qquad\text{(10)} There is no claim of operator-norm convergence of the unbounded generators themselves.

Theorem. For every compact K⊂{(u,λ)∈ℂ2:|u|<π}K\subset\{(u,\lambda)\in\mathbb C^2:|u|<\pi\}, uniformly on KK, ∥BN−B∥1+∥PN−P∥1+∥DNBN−DuB∥1+∥BNDN−BDu∥1≤CK/N.(11) \begin{split} &\|B_N-B\|_1+\|P_N-P\|_1\\ &\quad+\|D_NB_N-D_uB\|_1 +\|B_ND_N-BD_u\|_1\le C_K/N . \end{split} \qquad\text{(11)} The same bound holds after any fixed mixed derivatives in u,λu,\lambda, with a constant depending on their orders and KK. The symbol ∥⋅∥1\|\cdot\|_1 is trace norm on the stated orthonormal head space. No comparison with a full knot-period norm is made.

3. A weighted coefficient estimate

For λ\lambda in a fixed complex compact set, the generating functions are GN(z,λ)=∑apN,aza=(1+λ(z−1)/N)N−1,G(z,λ)=eλ(z−1).(12) G_N(z,\lambda)=\sum_a p_{N,a}z^a =(1+\lambda(z-1)/N)^{N-1},\qquad G(z,\lambda)=e^{\lambda(z-1)}. \qquad\text{(12)} On |z|=2|z|=2, the Taylor expansion of the logarithm gives (N−1)log⁡(1+λ(z−1)/N)=λ(z−1)+OK(N−1)(N-1)\log(1+\lambda(z-1)/N)=\lambda(z-1)+O_K(N^{-1}). The error is uniform for all sufficiently large NN. Exponentiation and the coefficient Cauchy formula therefore give |pN,a−pa|≤CKN−12−a,|pN,a|+|pa|≤CK2−a(a≥0).(13) |p_{N,a}-p_a|\le C_KN^{-1}2^{-a},\qquad |p_{N,a}|+|p_a|\le C_K2^{-a}\quad(a\ge0). \qquad\text{(13)} The first estimate includes the zero-extended finite coefficients. Summing coefficients above rr proves |TN,r−τr|≤CKN−12−r,|TN,r|+|τr|≤CK2−r.(14) |T_{N,r}-\tau_r|\le C_KN^{-1}2^{-r},\qquad |T_{N,r}|+|\tau_r|\le C_K2^{-r}. \qquad\text{(14)} In particular all fixed polynomially weighted ℓ1\ell^1 versions of these errors are OK(N−1)O_K(N^{-1}). These estimates prove the trace-class assertions for P,BP,B, and also sup⁡r(r+1)|Br+1,r|<∞\sup_r(r+1)|B_{r+1,r}|<\infty, which justifies the domain claim before (9).

For clarity, a diagonal matrix has trace norm equal to the sum of the absolute values of its entries. A shift ∑br|r+1⟩⟨r|\sum b_r|r+1\rangle\langle r| has the same formula for its trace norm: its adjoint times itself is diag⁡(|br|2)\operatorname{diag}(|b_r|^2). Thus these coefficient estimates control the entire operators, rather than each fixed index alone.

4. The original denominators and the current limit

Write k=r+1k=r+1. The exact factorization is NϵN,k−1(u)=2Ne−πik/Nsinh⁡((u−πik)/N).(15) N\epsilon_{N,k-1}(u) =2N e^{-\pi ik/N}\sinh((u-\pi ik)/N). \qquad\text{(15)} Fix |u|≤R<π|u|\le R<\pi. Since |sinh⁡(x+iy)|2=sinh⁡2x+sin⁡2y|\sinh(x+iy)|^2=\sinh^2x+\sin^2y, and |sin⁡y|≥(2/π)dist⁡(y,πℤ)|\sin y|\ge(2/\pi)\operatorname{dist}(y,\pi\mathbb Z), it follows for 1≤k<N1\le k<N that |NϵN,k−1(u)|≥cRmin⁡(k,N−k),|2(u−πik)|≥2(π−R)k.(16) |N\epsilon_{N,k-1}(u)| \ge c_R\min(k,N-k),\qquad |2(u-\pi ik)|\ge2(\pi-R)k . \qquad\text{(16)} Indeed the sine argument lies strictly between −π-\pi and zero, and its distance from either endpoint is at least (π−R)min⁡(k,N−k)/N(\pi-R)\min(k,N-k)/N. This also proves the asserted absence of finite poles.

For 1≤k≤N/21\le k\le N/2, Taylor’s formula for the two entire factors in (15) gives |NϵN,k−1(u)−2(u−πik)|≤CRk2/N,|1NϵN,k−1(u)−12(u−πik)|≤CR/N.(17) |N\epsilon_{N,k-1}(u)-2(u-\pi ik)| \le C_R k^2/N,\qquad \left|\frac1{N\epsilon_{N,k-1}(u)} -\frac1{2(u-\pi ik)}\right|\le C_R/N . \qquad\text{(17)} For the first bound use |u−πik|≤CRk|u-\pi ik|\le C_R k: the exponential correction is O(k2/N)O(k^2/N) and the hyperbolic-sine correction is O(k3/N2)O(k^3/N^2). Their arguments remain in a fixed compact set. Dividing by the two lower bounds (16) proves the second.

On this lower half, the difference of the shift coefficients in BNB_N and BB is consequently bounded by CR|TN,k−1−τk−1|/k+(CR/N)|TN,k−1|.(18) C_R\,|T_{N,k-1}-\tau_{k-1}|/k +(C_R/N)|T_{N,k-1}|. \qquad\text{(18)} Its sum is OK(N−1)O_K(N^{-1}) by (14). On N/2<k<NN/2<k<N, (16) and the exponential tails (14) bound the sum of both coefficient magnitudes by CK2−N/2C_K2^{-N/2}. The remaining infinite tail k≥Nk\ge N is bounded the same way. This proves the first term of (11); (13) proves its second term. The exact diagonal products (5),(9) and (14) prove the other two. No upper-endpoint denominator was replaced by a low-index limit.

All parameter-dependent trace-class series converge normally on compact sets in the stated domain. Prove (11) on a slightly larger compact polydisc about each point of KK, still inside |u|<π|u|<\pi. Applying the Cauchy integral formula to the coefficient errors and summing their absolute values proves every fixed derivative bound. Finite coverings handle a general compact KK. These are the actual scaled meridian derivatives: ∂u=(M/N)∂M\partial_u=(M/N)\partial_M, with the full Leibniz rule retained. They are not derivatives moving the root QNQ_N.

5. Normalization and source scope

For real λ≥0\lambda\ge0 at u=0u=0, all τr\tau_r are nonnegative, so there is an explicit trace norm: ∥B(0,λ)∥1=12π∑a≥1pa(λ)∑k=1a1k=12π∫0λ1−e−ttdt.(19) \|B(0,\lambda)\|_1 =\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{a\ge1}p_a(\lambda) \sum_{k=1}^{a}\frac1k =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^\lambda\frac{1-e^{-t}}t\,dt . \qquad\text{(19)} At zero the integrand has its removable value one. To verify the last equality, differentiate the absolutely convergent Poisson series. If Ha=∑k=1a1/kH_a=\sum_{k=1}^a1/k, its derivative is ∑apa(Ha+1−Ha)=∑apa/(a+1)=(1−e−λ)/λ\sum_a p_a(H_{a+1}-H_a) =\sum_a p_a/(a+1)=(1-e^{-\lambda})/\lambda. The series vanishes at λ=0\lambda=0.

Equations (5)–(11) retain the full finite cut and its endpoint current. They also imply OK(N−1)O_K(N^{-1}) error when the four trace-class operators in (11) are paired with any uniformly operator-bounded family of tests. No such bound for an arbitrary growing knot exterior is supplied by this boundary theorem. Differentiating (6) retains derivatives of that exterior and every other original factor.

6. An exponentially accurate finite window with its boundary current

There is a stronger approximation when the exact finite coefficients are retained. For 2≤m≤N2\le m\le N, censor the cut law at m−1m-1: PN[m]=∑a=0m−2pN,a|a⟩⟨a|+(∑a≥m−1pN,a)|m−1⟩⟨m−1|,BN[m]=−∑r=0m−2TN,rNϵN,r|r+1⟩⟨r|.(20) \begin{split} P_N^{[m]}&=\sum_{a=0}^{m-2}p_{N,a}|a\rangle\langle a| +\left(\sum_{a\ge m-1}p_{N,a}\right)|m-1\rangle\langle m-1|,\\ B_N^{[m]}&=-\sum_{r=0}^{m-2} \frac{T_{N,r}}{N\epsilon_{N,r}}|r+1\rangle\langle r|. \end{split} \qquad\text{(20)} Both are supported on indices 0,…,m−10,\ldots,m-1. The last mass is explicit, not discarded. Direct multiplication gives [DN,BN[m]]=PN[m]−P0,Tr⁡PN[m]=1.(21) [D_N,B_N^{[m]}]=P_N^{[m]}-P_0,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}P_N^{[m]}=1 . \qquad\text{(21)} At real 0≤λ/N≤10\le\lambda/N\le1 it is still a positive probe. For the original unmarked scalar, its evaluation is therefore unchanged exactly, at every mm; this fact is the old normalized-cut identity. Every marked comparison instead retains (21)’s exact defect. For m=1m=1, one may set PN[1]=P0,BN[1]=0P_N^{[1]}=P_0,B_N^{[1]}=0.

Uniformly on compact parameter sets as in (11), ∥PN−PN[m]∥1+∥BN−BN[m]∥1+∥DN(BN−BN[m])∥1+∥(BN−BN[m])DN∥1≤CK(CK/m)m.(22) \begin{split} &\|P_N-P_N^{[m]}\|_1+\|B_N-B_N^{[m]}\|_1\\ &\quad+\|D_N(B_N-B_N^{[m]})\|_1 +\|(B_N-B_N^{[m]})D_N\|_1 \le C_K(C_K/m)^m . \end{split} \qquad\text{(22)} The same statement holds for every fixed parameter derivative. The limiting operators have the identical censored construction and estimate, with DuD_u and their already specified products.

Here is a direct proof with no real-probability assumption. For |λ|≤L|\lambda|\le L, the original finite coefficients and their Poisson limits obey |pN,a|,|pa|≤eLLa/a!. |p_{N,a}|,\ |p_a| \le e^L L^a/a!. Use (N−1a)≤Na/a!\binom{N-1}{a}\le N^a/a! and |1−λ/N|N−1−a≤eL|1-\lambda/N|^{N-1-a}\le e^L for the finite bound. The probability error in (22) is at most 2∑a≥m|pN,a|2\sum_{a\ge m}|p_{N,a}|. The current and both product errors are bounded, using (16), by CK∑r≥m−1|TN,r|≤CKeL∑a≥m(a+1)La/a!C_K\sum_{r\ge m-1}|T_{N,r}| \le C_Ke^L\sum_{a\ge m}(a+1)L^a/a!. For m>2L+2m>2L+2, this last sum is bounded by a constant times mLm/m!mL^m/m!. The elementary inequality m!≥(m/e)mm!\ge(m/e)^m, obtained by integrating log⁡t\log t, proves (22) after increasing CKC_K. The finitely many smaller mm are absorbed in that constant. The same proof applies to the limit. Buffered Cauchy estimates give the derivative assertion.

Consequently, for any fixed c>0c>0, the rank window mN=⌈cN/log⁡N⌉=o(N)(23) m_N=\lceil cN/\log N\rceil=o(N) \qquad\text{(23)} has error at most e−cN+o(N)e^{-cN+o(N)} in (22), uniformly on compact parameter sets and for every fixed jet. It is understood for sufficiently large NN, so 2≤mN≤N2\le m_N\le N. This exponential accuracy uses (20)’s exact finite-NN coefficients. Replacing them by the limiting Poisson coefficients gives only (11)’s algebraic error.

For a specified original marked cut matrix satisfying ∥CN∥≤eCΓN+O(log⁡N)\|C_N\|\le e^{C_\Gamma N+O(\log N)}, choose c>CΓ+Ac>C_\Gamma+A, with A>0A>0. Trace duality and (21) then give |Tr⁡((PN−PN[mN])CN)|=O(e−AN),|Tr⁡((BN−BN[mN])[CN,DN])|=O(e−AN).(24) \begin{split} |\operatorname{Tr}((P_N-P_N^{[m_N]})C_N)|&=O(e^{-AN}),\\ |\operatorname{Tr}((B_N-B_N^{[m_N]})[C_N,D_N])| &=O(e^{-AN}). \end{split} \qquad\text{(24)} The second trace equals the first by the exact commutator identity, not by deleting a cutoff current. At the original complete root, this applies to fixed literal crossing/cup/cap diagrams with polynomially bounded marks and their standard framing/cut lines, or other explicitly bounded external factors of at most exponential growth. Indeed [P] gives ∥RN±1∥ph≤20N−1\|R_N^{\pm1}\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}\le20^{N-1} in the same orthonormal physical head basis. Its cups, ordinary caps, marked cap and pivots have polynomial norm, so a fixed finite tensor contraction has a bound of the required form. Fixed crossing powers may be expanded into their finitely many signed crossings. For a micro-meridian family of exteriors, (24) and its fixed jets require that exterior bound on the corresponding buffered compact parameter set; no uniform exterior estimate is inferred from (11). The full source can still have cancellations far below this absolute error, so (24) is not a scalar relative-error assertion.

7. Coverage and the boundary of the conclusion

The scalar Poisson approximation is classical; see Le Cam’s primary paper for coverage. The independent generating-function proof here also handles compact complex λ\lambda. [G] already supplies a bulk conditioned-Poisson Gaussian presentation of the finite cut. The additional statement here is trace-class convergence of the entire actual endpoint Ward current, its products with DuD_u, and its fixed micro-meridian jets.

This result neither evaluates FNF_N nor identifies the internal geometric centralizer chain. It does not project away Fourier aliases, replace the marked source by a positive trace, or suppress any source-current term. No geometric coefficient or volume lower bound is inferred from the endpoint model.
