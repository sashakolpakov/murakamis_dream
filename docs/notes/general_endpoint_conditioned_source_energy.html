An endpoint-conditioned energy for the original shared source

31 October 2026. Conditional general theorem. The finite shared-coefficient identities and local coefficient bounds below are proved. Sections 5–6 give an explicit compact shared-flow criterion: keep the endpoint entropy and relative-energy bound on the same flow, optionally taking their minimum with the older one-sided envelope. A strict score gap proves the full long-filling limit. The universal geometric inequality for that score remains open; no independent local norm or endpoint concentration supplies it.

1. Actual coefficients and both parity supports

Fix the original certified flat plat DD, with RR coupons, degree d=N−1d=N-1, ordinary face targets dd, and two protected targets zero. Keep the actual homogeneous source and its full polynomial resolvent numerator

VN(σ)=χN(σ)(σ−ΩN)−1,χN(σ)=2TN(σ/2)+2. V_N(\sigma)=\chi_N(\sigma)(\sigma-\Omega_N)^{-1}, \qquad \chi_N(\sigma)=2T_N(\sigma/2)+2.

The positive reference at both parities is

FN={−VN(−2),Neven,VN(−2)−N−2∂σVN(−2),Nodd. F_N= \begin{cases} -V_N(-2),&N\ \text{even},\\ V_N(-2)-N^{-2}\partial_\sigma V_N(-2),&N\ \text{odd}. \end{cases}\tag{1}

For a four-incidence exponent v=(vx,vb,vc,vy)≥0v=(v_x,v_b,v_c,v_y)\ge0 with |v|=d|v|=d, define

pN(v)=[xvxbvbcvcyvy]HFN,aN(v;σ)=[xvxbvbcvcyvy]HVN(σ). p_N(v)=[x^{v_x}b^{v_b}c^{v_c}y^{v_y}]H_{F_N}, \qquad a_N(v;\sigma)=[x^{v_x}b^{v_b}c^{v_c}y^{v_y}]H_{V_N(\sigma)}. \tag{2}

The reference satisfies pN(v)>0p_N(v)>0 for every such exponent, at both parities. In particular none of the inverses below divides by zero. Protected targets remove every array with positive degree at a protected incidence; they are never replaced by positive marked radii.

Let ΓN\Gamma_N be the exact original shared array set: an element 𝒗=(ve)\boldsymbol v=(v_e) satisfies all original face targets. Separate incidences retain their original quartet labels. The path endpoints are i=vbi=v_b and j=d−vcj=d-v_c, so the original classes are determined by each monomial:

CROSSLEFTRIGHTN=2mvb,vc≤m−1vc≥mvb≥mN=2m+1vb,vc≤mvc≥m+1vb≥m+1 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c} &\mathrm{CROSS}&\mathrm{LEFT}&\mathrm{RIGHT}\\ \hline N=2m&v_b,v_c\le m-1&v_c\ge m&v_b\ge m\\ N=2m+1&v_b,v_c\le m&v_c\ge m+1&v_b\ge m+1 \end{array}\tag{3}

They are disjoint and exhaustive. The odd central zero-step monomial is CROSS in this convention. The odd reference derivative retains the same endpoints, so the same partition applies to FNF_N.

For a subset AN⊆ΓNA_N\subseteq\Gamma_N specified by these supports, put

W(𝒗)=∏epN(ve),ℛ(𝒗;𝝈)=∏eaN(ve;σe)pN(ve),ZA=∑𝒗∈ANW(𝒗),SA(𝝈)=∑𝒗∈ANW(𝒗)ℛ(𝒗;𝝈). \begin{aligned} W(\boldsymbol v)&=\prod_e p_N(v_e),& \mathcal R(\boldsymbol v;\boldsymbol\sigma) &=\prod_e\frac{a_N(v_e;\sigma_e)}{p_N(v_e)},\\ Z_A&=\sum_{\boldsymbol v\in A_N}W(\boldsymbol v),& S_A(\boldsymbol\sigma) &=\sum_{\boldsymbol v\in A_N}W(\boldsymbol v) \mathcal R(\boldsymbol v;\boldsymbol\sigma). \end{aligned}\tag{4}

SAS_A is exactly the raw original shared numerator with this support restriction. There is no additional NRN^R, pivot, or independently normalized face contraction. The sign-adjusted convention differs by a common unit sign and gives the same inequalities. The original external character and Fourier-period factors remain outside the numerator.

2. The finite shared Cauchy inequality

Define

EA(𝝈)=∑𝒗∈ANW(𝒗)|ℛ(𝒗;𝝈)|2=∑𝒗∈AN∏e|aN(ve;σe)|2pN(ve). E_A(\boldsymbol\sigma) =\sum_{\boldsymbol v\in A_N}W(\boldsymbol v) |\mathcal R(\boldsymbol v;\boldsymbol\sigma)|^2 =\sum_{\boldsymbol v\in A_N} \prod_e\frac{|a_N(v_e;\sigma_e)|^2}{p_N(v_e)}. \tag{5}

Cauchy on this one shared array set proves

|SA(𝝈)|2≤ZAEA(𝝈)≤ZAEΓN(𝝈). |S_A(\boldsymbol\sigma)|^2 \le Z_AE_A(\boldsymbol\sigma) \le Z_AE_{\Gamma_N}(\boldsymbol\sigma).\tag{6}

The full energy is a concrete positive shared coefficient:

EΓN(𝝈)=[original targets]∏eKN(σe;xe,be,ce,ye),KN(σ;l)=∑|v|=d|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)lv. \begin{aligned} E_{\Gamma_N}(\boldsymbol\sigma) &=[\text{original targets}]\prod_e K_N(\sigma_e;x_e,b_e,c_e,y_e),\\ K_N(\sigma;l)&=\sum_{|v|=d}\frac{|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)}l^v. \end{aligned}\tag{7}

Equation (7) by itself supplies no domination by the endpoint source. It retains every original face demand and does not replace extraction by a product of independent local norms.

Equivalently, for Z=ZΓNZ=Z_{\Gamma_N} and μN(𝒗)=W(𝒗)/Z\mu_N(\boldsymbol v)=W(\boldsymbol v)/Z,

SAZ=𝔼μN[ℛ1A],EΓNZ=𝔼μN[|ℛ|2],|SA|Z≤μN(A)𝔼μN[|ℛ|2]. \frac{S_A}{Z}=\mathbb E_{\mu_N}[\mathcal R\,1_A],\qquad \frac{E_{\Gamma_N}}Z=\mathbb E_{\mu_N}[|\mathcal R|^2],\qquad \frac{|S_A|}Z \le\sqrt{\mu_N(A)\,\mathbb E_{\mu_N}[|\mathcal R|^2]}.\tag{8}

Endpoint concentration does not control an exponentially large ratio on a rare array. The new input is a second-moment bound on this same distribution.

Shared face rescaling does not improve (6). Multiplying each reference coefficient by the same face monomials multiplies WW, on every feasible array, by the fixed scalar ∏fLfd\prod_f L_f^d. It scales ZAZ_A and EAE_A reciprocally, leaving their product unchanged.

3. The sufficient rate and its filling consequence

Write ΦD=Vol⁡(YD)/(2π)\Phi_D=\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)/(2\pi). The endpoint source theorem gives N−1log⁡Z→ΦDN^{-1}\log Z\to\Phi_D. Let NCN\mathrm{NC}_N contain every array with at least one noncross port. Its unique-maximizer concentration gives

limsupN1Nlog⁡ZNC≤ΦD−γD,γD>0. \limsup_N\frac1N\log Z_{\mathrm{NC}} \le\Phi_D-\gamma_D,\qquad\gamma_D>0.\tag{9}

If this support is empty, its original remainder is zero. Otherwise a sufficient new estimate is

limsupN1Nlog⁡sup𝝈∈[−2,2]RENC(𝝈)≤ΦD+γD−εfor some ε>0. \limsup_N\frac1N\log \sup_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in[-2,2]^R}E_{\mathrm{NC}}(\boldsymbol\sigma) \le\Phi_D+\gamma_D-\varepsilon \quad\text{for some }\varepsilon>0.\tag{10}

Using the full energy in (10) is stronger. Equations (6) and (9) then give

limsupN1Nlog⁡sup𝝈∈[−2,2]R|SNC(𝝈)|≤ΦD−ε2. \limsup_N\frac1N\log \sup_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in[-2,2]^R}|S_{\mathrm{NC}}(\boldsymbol\sigma)| \le\Phi_D-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.\tag{11}

Thus an energy rate at most ΦD\Phi_D would suffice, but is not necessary: the endpoint mass gap leaves the additional budget γD\gamma_D in (10). For individual sectors with endpoint rate zsz_s and energy rate ese_s, the sufficient condition is zs+es<2ΦDz_s+e_s<2\Phi_D. The two optimizations must retain their own shared constraints; their maximizing flows need not agree.

Each SAS_A has degree at most dd in each spectral coordinate. Under σe=−2cos⁡(2πδe)\sigma_e=-2\cos(2\pi\delta_e), Bernstein’s bound extends (11) to a sufficiently thin fixed complex strip, with cost at most exp⁡(2πd∑e|Im⁡δe|)\exp(2\pi d\sum_e|\operatorname{Im}\delta_e|). Shrink the strip to retain a positive gap and apply Cauchy for fixed derivatives. The whole-domain current argument then bounds the entire summable charge series, retaining second-derivative endpoint currents. It uses no infinite sharp-cube localization.

Together with the ALL-CROSS long-filling theorem, (10) therefore implies the full invariant’s volume limit for DD and independently sufficiently long, fixed signed twists. The filled action approaches ΦD\Phi_D, so its loss can be made smaller than the fixed remainder gap. This is a conditional implication, not an established universal energy estimate.

4. Exact reduction to spectral coefficient suprema

Define

kN*(v)=1Nlog⁡maxσ∈[−2,2]|aN(v;σ)|,hN(v)=1Nlog⁡pN(v). k_N^*(v)=\frac1N\log\max_{\sigma\in[-2,2]}|a_N(v;\sigma)|, \qquad h_N(v)=\frac1N\log p_N(v).\tag{12}

The coefficient polynomial is nonzero: (1) excludes simultaneous vanishing of its endpoint value and first derivative. For nonempty ANA_N, put

JA,N=max𝒗∈AN∑e(2kN*(ve)−hN(ve)). J_{A,N}=\max_{\boldsymbol v\in A_N} \sum_e\bigl(2k_N^*(v_e)-h_N(v_e)\bigr).

Then, exactly,

eNJA,N≤sup𝝈∈[−2,2]REA(𝝈)≤|AN|eNJA,N. e^{NJ_{A,N}} \le \sup_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in[-2,2]^R}E_A(\boldsymbol\sigma) \le |A_N|e^{NJ_{A,N}}.\tag{13}

The upper bound treats each positive summand. For the lower bound choose a maximizing shared array, then maximize each of its coefficient polynomials in its independent spectral coordinate. All these choices occur in the same source. No shared face radius is separately optimized.

Since |AN|≤(d+33)R|A_N|\le\binom{d+3}{3}^R, the logarithmic error in (13) is OD(log⁡N/N)O_D(\log N/N). The uniform reference entropy replaces hN(v)h_N(v) by h(v/d)h(v/d) with uniform o(1)o(1) error. Thus the energy question is exactly the finite shared-flow condition

limsupNmax𝒗∈NCN∑e(2kN*(ve)−h(ve/d))<ΦD+γD. \limsup_N\max_{\boldsymbol v\in\mathrm{NC}_N} \sum_e\bigl(2k_N^*(v_e)-h(v_e/d)\bigr) <\Phi_D+\gamma_D.\tag{14}

No continuous limit for kN*k_N^* is assumed. A feasible array sequence exceeding this budget would disprove this sufficient route; a large local ratio without the remaining original targets would not.

5. A joint variational criterion on the original shared flow

Let 𝒫D\mathcal P_D be the compact polytope of nonnegative original four-incidence loads qeq_e, with ∑vqe,v=1\sum_vq_{e,v}=1 at every port, ordinary face sums one, and protected face sums zero. Repeated incidences, when present in an allowed source, are counted with their actual labels. Write h(q)h(q) for the proved continuous full reference entropy. In the closed CROSS region qb,qc≤1/2q_b,q_c\le1/2, it is the two-triangle entropy hC(q)h_C(q), including the constrained split of the outer loads.

The one-sided relative-energy theorem and the CROSS interpolation bound give, uniformly on the actual finite supports and on the whole real spectral interval,

1Nlog⁡|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)≤g(v/d)+o(1),g(q)={hC(q),qb,qc≤12,C(qx,qy,12−qx−qy),qc≥12 or qb≥12. \frac1N\log\frac{|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)} \le g(v/d)+o(1),\qquad g(q)= \begin{cases} h_C(q),&q_b,q_c\le\tfrac12,\\ C(q_x,q_y,\tfrac12-q_x-q_y),&q_c\ge\tfrac12 \text{ or }q_b\ge\tfrac12. \end{cases}\tag{15}

On either seam the retained triangle has middle load 1/2−qx−qy1/2-q_x-q_y, so the formulas agree. At (qb,qc)=(1/2,1/2)(q_b,q_c)=(1/2,1/2) both are zero. Thus gg is a well-defined continuous function on the full simplex. It does not reassign either middle load. A zero actual coefficient satisfies (15) with logarithm minus infinity.

Define on the same original feasible flow

H(𝒒)=∑eh(qe),G(𝒒)=∑eg(qe),𝒫DNC={𝒒∈𝒫D:maxemax(qe,b,qe,c)≥12}. H(\boldsymbol q)=\sum_eh(q_e),\qquad G(\boldsymbol q)=\sum_eg(q_e),\qquad \mathcal P_D^{\mathrm{NC}} =\left\{\boldsymbol q\in\mathcal P_D: \max_e\max(q_{e,b},q_{e,c})\ge\tfrac12\right\}. \tag{16}

The exact finite noncross set is contained in this closed set after scaling by dd. The set may also contain limiting boundary flows of patterns that are eventually infeasible; keeping them enlarges the maximum and is safe for this upper criterion. Empty maxima are minus infinity.

For each feasible finite array, uniform reference entropy and (15) give

∏e|aN(ve;σe)|≤exp⁡{N2[H(𝒗/d)+G(𝒗/d)]+o(N)}. \prod_e|a_N(v_e;\sigma_e)| \le\exp\left\{\frac N2 [H(\boldsymbol v/d)+G(\boldsymbol v/d)]+o(N)\right\}. \tag{17}

The error is uniform over all arrays and real spectral coordinates. Sum (17) over the original noncross arrays. Their number is polynomial in NN, and compactness and continuity then prove

limsupN1Nlog⁡sup𝝈∈[−2,2]R|SNC|≤12max𝒒∈𝒫DNC[H(𝒒)+G(𝒒)]. \limsup_N\frac1N\log \sup_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in[-2,2]^R}|S_{\mathrm{NC}}| \le\frac12\max_{\boldsymbol q\in\mathcal P_D^{\mathrm{NC}}} [H(\boldsymbol q)+G(\boldsymbol q)].\tag{18}

This is an arraywise inequality. It is stronger than replacing the right side by 12(max⁡H+max⁡G)\tfrac12(\max H+\max G), whose two maximizers may differ. It requires neither an unknown limiting spectral coefficient function k*k^* nor a minimax exchange.

Joint criterion. For the fixed certified diagram, the explicit condition

max𝒒∈𝒫DNC[H(𝒒)+G(𝒒)]<2ΦD \boxed{\displaystyle \max_{\boldsymbol q\in\mathcal P_D^{\mathrm{NC}}} [H(\boldsymbol q)+G(\boldsymbol q)]<2\Phi_D} \tag{19}

implies a fixed real spectral gap for the entire noncross numerator. The polynomial strip extension and whole-domain current argument of section 3 then prove the full invariant’s filled-volume limit for independently sufficiently long fixed signed twists, with its original mark, characters, and normalization. The ALL-CROSS relative asymptotic remains the leading term. Condition (19) is a proved sufficient reduction; its validity for every certified diagram is open.

6. Combining the energy bound with the earlier envelope

One can retain both available local estimates without comparing them. On the closed LEFT simplex let t=2(1−qc)t=2(1-q_c) and define

henv,L(q)={tC(qx/t,qb/t,qy/t),t>0,0,q=ec. h_{\mathrm{env},L}(q)= \begin{cases} t\,C(q_x/t,q_b/t,q_y/t),&t>0,\\ 0,&q=e_c. \end{cases}\tag{20}

RIGHT is the literal interchange of b,cb,c. This continuous perspective is the exact concave conjugate of the proved weighted upper envelope max⁡{log⁡c,Q(x,b,y)+12log⁡c}\max\{\log c,Q(x,b,y)+\tfrac12\log c\}: its two atoms have loads ece_c and α+ec/2\alpha+e_c/2, respectively. Finite positive outer and middle tilts, followed by a finite compact covering as in the local energy theorem, therefore give a uniform coefficient upper bound N−1log⁡|aN|≤henv,L(q)+o(1)N^{-1}\log|a_N|\le h_{\mathrm{env},L}(q)+o(1). This does not decompose the finite source into the two atoms or exchange an infimum over shared radii with their maximum. Protected faces continue to impose exponent zero; a sector with protected overloaded middle is identically absent.

Put h̃=(h+g)/2\widetilde h=(h+g)/2 and

hsharp(q)={hC(q),qb,qc≤12,min⁡{henv,L(q),h̃(q)},qc≥12,min⁡{henv,R(q),h̃(q)},qb≥12. h_{\mathrm{sharp}}(q)= \begin{cases} h_C(q),&q_b,q_c\le\tfrac12,\\ \min\{h_{\mathrm{env},L}(q),\widetilde h(q)\}, &q_c\ge\tfrac12,\\ \min\{h_{\mathrm{env},R}(q),\widetilde h(q)\}, &q_b\ge\tfrac12. \end{cases}\tag{21}

The formulas agree on their seams. Taking the minimum of two uniform bounds is valid; no assertion that h̃≤henv\widetilde h\le h_{\mathrm{env}} is needed. Repeating the same finite-array sum proves

limsupN1Nlog⁡sup𝝈∈[−2,2]R|SNC|≤max𝒒∈𝒫DNC∑ehsharp(qe). \limsup_N\frac1N\log \sup_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in[-2,2]^R}|S_{\mathrm{NC}}| \le\max_{\boldsymbol q\in\mathcal P_D^{\mathrm{NC}}} \sum_e h_{\mathrm{sharp}}(q_e).\tag{22}

Combined criterion. The compact, original-incidence condition

max𝒒∈𝒫DNC∑ehsharp(qe)<ΦD \boxed{\displaystyle \max_{\boldsymbol q\in\mathcal P_D^{\mathrm{NC}}} \sum_e h_{\mathrm{sharp}}(q_e)<\Phi_D} \tag{23}

has the same full signed long-filling consequence. It is sufficient whenever (19) holds. It also retains every previously established genuine sector or branch-cover certificate based on the one-sided perspective envelope: (21) only lowers that comparison on each covered original sector. To handle the closed-set convention, a flow with a strict overload is approximated by the proved full-support integer flow rounding while retaining that overload; a subsequence fixes its actual finite sector. Continuity then extends the corresponding certificate. At flows with only terminal seams, the score equals the endpoint entropy, whose strict gap on this closed noncross boundary is already proved. This is an implication between criteria, not a claim of a newly verified strict enlargement of the diagram classes.

No concavity of (21) is asserted or required. Equations (18) and (22) use direct compact maximization of the original flows, not unproved convexity or an independent optimization at each face. Proving (23) universally, or finding a feasible original flow that obstructs it, is the remaining geometric entropy problem.

7. Proved inputs and remaining limits

The CROSS cofactor interpolation theorem already gives coefficientwise O(log⁡2N)O(\log^2N) domination by the CROSS endpoint polynomial. That polynomial is at most HFNH_{F_N} at both parities. CROSS factors therefore have polynomial energy cost. The support-preserving endpoint estimates also bound (7) on small spectral polydisks. The general one-sided theorem now supplies the local triangle bound (15). The unresolved step is the global comparison (19) or (23) on the original shared-flow polytope.

A new protected-outer theorem computes kN*k_N^* and its relative-energy limit there, with uniform closed boundary control. It is a local input to (14), not a shared-flow estimate.

Earlier metrics do not supply (10). The complete-circle positive quotient bounds transmission on an already inserted LNL_N, not replacement by VNV_N or overlapping coupons. The full Fourier–Hermite Gram has an actual whole-source excess-energy example on a redundant normalization insertion, but is not the energy (7). The Newton-turn Gram keeps turns and coherent reflected circuits; it does not bound arbitrary source energies. The length-normalized star retains both coefficient and dual weights, which cannot be dropped.

The finite source/parity formulas, shared Cauchy inequality, weakest rate budget, spectral maximum reduction, and conditional whole-period transfer have independent analytical reviews. The new joint and combined-score reductions use the proved uniform local theorem; their global strict inequalities remain hypotheses, not general volume comparison theorems.

Root and both independent peers also reviewed the joint and combined-score reductions in sections 5–6: PASS. This review proves the conditional reductions, not their still-open universal strict entropy hypotheses.
