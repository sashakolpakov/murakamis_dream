A product domain and exact wire contour through a positive source point

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

Fix a positive configuration of the original same-index circle-tree edge geometry. There is a product of angular domains, independent of the level, which contains every full real angular interval and this configuration, and on which all stationary wire branches are single valued, even and holomorphic. The domains narrow near the angular endpoints; fixed-depth rectangular domains generally cannot do this.

The reciprocal factorial divisor theorem then gives an exact original source contour through this entire stationary graph. Its wire endpoints stay fixed, so no moving-wire seam current is introduced. A coherent local Morse segment can be included. This is a reachability theorem for a representative of the whole source, not an estimate on its connectors or a proof of a surviving geometric coefficient.

1. The original stationary relation

Let a finite graph have angular variables tvt_v, with

𝒮={t:|ℜt|<π},av=tan⁡(tv/2),𝒜=ℂ\(i[1,∞)∪−i[1,∞)).(1) \mathcal S=\{t:|\Re t|<\pi\},\qquad a_v=\tan(t_v/2),\qquad \mathcal A=\mathbb C\setminus \bigl(i[1,\infty)\cup-i[1,\infty)\bigr). \qquad\text{(1)}

The maps t↦tan⁡(t/2)t\mapsto\tan(t/2) and a↦2arctan⁡aa\mapsto2\arctan a are inverse biholomorphisms between these domains. The inverse is the branch through zero. Loops and repeated edges are allowed. Fixed outside angular parameters may be included in the geometric statements and then held fixed. Every variable used in the positive whole-phase Morse conclusion is assumed incident to at least one edge.

For a same-index edge e=vwe=vw, the original unsummed phase is

Fe(x;t,u)=b(x,t)+b(x,u),b(x,t)=−J(1/2−x+t/(2π))−J(1/2−x−t/(2π))−2J(x),J′(z)=Log⁡(2sin⁡πz).(2) F_e(x;t,u)=b(x,t)+b(x,u),\qquad b(x,t)=-J(1/2-x+t/(2\pi))-J(1/2-x-t/(2\pi))-2J(x), \quad J'(z)=\operatorname{Log}(2\sin\pi z). \qquad\text{(2)}

Branches in (2) are the continued original ones. The pinned unsummed-source calculation gives

qe2=1+av2+aw2,cot⁡(πxe*)=qe,He=−Fe,xx(xe*)=2πqe(1+qe2)2(1+av2)(1+aw2).(3) q_e^2=1+a_v^2+a_w^2,\qquad \cot(\pi x_e^*)=q_e,\qquad H_e=-F_{e,xx}(x_e^*) =\frac{2\pi q_e(1+q_e^2)^2} {(1+a_v^2)(1+a_w^2)}. \qquad\text{(3)}

Fix a configuration av*∈𝒜a_v^*\in\mathcal A at which every original edge is positive. The exact positive-edge criterion is

ℜqe>(ℜav*)2+(ℜaw*)2,qe(0,0)=1.(4) \Re q_e> \sqrt{(\Re a_v^*)^2+(\Re a_w^*)^2}, \qquad q_e(0,0)=1. \qquad\text{(4)}

No filling equation is needed until a geometric critical point of the whole phase is specified. In the source application below, the traced target angles have negative imaginary parts.

2. Independent tangent contractions and a product neighborhood

Lemma 1. If an original edge is positive at (a,b)(a,b), it remains positive at (λa,μb)(\lambda a,\mu b) for all

0≤λ,μ≤1.(5) 0\le\lambda,\mu\le1. \qquad\text{(5)}

Proof. Write a=A+iαa=A+i\alpha, b=B+iβb=B+i\beta, and

X=A2+B2,Y=α2+β2,C=Aβ−Bα.(6) X=A^2+B^2,\qquad Y=\alpha^2+\beta^2, \qquad C=A\beta-B\alpha. \qquad\text{(6)}

The pinned positive-edge theorem proves that, for X>0X>0, (4) is equivalent to C2<XC^2<X; this inequality also excludes a nonpositive real value of 1+a2+b21+a^2+b^2. For X=0X=0, the criterion is Y<1Y<1. If X>0X>0 and λμ>0\lambda\mu>0,

λ2μ2C2<λ2μ2(A2+B2)≤λ2A2+μ2B2.(7) \lambda^2\mu^2 C^2 <\lambda^2\mu^2(A^2+B^2) \le\lambda^2A^2+\mu^2B^2. \qquad\text{(7)}

If X=0X=0, independent contractions decrease YY. When one contraction is zero, the remaining endpoint belongs to 𝒜\mathcal A. The one-complete-end edge is positive there: if its tangent has nonzero real part, C=0<XC=0<X; if it is purely imaginary, membership in 𝒜\mathcal A gives modulus less than one. Both zero endpoints give the complete point. This proves every case. ▫\square

This is an independent-coordinate strengthening of the archived simultaneous contraction. It is not a path of solutions of fixed primitive filling equations.

Set

Kv=ℝ∪[−av*,av*].(8) K_v=\mathbb R\ \cup\ [-a_v^*,a_v^*]. \qquad\text{(8)}

The second set is the straight complex line segment. It lies in 𝒜\mathcal A. Sign changes do not change the positivity criterion in (6), so Lemma 1 applies to any pair of points in the two finite segments.

Theorem 2. There are symmetric star-shaped open sets Uv⊂𝒜U_v\subset\mathcal A, containing KvK_v, such that for every edge and every (a,b)∈Uv×Uw(a,b)\in U_v\times U_w,

1+a2+b2∉(−∞,0].(9) 1+a^2+b^2\notin(-\infty,0]. \qquad\text{(9)}

Consequently the angular domains

Vv=2arctan⁡Uv⊂𝒮(10) V_v=2\arctan U_v\subset\mathcal S \qquad\text{(10)}

are symmetric and simply connected, contain (−π,π)(-\pi,\pi) and tv*t_v^*, and have bounded imaginary parts. On their product all the functions in (3) have globally defined branches satisfying

ℜqe>0,0<ℜxe*<12,He≠0.(11) \Re q_e>0,\qquad 0<\Re x_e^*<\tfrac12, \qquad H_e\ne0. \qquad\text{(11)}

They are even in each independent endpoint angle. The branches agree with the original positive branches at the target. All these domains are chosen before NN.

Proof. On segment times segment, (9) follows from Lemma 1. If aa is real and b∈𝒜b\in\mathcal A, a nonpositive real value of 1+a2+b21+a^2+b^2 would require b2b^2 real. For real bb the sum is positive. For purely imaginary bb, its modulus is less than one, and the sum is again positive. Thus (9) holds on Kv×KwK_v\times K_w.

Here is a neighborhood construction which also treats the unbounded real tails. For small ϵ>0\epsilon>0, let

Uv={a:|ℑa|<ϵ}∪{a:dist⁡(a,[−av*,av*])<ϵ}.(12) U_v=\{a:|\Im a|<\epsilon\} \ \cup\ \{a:\operatorname{dist}(a,[-a_v^*,a_v^*]) <\epsilon\}. \qquad\text{(12)}

Each of the two sets is convex and contains zero, so the union is star shaped and symmetric. Choose ϵ<1/4\epsilon<1/4 and smaller than the positive distance of all the finite segments from the inverse-tangent cuts. Then Uv⊂𝒜U_v\subset\mathcal A.

The second set in (12) is uniformly bounded, say by MM, after an initial choice of ϵ≤1/4\epsilon\le1/4. If both arguments lie in the horizontal strips, then

ℜ(1+a2+b2)≥1−2ϵ2+(ℜa)2+(ℜb)2>0.(13) \Re(1+a^2+b^2) \ge1-2\epsilon^2+(\Re a)^2+(\Re b)^2>0. \qquad\text{(13)}

If one argument lies in a bounded segment neighborhood and the other in a horizontal-strip tail with |ℜa|>R|\Re a|>R, choose R2>M2+2R^2>M^2+2. The same real-part calculation gives positivity. The remaining pairs lie in a fixed compact set. Their limiting skeleton pairs satisfy (9), so their discriminants have positive distance from the closed nonpositive real ray. Decreasing ϵ\epsilon once more proves (9) for all these pairs. There are finitely many edges, so one common choice works.

The principal square root now gives ℜqe>0\Re q_e>0 throughout. The map

x(q)=12πiLog⁡q+iq−i,x(1)=1/4,(14) x(q)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Log}\frac{q+i}{q-i},\qquad x(1)=1/4, \qquad\text{(14)}

is the inverse of cot⁡πx\cot\pi x from the right half-plane to 0<ℜx<1/20<\Re x<1/2, with the logarithm specified by this normalization. It supplies xe*x_e^*. The factors qeq_e, qe±iq_e\pm i, and 1+av21+a_v^2 do not vanish, proving the last assertion in (11). The discriminant and its principal root are even in the endpoint angles; uniqueness of (14) proves the same for xe*x_e^* and HeH_e. Independent contraction to zero identifies the branches at the positive target.

Finally, the inverse tangent has bounded imaginary part on the horizontal strip in (12), because ϵ<1\epsilon<1, and on the bounded segment neighborhood away from its cuts. Thus each VvV_v has bounded imaginary part. Its simple connectedness and symmetry follow from (1) and (12). ▫\square

The product domains need not consist entirely of positive edge configurations. Equation (9), rather than positivity everywhere, is what is proved on their mixed real-axis/segment neighborhoods.

3. Exact factorial admissibility

Use D=N−1D=N-1, N≥2N\ge2, and the actual interpolation

BN(x,t)=NGN(D/2−Nx+Nt/(2π))GN(D/2−Nx−Nt/(2π))GN(Nx)2.(15) B_N(x,t)=\frac{N}{ G_N(D/2-Nx+Nt/(2\pi)) G_N(D/2-Nx-Nt/(2\pi))G_N(Nx)^2}. \qquad\text{(15)}

The pinned divisor theorem, derived from the original gamma product, states

ord⁡z=kGN(z)=⌊k/N⌋,k∈ℤ,(16) \operatorname{ord}_{z=k}G_N(z)=\lfloor k/N\rfloor, \qquad k\in\mathbb Z, \qquad\text{(16)}

and there are no other zeros or poles. In particular 1/GN1/G_N is holomorphic on ℜz<N\Re z<N. Its zeros at z=−rz=-r, r≥1r\ge1, have order ⌈r/N⌉\lceil r/N\rceil. This is an existing exact divisor result, not a new choice of factorial interpolation.

Lemma 3. Put

bN=−1/(2N),IN=[bN,bN+1].(17) b_N=-1/(2N),\qquad I_N=[b_N,b_N+1]. \qquad\text{(17)}

For every compact subset of

ℜx∈IN,|ℜt|<π,(18) \Re x\in I_N,\qquad |\Re t|<\pi, \qquad\text{(18)}

both BN(x,t)B_N(x,t) and BN(D/N−x,t)B_N(D/N-x,t) are holomorphic on a neighborhood, with no restriction on the finite imaginary heights.

Proof. The unshifted argument obeys ℜ(Nx)≤N−1/2<N\Re(Nx)\le N-1/2<N. For either shifted argument,

ℜ(D/2−Nx±Nt/(2π))≤N/2+N|ℜt|/(2π)<N.(19) \Re(D/2-Nx\pm Nt/(2\pi)) \le N/2+N|\Re t|/(2\pi)<N. \qquad\text{(19)}

Equation (16) proves the assertion. Reflection x↦D/N−xx\mapsto D/N-x preserves INI_N, proving the other orientation. Strictness of the bounds on a compact set supplies the asserted open neighborhood. ▫\square

In particular the entire graph in Theorem 2 is pole-free for the exact factorials. Their values can be zero. Negative real factorial arguments and the exact zeros in (16) are not removed from the source, and no nonvanishing Stirling amplitude is inferred across them. The statement concerns holomorphy of the reciprocal factorials, not a globally nonvanishing logarithm of BNB_N.

4. A representative of the original whole source

First take a closed network whose traced vertices and paired wires are those of the pinned joint-lens theorem. At each vertex retain its fixed signed power RnvR^{n_v}, its original full-Hermite coupon and any specified polynomial spectator. Direct and reversed halfwires use exactly their original arguments. The geometric stationary assignment in Theorem 2 is asserted for the same-index edges of (2); a reversed kernel is not silently assigned that phase.

Set

aN=π−π2N,η=(−1)D,WN(t)=e−iNt(1−ηe−iNt)2,CN,n=eπinD2/(4N).(20) a_N=\pi-\frac{\pi}{2N},\qquad \eta=(-1)^D,\qquad W_N(t)=\frac{e^{-iNt}}{(1-\eta e^{-iNt})^2},\qquad C_{N,n}=e^{\pi i nD^2/(4N)}. \qquad\text{(20)}

Choose full lower angular arcs Γv,N⊂Vv\Gamma_{v,N}\subset V_v from −aN-a_N to aNa_N, passing through tv*t_v^* when ℑtv*<0\Im t_v^*<0. They can be chosen with horizontal tangent at the target. Such arcs exist: in tangent coordinates the neighborhood (12) contains the full real interval and the lower radial arm to av*a_v^*. Two nearby sides of that arm, joined to the real spine, give a simple lower arc; a small local adjustment passes through the target with the stipulated tangent. Apply the biholomorphism (1), and make that last tangent adjustment in the open angular domain. The endpoints remain fixed. The arc may extend beyond their real coordinates but remains in 𝒮\mathcal S.

Together with its reflected upper arc, each lower arc encloses exactly the original physical angular grid, once. There are no additional grid poles in 𝒮\mathcal S. Thus the full-grid lens identity of the pinned source remains valid on these arcs. The odd-level simple spectral node and every double-node normal have their original contributions.

Fix real wire heights heh_e and write

Ae,N=bN+ihe,Be,N=bN+1+ihe.(21) A_{e,N}=b_N+ih_e,\qquad B_{e,N}=b_N+1+ih_e. \qquad\text{(21)}

For an edge assigned its stationary graph, use the broken path

ℒe,N(𝒕):Ae,N→xe*(𝒕)→Be,N.(22) \mathcal L_{e,N}(\mathbf t): A_{e,N}\longrightarrow x_e^*(\mathbf t) \longrightarrow B_{e,N}. \qquad\text{(22)}

Both segments lie in ℜx∈IN\Re x\in I_N, by (11), and depend holomorphically and evenly on their angular variables. Keep any other wire on its original horizontal interval. Parameterize each path on [0,1][0,1], with its midpoint at 1/21/2.

Theorem 4. Replacing every selected wire interval by (22) in the joint factorial-lens integral preserves the entire original source. In particular, for unit coupons the exact traced-network scalar is

ZN=𝔠N∫∏vΓv,N∫∏eℒe,N(𝒕)Ωfac,N,𝔠N=N|E|∏v∈V−N2CN,nvπi.(23) Z_N=\mathfrak c_N \int_{\prod_v\Gamma_{v,N}} \int_{\prod_e\mathcal L_{e,N}(\mathbf t)} \Omega_{{\mathrm{ fac}},N},\qquad \mathfrak c_N=N^{|E|}\prod_{v\in V} \frac{-N^2C_{N,n_v}}{\pi i}. \qquad\text{(23)}

Here Ωfac,N\Omega_{{\mathrm{ fac}},N} is exactly the pinned joint top form, with its fixed coordinate order. It contains every actual halfwire factor (15), all polynomial spectators, and ∏vsin⁡tvWN(tv)einvNtv2/(4π)\prod_v\sin t_v\,W_N(t_v)e^{in_vNt_v^2/(4\pi)}. For a nonunit coupon its original cosine factor is retained. Any pre-existing external framing normalization is unchanged.

Proof. The original horizontal joint integral is exact by the pinned lens theorem. Lemma 3 extends its holomorphic factorial domain from the closed substrip used there to all of (18). For fixed wire values the factorial and polynomial spectators are even and holomorphic on 𝒮\mathcal S, so its residue proof applies to the full lower arcs just constructed. The original physical values and first normals, and the ordinary free boundary polynomials when present, have not changed.

Now fix a tuple of angular points on these arcs. Interpolate pointwise between the horizontal path bN+r+iheb_N+r+ih_e and (22). The real part remains in the convex interval INI_N, and the two endpoints stay at (21). Lemma 3 makes this a compact pole-free homotopy. Cauchy’s theorem therefore preserves that wire integral. Apply the argument successively to every wire and then integrate the angular variables. All chains are compact at this fixed level. In the pullback of the joint top form, angular derivatives of a wire path wedge to zero against the retained full angular volume form, so this fiberwise calculation is exactly the stated joint integral.

Thus no moving seam current occurs. The result also agrees with the fixed-face relative-source identity: both wire endpoint faces remain fixed throughout. Broken paths are piecewise smooth chains; no embedding assumption on an intermediate wire path is needed. This proves (23). ▫\square

The result also holds as an identity in ordinary free boundary polynomials when those ends retain the cardinal representative, as required by the original compiler. It does not identify the asymptotic phase of that free polynomial with a factorial phase. Accordingly the stationary interpretation of a wire with such a free end requires its own boundary-phase input; the exact contour identity itself does not.

5. Coherent Morse segments and their limitation

For a compact angular product in Theorem 2, the holomorphic unit HeH_e has a coherent square root, fixed by the original positive value at completeness. A parameter Morse construction can be made uniformly on a neighborhood of this compact product.

For precision put A=cos⁡2(t/2)A=\cos^2(t/2), B=cos⁡2(u/2)B=\cos^2(u/2), z(x)=sin⁡2πxz(x)=\sin^2\pi x. Near x=xe*x=x_e^*, use the logarithm equal to zero there in

Fe,x(x)=Log⁡(A−z(x))(B−z(x))z(x)2.(24) F_{e,x}(x)=\operatorname{Log}\frac{(A-z(x))(B-z(x))}{z(x)^2}. \qquad\text{(24)}

All factors are units on the graph: besides sin⁡πxe*≠0\sin\pi x_e^*\ne0, the two differences equal A2/(A+B)A^2/(A+B) and B2/(A+B)B^2/(A+B). Integrating (24) from xe*x_e^* defines the original phase difference Fe(x)−Fe(xe*)F_e(x)-F_e(x_e^*), without resetting its critical constant. Division by (x−xe*)2(x-x_e^*)^2, followed by the coherent square root and the inverse function theorem, gives

x=Xe(ξ;𝒕),Xe(0;𝒕)=xe*,Fe(Xe(ξ);𝒕)=Fe(xe*;𝒕)−ξ2/2.(25) x=X_e(\xi;\mathbf t),\qquad X_e(0;\mathbf t)=x_e^*, \quad F_e(X_e(\xi);\mathbf t) =F_e(x_e^*;\mathbf t)-\xi^2/2. \qquad\text{(25)}

The maps are even in each angular variable. Compactness gives a common ρN>0\rho_N>0 such that Xe(ξ;𝒕)X_e(\xi;\mathbf t) is defined for |ξ|≤ρN|\xi|\le\rho_N on a neighborhood of all the angular paths and stays strictly inside the wire strip. Replace (22) by

Ae,N→Xe(−ρN;𝒕),ξ∈[−ρN,ρN]↦Xe(ξ;𝒕),Xe(ρN;𝒕)→Be,N.(26) A_{e,N}\longrightarrow X_e(-\rho_N;\mathbf t),\qquad \xi\in[-\rho_N,\rho_N]\longmapsto X_e(\xi;\mathbf t), \qquad X_e(\rho_N;\mathbf t)\longrightarrow B_{e,N}. \qquad\text{(26)}

The two connectors are straight. The same convex-strip homotopy proves (23) for this path. At a positive whole-phase critical point, horizontal angular tangents together with the real ξe\xi_e directions give precisely the adapted local contour of the pinned unsummed-source theorem.

The radius ρN\rho_N may shrink as the full angular paths approach their moving endpoints. A separate fixed compact neighborhood of the target has a fixed positive phase-Morse radius, because its phase and Hessian do not depend on NN. No assertion is made that this larger radius works over the whole product of full lenses, or that its actual factorial amplitude is uniformly nonzero.

In particular, (26) is a literal segment in a representative of the full finite source. Its two connectors, all other angular portions, all exact factorial zeros and the complete Hermite trace kernels remain in (23). There is no estimate showing that these terms are smaller than its geometric local contribution, or that their coherent sum cannot cancel that contribution.

6. Why a rectangular stationary graph is generally unavailable

The pinched product construction cannot be replaced by an arbitrary fixed-depth full-width rectangle. For an edge with two independent angles, set t=s−iht=s-ih, u=−s−ihu=-s-ih. Direct trigonometry gives

1+tan⁡2(t/2)+tan⁡2(u/2)=4−(cosh⁡h−cos⁡s)2(cosh⁡h+cos⁡s)2.(27) 1+\tan^2(t/2)+\tan^2(u/2) =\frac{4-(\cosh h-\cos s)^2}{(\cosh h+\cos s)^2}. \qquad\text{(27)}

For 0<h<2arsinh⁡10<h<2\operatorname{arsinh}1, this vanishes at s=arccos⁡(cosh⁡h−2)∈(0,π)s=\arccos(\cosh h-2)\in(0,\pi). The discriminant has nonzero derivative in either independent angular coordinate, since ∂tq2=a(1+a2)≠0\partial_t q^2=a(1+a^2)\ne0 there. A local holomorphic square root is impossible. Circling this divisor exchanges qq with −q-q, hence xx with 1−x1-x near the merging stationary value x=1/2x=1/2.

More exactly, a rectangle with real half-width aNa_N and height TT contains such a point whenever

T>hN:=arcosh⁡(2+cos⁡aN)=2arsinh⁡(sinπ4N).(28) T>h_N:=\operatorname{arcosh}(2+\cos a_N) =2\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\sin\frac{\pi}{4N}\right). \qquad\text{(28)}

Thus every fixed T>0T>0 fails for all sufficiently large NN. The construction of Theorem 2 avoids this divisor by narrowing the angular neighborhoods near ±π\pm\pi. It concerns the actual stationary relation, not a singularity of the finite cardinal source, which remains holomorphic. No fold-transport coefficient is inferred from the algebraic exchange of its two stationary branches.
