Independently long integer twists on weighted Conway trees

31 October 2026. The class is specified by literal ordered tangle operations. Every finite rooted ordered tree with at least two vertices gives such a presentation; its branching, valence and depth are unrestricted. A fixed knot-producing parity pattern and sufficiently large independent integer weights give the original invariant’s hyperbolic knot complement volume limit. General arborescent coverage and mixed flagged/unflagged source trees remain separate.

1. The weighted tangle presentation and theorem

Let 𝒯\mathcal T be a finite rooted ordered tree with H≥2H\ge2 vertices, numbered with root 00. There is one integer twist box at each vertex. Write TT for the original ordered quarter-turn and RnR^n for the original integral two-strand twist tangle. For each nonroot vertex define recursively Qv(𝒏)=T(Rnv∏w child of vQw(𝒏)), Q_v(\mathbf n)=T\!\left(R^{n_v}\prod_{w\text{ child of }v} Q_w(\mathbf n)\right), \tag{1} where the product keeps the given child order and an empty product is one. At the root retain the literal cylindrical closure K𝒯(𝒏)=C(T(Rn0)T(∏w child of 0Qw(𝒏))). K_{\mathcal T}(\mathbf n) =C\!\left(T(R^{n_0})\, T\!\left(\prod_{w\text{ child of }0}Q_w(\mathbf n)\right)\right). \tag{2}

The marked one-cut convention, terminal order and disk framings are part of (1)–(2). These are Conway tangle operations on integral rational pieces, so a one-component result is an arborescent knot. No conventional plumbing or numerator-closure isotopy is required by this definition.

Weighted-tree theorem. For every fixed 𝒯\mathcal T there is a finite B(𝒯)B(\mathcal T) such that, for every fixed tuple of integers 𝒏\mathbf n with |nv|>B(𝒯)|n_v|>B(\mathcal T), if the literal pairing in (1)–(2) has one component and no internal closed component, its complement is hyperbolic and limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K𝒯(𝒏))|=Vol⁡(S3\K𝒯(𝒏)). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N} \log\left|K_N(K_{\mathcal T}(\mathbf n))\right| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K_{\mathcal T}(\mathbf n)). \tag{3} The original reduced enhanced Kashaev evaluation is denoted by KNK_N. Both parities and all composite levels occur in the limit. The signs and magnitudes of the weights are independent; no ratio bound is imposed. The tree and all integer weights are fixed before NN tends to infinity. The threshold is allowed to depend on the whole tree.

Every tree in this statement has knot-producing parity choices. This is the constructive terminal-pairing conclusion of the general marked-mask geometry theorem, applied to the literal expression below. It does not say that every parity choice makes a knot. Full twists preserve the pairing.

Equivalently, first fix ϵv∈{0,±1}\epsilon_v\in\{0,\pm1\} giving a knot and write nv=ϵv−2pvn_v=\epsilon_v-2p_v. There is P(𝒯,ϵ)P(\mathcal T,\epsilon) such that (3) holds for all independently fixed signed integers |pv|>P(𝒯,ϵ)|p_v|>P(\mathcal T,\epsilon). Taking a maximum over the finitely many knot-producing parity patterns, and increasing the integer threshold, gives the displayed formulation in terms of |nv||n_v|.

6. More general presentations and the remaining boundary

The same conclusion holds for any fixed original strict h/T/product expression whose reduced Frobenius hub tree has exactly one flagged original leaf at every hub, and whose actual endpoint pairing gives a knot. The reduced-hub theorem is an exact identity for all independent physical colors and first normals. It transfers (8) to the original expression. Principle (9)–(11) then restores that expression’s own core packet, and its own marked geometry proves (16). No tangle isotopy or peripheral identification between rearranged expressions is needed.

For arbitrary strict syntax the exact reduced-hub criterion is at most one flagged leaf per hub. The no-flag case reduces to the already proved completed grammar; the present argument treats the all-hubs-flagged case. Mixtures still contain genuine turns without an adjacent original physical character. The full-Hermite turn norm has exponential rate 2G02G_0; charging that cost to an invented leaf would exceed the known geometric action. A sharp estimate for such mixed trees remains open.

The weighted-tree theorem permits arbitrary finite branching and unary continued-fraction chains, but uses the displayed literal closure. It is not a classification of all arborescent knot presentations, a uniform threshold over growing trees, or a generic main-meridian quantum A-polynomial theorem. Those broader parts of the active goal remain open.

Coverage and scope

The repository comparison identifies the earlier outer-kernel cancellation and the one-hub all-odd overlap with the belt source. The belt construction explicitly leaves branched multi-belt graphs outside its source argument. The new ingredient here is closure under nested T(RϵLjE)T(R^\epsilon L_jE) with distinct original characters, together with the complementary-color principle.

For literature coverage, van der Veen’s Whitehead-chain paper states a volume-conjecture theorem for an infinite family of links. It is a relevant comparator; no equivalence of its knot types with all presentations (1)–(2), or novelty classification, is proved here. Its proof is not an input to this argument.
