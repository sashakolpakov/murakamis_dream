A literal two-cell subdivision of the marked Hamiltonian wire

Private written proof, 17 November 2026. A two-tetrahedron ball with its actual link edge has the paired-boundary tensor Z−1/NZ^{-1}/N. Its composition with the normalized joining tensor NZNZ is the identity. We prove this in both endpoint orders, retaining the original fixed cut E00E_{00}, the face permutation maps, and all edge and vertex measures. No free-vertex deletion or global geometric-period assertion is used.

1. The ball, its orders, and its two charge assignments

Let N>2N>2 be odd, d=N−1d=N-1, qq primitive, and ϵ=1/2\epsilon=1/2 in ℤ/Nℤ\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z. All indices and charges are cyclic. Choose four distinct vertex decorations and compatible nonzero roots sijN=zi−zjs_{ij}^N=z_i-z_j, with sji=−sijs_{ji}=-s_{ij}. Normalization roots are the shared ones of [B].

Start with order (0,1,2,3)=(𝖯,L,𝖲,R).(1) (0,1,2,3)=(\mathsf P,L,\mathsf S,R). \qquad\text{(1)} Glue a positive and a negative tetrahedron, preserving vertex labels, on faces 00 and 22. These two faces form a disk. The result is a ball; its sole interior edge is 13=LR13=LR. Declare that edge to be the link. Its boundary is the union of two two-triangle disks, with material vertices LL and RR. At the LL disk the triangles are glued along 𝖯−L−𝖲\mathsf P-L-\mathsf S; at the RR disk along 𝖯−R−𝖲\mathsf P-R-\mathsf S. Their common boundary consists of two distinct 𝖯𝖲\mathsf P\mathsf S edges. Each material vertex is interior to its port disk, although all four vertices are on the boundary of the ball.

4. The boundary adapters and the exact physical word

Use the common poles 𝖯,𝖲\mathsf P,\mathsf S and root pairs (aL,bL)=(s01,s12),(aR,bR)=(s03,−s23). (a_L,b_L)=(s_{01},s_{12}),\qquad (a_R,b_R)=(s_{03},-s_{23}). 5. Sewing, the material-vertex measure, and the fixed cut

Two oppositely ordered port disks with common roots a,ba,b and complementary charges obey the exact bilinear identity [A] ∑α−,α+𝒜h(α−,α+;i)𝒜1−h(α+,α−;j)=N2(ab)dqiδij.(18) \sum_{\alpha_-,\alpha_+} \mathcal A_h(\alpha_-,\alpha_+;i) \mathcal A_{1-h}(\alpha_+,\alpha_-;j) =N^2(ab)^d q^i\delta_{ij}. \qquad\text{(18)} In this gluing the material port vertex becomes one interior vertex, and its two edges to the poles become non-link edges. Their actual factors are N−1N^{-1}, a−da^{-d}, and b−db^{-d}. Therefore the normalized joining tensor is exactly S=NZ.(19) S=NZ. \qquad\text{(19)} The collar has no interior vertex and its only interior edge is the link edge LRLR; it contributes no additional non-link weight. The two boundary pole-edge occurrences remain boundary data.

Inserting the collar in an old port identification replaces one interior material vertex by two. The corresponding old factor SS is replaced by two sewings and the literal ball (17): SK=KS=I,SKS=S.(20) SK=KS=I,\qquad SKS=S. \qquad\text{(20)} Thus the change of vertex count, the two sets of non-link edge weights, every decoration factor, and the original wire clock are all accounted for. This is an equality of boundary tensors. It holds against arbitrary exterior tensors on the specified paired images, including the actual crossing arrays of [S]. An arbitrary row on the full face-pair space is restricted to that image by its exact projector; no Hermitian row is substituted.

The literal planar cut is E00E_{00}, as proved in [A]. Since KK and SS are diagonal, [K,E00]=[S,E00]=0,SKSE00=SE00=NE00.(21) [K,E_{00}]=[S,E_{00}]=0,\qquad SKS E_{00}=S E_{00}=N E_{00}. \qquad\text{(21)} The cut can be placed on either side of the inserted neutral collar without changing its index or replacing it by a uniform state. In the face-pair realization its transported representative is ℰν=𝒜νE00ℒν,(ℰν)k,k′=fν(k)N2fν(k′),(22) \mathcal E_\nu=\mathcal A_\nu E_{00}\mathcal L_\nu,\qquad (\mathcal E_\nu)_{k,k'} =\frac{f_\nu(k)}{N^2f_\nu(k')}, \qquad\text{(22)} where the entries in (22) mean full face-pair entries with the indicated differences. The inverse dual ℒν\mathcal L_\nu, including its fν−1f_\nu^{-1}, is the one of [A].

For an oriented crossing diagram one may choose h=1h=1 on incoming crossing ports and h=0h=0 on outgoing ports. There are two of each, so the boundary sum is two, as required by [S, (8)]. Every joined wire has complementary charges. Recipe I or II in (2) selects the incoming and outgoing ends independently of their global vertex order. Consequently any finite number of these specified wire subdivisions preserves the finite paired-boundary contraction and its prescribed cut by repeated use of (20),(21). This statement concerns the stated port gluing; it does not assert a separate identification of a proposed whole triangulation.

All identities are finite holomorphic equalities on the same regular root domain, so their decoration derivatives retain the normalizations and inverse rows. No off-root level deformation, original meridian-normal adapter, positivity, or scalar lower bound follows. In particular the calculation supplies a material wire-resolution interface, not the further Hamiltonian detours through 𝖯,𝖲\mathsf P,\mathsf S, a free-vertex deletion, or a pushforward of a selected geometric integration cycle.
