Bounding a signed cap contribution by two derivatives of its pressure

The polynomial remaining after every other face has been summed contains information about cancellations that is lost in an estimate of each local factor. At odd levels it factors into linear forms with nonnegative coefficients. Two derivatives of its logarithm then suffice for a useful bound on its negative real axis. The finite inequality is proved first; sections 2–5 show exactly how it applies to the prescribed coefficient of the original source.

The application fixes the selected cap spectrum at +2+2 and all other spectra at −2-2. The uniform spectral extension allows the selected parameter to vary over [−2,2][-2,2]. Both applications retain the odd-level restriction. Neither bounds the entire filled-knot remainder.

1. Finite polynomial theorem

It is not the variance of the root parameters prp_r. The latter is νd−κd−νd2\nu_d-\kappa_d-\nu_d^2. The essential order is to average the squared factors at one common value of tt, and only then maximize. Maximizing every root factor separately loses this information.

3. Why the actual odd polynomial meets the finite hypothesis

At N=2m+1N=2m+1, the actual central endpoint numerator is exactly Pe,C(−2;x,b,c,y)=N2Dm(x,b,y)Dm(x,c,y),Dm(x,b,y)=det⁡(bI+x2H−+y2H+),(19) P_{e,\mathrm C}(-2;x,b,c,y) =N^2D_m(x,b,y)D_m(x,c,y),\qquad D_m(x,b,y)=\det\left(bI+\frac x2H_-+\frac y2H_+\right), \qquad\text{(19)} where the two physical Hermitian matrices H−,H+H_-,H_+ are positive definite. All normalizations in (19) are those of the original endpoint source. In particular the factor N2N^2 is retained.

If all surviving arguments have positive imaginary parts, the matrix in (19) has positive-definite imaginary part and is invertible. Each determinant is therefore real stable. Products, identification of shared variables, coefficient extraction by differentiation followed by evaluation at zero, and specialization of the protected variables to zero preserve real stability or yield the zero polynomial. These are applied to the actual full exterior in (11). When PN≠0P_N\ne0, binary homogeneity and nonnegative coefficients give exactly (1), with d=N−1d=N-1.

Nonvanishing for all sufficiently large odd levels follows from the same original positive coefficient packet used below. For a level where PN=0P_N=0, the sector itself is zero and every upper bound is automatic. Nothing here substitutes a positive comparator for an even-level numerator: that substitution would not preserve signed negative-axis values.

Combining (5), (15), and (18) gives the finite original-target estimate |SN|≤N2(1+π24)PN(1,1)RN−1(N−1)/2+|TN|,N odd.(20) |S_N|\le N^2\left(1+\frac{\pi^2}{4}\right) P_N(1,1)R_{N-1}^{(N-1)/2}+|T_N|, \qquad N\text{ odd}. \qquad\text{(20)}

5. A two-derivative sufficient criterion

Define ℒ(ν,κ)=−12log⁡max⁡{1−ν−κ,ν−κ,1−4κ}.(28) \mathscr L(\nu,\kappa) =-\frac12\log\max\{1-\nu-\kappa,\ \nu-\kappa,\ 1-4\kappa\}. \qquad\text{(28)} Equations (20), (25), and (27) prove limsupN→∞N odd1Nlog⁡|SN|≤max⁡{Mcap−ℒ(ν,κ),Φ−γ2}.(29) \boxed{\quad \limsup_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\text{ odd}}}\frac1N\log|S_N| \le\max\left\{M_{\mathrm{cap}}-\mathscr L(\nu,\kappa),\ \Phi-\frac\gamma2\right\}.\quad} \qquad\text{(29)} Thus ℒ(ν,κ)>Mcap−Φ\mathscr L(\nu,\kappa)>M_{\mathrm{cap}}-\Phi proves a strict absolute rate gap for this original sector, at the fixed spectra under consideration. If the difference is δ>0\delta>0, the limiting gap is at least min⁡(δ,γ/2)\min(\delta,\gamma/2). A bound with any smaller positive gap holds for all sufficiently large odd levels. A nonstrict inequality gives the weak absolute rate bound.

This criterion can be combined with the existing Mahler or higher-derivative criteria by taking their maximum. It is not claimed to dominate each of them for every root distribution. It instead controls the common negative-axis parameter directly. In the limiting root-measure notation of the existing pressure theorem, the conclusion is the additional explicit lower bound χD≥ℒ(ν,κ)\chi_D\ge\mathscr L(\nu,\kappa).

6. Scope and limitations

The existing negative-axis note proves the exact exterior extraction, finite moment reduction, omitted-tail estimate, odd real stability, and factorwise Mahler loss. The effective-response note proves the local complex pressure packet and its Schur-complement response. The root-pressure note proves that this germ determines the full root measure and the exact common-variable negative-axis loss; its explicit low-order bounds use the Mahler loss, including the conductance and fourth-derivative improvements. The finite squared-factor estimate (5) adds a direct two-derivative lower bound on that common-variable loss. It does not require continuing a classical saddle through the negative axis or reconstructing the limiting density.

The full original-source coefficient has been grouped before taking modulus in (17). Thus this estimate is compatible with the separate exponential lower bound for the sum of absolute original-array terms: cancellation is retained here, whereas that termwise sum discards it. That particular array lower bound was established on even levels; the present signed estimate is on odd levels. No same-level conclusion is inferred by comparing those two parity-restricted statements.

The conclusion concerns one genuine singleton sector and one mixed spectral tuple. It gives no bound for multiple noncentral coupons, all real spectra, a complex filling contour, odd endpoint derivatives, or the even source. In particular the actual even central cofactor is not generally real stable. Its coefficientwise positive comparison with a stable reference does not control signed negative-axis values. The strict fixed-spectrum odd result therefore is not a full remainder theorem and does not prove a new knot-volume equality.
