The preferred knot cusp along the angular filling path

1 November 2026.

A horospherical cross-section of the knot cusp is a Euclidean torus. Normalize the meridian’s translation to one. The translation of the preferred longitude, which follows the knot once and has linking number zero with it, is its preferred cusp shape.

The ordered page decomposition and its positive angular realization construct a representation of the original augmented exterior. This note identifies its preferred main-cusp translation. The argument does not use a quantum asymptotic to identify a representation or a cusp. The small-neighborhood argument below is used only to establish an identity of holomorphic germs. The identity then continues along the entire positive angular chart, even when the original auxiliary meridian logarithms are no longer small.

The geometric inputs are the ordered decomposition, the positive angular charts, and their actual filling completion. The analytic criterion constructs the path used in the quantitative volume theorem. The original marked inputs are the face-map theorem the original marked face maps and the full rational translation proof the preferred-longitude translation proof.

1. Statement

Fix an original ordered all-circle tree with at least one edge. Retain the original disk frames, half twists ϵv∈{0,1,−1}\epsilon_v\in\{0,1,-1\}, and one main knot component. Let ℰ\mathcal E be its physical quarter-turn edges. Set yv=tan⁡(τv/2),ke=(1+yv2+yw2)−1/2,tan⁡(πxe)=ke,xe(𝟎)=14,e=vw. y_v=\tan(\tau_v/2),\qquad k_e=(1+y_v^2+y_w^2)^{-1/2},\qquad \tan(\pi x_e)=k_e,\qquad x_e(\mathbf0)=\frac14, \quad e=vw. \tag{1} All roots and inverse functions use their branches at zero. The local positive chart is |τv|<10−2|\tau_v|<10^{-2}. The ordered page construction gives an actual marked representation there, with main meridian parabolic and primitive auxiliary longitude logarithm Vv=2iτvV_v=2i\tau_v.

Let LpathL_{\mathrm{path}} be exactly the framed main path in Sections 2–6 of the preferred-cusp note. Let λK\lambda_K be the preferred longitude in the augmented exterior. Write wϵw_\epsilon for the oriented writhe of its retained-half-twist diagram and ℓv\ell_v for the linking number of crossing circle vv with the main knot. In the coordinate where the main meridian translates by one, their translations in the angular representation are trn⁡(Lpath)=2i∑e∈ℰcot⁡(πxe)−∑vϵv,trn⁡(λK)=2i∑e∈ℰcot⁡(πxe)−∑vϵv−wϵ. \begin{split} \operatorname{trn}(L_{\mathrm{path}}) &=2i\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\cot(\pi x_e)-\sum_v\epsilon_v,\\ \operatorname{trn}(\lambda_K) &=2i\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\cot(\pi x_e) -\sum_v\epsilon_v-w_\epsilon . \end{split} \tag{2} Here trn\operatorname{trn} denotes Euclidean translation, not matrix trace. The signs are those of the original positive checked crossing and the actual oriented writhe.

For fixed integers pvp_v, put nv=ϵv−2pvn_v=\epsilon_v-2p_v, and follow the positive charge path Uv+pvVv=−2πic,0≤c≤1. U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi i c,\qquad 0\le c\le1. \tag{3} Assume the path stays in the chart and has Im⁡τv<0\operatorname{Im}\tau_v<0 for c>0c>0, as supplied by the curvature-supported certificate. The completion theorem gives a smooth actual filled knot complement at c=1c=1. Its preferred cusp is τK=−w𝒏−∑vnv+2i∑e∈ℰcot⁡(πxe(𝝉(1))). \boxed{\displaystyle \tau_K=-w_{\mathbf n}-\sum_vn_v +2i\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\cot(\pi x_e(\boldsymbol\tau(1))).} \tag{4} Here w𝒏w_{\mathbf n} is the oriented writhe of the diagram with the filled powers nvn_v. Equivalently, the last sum is 2i∑vw∈ℰ1+yv2+yw22i\sum_{vw\in\mathcal E}\sqrt{1+y_v^2+y_w^2}, with the same branches. There is no smallness condition on the total UvU_v in this conclusion.

2. Why actual representations can be compared near zero

There are two different geometric cell descriptions, so a source Hessian by itself does not identify their holonomies. We use the marked positive-gluing rigidity proved in the marked multicusp gluing theorem, Sections 2–3 and 5. It is also used in Section 2 of the original geometric deformation. Here are the precise parts needed.

The new complete triangulation consists of eight positive ideal tetrahedra per tree edge. Its face labels and peripheral paths are those of the original exterior, by the ordered page proof. The old complete construction gives that same marked exterior. Their complete holonomies are conjugate by an orientation-preserving marked isometry. This is the geometric uniqueness of the complete finite-volume metric. It can also be obtained from the existing maximal-volume conjugacy argument in the geometric conjugacy theorem, Section 1: the page representation is an actual representation of the original group and its positive complete tetrahedra have volume 2|ℰ|v8=2πF02|\mathcal E|v_8=2\pi F_0, the known complete volume. This use of rigidity is after the actual topological identification; an equality of cell volumes alone would not identify exteriors.

For completeness, the positive-gluing uniqueness argument applies with an auxiliary longitude rather than a meridian prescribed at each auxiliary cusp. If there are tt ideal tetrahedra and qq cusps, select t−qt-q independent edge equations and one primitive peripheral row at each cusp. The integral edge/peripheral identities identify the symplectic quotient of the edge row space with H1(∂M;ℝ)H_1(\partial M;\mathbb R). Thus these rows form a full-rank real Lagrangian matrix (AB)(A\ B). Choose the main meridian row and all auxiliary longitude rows. They are independent and isotropic because they are one primitive nonzero class on each different torus.

In the positive local orders write uj=log⁡zj,vj=log⁡(1−zj−1),Dj=dvjduj=1zj−1,Im⁡Dj<0. u_j=\log z_j,\qquad v_j=\log(1-z_j^{-1}),\qquad D_j=\frac{dv_j}{du_j}=\frac1{z_j-1},\qquad \operatorname{Im}D_j<0. \tag{5} The Jacobian at fixed chosen peripheral logs is A+Bdiag⁡(Dj)A+B\operatorname{diag}(D_j). If it killed a nonzero vector aa, then (a,diag⁡(Dj)a)(a,\operatorname{diag}(D_j)a) would lie in the complexification of a real Lagrangian subspace. Its symplectic pairing with its conjugate would vanish. The same pairing is, up to the fixed nonzero imaginary factor, ∑jIm⁡Dj|aj|2<0\sum_j\operatorname{Im}D_j|a_j|^2<0, a contradiction. The implicit-function theorem therefore gives one decorated shape germ for the main meridian log zero and the auxiliary logs Vv=2iτvV_v=2i\tau_v. The cited multicusp theorem proves the rank and decorated-representation statements for exactly these mixed peripheral coordinates. Its polarization is the all-cusp form of the proof in the polarized gluing argument, Section 1.

This is a statement about the entire nearby marked representation, not just a formal equality of its peripheral eigenvalues. To apply it to the old family, develop the vertices of the new ideal triangulation using the old representation and its recorded cusp flags. For a lifted ideal vertex gPvgP_v, use ρ(g)ξv\rho(g)\xi_v, where ξv\xi_v is the selected peripheral fixed point. This is independent of the coset representative. Every complete tetrahedron has four distinct vertices, so the resulting cross ratios are holomorphic nearby and remain positive. They obey all the same edge and peripheral equations, with the same log branches by continuity. This puts the old representation into the new shape chart and justifies applying its uniqueness. No individual tetrahedron or subtree is separately conjugated.

The old auxiliary longitude coordinates are valid near zero: the marked theorem gives a diagonal, nonzero derivative of VV with respect to the original auxiliary colors. Thus the old family can indeed be parametrized by these same Vv=2iτvV_v=2i\tau_v. The preceding uniqueness identifies its representation, with all original paths, with the new page representation on a neighborhood of zero.

3. Identification of the retained wire coordinates

Equality of representation germs must also use the same variables in the cotangent expression. Here that identification follows from the exact classical Legendre calculation, not a comparison of volumes.

In the original physical-edge variables the local angular action is b(x,θ)=∫0xlog⁡(2cos⁡2πu+2cos⁡θ)du−2J(x),J(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πu)du. b(x,\theta) =\int_0^x\log(2\cos2\pi u+2\cos\theta)\,du-2J(x), \qquad J(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi u)\,du . \tag{6} For a circle vertex, the analytic elimination of its original hidden variables gives stat⁡θv[∑e∼vb(xe,θv)+iϵvθv24π+iχvθv]. \operatorname{stat}_{\theta_v}\left[ \sum_{e\sim v}b(x_e,\theta_v) +\frac{i\epsilon_v\theta_v^2}{4\pi} +i\chi_v\theta_v\right]. \tag{7} This is the original selected circle action, with its original physical-edge indices: the one-leaf identity is derived explicitly in the regular crossing-circle action, Section 7, its circle-junction extension is in Section 9, and the joint-wire Legendre identity is (38)–(39) of the joint filling transform. The nondegenerate hidden Hessian makes these analytic eliminations unique near the original complete state. The identity retains the physical-edge variables before eliminating them.

There are no bare junctions in the present tree. Set θv=−τv\theta_v=-\tau_v, so that the original relation Vv=−2iθvV_v=-2i\theta_v becomes the geometric convention Vv=2iτvV_v=2i\tau_v. Separate evenness leaves bb unchanged. Every retained edge vwvw therefore has the stationary equation ∂x{b(x,τv)+b(x,τw)}=0. \partial_x\{b(x,\tau_v)+b(x,\tau_w)\}=0. \tag{8} Writing ce=cos⁡2πxec_e=\cos2\pi x_e, this is (ce+cos⁡τv)(ce+cos⁡τw)=(1−ce)2. (c_e+\cos\tau_v)(c_e+\cos\tau_w)=(1-c_e)^2. \tag{9} The branch through xe=1/4x_e=1/4 gives ce=1−cos⁡τvcos⁡τw2+cos⁡τv+cos⁡τw, c_e=\frac{1-\cos\tau_v\cos\tau_w} {2+\cos\tau_v+\cos\tau_w}, \tag{10} which is exactly (1). The derivative of (8) at zero is nonzero. Thus these are the retained old physical-turn labels as analytic germs, not merely variables with the same complete values.

The color itself also matches the primitive geometric meridian. Let Ψ(𝝉)\Psi(\boldsymbol\tau) be the sum of the stationary edge actions. The θ\theta-stationarity in (7) gives χv=−iΨτv+ϵvτv2π,−2πiχv=−2πΨτv−iϵvτv=Uv. \chi_v=-i\Psi_{\tau_v} +\frac{\epsilon_v\tau_v}{2\pi},\qquad -2\pi i\chi_v =-2\pi\Psi_{\tau_v}-i\epsilon_v\tau_v=U_v . \tag{11} The last expression is the actual page-by-page primitive word from the ordered decomposition. Near zero it has derivative dv−iϵvd_v-i\epsilon_v in τv\tau_v, where dvd_v is the circle’s degree. This is nonzero. Equation (11) is not used as a substitute for the representation comparison in Section 2.

4. Holomorphic continuation of the actual main translation

The old preferred-cusp proof establishes its rational identities before imposing any auxiliary filling equation. Its product potential, endpoint rebasing rule, actual quarter-turn maps, and pure and odd leaf words give trn⁡(Lpath)=2i∑ecot⁡(πxe)−∑vϵv. \operatorname{trn}(L_{\mathrm{path}}) =2i\sum_e\cot(\pi x_e)-\sum_v\epsilon_v . \tag{12} on the old nearby marked family. By Sections 2–3, (12) holds for the actual new page representation near zero.

Both sides are holomorphic on the connected angular chart. For the right side this follows directly from (1). For the left side, keep the actual based main meridian and longitudinal words. The meridian is nonidentity parabolic throughout the chart: it is conjugate to one of the nontrivial local pants meridians. Locally send its marked fixed point to infinity and normalize its translation to one. The commuting longitudinal holonomy is then a translation as well. Its coefficient is holomorphic and independent of the remaining translation in this normalization. These local functions agree on overlaps, so they define one holomorphic translation ratio on the entire chart.

Equivalently, choose the local trace-two unipotent lifts and write M=I+NM=I+N, L=I+λNL=I+\lambda N. Since N≠0N\ne0, the coefficient λ\lambda is a holomorphic function on any open set where a chosen entry of NN is nonzero. The formulas agree on overlaps. This description uses no logarithm of the main longitude and therefore has no 2πi2\pi i or integer translation ambiguity.

The identity theorem now extends (12) throughout that chart. A continuation along the positive charge path would suffice; no uniform neighborhood in the original summed meridian coordinates is required. The original endpoint-sign convention remains part of the word: when an arc is reversed, both its traversal and its unit-meridian orientation reverse. The affine multipliers −1-1 at odd seams are not replaced by +1+1.

The homological computation of the fixed word is independent of the representation. It is exactly [Lpath]=wϵ[μK]+∑vℓv[μv],λK=LpathμK−wϵ. [L_{\mathrm{path}}] =w_\epsilon[\mu_K]+\sum_v\ell_v[\mu_v],\qquad \lambda_K=L_{\mathrm{path}}\mu_K^{-w_\epsilon}. \tag{13} Hence the second identity in (2) continues with the first.

5. The preferred longitude after the actual fillings

The original crossing circles are pairwise unlinked, and their primitive disk longitudes satisfy [λv]=ℓv[μK][\lambda_v]=\ell_v[\mu_K]. Filling the original slopes μv+pvλv\mu_v+p_v\lambda_v gives λfill=λKμK∑vpvℓv2. \lambda_{\mathrm{fill}} =\lambda_K\mu_K^{\sum_vp_v\ell_v^2}. \tag{14} This remains a fixed word on the main boundary throughout the cone path. At c=1c=1 it is precisely the preferred zero-linking longitude of the smooth filled knot exterior. At intermediate cc its translation is simply the translation of that same boundary curve; no smooth filling at an intermediate nonintegral cone angle is asserted.

The disk orientations give ℓv2=2(1+ov)\ell_v^2=2(1+o_v) with ov=1o_v=1 for parallel strands and ov=−1o_v=-1 for antiparallel strands, while wϵ=∑vovϵvw_\epsilon=\sum_vo_v\epsilon_v and w𝒏=∑vovnvw_{\mathbf n}=\sum_vo_vn_v. Therefore −∑vϵv−wϵ+∑vpvℓv2=−∑vnv−w𝒏. -\sum_v\epsilon_v-w_\epsilon+\sum_vp_v\ell_v^2 =-\sum_vn_v-w_{\mathbf n}. \tag{15} Adding the meridian translations in (14) to (2) proves (4).

The new conclusion is a geometric identity on the positive angular continuation of this actual all-circle class. It does not extend the original real angle chamber, and it does not assert a formula on unrelated algebraic solutions. Its proof uses the exact marked representation, actual path words, local geometric uniqueness, and analytic continuation. The independent scalar calculation and the new source asymptotic may subsequently use (4), but neither was used to identify this cusp.
