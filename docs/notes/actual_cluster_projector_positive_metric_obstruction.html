Actual Racah cluster projectors cannot share an orthogonal positive metric

29 October 2026. This is an exact obstruction to one proposed shortcut for higher generic normals. Even the COMPLETE analytic root clusters of the two physical Casimirs need not be orthogonal projections for any common positive Hermitian form. A seven-level source example has rank-two cluster projectors P,Q with trace(PQ)>33. The bound would be at most two for orthogonal projections in any positive metric.

This does not refute the proved all-partner first-normal estimate, higher scalar bounds, or a suitably weighted nonorthogonal transport estimate. It rules out obtaining those conclusions by declaring the two actual cluster decompositions simultaneously orthogonal.

1. The second normal retains an actual cluster overlap

Use the same generic source as the all-partner first-normal theorem. For a pair of complementary internal channels write e=N-1-e, f=N-1-f, d_j=[2j+1], and

K_ij=[N]^2 S(e_i,f_j)^2 product_faces Delta^2,
e_0=e,e_1=e*, f_0=f,f_1=f*.

The proved exact source-projector identity is

sum_(i,j=0,1) d_(e_i)d_(f_j) K_ij
      =[N]^2 tr(E_e^S E_f^T),                 (1)

where E_e^S and E_f^T are the ANALYTIC complete cluster projectors on the actual saturated invariant lattice. Each has rank two when its two channels are distinct. The trace in (1) is the ordinary local multiplicity-space trace from the source identity; the original marked exterior has not been replaced by a trace invariant.

Let t=log(zeta/q), and let [t^r] denote Taylor COEFFICIENTS, rather than unnormalized derivatives. If

d_(e_i)d_(f_j)=a_ij,0+a_ij,1 t+a_ij,2 t^2+...,
K_ij=W_N^2+k_ij,1 t+k_ij,2 t^2+...,
[N]=c_N t+O(t^2),

then the second coefficient of (1) is EXACTLY

sum_ij(a_ij,0 k_ij,2+a_ij,1 k_ij,1+a_ij,2 W_N^2)
             =c_N^2 tr(E_e^S(q) E_f^T(q)).     (2)

Consequently the homogeneous first-normal Ward equation cannot simply be imposed at second order. The additional overlap is a real physical source quantity, rather than a removable convention.

2. An exact seven-level example

Take

N=7, D=6, q=exp(pi i/7),
a=b=c=d=2, e=f=2, e*=f*=4.

The ordinary invariant channel range is 0,1,2,3,4, so the saturated invariant lattice has rank five. The root eigenvalue of channel 2 coincides precisely with that of channel 4. None of channels 0,1,3 shares it. Thus the complete selected cluster has rank two in EACH pairing. Its projector is analytic at q by the proved cluster theorem.

Set r=q+q^-1=2cos(pi/7). In its real cyclotomic subfield,

r^3-r^2-2r+1=0,
W_7^2=(1+r)^2.                                (3)

Literal expansion of the FOUR actual seven-factorial germs in (1) gives

[t^0] sum_ij d_(e_i)d_(f_j) K_ij =0,
[t^1] sum_ij d_(e_i)d_(f_j) K_ij =0,
[t^2] sum_ij d_(e_i)d_(f_j) K_ij
                       =3444-5180r-6440r^2.   (4)

The leading quantum-integer coefficient is equally explicit:

c_7=-14/(q-q^-1),
c_7^2=-28-28r-56r^2.                          (5)

For an independent check of the division, ordinary polynomial reduction modulo the cubic in (3) gives

(-28-28r-56r^2)(24r^2+20r-11)
                      =3444-5180r-6440r^2.   (6)

Equations (1),(4)–(6) therefore prove the EXACT source identity

tr(E_2^S(q) E_2^T(q))=24r^2+20r-11.           (7)

Since pi/7<pi/3, one has r>1. Hence (7) is strictly greater than 33, in particular strictly greater than two. No numerical rounding, large-level extrapolation, or conjectural asymptotic is used.

The audit evaluates (4) directly in Q[q]/Phi_14(q), retaining the actual generic t-series. In this example every individual Racah range is tail-free, because the untouched opposite sum a+b+c+d=8<=12. This makes the finite coefficient certificate especially short; it does not substitute a semisimple root value for the source germ. The calculation uses three Taylor coefficients of the root-unit factorial products after canceling their actual common [7] zero.

3. Why this excludes every common orthogonal positive metric

Suppose H were a positive definite Hermitian form making BOTH P=E_2^S(q) and Q=E_2^T(q) self-adjoint. They are idempotents, so in an H-orthonormal basis they would be ordinary orthogonal projections. For an orthonormal basis u_1,u_2 of the range of P,

tr(PQ)=tr(PQP)=sum_(j=1)^2 <Q u_j,u_j>_H,
0<=<Q u_j,u_j>_H<=1.

Thus tr(PQ)<=2. This contradicts (7). Trace is invariant under simultaneous change of basis, so allowing a different physical gauge or a nondiagonal positive form does not repair the contradiction.

This excludes simultaneous positive self-adjointness of the ACTUAL complete cluster projectors, already at one finite level. It does not exclude a positive form in which those projectors are nonorthogonal, a metric adapted only to one pairing, a weighted source/dual energy bound, or a direct bound on the scalar generic germs. The exact generic bilinear source metric remains a separate, valid statement; a transpose identity must not be turned into a Hermitian orthogonality identity.

4. Consequence for the higher-normal proof path

The first-normal bound uses exact positive generic presentations on face-supported branches and first-order Ward/Fourier relations. A higher-order extension must retain the overlap in (2), together with its derivatives at subsequent orders, or find a different exact positive presentation. The actual cluster projectors cannot be made jointly orthogonal to bypass these terms.

This is an obstruction to that proposed METHOD, not a counterexample to the general volume limit or to polynomial higher-normal scalar bounds. Those bounds remain open outside the already proved face- supported and tail-free branches.

This excludes the stated common orthogonal metric shortcut, not a general volume conjecture.
