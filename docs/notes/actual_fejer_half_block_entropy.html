The actual Fejér half block: uniform coefficient entropy

, 31 October 2026. This gives the exact level-N half-block recurrence, its weighted rate, and the uniform exponential rate of EVERY coefficient, including the boundary degrees. It also fixes the complete-triangle normalization and disproves one proposed endpoint-convexity shortcut. These are local source theorems; no shared forest bound, representation, or filling-cycle conclusion is asserted. All limits below run through every even N tending to infinity.

1. The original finite polynomial and its exact recurrence

Let N=2m>=4, d=N-1, M=m-1, theta=pi/N, and

c_j=2cos((2j+1)theta), s_j=2sin(j theta),
a_j=2+c_j=4cos^2((j+1/2)theta), product_(j=0)^M a_j=2.

Use the original homogeneous source and its positive cross/half decomposition. Thus P=-H_(V_N(-2)), where V_N is the actual polynomial resolvent numerator, and P_left contains exactly the physical paths j to i with 0<=i<=j<=M. Their middle monomial is b^i c^(d-j). These pieces are not separately asserted to be covariant coupons.

For an interval i+1,…,j let K_(i,j) be its positive tiling continuant. A monomer at r has weight s_r^2(x+y); a dimer ending at r has weight s_r s_(r-1) a_(r-1)xy. The latter includes the UNUSED intermediate diagonal. Set K_(i,i)=1 and Q_(i,j)=K_(i,j)/product_(r=i+1)^j a_r. The actual path expansion is exactly

P_left=2 sum_(i<=j) b^i c^(d-j)
           product_(r<i)a_r product_(r>j)^M a_r K_(i,j)
      =4 sum_(i<=j) b^i c^(d-j) Q_(i,j)/a_i,          (1)

Q_(i,i)=1, Q_(i,i+1)=alpha_(i+1),
Q_(i,j)=alpha_j Q_(i,j-1)+beta_j Q_(i,j-2),
alpha_j=(s_j^2/a_j)(x+y),
beta_j=(s_j s_(j-1)/a_j)xy.                         (2)

All absent negative-index terms are zero. The original level remains N, not m. Define the strictly positive coefficients

A_N(i,j,k)=[x^k y^(j-i-k)]Q_(i,j), 0<=k<=j-i.

The corresponding coefficient of P_left is (4/a_i)A_N. The multiplier 4/a_i is between a positive constant and CN^2, uniformly in every row. Right paths are obtained by the literal row reflection j -> d-j and b <-> c, with the same x,y polynomial.

2. Uniform weighted rate, retaining both physical endpoints

Fix positive x,y, uniformly on compact positive sets. The limiting Perron root at t=j/N is

L(t;x,y)=[(x+y)sin^2(pi t)+sin(pi t)
   sqrt((x+y)^2 sin^2(pi t)+4xy cos^2(pi t))]
                                       /[2cos^2(pi t)],
L^2-(x+y)tan^2(pi t)L-xy tan^2(pi t)=0, L>0.        (3)

It increases strictly from zero to infinity. Uniformly over ALL rows,

sup_(0<=i<=j<=M) |N^-1 log Q_(i,j)(x,y)
           -integral_(i/N)^(j/N) log L(t;x,y)dt| ->0. (4)

Here is a proof with no endpoint gap. Fix eta>0. On eta N<=j<=(1/2-eta)N the coefficients of (2) stay in a compact positive set, converge uniformly to their limits, and vary by O_eta(1/N) per step. For a constant recurrence q_l=alpha q_(l-1)+beta q_(l-2), q_0=1,q_1=alpha, one has

q_l=(lambda_+^(l+1)-lambda_-^(l+1))/(lambda_+-lambda_-),
lambda_-<0<lambda_+, |lambda_-|/lambda_+<=r_eta<1.

Hence c_eta lambda_+^l<=q_l<=C_eta lambda_+^l, and the corresponding transfer-matrix powers obey the same upper exponential bound. Partition an arbitrary interior interval into blocks of length floor(sqrt N), with shorter end blocks. Positivity compares each block with its minimum and maximum constant coefficients. Retaining only tilings which do not cross a block boundary gives the lower bound; transfer matrices give the upper bound. The logarithmic block overhead is O_eta(sqrt N+1), and the accumulated variation cost is O_eta(sqrt N). Thus (4) holds uniformly for all interior intervals, including short ones.

The omitted endpoint intervals cost at most

C eta N log(2/eta)+C log N                         (5)

in absolute logarithmic size. For the upper bound use transfer norm at most product_j(1+alpha_j+beta_j). Near zero these coefficients are bounded; near M the denominator a_j is comparable to ((M-j+1/2)/N)^2, giving the sum of logarithms C eta N+2 sum_(r<=eta N)log(N/r). For the lower bound concatenate only monomers on the omitted sites. Near zero alpha_j>=c(j/N)^2, and near M it is bounded below by a positive constant; their negative logarithms satisfy (5). These arguments also apply to intervals entirely inside an endpoint portion. Finally log L=log t+O(1) near zero and log L=-2log(1/2-t)+O(1) near one half, so the omitted limiting integrals are O(eta log(2/eta)). Let N tend to infinity and then eta to zero. This proves (4), locally uniformly in the positive weights.

Write F(x,b,y) for the ABSOLUTELY normalized source triangle potential in the original action theorem, section6. Equation (1), (4), and polynomially many summands imply

lim N^-1 log P_left
  =max_(0<=mu<=nu<=1/2)
    [mu log b+(1-nu)log c+integral_mu^nu log L(t)dt]. (6)

For fixed mu the expression is convex in nu, so its maximum is at nu=mu or nu=1/2. The latter is (log c)/2+H(x,b,y), where

H=max_(0<=mu<=1/2)
      [mu log b+integral_mu^(1/2)log L(t)dt].

The unique maximizer obeys L(mu)=b, or tan(pi mu)=b/sqrt(b(x+y)+xy). Its envelope derivative with respect to log b is mu, exactly the source triangle middle angle divided by 2pi. Both H-(log b)/2 and F-(log b)/2 tend to zero as b tends to infinity: for H use the logarithmic endpoint tail, and for F use its original positive logdet integral and integrable cos^(-2) endpoint bound. Therefore H=F with NO missing additive constant. Also F>=(log b)/2, from the actual determinant’s b^M term. Consequently

lim N^-1 log P_left =max(log c,F(x,b,y)+(log c)/2),
lim N^-1 log P_right=max(log b,F(x,c,y)+(log b)/2),
lim N^-1 log P=max(F(x,b,y)+F(x,c,y),log b,log c).   (7)

The last formula uses the exact cross comparison. All weights in (7) are positive and fixed before N. It is NOT a coefficientwise bound by a complete coupon plus an identity coupon, and it licenses no exchange of infimum over shared radii with a maximum over local branches.

3. Actual coefficients in strict interiors

Put

D={0<=mu<=nu<=1/2, 0<=xi<=nu-mu},
g(t,l)=log L(t;exp(l),1)
       =l/2+log tan(pi t)+asinh(tan(pi t)cosh(l/2)),
Phi(mu,nu,l)=integral_mu^nu g(t,l)dt,
E(mu,nu,xi)=inf_(l real)[Phi(mu,nu,l)-xi l].        (8)

First consider compact subsets of 0<mu<nu<1/2, 0<xi<nu-mu. Normalize Q_(i,j) by product_(v=i+1)^j a’_v, where a’_v=s_v^2/a_v. The resulting monic degree-r polynomial, r=j-i, satisfies

p_0=1, p_1=x+y,
p_r=(x+y)p_(r-1)+gamma_r xy p_(r-2),
gamma_r=a_(i+r-1)/(s_(i+r)s_(i+r-1))>0.            (9)

Substituting x/y=-z^2 and dividing by z^r gives the Jacobi recurrence q_r(t)=t q_(r-1)(t)-gamma_r q_(r-2)(t), t=z^-1-z. Its symmetric tridiagonal matrix has positive off-diagonal entries, so its roots are real and simple. The map z>0 ->z^-1-z is bijective onto the real line. Thus p_r(x,1) has exactly r negative real roots. The compact-interval bounds on gamma give a uniform Jacobi norm bound, placing all these roots in a compact negative interval away from zero and infinity. The polynomial is reciprocal as well.

After a bounded tilt x=exp(l), y=1, its coefficient distribution is therefore a sum of r independent Bernoulli variables with probabilities uniformly away from zero and one. For k/r in a compact subset of (0,1), the unique exact-mean tilt stays bounded. Its probability at that mean is comparable to r^(-1/2): Fourier inversion has a Gaussian expansion on |theta|<=r^(-2/5), variance comparable to r, and modulus bounded by exp[-c r sin^2(theta/2)] elsewhere. Bounded third moments give the relative Gaussian error o(1). The logarithm of this probability is therefore O(log N).

The weighted theorem (4) and this exact coefficient identity give

N^-1 log A_N(i,j,k) -> E(mu,nu,xi)                (10)

for every sequence i/N->mu,j/N->nu,k/N->xi in a strict interior, locally uniformly there. To justify convergence of the exact tilts, the normalized log polynomials are convex and converge locally uniformly to differentiable strictly convex Phi. The two secant inequalities imply convergence of their first derivatives on compact intervals; the unique limiting tilt satisfies Phi_l=xi. The same root/variance bounds imply Phi_ll>0. No derivative of an unsupported weighted asymptotic has been taken.

4. The closed-domain theorem

The function E is continuous on ALL of D, and

sup_(0<=i<=j<=M,0<=k<=j-i)
  |N^-1 log A_N(i,j,k)-E(i/N,j/N,k/N)| ->0.         (11)

This also holds for the actual half-block coefficient (4/a_i)A_N, since its additional logarithm is uniformly O(log N).

For continuity set q=nu-mu, B=2 integral_mu^nu log tan(pi t)dt, and

delta_r(t)=asinh(tan(pi t)cosh(r/2))-r/2-log tan(pi t), r>=0.

This is positive, decreases to zero, and is dominated by the integrable delta_0: it is -log t+O(1) at zero and tends to log2 at one half. Its full-interval integral tends to zero as r tends to infinity. Exactly, with a=min(xi,q-xi),

E=B+inf_(r>=0)[a r+integral_mu^nu delta_r(t)dt].    (12)

Hence B<=E<=B+integral_mu^nu delta_0, which proves E->0 uniformly as q->0. As a->0 choose a large fixed r first; (12) gives E-B->0 uniformly. Where a>=a0>0 the minimizer is restricted to a common compact r interval, so continuity follows by compact minimization. In particular E(mu,nu,0)=E(mu,nu,nu-mu)=B.

For the uniform upper bound, every finite real l gives N^-1 log A_N<=N^-1 log Q_(i,j)(exp(l),1)-(k/N)l. At each point of D select a finite l within epsilon of its infimum, even when the infimum is reached only at infinity. Continuity gives a neighborhood where the same l works within 2epsilon. A finite cover of D and (4) for this finite set of tilts give the uniform upper bound.

For the lower bound consider any sequence tending to a point with 0<xi<q. Trim to i’=max(i,ceil(eta N)), j’=min(j,floor((1/2-eta)N)), and set r’=j’-i’, r=j-i, k’=floor(k r’/r). For small fixed eta, r’>0 and 0<=k-k’<=r-r’. Retain an arbitrary interior tiling and specified x- and y-monomers on the omitted sites. This is an actual subset of the original tilings, with no crossing dimer, and gives

A_N(i,j,k)>=A_N(i',j',k') product_(omitted v)a'_v. (13)

The omitted logarithmic cost is at most (5). Apply (10) to the strict retained interval, let N tend to infinity, and then eta to zero. Continuity gives the required lower rate. If xi=0 or q, including sequences whose integer k is not exactly extreme, the all-monomer term gives A_N>=binom(r,k) product a’_v>=product a’_v. Uniform log-sine/cosine Riemann estimates, including the upper half-step, give

N^-1 log product_(v=i+1)^j a'_v
              =B(i/N,j/N)+O(log N/N).             (14)

This also handles q=0. Compactness turns these sequential lower bounds into the uniform statement (11). It is an exponential-rate theorem, not a relative amplitude or derivative asymptotic.

5. Exact endpoint normalization and a finite entropy formula

Let R_M(b) be the original lower-block path numerator starting at M, with every ending b^i retained. Replace each unused spectral factor a_r, r<M, by a_r^=c_r-c_, where c_=-2cos(pi/N), to obtain D_b=R_M^(b). These definitions keep every original sine coefficient. The exact path comparisons give

D_b<=R_M<=sqrt(C_N)D_b,
C_N=4(4-delta)/(delta N^2)<2, delta=2-2cos(pi/N),
[b^i x^k y^(M-i-k)]R_M=(2/a_i)A_N(i,M,k),
B_cross=4(x+y)D_bD_c, B_cross<=P_cross<3B_cross.    (15)

For completeness, the comparison factor is the product over the lower unused rows of a_r/a_r^, bounded by sqrt(C_N). Splitting the paired root path at its compulsory central one-step gives the last exact factorization. The recorded complete-channel identity implies D_b=C_front D_M(x,b,y), where D_M is the original determinant and C_front=product_(r<M)a_r^=N/[2sin(pi/N)]. Thus this normalization differs from that determinant by only a polynomial scalar, not an unknown exponential constant.

It follows uniformly on the ENTIRE triangle simplex that its actual coefficient entropy is

C(alpha_x,alpha_b,alpha_y)=E(alpha_b,1/2,alpha_x),
alpha_x+alpha_b+alpha_y=1/2, alpha_j>=0,
C(alpha)=V(2pi alpha)/(2pi),
V(theta)=2sum_j[Lambda(theta_j/2)+Lambda((pi-theta_j)/2)],
Lambda(t)=-integral_0^t log|2sin w|dw.             (16)

The finite third degree is M-i-k, not N/2-i-k. Its bounded offset disappears only after (11) and uniform continuity. To prove the absolute last equality, for strict positive alpha choose x=tan(pi alpha_x), b=tan(pi alpha_b), y=tan(pi alpha_y). Their pairwise product sum is one. The unique maximizer in (6) with nu=1/2 is alpha_b, and the original triangle derivatives of F are exactly alpha_x,alpha_b,alpha_y. Differentiating this already proved smooth formula shows that log(x/y) is the correct minimizing tilt. Therefore E=F-sum alpha_j log L_j. The absolute source identity 2pi F=sum theta_j log L_j+V(theta) proves (16); continuity includes every boundary face. At a cross port the two triangular allocations and the ONE forced x-or-y unit in (15) must remain explicit before shared coefficient extraction.

There is also a finite formula for the general E. For mu<nu put

q=nu-mu, Dangle=2pi(xi-q/2),
a=cos(pi mu), b0=cos(pi nu),
kappa=sin Dangle/sqrt(a^2+b0^2-2a b0 cos Dangle),
alpha_x(t)=1/4-t/2+asin(kappa cos(pi t))/(2pi),
alpha_b(t)=t, alpha_y(t)=1/2-t-alpha_x(t).          (17)

Use the real principal arcsine. The domain |Dangle|<=pi q implies |kappa|<=1: the difference between the squared denominator and sin^2 Dangle is (cos Dangle-a b0)2-(1-a2)(1-b0^2)>=0. For strict outer degrees kappa=tanh(l/2) for the unique tilt, because direct integration gives alpha_x(t)=partial_l Phi(t,1/2,l) and Dangle=asin(kappa a)-asin(kappa b0). The right side is strictly increasing with the required range; this fixes the original branch after squaring. At xi=0,q take kappa=-1,+1. Subtracting the two correct endpoint Legendre identities gives

E(mu,nu,xi)=C(alpha(mu))-C(alpha(nu)).             (18)

Set E=0 when mu=nu. This is a finite Lobachevsky evaluation on the whole closed domain. Its corresponding left degree vector is alpha(mu) on (x,b,y), minus alpha(nu) on (x,c,y), plus e_c. The output triangle can be written with generalized angles (-theta_x,2pi-theta_c,-theta_y); the Lobachevsky reflection identities give its negative V. This is a signed-angle identity, not a developing map, an independent geometric volume bound, or a cycle-selection rule.

6. The fixed-outer-degree endpoint convexity claim is false

At l=0 one has g_l=1/2 and g_ll=sin(pi t)/4. At the balanced xi=(nu-mu)/2, the tilt is therefore zero. Hold mu and xi FIXED while varying nu. The exact Legendre Schur complement is

E_(nu,nu)=pi cos(pi nu)/[sin(pi nu)(1-sin(pi nu))]
                -pi/[cos(pi mu)-cos(pi nu)].       (19)

Indeed Phi_(nu,l)=1/2 and Phi_(l,l)=[cos(pi mu)-cos(pi nu)]/(4pi). At nu=1/4 the first term is pi/(1-1/sqrt2); for every 0<mu<1/4 the second term is larger. In particular mu=1/8,nu=1/4,xi=1/16 is a strict regular negative-curvature point.

Choose a sufficiently small fixed rational epsilon>0. For all large even N use rounded i=N/8,k=N/16,j0=N/4 and jplus/minus=N(1/4+/-epsilon). The uniform coefficient theorem and (19) give, for the ACTUAL coefficients mathcal A_N(j)=(4/a_i)A_N(i,j,k),

log mathcal A_N(j0)
  -[log mathcal A_N(jminus)+log mathcal A_N(jplus)]/2
                                   >=c_epsilon N>0. (20)

Thus coefficient log-convexity in this exact direction fails exponentially. The common source factor 4/a_i is retained. This does not disprove a shared forest bound: an exchange that changes other face degrees could behave differently. Holding the proportion xi/(nu-mu) fixed is a DIFFERENT direction and is not addressed by (19). Fixed-positive-radius endpoint convexity in (6) cannot be used after fixing xi without this Schur correction.

7. Validation and scope

The focused audit and saved results retain independent original numerator comparisons, positive paths, and the absolute triangle anchor. Finite closed-boundary rate and coefficient-curvature profiles are diagnostics for the displayed proofs, not substitutes for uniform estimates. The exact source interface is also covered by the frozen numerator and cross-half audits linked above.

The author run passes at 75 digits: 329 left and 329 right original numerator coefficients, 96 direct positive paths, 5 independent triangle anchors, 11 recurrence identities and 11 Jacobi/log-root identities, 52 actual negative roots, 12 closed-entropy integral reconstructions, and 64 extreme coefficient checks. It records 25 weighted-rate and 88 closed-coefficient profiles, and four increasing-level failures of the proposed coefficient log-convexity. The largest anchor or entropy reconstruction error is below 7e-76. These finite checks do not prove the uniform limits; those are proved above.

No shared entropy maximum is evaluated here; in particular no local max/min exchange, general forest-volume inequality, or signed long-filled knot asymptotic is claimed.
