Partial cone charges on positive paths

Research note, 3 November 2026.

For a finite original path with every crossing power at least eight, the stationary equations have a unique solution in one fixed complex box for every vector of cone charges between zero and one. The proof is a contraction of the exact return map. It does not require a variational existence theorem at each intermediate charge.

The imaginary part of each angle is bounded by its own charge times an explicit constant of order dv/nv2d_v/n_v^2. Integrating these bounds gives a change-of-action estimate supported only on the vertices whose charges change. The constants do not depend on path length or on the charges at other vertices.

These are statements about the original analytic phase and its marked period equations. No finite quantum invariant at a nonintegral cone charge is assumed, and no coefficient of the original quantum integration cycle is inferred.

4. Signed powers and filling every auxiliary vertex

The contraction does not use a complete outside endpoint, nor the positivity of the powers. These restrictions in the initial statement can be removed without changing its constants.

5. What the charge estimate does not distinguish

The edge phase is even separately in each angle on this complete chart. Flipping λv\lambda_v and tvt_v simultaneously therefore preserves (3), leaves every other angle unchanged, and preserves the stationary action. Uniqueness in Theorem 1 proves that these are the corresponding selected solutions. Consequently S(λ)S(\lambda) is even in each charge separately.

Thus (23) compares charge magnitudes and zeroed charges; it does not supply a strict gap between the different choices of signs of unit charges. Those contributions still require the original finite trace, with its orientations and coefficients.

Likewise, the bounds above do not estimate a high Newton-index tail or a mixed boundary current of the finite quantum source. They supply the marked phase and an explicit action budget for such a comparison. An upper bound for another contour piece must still be proved from its own exact finite-source expression.
