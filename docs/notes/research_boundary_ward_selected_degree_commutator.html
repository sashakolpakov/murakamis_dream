The actual degree-selected boundary Ward packet has a nonzero entry

Written research proof, 23 November 2026.

For the literal reduced boundary crossing, the entire resonant Ward sum is one donor-diagonal commutator. Its component with donor loss one has no triangular-transfer cancellation. An explicit physical input and output give a lower bound after both the required total degree selection and the physical weight restriction. This holds at every level, including even and composite levels, whenever the indicated physical weight exists.

The result is a lower bound for a full first-normal matrix packet. Its exhibited component can lie on an occupation boundary; it is not identified with a growing geometric mode. The original marked closing, arbitrary exterior and its possible scalar cancellation remain. No knot-volume or general short-component lower bound is claimed.

No physical order, endpoint or meridian-normal direction was removed to get this lower bound.

This estimates the actual degree-selected boundary normal packet, not merely the matrix before its required coefficient observation. The witnessing input is near occupation endpoints and has not been identified with a geometric growing packet. The remaining source functional in [W, (27)], its off-diagonal normal couplings, both reciprocal arrows and any arbitrary short exterior stay in place. They can annihilate or cancel matrix components even though (25) holds. Exact negative-letter and word cocycles retain their actual inverse and exterior factors. No general marked-scalar or geometric volume lower bound follows from (25) alone.

More specifically, the Ward arrow has total integer degree loss t>0t>0. For any degree-preserving exterior BextB_{\mathrm{ ext}}, its direct ordinary trace Tr⁡(Bext𝔇tPλ)\operatorname{Tr}(B_{\mathrm{ ext}}\mathfrak D_tP_\lambda) is zero. The original source is not that trace: [W, (24),(27)] first reconstructs the normal blocks from both reciprocal arrows and retains their diagonal and off-diagonal bilinear contractions. The donor projection is not an invariant-preserving replacement of that procedure. For a presentation with one boundary crossing, the complete Markov contraction [W, (29)] still leaves the arbitrary internal core and its unknown scalar; it is not a new nonvanishing argument.
