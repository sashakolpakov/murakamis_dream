Canonical polarized endpoints and the exact heat-readout quotient

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The canonical finite charge polarization and the initialized frozen-contour polarization need not have the same leading term away from unit charge. They nevertheless represent exactly the same original scalar after any signed heat evolution and final unit-charge readout. Their difference belongs to the precise kernel of all such readouts: the sum of charge parity sectors odd in at least one coordinate.

For the original same-index kernel network with complete outside data, the canonical polarization has an explicit complete-action endpoint term in its contractive charge half-planes. This statement includes all physical normals and both parities, and does not require a charged high-sector estimate. The quotient identity explains how that term can coexist with a geometrically initialized contour representative.

5. Exact removal and the remaining estimate

Every function in (20) is odd in yy. This is consistent with, but is not a substitute for, the exact parity theorem (9). The two signs’ asymptotics are proved in opposite half-planes; they must not be added outside their domains to infer a cosine asymptotic. The original cosine cancellation is instead an exact identity (6), with all normal data retained.

One may remove the difference 𝒟𝝈=ℱ𝝈−𝒢𝝈=∑⌀≠A⊆IPA𝒟𝝈(27) \mathcal D_{\boldsymbol\sigma} =\mathcal F_{\boldsymbol\sigma}-\mathcal G_{\boldsymbol\sigma} =\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq I}P_A\mathcal D_{\boldsymbol\sigma} \qquad\text{(27)} before estimating a final heat readout. Equations (9)–(12) prove that this removal has exactly zero effect on the prescribed scalar, at every level and every signed real power tuple. There is no unestimated residual boundary term in that identity. In particular the already initialized frozen-contour family can be used directly as a representative of the canonical family’s heat-readout class.

Subtracting only the first asymptotic term in (21) would not prove this: it is a local half-plane expression and leaves errors with the same complete exponential rate. Likewise subtracting a finite Taylor jet of the angular spectator generally lowers only the polynomial endpoint order. The exact parity decomposition is the relevant operation, and it retains the original scalar by (12).

The construction does not provide a new action-relative bound along an arbitrary heat path. The initialized contour representative still has its actual fixed growth envelope, shrinking certified domains, and possible large complementary contributions. A proposed geometric initializer for the canonical finite-QQ family cannot ignore (21) whenever its asserted action is strictly below eM∞eM_\infty. Passing to the exact quotient permits the contour representative; it does not by itself extend its proved coverage to arbitrary short fillings.
