The preferred knot-cusp shape from a Conway construction

1 November 2026.

A cusp of a hyperbolic knot complement has a Euclidean torus cross-section. Its meridian goes once around the knot. Its preferred longitude follows the knot once and has linking number zero with it. Normalize the meridian’s Euclidean translation to one; the longitude then translates by a complex number τmain\tau_{\mathrm{main}}, the preferred cusp shape. This note computes that number from the actual face maps of a Conway construction.

The constructions considered here begin with two-string tangles in a ball, with four ordered endpoints. They are joined by vertical stacking and quarter-turns in the projection plane, and then closed by the prescribed cylindrical matching. Each twist region has its original crossing circle: a small auxiliary circle bounding a disk punctured by the two main strands. The strict Conway construction, Sections 1–3, specifies the endpoint order, closing convention, and angle conditions. In its positive angle domain the leaf angles satisfy 0<x<1/20<x<1/2, and every retained product cell satisfies max⁡(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \max(x,y)<z<x+y<1. Write TT for the actual quarter-turn and CC for this cylindrical closure. The symbol RR, when used as a tangle rather than a quadrilateral parameter, denotes the original positive checked crossing. An elementary piece hϵh_\epsilon contains its retained crossing circle and the half twist ϵ\epsilon, after the quarter-turn; pϵp_\epsilon denotes the same piece before that turn. The forced flat final product is removed with its exact complementary-label relation. These hypotheses supply the actual complete augmented geometry, not just a combinatorial diagram.

The original ribbon cut identifies the two geometric balls and their face pairings with the augmented link exterior. The marked deformation continues those maps, keeps the main meridian parabolic, and fixes the primitive auxiliary filling curves. We use those geometric inputs throughout. The proof below first adds the translations of a specified longitudinal path, then determines its framing by homology. In particular, it does not identify a preferred longitude solely from a parabolic trace or from a quantum asymptotic.

The geometric result applies to the full strict class with one main knot component. Section 9 gives the quantum consequence for these strict presentations when the fixed signed twists are independently sufficiently long. Those analytic hypotheses are stated there; the geometric calculation is proved directly from the marked representation.

1. Statement and conventions

Fix a strict ordered Conway construction whose main strands form one component, with no additional internal main circle. Retain all original crossing circles and choose each half-twist sign ϵi∈{0,±1}\epsilon_i\in\{0,\pm1\} in its original leaf frame. The existing marked construction gives a holomorphic representation near its complete augmented holonomy, keeps the main meridian parabolic, and retains the original primitive auxiliary slopes. Let Ui,ViU_i,V_i be the continued squared-eigenvalue logarithms of the primitive auxiliary meridian and disk longitude, respectively. Fill the auxiliary circles with the original equations Ui+piVi=−2πi,ni=ϵi−2pi. U_i+p_iV_i=-2\pi i,\qquad n_i=\epsilon_i-2p_i. \tag{1} The main knot cusp is not filled.

Group consecutive products with no intervening quarter-turn into a product junction. The resulting calculation tree has a quarter-turn on each edge between junctions; an original crossing-circle operator is retained at its own vertex. Let ℰT\mathcal E_T be these actual quarter-turn edges, including those incident to an original circle vertex. If the ordered quadrilateral at the input of this turn has normalized angle label beb_e, define xe=1−bex_e=1-b_e. This is also the label shared by the two ends of the edge in the paired polynomial calculation, where it is called the covariant Newton label. A pair of successive turns may be retained or removed; its two cotangent terms cancel.

Let wϵw_\epsilon be the oriented writhe of the original main diagram with its retained half twists, using the same positive braid convention as the actual RR. Let ℓi\ell_i be the linking number of the main component with crossing circle ii, in its primitive disk marking.

The preferred main-cusp shape on the filled branch is τmain=2i∑e∈ℰTcot⁡(πxefill)−∑iϵi−wϵ+∑ipiℓi2. \boxed{\quad \tau_{\mathrm{main}} =2i\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_T}\cot(\pi x_e^{\mathrm{fill}}) -\sum_i\epsilon_i-w_\epsilon+\sum_i p_i\ell_i^2. \quad} \tag{2} The meridian translates by one, and the orientation is chosen so the cusp shape at the complete point has positive imaginary part. A crossing circle has ℓi=0\ell_i=0 when its two original strands are antiparallel, and ℓi=±2\ell_i=\pm2 when they are parallel. Full twists do not change this orientation relation. Consequently, if w𝒏w_{\mathbf n} is the oriented writhe after the indicated fillings, the integer in (2) is A=−∑ini−w𝒏=−2∑parallel ini. A=-\sum_i n_i-w_{\mathbf n} =-2\sum_{\text{parallel }i}n_i. \tag{3} The first expression is convention-safe: both its integers are taken in the actual original braid convention. In particular the statement does not identify an unmarked cusp lattice with a preferred longitude merely by choosing a positive imaginary part.

We prove (2) by first constructing a definite framed longitude in the augmented exterior, computing its translation by local composition, and then determining its homology. The proof is valid for the entire nearby geometric representation, before the auxiliary filling equations are imposed.

8. Scope and analytic continuation

The proof uses only the original ordered face maps, actual cusp paths, and homology of the specified disk fillings. It holds for the full small auxiliary deformation furnished by the marked geometric theorem, including arbitrary strict product/turn syntax and arbitrary retained half-twist patterns whose main strands form a knot. 10. Exact finite reproduction

The symbolic reproduction script checks 75 local identities. It constructs the meridian frames and the three-point maps of the prism directly, checks every product, turn and root translation, verifies the four odd path words, and checks the finite endpoint-orientation and filling-sign formulas. The matrix identities are exact rational identities in independent variables, not numerical samples of knots. They support the local calculations in the proof.
