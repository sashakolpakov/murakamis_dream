Actual cleared cross cofactors: interior starting-row packet

The whole-source local packet now assembles these results for the actual full numerator. The original global spectral filling period remains open.

31 October 2026. . This concerns the original finite homogeneous cofactor, not an arbitrary interpolation of its complete-channel samples. The middle face b is positive. The separate protected-middle proof treats b=0. Exactly one protected outer face x=0 or y=0 is included below; both outer faces zero are excluded.

1. The finite determinant and the exact physical-node adapter

Use the original level N, theta=pi/N, h=1/N, and put

alpha_j(u)=4cos^2((j+1/2)theta)+u, s_j=2sin(j theta), A=x+y, P=xy, p_j=s_j^2 A, q_j=s_j s_(j-1)alpha_(j-1)(u)P.

Let A[0,j](u)A_{[0,j]}(u) be the original upper two-diagonal matrix with diagonal alpha_r, entries -p_r at (r-1,r), and -s_r s_(r-1)P at (r-2,r). The absent physical entries are zero. The actual cleared cofactor is

Rj(b,u)=det⁡A[0,j](u)(1,b,…,bj)A[0,j](u)−1ej. R_j(b,u)=\det A_{[0,j]}(u)\,(1,b,\ldots,b^j)\, A_{[0,j]}(u)^{-1}e_j. \tag{1}

Equivalently, replace the LAST ROW of A_[0..j] by (1,b,…,b^j) and take its determinant. Row replacement proves (1) off the poles; the determinant proves its polynomial extension across every alpha_r=0. Thus it is not necessary to assume an invertible physical block.

The exact path formula is

R_j= sum_(i=0)^j b^i [prod_(r<i)alpha_r] K_(i,j), (2) K_(i,i)=1, K_(i,i-1)=0, K_(i,v)=p_v K_(i,v-1)+q_v K_(i,v-2).

Consequently R_0=1 and

R_j=b^j prod_(r<j)alpha_r+p_j R_(j-1)+q_j R_(j-2). (3)

There is a precise link to the previous positive q-Hahn determinant, but only at its actual spectral node. Write a_r=alpha_r(0), and fix 0<=a<=floor((N-1)/2). At u=-a_a, alpha_a=0, so for j>=a,

R_j(b,-a_a)=K_(a,j)(-a_a) R_a(b,-a_a), R_a(b,-a_a)=S_(2a) D_a(x,b,y). (4)

For the second identity use the original full-numerator value at that node: the all-CROSS formula equals N^2 times the literal complete-circle polynomial, including its beta and forced factors. The first identity makes its b and c dependence R_a(b)R_a(c). Comparing the monic ba,ca coefficients with the original D_a(b)D_a(c) gives (4), since

prod_(r<a)(a_r-a_a) =prod_(r<a)4sin((a+r+1)theta)sin((a-r)theta)=S_(2a).

The scalar outer factor is a nonzero polynomial (already nonzero at x>0,y=0), so cancellation takes place in the polynomial domain. The odd simple node is included. The case a=0 is the empty product1. Equation (4) does NOT identify generic-u R_j with a moving-dimensional q-Hahn determinant. Formula (1) is its actual off-node continuation.

2. Statement of the relative complex packet

Fix a compact positive real set with b>0, A>0 and x,y>=0. A zero outer face stays exactly zero; other faces vary in a small fixed complex logarithmic neighborhood. Let the terminal row n=n_N satisfy

n/N=1/2+O(1/N), n<=floor(N/2),

as for R_(m-2), R_(m-1) and the odd R_m. The constants in the O term are bounded. For all sufficiently large N there are holomorphic phases phi_N(t;u,x,b,y) and holomorphic units a_N(t;u,x,y), on a fixed complex neighborhood of the interior real starting saddle, such that

R_n(b,u)=sqrt(2pi N) a_N(t_N) exp[N phi_N(t_N)]/sqrt(-partial_t^2 phi_N(t_N)) *[1+O(N^-1)]. (5)

Here t_N is the unique nearby zero of partial_t phi_N, and the square root is continued from its positive real value at u=0. All finite prefix products and the actual terminal n remain in phi_N; no source scalar is discarded. The estimate is uniform on a sufficiently small FIXED complex u disk and the indicated face neighborhood, with fixed parameter derivatives on smaller neighborhoods. In particular R_n is a unit there for all sufficiently large N.

The phases converge with fixed derivatives to

phi(t;u)=t Log b+integral_0^t Log alpha(v,u)dv +integral_t^(1/2) Log lambda(v,u)dv, alpha(v,u)=4cos^2(pi v)+u, lambda(v,u)=2sin(pi v)[sin(pi v)A +sqrt(sin^2(pi v)(x-y)^2+(4+u)P)]. (6)

The square root and logarithms are continued from positive real values where their respective integrals occur. Only alpha on [0,t] is logged; it is uniformly nonzero there. Zeros of alpha near the UPPER terminal do not enter that logarithm or cause a zero of lambda. The limiting saddle is the analytic continuation of

sin^2(pi t_b(u))=(1+u/4)b^2/[(b+x)(b+y)], t_b(0) in (0,1/2). (7)

For x=0 this is (1+u/4)b/(b+y), and similarly for y=0. At u=0 the critical value in (6) is the original absolute triangle potential F(x,b,y). The finite terminal shift is retained in phi_N, not replaced by1/2 in the prefactor or finite action.

3. Uniform analytic dominant tail, including zero upper diagonals

Choose fixed 0<ell<t_b(0)<r<1/2 uniformly over the real compact, with the saddle separated from ell,r. On a larger real interval containing [ell,1/2], the transfer recurrence for K has

p(t)=4sin^2(pi t)A, q(t,h)=4sin(pi t)sin(pi(t-h)) [4cos^2(pi(t-h/2))+u]P.

At h=u=0 the two characteristic roots are lambda and lambda_-, with lambda a unit and |lambda_-/lambda|<=kappa<1. This remains true in a fixed small complex t/face/u neighborhood and for small h. It includes the terminal alpha=0 cases: only the smaller root may vanish. If P=0, q=0 and the recurrence is first order, with the same unit dominant root.

An exponentially accurate analytic ratio can be constructed directly. Start from the analytic positive characteristic root of z^2-pz-q=0, and iterate

r_(l+1)(t)=p(t)+q(t,h)/r_l(t-h). (8)

Use nested rectangles whose left real margin advances by h at each step; keep one extra shifted rectangle for the final residual. On a uniform O(h) neighborhood of the starting root, (8) preserves that neighborhood and has pointwise derivative modulus at most kappa’<1. Translation of the analytic seed costs O(h). After L=floor(cN) steps, where c is smaller than the available left margin, r_N is analytic, a unit, and

r_N(t)-p(t)-q(t,h)/r_N(t-h)=O(exp(-c_1 N)), r_N(t)=lambda(t,u)+O(h). (9)

The contraction estimate is between successive nested rectangles, not an assumed contraction on a fixed domain invariant under translation. Cauchy estimates on the retained common neighborhood give all fixed derivative bounds. The construction never divides by lambda_-.

For integer i/N in [ell,r], divide the actual K_(i,j) by Y_j=prod_(v=i+1)^j r_N(vh), with Y_i=1. In the exact zero-residual recurrence, consecutive differences of K/Y multiply by

-k_N(jh), k_N(t)=q(t,h)/[r_N(t)r_N(t-h)], |k_N|<=kappa’.

The initial values are K_(i,i)=1,K_(i,i-1)=0. Hence the dominant initial projection is the analytic truncated sum

a_N(t)=sum_(l=0)^(L’) (-1)^l prod_(v=1)^l k_N(t+vh), (10)

with L’=floor(c_2 N) smaller than every remaining terminal distance. The omitted tail is exponential. Its leading value is 1/(1+k_0(t)), a positive unit, and a_N differs from it by O(h). The residual (9) adds O(N exp(-c_1 N)) to the normalized solution: the difference recurrence and a geometric-series bound control the solution uniformly, then a discrete Gronwall factor is1+o(1). Thus uniformly in all these actual integer starts and complex parameters,

K_(i,n)=a_N(ih) prod_(v=i+1)^n r_N(vh) [1+O(exp(-c_3 N))]. (11)

4. Exponentially accurate analytic starting-row interpolation

On [0,r] and its fixed complex neighborhood, alpha(t+h/2,u) is an analytic unit. The midpoint prefix product prod_(v<i)alpha_v can therefore be interpolated by an optimally truncated Euler–Maclaurin primitive, anchored at0. The tail product in (11) has the same construction applied to Log r_N, anchored at the ACTUAL t=n/N. Uniform holomorphy gives C^k k! derivative bounds. Choosing the number of Bernoulli terms proportional to N gives an O(exp(-c_4 N)) remainder on smaller fixed neighborhoods, as in the proved analytic discrete primitive lemma for the original fold factors.

Explicitly, on a retained analytic strip of width d_0, the order-2K Euler–Maclaurin remainder for a log SUM is bounded by

C h^(2K-1) (2K)!/(2pi d_0)^(2K) <= C h^(-1) [C_0 K h]^(2K).

The same estimate holds for the midpoint rule, whose periodic Bernoulli functions have the same factorial bound. It follows from the integral remainder and |f^(2K)|<=C (2K)! d_0^(-2K); the strip and C are uniform for both Log alpha and the constructed Log r_N. Choose K=floor(c_0/h) with C_0 c_0<1. The polynomial h^(-1) is absorbed by shrinking the positive exponential constant. Thus the LOG-product error, and hence its relative product error, is O(exp(-c_4 N)), uniformly in the actual integer endpoints. The finite truncated primitive itself is holomorphic on the smaller strip used below.

Keep these finite primitives, rather than only their leading integrals. With the exact b^i factor and (10), they define

w_N(t)=a_N(t) exp[N phi_N(t)]

such that EACH actual summand of (2), for i/N in [ell,r], equals w_N(i/N)[1+O(exp(-c_5 N))]. The phase converges with fixed derivatives to (6). All primitive anchors, their endpoint half terms and finite terminal shifts stay in phi_N. This proves the normalization claim in (5), without inferring a continuum phase solely from node interpolation.

All starts OUTSIDE [ell,r], including i=0, remain in the argument. At positive faces and u=0 the uniform interval theorem gives their exact weighted rate. The limiting phase is strictly maximized at t_b(0), since its derivative is Log[b/L(t;x,y)] and L is strictly increasing from0 to infinity. Choose ell,r so those tails have a fixed gap c_6>0, uniformly over the real compact.

For small complex u, apply the original unused-row product bound to each term in (2). The product of all nonzero lower-row ratios is at most exp[N theta(|u|)+O(log N)], theta(v)=arcosh(1+v/2). This also covers odd terminal n=m: the cofactor excludes alpha_m=0. The original missing steps and all other starting rows are retained. A small logarithmic face perturbation costs exp(C rho N), since each term has the same finite homogeneous degree n. Taking the fixed u and face neighborhoods small enough makes their total cost less than c_6 N/4. The entire omitted starting-row sum is therefore exponentially below the continued saddle, not merely small at a real parameter.

5. The actual discrete saddle and its contour

At u=0, partial_t^2 phi(t)<0 uniformly on [ell,r]. On a sufficiently small fixed complex parameter neighborhood, the IFT gives a holomorphic t_N with partial_t phi_N(t_N)=0, t_N=t_b(u)+O(1/N), and Re[-partial_t^2 phi_N] bounded below by a positive constant near the continued horizontal saddle contour.

The finite-grid replacement is justified, including its vertical edges. Let ell_N,r_N^end be the half-grid boundaries around the integer starts in [ell,r]. For a fixed small height eta, the exact residue formula is

sum_i w_N(i/N) =N/(2i) integral_(rectangle) cot(pi N z) w_N(z) dz. (12)

On the upper/lower sides cot(pi N z)=-/+ i+O(exp(-2pi eta N)). On the vertical half-grid sides its modulus is at most1. The real endpoint gap persists along those sides for small eta and small complex parameters. On the horizontal sides the phase costs at most C eta^2 at the real reference, plus C rho from the parameter perturbation. Choose eta first so 2pi eta>C eta^2, then rho small enough for the cotangent error and vertical currents to have a uniform exponential gap relative to exp[N phi_N(t_N)]. Cauchy’s theorem now compares (12) with N times the ordinary integral along [ell_N,r_N^end]; the retained vertical connector terms have the same gap. No endpoint current has been declared identically zero.

The exponentially small interpolation error from section4 also stays exponentially small AFTER summation: its possible absolute-sum loss is O(rho N), and the parameter neighborhood was chosen smaller than its fixed exponential accuracy. Deform the ordinary interval to the horizontal segment through t_N, retaining both endpoint connectors. Uniform strict concavity along that segment gives the Gaussian formula (5), with relative O(N^-1) and the continued positive square root. Analyticity and Cauchy margins give the claimed fixed parameter derivatives of that relative error.

6. What this packet does and does not supply

The proof treats b>0 and every actual starting row; i=0 is exponentially subdominant in this chart. It includes x=0 or y=0, where q=0, (8) is exact, and a_N=1. It does NOT discard i=0 when b itself is the protected zero: that different chart is the separate protected-middle theorem.

Under u=-4sin^2(pi delta), the packet is holomorphic in delta on a fixed small cover neighborhood. In particular it covers selected fixed delta=O(1/n_twist) sectors once the signed twists are sufficiently long for this fixed diagram. This is a finite cofactor asymptotic, not a choice of a contributing charge or cycle in the full Gaussian period. The exact central cross formulas must still be assembled with their literal alpha prefactors, both parities and original shared coefficient extraction. Their leading central factors are nonzero near u=0, but the whole phase-sensitive period requires its own selection argument.

Once the separate b=0 packet and the exact central factors are included, there is a direct calibration of the local LEADING action. At every physical fold node, the all-cross polynomial is exactly N^2 B_(N,a). These nodes become dense in a fixed small real delta interval. Uniform convergence of the present normalized logarithm and the already proved complete fold interpolation therefore gives

psi_b(u(delta))+psi_c(u(delta)) =F(x,b,y)+F(x,c,y)-V(delta;x,b,c,y),

where psi_b=phi(t_b(u);u). The real node values are positive, fixing the logarithmic branch and absolute constant. Both sides are holomorphic, so the identity extends to the same small complex cover neighborhood, with shared-face derivatives. This identifies a leading local action; it does not identify the finite cofactor with the old interpolant or prove a global face-torus gap or selected Gaussian contribution.

Focused finite validation

Private helper ../experiments/audit_actual_cross_cofactor_interior_packet.py compares the row-replaced determinant directly with the cleared recurrence and compares its physical-node values with the independently built original positive q-Hahn matrices. At 85 digits, N=3,…,18, real/complex faces and each one-protected-outer specialization, it passes1056 row determinants, 352 matrix node adapters,352 exact sine scalars and960 tail factorizations; maximum relative error is below2e-85. These checks validate the finite adapter. The relative complex asymptotic is the analytical proof above, not a conclusion inferred from finite node or rate samples.
