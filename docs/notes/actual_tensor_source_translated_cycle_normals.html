The adjoint finite-jet connection on the original source cycle

30 October 2026. The exact tensor-source transport has a corresponding identity for the original cotangent period. Its adjoint acts on the cotangent density as well as on the explicit source gauge. The resulting double-pole and finite-part terms are retained. This does not make the finite head sum periodic or move its cycle to the geometric critical point.

1. The actual period and the specified source connection

Use the actual tensor-source connection. After the original integral affine head equations, write the primitive free lattice as h=h0+A x, x in C^k. Constant head factors remain their original source constants. Fix the finite channel labels, or retain the finite vector of label sectors when their characters differ. Every outside tensor, turn and peripheral character is part of the specified meromorphic source S(epsilon,x). At the original marked arc use its ordinary generic pivotal closure and subsequent exposed-trace normal, or an explicitly chosen covariant generic marked row. No generic continuation of the bare root mark is supplied by this theorem.

Initially assume S is root-regular in epsilon on a common neighborhood of the cycle, after retaining its actual meromorphic cancellations. If a chosen representation has a finite Laurent pole of order M, apply the statements to epsilon^M S through order J+M to recover the original Laurent jet through order J. One must not use only J translation terms before multiplying a source with such a pole.

Put P=pi i/Log(q0 exp(epsilon)), Delta=N-P, and P’=iN^2/pi. For b in Z^k, let D_b=sum b_j partial_(x_j). The source theorem proves

S(epsilon,x+Nb)=chi(b) G(x) exp(Delta D_b)[G(x)^-1 S], (1)

where chi(b) is the actual parity character and G is the explicit P-independent rational cup/character gauge. Its construction omits constant head factors; in particular it never inserts g(integer) for an identically constant cup argument. Formula (1) is meromorphic and its exponential is a FINITE differential operator modulo any fixed epsilon^(J+1).

The original lattice density and normalized period are

W(x)=product_(j=1)^k pi cot(pi x_j),
I_Gamma[S]=(2pi i)^(-k) integral_Gamma W(x) S(x) dx,  (2)

with dx=dx1 wedge … wedge dxk. Gamma is the original sum of compact local tori, or a specified closed cycle on the same source chart. It avoids the divisors of S, W, G and G^-1 on its surface on a common epsilon germ, or the genuinely canceled meromorphic integrands are used throughout. Its interior may contain the original cotangent and source poles. The source-null extension theorem allows its explicitly proved zero ordered tori to be included as well. Other polar strata are not implicitly added or removed.

2. Exact adjoint transport of every finite quantum jet

For every fixed J, on the original CLOSED cycle one has

I_Gamma[S(epsilon,x+Nb)]
  =chi(b)(2pi i)^(-k) integral_Gamma
      G^-1 exp(-Delta D_b)(W G) S dx
                                        mod epsilon^(J+1). (3)

All derivatives in (3) act on the actual top-form density W G. This is not a replacement of the original period by a period of S/G. The final meromorphic integrand is used with every cancellation and divisor retained, rather than evaluated factor by factor at G=0 or G=infinity.

Proof: for meromorphic f,g with no pole on the cycle,

integral_Gamma f D_b g dx=-integral_Gamma (D_b f) g dx.

Indeed D_b(fg) dx=d(fg contraction_b(dx)), whose integral on a closed cycle is zero. This argument needs no pole-free interior. Apply it r times to the r-th term of (1), with f=W G and g=G^-1 S. Since Delta is independent of x and belongs to (epsilon), only r<=J occurs for the root-regular source specified in section 1. For a Laurent source use the cleared source and the enlarged order stated there. This proves (3) without an infinite differential expansion or an analytic translation of Gamma by the small complex number Delta.

For the literal translated cycle Gamma+Nb, a change of variables also gives

I_(Gamma+Nb)[S]=I_Gamma[S(epsilon,x+Nb)],              (4)

because the original W is N-periodic. This is an identity of the specified continued source at that translated cycle. It does not say the shifted head values are admissible original states or vanish outside the physical basis. In particular the meromorphic continuation need not vanish at out-of-basis shifted indices.

If a geometric deformation from one cycle to the other crosses source divisors, their tubes and residues remain in that deformation. Equations (3),(4) compute the translated period; they do not identify the two cycles in the complement of those divisors.

3. The first normal and the original exposed trace normalization

Write S=S0+epsilon S1+O(epsilon^2). Formula (3) yields

I_(Gamma+Nb)[S0]=chi(b) I_Gamma[S0],
I_(Gamma+Nb)[S1]-chi(b) I_Gamma[S1]
  =chi(b) P' (2pi i)^(-k) integral_Gamma
                     G^-1 D_b(W G) S0 dx.            (5)

The sign is positive: Delta’=-P’ and the adjoint exponential is exp(-Delta D_b). The correction includes derivatives of BOTH W and G. Writing it as W D_b Log(WG) is permissible only as a meromorphic identity; the un-divided density in (5) specifies its actual periods.

For the original ordinary exposed-head closure, retain

nu_N=2N(-1)^N/(q0-q0^-1),
P'/nu_N=(-1)^(N+1) N sin(pi/N)/pi.                    (6)

When the original root closure I_Gamma[S0] is zero, its translated root closure is also zero by (5), so division of the first-normal identity by nu_N gives the exact corresponding marked-period difference. Its correction density has the nonzero order-one scalar in (6), rather than an arbitrarily assigned boundary coefficient. It is not yet a nonzero global geometric contribution.

For a primitive direction with several nonzero b_j, D_b differentiates the corresponding original cotangent factors. A chosen degree h need not be a primitive free variable. For h=A0 x_j+B0 at fixed other coordinates, the weight is (pi/A0)cot(pi(h-B0)/A0) dh. The finite-part rules of the boundary theorem apply with that weight. Neither (3) nor (5) replaces it by pi cot(pi h) dh.

4. A literal cup exhibits a nonzero double-pole correction

For one varying cup argument, its source gauge is

g(x)=Y/(1-Y), Y=exp(2pi i x),
pi cot(pi x)=-pi i(1+2g(x)).

Let w=pi cot(pi x). The correction density in (5) is

g^-1 (w g)'=w'+w * 2pi i/(1-Y).                     (7)

At an integer n, with y=x-n, this equals

-2/y^2+pi i/y+O(1).

Consequently its residue against a root source regular there is

pi i S0(n)-2 S0'(n).                               (8)

Both copies of the derivative matter: one comes from the original cotangent and one from the actual cup gauge.

For the ordered fixed-L cup with L=d=N-1, the exact fixed-root reflection gives p_d(0,x)=exp(pi i d x/N). On a physical n in [0,d], the difference of its translated and parity-weighted original first-normal cotangent periods is therefore exactly

(-1)^d N(N-2) exp(pi i d n/N).                       (9)

This is the ordered channel-cup normalization of the source theorem. For the physical cup, multiply by its original root scalar N q0(-d2); its generic scalar derivative cancels from the difference because the corresponding root difference is zero. The translated cup can have a generic pole at n+N. Its cotangent period is the actual Laurent finite part, not its value at that pole. Thus (9) is a concrete counterexample to silently declaring these continued periods periodic.

5. Scope of the cycle identity

The source connection, the original primitive lattice and the original weight together determine every finite-order correction in (3). The isolated wall residues and source-null ordered periods are already given by the boundary theorem. Their combination supplies exact finite-head transport with its boundary terms retained.

What remains separate is a deformation of the FULL original period through all its source divisors to a contributing geometric cycle, including its noncompact ends and the coherent outside contraction. No equality of the positive envelope with volume, no periodic extension of the physical basis, and no nonzero saddle coefficient follows from this adjoint identity.

Focused verification: adjoint and original cup audit and saved results. The audit checks 60 exact meromorphic adjoint residues through derivative order three, the actual cotangent double-pole coefficient, and 12 genuine translated first-normal cup finite parts obtained independently from the original moving-Gamma Laurent coefficients. The latter agree with (9) to relative error below 5.3e-38 at 100 digits. These are checks of the retained local periods, not evidence for a global saddle coefficient.

The result retains all source and gauge divisors and makes no contributing-cycle assertion.

No wrapped physical state or geometric contribution is inferred from the translated normal identities.
