The integral-color path: actual sources, pivots and dense resonances

Independent proof audit, 27 October 2026. The path qλ=eπiλ/Nq_\lambda=e^{\pi i\lambda/N}, M=−qλNM=-q_\lambda^N does give an honest NN-dimensional integral-color quotient and its actual crossings. It is not automatically the same off-root source as the auxiliary finite ADO lift with its root pivot frozen. This distinction is explicit below.

The ordinary-color bounds impose a second exact constraint: the actual color path has polynomial values on a sampling grid of spacing O(1/N)O(1/N), and its nearest higher-root value is literally one. A uniform positive dominant WKB solution on the real interval cannot describe this selected function.

1. Exact quotient and basis comparisons

On the common balanced Verma module, δ=1−QN,w=MQ−N−M−1,Q=q2. \delta=1-Q^N,\qquad w=M Q^{-N}-M^{-1},\qquad Q=q^2. The choice M=−qNM=-q^N has w=0w=0. The tail is invariant and the finite head is the genuine integral quotient, with Kej=qN−1−2jej,ℰej=q−N(1−QN−j)ej−1,Fej=(1−Qj+1)ej+1. K e_j=q^{N-1-2j}e_j,\quad \mathcal E e_j=q^{-N}(1-Q^{N-j})e_{j-1},\quad F e_j=(1-Q^{j+1})e_{j+1}. \tag{1} The original finite crossing is its literal quotient crossing. The word excursion adapter is proportional to δw\delta w, so it vanishes on this whole path, not merely at its root endpoint.

Let uju_j be the ordinary highest-weight basis with Fuj=uj+1Fu_j=u_{j+1}, Euj=[j]q[N−j]quj−1Eu_j=[j]_q[N-j]_q u_{j-1}, and ℰ=ρ2E\mathcal E=\rho^2E. Then ej=uj/(Q;Q)j e_j=u_j/(Q;Q)_{j} \tag{2} gives exactly (1). For every fixed NN, this gauge is invertible on 0<λ≤10<\lambda\le1, including at qNq_N; all factors have index j<Nj<N. It is singular at q=1q=1. The balanced coupon eij=1/Ne_{ij}=1/N becomes eijordinary=(Q;Q)jN(Q;Q)i. e^{\mathrm{ordinary}}_{ij} =\frac{(Q;Q)_j}{N(Q;Q)_i}. \tag{3} Its normalization remains one, but its separate source and co-source flags need not have a regular q=1q=1 limit.

There is a second useful exact gauge, regular also at q=1q=1. In the polynomial basis it gives ℰsym=v−1(Tq−Tq−1),Fsym=v(qnTq−1−q−nTq),n=N−1. \mathcal E_{\mathrm{sym}}=v^{-1}(T_q-T_q^{-1}),\qquad F_{\mathrm{sym}}=v(q^nT_q^{-1}-q^{-n}T_q),\quad n=N-1. The diagonal conjugation from (1) is Dj=(−1)jqj(j+1)/2−Nj[N−1j]Q. D_j=(-1)^j q^{j(j+1)/2-Nj}{N-1\brack j}_{Q}. \tag{4} Its adjacent ratio is the old ℰ\mathcal E coefficient divided by qj−q−jq^j-q^{-j}, which proves both generator comparisons. At q=1q=1, Dj=(−1)j(nj)D_j=(-1)^j\binom nj; at the actual root, Dj=qN−Nj−j(j+1)/2, D_j=q_N^{-Nj-j(j+1)/2}, of modulus one. Thus (4) is invertible along the closed path for fixed NN, but its condition number can be exponential in NN in the interior or at the classical endpoint. Exact finite equivalence does not imply equality of naive principal symbols in these different frames.

The normalized trace is basis invariant. On every one-cut diagonal array, including the scalar index-weighted arrays, the original balanced coupon equals this trace. This avoids the coordinate singularity of (3) for those evaluations. For off-diagonal Ward insertions the coupon must still be transported; normalized trace is not a replacement on all matrices.

2. The pivotal adapter is essential off the root

The generic integral-color quantum invariant uses the ordinary pivot gint=Kg_{\mathrm{int}}=K. The root ADO pivot is groot=K1−Ng_{\mathrm{root}}=K^{1-N}. Away from QN=1Q^N=1, S2=Ad⁡K≠Ad⁡K1−N. S^2=\operatorname{Ad}K\ne\operatorname{Ad}K^{1-N}. For example their conjugations of ℰ\mathcal E differ by Q−NQ^{-N}. Hence a frozen ADO right cap is not a generic Hopf pivotal cap, even though the quotient module and its crossings are genuine.

For a bb-strand knot braid of writhe 𝔴\mathfrak w, the ordinary integral twist and frozen ADO twist at α=n\alpha=n are θint=qn(n+2)/2,θroot=q−n2/2. \theta_{\mathrm{int}}=q^{n(n+2)/2},\qquad \theta_{\mathrm{root}}=q^{-n^2/2}. Their ratio is q−Nnq^{-Nn}. The exact difference of the two zero-framed state arrays is therefore the weight qNn(𝔴−b+1)(QN)∑closed strandsja. q^{Nn(\mathfrak w-b+1)} (Q^N)^{\sum_{\text{closed strands}}j_a}. \tag{5} It retains an actual number-operator clock, not just a scalar. At qNq_N its clock is one and its scalar is (−1)n(𝔴−b+1)=1(-1)^{n(\mathfrak w-b+1)}=1 by knot parity. Thus the ordinary reduced endpoint is the original KNK_N. At q=1q=1, the ordinary reduced period is also one. Neither endpoint equality removes the nonconstant adapter in between or its quantum-normal derivatives.

There is an explicit small example. For N=2N=2, use the stabilized unknot, the two-strand braid σ1\sigma_1. Its ordinary reduced value is one. With the frozen pivot K−1K^{-1} and frozen twist q−1/2q^{-1/2}, its normalized one-cut value instead is 1+q2−q−22=1+isin⁡(πλ). 1+\frac{q^2-q^{-2}}2 =1+i\sin(\pi\lambda). \tag{6} To check this, use the actual fundamental checked crossing q1/2I−q−1/2Uq^{1/2}I-q^{-1/2}U, with U|01,10=[q−1−1q−1]U|_{01,10}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}q&-1\\-1&q^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]. Closing the second leg with K−1K^{-1}, then applying the frozen twist correction, gives diagonal entries 11 and 1−q−2+q21-q^{-2}+q^2; their average is (6). It agrees at both endpoints but is not the integral-color invariant inside the interval.

This is a comparison of two explicit interpolations, not a claim that a generic ADO invariant was defined incorrectly. The valid actual interpolation is CN(q)C_N(q) with its ordinary caps and twist. At the root its reduced one-cut evaluation is regular even though its ordinary quantum dimension [N]qN[N]_{q_N} vanishes. One must not evaluate an unreduced closure divided by [N][N] as two separately vanishing quantities.

3. Exact sampling and the necessary nonuniformity

Set AN(λ)=CN(eπiλ/N). A_N(\lambda)=C_N(e^{\pi i\lambda/N}). For every integer r>Nr>N, the value at λ=N/r\lambda=N/r is an ordinary color at the principal root qrq_r. The actual unitary trace bound gives |AN(N/r)|≤[N]qrb−1≤(r/2)b−1. |A_N(N/r)|\le [N]_{q_r}^{b-1}\le (r/2)^{b-1}. \tag{7} On any fixed closed interval J⊂(0,1)J\subset(0,1), these sampling points have mesh OJ(1/N)O_J(1/N) and their bound is OJ(Nb−1)O_J(N^{b-1}).

Consequently a positive dominant WKB expansion with a uniformly nonvanishing subexponential amplitude cannot hold on any open real interval below one. Choose a point of positive continuous rate and a small compact interval on which it stays positive. A sampling point in that interval contradicts (7). More generally, if uN(λ)=N−1log⁡+|AN(λ)| u_N(\lambda)=N^{-1}\log^+|A_N(\lambda)| are equicontinuous on such a compact interval, then sup⁡JuN→0\sup_Ju_N\to0: compare every point with its neighboring sampling point. Thus a positive exponential spike requires loss of that equicontinuity.

There is an exact stronger endpoint ladder. At level r=N+kr=N+k, ordinary-color Weyl symmetry gives CN(qN+k)=Ck(qN+k),1≤k<N. C_N(q_{N+k})=C_k(q_{N+k}),\qquad 1\le k<N. \tag{8} For each fixed kk, the right side tends to one and is bounded. In particular AN(NN+1)=1 A_N\!\left(\frac{N}{N+1}\right)=1 \tag{9} for every knot. Its distance from the target is 1/(N+1)1/(N+1). If |KN||K_N| has positive exponential rate, a nonzero smooth dominant WKB amplitude cannot remain uniform even across this endpoint window.

An unconditional quantitative form is supλ∈[N/(N+1),1]|AN′(λ)|≥(N+1)(|KN|−1), \sup_{\lambda\in[N/(N+1),1]}|A_N'(\lambda)| \ge (N+1)(|K_N|-1), and hence supλ∈[N/(N+1),1]|q∂qCN(q)|≥N(N+1)π(|KN|−1). \sup_{\lambda\in[N/(N+1),1]} |q\partial_q C_N(q)| \ge \frac{N(N+1)}\pi(|K_N|-1). \tag{10} This follows by integrating the derivative along the interval, so it does not assume a real-valued period or a mean-value point for a complex function. The already proved figure-eight asymptotic makes (9)–(10) an actual exponential endpoint example. Ordinary-color unitarity controls values, not the normal derivatives needed here.

Pointwise asymptotics at an individual nonsampled irrational parameter are not excluded. Nor do these bounds exclude a geometric branch with a resonance-dependent source coefficient. They do exclude treating that coefficient as uniformly nonzero and smooth merely because the classical optimizer is.

A whole scaled boundary profile cannot be algebraic

The same exact ladder constrains the functions-before-jets program. Use c=2πic=2\pi i, H=log⁡QH=\log Q, and the scaled normal z=N2(H−c/N),𝔣N(z)=CN(exp[c2N+z2N2]). z=N^2(H-c/N),\qquad \mathfrak f_N(z)= C_N\!\left(\exp\!\left[\frac{c}{2N} +\frac{z}{2N^2}\right]\right). The nearby principal level N+kN+k is exactly zN,k=−ckNN+k→−ck,𝔣N(zN,k)=Ck(qN+k)→1. z_{N,k}=-\frac{ckN}{N+k}\longrightarrow-ck,\qquad \mathfrak f_N(z_{N,k})=C_k(q_{N+k})\longrightarrow1. \tag{11} Also 𝔣N(0)=KN\mathfrak f_N(0)=K_N.

Here is the precise conditional conclusion. Choose a subsequence with |KN|→∞|K_N|\to\infty, for example one having a positive exponential rate, and suppose 𝔣N/KN\mathfrak f_N/K_N converges locally uniformly to a holomorphic function gg on a connected domain containing zero and every point −ck-ck, k≥1k\ge1. Then g(0)=1,g(−ck)=0(k≥1). g(0)=1,\qquad g(-ck)=0\quad(k\ge1). \tag{12} Local uniform convergence permits the moving evaluations (11), so this needs no unproved interchange of pointwise limits.

Such a nonzero whole profile is not algebraic over ℂ(z)\mathbb C(z). Indeed, for an irreducible polynomial relation P(z,g)=0P(z,g)=0, the infinitely many zeros in (12) force P(z,0)P(z,0) to vanish identically. Thus ww divides P(z,w)P(z,w), forcing g=0g=0, contrary to g(0)=1g(0)=1.

Neither the required growth nor this normal-family convergence is asserted for a general knot. A germ on a small neighborhood of zero alone is not excluded, and the conclusion does not prohibit algebraic coefficients for an individual local geometric WKB branch. It says that, if a normalized whole selected boundary profile exists through all nearby resonances, an algebraic critical coefficient cannot be that entire profile. A genuinely nonalgebraic period function is required; its resonance zeros and one normalization do not determine it uniquely.

4. Principal flags depend on the valid source frame

The symmetric frame in (4) has principal functions e=2sinh⁡(sT/2)v,f=2vsinh⁡(s(1−T)/2),k=es/2−sT,s=2πiλ. e=\frac{2\sinh(sT/2)}v,\quad f=2v\sinh(s(1-T)/2),\quad k=e^{s/2-sT},\qquad s=2\pi i\lambda. Its two eigenflags are v+=es(1−T)/2v,v−=−e−sT/2sinh⁡(s(1−T)/2)sinh⁡(sT/2)v. v_+=e^{s(1-T)/2}v,\qquad v_-=-e^{-sT/2} \frac{\sinh(s(1-T)/2)}{\sinh(sT/2)}v. The plus flag has degree +1+1 and exactly the original Fubini–Study current on the real sphere at every level parameter. For 0<λ<10<\lambda<1, the inverse flag has degree −1-1, whereas at λ=1\lambda=1 its interior limit equals the plus flag and has degree +1+1.

Writing ε=1−λ\varepsilon=1-\lambda, the two inverse-flag bubbles are v=εy:v−→−1/y‾,V=1/v=εy:v−→y‾. v=\varepsilon y:\quad v_-\to-1/\bar y,\qquad V=1/v=\varepsilon y:\quad v_-\to\bar y . Each has degree −1-1. Accordingly its signed weak current has limit ωFS−δT=0−δT=1. \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}-\delta_{T=0}-\delta_{T=1}. For a direct check, the inverse-flag latitude tends to TT in the interior but has endpoint values 1,01,0; integration by parts against the angularly averaged test function gives exactly these two negative endpoint masses.

This verifies the current signs and shows why the earlier ordinary-falling-frame plus bubble is not intrinsic. The frame gauge can be nonuniform in NN. It does not permit discarding the selected inverse eigenflag, the two genuine endpoint bubbles in that frame, or the pivotal adapter (5). No whole quantum dressing or selected norm follows from the principal flag calculation alone.
