Global real-spectral gap of the actual all-CROSS source

, 31 October 2026. This estimates the original CROSS path sectors on the entire real spectral interval. The local bound is a weighted VALUE bound, not a coefficientwise strict inequality. The original face Cauchy period and its previously proved angular gap are retained when sewing. No moving one-sided sector is included.

1. Original cofactors and a uniform slow-transfer upper bound

Put h=1/Nh=1/N, sj=2sin⁡(πjh)s_j=2\sin(\pi jh),

αr(δ)=4cos⁡2(π(r+12)h)−4sin⁡2(πδ),A=x+y>0,P=xy≥0. \alpha_r(\delta)=4\cos^2\!\left(\pi(r+\tfrac12)h\right)-4\sin^2(\pi\delta), \qquad A=x+y>0,\quad P=xy\geq0.

where every nonzero face weight lies in a fixed positive compact and protected zeros stay EXACTLY zero. The terminal indices nn below satisfy n=N/2+O(1)n=N/2+O(1) and n≤N/2n\leq N/2, with a fixed bound on the O(1)O(1). The literal cofactor has its exact start decomposition

Rn(b,δ)=∑i=0nbi(∏r=0i−1αr(δ))Ki,n,Ki,i=1,Ki,i−1=0,Ki,j=sj2AKi,j−1+sjsj−1αj−1(δ)PKi,j−2. \begin{aligned} R_n(b,\delta)&=\sum_{i=0}^n b^i\left(\prod_{r=0}^{i-1}\alpha_r(\delta)\right)K_{i,n},\\ K_{i,i}&=1,\qquad K_{i,i-1}=0,\\ K_{i,j}&=s_j^2A K_{i,j-1}+s_js_{j-1}\alpha_{j-1}(\delta)P K_{i,j-2}. \end{aligned}\tag{1}

This is the original cleared cofactor, without a changed starting row. For b=0b=0 only i=0i=0 occurs. Negative α\alpha factors are not replaced in the tail recurrence by their absolute values.

Set Li,j=Ki,j/∏r=i+1jsrL_{i,j}=K_{i,j}/\prod_{r=i+1}^j s_r. Its exact transfer matrix is

Th(t,δ)=(2Asin⁡(πt)4P[cos⁡2(π(t−h/2))−sin⁡2(πδ)]10),t=jh. T_h(t,\delta)=\begin{pmatrix} 2A\sin(\pi t)&4P\left[\cos^2\!\left(\pi(t-h/2)\right)-\sin^2(\pi\delta)\right]\\ 1&0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad t=jh.\tag{2}

For real 0≤δ≤1/20\leq\delta\leq1/2 its limiting spectral radius is

ρ(t,δ)=Asin⁡(πt)+4Pcos⁡2(πδ)+(x−y)2sin⁡2(πt),Λ(t,δ)=2sin⁡(πt)ρ(t,δ). \begin{aligned} \rho(t,\delta)&=A\sin(\pi t)+\sqrt{4P\cos^2(\pi\delta)+(x-y)^2\sin^2(\pi t)},\\ \Lambda(t,\delta)&=2\sin(\pi t)\rho(t,\delta). \end{aligned}\tag{3}

Indeed the discriminant simplifies to 4[4Pcos⁡2(πδ)+(x−y)2sin⁡2(πt)]≥04[4P\cos^2(\pi\delta)+(x-y)^2\sin^2(\pi t)]\geq0. The eigenvalues are real and the positive root has magnitude at least that of the other root. In particular

ct≤ρ(t,δ)≤C,ct2≤Λ(t,δ)≤C,0<t≤12. ct\leq\rho(t,\delta)\leq C,\qquad ct^2\leq\Lambda(t,\delta)\leq C, \qquad0<t\leq\tfrac12.\tag{4}

uniformly in δ\delta, including x=yx=y and δ=1/2\delta=1/2. There the limiting matrix can be defective; no eigenbasis is inverted.

For every ε>0\varepsilon>0, uniformly in all i,ni,n, real δ∈[0,1/2]\delta\in[0,1/2], and all large NN,

|Ki,n|≤exp⁡{N∫i/N1/2logΛ(t,δ)dt+εN}. |K_{i,n}|\leq\exp\!\left\{N\int_{i/N}^{1/2}\log\Lambda(t,\delta)\,dt+\varepsilon N\right\}.\tag{5}

The same bound, with another εN\varepsilon N allowance, holds for all face weights in a sufficiently small FIXED complex neighborhood of their reference values, with protected zeros still zero, and δ\delta in a sufficiently thin FIXED complex strip around the real interval. The reference Λ\Lambda on the right is evaluated at the real weights and Re⁡δ\operatorname{Re}\delta. All choices depend on ε\varepsilon and the positive compact, not on the starting row or NN.

Here are details sufficient to include the degeneracy in this claim. First restrict tt to [η,1/2][\eta,1/2]. The spectral radius is bounded below there. For EVERY real reference matrix T=T0(t,δ)T=T_0(t,\delta), Cayley–Hamilton gives

∥Tk∥≤Cη(k+1)ρ(t,δ)k,k≥1. \|T^k\|\leq C_\eta(k+1)\rho(t,\delta)^k,\qquad k\geq1.\tag{6}

For distinct eigenvalues use the power divided difference, a sum of kk terms each bounded by ρk−1\rho^{k-1}; the same polynomial identity includes repeated eigenvalues by its derivative limit. Uniformly bounded ∥T∥\|T\| and ρ≥cη\rho\geq c_\eta supply the displayed constant. This is valid even on the entire coalescing line x=yx=y, δ=1/2\delta=1/2.

Choose a FIXED block length kk so log⁡[2Cη(k+1)]/k\log[2C_\eta(k+1)]/k is small. On each block, the exact product differs from the reference constant product by Ok(h)O_k(h), uniformly in the row and δ\delta. The same finite product estimate applies after a sufficiently small fixed perturbation of the face weights and δ\delta. Decrease that perturbation until its error is at most Cη(k+1)ρkC_\eta(k+1)\rho^k. Multiplying the block estimates and using the uniform continuity of log⁡ρ\log\rho on [η,1/2][\eta,1/2] gives the integral of log⁡ρ\log\rho plus the prescribed small per-row error. The finitely many incomplete blocks cost o(N)o(N). Thus no modulus gap, eigenvector condition number, or n-dependent perturbation disk is used.

On t<ηt<\eta use the bounded transfer entries directly, at cost CηNC^{\eta N}. The missing logarithmic integral is bounded in absolute value by Cηlog⁡(2/η)C\eta\log(2/\eta), uniformly, by (4). The additional exact factors sjs_j have the same elementary endpoint bound. First choose η\eta small, then kk large, then the fixed complex perturbation small, and finally NN large. This proves (5). Replacing n/Nn/N by 1/21/2 costs o(N)o(N), since nn differs from N/2N/2 by only O(1)O(1) and Λ\Lambda is bounded away from zero at 1/21/2.

2. Uniform prefix sums and the exact maximization

For real δ\delta the prefix logarithm obeys the uniform upper Riemann bound

∑r<ilog⁡|αr(δ)|≤N∫0i/Nlog⁡|4cos⁡(π(t+δ))cos⁡(π(t−δ))|dt+o(N). \sum_{r<i}\log|\alpha_r(\delta)| \leq N\int_0^{i/N}\log|4\cos(\pi(t+\delta))\cos(\pi(t-\delta))|\,dt+o(N).\tag{7}

A zero sampled factor makes that summand zero, so it causes no upper bound problem. To justify uniformity even near the endpoints, truncate the logarithm from below. The truncated function is jointly continuous on the compact (t,δ)(t,\delta) rectangle. Its integrals converge uniformly to the untruncated integrals: the two shifted cosine logarithms have uniformly integrable singularities. Equivalently the level sets of |4cos⁡(π(t+δ))cos⁡(π(t−δ))||4\cos(\pi(t+\delta))\cos(\pi(t-\delta))| below rr have length O(r)O(\sqrt r); integrating their distribution gives the uniform truncation error. The same argument with log⁡(|α|+r)\log(|\alpha|+r) proves an arbitrarily small additional integral error for a sufficiently small fixed COMPLEX spectral perturbation. No lower bound on the distance to a physical node is assumed. If b>0b>0 is perturbed in a small fixed complex face neighborhood, |bcomplex|i/bi≤eO(θN)\lvert b_{\mathrm{complex}}\rvert^i/b^i\leq e^{O(\theta N)} for i≤Ni\leq N, with θ\theta the relative neighborhood size. This is absorbed in the same εN\varepsilon N allowance. A protected b=0b=0 stays exactly zero and has only start 00.

Combining (1), (5), (7), and at most N+1N+1 starts gives the uniform weighted upper rate

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡|Rn(b,δ)|≤max0≤μ≤1/2Fabs(μ,δ),Fabs(μ,δ)=μlog⁡b+∫0μlog⁡|a(t)+u|dt+∫μ1/2log⁡Λ(t,δ)dt,a(t)=4cos⁡2(πt),u=−4sin⁡2(πδ). \begin{aligned} \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\lvert R_n(b,\delta)\rvert &\leq\max_{0\leq\mu\leq1/2}F_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mu,\delta),\\ F_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mu,\delta)&=\mu\log b+\int_0^\mu\log|a(t)+u|\,dt +\int_\mu^{1/2}\log\Lambda(t,\delta)\,dt,\\ a(t)&=4\cos^2(\pi t),\qquad u=-4\sin^2(\pi\delta). \end{aligned}\tag{8}

For b=0b=0 the right side means its sole allowed start μ=0\mu=0. The proof above yields an explicit UNIFORM εN\varepsilon N version of (8), also in the fixed small face/spectral neighborhoods described after (5). No rate of convergence stronger than that is needed for a strict gap.

For b>0b>0 put c=cos⁡(πδ)c=\cos(\pi\delta), z=sin⁡2(πμ)z=\sin^2(\pi\mu), w=c2/zw=c^2/z and D0=(x−y)2D_0=(x-y)^2. At a regular interior point,

∂μFabs=log⁡b|a(μ)+u|Λ(μ,δ),b|a+u|Λ=2b|w−1|A+D0+4Pw. \partial_\mu F_{\mathrm{abs}}=\log\frac{b\lvert a(\mu)+u\rvert}{\Lambda(\mu,\delta)}, \qquad\frac{b\lvert a+u\rvert}{\Lambda}= \frac{2b\lvert w-1\rvert}{A+\sqrt{D_0+4Pw}}.\tag{9}

When 0≤δ<1/20\leq\delta<1/2, the sign-change point is tc=1/2−δt_c=1/2-\delta. Before tct_c, ww decreases from infinity to 11 and the ratio in (9) strictly decreases from infinity to 00. For w>1w>1 its derivative has positive numerator

A+D0+2Pw+2PD0+4Pw, A+\frac{D_0+2Pw+2P}{\sqrt{D_0+4Pw}},

with the P=0P=0 case interpreted directly. Thus there is one maximum, at

sin⁡2(πμ*)=c2b2(b+x)(b+y),0<μ*<tc. \sin^2(\pi\mu_*)=\frac{c^2b^2}{(b+x)(b+y)},\qquad0<\mu_*<t_c.\tag{10}

After tct_c, ww decreases from 11 and the numerator 1−w1-w increases while the denominator decreases. The ratio is strictly increasing whenever this interval is nonempty. Therefore every post-tct_c critical point is a MINIMUM, and that part has its maximum at an endpoint.

The complete prefix integral is zero:

∫01/2log⁡|4cos⁡(π(t+δ))cos⁡(π(t−δ))|dt=0. \int_0^{1/2}\log|4\cos(\pi(t+\delta))\cos(\pi(t-\delta))|\,dt=0.\tag{11}

This follows by shifting the two intervals and using the reflection and period of log⁡|2cos⁡|\log|2\cos|; it also holds at δ=0,1/2\delta=0,1/2 by integrability. Consequently Fabs(1/2,δ)=12log⁡bF_{\mathrm{abs}}(1/2,\delta)=\tfrac12\log b. The pre-tct_c maximum is exactly the original triangle action Qb(δ)Q_b(\delta), with its complete normalization. Thus

maxμFabs(μ,δ)=max⁡{Qb(δ),12logb}. \max_\mu F_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mu,\delta)=\max\left\{Q_b(\delta),\tfrac12\log b\right\}.\tag{12}

For b=0b=0 it is Q0(δ)Q_0(\delta), without the second branch. At δ=1/2\delta=1/2, the ratio (9) is the constant b/max⁡(x,y)b/\max(x,y), so the same statement follows by its endpoint values. In particular Qb(1/2)=12log⁡max⁡(x,y)Q_b(1/2)=\tfrac12\log\max(x,y), understood as the continuous limit.

For clarity, the action identification in (12) uses the proved cofactor/marked-action identity near δ=0\delta=0. The real integral at μ*\mu_* has positive prefix factors and positive Λ\Lambda, and is real analytic on 0≤δ<1/20\leq\delta<1/2. Its defining formulas have no zero or branch crossing there. The identity therefore continues on this connected real interval. Its endpoint value follows either from (3) at δ=1/2\delta=1/2 or from uniform integrability in (8). The cases P=0P=0 and b=0b=0 use the exact first-order/sole-start formulas and the same limit.

3. Strict local spectral decrease, with the monomial exception

The actual QQ action has the exact real derivative

dQbdδ=−log⁡EbD,Eb=A+2bsin⁡2(πδ)+2sin⁡(πδ)bA+P+b2sin⁡2(πδ),D=A2−4Psin⁡2(πδ). \begin{aligned} \frac{dQ_b}{d\delta}&=-\log\frac{E_b}{\sqrt D},\\ E_b&=A+2b\sin^2(\pi\delta) +2\sin(\pi\delta)\sqrt{bA+P+b^2\sin^2(\pi\delta)},\\ D&=A^2-4P\sin^2(\pi\delta). \end{aligned}\tag{13}

For 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/2 this derivative is strictly negative whenever b>0b>0 or P>0P>0. The possible logarithmic singularity at δ=1/2\delta=1/2, x=yx=y is integrable. Also the interior maximum at δ=0\delta=0 proves Qb(0)>12log⁡bQ_b(0)>\tfrac12\log b when b>0b>0 and A>0A>0. Therefore, for each η>0\eta>0,

max⁡{Qb(δ),12logb}≤Qb(0)−γη,η≤δ≤12. \max\left\{Q_b(\delta),\tfrac12\log b\right\} \leq Q_b(0)-\gamma_\eta,\qquad\eta\leq\delta\leq\tfrac12.\tag{14}

with γη>0\gamma_\eta>0 on each fixed positive compact of the nonzero weights, provided b>0b>0 or P>0P>0. For b=0b=0 omit the second branch. The sole exception b=0b=0, P=0P=0 is the literal one-variable monomial; its cofactor is independent of δ\delta, and no false gap is claimed.

Every actual port has A=x+y>0A=x+y>0: its outer pair is MATCHING, whereas the two protected faces share the original MAIN edge. It has at least one nonmonomial triangle. Indeed, if one outer face vanishes, there remains only one other protected zero, so at least one middle is positive. If both outers are positive, P>0P>0 in both triangles. Thus each port’s product of two cofactors has a strict gap in (14) when its spectral δ\delta stays away from 00.

The exact even/odd central cofactor formulas contain two or three products, each with BOTH triangle cofactors and terminal indices N/2+O(1)N/2+O(1). All central coefficients have bounded degree and bounded size on the fixed face/spectral compact. Therefore (8)-(14) imply

|PN,cross(δ;positive faces)|≤exp⁡{N[Qb(0)+Qc(0)−γη+o(1)]},η≤|δ|≤12. |P_{N,\mathrm{cross}}(\delta;\text{positive faces})| \leq\exp\!\left\{N\left[Q_b(0)+Q_c(0)-\gamma_\eta+o(1)\right]\right\}, \qquad\eta\leq|\delta|\leq\tfrac12.\tag{15}

Uniform ε\varepsilon versions hold on a small fixed complex neighborhood of the reference faces and a fixed thin complex δ\delta strip. This follows from the proof of (5),(7), not from coefficientwise domination by the value at positive faces. For all δ\delta the same estimate without −γη-\gamma_\eta holds. In particular every other port has a controlled endpoint rate while one separated port supplies a strict loss.

4. Sewing the original marked Cauchy period

Fix the actual positive complete ordinary radii, with both protected variables zero. Their endpoint weighted exponent after the original degree-dd targets is ΦD\Phi_D. Take the exact all-CROSS marked coefficient CN(𝜹)C_N(\boldsymbol\delta), with its original simultaneous-rotation quotient or, equivalently, the full original Cauchy torus.

Outside a fixed neighborhood of the common ordinary-face phase, the already proved global cofactor angular theorem supplies a strict exponential gap, uniformly for ALL real spectral deltas. Its proof retains all terminal cofactors and every central summand; it also holds in a sufficiently thin fixed complex spectral strip.

Inside a sufficiently small fixed common-phase neighborhood, use (15) and its proved small complex FACE extension. If max⁡i|Re⁡δi|≥η\max_i|\operatorname{Re}\delta_i|\geq\eta, at least one port supplies γη\gamma_\eta, while all the other ports cost only their endpoint rates and arbitrarily small chosen errors. Since the graph is fixed, choose those errors so their sum is less than half that loss. The common face phase cancels exactly by total degree and original targets. No independent face radii are optimized.

It follows that for each fixed η>0\eta>0 there exist τ,γ>0\tau,\gamma>0, N0N_0 such that

|CN(𝜹)|≤eN(ΦD−γ). |C_N(\boldsymbol\delta)|\leq e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma)}.\tag{16}

for N≥N0N\geq N_0, all Re⁡δi∈[−1/2,1/2]\operatorname{Re}\delta_i\in[-1/2,1/2] with max⁡i|Re⁡δi|≥η\max_i|\operatorname{Re}\delta_i|\geq\eta, and |Im⁡δi|≤τ|\operatorname{Im}\delta_i|\leq\tau. To include boundary neighborhoods cleanly, first prove the real decrease for η/2\eta/2 and then shrink τ\tau. Every fixed derivative has the same strict rate on a smaller strip by Cauchy’s estimate. Both level parities are included.

This is an actual GLOBAL spectral gap of the all-CROSS shared source, away from the geometric point on the centered fold cover. It is not a coefficientwise strict inequality and it is not a global FULL-V theorem. Genuine moving one-sided paths can violate the corresponding full coefficient bound, as the previously recorded σ=3\sigma=\sqrt3 example shows. Any use of (16) in the original Fourier period must still retain whole-domain charge summation and its endpoint currents. The whole all-CROSS period theorem performs that transfer and obtains the filled-volume asymptotic for this sector. It does not identify the all-CROSS sector with the full invariant.

Scope and focused finite checks

The equality in (12) concerns the absolute envelope. Equation (8) is a limsup bound for the actual cofactor; cancellation can make its rate smaller.

The focused audit and its results check four symbolic identities, 120 original recurrence/start-sum identities, 26,880 rescaled tail entries and 240 finite characteristic identities at 65 digits, across both parities and all stated zero patterns. Author replay maximum relative identity error was 1.26e-64. Its 120 finite weighted-rate comparisons are explicitly diagnostics. They do not substitute for the uniform proof or numerically verify the original shared coefficient gap.
