A source normal and a tangential error certificate

21 November 2026. Research note. Previous finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The normalized Conway source is a sum of squared recursion residuals and one complex closing component. Its linearization therefore has a one-dimensional complex normal. Projecting onto that normal gives a computable scalar quantity. The error in its prediction of the minimum is bounded by the tangential energy, with constants independent of the level and the number of vertices.

The resulting radius vanishes at the minimizing field, even though the recursion residual there need not vanish. This differs from the earlier complex-source error disk, whose radius uses the full residual. No geometric trial field or new volume identity is supplied below.

The resulting local maps Φv\Phi_v, and the normalized original closing row LL, have norm at most σ\sigma. 2. The exact normal and tangential energies

Since SS is invertible, JJ is injective. Its image has complex codimension one in 𝒳⊕ℂ\mathcal X\oplus\mathbb C. Its orthogonal complement is spanned by (d*1), \binom{d^*}{1}, because −S*d*+c*=0-S^*d^*+c^*=0. Let PnP_{\mathrm{ n}} and PtP_{\mathrm{ t}} denote the orthogonal projections onto this normal line and onto ran⁡J\operatorname{ran}J, respectively. Define 𝔫(x)=∥PnF(x)∥2=|z+dr|21+ν,𝔱(x)=∥PtF(x)∥2=E(x)−𝔫(x).(8) \boxed{\quad \mathfrak n(x)=\|P_{\mathrm{ n}}F(x)\|^2 =\frac{|z+dr|^2}{1+\nu},\qquad \mathfrak t(x)=\|P_{\mathrm{ t}}F(x)\|^2=E(x)-\mathfrak n(x). \quad} \qquad\text{(8)} Both quantities are nonnegative. This is a projection in the lifted residual space, not deletion of a physical boundary channel: every Hermite direction still occurs in 𝒳\mathcal X. In particular 𝔫(x)\mathfrak n(x) may be zero; orthogonality does not prove that the coherent scalar (7) survives.

4. A two-sided certificate for the minimum

For any displacement hh, the bilinear tree gives the exact expansion F(x+h)=F(x)+Jh+(𝒬(h),0),∥𝒬(h)∥≤∥h∥232.(18) F(x+h)=F(x)+Jh+(\mathcal Q(h),0),\qquad \|\mathcal Q(h)\|\le\frac{\|h\|^2}{32}. \qquad\text{(18)} Here 𝒬v(h)=Φv(hL,hR)\mathcal Q_v(h)=\Phi_v(h_L,h_R) at a binary product and is zero elsewhere. Different sibling pairs are disjoint, so the stated bound follows from ∑v∥hL∥2∥hR∥2≤∥h∥4/4\sum_v\|h_L\|^2\|h_R\|^2\le\|h\|^4/4. There is no higher Taylor remainder.

Theorem. Every feasible trial field satisfies max⁡{0,𝔫(x)−3252048𝔱(x)}≤e*≤𝔫(x)+249𝔱(x).(19) \boxed{\quad \max\left\{0,\sqrt{\mathfrak n(x)} -\frac{325}{2048}\mathfrak t(x)\right\} \ \le\sqrt{e_*}\ \le\ \sqrt{\mathfrak n(x)}+\frac{2}{49}\mathfrak t(x). \quad} \qquad\text{(19)} All constants are independent of NN, the tree size and the fixed crossing powers after their original budgets are retained.

Proof. Strong monotonicity from (3), with ∇E(x*)=0\nabla E(x_*)=0, and (10) give ∥x*−x∥2≤∥∇E(x)∥2≤32564𝔱(x).(20) \|x_*-x\|^2\le\|\nabla E(x)\|^2 \le\frac{325}{64}\mathfrak t(x). \qquad\text{(20)} Apply PnP_{\mathrm{ n}} to (18) with h=x*−xh=x_*-x. The linear term disappears exactly. The reverse triangle inequality yields e*=∥F(x*)∥≥𝔫(x)−∥𝒬(x*−x)∥≥𝔫(x)−3252048𝔱(x). \sqrt{e_*}=\|F(x_*)\| \ge\sqrt{\mathfrak n(x)}-\|\mathcal Q(x_*-x)\| \ge\sqrt{\mathfrak n(x)}-\frac{325}{2048}\mathfrak t(x). Clipping the right side at zero proves the lower bound.

For the upper bound, use the feasible field (16) in (18). Equations (13) and (18) imply e*≤∥F(y)∥≤𝔫(x)+132∥δ∥2≤𝔫(x)+249𝔱(x). \sqrt{e_*}\le\|F(y)\| \le\sqrt{\mathfrak n(x)}+\frac1{32}\|\delta\|^2 \le\sqrt{\mathfrak n(x)}+\frac{2}{49}\mathfrak t(x). This proves (19). ▫\square

The radius now uses the tangential energy, which vanishes at stationarity. It is not the previous bound for the complex error |KN/B−w||K_N/B-w| in terms of ∥r∥2\|r\|^2. Equation (19) controls the minimum and hence the magnitude of the original source through (2); it does not identify its complex phase with ww.

5. An explicit original-source lower witness

Combining (2) and (19) gives B[𝔫−3252048𝔱]+≤|KN|≤γB(𝔫+249𝔱).(21) \boxed{\quad B\left[\sqrt{\mathfrak n}-\frac{325}{2048}\mathfrak t\right]_+ \le |K_N| \le\sqrt\gamma B \left(\sqrt{\mathfrak n}+\frac{2}{49}\mathfrak t\right). \quad} \qquad\text{(21)} Here [u]+=max⁡(0,u)[u]_+=\max(0,u), and both energies are evaluated at the chosen field. The only source-dependent scalar in them is the computable coherent correction (7), not an assumed value of KNK_N.

For a fixed construction, suppose feasible fields xNx_N satisfy 𝔫(xN)>0\mathfrak n(x_N)>0 eventually and 𝔱(xN)≤a𝔫(xN),0≤a<2048325,(22) \mathfrak t(x_N)\le a\sqrt{\mathfrak n(x_N)},\qquad 0\le a<\frac{2048}{325}, \qquad\text{(22)} with aa independent of NN. If 2πNlog⁡(Btot𝔫(xN))→V,(23) \frac{2\pi}{N}\log\bigl(B_{\mathrm{ tot}} \sqrt{\mathfrak n(x_N)}\bigr)\longrightarrow V, \qquad\text{(23)} then (21) proves 2πNlog⁡|KN|→V.(24) \frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N|\longrightarrow V. \qquad\text{(24)} More explicitly the ratio to the quantity inside the logarithm in (23) lies between the positive constants 1−325a/20481-325a/2048 and γ(1+2a/49)\sqrt\gamma(1+2a/49). The discrepancy of the scaled logarithms is therefore O(N−1)O(N^{-1}), with these explicit constants.

A volume proof must still construct actual geometric fields satisfying (22) and identify the rate (23). Angle stationarity alone does not control 𝔱\mathfrak t, which contains the complete quantum linearization. Fixed short subtrees may be contracted with their full packets before applying the same argument, but no lower bound for their geometric coupling is inferred here. No new filling case or finite-exception theorem is claimed.
