The original cap/CROSS contraction on a complex two-spectrum neighborhood

31 October 2026. Both polynomial spectral parameters, both projectors, and the odd central transition are retained. The statement is restricted to the scope below.

1. The original coefficient and the claim

Keep the exact two-port incidence

E=(A,0,C,Z),F=(0,A,0,C). E=(A,0,C,Z),\qquad F=(0,A,0,C).

with A incident only to these ports and its unchanged target A^d, d=N−1. Write

θ=π/N,cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)θ),sj=2sin⁡(jθ),aj=2+cj,γj=2−cj,Bj=∏r=1jsr2,M=⌊N/2⌋−1,H=⌊d/2⌋,Jn=[max⁡(0,n−H),min⁡(M,n)]∩ℤ. \begin{aligned} \theta&=\pi/N,& c_j&=2\cos((2j+1)\theta),&s_j&=2\sin(j\theta),\\ a_j&=2+c_j,&\gamma_j&=2-c_j,&B_j&=\prod_{r=1}^j s_r^2,\\ M&=\lfloor N/2\rfloor-1,& H&=\lfloor d/2\rfloor,\\ J_n&=[\max(0,n-H),\min(M,n)]\cap\mathbb Z. \end{aligned}

The cap parameter is sigma, near +2; the E parameter is tau, near −2. Define the genuine polynomial cap weights

gj(σ)=σ−cj,bj(σ)=Bj∏r=j+1dgr(σ),χN(σ)=∏r=0dgr(σ). \begin{aligned} g_j(\sigma)&=\sigma-c_j,\\ b_j(\sigma)&=B_j\prod_{r=j+1}^{d}g_r(\sigma),\\ \chi_N(\sigma)&=\prod_{r=0}^{d}g_r(\sigma). \end{aligned}

Let C_(j,h)(tau) be (−1)^d times the coefficient of A^j C^h Z^(d−j−h) in the full original H_(V_N(tau))(A,0,C,Z). Thus C_(j,h)(−2)=C_(j,h) is the old nonnegative raw endpoint coefficient. Apply the actual CROSS cutoff h<=H and RIGHT cutoff j<=M only in the final sum

Tn(σ,τ)=∑j∈Jn(−1)jbj(σ)Cj,n−j(τ),0≤n≤N−2. T_n(\sigma,\tau)=\sum_{j\in J_n}(-1)^j b_j(\sigma)C_{j,n-j}(\tau), \qquad 0\le n\le N-2.\tag{1}

The remaining original degrees are C^n Z^(d−n). The common raw sign (−1)^d does not affect its modulus and has not been changed. Let D_n^ref be the same fixed positive comparator in the all-demand theorem: even raw positive endpoint at both ports; odd actual regularizer V_N(−2)−N^(−2)V_N’(−2) at both reference ports. The numerator (1) is always RAW.

Theorem. There are epsilon_0>0 and omega(epsilon)->0 such that, for 0<epsilon<=epsilon_0,

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡max0≤n≤N−2sup|σ−2|≤ε|τ+2|≤ε|Tn(σ,τ)|Dnref≤ω(ε). \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log \max_{0\le n\le N-2} \sup_{\substack{|\sigma-2|\le\varepsilon\\|\tau+2|\le\varepsilon}} \frac{|T_n(\sigma,\tau)|}{D_n^{\mathrm{ref}}} \le\omega(\varepsilon).\tag{2}

Both disks are complex. Every spectral root is included, and the level runs through both parities. In particular any two shrinking radii tending to zero give a uniform subexponential comparison: fix epsilon first, use eventual inclusion in its polydisk, then let epsilon decrease to zero in (2).

This is a statement for the exact internal A^d contraction, not for a larger star or a whole invariant. No polynomial factor or spectral estimate for arbitrary incidence is asserted.

2. Polynomial source identities, including the odd transition

Cj,h(τ)=v(τ)TB̂j(ZN)ed−j−h,vr(τ)=(−1)d+rBr∏l=r+1d(τ−cl). \begin{aligned} C_{j,h}(\tau)&=v(\tau)^T\widehat B_j(Z_N)e_{d-j-h},\\ v_r(\tau)&=(-1)^{d+r}B_r\prod_{l=r+1}^{d}(\tau-c_l). \end{aligned}

for arbitrary polynomial coupons, before endpoint specialization. Choose E=V_N(tau), F=Bhat_j, G=g_n=product_(r<n)(z−c_r), n=j+h. The actual protected-face monomials are

The unchanged three face targets therefore give the raw scalar (−1)^(d+n)B_n C_(j,h)(tau). On the Newton side it equals (−1)^n B_n alpha_(V_N(tau))^T Bhat_j(Z_N)e_(d−n). Cancelling B_n, with alpha_(V_N(tau),r)=(−1)^r B_r product_(l>r)(tau−c_l), proves the displayed v(tau) formula with exactly its (−1)^d sign. No extra N occurs. This is a general physical polynomial identity, not endpoint interpolation.

The Newton relations (c_j−Z)Bhat_j=s_(j+1)^2 Bhat_(j+1), Z_Ne_k=c_ke_k−s_(k+1)^2e_(k+1), and the literal row reflection c_(d−n)=c_n, s_(d−n+1)=s_n then give for both spectra

These are polynomial identities, including when g_j or chi_N vanishes. For n<=M the original cutoffs are redundant, so

For n>H set L=n−H. Summing (3) over the actual moving range J_n=[L,M] and retaining the adjacent-projector difference yields

gnTn+sn2Tn−1=(gL−1−gn)tL−1(n)−gM+1tM+1(n). g_nT_n+s_n^2T_{n-1} =(g_{L-1}-g_n)t_{L-1}(n)-g_{M+1}t_{M+1}(n).\tag{5}

At even N=2m these currents contain C_(n−m,m)(tau) and C_(m,n−m)(tau). At odd N=2m+1 they contain C_(n−m−1,m+1)(tau) and C_(m,n−m)(tau). The first odd current DOES NOT generally vanish when tau differs from −2.

There is one transition not covered by either n<=M or n>H: at odd level n=H=m, J_n=J_(n−1)=[0,M]. Summing (3) there gives

gmTm+sm2Tm−1=χN(σ)C0,m(τ)+(−1)Msm2bM(σ)Cm,0(τ). g_mT_m+s_m^2T_{m-1} =\chi_N(\sigma)C_{0,m}(\tau) +(-1)^M s_m^2 b_M(\sigma)C_{m,0}(\tau).\tag{6}

Equations (4), (5), and (6) cover EVERY transition. No central zero, lower-current term, or top coefficient is omitted.

3. Existing coefficientwise majorants at the fixed original root

Let kappa=arcosh(1+epsilon/2). The literal cap product gives

|bj(σ)|≤UjeNκ,|χN(σ)|≤4eNκ,Uj=bj(2). |b_j(\sigma)|\le U_j e^{N\kappa},\qquad |\chi_N(\sigma)|\le4e^{N\kappa},\qquad U_j=b_j(2).\tag{7}

uniformly in its complex disk. For j<=M+1, U_j<=C N^2, including the single current index above the cap cutoff. All divisions used to prove (7) are by fixed gamma_r>0, never by varying g_r.

For E, use the EXISTING source-proved coefficientwise comparison in odd regularization, section 4, equations (13)–(15), and its even comparison in section 5. Denote the positive full reference coefficients by C_(j,h)^ref: they are C_(j,h) at even level, and Cbar_(j,h) from the fixed-root regularizer at odd level, including outside the CROSS cutoff. Then for |tau+2|<=epsilon,

|Cj,h(τ)|≤QN(ε)Cj,href,QN(ε)≤(1+N2ε)eNκ. |C_{j,h}(\tau)|\le Q_N(\varepsilon)C_{j,h}^{\mathrm{ref}},\qquad Q_N(\varepsilon)\le(1+N^2\varepsilon)e^{N\kappa}.\tag{8}

Here is why this comparison is root-safe. Each path has distinct unused spectral factors. At even level all a_r>0, and the product of their possible ratios is at most product_r(1+epsilon/a_r)<=exp(N kappa). At odd level a_m=0 is the sole central zero. A path retaining it is bounded by epsilon times the product of its noncentral factors, which is paid by N^2 epsilon times its positive derivative-reference term. Paths without it are paid by the raw reference term. The noncentral product is

∏r≠m(1+εar)=[sinh⁡(Nκ/2)Nsinh⁡(κ/2)]2≤e(N−1)κ. \prod_{r\ne m}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{a_r}\right) =\left[\frac{\sinh(N\kappa/2)}{N\sinh(\kappa/2)}\right]^2 \le e^{(N-1)\kappa}.

This proves (8), and shows explicitly why no vanished a_m is divided out. The comparison applies coefficientwise to the full source, hence to either original projected part and both currents.

The same source results give uniform CLOSED coefficient entropy for C^ref. On CROSS degrees it is the two-triangle maximum; on the adjacent one-sided seam it is the same continuous full endpoint entropy. These are actual coefficient upper/lower theorems, not merely weighted capacities.

Two corner consequences will be used. Let q_L=(0,0,0,1) and q_R=(1/2,0,1/2,0), in degree order (A,input middle,C,Z). Their reference entropies are zero. There are nonnegative e_L(eta),e_R(eta)->0 such that the original coefficients in eta-neighborhoods of these corners obey the uniform upper rates e_L(eta), e_R(eta). The precise fixed multiplicative enlargement of the neighborhoods is immaterial and is included in these definitions.

4. A complete comparator-rate lemma, including the high corner

The all-demand proof used the rate

J(u)=f(u−12)+maxu−1/2≤a≤1/2{3f(a)+f(a−u+12)},f(t)=−∫0tlog⁡tan⁡(πs)ds. \begin{aligned} J(u)&=f(u-\tfrac12)+\max_{u-1/2\le a\le1/2} \{3f(a)+f(a-u+\tfrac12)\},\\ f(t)&=-\int_0^t\log\tan(\pi s)\,ds. \end{aligned}\tag{9}

We need the same actual coefficient estimate beyond its former middle interval. In fact, uniformly for M<n<=N−2,

1Nlog⁡Dnref=J(n/d)+o(1). \frac1N\log D_n^{\mathrm{ref}}=J(n/d)+o(1).\tag{10}

and n=M has rate J(1/2)+o(1). This includes sequences tending to n/d=1. Here is the finite-support lower check for that extension.

The existing positive convolution gives the continuous objective

E(a,v;u)=2f(a)+f(v−a)+f(u−v)+f(u−a)−2f(v),u−12≤a≤v≤12. E(a,v;u)=2f(a)+f(v-a)+f(u-v)+f(u-a)-2f(v), \qquad u-\tfrac12\le a\le v\le\tfrac12.\tag{11}

Its derivative in v in the interior is

log⁡tan⁡(π(u−v))tan⁡2(πv)tan⁡(π(v−a))≥0. \log\frac{\tan(\pi(u-v))\tan^2(\pi v)}{\tan(\pi(v-a))}\ge0.

because u>=1/2, so tan(pi(u−v))>=cot(pi v), while tan(pi(v−a))<=tan(pi v). Thus its closed maximum is v=1/2 and equals (9). All actual allocations have limiting data in this compact domain. The closed coefficient upper, polynomial number of terms, and continuity give a uniform upper for (10), including its endpoints. A subsequence compactness argument avoids imposing an interior-optimizer assumption at u=1.

For the LOWER, choose the actual cap index j=M, whose reference weight is polynomial: V_M=N at even level and V_Mo=1/[4cos2(theta/2)] at odd level. At even level use the exact lower block

4(A+Z)RM*(0)RM*(C)≤PE,CROSS. 4(A+Z)R_M^*(0)R_M^*(C)\le P_{E,\mathrm{CROSS}}.

with its genuine modified path numerators and original scalar normalization, as in the closed half-block theorem, section 5. If one rewrites R_M^* using that theorem’s determinant D_M, its scalar C_front=N/[2sin(pi/N)] must multiply EACH factor; it has not been removed here. Choose the forced Z unit. The actual first-triangle A-degree then ranges over

n−M≤k≤M(N even). n-M\le k\le M\qquad(N\text{ even}).\tag{12}

The two remaining triangle degrees are exactly M. At odd level the true positive D_m convolution gives instead

n−m≤k≤M(N odd). n-m\le k\le M\qquad(N\text{ odd}).\tag{13}

with both triangle degrees m=N/2−1/2. The raw-to-regularized CROSS comparison is by uniform positive factors. These are the original integer intervals, not enlarged supports.

After dividing by d, the intervals (12), (13) are within O(1/d) of [u−1/2,1/2] at both endpoints and are nonempty for every n<=N−2. Clamp a maximizing a in (9) to the actual interval, then round to an integer. The total change is O(1/d), even when the interval is a singleton near n=N−2. The uniform CLOSED triangle lower theorem and continuity therefore give the matching lower rate J(u). The forced Z and the true R_M^* scalar have remained in this lower estimate throughout. This proves (10).

As already proved for (9), J is continuous on [1/2,1], strictly concave in the interior, with a unique peak u_* in (1/2,3/4). It is increasing before the peak and decreasing afterwards. Also, choosing a=u−1/2 gives

J(u)≥4f(u−12)≥0,J(1)=0. J(u)\ge4f(u-\tfrac12)\ge0,\qquad J(1)=0.\tag{14}

These properties and (10) do not depend on either varying spectrum.

For n<=b, no varying a-factor is close to a zero. Writing zeta=tau+2,

This elementary lower is used only before b. No such nonvanishing claim is made through the central rows.

There is no reciprocal chi_N. Choose small complex delta,beta with

Let s(t)=sin(pi t)/(pi t) and L_delta(t)=log s(t+delta)+log s(t−delta)−2log s(t), with the fixed analytic sinc logarithm. Define m=N/2, not necessarily an integer. The two exact Gamma multipliers are

Equation (20) is exactly G_delta(j,k)H_beta(j,k) times the exponential of the midpoint sum of

Both nearby endpoint zero pairs have therefore been removed from the Euler–Maclaurin input and placed in EXACT Gamma factors.

For each fixed contour height Y this is analytic and nonzero on

The constants may depend on each fixed Y; they are uniform in both spectral parameters. Adding the second smooth logarithm only changes a finite prefactor, absorbed by 2pi/5>6/5.

7. The calibrated action and the contour estimate

The leading logarithm of G_delta is the difference of

N[(u+δ)Log⁡(u+δ)+(u−δ)Log⁡(u−δ)−2uLog⁡u] N\bigl[(u+\delta)\operatorname{Log}(u+\delta)+(u-\delta)\operatorname{Log}(u-\delta)-2u\operatorname{Log}u\bigr]

between u=z/N and a. The leading logarithm of H_beta is the NEGATIVE difference of the same expression with u replaced by 1/2−u and delta by beta. The exact normalization makes both differences vanish at a. Stirling errors are O_(eta,Y)(log N), uniformly in both disks; all arguments remain in the right half-plane. The smooth midpoint terms have the corresponding integrals with O_Y(N^(−1)) corrections.

Differentiating these leading expressions and adding the smooth terms gives exactly

Ψ′(u)=Log⁡(1+σ−24sin⁡2(πu))+Log⁡(1−τ+24cos⁡2(πu)),Ψ(a)=0. \Psi'(u)=\operatorname{Log}\left(1+\frac{\sigma-2}{4\sin^2(\pi u)}\right) +\operatorname{Log}\left(1-\frac{\tau+2}{4\cos^2(\pi u)}\right), \qquad\Psi(a)=0.\tag{24}

The small logarithms are the branches zero at the base spectra. Exponentiating verifies the sine/cosine identities in (20), and analytic continuation in the small delta,beta disks fixes the branches. Normalization at a fixes the integration constant. There is no unrecorded branch-dependent scalar.

For eta/2<=x<=1/2−eta/2, use

|sin⁡(π(x+iy))|2=sin⁡2(πx)+sinh⁡2(πy),|cos⁡(π(x+iy))|2=cos⁡2(πx)+sinh⁡2(πy). \begin{aligned} |\sin(\pi(x+iy))|^2&=\sin^2(\pi x)+\sinh^2(\pi y),\\ |\cos(\pi(x+iy))|^2&=\cos^2(\pi x)+\sinh^2(\pi y). \end{aligned}

Integrating first horizontally from a to x and then vertically, the elementary integrals of 1/(x2+y2) and 1/((1/2−x)2+y2) prove

|Ψ(x+iy)|≤Cε/η=:B. |\Psi(x+iy)|\le C\varepsilon/\eta=:B.\tag{25}

for ALL heights y. This bound is independent of the fixed Y used in the interpolation. It applies on the full enlarged rectangle for sufficiently large N.

The calibrated growth formula is consequently

log⁡|I(z)|=Nℜ[2f(z/N)−2f(a)+Ψ(z/N)]+Oη,Y(log⁡N),log⁡|Pk,n|=−2N[f(n/N)−f(a)]−NℜΨ(n/N)+Oη(log⁡N). \begin{aligned} \log|I(z)|&=N\Re[2f(z/N)-2f(a)+\Psi(z/N)]+O_{\eta,Y}(\log N),\\ \log|P_{k,n}|&=-2N[f(n/N)-f(a)]-N\Re\Psi(n/N)+O_\eta(\log N). \end{aligned}\tag{26}

Apply the exact alternating residue argument to (22). Enclose the integers k,…,n by real sides k−1/4,n+1/4 and imaginary sides ±NY, integrate pi I(z)csc(pi z), then subtract the exact first term I(k)=1. After multiplication by P_(k,n), the leading exponent is

N[h(x,y)−2f(n/N)+2B],h(x,y)=ℜ[2f(x+iy)]−π|y|. N[h(x,y)-2f(n/N)+2B],\qquad h(x,y)=\Re[2f(x+iy)]-\pi|y|.\tag{27}

The endpoint estimates give h(x,y)<=2f(x), and on the horizontal sides

|h(x,Y)|≤εY=2e−2πYπ(1−e−4πY)→0. |h(x,Y)|\le\varepsilon_Y=\frac{2e^{-2\pi Y}}{\pi(1-e^{-4\pi Y})}\longrightarrow0.

The left side costs at most 2Nf(a)+2NB; the right side costs 2NB+O_eta(1); the horizontal sides cost N(epsilon_Y+2B). Contour lengths and all Gamma corrections are only polynomial in N for fixed eta,Y.

The error (23) remains negligible AFTER propagation and forcing:

|χNPk,n|∑j=kn|I(j)−Ak,j|≤exp⁡{N[2max⁡f−6/5+2B+κ]+Oη(log⁡N)}. |\chi_NP_{k,n}|\sum_{j=k}^{n}|I(j)-A_{k,j}| \le\exp\{N[2\max f-6/5+2B+\kappa]+O_\eta(\log N)\}. \tag{28}

Choose eta_0,c_0 small so that 2B+kappa<1/10. Since 2max f<1, this is exponentially small. The parameter bounds in (17), needed to convert back to the fixed comparator, add at most C epsilon/eta to a rate; the same smallness choice leaves a strict error margin after that conversion as well.

The initial value in (19), by (15), (17), and bounded g_k, has rate at most 2kappa+e_L(eta)+C epsilon/eta. Its homogeneous propagator in (26) costs at most 2f(a)+B. The forcing term is bounded by the contour estimate just described. Fix epsilon,eta,Y, let N grow, and only then let Y grow. Equations (17), (19), and (26)–(28) prove the STRONG core estimate

limsupN1Nlog⁡maxk≤n≤bsupdisks|Tn(σ,τ)|C0,n0≤Ωcore,Ωcore=2κ+eL(η)+2f(η)+Cε/η. \begin{aligned} \limsup_N\frac1N\log\max_{k\le n\le b}\sup_{\mathrm{disks}} \frac{|T_n(\sigma,\tau)|}{C_{0,n}^{0}}&\le\Omega_{\mathrm{core}},\\ \Omega_{\mathrm{core}}&=2\kappa+e_L(\eta)+2f(\eta)+C\varepsilon/\eta. \end{aligned}\tag{29}

The stronger denominator C_(0,n)^0 is retained deliberately: using only D_n^ref here would lose the small absolute boundary rate needed for the next strip. No normalization is continued past b.

8. Raw propagation across the central low strip

For b<n<=M use the polynomial recurrence (4) itself. The factor g_n(sigma) stays uniformly close to 4. The old low ratio s_n^2/gamma_n is at most one, and replacing gamma_n by g_n changes its modulus by at most exp(C epsilon). Hence every raw forward propagator across this strip costs at most exp(CN epsilon). There is no division by c_(n−1)−tau or C_(0,n)(tau), which may vanish on these rows.

For this step use the EXACT PREFIX PRODUCT in (4), not a replacement of the raw C_0 coefficient by an unspecified reference coefficient. For n<=M every a_r in that prefix is positive; at odd level the central a_m=0 is outside it. Consequently

|C0,n(τ)|≤C0,n0∏r=0n−1(1+εar)≤eNκC0,n0. |C_{0,n}(\tau)|\le C_{0,n}^{0}\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{a_r}\right) \le e^{N\kappa}C_{0,n}^{0}.\tag{29a}

The last inequality uses the full positive product at even level, and the noncentral product displayed in section 3 at odd level. Thus it compares to the RAW C_(0,n)^0 exactly, with no need to identify it with the odd regularized coefficient.

For b<=n<=M, equation (16) and f(1/2−v)=f(v) imply log C_(0,n)^0<=2N f(eta)+o(N). Thus the forcing in (4) has rate at most 2kappa+2f(eta). At the starting row b, the stronger estimate (29) has absolute rate at most Omega_core+2f(eta). Summing the O(N) raw recurrence terms yields

|Tn(σ,τ)|≤exp⁡{N[2κ+eL(η)+4f(η)+Cε/η+o(1)]},b<n≤M. |T_n(\sigma,\tau)| \le\exp\{N[2\kappa+e_L(\eta)+4f(\eta)+C\varepsilon/\eta+o(1)]\}, \qquad b<n\le M.

The fixed comparator has D_nref>=C_(0,n)0/4>=N^(−C). Combining this strip with (15), (16), and (29) gives the full-low comparison

limsupN1Nlog⁡maxn≤Msupdisks|Tn|Dnref≤Ωlow,Ωlow=2κ+eL(η)+4f(η)+Cε/η. \begin{aligned} \limsup_N\frac1N\log\max_{n\le M}\sup_{\mathrm{disks}} \frac{|T_n|}{D_n^{\mathrm{ref}}}&\le\Omega_{\mathrm{low}},\\ \Omega_{\mathrm{low}}&=2\kappa+e_L(\eta)+4f(\eta)+C\varepsilon/\eta. \end{aligned}\tag{30}

This is precisely where a raw recurrence is necessary: the nearby tau roots in the central rows have NOT been divided out.

9. The high corner is bounded directly

For n>=n_R, the original range J_n forces BOTH j/d and (n−j)/d to lie within O(eta)+O(1/N) of 1/2, while (d−n)/d is O(eta). Thus every E reference degree approaches q_R. Equations (7), (8), the polynomial bound for U_j, and the definition of e_R give

maxn≥nRsupdisks|Tn|≤exp⁡{N[2κ+eR(η)+o(1)]}. \max_{n\ge n_R}\sup_{\mathrm{disks}}|T_n|\le\exp\{N[2\kappa+e_R(\eta)+o(1)]\}.\tag{31}

This is an absolute coefficient bound ONLY in this shrinking corner. It does not discard cancellation in an interior region. By the genuine comparator lower (10) and J>=0, D_n^ref>=exp(−o(N)) uniformly here. Consequently

limsupN1Nlog⁡maxn≥nRsupdisks|Tn|Dnref≤2κ+eR(η). \limsup_N\frac1N\log\max_{n\ge n_R}\sup_{\mathrm{disks}}\frac{|T_n|}{D_n^{\mathrm{ref}}}\le2\kappa+e_R(\eta).\tag{32}

No high-demand monotonicity for the varying E coefficients is assumed; it would generally be unavailable.

10. Both projected currents on the remaining middle

For M<n<n_R use (5), except for the odd transition (6). The cap current index is M+1, so its reference E coefficient tends to the CROSS data with A-load 1/2. Its closed reference rate is J(n/d), exactly as in the endpoint all-demand proof.

The lower current has h=H+1 at BOTH parities. Its normalized data approach the original one-sided seam

(qx,qb,qc,qy)=(t,0,12,12−t),t=n/d−12. (q_x,q_b,q_c,q_y)=(t,0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12-t),\qquad t=n/d-\tfrac12.

The full reference closed entropy there is 2f(t), and 2f(t)<=J(n/d) by (14). At odd level this uses the actual positive derivative-reference coefficient for a path whose raw central zero no longer vanishes; it does not reuse the former odd zero simplification. Equations (7), (8) thus bound BOTH currents by

exp⁡{N[J(n/d)+2κ+o(1)]}. \exp\{N[J(n/d)+2\kappa+o(1)]\}.\tag{33}

At the odd transition n=m, the extra coefficient C_(0,m)(tau) in (6) has reference corner rate zero, while chi_N has rate at most kappa. Its total rate is at most 2kappa; the other transition current has rate J(1/2)+2kappa. Thus (33) also bounds the complete transition forcing. No missing odd index is hidden in the generic moving-range formula.

On the interval M<=n<=n_R, the fixed s_n^2 and gamma_n have lower bounds c eta^2. The endpoint raw forward and backward products are bounded by a constant depending on eta: each logarithmic step is O(1/(N eta)), so a product of at most N steps has logarithm O(1/eta), not O(N). Perturbing gamma_n to g_n(sigma) changes a logarithmic modulus by at most C epsilon/eta^2 per step. Therefore EVERY raw propagator over this interval is bounded by

Cηexp⁡(CNε/η2). C_\eta\exp(CN\varepsilon/\eta^2).\tag{34}

in either direction. Forcing denominators g_n or s_n^2 cost only fixed eta-dependent constants. No tau factor is divided out during this projected propagation.

The comparator J increases to its unique peak and decreases after it. For eta small, n_R/d lies to the right of that peak. Propagate from M up to the peak using (30), and backward from n_R down to the peak using (32). Each encountered boundary or forcing term has comparator rate no greater than the target J in the chosen direction. There are O(N) such terms. Equations (10), (33), and (34) prove the remaining ratio bound with exponent at most

max⁡{Ωlow,2κ+eR(η)}+Cε/η2. \max\{\Omega_{\mathrm{low}},2\kappa+e_R(\eta)\}+C\varepsilon/\eta^2.

For example all demands are bounded at rate

Integer rounding near the peak, b, or n_R costs o(1) by the closed continuity already used in (10) and (16).

Choose eta=epsilon^(1/4) for sufficiently small epsilon. Then epsilon<=c_0 eta^2, and every term of (35) tends to zero:

This proves (2). A monotone upper envelope may be used if desired.
