A complete-edge gap at every positive internal pair

Research proof, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The coupled complete-end pair equations improve the height bound to H<107/100H<107/100. On the resulting angular box the complete-edge action exceeds the entire radical’s growth by a fixed positive amount. Consequently the nonzero logarithmic asymptotic for the low complete-end kernel also holds for the full kernel, through both level parities, on a fixed complex neighborhood containing every selected angle of these pairs. The other outside endpoint remains complete.

1. The height bound uses the actual two incidences

Fix integer powers n,p≥3n,p\ge3, charges in [0,1]2[0,1]^2, and the positive geometric solution with one complete outside endpoint attached to each selected vertex. By the pair angle theorem, every active selected angle t=a−iHt=a-iH satisfies 0<a<π/2,H>0.(1) 0<a<\pi/2,\qquad H>0. \qquad\text{(1)} The cosine maximum principle also gives |cos⁡(u/2)|<1|\cos(u/2)|<1 at an active neighbor; a complete or zero-charge neighbor has cosine one.

The two original principal returns at tt are r0=asinh⁡(tan⁡(t/2)),r1=asinh⁡(tan⁡(t/2)cos⁡(u/2)).(2) r_0=\operatorname{asinh}(\tan(t/2)),\qquad r_1=\operatorname{asinh}(\tan(t/2)\cos(u/2)). \qquad\text{(2)} Their positive real parts obey ℜr0+ℜr1=nH/2\Re r_0+\Re r_1=nH/2. Since |tan⁡(t/2)|2=cosh⁡H−cos⁡acosh⁡H+cos⁡a<1,(3) |\tan(t/2)|^2 =\frac{\cosh H-\cos a}{\cosh H+\cos a}<1, \qquad\text{(3)} and |sinh⁡(k+ig)|2=sinh⁡2k+sin⁡2g|\sinh(k+ig)|^2=\sinh^2 k+\sin^2g, one has ℜr1<asinh⁡1\Re r_1<\operatorname{asinh}1. At fixed HH, the real part of r0r_0 increases with aa: its derivative is 12ℜsec⁡(t/2)>0\frac12\Re\sec(t/2)>0, on the branch continued from completeness. At a=π/2a=\pi/2 its value is exactly asinh⁡sech⁡H\operatorname{asinh}\sqrt{\operatorname{sech}H}. Therefore 3H2≤nH2<asinh⁡sech⁡H+asinh⁡1.(4) \frac{3H}{2}\le\frac{nH}{2} <\operatorname{asinh}\sqrt{\operatorname{sech}H} +\operatorname{asinh}1 . \qquad\text{(4)}

Proposition 1. Every active height in this family satisfies H<107/100.(5) H<107/100. \qquad\text{(5)} Indeed the right side of (4) decreases with HH. At h0=107/100h_0=107/100, cosh⁡(h0)sinh⁡2(18/25)>1,sinh⁡(441/500)>1,18/25+441/500=801/500<321/200=3h0/2.(6) \begin{split} \cosh(h_0)\sinh^2(18/25)&>1,\\ \sinh(441/500)&>1,\qquad 18/25+441/500=801/500<321/200=3h_0/2. \end{split} \qquad\text{(6)} All three comparisons are exact. For the first two it suffices to retain the terms through degree six of the positive cosh series and through degree seven of the positive sinh series. Thus the right side of (4) is less than 801/500801/500 if H≥h0H\ge h_0, a contradiction. Inactive angles are zero.

2. A uniform gap on the whole pair box

Let ℛ={a−iH:0≤a≤π/2,0≤H≤107/100}.(7) \mathcal R=\{a-iH:0\le a\le\pi/2,\ 0\le H\le107/100\}. \qquad\text{(7)} Keep the original complete-end phase, with its complete constant: ψ(t,0)=−4J(1/4+t/(4π))−4J(1/4−t/(4π)),J′(z)=Log⁡(2sin⁡πz),J(0)=0.(8) \psi(t,0)=-4J(1/4+t/(4\pi))-4J(1/4-t/(4\pi)), \quad J'(z)=\operatorname{Log}(2\sin\pi z),\quad J(0)=0. \qquad\text{(8)} All factorial arguments in (8) stay in their regular strips on a neighborhood of this rectangle.

Theorem 2. Uniformly on ℛ\mathcal R, ℜψ(a−iH,0)−H>1/500.(9) \Re\psi(a-iH,0)-H>1/500. \qquad\text{(9)}

3. The full complete-end kernel at both parities

Let 𝒦N(t)\mathcal K_N(t) be the original low complete-end factor, with indices 0≤j≤N−1−⌊N/2⌋0\le j\le N-1-\lfloor N/2\rfloor. The odd last-row theorem and even low-index comparison give on a fixed complex neighborhood of ℛ\mathcal R 𝒦N(t)=exp⁡{Nψ(t,0)+eN(t)},|∂tℓeN(t)|≤Cℓlog⁡N(16) \mathcal K_N(t)=\exp\{N\psi(t,0)+e_N(t)\},\qquad |\partial_t^\ell e_N(t)|\le C_\ell\log N \qquad\text{(16)} for every fixed ℓ\ell, after shrinking that neighborhood for derivative estimates. This use is legitimate because ℜ(−2cos⁡t)≤0\Re(-2\cos t)\le0 on ℛ\mathcal R.

Write DN(t)=KN(t,0)−𝒦N(t)D_N(t)=K_N(t,0)-\mathcal K_N(t). The original radical Fourier bound gives, at both parities, |DN(t)|≤N2e(N−1)|ℑt|.(17) |D_N(t)|\le N^2e^{(N-1)|\Im t|}. \qquad\text{(17)} At odd levels it is identically zero. This bound needs no restriction such as |ℑ(−2cos⁡t)|>1|\Im(-2\cos t)|>1.

Corollary 3. There is a fixed complex neighborhood 𝒱\mathcal V of ℛ\mathcal R on which, through all sufficiently large integer levels, KN(t,0)=exp⁡{Nψ(t,0)+êN(t)}≠0,|∂tℓêN(t)|≤Cℓlog⁡N,KN(t,0)/𝒦N(t)=1+O(e−cN)(18) \begin{split} K_N(t,0)&=\exp\{N\psi(t,0)+\widehat e_N(t)\}\ne0,\\ |\partial_t^\ell\widehat e_N(t)|&\le C_\ell\log N,\\ K_N(t,0)/\mathcal K_N(t)&=1+O(e^{-cN}) \end{split} \qquad\text{(18)} for some c>0c>0, with the same exponential relative error for every fixed derivative on smaller neighborhoods.

Proof. By compactness, (9) persists with a smaller positive gap for ℜψ(t,0)−|ℑt|\Re\psi(t,0)-|\Im t| on a complex neighborhood of ℛ\mathcal R. Equations (16)–(17) then bound |DN/𝒦N||D_N/\mathcal K_N| by a polynomial times a strictly decaying exponential, uniformly there. For all large levels this ratio has modulus less than 1/21/2. Define êN=eN+Log⁡(1+DN/𝒦N)\widehat e_N=e_N+\operatorname{Log}(1+D_N/\mathcal K_N) using the logarithm near zero. Cauchy’s formula on smaller neighborhoods proves all derivative statements. ▫\square

The normal derivatives here are in the selected ordinary spectral variable w=−2cos⁡tw=-2\cos t, on neighborhoods of its nonzero geometric selected angles. Since sin⁡t≠0\sin t\ne0 there, fixed ww-derivatives follow from (18). The other boundary argument of the kernel remains exactly complete. This theorem does not analytically continue the estimates to a fixed nonzero neighborhood in that outside argument.

In particular the local internal-pair Gaussian theorem may use the full original complete-end kernels at both parities. The global low-wire and trace cycle still needs its own bounds. There is no contradiction with the even-level bare-kernel counterexamples at homogeneous longer-path angles with ℜt>π/2\Re t>\pi/2, outside ℛ\mathcal R.
