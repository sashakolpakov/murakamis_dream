Factorial chambers for the actual Newton gates

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

The canonical factorial already has uniform asymptotics on compact subsets of both open half-planes, without a restriction on the real part. Near a real noninteger argument outside its original strip, the missing factors are explicit level-dependent Fourier factors. Retaining them gives a local meromorphic normal form for every algebraically regular multiplication or signed-crossing gate.

For any collection of nonconstant wrapped real-affine arguments, a common imaginary direction puts their Fourier factors and inverse factors in a pointed cone. Their joint constant Fourier coefficient is then exactly one, even if the affine forms are dependent. This concerns the distinguished sector of that specified chamber. A previously marked geometric action can differ by a Fourier character; its sector is not relabeled as the constant one. Nothing here computes the coefficient of the original integration cycle.

Fix an integer kk, a compact set in k<ℜξ<k+1k<\Re\xi<k+1, and a bounded complex offset aa. For τ=±1\tau=\pm1, extend JτJ_\tau to this strip from its indicated half-plane, using (4) and the original primitive at ξ−k\xi-k. At a real argument outside (0,1)(0,1), the factor in (9) cannot in general be dropped. Its zero or pole lattice is Nξ+a∈ℤN\xi+a\in\mathbb Z, with signed order kk. Nonvanishing of sin⁡(πξ)\sin(\pi\xi) excludes the macroscopic factorial wall, not this level-dependent lattice.

Exponentially small Fourier factors in one half-plane need not be small in the other one.

3. The literal multiplication and crossing factors

For actual index variables I,J,RI,J,R, retain the canonical interpolation of the original multiplication coefficient: μN(I,J,R)=GN(I+J)GN(R)GN(I)GN(J)GN(R−I)GN(R−J)GN(I+J−R).(13) \mu_N(I,J,R)= \frac{G_N(I+J)G_N(R)} {G_N(I)G_N(J)G_N(R-I)G_N(R-J)G_N(I+J-R)}. \qquad\text{(13)} On its original supported integer set this is the exact positive Newton multiplication table, including the original repeated-root algebra. The boundary operator Cσ,NC_{\sigma,N} is the transpose of (16). The three factorial arguments are x,y,x−yx,y,x-y, of multiplicities 1,−1,−11,-1,-1, with offsets aI,aJ,aI−aJa_I,a_J,a_I-a_J. At any point where all seven sines in (14), or all three crossing sines, are nonzero, choose a small fixed neighborhood. 4. Joint Fourier factors in a pointed cone

Let 𝒛∈ℂd\mathbf z\in\mathbb C^d be independent retained label coordinates, and let the wrapped arguments be real affine forms ℓj(𝒛)=𝒂j⋅𝒛+bj,𝒂j∈ℤd\{0},bj∈ℝ.(18) \ell_j(\mathbf z)=\mathbf a_j\cdot \mathbf z+b_j,\qquad \mathbf a_j\in\mathbb Z^d\setminus\{0\}, \quad b_j\in\mathbb R. \qquad\text{(18)} The actual gate forms (14), and their original integer complementary identifications, have this form. Write h=ℑ𝒛*h=\Im\mathbf z_* at the chart center. Choose a real vv with 𝒂j⋅v≠0\mathbf a_j\cdot v\ne0 for every form that is real there. For sufficiently small fixed ε>0\varepsilon>0, the vector v*=h+εvv_*=h+\varepsilon v has nonzero pairing with every 𝒂j\mathbf a_j, and those pairings preserve the signs of all nonreal arguments. Set τj=sgn⁡(𝒂j⋅v*),ej=νjkj.(19) \tau_j=\operatorname{sgn}(\mathbf a_j\cdot v_*), \qquad e_j=\nu_jk_j. \qquad\text{(19)} Thus the choice keeps every nonreal canonical half-plane branch and chooses a coherent side at the real arguments. Along 𝒛=𝒛*+itv*\mathbf z=\mathbf z_*+itv_*, t>0t>0 fixed and small, every exponential in (17) has modulus less than one for all sufficiently large NN, also with the actual bounded offsets. If all arguments are real at the center, hh is orthogonal to all their gradients and this reduces to a generic imaginary direction.

Theorem 2. Expand every factor of 𝔚N(𝒛)=∏j(1−eτj2πi(Nℓj(𝒛)+aj))ej(20) \mathfrak W_N(\mathbf z) =\prod_j (1-e^{\tau_j2\pi i(N\ell_j(\mathbf z)+a_j)})^{e_j} \qquad\text{(20)} in its convergent binomial series on this common side. This includes negative eje_j and arbitrary linear dependencies between the affine forms.

Proof. For integer ee, the series (1−u)e=∑r≥0(−1)r(er)ur(1-u)^e=\sum_{r\ge0}(-1)^r\binom er u^r is finite if e≥0e\ge0 and absolutely convergent for |u|<1|u|<1 if e<0e<0. Its constant term is one. Thus no nonzero tuple of choices can have total frequency zero. The tuple of all zero choices gives coefficient one.

The finite product converges normally on smaller regions where all |uj|<1|u_j|<1 uniformly. There are finitely many such tuples. Affine constants and literal offsets change their scalar coefficients, not their frequencies or this argument. ▫\square

All seven forms in (14), and all three crossing forms, are nonconstant in the retained labels of the actual gate, so the theorem applies directly to each gate and to any collection before imposing further identifications. It also applies after actual affine wire identifications whenever the wrapped forms remain nonconstant and have real affine constants in the remaining coordinates: choose the pointing vector in that coordinate space. Any form that becomes constant instead is retained as its explicit scalar factor in (20); its zero cannot be excluded by this Fourier argument. In particular an exterior parameter is not silently varied to make such a form nonconstant. With fixed complex affine constants, the same cone proof applies if a common pointing vector for the prescribed signs exists; its existence is not inferred from hh in that case.

Theorem 2 concerns 𝔚N\mathfrak W_N, not the Fourier expansion of the complete action, normalized remainder, or lattice kernel. It proves that the wall-transition factors do not cancel their specified zero-frequency sector. It does not say that all their other coefficients are nonzero.

5. Positive prefixes and the retained geometric mark

The original positive-prefix theorem proves that all seven arguments of every proper multiplication gate and all three arguments of a signed crossing have nonzero sines at its specified positive primitive point. The construction leaves one incident factor for the final complementary pairing; it does not treat that final pairing as a strict multiplication gate.

Consequently every gate in that algebraically regular resolution has a local factorial description (17): use a half-plane chart at a nonreal argument and a real-chamber chart otherwise. This fills the local analytic description without assuming that all arguments have real part between zero and one. It retains every NN-dependent Fourier factor and does not assert a nonzero unfactored symbol at a real out-of-support argument. The exact complementary pairing and its N−1N-1 reversal stay unchanged.

A geometric primitive already marked by continuation must also stay unchanged. Here is the branch bookkeeping explicitly. For a single argument, two continued primitives with the same original normalization can differ locally by 2πi(wξ−h),w,h∈ℤ.(22) 2\pi i(w\xi-h),\qquad w,h\in\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(22)} Indeed continuation of log⁡(2sin⁡πξ)\log(2\sin\pi\xi) around its simple zero at an integer rr adds 2πi2\pi i; its primitive changes by 2πi(ξ−r)2\pi i(\xi-r). Composing finitely many signed loops gives (22). A change of derivative branch also changes the offset prefactor by the exact constant exp⁡{2πi(a+12)w}\exp\{2\pi i(a+\tfrac12)w\}.

For several arguments, if the previously marked phase is Sgeom(𝒛)=Schamber(𝒛)+2πi(λ⋅𝒛+γ),(23) S_{\mathrm{ geom}}(\mathbf z) =S_{\mathrm{ chamber}}(\mathbf z) +2\pi i(\lambda\cdot\mathbf z+\gamma), \qquad\text{(23)} retain that character and its associated offset constants. This includes any Fourier character already present in the spectral–Newton adapter, such as its signed-crossing shift −2πiσx-2\pi i\sigma x; it is not restricted to new factorial monodromy. Relative to eNSgeome^{NS_{\mathrm{ geom}}}, the Fourier factor in (17) is multiplied by e−2πiN(λ⋅𝒛+γ)e^{-2\pi iN(\lambda\cdot\mathbf z+\gamma)}. Thus the wall-factor coefficient associated to this geometric mark comes from its coefficient at frequency λ\lambda, not automatically from frequency zero. Theorem 2 identifies it with the nonzero constant coefficient only when the character has the corresponding zero frequency; it does not remove a nonzero λ\lambda.

Even when that local branch agrees, the original finite support cycle, the other Fourier modes, endpoints, shared-wire contractions and first-normal closing terms remain. No intersection number or full geometric contribution is deduced from the factorial chamber or its unit remainder.
