A character metric for the actual whole-meridian peripheral action

Written proof, 23 November 2026. The actual reduced knot family has a positive whole-meridian metric in which both fundamental peripheral actions have norm at most two. Evaluation at the complete color has an explicit polynomial dual norm on every finite Laurent-degree space. These bounds retain the whole family and its meridian normals. They do not give a lower bound for its original marked scalar.

1. The actual source and its boundary action

Fix N≥2N\ge2, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and the marked meridian M=−qxM=-qx. Let FN(M)F_N(M) be the original reduced, zero-framed ADO knot source, with unknot value one and complete value KN=FN(1)K_N=F_N(1). The complete sources [P] and [W] prove, in these conventions, FN(M−1)=FN(M),ΛF=−f(qM)F(qM)+f(q−1M)F(q−1M)f(M),𝖴F=(M+M−1)F,f=M−M−1.(1) F_N(M^{-1})=F_N(M),\qquad \Lambda F=-\frac{f(qM)F(qM)+f(q^{-1}M)F(q^{-1}M)}{f(M)}, \qquad \mathsf U F=(M+M^{-1})F,\quad f=M-M^{-1}. \qquad\text{(1)} Here Λ\Lambda adds the specified zero-framed fundamental longitude, and 𝖴\mathsf U adds the specified fundamental meridian. Equation (1) holds for every intermediate link insertion, with word order retained. It is a whole-meridian identity at the fixed root qq.

By [J], FNF_N is a Laurent polynomial; (1) supplies its Weyl symmetry. We work on the full ladder 𝒫=ℂ[M,M−1]M↦M−1,𝒫D={F∈𝒫:supp⁡MF⊂[−D,D]}.(2) \mathscr P=\mathbb C[M,M^{-1}]^{M\mapsto M^{-1}},\qquad \mathscr P_D=\{F\in\mathscr P:\operatorname{supp}_M F\subset[-D,D]\}. \qquad\text{(2)} The original knot family is also even in MM. Fundamental meridian insertions can change that parity, so the ladder (2) includes both parities. No exponents are identified modulo NN or 2N2N.

The actual full modified period is still 𝒩N(M)=dN(M)FN(M),dN(M)=iN+3M−M−1MN−M−N,𝒩N(1)=iN+3NKN.(3) \mathcal N_N(M)=d_N(M)F_N(M),\qquad d_N(M)=i^{N+3}\frac{M-M^{-1}}{M^N-M^{-N}},\qquad \mathcal N_N(1)=\frac{i^{N+3}}N K_N. \qquad\text{(3)} The metric below is placed on the reduced Laurent family. It does not replace the meromorphic multiplier in (3).

2. A simultaneous bounded metric

For n≥0n\ge0, put χn(M)=Mn+1−M−n−1M−M−1=∑r=0nMn−2r.(4) \chi_n(M)=\frac{M^{n+1}-M^{-n-1}}{M-M^{-1}} =\sum_{r=0}^n M^{n-2r}. \qquad\text{(4)} These Laurent polynomials form a basis of 𝒫\mathscr P, since the leading coefficient in degree nn is one. Define ∥F∥H2=2π∫0π|F(eiθ)|2sin⁡2θdθ.(5) \|F\|_{\mathrm H}^2 =\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi |F(e^{i\theta})|^2\sin^2\theta\,d\theta. \qquad\text{(5)} This is the usual character Haar metric. The proof needed here is elementary: χn(eiθ)sin⁡θ=sin⁡((n+1)θ)\chi_n(e^{i\theta})\sin\theta=\sin((n+1)\theta), and integration of products of sines gives ∥∑n=0Danχn∥H2=∑n=0D|an|2.(6) \left\|\sum_{n=0}^D a_n\chi_n\right\|_{\mathrm H}^2 =\sum_{n=0}^D|a_n|^2. \qquad\text{(6)} In particular the zeros of the density at the endpoints do not make this a degenerate norm on Laurent polynomials.

Multiplying (4) by ff, applying the shifts in (1), and collecting the two antisymmetric monomials gives exactly Λχn=−(qn+1+q−n−1)χn,𝖴χn=χn+1+χn−1,χ−1=0.(7) \Lambda\chi_n=-(q^{n+1}+q^{-n-1})\chi_n, \qquad \mathsf U\chi_n=\chi_{n+1}+\chi_{n-1},\quad \chi_{-1}=0. \qquad\text{(7)} Thus the apparent quotients at M=±1M=\pm1 in (1) cancel in the Laurent-polynomial ring, before any endpoint evaluation. Since |qn+1+q−n−1|≤2|q^{n+1}+q^{-n-1}|\le2, (6) proves ∥ΛF∥H≤2∥F∥H,∥𝖴F∥H≤2∥F∥H.(8) \|\Lambda F\|_{\mathrm H}\le2\|F\|_{\mathrm H},\qquad \|\mathsf U F\|_{\mathrm H}\le2\|F\|_{\mathrm H}. \qquad\text{(8)} For the second inequality one can also use the multiplication formula 𝖴=2cos⁡θ\mathsf U=2\cos\theta directly in (5). Both operators extend to bounded self-adjoint operators on the Hilbert completion. The finite degree maps are Λ:𝒫D→𝒫D\Lambda:\mathscr P_D\to\mathscr P_D and 𝖴:𝒫D→𝒫D+1\mathsf U:\mathscr P_D\to\mathscr P_{D+1}; no upper-degree compression is part of this statement.

Let 𝒲\mathcal W be an ordered word of length ss in these two actual peripheral operations, with mm meridian letters. Repeated application of (1) identifies its result with the actual descendant source, and (8) gives FN𝒲=𝒲FN∈𝒫D+m,∥FN𝒲∥H≤2s∥FN∥H(FN∈𝒫D).(9) F_N^{\mathcal W}=\mathcal W F_N\in\mathscr P_{D+m},\qquad \|F_N^{\mathcal W}\|_{\mathrm H}\le2^s\|F_N\|_{\mathrm H} \quad(F_N\in\mathscr P_D). \qquad\text{(9)} For a finite linear combination ∑wcw𝒲w\sum_w c_w\mathcal W_w, the corresponding cost is ∑w|cw|2|w|\sum_w|c_w|2^{|w|}. It is independent of the level for a fixed peripheral expression. There is no claim of such independence for expressions whose length grows with NN.

3. The original complete-color functional

On 𝒫D\mathscr P_D, complete evaluation is ev⁡1F=∑n=0D(n+1)an,CD2:=∥ev⁡1∥2=∑n=0D(n+1)2=(D+1)(D+2)(2D+3)6.(10) \operatorname{ev}_1 F=\sum_{n=0}^D(n+1)a_n,\qquad C_D^2:=\|\operatorname{ev}_1\|^2 =\sum_{n=0}^D(n+1)^2 =\frac{(D+1)(D+2)(2D+3)}6. \qquad\text{(10)} Cauchy–Schwarz proves the upper bound, and the vector ∑n=0D(n+1)χn\sum_{n=0}^D(n+1)\chi_n proves equality. Consequently the actual marked descendants obey |FN𝒲(1)|≤2sCD+m∥FN∥H.(11) |F_N^{\mathcal W}(1)| \le 2^s C_{D+m}\|F_N\|_{\mathrm H}. \qquad\text{(11)} In particular this uses the original KN=FN(1)K_N=F_N(1), without changing the closing to a discrete positive-color average.

For completeness, fixed-root meridian normals have polynomial dual bounds too. Let ∂u=M∂M\partial_u=M\partial_M. Each monomial in (4) has exponent of absolute value at most nn, whence, for j≥0j\ge0, sup|M|=1|∂ujF(M)|≤DjCD∥F∥H,F∈𝒫D,(12) \sup_{|M|=1}|\partial_u^j F(M)| \le D^j C_D\|F\|_{\mathrm H},\qquad F\in\mathscr P_D, \qquad\text{(12)} where D0=1D^0=1, including D=0D=0. For D=0,j>0D=0,j>0 the derivative is zero. Thus whole-meridian data are retained rather than being replaced by root-orbit values. In the odd normal lattice of [P], wN=fFN,wN(1)=0,KN=12∂uwN(1).(13) w_N=fF_N,\qquad w_N(1)=0,\qquad K_N=\tfrac12\partial_u w_N(1). \qquad\text{(13)} Indeed ∂uf(1)=2\partial_u f(1)=2. This is the same original normal functional, now evaluated on its full Laurent representative.

There is also a direct comparison with the full-circle Fourier norm ∥F∥c2=(2π)−1∫02π|F(eiθ)|2dθ\|F\|_{\mathrm c}^2=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}|F(e^{i\theta})|^2d\theta. Weyl symmetry and (5) give ∥fF∥c2=2∥F∥H2\|fF\|_{\mathrm c}^2=2\|F\|_{\mathrm H}^2. Since ∥χn∥c2=n+1\|\chi_n\|_{\mathrm c}^2=n+1, the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz, together with |f|≤2|f|\le2, give 12∥F∥H≤∥F∥c≤(D+1)(D+2)2∥F∥H.(14) \frac1{\sqrt2}\|F\|_{\mathrm H} \le\|F\|_{\mathrm c} \le\sqrt{\frac{(D+1)(D+2)}2}\,\|F\|_{\mathrm H}. \qquad\text{(14)} Thus the new metric differs from the whole Fourier coefficient norm by at most a polynomial in the actual degree. This comparison is with the full Fourier ladder, not with aliased samples on one orbit.

4. The quantitative source consequence and its limit

Fix a bb-strand braid presentation of length ℓ\ell, writhe 𝔴\mathfrak w, and one closed component. The complete proof [J, Section 1] derives from the literal canceled crossing entries FN(M)=fN(M2),supp⁡fN⊂[−AN,AN],A=max⁡(1,⌈3ℓ+|b−1−𝔴|2⌉).(15) F_N(M)=f_N(M^2),\qquad \operatorname{supp} f_N\subset[-AN,AN],\qquad A=\max\!\left(1,\left\lceil\frac{3\ell+|b-1-\mathfrak w|}{2} \right\rceil\right). \qquad\text{(15)} Hence D≤2AND\le2AN is valid for the original source at every level. For every fixed peripheral word and fixed meridian derivative order, (11)–(12) have only polynomial level cost: |FN𝒲(1)|≤CA,𝒲N3/2∥FN∥H,|∂ujFN𝒲(1)|≤CA,𝒲,jNj+3/2∥FN∥H.(16) |F_N^{\mathcal W}(1)|\le C_{A,\mathcal W}N^{3/2}\|F_N\|_{\mathrm H}, \qquad |\partial_u^jF_N^{\mathcal W}(1)| \le C_{A,\mathcal W,j}N^{j+3/2}\|F_N\|_{\mathrm H}. \qquad\text{(16)} All original peripheral framing and pivotal factors are those already fixed in (1). For the full modified period, its complete value has the additional explicit multiplier iN+3/Ni^{N+3}/N from (3).

The polynomial and its finite jets have not been quotiented by a root-of-unity vanishing ideal. Such a quotient can forget alias and wall data even though it retains all root values. Likewise no orthogonal compression of the character ladder has been inserted into the actual peripheral composition.

The character basis and Haar metric are classical. The quantitative conclusion here is their simultaneous application to the actual normalization-matched whole-meridian source, its peripheral words, and its specified complete-color functional. The bounded actions and polynomial dual cost are upper estimates. They give neither a lower bound for KNK_N, nor a comparison of this whole-meridian norm with the norm of the original finite all-color value orbit. Such a comparison would require control of the retained alias and normal channels. No geometric contribution or volume limit is inferred.

Everything here holds at fixed q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}. Meridian derivatives in (12)–(13) are not off-root quantum-normal derivatives. The different normal paths, pivotal derivatives, and endpoint corrections in the complete-color divided Ward source remain separate data.
