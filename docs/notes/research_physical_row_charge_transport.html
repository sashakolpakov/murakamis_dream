Positive physical-row charge transport and endpoint-window stability

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

An original kernel evaluated at a physical color of index aa has a positive Fourier law supported on [−a,a][-a,a]. Inside a surviving full trace, this is an exact positive convolution of the original charge parameter. Its coefficient mass costs O(alog⁡(N/a))O(a\log(N/a)), and its charge shifts cost a/Na/N. This gives a quantitative endpoint-window stability lemma for any supplied uniform charged descendant estimate. It does not supply that estimate or an arbitrary-short-filling theorem.

4. A usable endpoint-window lemma

Consider the omitted-row source of the adopted outer-row theorem. At each v∈Uv\in U its exact weight is Ωv,av\Omega_{v,a_v}, allowing a fixed number of shared microscopic endpoint coupons with |α|≤A<π|\alpha|\le A<\pi. All the other original factors are as in (14). In particular |Ωv,av|≤CN3/2.(18) |\Omega_{v,a_v}|\le C N^{3/2}. \qquad\text{(18)} Start with unit charge at every traced vertex, and retain the original integer powers. Restrict the exact row sum to 0≤av≤εN0\le a_v\le\varepsilon N, also respecting its original tail support. Call that restricted contribution TU,NεT_{U,N}^\varepsilon.

Endpoint-window lemma. Suppose there are r0,Δ>0r_0,\Delta>0, a reference rate MM, and constants C,pC,p such that the actual descendant sources in (14) satisfy |ZG′,NAN,𝒂(𝒏Y,𝝌)|≤CNpeN(M−Δ)for χw∈(1+N−1ℤ)∩[1−r0,1],(19) \left|Z_{G',N}^{A_{N,\mathbf a}} (\mathbf n_Y,\boldsymbol\chi)\right| \le C N^p e^{N(M-\Delta)} \quad\text{for }\chi_w\in(1+N^{-1}\mathbb Z)\cap[1-r_0,1], \qquad\text{(19)} uniformly over all physical parameters 𝒂\mathbf a in the indicated window and all retained insertions. Then, whenever dε≤r0d\varepsilon\le r_0, |TU,Nε|≤C′Np+dU+(5/2)|U|eN[M−Δ+dUh(ε)].(20) |T_{U,N}^\varepsilon| \le C' N^{p+d_U+(5/2)|U|} e^{N[M-\Delta+d_Uh(\varepsilon)]}. \qquad\text{(20)} In particular a fixed sufficiently small ε>0\varepsilon>0 preserves half the supplied margin: dUh(ε)≤Δ/2⇒|TU,Nε|≤C′Np′eN(M−Δ/2).(21) d_Uh(\varepsilon)\le\Delta/2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |T_{U,N}^\varepsilon|\le C' N^{p'} e^{N(M-\Delta/2)}. \qquad\text{(21)} The same choice can serve the finitely many evaluated subsets of a fixed graph, if (19) holds uniformly for them.

Proof. Apply the exact unit-charge reflection after (17). Equation (14) then evaluates the source only at the lattice charge window in (19). The expectation has total mass one, so (19) bounds it without replacing the descendant by a physical norm. Apply (16), multiply by (18), and sum at most N|U|N^{|U|} physical assignments. The original evaluated crossing values have modulus one at real, in particular integer, powers. This proves (20); h(ε)→0h(\varepsilon)\to0 proves (21). ▫\square

This is a stability input for an induction whose descendant estimates are already available uniformly in charge. It does not infer (19) from a bound known only at 𝝌=𝟏\boldsymbol\chi=\mathbf1, from classical action concavity, or from a nonzero boundary state. Other endpoint windows and vertical currents remain.

5. The retained integer phase and the limit of absolute estimates

For odd integer nn the linear character is one; for even integer nn it is (−1)a(-1)^a. The quadratic phase remains in both cases. For a shared product, each summand of its exact weight retains this character; its other physical row factors are unchanged. No integer-only identity has been differentiated in nn.

6. Coverage and direct sources

The following earlier constructions were checked for overlap: the complete-source Pick observation treats a Cauchy observation of the complete row; the entropy Fourier embedding represents a classical entropy defect; the shared-cycle Fourier compiler uses positive magnetic-polynomial coefficients with matching flows and the original cycle characters. The present additional input is the positive physical-row law, its support and coefficient-mass control, and the resulting endpoint-window stability estimate.
