An unconditional finite q-Chu reduction of the actual five-crossing source

28 October 2026. The actual three-index period in the whole-source calculation has a two-index whole-meridian reduction by elementary finite q-binomial identities. This reduction does not assume the candidate Newton coefficients in its Section 6. The further transform is now proved in the full cyclotomic proof; this note records the independent double-sum route.

Write q=Q here (the Gaussian base, not the half-root), d=N-1, F_a=(q;q)_a, and H_a(z)=sum_j {a choose j}_q z^j. Every denominator below has index less than N.

1. A reusable terminating three-factor transform

For k with F_1,...,F_k nonzero, and arbitrary a,b, exactly

sum_(ell=0)^k (q^-k; q)_ell (a;q)_ell (b;q)_ell
                                  q^ell/F_ell
   =sum_(r=0)^k {k choose r}_q
                            a^(k-r)b^r(a;q)_r.   (1)

This is a polynomial identity in a,b, without a genericity condition on their Pochhammer factors.

First prove the finite q-Chu identity

sum_(s=0)^n (q^-n;q)_s(a;q)_s q^s/F_s=a^n. (2)

Expand (a;q)_s by the finite q-binomial theorem and interchange the finite sums. For its coefficient with index r the remaining sum is

sum_(t=0)^(n-r)(q^(-(n-r));q)_t q^t/F_t
                              =(q^(1-(n-r));q)_(n-r).

The last equality is the same finite q-binomial theorem. It is zero unless r=n, because otherwise one factor is 1-q^0. The r=n term simplifies to a^n, proving (2).

To prove (1), expand (b;q)_ell, put ell=r+s, and use (2) with length k-r and parameter a q^r. The inner sum is (a q^r)^(k-r). Substitute

(q^-k;q)_r/F_r=(-1)^r q^(-kr+r(r-1)/2){k choose r}_q.

All signs and q powers cancel, leaving exactly (1).

2. Applying it to the ORIGINAL triple source

Start with the proved actual source formula

F_N(Y)=sum_(m+ell<=d)sum_(j=0)^m
   Y^(2(d-m))q^(j(m+1)+m+ell+2)
   {m+ell choose ell}_q {m choose j}_q
   (q/Y;q)_(m+ell)(q^(j+1)/Y;q)_ell.        (3)

Replace j by m-j and put k=d-m. At a primitive Nth root, q^(m+1)=q^-k and q^(m(m+2)+2)=q^(k^2+1). The ell sum is the left side of (1) with

a=q^-k/Y, b=q^(-k-j)/Y.

Its r-th term is Y^-k q^(-k^2-jr){k choose r}(q^-k/Y;q)_r. The prefactor in (3) consequently simplifies to q Y^k. Set n=k-r and use the exact contiguous product

(q/Y;q)_(d-k)(q^-k/Y;q)_(k-n)
                                    =(q/Y;q)_(d-n),

which follows from q^N=1 before any Y evaluation. One obtains

F_N(Y)=q sum_(0<=n<=k<=d)
      Y^k {k choose n}_q (q/Y;q)_(d-n)
                              H_(d-k)(q^n).       (4)

This is already an exact simpler finite source transform. It preserves arbitrary Y and all of its meridian derivatives.

3. One more finite q-binomial sum gives a DOUBLE sum

Expand H in (4). Put k=n+r and d-k=j+s, so n+r+j+s=d. The root reflection identity gives

F_(n+r) F_(j+s)
    =N(-1)^(j+s)q^((j+s)(j+s+1)/2).        (5)

Fix n,j and set L=d-n-j=r+s. The remaining r,s sum is

sum_(s=0)^L (-1)^s q^(s(s+1)/2+js)Y^-s
                                  /(F_s F_(L-s))
                       =(q^(j+1)/Y;q)_L/F_L.     (6)

Equation (6) is the ordinary finite q-binomial theorem. Thus the whole actual reduced scalar has the unconditional formula

F_N(Y)=Nq sum_(n,j>=0,n+j<=d)
   (-1)^j q^(j(j+1)/2+nj) Y^(d-j)
   (q/Y;q)_(d-n)(q^(j+1)/Y;q)_(d-n-j)
                 /[F_n F_j F_(d-n-j)].     (7)

The factor N in (5)-(7) is the exact full root factorial, not a normalization chosen after evaluating Y. The original Cartan, turning, cut and framing factors are already included in (3), so remain included in (7). The separate full ADO modified-dimension factor multiplies both unchanged.

Equivalently, with k=d-n, (7) is

Nq Y^d sum_(k=0)^d (q/Y;q)_k^2/[F_(d-k)F_k]
   * sum_(j=0)^k (q^-k;q)_j Y^-j
                                  /[F_j(q/Y;q)_j]. (8)

Formula (8) is initially read after cancelling Pochhammer denominators termwise back to (7); no division at a Y source zero is implied.

4. Relation to the proved cyclotomic formula

Putting t=q^-1, the proved cyclotomic form in the original note is

F_N(Y) =sum_(n=0)^d t^n(tY;t)_n(t/Y;t)_n
                   sum_(j=0)^n t^(j(j+1)){n choose j}_t. (9)

The inner normalized coefficient is the elementary Bailey convolution sum_j t^(j^2+j)/[F_j(t)F_(n-j)(t)]. Equations (1)-(8) give an independent double-sum expression. The full proof instead uses the symmetric three-factor expansion, a generic Newton connection, and an exact root Rogers-polynomial convolution to prove (9). Repeated ordinary-color nodes are not used as an interpolation proof.

Exact audit checks (1) at generic finite parameters and compares (3), (4), (7), and the original literal source at primitive roots with unrestricted whole-meridian evaluations. The displayed finite identities, not the samples, establish their scope.
