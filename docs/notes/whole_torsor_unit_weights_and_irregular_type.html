Whole torsor units, level weights, and the finite jet depth for a norm

Independent function-level theorem, 27 October 2026. There is a sharp distinction between a fixed algebraic comparison section and a horizontal period on a fixed finite-type algebraic connection. A classically normalized algebraic unit can change a leading regulator rate when the level character amplifies its first unseen jet. Conversely a finite jet depth suffices once that character weight is bounded. These are statements about whole functions, not independently chosen levelwise tails.

They complement exact cyclotomic Mellin uniqueness. No modification below is asserted to preserve the actual knot source.

1. A sharp unit/weight theorem

Let tN=1/Nt_N=1/N. Suppose holomorphic units uj(t)u_j(t) on one common marked neighborhood satisfy, uniformly in their index, uj(t)=1+O(tk),k≥1. u_j(t)=1+O(t^k),\qquad k\ge1. The number of factors may depend on NN. For integer characters mN,jm_{N,j} assume the total weight bound ∑j|mN,j|≤CNd. \sum_j |m_{N,j}|\le C N^d . Then the whole scalar comparison aN=∏juj(tN)mN,j a_N=\prod_j u_j(t_N)^{m_{N,j}} satisfies |log⁡|aN||≤C′Nd−k,1Nlog⁡|aN|=O(Nd−k−1). |\log|a_N||\le C' N^{d-k},\qquad \frac1N\log|a_N|=O(N^{d-k-1}). \tag{1} Thus it has subexponential norm whenever k≥dk\ge d.

Proof: choose the logarithm with log⁡uj(0)=0\log u_j(0)=0. Uniformity and the convergent logarithm imply |log⁡uj(t)|≤C″|t|k|\log u_j(t)|\le C''|t|^k on a smaller common neighborhood. Sum these bounds with the absolute character weights. Both positive and negative exponents are covered. In particular (1) bounds the comparison and its inverse, so multiplying a nonzero selected scalar by it preserves every existing leading exponential rate. It does not estimate an internal sum with cancelling coefficients.

The threshold is sharp for d≥2d\ge2. If one unit has log⁡u(t)=αtd−1+O(td)\log u(t)=\alpha t^{d-1}+O(t^d) and mN/Nd→κm_N/N^d\to\kappa, then 1Nlog⁡|u(tN)mN|→κRe⁡α. \frac1N\log|u(t_N)^{m_N}| \longrightarrow\kappa\,\operatorname{Re}\alpha . \tag{2} For a quadratic character weight O(N2)O(N^2), agreement through the first physical-arc Taylor coefficient, u=1+O(t2)u=1+O(t^2), therefore suffices. Classical agreement u(0)=1u(0)=1 alone does not.

The uniform remainder and total weight are substantive hypotheses. They cannot be inferred from separate algebraicity at each level or from a common classical value.

2. A single algebraic unit can change the leading rate

Put c=2πic=2\pi i, QN=ec/NQ_N=e^{c/N}, and take the whole rational unit u(Q)=1+i(Q−1)∈ℚ(i)(Q),u(1)=1. u(Q)=1+i(Q-1)\in\mathbb Q(i)(Q),\qquad u(1)=1. For all sufficiently large NN, it is nonzero and log⁡u(ect)=−2πt+O(t2). \log u(e^{ct})=-2\pi t+O(t^2). Consequently limN→∞1Nlog⁡|u(QN)N2|=−2π. \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N \log|u(Q_N)^{N^2}|=-2\pi . \tag{3} Its inverse has the opposite rate. There is also a rational-coefficient example: u(Q)=(1+Q)/2u(Q)=(1+Q)/2. 3. Finite type of a connection is not bounded algebraic section complexity

The preceding amplified functions occur on a fixed finite-type algebraic connection. Work over the punctured tt-base and the open where u(Q)≠0u(Q)\ne0. Introduce coordinates L,WL,W, with W≠0W\ne0, and the algebraic differential laws dL=duu,dlog⁡W=t−dduu−dt−d−1Ldt. dL=\frac{du}{u},\qquad d\log W=t^{-d}\frac{du}{u} -d\,t^{-d-1}L\,dt . \tag{6} This is a full flat connection: on its horizontal sections the right side for dlog⁡Wd\log W is d(t−dL)d(t^{-d}L). Equivalently the two mixed derivatives commute directly. There is no claim that the naive product translation on L,WL,W makes (6) a regular principal connection at t=0t=0.

On the marked branch L=Log⁡uL=\operatorname{Log} u, its horizontal period is W=exp⁡(t−dLog⁡u). W=\exp(t^{-d}\operatorname{Log} u). Restricting t=1/Nt=1/N gives exactly u(Q)Ndu(Q)^{N^d}. The connection has fixed algebraic rank and presentation, while its marked horizontal period is not generally an algebraic function of the base. Its singularity at t=0t=0 retains the large character and cannot be omitted.

Therefore a claim of finite-type algebraic torsor or connection does not by itself supply the bounded algebraic comparison SECTION required by the existing reconstruction and norm theorems. Conversely (1) identifies a finite sufficient jet depth when the relevant character degrees are known.

This does not refute the flat pointed-comparison lifting lemma: (6) is singular at the classical boundary and does not give a regular pointed comparison there. It also does not preserve the actual finite-level source equation or its pointing. Such source conditions are precisely what can exclude (3)–(5).

4. Which jets and which functions

The three distinct uniqueness assertions should remain separate:

  • If two functions are holomorphic at the same ordinary point and all their Taylor coefficients agree, they are the same analytic germ. Marked continuation then fixes them on a connected common continuation domain.
  • Equality of asymptotic formal power series at an irregular puncture is weaker. Exponentially flat sectorial differences can remain, as proved in the earlier function audit.
  • Varying finite-dimensional root fibers are not one analytic function unless a whole family has been constructed. Here exact source and pole conditions are decisive: the bounded-jet Mellin theorem proves equality of the entire rational root function from orders 0,…,D(s−1)0,\ldots,D(s-1), in its specified finite X-window and cyclotomic numerator lattice.

The Taylor order in (1) is along the physical arc t↦(t,Q=ect)t\mapsto(t,Q=e^{ct}) at t=0t=0. It must not be confused with a quantum-normal Taylor derivative at a fixed root Q=QN(1+ϵ)Q=Q_N(1+\epsilon), where NN is held fixed. Exact root-normal source identities and bounded whole presentations can relate these data; classical shape etaleness alone cannot.

5. Consequence for the optimizer/torsor proposal

The algebraic Rivin optimizer fixes the chosen shape branch and its local critical field. A marked Bloch primitive then fixes the geometric action, with its original peripheral signs. The new doubled-phase optimizer also gives an algebraic positive Hessian. These are meaningful whole geometric data.

What remains is a source-compatible comparison, not another classical uniqueness argument. One concrete sufficient route is now finite: construct a candidate whole root Mellin function with the ACTUAL cyclotomic numerator lattice and ACTUAL source equation through the fixed quantum order of the bounded-jet theorem. That theorem then identifies it exactly with the selected actual root function, including its residue.

To infer its norm from a regulator factor, its remaining comparison units must either belong to the bounded algebraic section class already covered by norm control, or satisfy an explicit level-weight estimate such as (1). Finite-type period connections and classical unit normalization alone are insufficient by (3)–(6). No existence of that comparison, selected geometric coefficient, global dominance, or volume limit is claimed here.

The complementary doubled-phase theorem constructs the whole classical period connection explicitly, and the critical-source theorem gives a finite criterion for a genuinely descended source density with polynomial norm bounds. Their proofs are recorded in the independent audit.
