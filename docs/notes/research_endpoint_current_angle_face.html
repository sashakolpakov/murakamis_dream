When an endpoint current has an original angle-boundary action

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

The exact grouped endpoint current deletes vertices and decorates the remaining forest with full Ward coupons. Its deleted-forest action is not automatically a boundary value of the original constrained angle problem. There is an explicit necessary and sufficient condition for the endpoint-pinned boundary realization that preserves the surviving angle data. When it holds, the action comparison is exact and the original convex minimum gives a strict gap.

The condition can fail at positive primitive fillings. Its failure does not prove that the current has a larger action; it identifies why the proposed boundary-value proof cannot cover all currents. No quantum contour or scalar asymptotic is inferred from the angle comparison alone.

1. The original polytope and the deleted angle assignment

Let GG be the original finite circle tree, with selected vertices SS, fixed full outside angles tb=sb+ihbt_b=s_b+i h_b, and signed integer powers nvn_v. Include every edge incident to SS, counted once, and suppose each selected vertex has positive original degree. The full primitive equations are assumed to have a positive solution in the original edge decomposition.

Use the original real angle variables sv=ℜtvs_v=\Re t_v and gv,e=ℑrv,eg_{v,e}=\Im r_{v,e}. The closed angle polytope is given by |sv|+2|gw,e|≤π,|sw|+2|gv,e|≤π(e={v,w}),(1) |s_v|+2|g_{w,e}|\leq\pi,\qquad |s_w|+2|g_{v,e}|\leq\pi \quad(e=\{v,w\}), \qquad\text{(1)} ∑e∋vgv,e−nv2sv=−π(v∈S).(2) \sum_{e\ni v}g_{v,e}-\frac{n_v}{2}s_v=-\pi \quad(v\in S). \qquad\text{(2)} The prescribed outside sbs_b’s are unchanged. The original energy is ℰG=4∑e(EP,e+EQ,e)−∑b∉Shb∑e∋bgb,e.(3) \mathcal E_G =4\sum_e(E_{P,e}+E_{Q,e}) -\sum_{b\notin S}h_b\sum_{e\ni b}g_{b,e}. \qquad\text{(3)} The square coordinates and their energy are those of the pinned convex variation theorem. In particular, if vv is the first endpoint, AP=sv/4−gw,e/2,AQ=sv/4+gw,e/2,yP=gv,e/2+sw/4,yQ=gv,e/2−sw/4.(4) A_P=s_v/4-g_{w,e}/2,\quad A_Q=s_v/4+g_{w,e}/2,\qquad y_P=g_{v,e}/2+s_w/4,\quad y_Q=g_{v,e}/2-s_w/4. \qquad\text{(4)}

Let z0z_0 be the positive full solution, mm the number of included edges, and MGM_G its calibrated real action. The existing theorems give MG=mM∞−ℰG(z0)/π,ΔG=min∂𝒫GℰG−ℰG(z0)>0.(5) M_G=mM_\infty-\mathcal E_G(z_0)/\pi,\qquad \Delta_G=\min_{\partial\mathcal P_G}\mathcal E_G -\mathcal E_G(z_0)>0. \qquad\text{(5)} Here M∞=ψ(0,0)=4GCat/πM_\infty=\psi(0,0)=4G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/\pi, where GCat=∑j≥0(−1)j/(2j+1)2G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}=\sum_{j\geq0}(-1)^j/(2j+1)^2. The boundary and interior are relative to the original affine constraint space.

Fix a nonempty removed set C⊆SC\subseteq S. Delete every edge incident to CC, and let rr be the number of deleted edges. Retain an angle assignment z′z' on the resulting forest that satisfies its weak edge inequalities and all its surviving primitive rows, with |sw|≤π|s_w|\leq\pi at every surviving vertex, including isolated ones. All surviving real angles, incidence variables and outside data are prescribed by z′z'. An isolated surviving vertex must still satisfy its row (2) with an empty incidence sum; it is not silently removed. If these conditions have no solution, there is no assignment z′z' to which the theorem applies. A degenerate assignment is given only its angle-energy value (16), not a geometric or quantum action without further justification.

For a removed vertex let dC(v)=#{e={v,w}:w∉C},Lv(z′)=∑e={v,w}w∉C(1−|sw|/π).(6) d_C(v)=\#\{e=\{v,w\}:w\notin C\},\qquad L_v(z')=\sum_{\substack{e=\{v,w\}\\w\notin C}} (1-|s_w|/\pi). \qquad\text{(6)} All incidences in this sum are counted individually, including those to prescribed outside vertices.

2. Exact feasibility of the endpoint face

The actual current is supported at c0=2cos⁡(π/N)c_0=2\cos(\pi/N) in the original spectral coordinate c=−2cos⁡tc=-2\cos t. Its two real angular endpoints tend to s=πs=\pi and s=−πs=-\pi. Keep a specified sign εv∈{−1,1}\varepsilon_v\in\{-1,1\} at each removed vertex.

Theorem 1. There is an extension z̃\widetilde z of z′z' to the original closed polytope, with sv=εvπ(v∈C),(7) s_v=\varepsilon_v\pi\quad(v\in C), \qquad\text{(7)} if and only if |εvnv−2|≤Lv(z′)(v∈C).(8) |\varepsilon_v n_v-2|\leq L_v(z') \quad(v\in C). \qquad\text{(8)} Here an extension retains all surviving coordinates and all original rows (2). If the endpoint signs may be chosen independently, the equivalent condition is ||nv|−2|+∑e={v,w}w∉C|sw|/π≤dC(v)(v∈C).(9) \boxed{\qquad \bigl||n_v|-2\bigr| +\sum_{\substack{e=\{v,w\}\\w\notin C}}|s_w|/\pi \leq d_C(v)\quad(v\in C). \qquad} \qquad\text{(9)}

Proof. Equation (1) at a removed endpoint immediately forces gw,e=0whenever v∈C,e={v,w}.(10) g_{w,e}=0 \quad\text{whenever }v\in C,\ e=\{v,w\}. \qquad\text{(10)} Consequently both incidences of an internal CC-edge are zero. At a surviving vertex every restored incidence is zero, so its old primitive row remains exactly the deleted-forest row.

At v∈Cv\in C, the only adjustable incidences are on its edges to w∉Cw\notin C. Independently, each has the allowed closed interval −π−|sw|2≤gv,e≤π−|sw|2.(11) -\frac{\pi-|s_w|}{2}\leq g_{v,e} \leq\frac{\pi-|s_w|}{2}. \qquad\text{(11)} Its primitive row requires ∑e∋ve∉E(C)gv,e=π2(εvnv−2).(12) \sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\notin E(C)}}g_{v,e} =\frac\pi2(\varepsilon_v n_v-2). \qquad\text{(12)} The sum of the intervals (11) is the closed interval with endpoint magnitudes πLv/2\pi L_v/2. Thus (12) is possible exactly when (8) holds. These choices are independent for different removed vertices, even if they share a surviving neighbor: the opposite incidences at that neighbor have already been fixed to zero. This proves both necessity and sufficiency, including equality and dC(v)=0d_C(v)=0.

Finally min⁡ε=±1|εn−2|=||n|−2|\min_{\varepsilon=\pm1}|\varepsilon n-2|=||n|-2|. Since the signs have no further coupling, this proves (9). ▫\square

This theorem concerns an angle-boundary value. It does not assert a matched complex degeneration along which every reconstructed imaginary angle remains the prescribed one. At corner squares those reconstructions can depend on the approach. Such a complex path is unnecessary for the energy comparison below and is not supplied by the theorem.

3. The exact boundary energy and the strict action gap

The absolute energy of a removed edge is fixed, not an estimate.

Lemma 2. The square energy is constant on its entire boundary, with value E▫|∂[−π/4,π/4]2=GCat/2=πM∞/8.(13) E_\square\big|_{\partial[-\pi/4,\pi/4]^2} =G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/2=\pi M_\infty/8. \qquad\text{(13)}

Proof. On an open vertical side the derivative in the side direction is the continuous limit Ey(A,y)=12arsinh⁡(tan⁡(2y)cos⁡(2A))→0.(14) E_y(A,y)=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh} (\tan(2y)\cos(2A))\longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(14)} The convergence is uniform on compact subintervals of that side, so integrating before taking the limit shows that its value is constant. The same argument works on a horizontal side. The established continuous extension to the closed square joins their constants at the corners.

At y=0y=0, integrate the other original derivative: E▫(π/4,0)=12∫0π/4arsinh⁡(tan⁡(2A))dA=14∫0π/2log⁡(sec⁡u+tan⁡u)du=12∫π/4π/2log⁡tan⁡vdv=GCat/2.(15) \begin{split} E_\square(\pi/4,0) &=\frac12\int_0^{\pi/4}\operatorname{arsinh}(\tan(2A))\,dA\\ &=\frac14\int_0^{\pi/2}\log(\sec u+\tan u)\,du =\frac12\int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2}\log\tan v\,dv =G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/2. \end{split} \qquad\text{(15)} The last equality follows by reflection from ∫0π/4log⁡tan⁡vdv=−GCat\int_0^{\pi/4}\log\tan v\,dv=-G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}. For instance, integrate the Abel-regularized sine/cosine logarithm series and then let its regularization tend to one; the integrated series is absolutely convergent. The normalization of M∞M_\infty in (5) gives the second equality in (13). ▫\square

Let ℰG\C(z′)\mathcal E_{G\setminus C}(z') denote the sum (3) over surviving edges, with the same outside term on those edges, and put ℳG\C(z′)=(m−r)M∞−ℰG\C(z′)/π.(16) \mathcal M_{G\setminus C}(z') =(m-r)M_\infty-\mathcal E_{G\setminus C}(z')/\pi. \qquad\text{(16)} This definition is an angle-energy value; no stationary or quantum interpretation is assumed at a general z′z'.

Theorem 3. Whenever (8) holds, every extension constructed in Theorem 1 satisfies the exact identity ℰG(z̃)=ℰG\C(z′)+rπM∞.(17) \mathcal E_G(\widetilde z) =\mathcal E_{G\setminus C}(z')+r\pi M_\infty. \qquad\text{(17)} Consequently ℳG\C(z′)≤MG−ΔG/π<MG.(18) \boxed{\qquad \mathcal M_{G\setminus C}(z') \leq M_G-\Delta_G/\pi<M_G . \qquad} \qquad\text{(18)} If z′z' is a positive primitive solution of the deleted forest, with its original outside data, then (16) is its actual calibrated classical action. Thus (18) is a strict geometric-action comparison in that case.

Proof. Every deleted edge has at least one removed endpoint. If that endpoint is first in (4), equations (7) and (10) put both square AA-coordinates on the boundary. If it is second, both square yy-coordinates are on the boundary. Lemma 2 therefore gives 4(EP,e+EQ,e)=πM∞(19) 4(E_{P,e}+E_{Q,e})=\pi M_\infty \qquad\text{(19)} for each deleted edge, including an edge with two removed endpoints. Every surviving square is unchanged. Any original outside incidence on a deleted edge is zero by (10), so the outside linear term in (3) loses exactly zero. Summing proves (17).

Because C≠⌀C\ne\varnothing and its vertices have positive original degree, at least one square of z̃\widetilde z lies on its boundary. The full original polytope has an interior positive solution. Hence z̃∈∂𝒫G\widetilde z\in\partial\mathcal P_G, and (5) gives ℰG(z̃)≥ℰG(z0)+ΔG\mathcal E_G(\widetilde z)\geq\mathcal E_G(z_0)+\Delta_G. Combining this with (17) gives (18). For a positive deleted-forest solution, the pinned action-energy identity identifies (16) with its actual action, with the unchanged outside imaginary-angle term. An isolated surviving vertex, if present, has period equation nvtv=2πn_vt_v=2\pi. Thus nv≠0n_v\ne0, its tvt_v is real and its crossing-plus-charge action is purely imaginary; it contributes zero to both sides of (16). This observation does not supply its quantum asymptotic. ▫\square

4. What this says about the grouped original currents

The actual finite source identity is kept before making a comparison. For the removed set CC, its simultaneous one-step Ward insertions give exactly ℰC,N(𝒘)=∑M⊂E(Codd)MmatchingdN|M|∏v∈C\V(M)Av(𝒘),(20) \mathcal E_{C,N}(\mathbf w)= \sum_{\substack{M\subset E(C_{\mathrm{ odd}})\\M\ {\mathrm{ matching}}}} d_N^{|M|}\prod_{v\in C\setminus V(M)}A_v(\mathbf w), \qquad\text{(20)} with the original monomers, outside coupons TΠ0T\Pi_0, and dN=s12(N2−1)/3d_N=s_1^2(N^2-1)/3 from the grouped-current theorem. All first and mixed normals are included, and shared outside variables remain shared. Multiplying (20) by the original exterior and performing its prescribed traces is the exact current. The removed crossing carriers are the original modulus-one units. This is the specified simultaneous one-step current. An arbitrary logarithmic-window translation or other relative-cycle defect is not identified with (20).

An even zero power makes this particular current identically zero. Otherwise the matching boundary-unit theorem proves that (20) is a unit at every full physical boundary assignment for N≥3N\geq3, with polynomially bounded inverse mixed normals. Thus a failure of the angle-face condition is not explained by algebraic vanishing of the grouped current.

If a complete contour theorem for the deleted exterior already justifies the full Ward representatives and its selected nonzero packet, Section 3 of the grouped-current theorem gives a nonzero common-phase leading coefficient for (20). Its extra factors are powers of log⁡N\log N and fixed powers of NN; its exponential action is the deleted exterior’s action. When that action satisfies the endpoint-face test, (18) then puts this packet strictly below the original positive action. A whole-current bound still requires the stated exterior contour and complement bounds. In particular, ordinary free-boundary polynomial evaluation is not replaced by a different Hermite representative without its original trace adapter.

The endpoint-face test is not automatic. It is already impossible if, for one removed vertex, |nv|>dC(v)+2.(21) |n_v|>d_C(v)+2. \qquad\text{(21)} This failure occurs within the original positive geometry: the isolated positive-branch theorem recalled in the pinned action note applies for d+|n|>2d+|n|>2, whereas removing that selected vertex with |n|>d+2|n|>d+2 violates (9) for every endpoint sign. This observation is only a nonvacuity check of the face obstruction, not an action-gap counterexample or a new knot case.

More generally the actual neighboring real angles enter (9), so even the degree inequality alone is insufficient. Internal edges of the removed set provide no capacity in (12): both of their incidences are forced zero. These are constraints of the original primitive rows and original endpoint support, not a norm estimate.

Thus one cannot identify every decorated deleted forest with the endpoint boundary of the original constrained problem and invoke convexity without a test. Failure of (9) does not disprove a strict action gap obtained by another argument. Nor does the boundary identity construct the relative contour, bound other lattice modes, or establish the desired arbitrary-short-filling volume theorem.
