Finitely many exceptional assignments for a two-circle template

Research consequence, 2 November 2026. This note applies the proved terminal-filling theorem to a tree consisting of one edge. It does not prove the finite-exception statement for arbitrary trees or Conway templates. The frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

1. The knot family and the claim

Use the physical tangle conventions of the circle-tree geometry paper. Let RR be the elementary crossing, TT the prescribed quarter-turn, and CC cylindrical closure. The one-edge tree has the closure

K(a,b)=C(RaT(Rb)). K(a,b)=C\bigl(R^aT(R^b)\bigr).

Fix the original endpoint marking, disk frames, and carrier parities. Thus a=η1−2p1a=\eta_1-2p_1 and b=η2−2p2b=\eta_2-2p_2, with fixed η1,η2∈{−1,0,1}\eta_1,\eta_2\in\{-1,0,1\} and p1,p2∈ℤp_1,p_2\in\mathbb Z. Restrict attention to assignments whose closure is a hyperbolic knot. The parameters count assignments; no identification of distinct assignments representing the same knot is made.

Corollary. There is a finite number B*B_*, depending on this fixed marked template, such that every hyperbolic knot K(a,b)K(a,b) with

max⁡{|a|,|b|}≥B* \max\{|a|,|b|\}\ge B_*

satisfies the original Kashaev volume identity

limN→∞2πNlog⁡|⟨K(a,b)⟩N|=Vol⁡(S3\K(a,b)). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|\langle K(a,b)\rangle_N| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K(a,b)).

The twists are fixed before NN tends to infinity, and the limit is through all integer levels. Consequently only finitely many original filling assignments in this two-parameter family remain untreated. The corollary does not claim that the remaining assignments violate the volume identity.

2. The three elementary powers have no hyperbolic descendants

For the zero power, T(R0)T(R^0) is the cup-cap tangle, rather than the through identity. It changes the cylindrical closure into the cap closure:

K(a,0)≃D(Ra). K(a,0)\simeq D(R^a).

Here DD denotes the cap closure. Each crossing can be pulled to an end cap and removed as a curl. The resulting unframed knot is the unknot. The precise cap identities and the retained components are proved in the tangle reduction paper.

A quarter-turn of a single crossing gives the inverse crossing in the original vertical frame:

T(R)=R−1,T(R−1)=R. T(R)=R^{-1},\qquad T(R^{-1})=R.

This is a local equality of the two one-crossing diagrams with their ordered endpoints. It is also reflected by the exact turned crossing identities ℋ1=R−1\mathcal H_1=R^{-1} and ℋ−1=R\mathcal H_{-1}=R in the finite transform calculation. Therefore

K(a,1)≃C(Ra−1),K(a,−1)≃C(Ra+1). K(a,1)\simeq C(R^{a-1}),\qquad K(a,-1)\simeq C(R^{a+1}).

The latter closures are two-strand torus links. When they have one component, they are unknots or torus knots and are not hyperbolic. The elementary group argument excluding these knots is included in the tangle reduction paper. Its closed-turn and cyclicity isotopies, proved in the collar calculation, also give

C(RaT(Rb))≃C(T(Ra)Rb)≃C(RbT(Ra)). C(R^aT(R^b))\simeq C(T(R^a)R^b) \simeq C(R^bT(R^a)).

They retain the physical rotations and crossing signs. Thus the same conclusion holds when a∈{0,1,−1}a\in\{0,1,-1\}.

It follows that every hyperbolic knot in this family has

|a|≥2,|b|≥2. |a|\ge2,\qquad |b|\ge2.

This is a statement about the actual knot diagrams. A vanishing or small exponential rate of a polynomial would not by itself establish the required topological exclusion.

3. Closing the finitely many short-coordinate families

Let B0≥3B_0\ge3 be an integer threshold for the all-long theorem on this fixed one-edge template. It proves the identity whenever both |a|≥B0|a|\ge B_0 and |b|≥B0|b|\ge B_0, with the allowed fixed parities.

For each allowed fixed integer cc with 2≤|c|<B02\le|c|<B_0, the terminal-filling theorem applies with the first vertex fixed at a=ca=c. It supplies a finite threshold B1,cB_{1,c} for the other power. The same theorem with the second vertex fixed supplies B2,cB_{2,c}. Both vertices are original terminal vertices, so this use does not require a theorem about a vertex that becomes terminal after a previous short filling.

Choose an integer

B*≥max⁡(B0,{B1,c:2≤|c|<B0},{B2,c:2≤|c|<B0}), B_*\ge\max\left(B_0, \{B_{1,c}:2\le|c|<B_0\}, \{B_{2,c}:2\le|c|<B_0\}\right),

where only the allowed parity values are included. These are finite sets. No uniform bound for all fixed terminal powers is needed.

Suppose K(a,b)K(a,b) is hyperbolic and max⁡{|a|,|b|}≥B*\max\{|a|,|b|\}\ge B_*. Section 2 excludes the powers 0,±10,\pm1. If both powers have magnitude at least B0B_0, the original all-long theorem applies. Otherwise exactly one power is in the finite range 2≤|c|<B02\le|c|<B_0, while the other has magnitude at least B*B_*. The corresponding terminal-filling theorem applies. This proves the corollary.

All unresolved assignments lie in the finite square |a|<B*,|b|<B*|a|<B_*, |b|<B_*. The affine maps from p1,p2p_1,p_2 to a,ba,b are injective, so the corresponding set of original surgery assignments is finite as well.

4. Why this does not yet extend to arbitrary trees

The later isolated-vertex theorem now estimates every original vertex with degree dvd_v and fixed power nvn_v satisfying dv+|nv|>2d_v+|n_v|>2, with all its boundary variables retained. Its assembly theorem permits any independent set of such fixed vertices before independently long remaining fillings. Thus isolated internal short powers are no longer the missing case.

Arbitrary connected sets of adjacent short fillings still need a joint boundary estimate. Several specific connected families have been proved, but the finite-branching argument for arbitrary templates requires a theorem at every partial assignment it reaches. It must also retain the original parameter fibers in zero-power and cap reductions: a finite untreated set of reduced knots can have infinitely many original assignments. The one-edge corollary is therefore a completed special case of the finite-exception target, rather than its general proof.
