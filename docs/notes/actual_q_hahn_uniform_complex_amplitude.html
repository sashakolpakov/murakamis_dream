A uniform nonzero complex amplitude for the actual q-Hahn column

Subsequent extension: joint pair-degree and magnetic amplitudes are now proved on a central complex domain, using the original finite kernel and an exact accretive determinant. The present theorem and its proof retain the real-parameter hypothesis stated below.

30 October 2026. The original finite q-Hahn polynomial has a full leading asymptotic, with a specified nonzero amplitude and relative error O(1/N), uniformly on compact subsets of the right half-plane. Its magnetic parameters are the ACTUAL scaled integers, varying in a compact strict REAL parameter set. No rate of convergence of those parameters is assumed.

The proof starts with the previously proved actual logarithmic limit, then uses the exact finite difference equation to improve its error. A positive real Gaussian sum fixes the actual multiplicative constant. Thus the transport equation below describes the original polynomial, not merely a formal solution with an unspecified connection coefficient.

This theorem does not interpolate the discrete magnetic parameters holomorphically, take a generic-q first normal, or control the assembled global magnetic/channel sum.

1. Original finite source and theorem

Let 0<=j<=t<N, 0<=u<=t, v=t-u, theta=pi/N, and

s_n=product_(r=1)^n 2 sin(r theta),
beta(n,a)=s_n/(s_a s_(n-a)),
E_a=beta(j,a) beta(u,a) beta(v,j-a)/beta(t,j),
P_N(w)=sum_(a=a0)^a1 E_a w^a,
a0=max(0,j-v), a1=min(j,u).                            (1)

These are exactly the coefficients of the original complete-circle phase compiler. Put

lambda_N=(tau,xi,upsilon)=(t/N,j/N,u/N),
D={0<xi<tau<1, 0<upsilon<tau}.

All uniform statements below take lambda_N in a fixed compact subset K of D. In particular all saddles at w=1 stay a uniform distance from the factorial boundaries, even when the two descriptions of a support endpoint exchange at upsilon=xi or upsilon+xi=tau.

Use the original energy

J(s)=integral_0^s log(2 sin(pi y))dy,
Bcal(b,a)=J(b)-J(a)-J(b-a),
Ecal_lambda(alpha)=Bcal(xi,alpha)+Bcal(upsilon,alpha)
                   +Bcal(tau-upsilon,xi-alpha)-Bcal(tau,xi).

For positive real w, let alpha_lambda(w) be the unique interior maximum of Ecal_lambda(alpha)+alpha log w. The selected complex action theorem continues both alpha and its critical value psi_lambda holomorphically to Re w>0, with

psi_lambda(w)=Ecal_lambda(alpha_lambda(w))
                      +alpha_lambda(w) Log w,
w psi_lambda'(w)=alpha_lambda(w).                    (2)

All logarithms continue from their real values at w=1. Define, with derivatives in the variable s,

A_lambda(s)=sin^2(pi s) sin(pi(tau-upsilon-xi+s)),
B_lambda(s)=sin^2(pi(xi-s)) sin(pi(upsilon-s)),
F_lambda(s,w)=A_lambda(s)-w B_lambda(s),
C_lambda=(sin(pi xi)/2)
   sqrt[sin(pi upsilon) sin(pi(tau-upsilon))
                     sin(pi(tau-xi))/sin(pi tau)].

The root selected in (2) satisfies F_lambda(alpha,w)=0. Define

Acal_lambda(w)=sqrt(2 pi) C_lambda
      /sqrt[B_lambda(alpha_lambda(w))
                    F_(lambda,s)(alpha_lambda(w),w)].             (3)

The square root is the holomorphic continuation of its positive real value at w=1. It is well-defined and never zero on Re w>0.

Theorem. Uniformly for lambda_N in K and w in any compact subset of Re w>0,

P_N(w)=N^(-1/2) Acal_(lambda_N)(w)
             exp[N psi_(lambda_N)(w)] (1+O(N^(-1))).               (4)

The relative error is holomorphic in w. On smaller compact subsets each fixed number of its w derivatives is O(N^(-1)) as well. Formula (4) uses the actual lambda_N, not an arbitrarily chosen limiting triple. Replacing lambda_N by its limit without controlling the displacement would in general change the leading exponential and the amplitude.

2. Exact finite difference equation, including endpoints

Let D_w=w d/dw and h=1/N. The coefficient ratio in (1) gives the exact identity

sin^2(pi h D_w) sin(pi h(D_w+v-j)) P_N(w)
  =w sin^2(pi h(j-D_w)) sin(pi h(u-D_w)) P_N(w).       (5)

For every supported a, its coefficient equation is

sin^2(a theta) sin((v-j+a)theta) E_a
  =sin^2((j-a+1)theta) sin((u-a+1)theta) E_(a-1).

At the lower endpoint either a=0 or v-j+a=0. At the first integer above the upper endpoint either j-a+1=0 or u-a+1=0. Thus the zero coefficients outside the support satisfy the SAME equation; there is no endpoint source term in (5). In functional calculus notation,

[A_lambda(h D_w)-w B_lambda(h D_w)]P_N=0.             (6)

Every sine product in (6) is a finite linear combination of the four shifts w -> exp(i pi k h)w, k=-3,-1,1,3. In particular (6) is an exact finite-shift identity, not an infinite differential expansion.

3. Uniform simplicity and the actual derivative bootstrap

The negative-root theorem gives positive rho_r and

P_N(w)/P_N(1)=w^a0 product_r (w+rho_r)/(1+rho_r).

It fixes a holomorphic Log P_N real at w=1. Put

G_N(w)=Log P_N(w)/N,   a_N(w)=D_w G_N(w).

The actual leading-limit theorem proves

a_N-alpha_(lambda_N) -> 0                            (7)

uniformly on larger compact subsets of the right half-plane, also when the real parameters vary in K. Fixed w derivatives converge there too. Alternatively the explicit expression

a_N=a0/N+(1/N)sum_r w/(w+rho_r)

directly bounds every fixed derivative on those compact subsets. These bounds are independent of the positions of the negative roots.

For completeness, the selected branch is uniformly simple here. Its positive limiting root measure gives

alpha_lambda'(w)=integral rho/(w+rho)^2 dmu_lambda(rho).

For Re w>0 the integrands lie in an angular sector of width less than pi, so their positive integral cannot vanish. Its measure has positive mass strictly between zero and infinity. The support-strip property of alpha makes A and B nonzero. Differentiating its saddle equation gives

F_s(alpha,w) D_w alpha=w B(alpha)=A(alpha),
Ecal''(alpha)=-F_s(alpha,w)/A(alpha).                 (8)

Consequently F_s is nonzero. Continuity in the real parameters and compactness make its modulus bounded away from zero on each of the sets used below. Complex conjugation supplies the same statement in the lower right quadrant.

Fix nested compact neighborhoods within Re w>0. For each fixed c=i pi k, k in {-3,-1,1,3}, all small shifts stay in the larger one. Taylor’s theorem along the short logarithmic segment, using the uniform derivative bounds, yields

P_N(exp(c h)w)/P_N(w)
  =exp(c a_N(w))
       [1+(h c^2/2)D_w a_N(w)+O(h^2)].              (9)

Indeed the exponent before exponentiating is

N[G_N(exp(c h)w)-G_N(w)]
    =c a_N+(h c^2/2)D_w a_N+O(h^2).

This is an estimate for the actual polynomial ratios. Inserting all four shifts into (6), with their exact sine coefficients, proves

0=F(a_N,w)+(h/2)(D_w a_N) F_ss(a_N,w)+O(h^2).        (10)

Equation (7) and the uniformly simple zero first imply

a_N-alpha=O(h).

To justify this improvement, use the uniform expansion F(a_N,w)=F_s(alpha,w)(a_N-alpha)+O(|a_N-alpha|^2), absorb the quadratic term using (7), and use boundedness of D_w a_N in (10). Cauchy’s estimate on a slightly larger compact set then gives

D_w a_N-D_w alpha=O(h)

and the analogous estimate for every fixed further derivative. Substitution back into (10) now proves the sharper actual estimate

a_N=alpha-(h/2)(D_w alpha)F_ss(alpha,w)/F_s(alpha,w)
               +O(h^2).                             (11)

The order h^2 remainder is justified by the first improvement and Cauchy, not assumed as a WKB ansatz.

Differentiating (3) and using (8) gives the exact transport identity

D_w log Acal_lambda
   =-(1/2)(D_w alpha)F_ss(alpha,w)/F_s(alpha,w).       (12)

For example, differentiate log(B(alpha)F_s(alpha,w)). Its two extra terms cancel:

(B'/B)D_w alpha+w F_sw/F_s
   =(B'/B)(wB/F_s)-wB'/F_s=0.

Thus (11) identifies the logarithmic derivative of the ACTUAL source with the explicit amplitude to error O(h^2).

4. The real sum fixes the constant, with arbitrary lattice offset

On a compact interior subinterval the sine-factorial estimate is

s_n=sqrt[2N sin(pi n/N)] exp[N J(n/N)](1+O(N^(-1))).

One elementary derivation subtracts log x from log(2 sin pi x), applies the trapezoidal estimate to the resulting smooth function, and applies the usual integral remainder estimate for log(n!). The errors and their required finite derivatives are uniform away from 0 and 1. Applied to every factor of (1), this gives

E_a=N^(-1) C_lambda
       /sqrt[A_lambda(a/N)B_lambda(a/N)]
          exp[N Ecal_lambda(a/N)](1+O(N^(-1))).       (13)

The positive real saddle alpha=alpha_lambda(1) stays uniformly inside the support. Strict concavity and the global factorial O(log N) bound give an exponential loss outside a fixed saddle neighborhood. Inside that neighborhood put z=(a-N alpha)/sqrt N. Taylor expansion of the phase and the smooth prefactor in (13) gives a Gaussian times

H(alpha)+N^(-1/2)[H'(alpha)z
           +H(alpha)Ecal'''(alpha)z^3/6]
            +O(N^(-1)(1+|z|^8)),

with a uniformly integrable Gaussian majorant; one may first restrict |z|<=N^(1/20), whose complementary tails are exponentially small. Here H=C_lambda/sqrt(AB). The term of order N^(-1/2) is ODD.

No arithmetic assumption about N alpha is needed. For any shifted grid h_Z(Z+eta), h_Z=N^(-1/2), and a smooth Gaussian times a fixed polynomial f, the centered-cell Taylor formula gives

|h_Z sum_(n in Z) f(h_Z(n+eta))-integral_R f|
                     <= (h_Z^2/8) integral_R |f''|.                (14)

The bound is independent of eta: integrate the Taylor remainder over each cell centered at its grid point, then sum the disjoint cells. The same estimate applies to the odd term, whose integral is zero. Hence the arbitrary fractional saddle offset introduces an error O(N^(-1)), not an uncontrolled O(N^(-1/2)) correction. Gaussian tails allow the finite interval to be replaced by that whole translated grid.

The resulting ACTUAL positive sum is

P_N(1)=N^(-1/2)
   [sqrt(2 pi) C_lambda
      /sqrt(A(alpha)B(alpha)(-Ecal''(alpha)))]
               exp[N psi_lambda(1)](1+O(N^(-1))).

By (8), A(-Ecal’’)=F_s, so the bracket is precisely Acal_lambda(1). This proves the real normalization in (4), uniformly in lambda_N.

5. Integrating the proved derivative error

The function

H_N=Log P_N-N psi_(lambda_N)+(1/2)log N-log Acal_(lambda_N)

uses the branches fixed at w=1. Sections 3 and 4 prove

D_w H_N=O(N^(-1)),   H_N(1)=O(N^(-1)).

Join 1 to each w in a chosen compact subset of Re w>0 by its straight segment. The union of these segments is a compact set bounded away from zero and the imaginary axis. A slightly larger compact set supplies all the preceding derivative estimates. Integrating H_N’ on those bounded-length segments proves H_N=O(N^(-1)) uniformly. Exponentiation proves (4). Cauchy’s estimate on nested compact sets then proves the asserted relative derivative bounds.

This is where both inputs are essential: negative roots gave the actual leading selection and bounded holomorphic derivatives, while the positive real sum fixed the multiplicative constant left open by the finite difference equation.

6. The original physical column and both integer offsets

In the complete-circle chamber the exact compiler uses

f_u(c_j)=(-1)^u q^[-(j+1)u] P_N(q^delta),
q=exp(pi i/N), delta=t+j-(N-1).

Let w_star=exp[pi i(tau+xi-1)]. Then q^delta=w_star exp(pi i/N). Keeping both occurrences of +1, formula (4) gives

f_u(c_j)=N^(-1/2) Acal_(lambda_N)(w_star)
       exp[pi i(alpha_(lambda_N)(w_star)-upsilon)]
       exp{N[psi_(lambda_N)(w_star)
                        +pi i upsilon(1-xi)]}
       (1+O(N^(-1))).                               (15)

In detail, moving w_star to w_star exp(pi i/N) contributes exp(pi i alpha)(1+O(N^(-1))), while q1 contributes the otherwise easily lost factor exp(-pi i upsilon).

For actual complete-circle channels xi<=1/2 and tau+xi>=1-1/N. On a fixed strict compact parameter set tau<1, these w_star and their exact shifted values stay in a compact right-half-plane set. Thus (15) holds at both level parities, at the folded endpoint, and at composite levels. The high physical degree block inherits the formula by its already proved exact index translation, with the same transported column convention.

The scope is a single ACTUAL local column, with real magnetic indices. Nothing here selects a global magnetic integration cycle, proves cancellation bounds for the assembled plat, or differentiates the root formula in the generic-q normal direction.

Audit: finite equation, amplitude, and physical offsets. The finite computations test conventions and the predicted error; the all-level uniform statement is proved by (5)–(14).

The magnetic parameter remains strictly real. No assembled-source conclusion follows from the local complex-amplitude theorem.

The theorem does not extend to complex magnetic parameters or an assembled-source asymptotic.
