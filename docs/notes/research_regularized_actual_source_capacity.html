A regularized convex problem for the actual main-color energy

20 November 2026. Research note; the manuscripts and previous research finals remain unchanged.

The full matrix balance required by the tree capacity theorem can always be obtained for the actual all-main-color source by minimizing one explicit regularized convex objective. The definition uses the original local tensors, their norms, and their original cap. It uses no unknown Kashaev value. The ordinary trivial main color supplies the lower bound needed for existence of a minimizer.

The resulting unpenalized objective approximates the logarithm of the actual positive energy with an absolute error of order N−1N^{-1}. Thus a separate nonzero cycle coefficient is not an additional premise in this formulation. The remaining volume problem is to compare this positive variational value with the Casson–Rivin geometric value. That comparison is not proved here.

1. The prescribed tree and its positive objective

Use the vector-valued tree compiler of A positive variational formula for an actual source on a tree, Section 5. At level N≥2N\ge2, its output is YN=(c1,…,cN),∥YN∥2=EN=|KN|2+RN,0≤RN≤(N−1)(N/2)2b−2.(1) Y_N=(c_1,\ldots,c_N),\qquad \|Y_N\|^2=E_N=|K_N|^2+R_N, \qquad 0\le R_N\le(N-1)(N/2)^{2b-2}. \qquad\text{(1)} Here bb is the fixed braid width, cjc_j are the original reduced main-color values, and the prescribed partial pivotal trace and marked cut are part of the root tensor. In particular c1=1c_1=1, so ∥YN∥≥1.(2) \|Y_N\|\ge1. \qquad\text{(2)} This is an unconditional ordinary-color anchor. It does not assert KN≠0K_N\ne0.

Let the tree have m≥2m\ge2 vertices. Its internal edge spaces are HeH_e, of dimensions nen_e. They may be the full bundled operator spaces of that compiler; the theorem does not restrict gauges to individual physical wires. Write Tv,NT_{v,N} for its actual local tensors. They are all nonzero, since a zero local tensor would make the output zero, contrary to (2).

For positive Hermitian edge matrices H=(He)eH=(H_e)_e, let LN(H)=∑vlog⁡∥Tv,N∥H,FN(H)=eLN(H).(3) L_N(H)=\sum_v\log\|T_{v,N}\|_H, \qquad F_N(H)=e^{L_N(H)}. \qquad\text{(3)} The local norms include HeH_e at one end and H¯e−1\overline H_e^{-1} at the other, precisely as in the direct source. That source proves LN(H)≥log⁡∥YN∥≥0,infHLN(H)=log⁡∥YN∥.(4) L_N(H)\ge\log\|Y_N\|\ge0, \qquad \inf_H L_N(H)=\log\|Y_N\|. \qquad\text{(4)} It also proves geodesic convexity of LNL_N.

2. An elementary coercive regularizer

Define Z(H)=1+∑etr⁡(He+He−1),ℛ(H)=log⁡Z(H),JN,ε(H)=LN(H)+εℛ(H),ε>0.(5) Z(H)=1+\sum_e\operatorname{tr}(H_e+H_e^{-1}), \qquad \mathcal R(H)=\log Z(H), \qquad J_{N,\varepsilon}(H)=L_N(H)+\varepsilon\mathcal R(H), \quad\varepsilon>0. \qquad\text{(5)} All coefficients in this objective are known from the original finite source. In particular its regularizer has no selected invariant in it.

Proposition 1. The function JN,εJ_{N,\varepsilon} is strictly geodesically convex and has a unique global minimizer in the product of the positive cones.

Proof. Consider a simultaneous geodesic He(t)=PeetXePeH_e(t)=P_e e^{tX_e}P_e, with Pe>0P_e>0 and Xe=Xe*X_e=X_e^*. After diagonalizing XeX_e, both tr⁡(PeetXePe),tr⁡(Pe−1e−tXePe−1) \operatorname{tr}(P_e e^{tX_e}P_e),\qquad \operatorname{tr}(P_e^{-1}e^{-tX_e}P_e^{-1}) are finite sums of real exponentials with positive coefficients. The same is true of Z(H(t))Z(H(t)), including its constant term one. Its logarithm is convex by the variance formula for the second derivative of a logarithmic exponential sum. On a nonconstant geodesic, some XeX_e has a nonzero eigenvalue. Its exponential and the constant term one both have positive weights and distinct exponents. The variance is then strictly positive. Together with (4), this proves strict convexity.

For existence, take a minimizing sequence with objective at most JN,ε(I)+1J_{N,\varepsilon}(I)+1. By (4), its regularizer is bounded above. Thus ZZ is bounded. Every eigenvalue λ\lambda of every edge matrix satisfies a common bound on λ+λ−1\lambda+\lambda^{-1}. The matrices consequently lie in a compact set bounded away from the boundary of the positive cone. The finite tensor norms are continuous and nonzero there. A convergent subsequence attains the infimum. Two different minimizers would contradict strict convexity on the positive-matrix geodesic joining them. ▫\square

No effective numerical minimization is needed in what follows.

5. The optimizing matrices are algebraic at a fixed level

There is also a finite-level algebraic statement, distinct from any bound as NN grows. Suppose all local tensor entries are algebraic numbers and ε\varepsilon is positive rational. These assumptions hold for the original root-of-unity matrices and (9). Then every entry of the minimizing matrices is algebraic.

To prove this, use real coordinates for the Hermitian matrices Pe=He1/2P_e=H_e^{1/2}. Their real and imaginary entries form an open coordinate domain. Each squared local norm sv=∥Tv,N∥H2s_v=\|T_{v,N}\|_H^2 and ZZ is a rational function in these coordinates with algebraic coefficients. They have no zero denominator and are positive in this domain. Consequently dJN,ε=12∑vdsvsv+εdZZ(11b) dJ_{N,\varepsilon} =\tfrac12\sum_v\frac{ds_v}{s_v} +\varepsilon\frac{dZ}{Z} \qquad\text{(11b)} is a vector of rational functions. Clearing nonzero denominators in its stationary equations gives polynomial equations with algebraic coefficients.

At the minimum the Hessian is positive definite: along every nonzero tangent the second derivative of the regularizer is strictly positive, and the local norm objective is geodesically convex. Because the first derivative vanishes there, the ordinary coordinate Hessian has the same positive quadratic form. The Jacobian of the cleared stationary equations is therefore invertible.

For completeness, a solution of polynomial equations in characteristic zero with invertible Jacobian has algebraic coordinates over their coefficient field. Otherwise its finitely generated coordinate field has a nonempty transcendence basis. Differentiation in one basis variable extends through its finite separable algebraic extension: differentiate each minimal polynomial and divide by its nonzero derivative. Applying this derivation to the stationary equations and using their invertible Jacobian makes the derivatives of every coordinate zero. That contradicts differentiation of the chosen transcendence basis variable. This proves the assertion.

The size and degree of these algebraic points are not bounded here uniformly in NN. Algebraicity alone does not determine the limiting value of (3), and no such inference is used.

6. A regularized flow on the actual compiler edges

The minimizing metrics can also be specified by a convergent flow. Keep NN and ε>0\varepsilon>0 fixed, and put Pe=He1/2,Ge=Δe+2εZ(H)(He−He−1).(11c) P_e=H_e^{1/2},\qquad G_e=\Delta_e+\frac{2\varepsilon}{Z(H)}(H_e-H_e^{-1}). \qquad\text{(11c)} Starting at any positive metrics, solve Ḣe=−12PeGePe.(11d) \dot H_e=-\tfrac12 P_eG_eP_e. \qquad\text{(11d)} Everything on the right is computed from the original local tensors, the current edge metrics, and the one positive scalar ZZ. In particular no value of KNK_N or selected geometric coefficient is used in this equation.

The flow exists for all nonnegative time and converges to the unique minimizer of (5). Here is a direct verification. In the positive-matrix metric ⟨U,V⟩H=tr⁡(H−1UH−1V)\langle U,V\rangle_H=\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}UH^{-1}V), the derivative formulas above give gradient 12PeGePe\tfrac12 P_eG_eP_e. Thus dJN,εdt=−14∑etr⁡(Ge2).(11e) \frac{dJ_{N,\varepsilon}}{dt} =-\tfrac14\sum_e\operatorname{tr}(G_e^2). \qquad\text{(11e)} The entire trajectory stays in a compact positive sublevel set, by Proposition 1’s coercivity proof and (4). The smooth vector field is bounded there, so finite-dimensional local existence continues for all time. Equation (11e) and the lower bound on JJ show that the squared norm of GG is integrable in time. It is uniformly continuous, since both the vector field and its derivatives are bounded on that compact set. It therefore tends to zero: otherwise disjoint intervals of uniformly positive length would give an infinite integral. Every accumulation point is consequently critical. Geodesic convexity makes each critical point a global minimum, and strict convexity makes that point unique. Compactness then gives convergence of the entire trajectory.

This is a full matrix flow on the compiler edges, with its original source retained. It has not been identified with a classical flow of dihedral angles on an ideal triangulation.

7. What is now required for the finite-exception program

For a fixed hyperbolic knot of complement volume V>0V>0, (1) and (10)–(11) give the equivalence limN2πNlog⁡|KN|=V⇔limN2πNlog⁡F̂N=V.(12) \lim_N\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N|=V \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lim_N\frac{2\pi}{N}\log\widehat F_N=V. \qquad\text{(12)} The implication from energy to the original scalar uses the polynomial ordinary-color remainder, as proved in the direct source. It does not assume eventual nonvanishing of KNK_N.

Consequently the remaining task in this formulation is a quantum to classical variational comparison. One must determine the leading unpenalized objective at these actual full matrix minimizers and show that it is the hyperbolic volume. Classical angle balance by itself does not prove that comparison. The construction above does supply the full matrix balance, including the original source, but gives no description of the minimizing matrices by geometric shapes.

Fix any short partial assignment. For each completion of the remaining integer coefficients that gives a one-component knot, apply the construction to that completed knot’s original compiler. Its ordinary trivial main color again equals one, so existence and the estimates above apply to that completion. This makes no assertion about the source of an intermediate unfilled link or an open family of boundary variables, and requires no intermediate exterior to be hyperbolic. To obtain the proposed finite-exception theorem, the comparison in (12) must hold for all sufficiently long remaining fillings after each required fixed short partial assignment. The coefficients are fixed before the level tends to infinity. Infinite fibers representing a fixed hyperbolic core still require the same volume comparison for that core.

This reformulation has removed an independent lower-bound hypothesis for the closing overlap. It has not proved (12)’s geometric limit, exchanged an uncontrolled infimum and limit, or added a new knot class with a proved volume identity.
