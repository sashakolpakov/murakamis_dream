A homogeneous Bargmann kernel for the physical Krein pairing

Written research proof, 23 November 2026. The physical Hermitian pairing has an exact double-Gaussian realization with one homogeneous binomial kernel. The actual factorial frame is conformal for this pairing, with scalar NN per head. Its paired gluing therefore requires neither an incomplete-exponential approximation nor an unknown factorial-frame metric. Original exterior rows and both cap types remain.

The form is indefinite. Its explicit nonzero kernel symbol is not a noncancellation theorem for an arbitrary marked closing. In particular, the physical operator, coefficient-form matrix, and Gaussian Riesz operator are distinguished throughout.

1. Original finite coefficients and Gaussian moments

Fix N≥2N\geq2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, Q=q2Q=q^2, and x=−q−1x=-q^{-1}. On the original balanced head put Fa=(Q;Q)a,GF=diag⁡(Fa),HN=q−1Z−1Rd,HN|a⟩=qQa|d−a⟩.(1) F_a=(Q;Q)_a,\quad G_F=\operatorname{diag}(F_a),\qquad H_N=q^{-1}Z^{-1}R_d,\quad H_N|a\rangle=qQ^a|d-a\rangle . \qquad\text{(1)} Here Z|a⟩=Qa|a⟩Z|a\rangle=Q^a|a\rangle, Rd|a⟩=|d−a⟩R_d|a\rangle=|d-a\rangle, and 0≤a≤d0\leq a\leq d. Thus HN†=HNH_N^\dagger=H_N, HN2=IH_N^2=I. Use exactly the finite Gaussian calculus of [B]: Pu(z)=∑a=0duaza,dμN(z)=Nπe−N|z|2d2z,ha=a!Na,∫zaz‾bdμN(z)=δabha,𝒦A(z,w)=∑a,b=0dzaAab(Nw)bb!.(2) \begin{gathered} P_u(z)=\sum_{a=0}^d u_az^a,\qquad d\mu_N(z)=\frac N\pi e^{-N|z|^2}\,d^2z,\qquad h_a=\frac{a!}{N^a},\\ \int z^a\bar z^{\,b}\,d\mu_N(z)=\delta_{ab}h_a,\qquad \mathcal K_A(z,w)=\sum_{a,b=0}^d z^aA_{ab}\frac{(Nw)^b}{b!}. \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(2)} Angular integration and one elementary gamma integral prove the moment identity. Finite kernels compose with this measure and have trace integral Tr⁡A\operatorname{Tr}A. The identity kernel is Ed(Nzw)E_d(Nzw), Ed(s)=∑a=0dsa/a!E_d(s)=\sum_{a=0}^d s^a/a!, retaining its full cutoff.

2. Exact paired integral

Let h=diag⁡(ha)h=\operatorname{diag}(h_a). The Gaussian Riesz operator of the coefficient form u†HNvu^\dagger H_Nv is h−1HNh^{-1}H_N. Its exact kernel is 𝒦N(z,w):=𝒦h−1HN(z,w)=qNdd!(z+Qw)d.(3) \boxed{\quad \mathscr K_N(z,w):=\mathcal K_{h^{-1}H_N}(z,w) =q\frac{N^d}{d!}(z+Qw)^d .\quad} \qquad\text{(3)} Indeed column bb has its sole nonzero entry in row d−bd-b, giving coefficient qQbNd/[(d−b)!b!]qQ^bN^d/[(d-b)!b!]. The ordinary binomial theorem proves (3), including both endpoints. All its scalars remain.

For arbitrary full coefficient vectors u,vu,v, u†HNv=∬Pu(z)¯𝒦N(z,w‾)Pv(w)dμN(z)dμN(w).(4) \boxed{\quad u^\dagger H_Nv= \iint\overline{P_u(z)}\,\mathscr K_N(z,\bar w)P_v(w) \,d\mu_N(z)d\mu_N(w).\quad} \qquad\text{(4)} The first integral pairs a row index with u‾aha\bar u_a h_a; the second pairs (Nw‾)b/b!(N\bar w)^b/b! with vbv_b. Their result is u†h(h−1HN)vu^\dagger h(h^{-1}H_N)v. All sums are finite, so the double Gaussian integral is absolutely convergent without asymptotic assumptions. The kernel is Hermitian in the paired variables, since q‾Q−d=q\bar qQ^{-d}=q.

For clarity, the operator HNH_N itself has a different kernel: 𝒦HN(z,w)=qzdEd(QNw/z).(5) \mathcal K_{H_N}(z,w)=qz^dE_d(QNw/z). \qquad\text{(5)} Its apparent singularity is removable by the finite sum. A cutoff transition in (5) is not an obstruction to (4), which uses (3). Also (h−1HN)2(h^{-1}H_N)^2 has diagonal entries 1/(hahd−a)1/(h_ah_{d-a}); the Gaussian Riesz operator is not an involution. Only the coefficient-form matrix HNH_N squares to the identity.

3. The actual factorial congruence and row transport

There is an exact phased complement, stronger than a modulus identity: FaFd−a¯=N,GF†HNGF=NHN.(6) F_a\overline{F_{d-a}}=N,\qquad \boxed{\quad G_F^\dagger H_NG_F=NH_N.\quad} \qquad\text{(6)} Indeed Fd=NF_d=N, and its factors from a+1a+1 through dd are the factors 1−Q−j1-Q^{-j}, 1≤j≤d−a1\leq j\leq d-a, in reverse order. Thus Fd/Fa=Fd−a¯F_d/F_a=\overline{F_{d-a}}. Inserting this in each anti-diagonal entry proves the second formula.

On rr heads put G=GF⊗rG=G_F^{\otimes r} and ℋr=HN⊗r\mathscr H_r=H_N^{\otimes r}. Then (Gu)†ℋr(Gv)=Nru†ℋrv.(7) (Gu)^\dagger\mathscr H_r(Gv)=N^r u^\dagger\mathscr H_rv. \qquad\text{(7)} The right side is the tensor product of the paired integrals (4), with the displayed NrN^r. This is precisely the factorial frame used in [B], with all phases rather than only its singular values. For T=G−1AGT=G^{-1}AG, any already proved identity A†ℋrA=ℋrA^\dagger\mathscr H_rA=\mathscr H_r therefore implies T†ℋrT=ℋrT^\dagger\mathscr H_rT=\mathscr H_r. This applies to the unreduced signed physical words of [H]. It does not identify the reduced word of [B] with such an isometry after a head is eliminated.

Congruence is not similarity. The actual physical operator HNH_N, written in factorial coordinates, is GF−1HNGF|a⟩=qQa|Fa|2N|d−a⟩.(8) G_F^{-1}H_NG_F|a\rangle =qQ^a\frac{|F_a|^2}{N}|d-a\rangle . \qquad\text{(8)} It is neither the coefficient-form matrix HNH_N nor the Gaussian Riesz matrix h−1HNh^{-1}H_N. No positive condition-number estimate for the factorial frame follows from (6).

Every original bilinear row λ\lambda has the explicit coefficient form-dual vector zλ=ℋrλ†z_\lambda=\mathscr H_r\lambda^\dagger, so that λy=zλ†ℋry\lambda y=z_\lambda^\dagger\mathscr H_ry. In factorial coordinates y=Guy=Gu, the actual row is η=λG\eta=\lambda G. Its corresponding vector satisfies vη=ℋrη†=NrG−1zλ,λy=vη†ℋru.(9) v_\eta=\mathscr H_r\eta^\dagger=N^rG^{-1}z_\lambda, \qquad \lambda y=v_\eta^\dagger\mathscr H_ru . \qquad\text{(9)} Equations (4),(9) preserve an arbitrary original exterior. The original Cartan-weighted traces and first meridian derivative in [B] are applied by linearity to the same coefficient identities. They are not replaced by a squared norm or an independently selected row.

4. Literal ordinary and marked cap functions

Set c(z)a=(Nz‾)aa!,ℱN(t)=∑a=0dFataa!,BN(z,w)=∑a=0d(da)qa2zawd−a.(10) c(z)_a=\frac{(N\bar z)^a}{a!},\qquad \mathcal F_N(t)=\sum_{a=0}^d F_a\frac{t^a}{a!},\qquad B_N(z,w)=\sum_{a=0}^d\binom da q^{a^2}z^aw^{d-a}. \qquad\text{(10)} These are specified full finite packets and polynomials. The unrestricted marked row is ℓm|a,b⟩=xb/N2\ell_m|a,b\rangle=x^b/N^2 [H]. Hence ℓm(GFc(z)⊗GFc(w))=N−2ℱN(Nz‾)ℱN(Nxw‾).(11) \boxed{\quad \ell_m(G_Fc(z)\otimes G_Fc(w)) =N^{-2}\mathcal F_N(N\bar z)\mathcal F_N(Nx\bar w). \quad} \qquad\text{(11)} The ordinary cap is ℓo|a,b⟩=xaδa+b,d/N\ell_o|a,b\rangle=x^a\delta_{a+b,d}/N. Using FaFd−a=N(−1)aQa(a+1)/2F_aF_{d-a}=N(-1)^aQ^{a(a+1)/2} gives ℓo(GFc(z)⊗GFc(w))=Ndd!BN(z‾,w‾).(12) \ell_o(G_Fc(z)\otimes G_Fc(w)) =\frac{N^d}{d!}B_N(\bar z,\bar w). \qquad\text{(12)} Indeed xa(−1)aQa(a+1)/2=qa2x^a(-1)^aQ^{a(a+1)/2}=q^{a^2}. Only when the actual assembled source forces this pair to integer degree dd may the marked row be replaced by its restriction ℓm,d\ell_{m,d}. For that restriction the exact formula is ℓm,d(GFc(z)⊗GFc(w))=xdNd−1d!BN(Qz‾,w‾).(13) \ell_{m,d}(G_Fc(z)\otimes G_Fc(w)) =\frac{x^dN^{d-1}}{d!}B_N(Q\bar z,\bar w). \qquad\text{(13)} Its extra phase is q2a=Qaq^{2a}=Q^a. Equation (13) is not a formula for the unrestricted row on an arbitrary exterior; (11) is.

These cap functions retain a known finite Gaussian chirp: BN(z,w)=exp⁡[πiN(z∂z)2](z+w)d,ℱN′(t)=ℱN(t)−QℱN(Qt),ℱN(0)=1.(14) B_N(z,w)=\exp\!\left[\frac{\pi i}{N}(z\partial_z)^2\right](z+w)^d, \qquad \mathcal F_N'(t)=\mathcal F_N(t)-Q\mathcal F_N(Qt),\quad \mathcal F_N(0)=1 . \qquad\text{(14)} The first equality is a finite diagonal operation on degrees through dd. The second follows from Fa+1=(1−Qa+1)FaF_{a+1}=(1-Q^{a+1})F_a; the potential top term is zero because 1−QN=01-Q^N=0. No reflection rule for a separated ray moment is being assumed. The original cup, pivots, framing/peripheral lines, and the full marked row remain in (9) or (11); none becomes (3) by a choice of metric.

5. Local symbol and exact coefficient-dual packet inversion

For independent complex arguments let LN=z+QwL_N=z+Qw and L=z+wL=z+w. The only zero divisor of (3) is LN=0L_N=0, of order dd. On a fixed compact chart where L≠0L\ne0, choose a local logarithm. Stirling’s formula and the first quantum shift give uniformly 𝒦N(z,w)=N−1/2exp⁡(2πiw/L)2πLexp⁡{N(1+Log⁡L)}(1+O(N−1)).(15) \mathscr K_N(z,w)=N^{-1/2} \frac{\exp(2\pi i w/L)}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,L} \exp\{N(1+\operatorname{Log} L)\}(1+O(N^{-1})). \qquad\text{(15)} Indeed Nd/d!=eN/2πN(1+O(N−1))N^d/d!=e^N/\sqrt{2\pi N}(1+O(N^{-1})), and NLog⁡LN=NLog⁡L+2πiw/L+O(N−1)N\operatorname{Log} L_N=N\operatorname{Log} L+2\pi i w/L+O(N^{-1}). The exact phase qq and shift QQ remain in (3); the shift produces the nonzero finite amplitude factor in (15).

There is also an exact action on coefficient polynomials, without any incomplete-exponential estimate: PHNu(z)=qzdPu(Q/z).(16) \boxed{\qquad P_{H_Nu}(z)=qz^dP_u(Q/z).\qquad} \qquad\text{(16)} It is a polynomial even at zero. If a supplied actual coefficient packet has a relative expansion PuN(w)=cNNaA(w)eNϕ(w)(1+O(N−1))P_{u_N}(w)=c_NN^aA(w)e^{N\phi(w)}(1+O(N^{-1})) on a fixed neighborhood of f≠0f\ne0, then on a corresponding neighborhood of f−1f^{-1}, (16) gives PHNuN(z)=cNNaqA(Q/z)zexp⁡{N[Log⁡z+ϕ(Q/z)]}(1+O(N−1)).(17) P_{H_Nu_N}(z)=c_NN^a\frac{qA(Q/z)}z \exp\{N[\operatorname{Log} z+\phi(Q/z)]\}(1+O(N^{-1})). \qquad\text{(17)} The relative error is merely pulled back. The amplitude is nonzero if the input amplitude is. Formula (17) concerns the coefficient HNH_N-action or the specified coefficient form-dual packet. It does not replace the physical similarity (8) or the Gaussian Riesz operator by that action.

At principal order the occupation transforms as z∂z[Log⁡z+ϕ(1/z)]=1−wϕ′(w)z\partial_z[\operatorname{Log} z+\phi(1/z)]=1-w\phi'(w), w=1/zw=1/z. Thus the lower resolved point (f,0)(f,0) of [B] is paired with an upper occupation, with its distinct logarithmic mark retained. The form kernel is regular at the associated matched seam: complexify the Gaussian variables to (z,ζ,w,ω)(z,\zeta,w,\omega). Its principal seam phase is Log⁡(z+ω)−zζ−wω\operatorname{Log}(z+\omega)-z\zeta-w\omega, giving ζ=w=1z+ω,ζz+wω=1.(18) \zeta=w=\frac1{z+\omega},\qquad \zeta z+w\omega=1 . \qquad\text{(18)} This is the Gaussian FORM-dual seam: its left row coordinates are (ζ,z)(\zeta,z). It is different from (17), whose coefficient-action output has polynomial coordinate zoutz_{\mathrm{ out}}. The two maps are not identified by their common occupation reflection. For the lower right point (w,ω)=(f,0)(w,\omega)=(f,0), this gives (ζ,z)=(f,f−1)(\zeta,z)=(f,f^{-1}) and kernel linear form f−1≠0f^{-1}\ne0. The mixed kernel derivative is −1/(z+ω)2≠0-1/(z+\omega)^2\ne0. This verifies a nonzero local seam symbol on these matched charts, uniformly for ff in compact subsets of ℂ*\mathbb C^*.

It does not prove that the actual marked exterior is that dual packet, that a full composed stationary Hessian is nondegenerate, or that complementary regions have smaller rate. All finite integrals above remain whole; no source-current component or integration region has been discarded. The caps (11)–(13) still require their actual phase analysis in a general short component. No scalar lower bound follows from the indefinite form or local ellipticity alone.
