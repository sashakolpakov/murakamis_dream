Regular bends for the external braid representation and the Newton turn

Private research proof, 14 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The external repository at commit e956fb919ac7d1bbc7cb3d3873fbac993feb94b7 implements braid matrices and a prescribed reduced trace. It does not implement a cup, cap, or quarter-turn API. This note specifies a self-dual boundary extension of that braid representation and checks its bends explicitly. At the principal root its turn becomes the recorded full Newton reversal, with the original cup and cap scalars. No equality of unmarked closed scalars is used to identify open operators.

1. A regular external cup and cap

Fix N≥2N\ge2, put d=N−1d=N-1, and use ζ=A−2,v=A2=ζ−1,q=eπi/N,ρζ=ζ−ζ−1.(1) \zeta=A^{-2},\qquad v=A^2=\zeta^{-1},\qquad q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad \rho_\zeta=\zeta-\zeta^{-1}. \qquad\text{(1)} All parameter families below are taken near ζ=q\zeta=q. We use [a]ζ=(ζa−ζ−a)/(ζ−ζ−1)[a]_\zeta=(\zeta^a-\zeta^{-a})/(\zeta-\zeta^{-1}) and [a]ζ!=∏r=1a[r]ζ[a]_\zeta!=\prod_{r=1}^a[r]_\zeta, with [0]ζ!=1[0]_\zeta!=1; the same convention applies to vv. The external divided-power basis e0,…,ede_0,\ldots,e_d has Kvei=vd−2iei,Evei=[N−i]vei−1,Fvei=[i+1]vei+1,(2) K_v e_i=v^{d-2i}e_i,\qquad E_v e_i=[N-i]_v e_{i-1},\qquad F_v e_i=[i+1]_v e_{i+1}, \qquad\text{(2)} with missing endpoint vectors zero. The coproduct is ΔEv=Ev⊗Kv+1⊗Ev,ΔFv=Fv⊗1+Kv−1⊗Fv.(3) \Delta E_v=E_v\otimes K_v+1\otimes E_v, \qquad \Delta F_v=F_v\otimes1+K_v^{-1}\otimes F_v. \qquad\text{(3)} The external positive-crossing formula is the checked universal crossing in this basis: EvkE_v^k supplies the product of [N−i]v,…,[N−i+k−1]v[N-i]_v,\ldots,[N-i+k-1]_v, and Fvk/[k]v!F_v^k/[k]_v! supplies [j+kk]v{j+k\brack k}_v. Thus (2) is a concrete realization of the coefficients used by the external code, not an independently chosen representation with the same dimension.

Define ri(ζ)=[i]ζ![d−i]ζ![d]ζ!,c0(ζ)=Nζd(d−1)/2, r_i(\zeta)=\frac{[i]_\zeta![d-i]_\zeta!}{[d]_\zeta!}, \qquad c_0(\zeta)=N\zeta^{d(d-1)/2}, and the two coefficient lists ci(ζ)=c0(ζ)(−1)ivi(d−i−1)ri(ζ),bi(ζ)=(−1)dvdc0(ζ)(−1)ivi(i−d−1)ri(ζ)−1.(4) \begin{split} c_i(\zeta)&=c_0(\zeta)(-1)^i v^{i(d-i-1)}r_i(\zeta),\\ b_i(\zeta)&=\frac{(-1)^d v^d}{c_0(\zeta)} (-1)^i v^{i(i-d-1)}r_i(\zeta)^{-1}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(4)} The cup pvp_v and cap ℓv\ell_v are pv(1)=∑i=0dciei⊗ed−i,ℓv(ei⊗ej)=bi𝟏i+j=d.(5) p_v(1)=\sum_{i=0}^d c_i e_i\otimes e_{d-i}, \qquad \ell_v(e_i\otimes e_j)=b_i\mathbf1_{i+j=d}. \qquad\text{(5)} Every factor [a]ζ[a]_\zeta, 1≤a<N1\le a<N, is nonzero at qq. Thus these are regular families, with no quantum-dimension denominator.

Lemma 1. The maps (5) are invariant for (2)–(3), and both snake contractions equal the identity.

Proof. The weight of every supported pair is zero. The raising equation for the cup and the lowering equation for the cap are, respectively, ci+1ci=−vd−2i−2[i+1]v[d−i]v,bi+1bi=−v−d+2i[d−i]v[i+1]v.(6) \frac{c_{i+1}}{c_i} =-v^{d-2i-2}\frac{[i+1]_v}{[d-i]_v}, \qquad \frac{b_{i+1}}{b_i} =-v^{-d+2i}\frac{[d-i]_v}{[i+1]_v}. \qquad\text{(6)} They follow immediately from (4), since [a]v=[a]ζ[a]_v=[a]_\zeta. Substitution in the other two equations gives ΔFvpv=0\Delta F_v p_v=0 and ℓvΔEv=0\ell_v\Delta E_v=0 as well. More explicitly, the coefficient at ea⊗ed+1−ae_a\otimes e_{d+1-a} in the first is [a]vca−1+v−d+2a[d+1−a]vca=0[a]_v c_{a-1}+v^{-d+2a}[d+1-a]_v c_a=0; the corresponding cap equation is v−d−2+2a[d+1−a]vba−1+[a]vba=0v^{-d-2+2a}[d+1-a]_v b_{a-1}+[a]_v b_a=0.

For the snakes, ri=rd−ir_i=r_{d-i} and direct multiplication give cibd−i=bicd−i=1.(7) c_i b_{d-i}=b_i c_{d-i}=1. \qquad\text{(7)} Each contraction has exactly one surviving summand at a given input index, with coefficient (7). This proves both identities. ▫\square

The invariant cup line is one-dimensional: the nonzero adjacent coefficients in (6) determine it from c0c_0. The cap is then fixed by the snakes. The choice of c0c_0 in (4) specifies the normalization needed below. Replacing the cup and cap by reciprocal nonzero scalar multiples does not change their quarter-turn contraction.

There is a convention worth keeping explicit. The all-primal loop is ℓvpv=(−1)d[N]v.(8) \ell_v p_v=(-1)^d[N]_v. \qquad\text{(8)} Indeed cibi=(−1)dvd−2ic_i b_i=(-1)^d v^{d-2i}. This is the signed self-dual convention, not a claim that the external code’s prescribed KvK_v-trace has acquired the factor (−1)d(-1)^d. That trace and its marked cut must be transported separately.

2. The actual change of basis and its root value

In the generic balanced module use K|a⟩=ζd−2a|a⟩,ℰ|a⟩=(ζ−N−ζN−2a)|a−1⟩,ℱ|a⟩=(1−ζ2a+2)|a+1⟩.(9) K|a\rangle=\zeta^{d-2a}|a\rangle,\quad \mathcal E|a\rangle=(\zeta^{-N}-\zeta^{N-2a})|a-1\rangle, \quad \mathcal F|a\rangle=(1-\zeta^{2a+2})|a+1\rangle. \qquad\text{(9)} These are the regular highest-weight family in the Newton manuscript. Its explicit basis change from the ordinary basis uau_a is |a⟩=ρζa(ζ2;ζ2)aua.(10) |a\rangle=\frac{\rho_\zeta^a}{(\zeta^2;\zeta^2)_a}u_a. \qquad\text{(10)} Every denominator in (10) is nonzero near the selected root.

The local crossing adapter uses the regular anti-diagonal matrix Tζei=ti(ζ)|d−i⟩,ti(ζ)=ζ−i(i−1)/2.(11) T_\zeta e_i=t_i(\zeta)|d-i\rangle, \qquad t_i(\zeta)=\zeta^{-i(i-1)/2}. \qquad\text{(11)} It respects the generator structure, not only the crossing matrix: direct substitution gives TζKvTζ−1=K,TζEvTζ−1=−ζ−2ρζ−1ℱK,TζFvTζ−1=−ζ2ρζ−1K−1ℰ.(12) \begin{split} T_\zeta K_vT_\zeta^{-1}&=K,\\ T_\zeta E_vT_\zeta^{-1} &=-\zeta^{-2}\rho_\zeta^{-1}\mathcal F K,\\ T_\zeta F_vT_\zeta^{-1} &=-\zeta^2\rho_\zeta^{-1}K^{-1}\mathcal E. \end{split} \qquad\text{(12)} For example, the first raising coefficient on the left is [a+1]ζζd−a−1[a+1]_\zeta\zeta^{d-a-1}. Also Δ(ℱK)=ℱK⊗K+1⊗ℱK,Δ(K−1ℰ)=K−1ℰ⊗1+K−1⊗K−1ℰ.(13) \Delta(\mathcal F K)=\mathcal F K\otimes K+1\otimes\mathcal F K, \quad \Delta(K^{-1}\mathcal E)=K^{-1}\mathcal E\otimes1 +K^{-1}\otimes K^{-1}\mathcal E. \qquad\text{(13)} Thus (12) respects the displayed coproducts. 3. Quarter-turns and the full Newton algebra

Define the external quarter-turn by the actual contraction 𝒯v(B)=(I⊗2⊗ℓv)(I⊗B⊗I)(pv⊗I⊗2).(17) \mathcal T_v(B)= (I^{\otimes2}\otimes\ell_v)(I\otimes B\otimes I) (p_v\otimes I^{\otimes2}). \qquad\text{(17)} Write T2,ζ=Tζ⊗TζT_{2,\zeta}=T_\zeta\otimes T_\zeta. With the regular transported bends from Section 2, the corresponding balanced turn satisfies T2,ζ𝒯v(B)T2,ζ−1=𝒯bal,ζ(T2,ζBT2,ζ−1).(18) T_{2,\zeta}\mathcal T_v(B)T_{2,\zeta}^{-1} =\mathcal T_{\mathrm{bal},\zeta} (T_{2,\zeta}BT_{2,\zeta}^{-1}). \qquad\text{(18)} This follows by substituting the two tensors from (5) and (11) into (17). Every internal index has one factor tit_i and its inverse; they cancel. The two factors at the cup and cap are precisely those in their transported coefficients. The four remaining boundary factors are the two outer matrices in (18). This is an entrywise finite tensor identity, without a semisimple decomposition.

At ζ=q\zeta=q, (15) identifies the balanced turn in (18) with the exact turn in the Newton manuscript. Let PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,er(z)=∏j<r(q2j+1+q−2j−1−z)∏j=1r4sin⁡2(πj/N),0≤r<N, P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\quad e_r(z)=\frac{\prod_{j<r}(q^{2j+1}+q^{-2j-1}-z)} {\prod_{j=1}^r4\sin^2(\pi j/N)}, \qquad 0\le r<N, and Ωext=T2,q−1ΩT2,q\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}=T_{2,q}^{-1}\Omega T_{2,q}. The full commutant and Newton-turn propositions therefore give 𝒯v(er(Ωext))=ed−r(Ωext).(19) \boxed{\quad \mathcal T_v\bigl(e_r(\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}})\bigr) =e_{d-r}(\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}). \quad} \qquad\text{(19)} The underlying matrix algebra is faithful to ℂ[z]/PN(z)\mathbb C[z]/P_N(z). Its double-root blocks retain both f(c)f(c) and f′(c)f'(c); hence (19) includes every Casimir first normal. It is stronger than equality at the distinct eigenvalues.

Equations (4)–(13) and (17)–(18) are regular parameter identities, so they can be differentiated in ζ\zeta to any fixed order while retaining the derivatives of Tζ,pv,ℓvT_\zeta,p_v,\ell_v. Formula (19) is the root specialization. It does not license replacing the generic turn by constant reversal when taking parameter derivatives.

For completeness, the original marked cap can also be retained entrywise at the root. If mab=xb/N2m_{ab}=x^b/N^2, its transported row is mijext=ti(q)tj(q)xd−jN2,(20) m^{\mathrm{ext}}_{ij} =\frac{t_i(q)t_j(q)x^{d-j}}{N^2}, \qquad\text{(20)} without a condition i+j=di+j=d. On the cup (5), its NN surviving summands are each 1/N1/N, so mextpv=1m^{\mathrm{ext}}p_v=1. The marked row is not substituted for the ordinary cap in (17). Nor is a generic extension of this marked row being inferred from its root value; the external code’s prescribed open trace has its separately specified generic family.

4. Sources and limits of the conclusion

The external entry formula is in python/rivin_jones/r_matrix.py at the commit stated above, SHA256 b6c5b48a9094587faa2ea6a9467ff801d838cfb007974a0fcd22a6e11aabfa76. Its actual crossing comparison under (11), including the reversed crossing sign, is proved separately in research_rivin_jones_local_crossing_adapter.md.

The physical pair algebra, generic balanced family, literal maps (16), and full Newton-turn proposition used in (19) are in Sections 1–2 of papers/newton-calculus/finite.tex, SHA256 2db2d7e15df0210c32bace4c761bdabadbb5d67140473a61425dac4d1e382f8e. The proof there uses physical block support and the unique outermost tridiagonal path, so the repeated-root coefficients are not inferred from a limit of eigenvalue-only identities.

This result supplies a regular local boundary adapter for bends. It does not assert that the external repository already offers such a boundary API, that its prescribed cut trace can be replaced on an arbitrary exterior by a scalar average, or that a resulting quantum period has a nonzero geometric asymptotic coefficient.
