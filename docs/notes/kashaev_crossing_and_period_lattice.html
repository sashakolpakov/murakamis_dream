The specified Kashaev crossing and its period pairing

Research note, 25 October 2026. This uses Kashaev’s crossing weights, q-alg/9504020, equations (2.7)–(2.12), and the enhanced braid definition recorded in Murakami–Murakami, math/9905075, Sections 1 and 4. Their comparison proofs are not premises. We derive the Fourier identity and normalization comparison below directly from finite products.

This is an independent verification of a known comparison, not a new volume theorem. It fixes the literature state to which a quantum A-module argument must apply. All integers N>=2, including even and composite levels, are allowed.

The subsequent Casimir/lattice comparison expresses this same crossing and its inverse by confluent Casimir polynomials, retaining the double-point derivatives and exact writhe phase. It also derives the actual pivotal closing trace on that spectral algebra and identifies the operators through the singular cyclic recoupling lattice.

1. The Fourier transform is triangular

Put omega=exp(2pii/N), p_j=product_(h=1)j(1-omegah), p_0=1, and bar_p_j=product_(h=1)j(1-omega(-h)). Let R^o be exactly the unbraided matrix in Kashaev’s equation (2.12). Use

F_ij=omega^(ij)/sqrt(N),       A=(F^(-1) tensor F^(-1))
                                R^o(F tensor F).

Fourier identity. With indices in {0,…,N-1}, the only nonzero entries are

A_(a-k,b+k ; a,b)
   =omega^(1+(a-k)(b+1))
      p_a p_(b+k)/(p_(a-k) p_b p_k),
0<=k<=min(a,N-1-b).                               (1.1)

In particular, conservation here is equality of ordinary integer sums, not just equality modulo N.

Direct proof

For a nonzero original entry introduce residues

u=[j-i-1], v=[i-l], t=[l-k], h=[k-j].

The two theta factors say u+t<N and v+h<N. Their total is congruent to -1 modulo N, so u+v+t+h=N-1. Conversely every such composition and one free j specify exactly one original entry. In these coordinates,

i=j-1-u, k=j+h, l=j+h+t  (mod N),
R^o_(i,j ; k,l)=N omega^(1-(t+h)(u+h))
                       /(p_u bar_p_v p_t bar_p_h). (1.2)

The free j-sum in the Fourier transform gives the conservation delta. Put z=t+h. The remaining sum factors into

A_(c,d ; a,b)=delta_(a+b=c+d mod N)
  sum_(z=0)^(N-1) omega^(1+c-z^2+z(a+b))
    S_(N-1-z)(omega^(c-z)) S_z(omega^(z-a)),       (1.3)

where the elementary finite binomial identity is

S_m(X)=sum_(j=0)^m X^j/(p_j bar_p_(m-j))
      =product_(j=1)^m(X-omega^j)/p_m.            (1.4)

For a direct induction, use bar_p_m=(-1)^m omega^(-m(m+1)/2)p_m and compare coefficients in (1-omegam)S_m(X)=(X-omegam)S_(m-1)(X).

The first factor in (1.3) vanishes unless c<=z; the second vanishes unless z<=a. Using p_(N-1)=N and the same formula for bar_p_j, the surviving sum, with m=a-c, is

omega^(1+c(b+1)) (p_a/p_c)
  sum_(j=0)^m (-1)^j omega^(j(j-1)/2)
                      omega^((b+1)j)/(p_j p_(m-j))
=omega^(1+c(b+1)) (p_a/p_c)
                       (omega^(b+1);omega)_m/p_m. (1.5)

The last equality follows from the same finite binomial identity. If b+m>=N, this Pochhammer symbol contains 1-omega^N and vanishes. Otherwise d=b+m as an ordinary integer and the symbol is p_(b+m)/p_b. This proves (1.1), with no primality assumption.

2. A literal comparison with the root-of-unity quantum-group matrix

Let s=exp(pi*i/N), H_j=N-1-2j, and use the N-dimensional highest-weight module with

H v_j=H_j v_j, K v_j=s^(H_j)v_j,
E v_j=[j]_s^2 v_(j-1), F_low v_j=v_(j+1).

At the endpoints the missing vectors are zero. These are the specialized usual matrices E v_j=[j]_s[N-j]s v(j-1), since [N-j]_s=[j]_s. Let

R^q=s^(H tensor H/2)
   sum_(k=0)^(N-1) s^(k(k-1)/2)(s-s^(-1))^k/[k]_s!
                                  E^k tensor F_low^k. (2.1)

Every denominator in (2.1) is nonzero. Put

G_j=p_j/(1-omega)^j=s^(j(j-1)/2)[j]_s!,
D_j=(-s)^j,
gamma=omega*s^(-(N-1)^2/2).

Then (1.1) gives exactly

A=gamma (D G tensor G) R^q
                     (G^(-1) tensor G^(-1)D^(-1)). (2.2)

Here G,D are diagonal. To verify (2.2), substitute the matrix elements of E^k and F_low^k in (2.1), replace each [j]_s! by s(-j(j-1)/2)p_j/(1-omega)j, and cancel. The remaining exponent and factorials are precisely those in (1.1). Thus exponentially large factorials in G have not been declared harmless; their positions in the exact conjugations remain specified.

3. The published enhancement and every braid word

Let P swap tensor factors, and let S v_j=v_(j+1 mod N). In the enhanced Kashaev convention used here,

B_K=P(R^o)^T,       mu_K=-s S,       alpha_K=-s,
beta_K=1.                                         (3.1)

The transpose/order in (3.1) matters. Substituting (2.8) of Kashaev in P(Ro)T gives exactly the four-index enhanced crossing definition. For the quantum-group dual convention put

B_q=P(R^q)^T,       mu_q=K^(-1),
alpha_q=s^((N^2-1)/2),       beta_q=1.             (3.2)

Equation (2.2), transposed and with the tensor factors exchanged, gives a similarity of B_K with gamma*B_q. For a braid on b strands, define

U_b=F^(-tensor b) tensor_(j=1)^b (G^(-1)D^(j-1)).

The meaning is the product of F^(-tensor b) with the displayed diagonal tensor product. Since B_q conserves the integer weight sum, it commutes with D tensor D. Thus the same U_b works simultaneously for every adjacent generator and its inverse:

rho_K(word)=gamma^(w(word)) U_b rho_q(word) U_b^(-1).
                                                           (3.3)

The enhancement also matches exactly:

U_b mu_q^(tensor b) U_b^(-1)=mu_K^(tensor b),
gamma*alpha_q=-s=alpha_K.                         (3.4)

For the first identity, diagonal G,D commute with mu_q and F(-1)diag(omegaj)F=S; also s^(-(N-1))=-s.

The quantum-group partial traces have values alpha_q and alpha_q^(-1). One way to verify the scalars is to use the coproduct intertwining equations for (2.1) and trace cyclicity: the weighted partial traces commute with the dual module’s generators. These act irreducibly because their adjacent coefficients are nonzero and the K eigenvalues are distinct. The traces are therefore scalar. The positive trace at v_(N-1) and negative trace at v_0 each have a single contributing term, giving s((N2-1)/2) and its inverse. The coproduct equations themselves reduce to the coefficient recurrence

c_(k+1)/c_k=s^k(s-s^(-1))/[k+1]_s

of the finite sum (2.1). These checks are elementary algebra and do not invoke a global state-integral theorem.

For a braid closure with one component, cut the first strand and take the weighted partial trace over the others. The unnormalized operator is scalar: it is a quantum-group endomorphism of the simple color. Conjugations on the traced factors cancel by cyclicity; conjugation on the open factor cancels because this operator is scalar. Finally the factors gamma^(w) cancel against alpha_K^(-w) by (3.4).

Consequence. The normalized enhanced Kashaev period in (3.1) equals the reduced N-colored quantum-group period at s=exp(pi*i/N), with unknot value one. This holds for every braid presentation of a knot and every N>=2. It is a direct independent verification of the standard enhanced-state comparison, not an appeal to its published proof. The usual quantum-group parameter for the colored Jones polynomial is omega=s^2; the chosen dual and tensor-order convention is retained.

4. A printed phase needs attention

The literal candidate inverse printed in Kashaev’s (2.15), combined with the normalization (2.12), fails the identity (2.16) already at N=2: the product is -I. This is checked in characteristic zero with rational entries. The exact cyclotomic diagnostics at further levels give omega*I. We use the actual matrix inverse in (3.3), determined by (2.2), rather than silently deleting this phase.

This observation concerns a printed normalization identity, not a counterexample to the knot volume conjecture. The enhanced definition (3.1) specifies its own actual inverse and scalar normalization.

5. Reproducible algebraic checks and scope

experiments/audit_kashaev_crossing.py evaluates the original four-index weight independently of (1.1), checks its complete Fourier transform, the quantum-group matrix comparison, and the enhanced partial traces. It includes even and composite levels. Prime-field diagnostics are supplementary to the characteristic-zero identities proved above.

The coefficient and enhancement identities fix a literature state uniformly, with no FAMED hypothesis or knot census. They do not prove the AJ conjecture or the Kashaev volume conjecture. In particular, they do not make the AK/Faddeev contour state equal to this finite period. That is a different comparison.

A standard noncommutative A-polynomial can now be discussed together with this distinguished reduced quantum-group period, rather than an arbitrary solution of its recurrence. Root-of-unity periodicity and the geometric q->1 branch must still be distinguished in its period-torsor comparison.
