The one-cap exterior has an original group representation, with a positive pyramid deficit

31 October 2026. analytical research theorem. Unpublished. Hypotheses are the strict ONE-cap stationary configuration of the geometric-double theorem: the original simple matched sphere has protected MAIN edge uv, F=(u,A,v,C), all other original ports CROSS, all ordinary incidences positive and finite common weights. The cap split is 1/4<nu<1/2.

This constructs a representation of the original UNTWISTED drilled group using the positive exterior packing. It is NOT the failed direct specialization of the signed sigma=+2 source chart. The latter’s raw central/noncentral obstruction is unchanged. The construction gives a quantified absolute budget, not the desired nonpositive cap deficit.

1. Boundary packing and the actual ordered collar

Normalize the four boundary contacts as

uA=1,AC=0,Cv=−1,uv=∞. uA=1,\qquad AC=0,\qquad Cv=-1,\qquad uv=\infty.\tag{1}

Put k=cot(pi nu), phi=atan k, so 0<k<1 and phi=pi/2-pi nu in (0,pi/4). The actual white circles have centers

center⁡(A)=1+ik2,center⁡(C)=−1+ik2,radius⁡(A)=radius⁡(C)=1+k22,u:X−kY=1,v:X−kY=−1. \begin{aligned} \operatorname{center}(A)&=\frac{1+ik}{2},& \operatorname{center}(C)&=-\frac{1+ik}{2},\\ \operatorname{radius}(A)&=\operatorname{radius}(C) =\frac{\sqrt{1+k^2}}2,& u:&\ X-kY=1,\qquad v:\ X-kY=-1. \end{aligned}\tag{2}

The recorded exterior D lies on the LOWER side of the real contact line. In particular the A fan is the minor arc from0 to1 below the line, with angle pi-2phi=2pi nu. This fixes the side and orientation; the circles alone would not select which complementary disk is D.

Write d0=sqrt(1+k^2). The following anti-Mobius reflection matrices have determinant -1 and the original coorientations:

JA=d0−1(1+ik02−1+ik),JC=d0−1(−1−ik021−ik),Ju=d0−1(1−ik−20−1−ik),Jv=d0−1(−1+ik−201+ik). \begin{aligned} J_A&=d_0^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1+ik&0\\2&-1+ik\end{pmatrix},& J_C&=d_0^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}-1-ik&0\\2&1-ik\end{pmatrix},\\ J_u&=d_0^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1-ik&-2\\0&-1-ik\end{pmatrix},& J_v&=d_0^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}-1+ik&-2\\0&1+ik\end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned}\tag{3}

Thus every original MAIN product at this port has trace -2. Set

Ab=JAJu¯,Bb=JCJA¯,Av=JvJu¯,Bv=JCJv¯,Praw=BbAb=BvAv=JCJu¯. \begin{aligned} A_b&=J_A\overline{J_u},& B_b&=J_C\overline{J_A},\\ A_v&=J_v\overline{J_u},& B_v&=J_C\overline{J_v},\\ P_{\mathrm{raw}}&=B_bA_b=B_vA_v=J_C\overline{J_u}. \end{aligned}\tag{4}

Its raw trace is -2(3-k2)/(1+k2)<-2. The harmless central choice P=-P_raw has positive trace. Define the projective collar

𝒞=(a−1−11),a=−1+3ik1+ik=1−2e−2iϕ. \mathcal C=\begin{pmatrix}a&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad a=\frac{-1+3ik}{1+ik}=1-2e^{-2i\phi}.\tag{5}

Direct multiplication gives the literal ordered relations

𝒞Ab𝒞−1=Av,𝒞Bb𝒞−1=Bv,𝒞Praw=Praw𝒞. \mathcal C A_b\mathcal C^{-1}=A_v,\qquad \mathcal C B_b\mathcal C^{-1}=B_v,\qquad \mathcal C P_{\mathrm{raw}}=P_{\mathrm{raw}}\mathcal C.\tag{6}

It sends the recorded flags 1->infinity and0->-1. It preserves each of the two outer white planes: projectively 𝒞\mathcal C J_u=J_u bar(𝒞\mathcal C) and 𝒞\mathcal C J_C=J_C bar(𝒞\mathcal C). The scalar in both raw identities is (k+i)/(k-i), so no equality of unscaled anti-linear matrices is being asserted. The determinant is -2 exp(-2i phi). Hence

Ĉ=𝒞i2e−iϕ,det⁡Ĉ=1,Tr⁡Ĉ=22sin⁡ϕ=2cos⁡ψ,0<ψ<π2. \widehat C=\frac{\mathcal C}{i\sqrt2e^{-i\phi}},\qquad \det\widehat C=1,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}\widehat C=2\sqrt2\sin\phi=2\cos\psi, \qquad 0<\psi<\frac\pi2.\tag{7}

The actual untwisted auxiliary meridian is the projective Ĉ−1\widehat C^{-1}; the preferred longitude is P (up to the recorded central sign). There is no introduced framing, half twist or replacement peripheral word. Every other original coupon has its usual tangent-circle collar from the complete positive exterior packing.

2. Both auxiliary flags, with their side fixed

The common fixed points of P and 𝒞\mathcal C solve

O2−2e−2iϕO−1=0. O^2-2e^{-2i\phi}O-1=0.

Write alpha=arcsin(sqrt(2)sin phi) in (0,pi/2), ell=asinh(cos alpha)>0. They are

Oout=eℓ−iα,Oin=−1Oout=e−ℓ−i(π−α). O_{\mathrm{out}}=e^{\ell-i\alpha},\qquad O_{\mathrm{in}}=-\frac1{O_{\mathrm{out}}} =e^{-\ell-i(\pi-\alpha)}.\tag{8}

Both lie STRICTLY BELOW the real line, on the existing exterior side. For the explicit P=-P_raw in (4), O_in is the expanding flag and O_out the contracting flag. Ĉ\widehat C has eigenvalues exp(-i psi), exp(+i psi) there respectively, with psi=pi/2-alpha. Thus the meridian eigenvalue at the expanding longitude flag is exp(+i psi). Using the other flag does not change the volume calculation below.

Reflection r_u swaps these flags: r_u P_raw r_u=P_raw^-1 and P_raw is genuinely loxodromic. The original cap parameter is NOT a real spectral delta in the earlier intersecting/signed source chart. No identification with that chart’s local source action or its regulator primitive is used here.

3. Original group sewing and its non-reflected auxiliary flag

Use the single global white circle family supplied by the geometric double, restricted to D and its four boundary faces. At every original MAIN edge the two adjacent white circles are tangent, so its ordered head is the actual noncentral parabolic reflection product. The reflection prefixes at a Morse slice telescope exactly. A cup/cap inserts F,G,F and its two ordered heads multiply to identity. Every unchanged CROSS box has its original primitive collar. At F use (6). These are precisely the original untwisted van Kampen/Morse relations; no local spectral interpretation is required to construct the group representation. This is the algebraic part of the variable-headcount group dictionary, independent of its source-action application.

Here is an explicit compatible ideal-chain decoration, which avoids the false assertion that the chosen cap flag lies on both outer white planes. On the first original cut ball assign all unchanged contact vertices their exterior packing contacts. Assign its matching uC vertex one selected O in (8). On the second ball reflect every OTHER contact vertex by the ONE global r_u, but assign the cap vertex the SAME O, not r_u(O). This exception is essential.

The original white pairing for face f is r_f r_u. On white u this is identity, so it matches O to O. On white C it is P_raw, which also fixes O. These are the only white faces containing this cap vertex. All their other vertices match by the usual reflection argument. Every other white polygon remains exactly the reflected polygon. Therefore the entire triangulated white face chains pair correctly, although the two altered polygons need no longer lie in white planes. Use the existing original abstract fan triangulations, chosen compatibly across paired faces. Degree one comes from the original positive polyhedron’s abstract domain certificate, not from convexity or planarity of these newly developed polygons.

The first-ball shaded cap pairing is 𝒞\mathcal C with its original permutation

(O,1,0)↦(O,∞,−1). (O,1,0)\longmapsto(O,\infty,-1).

The second-ball pairing is r_u 𝒞\mathcal C r_u. It fixes the SAME O because r_u swaps the two fixed flags and 𝒞\mathcal C fixes both. It sends the other two reflected contacts in the same recorded order. All other shaded pairings are unchanged. Consequently these assignments give the original degree-one relative fundamental chain, with the original within-copy untwisted permutations and one common choice of all other flags. They are not the differently marked chain of the doubled link.

The auxiliary peripheral subgroup is of axis type, generated by the elliptic Ĉ−1\widehat C^{-1} and loxodromic P with common fixed flags. All other cusps remain boundary-parabolic. The already proved general-peripheral volume theorem, section 2, identifies the sum of the developed ideal tetrahedron volumes with the canonical representation volume also in this axis case. Its explicit cusp homotopy has boundary flux O(y^-2); no horosphere preservation is asserted for P, and no extra arbitrary peripheral constant can be added to this ideal-chain calculation.

4. The cap is a pyramid subtraction, not a zero-volume patch

In the convex doubled polyhedron the four contacts (1) lie in the vertical geodesic plane over the real line. They form an ideal quadrilateral K. The intersections of this plane with the four boundary white planes are its four sides. Convexity shows the plane’s intersection with the doubled polyhedron is EXACTLY K: K is contained by convexity of its four vertices, and the four white halfspaces cut out no larger region. The plane splits the doubled polyhedron into the two exterior pieces. In this normalization the copy involution is the orientation-preserving half-turn z↦−zz\mapsto-z, which exchanges their sides; it is not reflection in the real contact line. Thus equality of the two volumes does not presume a reflected packing. The recorded D side is the lower side: absence of the boundary diagonal uC forces an interior neighbor in the A fan, whose positive angle lies on the minor arc below the real line. This fixes which of the two components is D, consistently with (2). Each has volume

VD=πMcap. V_D=\pi M_{\mathrm{cap}}.\tag{9}

The first original ball’s boundary is obtained from the D piece by replacing K with the four-triangle cone to O. Two of these triangles are the original shaded cap faces; the other two are the additions to the white u and C faces. This is a statement about the oriented abstract boundary chain, not a convexity claim for its new image.

Since O lies on the EXISTING D side of K, that oriented replacement SUBTRACTS the volume of the ideal pyramid with base K and apex O. Using Vol[infinity,0,1,z]=D(z), its positive volume is

W(O)=D(−O)−D(O)>0,ℑO<0. W(O)=D(-O)-D(O)>0,\qquad \Im O<0.\tag{10}

Here is a chain-level sign check in this tetrahedron convention. The outward cut face is

K=[∞,−1,0]+[∞,0,1]. K=[\infty,-1,0]+[\infty,0,1].

The NEW-ball-minus-exterior chain is

S=[O,∞,1,0]+[O,∞,0,−1],∂S=fan⁡O(∂K)−K. S=[O,\infty,1,0]+[O,\infty,0,-1],\qquad \partial S=\operatorname{fan}_O(\partial K)-K.

Its boundary is exactly fan_O(boundary K)-K, and its volume is D(O)-D(-O). This fixes the subtraction sign without identifying the orientation of a listed pairing tuple with an oriented face chain. This provides an absolute orientation/calibration, not a differential guess or a claim that the four flat contacts alone make the cap zero. Thus the first original ball has signed volume V_D-W(O).

Reflecting the second ball back by r_u and reversing its abstract orientation gives the same exterior D piece but the OTHER auxiliary flag r_u(O). Formula (8) and D(1/z)=-D(z) imply

W(Oin)=W(Oout). W(O_{\mathrm{in}})=W(O_{\mathrm{out}}).

The exact two-ball canonical-chain identity is therefore

Vol⁡(ρ)=2πMcap−2W(Oout). \operatorname{Vol}(\rho)=2\pi M_{\mathrm{cap}}-2W(O_{\mathrm{out}}). \tag{11}

Both the factor two and the sign come from the literal ball pairings. The original all-CROSS source, source spectral phase, and its possible cancellation have not been changed or identified with this representation.

5. Explicit positive budget and its sharp elementary range

Put O=O_out from (8). One has

|O−1|2=2,1+O1−O=−O¯. |O-1|^2=2,\qquad \frac{1+O}{1-O}=-\overline O.

The Bloch–Wigner differential

dD(z)=log⁡|z|dArg⁡(1−z)−log⁡|1−z|dArg⁡z dD(z)=\log|z|\,d\operatorname{Arg}(1-z) -\log|1-z|\,d\operatorname{Arg}z

then gives dW=2ell d alpha. At alpha=0 the two tetrahedra are flat and W=0, fixing the absolute constant. Consequently

W(ν)=2∫0αarsinh⁡(cos⁡t)dt,α=arcsin⁡(2cos⁡(πν)),dWdν=−22πsin⁡(πν)arsinh⁡−cos⁡(2πν)−cos⁡(2πν). \begin{aligned} W(\nu)&=2\int_0^\alpha\operatorname{arsinh}(\cos t)\,dt, &\alpha&=\arcsin\bigl(\sqrt2\cos(\pi\nu)\bigr),\\ \frac{dW}{d\nu} &=-2\sqrt2\pi\sin(\pi\nu)\, \frac{\operatorname{arsinh}\!\sqrt{-\cos(2\pi\nu)}} {\sqrt{-\cos(2\pi\nu)}}. \end{aligned}\tag{12}

Equivalently W=integral_psi^(pi/2) 2asinh(sin t)dt. In particular W is strictly positive and strictly decreasing for 1/4<nu<1/2. Its endpoint values are

W(12)=0,W(14)=2G,0<W<2G. W(\tfrac12)=0,\qquad W(\tfrac14)=2G,\qquad 0<W<2G.\tag{13}

where G=integral_0^1 atan(t)dt/t is Catalan’s constant. To check the upper endpoint without a special-value import, differentiate I(a)=integral_0^(pi/2) asinh(a cos t)dt. The substitution u=sin t gives I’(a)=atan(a)/a and I(0)=0, hence I(1)=G.

Apply the actual original representation-volume bound to (11):

Mcap−ΦD≤W(ν)π<2Gπ=C0. M_{\mathrm{cap}}-\Phi_D \le\frac{W(\nu)}\pi<\frac{2G}\pi=C_0.\tag{14}

There is also a strict representation-theoretic improvement at any fixed configuration. The actual primitive auxiliary meridian has trace squared 4cos^2(psi)<4. The fixed-peripheral-word gap gives some g>0 for the fixed original exterior and meridian (uniform when nu ranges in a compact subset of (1/4,1/2) with those data fixed), with

Mcap−ΦD≤W(ν)π−g2π<W(ν)π. M_{\mathrm{cap}}-\Phi_D \le\frac{W(\nu)}\pi-\frac g{2\pi} <\frac{W(\nu)}\pi.\tag{15}

No available estimate asserts g>=2W, so this still does not prove the desired nonpositive deficit. The word is the ONE fixed original auxiliary meridian; no level-dependent peripheral power is used.

Equivalently the original constrained defect obeys Delta_cap<=C0+W(nu)/pi<2C0. This is a uniform complexity-independent bound for a strict ONE-cap-only stationary configuration. It is weaker than Delta_cap<=C0; it does not settle the desired absolute cap comparison. A signed-source loss exceeding W(nu)/pi would suffice to close that configuration’s absolute budget, but no such loss is asserted here. In particular a positive local loss, or a single opposite-face cofactor estimate, need not pay this entire amount.

Scope and proof dependencies

The original source incidence, mark and unsigned cap action are those of the original cap-capacity budget. The genuine exterior and its exact capacity are supplied by the one-cap geometric double. The group sewing is the literal reflection-prefix/Morse relation from the actual Morse group dictionary, section 2, used only as an algebraic group construction. The ordered within-copy shaded permutations and degree-one two-ball chain are those of the original cut-ball chain, section 3; its old all-reflected auxiliary-flag choice is explicitly REPLACED at this one cap. The canonical ideal-chain volume equality for an axis cusp is canonical volume for general peripheral images, section 2. The optional strict inequality (15) uses the fixed-peripheral-word volume gap.

In particular the old finite-collar obstruction at the signed real spectral cap remains valid and is not overridden by this different positive exterior chart.
