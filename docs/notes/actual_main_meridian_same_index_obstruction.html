Why a moving multiplication trace cannot be bounded at the same indices

1 November 2026.

A product junction in a Conway calculation multiplies several tangle operators before taking their trace. After expanding those operators in the complete Newton basis, one obtains traces of products of basis polynomials. A tempting estimate would bound each moving trace by a polynomial in the level times its complete trace at exactly the same basis indices. This estimate is false, even for a junction with nine incident factors and a strictly positive complete trace.

Here “high” refers to a Newton basis index greater than ⌊(N−1)/2⌋\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor. It does not refer to the physical color of an auxiliary crossing circle. The example has exactly one high index and satisfies the complete pairwise support inequalities. Its moving-to-complete ratio grows exponentially along both level parities.

The proof below keeps the exact quotient trace, including derivatives at repeated roots. The conclusion concerns an individual tensor entry, not a knot invariant or a whole tangle contraction. The reflected Fourier estimate and the general component comparison explain how the full source can still be controlled: they compare the change in the trace with a different, reflected index and retain the neighboring branch profiles.

3. Removing the repeated upper nodes

A divided difference records polynomial interpolation at several points; when points repeat, it records derivatives there as well. 5. What the obstruction rules out

The family disproves a uniform pointwise estimate |ϕs(∏ei)|≤NCRϕ0(∏ei)|\phi_s(\prod e_i)|\le N^{C_R}\phi_0(\prod e_i) on all complete-admissible tuples of a fixed valence. Allowing a prefactor depending on RR does not help. There is a strictly positive complete denominator, exactly one high input, and an explicit exponential lower bound. The same construction applies at both even and odd levels.

The indices lie on complete pair-equality faces. The example does not disprove an estimate restricted to a fixed strict compact interior window. More fundamentally, a tensor entry does not include the neighboring physical coefficient profiles or the sum over shared indices. Those additional factors and correlations are part of the whole Conway source. This is therefore not a counterexample to a knot-volume theorem.

The useful replacement is a bound on the difference between the moving and complete traces. In the exact Fourier formula for that difference, the first Fourier contribution cancels. The reflected Fourier theorem bounds the remaining contribution by a complete trace with the high index hh replaced by the low index d−hd-h, with a strict exponential improvement on the relevant regular windows. It does not assert the false comparison at unchanged indices proved impossible here.

The general component theorem combines this reflected estimate with exact Frobenius contraction and the original coupled positive action. Reflection can fuse two junctions in a comparison tree; the geometric pressure of that tree is no greater than the original pressure. These comparison operations bound correction terms without changing the actual knot, the original finite source, or its marked filling action. Thus the obstruction explains why that proof retains whole contractions instead of using an estimate on each unchanged tensor entry.
