A global spectral bound for an actual diagonal source sector

analytical theorem, 31 October 2026. This concerns the original homogeneous face polynomial and its original shared marked coefficient. The triangular-basis diagonal sector is not identified with the identity operator. A coefficientwise inequality reduces its absolute bound to an actual identity-inserted positive source.

1. The exact diagonal polynomial and a uniform real bound

Let N≥3N\geq3, d=N−1d=N-1, and

χN(σ)=2TN(σ/2)+2,cj=2cos⁡(2j+1)πN,0≤j<N. \chi_N(\sigma)=2T_N(\sigma/2)+2, \qquad c_j=2\cos\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{N},\quad 0\leq j<N.

In the original triangular face representation, Ω\Omega has diagonal cjc_j and lowers the face degree by one or two. The zero-step part of its actual resolvent numerator is

VN(σ)=χN(σ)(σ−Ω)−1,DN(σ;b,c)=∑j=0ddj(σ)bjcd−j,dj(σ)=χN(σ)σ−cj. \begin{aligned} V_N(\sigma)&=\chi_N(\sigma)(\sigma-\Omega)^{-1},\\ D_N(\sigma;b,c)&=\sum_{j=0}^{d}d_j(\sigma)b^jc^{d-j}, &d_j(\sigma)&=\frac{\chi_N(\sigma)}{\sigma-c_j}. \end{aligned}\tag{1}

Every quotient is a polynomial, including all repeated and simple spectral nodes. This is exactly the part of HVNH_{V_N} with zero outer exponents. The middle names b,cb,c and their original orientation are unchanged. It is a restriction of the homogeneous coefficient polynomial, not an assertion that the physical kernel has been diagonalized or made into an identity.

For every real σ∈[−2,2]\sigma\in[-2,2],

|dj(σ)|≤N2. |d_j(\sigma)|\leq N^2.\tag{2}

Indeed, χN(cj)=0\chi_N(c_j)=0 and the real mean-value integral gives

dj(σ)=∫01χN′(cj+t(σ−cj))dt. d_j(\sigma)=\int_0^1\chi_N'\bigl(c_j+t(\sigma-c_j)\bigr)\,dt.

For z=2cos⁡θz=2\cos\theta,

χN′(z)=NUN−1(cos⁡θ),UN−1(cos⁡θ)=∑r=0N−1ei(N−1−2r)θ. \chi_N'(z)=N U_{N-1}(\cos\theta),\qquad U_{N-1}(\cos\theta)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}e^{i(N-1-2r)\theta}.

Its modulus is at most NN, including the endpoint limits. Thus the removable values are handled automatically, and

|DN(σ)|coef≤N2H1(b,c),H1(b,c)=∑j=0dbjcd−j. |D_N(\sigma)|_{\mathrm{coef}}\leq N^2 H_1(b,c), \qquad H_1(b,c)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b^jc^{d-j}.\tag{3}

This is an upper bound, not an equality or a phase-preserving replacement of djd_j by 11. The weight in (1) depends on both jj and σ\sigma.

Since djd_j has degree dd, there is also a uniform complex-cover bound:

|dj(−2cos⁡(2πδ))|≤N2e2πd|Im⁡δ|. \bigl|d_j(-2\cos(2\pi\delta))\bigr| \leq N^2 e^{2\pi d|\operatorname{Im}\delta|}.\tag{4}

For completeness, if a polynomial pp of degree dd is bounded by MM on [−1,1][-1,1], then w−dp((w+w−1)/2)w^{-d}p((w+w^{-1})/2) is holomorphic outside the unit disk and at infinity, with boundary modulus at most MM. The exterior maximum principle gives |p((w+w−1)/2)|≤M|w|d|p((w+w^{-1})/2)|\leq M|w|^d. Apply this with p(z)=dj(−2z)p(z)=d_j(-2z) and |w|=e2π|Im⁡δ||w|=e^{2\pi|\operatorname{Im}\delta|}. No division by distance to a spectral node occurs.

2. The positive identity-inserted reference at both parities

For the original fixed certified diagram DD, let FNF_N denote its actual positive Fejér reference coupon:

FN={(−1)dVN(−2),N even,VN(−2)−N−2VN′(−2),N odd. F_N= \begin{cases} (-1)^dV_N(-2),&N\text{ even},\\ V_N(-2)-N^{-2}V_N'(-2),&N\text{ odd}. \end{cases}

Fix a nonempty set II of ports carrying the literal identity coupon. Let ZI,NZ_{I,N} be the original shared marked coefficient with H1H_1 at II and HFNH_{F_N} elsewhere. Every original ordinary target is dd; the two original protected targets are zero.

The strict identity-insertion theorem, Section 6, gives at even levels a compact full-flow maximum WI<ΦDW_I<\Phi_D, where ΦD=Vol⁡(YD)/(2π)\Phi_D=\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)/(2\pi), or a zero source when the flow is infeasible. Its proof uses an actual representation of the original drilled group killing an essential deleted auxiliary meridian, together with the canonical general-peripheral volume bound. It does not bound independent pieces with unrelated marks.

The same rate applies at odd levels. This does not differentiate an even identity: the odd regularized coefficient theorem gives the same uniform local full-simplex entropy hh for HFNH_{F_N}, while H1H_1 has exact coefficient 11 on its middle edge and zero elsewhere. Removing the two outer arcs at identity ports leaves the same integral bipartite-flow rounding with literal degree dd. Compact Laplace therefore gives, at both parities,

limN→∞1Nlog⁡ZI,N=WI<ΦD. \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log Z_{I,N}=W_I<\Phi_D.\tag{5}

If the flow is infeasible, every finite coefficient is zero. This includes an identity edge joining the two protected roots: it cannot supply positive degree dd to either zero target. No protected radius is replaced by a positive surrogate.

3. Two strict-gap consequences for the actual spectral sectors

First take one port ii in the pure-middle diagonal sector (1), with arbitrary σi∈[−2,2]\sigma_i\in[-2,2]. Allow every other port to retain its full numerator, provided |σj+2|≤r|\sigma_j+2|\leq r. The original unused-row majorant, Section 3, gives

|HVN(σj)|coef≤MN(r)HFN,MN(r)≤poly⁡(N)eNθ(r),θ(r)=arcosh⁡(1+r/2). |H_{V_N(\sigma_j)}|_{\mathrm{coef}}\leq M_N(r)H_{F_N}, \qquad M_N(r)\leq\operatorname{poly}(N)e^{N\theta(r)}, \qquad\theta(r)=\operatorname{arcosh}(1+r/2).\tag{6}

At odd levels the factor max⁡(1,N2r)\max(1,N^2r) in MNM_N is essential. Bare HVN(−2)H_{V_N(-2)} is not an odd majorant. Taking absolute coefficients before multiplying and extracting the one original marked target proves the finite inequality

|Zdiagi;fullelsewhere(𝝈)|≤N2MN(r)R−1Z{i},N. |Z_{\mathrm{diag}\ i;\,\mathrm{full\ elsewhere}}(\boldsymbol\sigma)| \leq N^2M_N(r)^{R-1}Z_{\{i\},N}.\tag{7}

Choose fixed r>0r>0 so (R−1)θ(r)(R-1)\theta(r) is smaller than half the positive gap ΦD−W{i}\Phi_D-W_{\{i\}}. Equation (5) gives γ>0\gamma>0 and N0N_0 such that, uniformly in this region and at both parities,

|Zdiagi;fullelsewhere(𝝈)|≤eN(ΦD−γ),N≥N0. |Z_{\mathrm{diag}\ i;\,\mathrm{full\ elsewhere}}(\boldsymbol\sigma)| \leq e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma)},\qquad N\geq N_0.\tag{8}

If the identity reference is zero, the sector is identically zero. Constants depend on the original fixed diagram. No independently framed norm or unproved physical smoothing has been used.

Second, if all other ports are restricted to their actual CROSS sectors, those ports may range over the entire real spectral interval. The exact cofactor interpolation theorem, Section 8, gives

|PN,cross(σ)|coef≤Clog⁡2(N+1)PN,cross(−2)≤Clog⁡2(N+1)HFN,σ∈[−2,2]. |P_{N,\mathrm{cross}}(\sigma)|_{\mathrm{coef}} \leq C\log^2(N+1)P_{N,\mathrm{cross}}(-2) \leq C\log^2(N+1)H_{F_N},\qquad \sigma\in[-2,2].\tag{9}

Combining (3) and (9) with the same marked extraction gives (8) uniformly over the full real spectral cube. The proof applies to any fixed nonempty collection II of diagonal ports, using ZI,NZ_{I,N}, with CROSS elsewhere. This is an inequality for every original coefficient; it neither identifies the restricted quantum sector with a smoothed representation nor asserts absence of cancellation.

Both conclusions persist on a sufficiently small fixed complex strip in each globally varying δ\delta coordinate. Equation (4) and the strip version of (9) add at most 2πN∑j|Im⁡δj|2\pi N\sum_j|\operatorname{Im}\delta_j| to the exponent. Shrink the strip so this and the cost in (6) fit within the strict gap in (5). Polynomial factors contribute o(N)o(N).

4. The entire original Fourier period of the diagonal/CROSS sector

Use the global case of Section 3: a fixed nonempty set II is pure diagonal and every other port is CROSS. For a disjoint path partition, remove the odd central zero-step monomial from CROSS and assign it to the diagonal class. This is only a coefficient projection onto positive outer degree, so (9) remains an upper bound. The exact sectors are then disjoint at both parities.

Let ZN(𝝈)Z_N(\boldsymbol\sigma) be this shared sector polynomial. It has degree at most dd in each spectral variable. The preceding proof supplies fixed τ,γ>0\tau,\gamma>0 such that

|ZN(−2cos(2πδ1),…,−2cos(2πδR))|≤eN(ΦD−γ) \left|Z_N\bigl(-2\cos(2\pi\delta_1),\ldots,-2\cos(2\pi\delta_R)\bigr)\right| \leq e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma)}\tag{10}

for all large NN, uniformly when |Im⁡δi|≤τ|\operatorname{Im}\delta_i|\leq\tau and for all real parts. Real periodicity follows from the polynomial cosines. Shrink τ\tau once for Cauchy estimates; it is independent of the crossing powers.

Insert this sector into the exact original Fourier formula, retaining its scalar, charge factors, sine factors and parity. Write

BN(𝜹)=(∏isin(2πδi))ZN(−2cos⁡(2π𝜹))eπiN∑iniδi2,JN,ℓ=∫[−1/2,1/2]RBN(𝜹)e2πiN∑iℓiδid𝜹. \begin{aligned} B_N(\boldsymbol\delta) &=\left(\prod_i\sin(2\pi\delta_i)\right) Z_N(-2\cos(2\pi\boldsymbol\delta)) e^{\pi iN\sum_i n_i\delta_i^2},\\ J_{N,\boldsymbol\ell} &=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^R}B_N(\boldsymbol\delta) e^{2\pi iN\sum_i\ell_i\delta_i}\,d\boldsymbol\delta. \end{aligned}

Every nin_i is a fixed integer, with independent signs allowed. At either endpoint of coordinate ii,

BN|δi=±1/2=0,∂iBN|δi=1/2=∂iBN|δi=−1/2. B_N|_{\delta_i=\pm1/2}=0, \qquad \partial_iB_N|_{\delta_i=1/2} =\partial_iB_N|_{\delta_i=-1/2}.\tag{11}

The sine vanishes, its derivative is −2π-2\pi at both endpoints, the source sees σi=2\sigma_i=2 at both, and the Gaussian is eπiNni/4e^{\pi iNn_i/4} at both. Only the sine-derivative term survives. These identities persist after derivatives in other coordinates. The Fourier endpoint factor is the same (−1)Nℓi(-1)^{N\ell_i} at both ends.

Three integrations by parts therefore give

Ji=(2πiNℓi)−3{[∂i2BNe2πiNℓiδi]−1/21/2−∫−1/21/2∂i3BNe2πiNℓiδidδi}. \begin{aligned} J_i=(2\pi iN\ell_i)^{-3}\bigg\{& \left[\partial_i^2 B_N e^{2\pi iN\ell_i\delta_i}\right]_{-1/2}^{1/2}\\ &-\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\partial_i^3 B_N e^{2\pi iN\ell_i\delta_i}\,d\delta_i\bigg\}. \end{aligned}\tag{12}

The first two endpoint terms vanish by (11); the second-derivative endpoint difference in (12) is retained. Repeat in every coordinate. The boundary operations commute with the other derivatives and preserve their endpoint identities. There are at most 3R3^R terms, each with at most three derivatives per coordinate and evaluations or integrals over intervals of length one.

Cauchy applied to the source alone in (10) bounds all such fixed-order derivatives by CτeN(ΦD−γ)C_\tau e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma)}. On the real cube the Gaussian has modulus one, and its kkth derivative in coordinate ii is bounded by Ck(1+N|ni|)kC_k(1+N|n_i|)^k. Thus

|JN,ℓ|≤CD,τ,R(∏i(1+|ni|)3)eN(ΦD−γ)∏i|ℓi|−3. |J_{N,\boldsymbol\ell}| \leq C_{D,\tau,R}\left(\prod_i(1+|n_i|)^3\right) e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma)}\prod_i|\ell_i|^{-3}.\tag{13}

The derivative cost N3RN^{3R} has canceled the integration-by-parts denominator. No Gaussian growth on the complex strip is used. The entire sector stays on the original whole real cube.

The original charge weights have modulus ∏i|ℓi|\prod_i|\ell_i|; the parity and original framing scalar have modulus one. Since ∑ℓ≠0|ℓ|−2=π2/3\sum_{\ell\ne0}|\ell|^{-2}=\pi^2/3, the complete charge sum is absolutely bounded by

|IN,𝒏;diagI,CROSSelsewhere|≤CD(∏i(1+|ni|)3)eN(ΦD−γ). |I_{N,\mathbf n;\,\mathrm{diag}\ I,\,\mathrm{CROSS\ elsewhere}}| \leq C_D\left(\prod_i(1+|n_i|)^3\right)e^{N(\Phi_D-\gamma)}.\tag{14}

This includes all charges and the actual second-derivative currents. It is not a sharp-cube localization or separately divergent inside/outside charge sum. Multilinearity of the finite source and fixed-level absolute Fourier convergence justify taking this polynomial sector; (13) additionally proves its uniform summability.

There are finitely many diagonal/CROSS sectors for fixed DD, so the same conclusion with a smaller gap holds for their union. For sufficiently long fixed independent signed twists, the proved local unit-charge filling rate exceeds ΦD−γ/2\Phi_D-\gamma/2. Equation (14) is exponentially smaller than that nonzero local contribution. Constants may grow polynomially with the fixed powers; no joint growing-power/level asymptotic is claimed.

The first case of Section 3, where other full ports are controlled only near −2-2, does not automatically have this whole-period conclusion. Equation (14) explicitly uses the global CROSS bound at every other port.

5. Remaining scope and verification

The result is compatible with failure of a global coefficientwise endpoint bound for the full numerator. That failure already occurs at σ=3\sigma=\sqrt3 for a genuinely one-sided path with positive outer exponent, not for (1). Such a remote moving port is not covered by (9) or a small spectral neighborhood in (6). The short-moving extension treats a separate small outer-degree corridor; it does not exhaust macroscopic one-sided paths.

The focused audit and recorded results retain the exact finite source.

The finite audit checks zero-step and divided-Chebyshev formulas, removable node values, parity sectors and marked sector polynomials. Its four nonzero fixtures include whole-interval three-integration-by-parts identities with nonzero second-derivative endpoint currents. These checks supplement the entropy and infinite-charge proofs.
