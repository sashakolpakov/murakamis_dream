The actual Mellin period is transcendental for every nontrivial adequate knot

Research theorem, 27 October 2026. The actual Fourier–Mellin section has a bounded rational source and a specified formal source lattice. For every nontrivial adequate knot that selected section is transcendental over C(Q,X). This follows from the already proved quadratic color-degree span and an elementary valuation bound for algebraic power series, proved below without an Eisenstein theorem.

This is a theorem about the actual pointed period for a wide knot class, including hyperbolic alternating knots. It does not identify its geometric A-component or prove its root-value volume growth.

1. Algebraic power series have linearly bounded coefficient valuations

Let K be a characteristic-zero discretely valued field, with valuation v, valuation ring R and uniformizer pi. Completeness is unnecessary. Let

f(X)=sum_(n>=0)c_n X^n in K[[X]]

be algebraic over K(X). Then there are constants A,B>=0 such that

v(c_n)>=-A-Bn for all nonzero coefficients.  (1.1)

Here and below zero coefficients satisfy the bound by v(0)=infinity.

Proof. Choose a polynomial P(X,Z) in K[X,Z] for which P(X,f)=0 and P_Z(X,f)!=0. Such a choice exists because the minimal polynomial is separable in characteristic zero. Put

m=ord_X P_Z(X,f), choose k>m,
p(X)=sum_(n=0)^(k-1)c_n X^n,
f=p+X^k w.

Since p-f has order at least k>m,

ord_X P_Z(X,p)=m.

Taylor expansion gives a polynomial

F(X,W)=X^(-m-k) P(X,p+X^k W) in K[X,W].    (1.2)

Indeed the linear term is divisible by X^(m+k). The terms of degree j>=2 in W have exponent at least kj, and kj-(m+k)=k(j-1)-m>0. The constant term P(X,p) is divisible by X^(m+k) because substituting w makes the entire Taylor sum zero. At X=0, therefore,

F(0,W)=aW+b, a=[X^m]P_Z(X,p)!=0.

Let w_0=-b/a, which is the constant coefficient of w. Set W=w_0+V and divide by a. The resulting polynomial has the exact form

V+X H(X,V)=0, H in K[X,V].                  (1.3)

This is the useful normalization: all other monomials have positive X order. Write H=sum h_(i,j) X^i V^j. Choose an integer B>=0 with

v(h_(i,j))+B(i+1)>=0 for every nonzero h_(i,j). (1.4)

There are finitely many coefficients, so such a B exists. Under X=pi^B x the selected series z(x)=V(pi^B x) satisfies

z+x H_B(x,z)=0,
H_B(x,z)=pi^B H(pi^B x,z) in R[x,z].        (1.5)

Its constant term is zero. In the coefficient recursion, the coefficient of x^n on the right depends only on earlier coefficients of z because of the outer factor x. Induction shows z belongs to xR[[x]]. Thus, writing V=sum_(r>=1)v_r X^r,

v(v_r)>=-Br.

For n>=k+1 one has c_n=v_(n-k), and hence v(c_n)>=-B(n-k)>=-Bn. The finitely many coefficients through c_k are covered by increasing A. This proves (1.1).

No analytic convergence and no a priori integrality of the original coefficients is used. The truncation removes the possible singular derivative, normalization gives one unit linear coefficient, and a single rescaling of X makes the finite recursion integral.

2. Consequence for rational-function coefficients in Q

Apply the theorem to K=C(Q), first with the valuation at Q=0 and then with the valuation at Q=infinity. For a Laurent polynomial c_n(Q), these valuations are respectively

v_0(c_n)=mindeg_Q c_n,
v_infinity(c_n)=-maxdeg_Q c_n.

Consequently an algebraic f(X) with Laurent-polynomial coefficients must satisfy

mindeg_Q c_n>=-A_0-B_0n,
maxdeg_Q c_n<= A_infinity+B_infinity n,
span_Q c_n=O(n).                            (2.1)

The algebraic relation may have arbitrary complex coefficients; their Q-valuations are zero. Thus this conclusion applies to algebraicity over C(Q,X), not only over the rational number field. It also covers any finite algebraic cover supplying a scalar branch in the selected integer-power series lattice.

3. The actual adequate color coefficients violate this bound

The actual fixed-color functions C_n(Q) are Laurent polynomials, and the adequacy proof establishes their extreme degrees directly from adequate states, cabling and threading. For an adequate diagram with c>0 crossings and state-circle counts v_A,v_B it gives, in the q parameter,

span_q C_n=c(n-1)^2+(v_A+v_B-2)(n-1).

The actual Q-polarization has Q=q^2 and C_n even in q, so

span_Q C_n=(1/2)c(n-1)^2
                +(1/2)(v_A+v_B-2)(n-1).    (3.1)

In particular it has a strictly positive quadratic coefficient. For a reduced connected alternating diagram this simplifies to span_Q C_n=c n(n-1)/2. The zero-framing monomial does not change the span.

Choose the fixed n_0 of the actual Fourier–Mellin construction and retain its exact selected section

Z(Q,X)=sum_(n>=n_0) C_n(Q) X^n.              (3.2)

If Z were algebraic over C(Q,X), equation (2.1) would bound its coefficient spans linearly. This contradicts (3.1) for every nontrivial adequate knot. We have therefore proved

Z(Q,X) is transcendental over C(Q,X).       (3.3)

Subtracting or adding any fixed initial-color polynomial preserves algebraicity. Thus the conclusion holds for every fixed starting color, including the start-one generating period.

4. The precise source/lattice conclusion

The actual bounded-source identity is

P^M Z=B,
Z in X^(n_0) C(Q)[[X]],                     (4.1)

where B is the fixed nonzero Laurent source from finitely many small colors. The source-lattice condition makes this solution unique, by the leading-coefficient argument of the Fourier–Mellin construction. Hence no scalar section on a finite algebraic cover, satisfying this actual source and this formal lattice, can replace Z. The selected whole period is necessarily transcendental.

There is a useful operator consequence. Every nonzero actual Q-operator of a nontrivial adequate knot has positive generic Y-exponent span. Otherwise all its Y exponents would be a single m, and its Fourier image would have the form A(Q,X)S^m with A!=0. Equation (4.1) would make S^m Z=B/A rational, and hence Z rational, contradicting (3.3). Thus the actual Mellin source object has positive difference order for this whole knot class; no artificial factor is needed to create that order. This is not a claim about the operator’s specialized symbol at Q=1.

This does not contradict algebraicity of the underlying equations, gluing place, comparison torsor or connection. Their marked periods can be transcendental. Nor does it contradict rationality of the root fibers: the original cyclotomic residue construction gives a rational function at each Q_N whose degree grows with N.

The assertion is not generic affine nonsplitting without the lattice. A rational or algebraic particular solution outside X^(n_0)[[X]] could coexist with the selected transcendental solution, differing by a homogeneous solution. We have not excluded that possibility. The theorem excludes a pointed algebraic splitting compatible with the actual source lattice and coefficient cut.

Finally, quadratic degree span gives no lower bound for a specified root value. Cancellation can still occur at Q_N. The exact identity <K>_N=[X^N]Z(Q_N,X), or its infinity-residue version, remains the original volume target. No exponential regulator estimate is inferred from transcendence alone.
