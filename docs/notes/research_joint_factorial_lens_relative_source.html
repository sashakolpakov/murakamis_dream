Joint factorial lenses for the original finite source

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

The restored Newton network has an exact joint integral in its wire indices and angular variables. This note places that integral on lower angular lenses in a common holomorphic factorial domain. It also gives an explicit relative primitive for the difference between the factorial and finite-band polynomial representatives. Thus compact joint deformations with the specified coordinate faces fixed preserve the full original source. No edge kernel is replaced by a single asymptotic branch.

The result does not establish the geometric coefficient or an exponential bound on the remainder of such a joint contour.

1. Original source and the common domain

Fix N≥2N\ge2, D=N−1D=N-1, η=(−1)D\eta=(-1)^D, and PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,ϕN(f)=ηN2[zD]rem⁡PNf.(1) P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\qquad \phi_N(f)=\eta N^2[z^D]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}f . \qquad\text{(1)} Use the original Newton polynomials eje_j, physical crossing RR, and kernels KT(z,w)=∑j=0Dej(z)ej(w),KI(z,w)=∑j=0Dej(z)eD−j(w).(2) K_T(z,w)=\sum_{j=0}^D e_j(z)e_j(w),\qquad K_I(z,w)=\sum_{j=0}^D e_j(z)e_{D-j}(w). \qquad\text{(2)} Take any finite network compiled from these operations as in the pinned source compiler. Let VV be its traced vertices and EE its paired wire indices. Loops and repeated edges are permitted. Every wire index occurs in precisely two halfwire factors. Each halfwire uses either xex_e or D/N−xeD/N-x_e, according to its original direct or reversed index. Free spectral variables are ordinary polynomial variables and are not reduced.

At a traced vertex vv, allow any fixed signed power RnvR^{n_v} and the canonical full-Hermite coupon 𝒞N,χv\mathcal C_{N,\chi_v} of the pinned lens theorem. Here χv\chi_v may be complex; its value at z=−2cos⁡tz=-2\cos t is represented by cos⁡(N(1−χv)t)\cos(N(1-\chi_v)t), with its prescribed first normals. The original source is recovered when every χv=1\chi_v=1. Any additional specified polynomial spectators are retained.

Write BN(x,t)=NGN(D/2−Nx+Nt/(2π))GN(D/2−Nx−Nt/(2π))GN(Nx)2,B̃N(x,z)=∑j=0Dsin⁡(πN(x−j/N))Nsin⁡(π(x−j/N))ej(z).(3) \begin{split} B_N(x,t)&=\frac{N}{ G_N(D/2-Nx+Nt/(2\pi))G_N(D/2-Nx-Nt/(2\pi))G_N(Nx)^2},\\ \widetilde B_N(x,z)&=\sum_{j=0}^D \frac{\sin(\pi N(x-j/N))}{N\sin(\pi(x-j/N))}\,e_j(z). \end{split} \qquad\text{(3)} The removable values in the second line are used. Its frequencies in xx are −D/2,−D/2+1,…,D/2-D/2,-D/2+1,\ldots,D/2. The full Hermite class of BN(x,t)B_N(x,t), with z=−2cos⁡tz=-2\cos t, equals that of B̃N(x,z)\widetilde B_N(x,z). At x=j/Nx=j/N, both equal ej(z)e_j(z) as an ordinary polynomial identity.

Put aN=π−π2N,IN=[−1/(2N),1−1/(2N)].(4) a_N=\pi-\frac{\pi}{2N},\qquad I_N=[-1/(2N),\,1-1/(2N)] . \qquad\text{(4)} Use ℜxe∈IN\Re x_e\in I_N and |ℜtv|≤aN|\Re t_v|\le a_N. The compiler’s direct divisor check applies simultaneously to both halfwire orientations: the two shifted factorial arguments in (3) have real parts in [−N+1/4,N−1/4],ℜ(Nxe)∈[−1/2,N−1/2].(5) [-N+1/4,\,N-1/4],\qquad \Re(Nx_e)\in[-1/2,N-1/2]. \qquad\text{(5)} Consequently all factorial halfwires are holomorphic on a neighborhood of any compact subset of this product strip, at arbitrary fixed finite imaginary heights. The reflection x↦D/N−xx\mapsto D/N-x preserves INI_N.

At a free spectral endpoint use B̃N(x,z)\widetilde B_N(x,z), not BN(x,t)B_N(x,t). This distinction preserves the entire boundary polynomial. At traced endpoints either representative is allowed.

2. A full-grid lens inside the factorial domain

Set WN(t)=e−iNt(1−ηe−iNt)2,CN,n=qnD2/4,q=eπi/N.(6) W_N(t)=\frac{e^{-iNt}}{(1-\eta e^{-iNt})^2},\qquad C_{N,n}=q^{nD^2/4},\qquad q=e^{\pi i/N}. \qquad\text{(6)} The physical grid is τj=π−(2j+1)π/N\tau_j=\pi-(2j+1)\pi/N, 0≤j≤D0\le j\le D. All these points lie strictly between −aN-a_N and aNa_N; neither endpoint is a pole of WNW_N.

Let A(t)A(t) be even and holomorphic on a vertical strip containing [−aN,aN][-a_N,a_N], and let [A][A] denote its full spectral Hermite class. This class exists because an even function near t=0t=0 is holomorphic in z=−2cos⁡tz=-2\cos t there; elsewhere the physical angular-to-spectral map is unramified. If Γ\Gamma runs from −aN-a_N to aNa_N, with interior in the lower half-plane and inside that strip, then ϕN(Rn𝒞N,χ[A])=−N2CN,nπi∫Γsin⁡teinNt2/(4π)cos⁡(N(1−χ)t)A(t)WN(t)dt.(7) \boxed{\quad \phi_N(R^n\mathcal C_{N,\chi}[A]) =-\frac{N^2C_{N,n}}{\pi i} \int_\Gamma \sin t\,e^{inNt^2/(4\pi)} \cos(N(1-\chi)t)A(t)W_N(t)\,dt . \quad} \qquad\text{(7)}

Indeed the numerator other than WNW_N is odd, and WNW_N is even. Adjoin the reflected upper arc −Γ-\Gamma to form the positively oriented lens around the entire physical grid. The exact derivative-sampling residue calculation of the lens theorem gives (7), with the two reflected integrals equal. Only evenness and holomorphy of AA, and its physical values and first normals, are used. It need not be a polynomial. The odd-level simple spectral node at t=0t=0 has precisely its original contribution; no extra normal is imposed.

Unlike the endpoints ±π\pm\pi, the present endpoints need not annihilate the sine factor. Short endpoint connectors are therefore generally ON(ε)O_N(\varepsilon), which still tends to zero at fixed NN. There is no endpoint residue or half-weight. No uniform large-NN connector estimate is asserted.

3. The exact joint integral

Fix horizontal wire intervals IN+iheI_N+i h_e and lower angular arcs Γv:−aN→aN\Gamma_v:-a_N\to a_N as above. Let d=|E|+|V|d=|E|+|V|, and order the complex coordinates once and for all.

Let FcarF_{\mathrm{ car}} be the product of all halfwire factors, using B̃N(x,−2cos⁡t)\widetilde B_N(x,-2\cos t) at traced endpoints and the same polynomial representative at every free endpoint. Retain the specified polynomial spectators in this product. Let FfacF_{\mathrm{ fac}} replace the traced halfwires by (3), with their actual direct or reversed arguments; the free endpoints remain polynomial. Both functions are even in every angular coordinate.

Define the two top forms, with the same chosen coordinate order, by Ω•=F•(𝒙,𝒕;𝒖)∏v∈V{sintvWN(tv)einvNtv2/(4π)cos(N(1−χv)tv)}d𝒙d𝒕,•∈{car,fac}.(8) \Omega_\bullet= F_\bullet(\mathbf x,\mathbf t;\mathbf u) \prod_{v\in V} \left\{\sin t_v\,W_N(t_v) e^{in_vNt_v^2/(4\pi)} \cos(N(1-\chi_v)t_v)\right\} \,d\mathbf x\,d\mathbf t , \qquad \bullet\in\{{\mathrm{ car}},{\mathrm{ fac}}\}. \qquad\text{(8)} Here 𝒖\mathbf u denotes all unreduced free spectral variables. For the product chain ℒ=∏e(IN+ihe)×∏vΓv\mathcal L=\prod_e(I_N+i h_e)\times\prod_v\Gamma_v, the exact compiled source is ZN(𝒖)=N|E|∏v∈V(−N2CN,nvπi)∫ℒΩfac=N|E|∏v∈V(−N2CN,nvπi)∫ℒΩcar.(9) \boxed{\quad Z_N(\mathbf u)= N^{|E|}\prod_{v\in V} \left(-\frac{N^2C_{N,n_v}}{\pi i}\right) \int_{\mathcal L}\Omega_{\mathrm{ fac}} = N^{|E|}\prod_{v\in V} \left(-\frac{N^2C_{N,n_v}}{\pi i}\right) \int_{\mathcal L}\Omega_{\mathrm{ car}}. \quad} \qquad\text{(9)}

For the cardinal representative, each paired index has integer frequencies in [−D,D][-D,D], so its original NN-point sum is exactly NN times the period integral. Apply (7) at every traced vertex. This proves the second expression in (9). The factorial expression follows either by equality of every full Hermite trace, or by the explicit relative identity below. All integrations are over compact chains, and all free-variable coefficients remain unchanged. Thus (9) also holds after any finite number of free-boundary derivatives.

4. The relative primitive of the representative correction

The exact identity PN(−2cos⁡t)WN(t)=(−1)N(10) \boxed{\quad P_N(-2\cos t)\,W_N(t)=(-1)^N \quad} \qquad\text{(10)} follows by expanding PN(−2cos⁡t)=(−1)NeiNt(1−ηe−iNt)2.(11) P_N(-2\cos t) =(-1)^N e^{iNt}(1-\eta e^{-iNt})^2 . \qquad\text{(11)} It retains both parities and every repeated root.

Telescope the replacement Fcar→FfacF_{\mathrm{ car}}\to F_{\mathrm{ fac}} one traced vertex at a time. The difference at vertex vv is divisible by PN(−2cos⁡tv)P_N(-2\cos t_v), so Ffac−Fcar=∑v∈VPN(−2cos⁡tv)Av(𝒙,𝒕;𝒖),(12) F_{\mathrm{ fac}}-F_{\mathrm{ car}} =\sum_{v\in V}P_N(-2\cos t_v)A_v(\mathbf x,\mathbf t;\mathbf u), \qquad\text{(12)} where each AvA_v is jointly holomorphic in the common product strip and even in every angular variable.

To verify holomorphic division, at a paired angular root the full-Hermite identity gives value and first angular derivative zero, matching the double zero of PN(−2cos⁡t)P_N(-2\cos t). When NN is odd, its simple spectral root −2-2 pulls back to a double angular zero at t=0t=0; evenness and value equality give that same order. There are no further zeros of PNP_N inside the angular strip. The quotient consequently has no pole there, including at N=2N=2.

After multiplying the vv-th summand of (12) by the factors in (8), use (10). The resulting top-form coefficient is holomorphic in tvt_v, odd in tvt_v, and may retain only the original WN(tw)W_N(t_w) poles in the other variables. Write that coefficient as HvH_v. Define its primitive by Qv(𝒙,𝒕;𝒖)=∫−aNtvHv(𝒙,t1,…,τ,…;𝒖)dτ.(13) Q_v(\mathbf x,\mathbf t;\mathbf u) =\int_{-a_N}^{t_v} H_v(\mathbf x,t_1,\ldots,\tau,\ldots;\mathbf u)\,d\tau . \qquad\text{(13)} The integration in the vv-th strip is path independent: its coefficient is holomorphic there, including at the physical nodes. It is holomorphic in all remaining variables off their original angular poles. Oddness gives Qv|tv=−aN=Qv|tv=aN=0.(14) Q_v|_{t_v=-a_N}=Q_v|_{t_v=a_N}=0 . \qquad\text{(14)}

If tvt_v is coordinate number j(v)j(v), let Θv=(−1)j(v)−1Qv\Theta_v=(-1)^{j(v)-1}Q_v times the ordered wedge of all coordinate differentials except dtvdt_v. Then Ωfac−Ωcar=dΘ,Θ=∑vΘv.(15) \boxed{\qquad \Omega_{\mathrm{ fac}}-\Omega_{\mathrm{ car}}=d\Theta,\qquad \Theta=\sum_v\Theta_v . \qquad} \qquad\text{(15)} Every derivative of QvQ_v in another coordinate wedges with the already present differential and vanishes. This proves (15) directly, without an asymptotic argument.

The pullback of Θ\Theta vanishes on every prescribed coordinate face. On tv=±aNt_v=\pm a_N, its own summand vanishes by (14); every other summand contains dtvdt_v. On a wire face xe=−1/(2N)+ihex_e=-1/(2N)+ih_e or 1−1/(2N)+ihe1-1/(2N)+ih_e, every summand contains dxedx_e. This is the precise relative boundary condition used here.

5. Joint deformation and its limits

Theorem. Let ℒ′\mathcal L' be obtained from ℒ\mathcal L by a compact piecewise smooth joint homotopy in the common product domain, avoiding the physical angular poles. Require throughout the homotopy that each angular endpoint face keeps its coordinate tv=±aNt_v=\pm a_N, and each wire seam face keeps its original coordinate xe=−1/(2N)+ihex_e=-1/(2N)+ih_e or 1−1/(2N)+ihe1-1/(2N)+ih_e. Other coordinates may vary on these faces. Then (9) holds with ℒ′\mathcal L' in place of ℒ\mathcal L, for either representative, and the two representatives give the same integral on ℒ′\mathcal L'.

Proof. The forms (8) are closed holomorphic top forms away from their angular poles. On every side face of the homotopy the fixed coordinate has zero differential, so the corresponding top-form integral is zero. Stokes’ theorem proves invariance. Equation (15) and the relative boundary condition prove equality of the two representatives on the deformed chain. All statements are coefficientwise in the free polynomial variables. ▫\square

This is a joint statement: angular and wire directions may move together in the interior. It does not assert that the factorial integrand is pointwise periodic. In fact its exact period defect is BN(x+1,t)−ηBN(x,t)=(−1)NPN(−2cos⁡t)4sin⁡2(πNx)BN(x,t).(16) B_N(x+1,t)-\eta B_N(x,t) =\frac{(-1)^N P_N(-2\cos t)} {4\sin^2(\pi Nx)}B_N(x,t). \qquad\text{(16)} The equality is meromorphic, with removable values interpreted. In particular, a deformation of the wire seams depending on angular coordinates is not covered by the coordinate-fixed-face theorem. Its transgression must be retained or separately proved to vanish. The cardinal representative is pointwise periodic after paired halfwires are multiplied, but its geometric asymptotics cannot be borrowed from the factorial representative merely by discarding (15).

For a supplied compact family of positive angular points, |ℜtv|<π|\Re t_v|<\pi leaves a uniform margin, so these angular points lie within (4) for all sufficiently large NN. This statement supplies room for a deformation; it does not prove positivity along a charge path or a global steepest-descent cycle. The exact factors WNW_N, both coupon exponentials, all first normals and the canonical signed crossing factors remain in (8). No Fourier alias is discarded.

The remaining source problem is to choose and estimate a whole joint representative of this fixed relative class: one must prove that its geometric local piece has a nonzero net coefficient and that its remaining pieces have smaller rate. The present exact primitive does not provide that estimate. In particular, an arbitrary connecting chain in (9) has not been identified with a scalar Ward deletion, so a classical action gap for those specific deletions does not by itself bound this chain.
