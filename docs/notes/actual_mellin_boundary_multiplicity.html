The boundary term and repeated monodromy sector of the Mellin equation

31 October 2026.

The Fourier–Mellin construction packages the ordinary color invariants of a knot into a generating function. Its equation has a nonzero inhomogeneous term, determined by finitely many starting colors. That term is essential: after converting the equation to a homogeneous companion system, it provides a linear functional that detects the selected solution.

For a knot with positive exponential growth of its Kashaev invariant, this homogeneous companion necessarily has at least two independent rational directions fixed by root monodromy. Thus a simple monodromy eigenvalue is not the reason its actual solution is selected. The selection also uses the prescribed source and the allowed expansion at the origin. The proof below keeps those data and explains why the Mellin and main-meridian realizations cannot be identified by merely renaming their variables.

1. The meridian equation and its ordinary colors

Work over ℂ(q)\mathbb C(q) and set Q=q2Q=q^2. The meridian multiplication operator YY and shift TT satisfy

TY=QYT. TY=QYT. \tag{1}

At level N≥2N\ge2, put qN=eπi/Nq_N=e^{\pi i/N} and QN=qN2Q_N=q_N^2. The marked meridian eigenvalue is M=−qNxM=-q_Nx, where xx is the exponential unrolled color parameter, and Y=M2Y=M^2. Write fN(Y)f_N(Y) for the actual reduced, zero-framed ADO state. Its fixed normalization is

fN(1)=KN,fN(QNr)=Cr(qN)(1≤r≤N),C1(q)=1. f_N(1)=K_N,\qquad f_N(Q_N^r)=C_r(q_N)\quad(1\le r\le N), \qquad C_1(q)=1. \tag{2}

Here KNK_N is the reduced Kashaev invariant, and Cr(q)C_r(q) is the reduced ordinary invariant in dimension rr. The equality at r=Nr=N means CN(qN)=KNC_N(q_N)=K_N. These are the actual color specializations, with the squared-meridian convention retained.

Choose a specified all-level annihilator, whose existence follows from the finite cut-state construction. After clearing its coefficient denominators and shifting its order once, write it as

P=∑j=0daj(q,Y)Tj,aj∈ℂ[q±1,Y±1],d≥1,a0ad≠0. P=\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_j(q,Y)T^j,\qquad a_j\in\mathbb C[q^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}],\qquad d\ge1,\quad a_0a_d\ne0. \tag{3}

Its root equation holds at all sufficiently large levels. Keeping ℂ(q)\mathbb C(q) as the coefficient field avoids assuming that a chosen operator descends in its coefficients to ℂ(Q)\mathbb C(Q).

For every fixed positive integer nn, apply its root equation at Y=QNnY=Q_N^n, taking N>n+dN>n+d. Equation (2) gives a relation among fixed ordinary colors at infinitely many distinct qNq_N. Since those colors are Laurent polynomials, rational-function rigidity proves

∑j=0daj(q,Qn)Cn+j(q)=0(n≥1). \sum_{j=0}^{d}a_j(q,Q^n)C_{n+j}(q)=0 \qquad(n\ge1). \tag{4}

This is the color recurrence used below. Its coefficients, ordering, and initial colors are those of the specified knot equation.

2. Starting the generating series creates a nonzero source

Let AqA_q be the difference between the largest and smallest qq exponents in the collected Laurent monomials of ad(q,Y)a_d(q,Y). Choose a fixed starting color

n0≥d+Aq+1,Z(q,X)=∑n≥n0Cn(q)Xn. n_0\ge d+A_q+1, \qquad Z(q,X)=\sum_{n\ge n_0}C_n(q)X^n. \tag{5}

The series is initially formal at X=0X=0. No convergence for generic qq is assumed. The choice of n0n_0 makes every substitution ad(q,Qr)a_d(q,Q^r) with r≥n0−dr\ge n_0-d nonzero. Indeed, distinct monomials quYvq^uY^v become qu+2rvq^{u+2rv}; a collision with different vv would require 2r≤Aq2r\le A_q. Monomials with the same vv were already collected.

Let SXg(X)=g(QX)S_Xg(X)=g(QX). The exact quantum-torus Fourier map is

Φ(Y)=SX,Φ(T)=X−1,X−1SX=QSXX−1. \Phi(Y)=S_X,\qquad \Phi(T)=X^{-1},\qquad X^{-1}S_X=QS_XX^{-1}. \tag{6}

Write aj(q,Y)=∑majm(q)Yma_j(q,Y)=\sum_m a_{jm}(q)Y^m. Its ordered action on (5) is

A:=Φ(P)=∑j,majm(q)Q−mjX−jSXm,AZ=ℬ, A:=\Phi(P)=\sum_{j,m}a_{jm}(q)Q^{-mj}X^{-j}S_X^m, \qquad AZ=\mathcal B, \tag{7}

where

ℬ(q,X)=∑j,majm(q)Q−mjX−j∑r=n0n0+j−1Cr(q)QmrXr. \mathcal B(q,X)= \sum_{j,m}a_{jm}(q)Q^{-mj}X^{-j} \sum_{r=n_0}^{n_0+j-1}C_r(q)Q^{mr}X^r. \tag{8}

To obtain (8), multiply (4) by XnX^n and sum over n≥n0n\ge n_0. For a term with shift jj, the substitution r=n+jr=n+j leaves out exactly the colors n0,…,n0+j−1n_0,\ldots,n_0+j-1. Those omitted terms are the inner sum in (8); it is empty for j=0j=0. The factor Q−mjQ^{-mj} comes from the operator ordering in (6).

The source is a Laurent polynomial in XX, supported between n0−dn_0-d and n0−1n_0-1. Its least coefficient is

[Xn0−d]ℬ=Cn0(q)ad(q,Qn0−d)≠0. [X^{n_0-d}]\mathcal B =C_{n_0}(q)a_d(q,Q^{n_0-d})\ne0. \tag{9}

Only j=dj=d contributes to this coefficient. The second factor is nonzero by the monomial argument above, and Cn0(1)=1C_{n_0}(1)=1 makes the first nonzero as well. Thus this finite-start construction has a nonzero source. Its least coefficient is a fixed nonzero rational function of qq, so its specialization is nonzero at all but finitely many qNq_N.

The source equation also determines the actual series uniquely in the prescribed space Xn0ℂ(q)[[X]]X^{n_0}\mathbb C(q)[[X]]. If a nonzero homogeneous difference had least exponent r≥n0r\ge n_0, its image under AA would have a nonzero coefficient at Xr−dX^{r-d}, namely ad(q,Qr−d)a_d(q,Q^{r-d}) times that least coefficient. This is impossible. This is the finite-start source construction; it is stronger than choosing any rational solution of its root specialization.

3. Root rationality and the finite Fourier comparison

The actual root color-periodicity theorem gives Cn+N(qN)=Cn(qN)C_{n+N}(q_N)=C_n(q_N) for all n≥1n\ge1, including the multiples of NN. Consequently, for N≥n0N\ge n_0,

ZN(X):=Z(qN,X)=∑r=1N−1Cr(qN)Xr+KNXN1−XN−pNpre(X),pNpre(X)=∑r=1n0−1Cr(qN)Xr. Z_N(X):=Z(q_N,X) =\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}C_r(q_N)X^r+K_NX^N} {1-X^N} -p_N^{\mathrm{pre}}(X),\qquad p_N^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)=\sum_{r=1}^{n_0-1}C_r(q_N)X^r. \tag{10}

This is the rational function with the same formal expansion at zero. Where the fixed operator specializes regularly, it satisfies ANZN=ℬNA_NZ_N=\mathcal B_N as a rational identity. It has at most simple poles at the NNth roots of unity, and its residue retains the original scalar:

Res⁡X=∞ZN(X)dXX=KN. \operatorname{Res}_{X=\infty}Z_N(X)\frac{dX}{X}=K_N. \tag{11}

Indeed the fraction in (10) tends to −KN-K_N at infinity, while pNprep_N^{\mathrm{pre}} has no constant term. The full modified ADO value remains iN+3KN/Ni^{N+3}K_N/N at completeness; (11) does not replace its whole meridian normalization by a different one.

The central coordinates explain the limit of this Fourier comparison. At a primitive level-NN root, write

U=YN,V=TN,UX=XN,VX=SXN. U=Y^N,\quad V=T^N,\qquad U_X=X^N,\quad V_X=S_X^N. \tag{12}

The algebra map (6) sends them to

Φ(U)=VX,Φ(V)=UX−1. \Phi(U)=V_X,\qquad \Phi(V)=U_X^{-1}. \tag{13}

In the rational main-meridian realization, TT acts by the root shift of YY, so V=1V=1 and UU varies. Its Fourier image is therefore supported on UX=1U_X=1, with VXV_X variable. In the rational Mellin realization, SXS_X acts by the root shift of XX, so instead VX=1V_X=1 and UXU_X varies. In the original coordinates this is U=1U=1, with V=X−NV=X^{-N} variable. These are two different central divisors, meeting at (1,1)(1,1). An isomorphism over a fixed center cannot equate their distinct annihilator ideals.

At their intersection there is a genuine finite Fourier map. Its domain is the orbit ring ℂ[Y±1]/(YN−1)\mathbb C[Y^{\pm1}]/(Y^N-1), equivalently the functions on the NN meridian root points. A rational function may be used only when it is regular at those points; the actual fNf_N is Laurent polynomial and meets this condition. Put

ℱN(h)=∑r=0N−1h(QNr)Xrin ℂ[X]/(XN−1). \mathscr F_N(h)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}h(Q_N^r)X^r \quad\text{in }\mathbb C[X]/(X^N-1). \tag{14}

Termwise multiplication and cyclic reindexing prove ℱN(Yh)=SXℱN(h)\mathscr F_N(Yh)=S_X\mathscr F_N(h) and ℱN(Th)=X−1ℱN(h)\mathscr F_N(Th)=X^{-1}\mathscr F_N(h). For h=fNh=f_N, this produces KN+∑r=1N−1Cr(qN)XrK_N+\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}C_r(q_N)X^r. It is precisely the class of the numerator

(1−XN)(ZN+pNpre)=∑r=1N−1Cr(qN)Xr+KNXN. (1-X^N)(Z_N+p_N^{\mathrm{pre}}) =\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}C_r(q_N)X^r+K_NX^N. \tag{15}

modulo XN−1X^N-1. Thus the finite map compares the meridian orbit with the Mellin pole numerator. It does not evaluate ZNZ_N at a pole or identify their meridian neighborhoods. In particular it does not replace the retained normal coordinate of the ramified Weyl fibre by an ordinary orbit value.

4. Why the root clock cannot be lifted as a finite matrix gauge

The distinction persists in the first quantum-normal direction. Vary the parameter through dual numbers by Q=QN(1+ϵ)Q=Q_N(1+\epsilon), ϵ2=0\epsilon^2=0; the compatible half-root is q=qN(1+ϵ/2)q=q_N(1+\epsilon/2). In the whole quantum torus, (1) implies

VU=QN2UV=(1+N2ϵ)UV,[V,U]=N2ϵUV. VU=Q^{N^2}UV=(1+N^2\epsilon)UV, \qquad [V,U]=N^2\epsilon UV. \tag{16}

Suppose a nonzero finite free matrix realization of the intersection fibre lifted through this parameter change, with both central actions reducing to identity. It would have U=I+ϵEU=I+\epsilon E and V=I+ϵFV=I+\epsilon F, whose commutator is zero modulo ϵ2\epsilon^2. Equation (16) instead gives N2ϵIN^2\epsilon I, a contradiction in characteristic zero. For the usual NN-dimensional clock matrices the same obstruction follows by taking determinants in TY=QYTTY=QYT, which would require QN=1Q^N=1 rather than 1+Nϵ1+N\epsilon.

This rules out lifting (14) as an ordinary finite matrix gauge through the quantum-normal direction. It does not rule out a comparison with differential normal operators or retained relative sources. The first quantum-normal construction includes a derivation because an off-root shift no longer has finite order. That derivation and its source cannot be inferred from the root Fourier matrix alone.

5. Positive growth forces two rational companion directions

Assume now that the knot has positive exponential complete growth:

liminfN→∞1Nlog⁡|KN|>0. \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|K_N|>0. \tag{17}

This is supplied, for example, by the established strict-tree and net-twist volume theorems for their stated knot classes. The next argument only uses (17).

For every coefficient in (3), form the fixed Laurent polynomial ri(q)=ai(q,q−2i)r_i(q)=a_i(q,q^{-2i}). If it were nonzero, it would have a finite vanishing order νi\nu_i at q=1q=1, and hence |ri(qN)|≥cN−νi|r_i(q_N)|\ge cN^{-\nu_i} for all large NN. Evaluate the actual root equation at Y=QN−iY=Q_N^{-i}. When N>dN>d, its iith term is ri(qN)KNr_i(q_N)K_N and all other terms are ordinary-color values. Those values are polynomially bounded in NN by the positive ordinary-color trace. The fixed Laurent coefficients are bounded on the root torus, so this equation would bound KNK_N polynomially as well, contradicting (17). Therefore every rir_i is identically zero.

Laurent-polynomial division now gives the exact right factor

P=B(Y−1),A=Φ(B)(SX−1). P=B(Y-1),\qquad A=\Phi(B)(S_X-1). \tag{18}

Indeed the iith coefficient of B(Y−1)B(Y-1) is bi(q,Y)(QiY−1)b_i(q,Y)(Q^iY-1). This is the boundary-column argument, applied here to the positive growth already known. In particular A(1)=0A(1)=0. Any fixed left shift used to give AA nonnegative SXS_X order is applied to its source as well and preserves this identity. In that normalization AA has positive SXS_X order. Otherwise it would be multiplication by a nonzero rational function, which cannot annihilate 11.

Retain the canonical homogeneous equation of the sourced Mellin system:

Â=(SX−σ(ℬ)/ℬ)A,σg(X)=g(QX). \widehat A=(S_X-\sigma(\mathcal B)/\mathcal B)A, \qquad \sigma g(X)=g(QX). \tag{19}

For every sufficiently large NN, both 11 and ZNZ_N are rational solutions of (19). They are independent over ℂ(XN)\mathbb C(X^N): if ZNZ_N were central, then ANZN=ZNAN(1)=0A_NZ_N=Z_NA_N(1)=0, contradicting ℬN≠0\mathcal B_N\ne0.

This gives two independent vectors in the monodromy eigenspace, not merely two scalar formulas. Let 𝖢N\mathsf C_N be the forward solution companion of (19), let Jf=(f,SXf,…)tJf=(f,S_Xf,\ldots)^t be its finite jet vector, and set 𝖳0=𝖢N−1SX\mathsf T_0=\mathsf C_N^{-1}S_X, H0=𝖳0NH_0=\mathsf T_0^N. The positive order of AA makes (19) have at least two companion coordinates. Both J1J1 and JZNJZ_N are fixed by 𝖳0\mathsf T_0. If these vectors were dependent over ℂ(X)\mathbb C(X), their first coordinates would force JZN=ZNJ1JZ_N=Z_NJ1. Their second coordinates would then give SXZN=ZNS_XZ_N=Z_N, again making ZNZ_N central. Thus

dim⁡ℂ(X)ker⁡(H0−I)≥2,ddtdet(tI−H0)|t=1=0. \dim_{\mathbb C(X)}\ker(H_0-I)\ge2, \qquad \left.\frac{d}{dt}\det(tI-H_0)\right|_{t=1}=0. \tag{20}

The source and the companion endpoints exclude only finitely many specialization levels. Equation (20) concerns this canonical homogenization of a denominator-cleared reduced-state operator. It does not assert a Jordan block, specify the full dimension of the eigenspace, or apply to every alternative minimal operator.

Nor does it contradict selection of the actual series. The extra constant solution is outside Xn0ℂ[[X]]X^{n_0}\mathbb C[[X]]. The source equation and that starting condition still distinguish ZZ from the larger rational solution space. At roots, the cyclotomic quantum-jet theorem retains further source and pole-lattice data when selecting the entire rational function.

6. The source row still detects the selected solution

Write the normalized Mellin operator as A=∑j=0rcj(q,X)SXjA=\sum_{j=0}^{r}c_j(q,X)S_X^j. Its canonical homogenization (19) has order r+1r+1. On its companion vectors there is the explicit row

ℓ=1ℬ(c0,…,cr),ℓJf=Af/ℬ. \ell=\frac1{\mathcal B}(c_0,\ldots,c_r),\qquad \ell Jf=Af/\mathcal B. \tag{21}

Equation (19) says that this quotient is shift-invariant. In companion form, σ(ℓ)𝖢=ℓ\sigma(\ell)\mathsf C=\ell, so at a root

ℓH0=ℓ,ℓJ1=0,ℓJZN=1. \ell H_0=\ell,\qquad \ell J1=0,\qquad \ell JZ_N=1. \tag{22}

The row kills the image of H0−IH_0-I but does not kill the selected vector. Therefore the natural map from the kernel to the cokernel of H0−IH_0-I is nonzero on JZNJZ_N. This is the observable-source criterion, which remains valid even though the simple-root test in (20) fails. It detects a specified direction rather than asserting semisimplicity of the entire monodromy.

The algebraic boundary module explains the distinction. Before localization it is ΔY=ℛQ/ℛQ(Y−1)\Delta_Y=\mathcal R_Q/\mathcal R_Q(Y-1), where ℛQ\mathcal R_Q is the quantum torus (1). Passing to rational meridian coefficients kills it because Y−1Y-1 becomes invertible. Its Fourier image is ℛX/ℛX(SX−1)\mathcal R_X/\mathcal R_X(S_X-1), which survives over ℂ(q,X)\mathbb C(q,X) as a rank-one trivial difference module. The two rational localizations thus retain different boundary data. The row (21) is an observable of the sourced Mellin presentation; it is not automatically a row for a localized main-meridian companion. The source-symbol comparison also gives explicit examples where an extra homogeneous factor changes that test.

What remains is a source-and-lattice comparison, not a choice of a preferred eigenvalue by its size or multiplicity. A proposed geometric realization must retain the finite-start source, the cyclotomic pole numerator, the normal derivatives, and the marked period functional through the change of polarization. The root Fourier identity proves the finite numerator comparison, while (16) explains why it does not supply the quantum-normal lift. Neither (20) nor (22) identifies a geometric factor of the classical main-meridian operator.
