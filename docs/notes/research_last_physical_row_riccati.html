Riccati transport through the last physical row

Research proof, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The absolute normalization of the physical Newton rows extends to their last row. At odd levels this last row is the complete color −2-2. Consequently the complete-end kernel itself has the geometric exponential rate, with a nonzero holomorphic factor of polynomial size, on every compact subset of the free-color half-plane ℜw<2\Re w<2.

This result estimates the coherent finite polynomial. An individual Newton summand can have a larger exponential rate there. At even levels the last physical color is different from −2-2, and the conclusion about the complete-end kernel requires a separate argument.

1. Original rows and the assertion

Use the original rows Fj,N(w)=KN(cj,w),cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)θ),θ=π/N,JN=⌊(N−1)/2⌋.(1) F_{j,N}(w)=K_N(c_j,w),\qquad c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\theta),\qquad \theta=\pi/N,\qquad J_N=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor . \qquad\text{(1)} In particular F0,N=1F_{0,N}=1. 5. The actual complete-end kernel at odd levels

Define the holomorphic function Ψ(w)=∫01/2Log⁡λ(x,w)dx.(23) \Psi(w)=\int_0^{1/2}\operatorname{Log}\lambda(x,w)\,dx . \qquad\text{(23)} The integrable logarithmic singularity at x=0x=0 is the one handled by the absolute normalization theorem. The integral is holomorphic on ℜw<2\Re w<2. On the original geometric branch w=−2cos⁡t,Ψ(w)=ψ(t,0).(24) w=-2\cos t,\qquad \Psi(w)=\psi(t,0). \qquad\text{(24)} This is the normalized phase identity in the physical-row note: the opposite endpoint of its integral is ψ(π,t)=0\psi(\pi,t)=0.

Corollary 2. If NN is odd, then on every fixed compact subset of ℜw<2\Re w<2, KN(w,−2)=exp⁡{NΨ(w)+EN(w)},|∂wℓEN(w)|≤Cℓlog⁡N(25) K_N(w,-2)=\exp\{N\Psi(w)+E_N(w)\},\qquad |\partial_w^\ell E_N(w)|\le C_\ell\log N \qquad\text{(25)} for every fixed ℓ≥0\ell\ge0 and all sufficiently large odd NN. In particular the factor eENe^{E_N} is holomorphic and nonzero, and its modulus is bounded above and below by fixed powers of NN.

Indeed cJN=−2c_{J_N}=-2 at odd levels, so symmetry of the original kernel gives KN(w,−2)=FJN,N(w)K_N(w,-2)=F_{J_N,N}(w) exactly. The difference between the integral in (6) and (23), multiplied by NN, is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}): the omitted interval has length 1/(2N)1/(2N) and Log⁡λ(x,w)=O(1/2−x)\operatorname{Log}\lambda(x,w)=O(1/2-x) there. This proves (25).

At even levels Theorem 1 applies to the actual last row cJN=−2cos⁡(π/N)c_{J_N}=-2\cos(\pi/N). It is not an estimate of KN(w,−2)K_N(w,-2). An extrapolation in the physical-row label or a claim about its first-normal correction is not used.

The logarithmic derivative bounds in (25) have a useful local consequence. On a fixed complex neighborhood, the nonzero factor changes by 1+O((log⁡N)/N)1+O((\log N)/\sqrt N) over a bounded Gaussian window of radius N−1/2N^{-1/2}. Thus it can be kept as an NN-dependent nonzero amplitude in a local saddle calculation. A full-source lower bound still needs an original contributing cycle and a bound on its complement; neither follows merely from (25).
