The marked crossing-circle rectangle has ratio at most one

A crossing circle in an augmented link surrounds two main strands. Its complete cusp has a distinguished rectangle determined by the original crossing disk and its meridian paths. This note proves that the positive ratio cc of that disk-framed rectangle satisfies 0<c≤1. 0<c\le1. \tag{1} The hypothesis is an actual complete right-angled matching-polyhedron certificate, including its labeled face pairings and primitive peripheral curves. An abstract ideal rectangle without those identifications need not satisfy (1).

The consequence is a restriction on the trace used by the quantum recoupling formula: it lies at or below −2-2. The proof is geometric but short. The marked paths make the target an element of the complete manifold’s holonomy group. If c>1c>1, that element would be an elliptic rotation with an interior fixed axis, contradicting the free deck action. The endpoint c=1c=1 is a nonidentity parabolic element and is allowed.

1. The rectangle and the based meridian matrices

Assume the labeled cut, complete cusp metric and primitive crossing-circle paths of the matching-polyhedron certificate. In its disk-framed coordinate, the rectangle has vertices (1,E,−1,−E),E=e2πix,0<x<12,c=cot⁡(πx)>0. (1,E,-1,-E),\qquad E=e^{2\pi i x},\qquad 0<x<\tfrac12,\qquad c=\cot(\pi x)>0. The parameter xx describes this rectangle. The number cc is its ratio in the stated disk framing, not an arbitrary choice of Euclidean scale.

Let A,BA,B be determinant-one lifts of the two based main-meridian holonomies. Their basepaths are those of the original threading diagram. They can be normalized as A=−(1101),B=−(10−41). A=-\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=-\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-4&1\end{pmatrix}. Put P=BAP=BA, H=P+IH=P+I, so H2=0H^2=0. The pure transport through the crossing disk has the form C=I+bHC=I+bH, and the transported outgoing meridian is Bout=CBC−1B_{\mathrm{out}}=CBC^{-1}.

The retained half-twist sign is ϵ∈{0,+1,−1}\epsilon\in\{0,+1,-1\}. Let τϵ\tau_\epsilon be the primitive cusp shape in that actual signed diagram. The collar calculation and quantum sign calibration give τϵ=2ic1−ϵic,b−ϵ2=1τϵ,b=−i2c. \tau_\epsilon=\frac{2ic}{1-\epsilon ic},\qquad b-\frac\epsilon2=\frac1{\tau_\epsilon},\qquad b=-\frac{i}{2c}. \tag{2} Thus the same coefficient bb is obtained for all three retained signs.

The inverse cup bend in the quantum contraction fixes the target word. It is W=B−1Bout,Tr⁡W=2+16b2=2−4c2. For a half-twisted disk, that does not follow by simply assuming that the pure transport CC is itself a group element. The next step supplies the actual words.

3. Completeness rules out a ratio greater than one

The certified metric is complete, and its universal developing cover is hyperbolic three-space. Its holonomy acts by deck transformations and hence freely: no nonidentity element fixes an interior point.

Suppose c>1c>1. Equation (3) gives −2<Tr⁡W<2-2<\operatorname{Tr}W<2. The determinant-one matrix then has distinct eigenvalues eiθ,e−iθe^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta}, with 0<θ<π0<\theta<\pi. Its projective action is a nontrivial rotation about the geodesic joining its two ideal fixed points. That rotation fixes the interior points of its axis, contradicting the free deck action. Consequently c≤1c\le1, which proves (1).

This argument uses the actual marked word (6). It needs no additional conjectural packing inequality. It also explains the role of the hypothesis: an abstract rectangle does not supply a deck transformation, so the same contradiction would not be available.

At equality, the target is parabolic rather than the identity. In the chosen lift, W=(8b2−4b+14b216b28b2+4b+1). W=\begin{pmatrix} 8b^2-4b+1&4b^2\\ 16b^2&8b^2+4b+1 \end{pmatrix}. When c=1c=1, equation (2) gives b=−i/2b=-i/2, and W+I=(2i−1−4−2i)≠0,(W+I)2=0. W+I=\begin{pmatrix}2i&-1\\-4&-2i\end{pmatrix}\ne0, \qquad (W+I)^2=0. A nontrivial parabolic transformation has no interior fixed point, so completeness does not exclude this case. A strict inequality would require another argument about this particular word.

4. Consequence for the spectral target

For every crossing circle covered by the complete marked certificate, c<1:Tr⁡W<−2,c=1:Tr⁡W=−2. \begin{array}{ll} c<1:&\operatorname{Tr}W<-2,\\ c=1:&\operatorname{Tr}W=-2. \end{array} Thus the target is either real and outside the physical Casimir interval [−2,2][-2,2], or at its parabolic endpoint. The formal branch c>1c>1 still exists in the local algebraic four-parabolic relation, but it cannot be the complete geometry with these marked identifications.

The conclusion concerns the trace, not every entry of a surrounding operator. It does not place all physical rows in a favorable support domain, identify the surrounding continuant state, or prove that a contour contribution survives. Those are separate analytical tasks. The focused calculation checks the scalar cancellation, the three based words, the trace and the nonidentity endpoint matrix through sixteen exact identities; the free-deck argument above proves the general geometric restriction.
