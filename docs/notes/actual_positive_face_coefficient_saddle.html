The positive face coefficient has one global saddle

30 October 2026. The coefficient below has an exact compact torus integral and a uniform finite Gaussian asymptotic through both folded parities. The determinant contraction proves the global angular gap. This is a coefficient theorem; its identification with the original source uses the separately checked marked face-grid identity. It does not estimate external filling colors. Setting the two marked face variables to zero uses only a connected geometric certificate; it needs neither the finite-r Bessel chart nor a one-main-component hypothesis.

1. The polynomial and its prescribed coefficient

Fix the labeled original right-angled matching graph with R crossing circles and R+2 white faces. Let u,v be the two marked faces. Set their variables to zero. Give the other R faces positive variables l_f. For each actual label vector a=(a_i), let

P_N(l)=product_i [D_ai(x_i,b_i,y_i)D_ai(x_i,c_i,y_i)
                                  P_si(x_i,y_i)],
s_i=N-1-2a_i, d=N-1.

Every argument here is its original shared face variable, including its specified zero when the face is u or v. The local source factorization proves that P_N is homogeneous of degree dR with nonnegative coefficients. The coefficient of interest is

A_N=[product_(f!=u,v) l_f^d] P_N(l).                 (1)

All scalar factors k_ai beta_ai^2, original half twists, and the exact finite-grid measure are outside P_N and must be restored by the source identity. They are not absorbed in A_N.

Assume S=sum_i s_i=o(N), with the graph fixed. Both maximal-fold parities and arbitrary integer near-fold label sequences are included. Let l^geom be the positive finite white-circle normal coefficients from the certified packing with u,v represented by parallel lines. Fix one finite face variable to1 by homogeneity, leaving n=R-1 logarithmic variables.

2. An exact global determinant contraction

For any positive semidefinite matrices X_1,…,X_t with M=sum X_j>0, put A_j=M^-1/2 X_j M^-1/2. Then sum A_j=I. For real theta_j put

B(theta)=sum_j exp(i theta_j) A_j.

This is a contraction: Vz=(A_j^1/2 z)_j is an isometry and B=V* diag(exp(i theta_j))V. Cyclicity of trace gives exactly

Tr(I-B*B)=sum_(j<k)|exp(i theta_j)-exp(i theta_k)|^2
                                           Tr(A_j A_k). (2)

Every pair trace is nonnegative. Since the squared singular values lambda_l of B are at most1, log lambda_l<=lambda_l-1 proves

|det B|<=exp[-(1/2)sum_(j<k)|exp(i theta_j)
                          -exp(i theta_k)|^2 Tr(A_j A_k)]. (3)

A zero determinant satisfies the same bound by continuity. No limiting matrix, independent-entry positivity, or commutativity is required.

Apply (3) to each original D_a, with its nonzero summands bI, xH_-/2,yH_+/2. When all three are present, the two pair traces involving the identity summand have lower bounds cN from the exact bulk-square argument in the curvature theorem. For a two-variable220/022 determinant its single pair trace has the same bound. For202, with b=0, the pair trace is precisely its log-radius variance; the exact positive-polynomial variance proof gives cN. A one-variable200/002 determinant is a monomial and has no angular gap. All constants are uniform on positive compact radius sets near l^geom.

The resulting active pair graph connects all R finite faces. Indeed each shaded triangle equates its finite-face potentials, and the exact rank proof shows that deleting u,v leaves a connected graph. The forced polynomials P_s also have nonnegative coefficients, so their modulus ratio on the torus is at most1 and cannot weaken (3). Consequently, after fixing one phase,

|P_N(l exp(i theta))/P_N(l)|
       <=exp[-c_D N dist(theta,0)^2]               (4)

on the entire n-dimensional torus, with its usual periodic distance. To see the uniform comparison, use a fixed spanning tree: the complex unit phase difference of any vertex from the fixed vertex is at most the sum of the edge differences along its path. Compactness then compares their squared sum with squared torus distance. The only common zero is the constant phase. Every edge row has literal coefficients +1,-1, so there are no other angular aliases.

3. The finite positive critical radius

In logarithmic finite-face coordinates define

Phi_N(z)=log P_N(l^geom exp z)-d sum_f z_f.          (5)

The actual circle-center angle force at the fold gives

N^-1 partial Phi_N(0) ->0.                         (6)

For each shaded triangle the determinant derivative tends to sum_j theta_j dlog l_j/(2pi). The consecutive center triangles give angle sum2pi at each finite white circle, so the total is1 per finite face; d/N tends to1. The retained P_s derivatives are O(S), and hence vanish after division by N. This uses the uniform original finite angle-force theorem, not independent local maximization.

The finite Hessian is real positive on the fixed gauge slice, and its bounds are c_D N I<=H_N<=C_D N I on a fixed small real neighborhood. Its fixed higher derivatives are O(N) on a uniform complex neighborhood: preconditioning a D determinant gives a small perturbation of I, while near-fold forced roots stay separated from the positive radius compact. These are the same finite curvature and holomorphic bounds already proved.

The quantitative implicit-function argument now gives a real critical radius l_N=l^geom exp z_N with z_N=o(1). Use the Hessian at zero as preconditioner: the normalized gradient error tends to zero, and the Newton remainder is O(|z|^2) with uniform constant, so contraction on a ball of radius C|partial Phi_N(0)|/N gives existence and uniqueness there. Strict convexity of log P_N on the gauge slice implies that no other real critical radius exists. Strictness follows directly from the positive determinant monomial support and the connected active pair graph. Let H_N be the Hessian at this actual finite critical radius.

4. The exact coefficient integral and its nonzero asymptotic

The Cauchy coefficient formula, with one variable fixed by homogeneity, is exactly

A_N=P_N(l_N)/product_f l_N,f^d
    *(2pi)^(-n) integral_[−pi,pi]^n
      [P_N(l_N exp(i theta))/P_N(l_N)]
                     exp[-i d sum_f theta_f] dtheta. (7)

There is no unknown source-cycle coefficient in this integral for A_N. Equation (4) makes the part outside any fixed small neighborhood exponentially small relative to its prefactor. At the origin the exact critical equation removes the linear term and the exponent equals

-(1/2)theta^T H_N theta + O(N|theta|^3).

Uniform fourth and higher derivative bounds, rescaling by N^-1/2, and symmetry of the Gaussian give

A_N= P_N(l_N)/product_f l_N,f^d
      /[(2pi)^(n/2) sqrt(det H_N)] *[1+O(N^-1)].   (8)

The cubic term is odd, so its first Gaussian integral is zero; its square and the quartic term cost O(N^-1). Gaussian domination from (4) controls the remaining expansion and tails. The square root is positive. Thus the coefficient is strictly positive for all sufficiently large N in this near-fold window, and has the displayed global relative asymptotic. This proves more than a constructed local contour statement.

5. Absolute value and remaining source tasks

The absolute facet-coning computation of the endpoint determinant action is

2pi lim (1/N)log[P_N(l^geom)/product_f(l_f^geom)^d]
         =2 Vol(Q)=Vol(Y_D).                      (9)

For clarity its geometric input is explicit: each white cyclic-face pyramid contributes the sum of Lambda(theta_j/2), and each shaded contact triangle coned to the marked ideal vertex contributes the sum of Lambda((pi-theta_j)/2). These actual coned facets tile Q. The source Legendre potential is twice this sum, with additive constant fixed by the exact single-positive-variable Cholesky product. This uses the absolute marked action proof, not a volume chosen to normalize P_N.

The shift z_N=o(1) changes the normalized saddle value by o(1), and the Hessian prefactor is subexponential. All exact circle scalars beta_ai also have normalized log tending to zero when S=o(N). Therefore the original fixed near-fold sector, ONCE the exact marked coefficient identity is restored, has absolute rate Vol(Y_D)/(2pi). Its retained half-twist scalar has modulus1 but may affect sums of different sectors.

Combining this coefficient theorem with a common-radius all-channel upper bound gives a whole-source equality for the untwisted complete source, since all its sectors are nonnegative in the specified common normalization. The complete untwisted source theorem performs this combination with the exact measure and scalar. Signed sums and mixed external filling colors remain distinct tasks. No filled-knot volume equality is asserted.
