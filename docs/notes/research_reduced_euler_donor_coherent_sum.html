A finite donor coherent sum for the reduced Euler star

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The actual lower-donor kernel can be summed over its integer donor index, using a specified finite factorial coherent vector. At the physical imaginary quantum parameter this gives a nonzero coherent Gaussian kernel on the O(N)O(\sqrt N) coherent scale. The linear characteristic may vanish. No interpolation in a complex donor occupation is used.

The donor truncation has a superexponentially small relative error. The recipient projectors have such an error only under an explicit macroscopic upper margin. They remain exact outside that margin. A nonzero local matrix kernel is not a nonzero closed source coefficient.

The sole complete proof direct is W, the exact Euler-star word kernel. All additional graph, generating-function, and tail arguments are proved here.

1. The specified finite coherent input

Retain W’s notation d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,h=2πi/N,x=−M/q,fa=(Q;Q)a,GFwa=fawa,(1) d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad h=2\pi i/N,\quad x=-M/q,\quad f_a=(Q;Q)_a,\quad G_Fw_a=f_aw_a, \qquad\text{(1)} with N≥2N\ge2, M≠0M\ne0, and r≥2r\ge2 heads. Let 𝒢=GF⊗r\mathcal G=G_F^{\otimes r}, and fix the actual integer recipient input b=(b2,…,br)b=(b_2,\ldots,b_r), 0≤bj≤d0\le b_j\le d. On the donor use the exact finite vector ψN(λ)=GF∑a=0dλaa!wa.(2) \psi_N(\lambda) =G_F\sum_{a=0}^d\frac{\lambda^a}{a!}w_a. \qquad\text{(2)} The recipient inputs in this same presentation are GFwbjG_Fw_{b_j}; if their original basis is used instead, the corresponding nonzero fbjf_{b_j} factors must be retained.

Let Λz\Lambda_z evaluate normalized basis monomials wa0⊗wa2⊗⋯w_{a_0}\otimes w_{a_2}\otimes\cdots at z0a0∏j=2rzjajz_0^{a_0}\prod_{j=2}^rz_j^{a_j}. Differentiating (3) aa times in λ\lambda at zero recovers the corresponding actual donor column, with its specified factorial basis factors. Thus (2)–(4) are a finite kernel presentation, not a replacement for the original cut or a choice of a new preferred source vector.

3. A connected-graph identity with unit extra-edge factors

For a fixed set of ss ordered sites and fixed labels ℓ1,…,ℓs\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s, write uij=ehB(ℓi,ℓj)−1,i<j,v=maxj,k|ehB(j,k)−1|≤2|h|.(11) u_{ij}=e^{hB(\ell_i,\ell_j)}-1,\qquad i<j,\qquad v=\max_{j,k}|e^{hB(j,k)}-1|\le2|h|. \qquad\text{(11)} Let wsw_s be the sum over all connected graphs on these sites of ∏{i,j} edgeuij\prod_{\{i,j\}\text{ edge}}u_{ij}, followed by the finite label sum weighted by ∏icℓi\prod_i c_{\ell_i}. For s=1s=1, define w1=Hw_1=H.

We need a bound that keeps the cancellations among extra edges. Root each connected graph at site 11, and let its levels be the graph distances from that root. For each other vertex select its smallest-labeled neighbor in the preceding level as parent. This gives a rooted tree TT. Define R(T)R(T) by adjoining to TT:

  • every edge between two vertices in the same level;
  • an edge between adjacent levels when its vertex in the preceding level has label at least that of the selected parent of the later-level vertex.

Every graph producing TT lies between TT and R(T)R(T). Conversely every graph in that interval has exactly these levels and selected parents. Indeed an added edge changes level by at most one, so it cannot shorten a path to the root below the tree level; tree paths attain that level. The parent condition preserves the smallest preceding-level neighbor. These intervals therefore partition the connected graphs, without overlap.

For arbitrary complex edge variables this proves the exact polynomial identity ∑connected graphs G∏e∈Gue=∑rooted trees T(∏e∈Tue)(∏e∈R(T)\T(1+ue)).(12) \sum_{\text{connected graphs }G}\prod_{e\in G}u_e =\sum_{\text{rooted trees }T} \left(\prod_{e\in T}u_e\right) \left(\prod_{e\in R(T)\setminus T}(1+u_e)\right). \qquad\text{(12)} There is no inequality or positivity assumption in (12). At imaginary hh, each extra-edge factor has modulus one. The number of rooted trees is at most ss−1s^{s-1}, by assigning a parent to each non-root vertex. Taking absolute values only after (12), and then summing labels, gives |ws|≤C0sss−1vs−1(s≥2).(13) |w_s|\le C_0^s s^{s-1}v^{s-1} \qquad(s\ge2). \qquad\text{(13)} This is where the physical ray is used. Bounding each extra edge by 1+|ue|1+|u_e| before summation would not give the convergent estimate needed below.

4. Exact exponential generating function and its logarithm

Expand the pair factors in (7) into graphs and group every graph into its connected components. A component on an ordered subset SS has weight w|S|w_{|S|}. This assertion uses order: the increasing relabeling S→{1,…,|S|}S\to\{1,\ldots,|S|\} preserves which endpoint of each pair comes first. The energies (6) are not replaced by symmetric label functions.

If a partition has msm_s components of size ss, the number of such partitions is a!/∏s((s!)msms!)a!/\prod_s((s!)^{m_s}m_s!). Consequently, as formal power series at λ=0\lambda=0, ∑a≥0λaa!Ka=exp⁡(∑s≥1λss!ws).(14) \sum_{a\ge0}\frac{\lambda^a}{a!}K_a =\exp\left(\sum_{s\ge1} \frac{\lambda^s}{s!}w_s\right). \qquad\text{(14)} This also proves the normalization of the singleton term: it is λH\lambda H, not a scalar-normalized replacement.

The connected series converges whenever θ=e|λ|C0v<1.(15) \theta=e|\lambda|C_0v<1. \qquad\text{(15)} For example ss−1/s!≤ess^{s-1}/s!\le e^s, obtained from the elementary integral lower bound for log⁡(s!)\log(s!), makes its absolute tail a geometric series. Both sides of (14) are analytic there and have identical Taylor series, so equality holds. In particular ℰh≠0\mathcal E_h\ne0 on (15), and its holomorphic logarithm with value zero at λ=0\lambda=0 is exactly the connected series. This conclusion holds even at H=0H=0.

The two-site term is w2=∑j,kcjck(ehB(j,k)−1).(16) w_2=\sum_{j,k}c_jc_k(e^{hB(j,k)}-1). \qquad\text{(16)} Taylor’s formula along the imaginary segment gives |w2−hA|≤2C02|h|2.(17) |w_2-hA|\le2C_0^2|h|^2. \qquad\text{(17)} For θ≤1/2\theta\le1/2, (13) bounds the remaining terms by ∑s≥3|λ|ss!|ws|≤2e3|λ|3C03v2.(18) \sum_{s\ge3}\frac{|\lambda|^s}{s!}|w_s| \le 2e^3|\lambda|^3C_0^3v^2. \qquad\text{(18)} Thus the exact connected logarithm satisfies log⁡ℰh=λH+hλ22A+OC0((|λ|2+|λ|3)|h|2).(19) \boxed{\quad \log\mathcal E_h =\lambda H+\frac{h\lambda^2}{2}A +O_{C_0}\bigl((|\lambda|^2+|\lambda|^3)|h|^2\bigr). \quad} \qquad\text{(19)} For any fixed CC and |λ|≤CN|\lambda|\le C\sqrt N, (15) holds for all sufficiently large NN on compact bounded coefficient sets, and ℰh=exp⁡(λH+hλ22A)(1+O(N−1/2)).(20) \mathcal E_h =\exp\left(\lambda H+\frac{h\lambda^2}{2}A\right) (1+O(N^{-1/2})). \qquad\text{(20)} All actual NN-dependent coefficients in (5), including the half-shifts in x=−M/qx=-M/q, remain in H,AH,A. No principal substitution has removed them.

5. Relative control of the finite donor and recipient tails

Fix a compact coefficient neighborhood with C0≤C*C_0\le C_*, and |λ|≤CN|\lambda|\le C\sqrt N. Put D*=|λ|C*D_*=|\lambda|C_*. For an integer L≥2D*L\ge2D_*, successive terms give ∑a=L∞D*aa!≤2D*LL!≤2(eD*L)L.(21) \sum_{a=L}^\infty\frac{D_*^a}{a!} \le 2\frac{D_*^L}{L!} \le2\left(\frac{eD_*}{L}\right)^L. \qquad\text{(21)} The factorial bound follows by integrating log⁡t\log t; no asymptotic factorial formula is needed.

Let the finite unprojected donor polynomial be ℰNfin=∑a=0dλaa!Ka.(22) \mathcal E_{N}^{\mathrm{ fin}} =\sum_{a=0}^d\frac{\lambda^a}{a!}K_a. \qquad\text{(22)} Equations (9),(21), with L=NL=N, show that its difference from (10) is at most exp⁡(−12NlogN+O(N)).(23) \exp\left(-\tfrac12N\log N+O(N)\right). \qquad\text{(23)} On the other hand the exponential on the right of (20) has modulus at least e−C1N−C2e^{-C_1\sqrt N-C_2}, uniformly. The finite unprojected donor kernel is nonzero for all sufficiently large NN, with the same estimate (20).

Consequently the actual factored kernel (4), under (25), satisfies for either sign 𝒞N,b±=exp⁡(λH±+hλ22A±)(1+O(N−1/2)),|λ|≤CN.(27) \boxed{\quad \mathcal C_{N,b}^\pm =\exp\left(\lambda H_\pm+ \frac{h\lambda^2}{2}A_\pm\right) (1+O(N^{-1/2})), \qquad |\lambda|\le C\sqrt N . \quad} \qquad\text{(27)} It is nonzero for all sufficiently large NN, including when H±=0H_\pm=0. The full matrix kernel has the additional exact monomial in (3); its nonvanishing also requires that monomial to be nonzero. At λ=0\lambda=0, the factored kernel is exactly one.

All these bounds hold on slightly larger complex coefficient neighborhoods with the same bound C*C_*. Cauchy estimates therefore give the same O(N−1/2)O(N^{-1/2}) bound for every fixed derivative of the normalized error in the coherent variables and in MM at fixed qq. The integer occupations bjb_j, and hence their projector thresholds, remain fixed when taking those derivatives.

6. What the coherent sum transports

The donor index was summed with every coefficient of the specified finite vector (2). It was not analytically continued to a complex value. Formula (7) beyond a=da=d was used only to prove the tail estimate; it is not a new infinite head.

The unitary extra-edge estimate in (12)–(13) uses imaginary hh. No zero-free neighborhood in a complex quantum parameter or shortcut for closed quantum normals follows. The actual meridian dependence in (1),(5) remains holomorphic and is included in the stated fixed-qq derivative estimates.

If the upper margin (25) fails, the exact formula (4) still holds, but this proof does not approximate its projected value by (20). In particular upper recipient sectors may change or cancel the kernel. The original source can require such sectors; they are not discarded by their absence from the estimate. Nor does (27) estimate a donor sector near a=da=d before the coherent sum.

The original recipient clocks are QbjQ^{b_j}. Holomorphic coefficient parameters in (5) do not prove an interpolation in complex recipient occupations, or produce a physical integer sequence approaching arbitrary complex clocks. The resolved principal identification in W remains a separate statement about the correspondence.

Finally every factorial basis change, neighboring operator, Cartan factor, pivot, scalar line and closing first-jet functional must remain in a full word. The map (3) retains them by transporting the source and dual along with the matrix. A local nonzero kernel, even one proved by an exact complex-weighted sum, does not imply noncancellation of their subsequent contraction or a geometric cycle coefficient.

Coverage and complete proof source

The graph interval partition is a standard cluster-expansion technique; the primary abstract of Fernández–Procacci was checked for coverage of the Penrose tree identity. No external theorem is used in the proof above. The source-specific result is the exact Euler-star coherent sum with its original finite truncations and relative tails.
