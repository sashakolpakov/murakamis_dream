The residual Jacobian and Gaussian determinant of the Conway minimum

21 November 2026. Research note; previous finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The normalized source minimum has a uniformly bounded real Hessian, but its number of variables grows with the quantum level. Bounding its Gaussian determinant by a power of the Hessian condition number would therefore introduce an artificial exponential factor. The tree structure and the Hilbert–Schmidt normalization remove that loss. The determinant below stays within universal constants of one.

This controls a Gaussian normalization, not the value of the minimum. No simultaneous limit in quantum level and temperature, or comparison with hyperbolic volume, is claimed.

1. Actual normalized data and residual coordinates

Use the actual complete Conway compiler of the convex source theorem. There are ss proper vertices. At level NN, each proper packet lies in the full complex Newton space HNH_N, including its confluent normal directions. Put D=NsD=Ns and identify their direct sum with ℂD\mathbb C^D.

Every prescribed leaf, linear gate, bilinear gate and original closing row has been divided by its stated budget 16max⁡(1,∥Tv∥HS)16\max(1,\|T_v\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}). Thus its Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and therefore its operator norm, is at most σ=1/16\sigma=1/16. Write Φv\Phi_v for these local maps, and cc for the row on the whole direct sum which is the normalized original closing row at the top packet and zero elsewhere. Define Gv(x)=Φv((xj)j child of v)−xv,E(x)=∥G(x)∥2+|cx|2.(1) G_v(x)=\Phi_v((x_j)_{j\text{ child of }v})-x_v,\qquad E(x)=\|G(x)\|^2+|cx|^2. \qquad\text{(1)} The feasible set is the product 𝒟\mathcal D of the proper unit balls. All scalar source budgets are retained; if aa is the exact normalized recursion, then G(a)=0,∥av∥≤σ,k=ca=KN/Btot,|k|≤σ2.(2) G(a)=0,\qquad \|a_v\|\le\sigma,\qquad k=ca=K_N/B_{\mathrm{ tot}},\quad |k|\le\sigma^2. \qquad\text{(2)} The cited theorem proves that EE has a unique interior minimizer x*x_* on 𝒟\mathcal D, and I≤Dℝ2E≤3II\le D_{\mathbb R}^2E\le3I there. Set r*=G(x*)r_*=G(x_*). Evaluating at aa gives ∥r*∥2≤E(x*)≤|k|2≤σ4.(3) \|r_*\|^2\le E(x_*)\le|k|^2\le\sigma^4. \qquad\text{(3)} Real inner products mean Re⁡(u*v)\operatorname{Re}(u^*v), and real Lebesgue measure is used throughout.

Order children before parents. Then T(x)=DG(x)=−I+A(x),A(x)s=0,detℂT(x)=(−1)D.(4) T(x)=DG(x)=-I+A(x),\qquad A(x)^s=0,\qquad \det_{\mathbb C}T(x)=(-1)^D. \qquad\text{(4)} In fact GG is a polynomial automorphism of ℂD\mathbb C^D: given rr, its inverse is obtained successively from xv=Φv((xj)j)−rvx_v=\Phi_v((x_j)_j)-r_v. Consequently its real Jacobian is exactly one everywhere.

There is no lower minimum hidden outside the feasible balls. If x∉𝒟x\notin\mathcal D, choose a violating vertex all of whose children are in their unit balls. Then ∥Φv∥≤σ\|\Phi_v\|\le\sigma and ∥xv∥>1\|x_v\|>1, so E(x)≥∥Gv(x)∥2>(1−σ)2.(5) E(x)\ge\|G_v(x)\|^2>(1-\sigma)^2. \qquad\text{(5)} This exceeds (3). Since a polynomial automorphism is proper (its continuous polynomial inverse takes compact sets to compact sets), EE is coercive. Thus x*x_* is also its unique global minimum on the entire real coordinate space.

2. A determinant bound independent of the number of variables

Let T*=DG(x*),d*=c(I−A(x*))−1,H*=Dℝ2E(x*).(6) T_*=DG(x_*),\qquad d_*=c(I-A(x_*))^{-1},\qquad H_*=D_{\mathbb R}^2E(x_*). \qquad\text{(6)} The row d*d_* is the actual closing row transported backwards through the linearized tree. Since each child derivative has norm at most σ\sigma, each parent has at most two children, and each child belongs to one parent, ∥A(x*)∥≤2σ≤18,∥T*−1∥≤87,∥d*∥≤114.(7) \|A(x_*)\|\le\sqrt2\sigma\le\tfrac18,\qquad \|T_*^{-1}\|\le\tfrac87,\qquad \|d_*\|\le\tfrac1{14}. \qquad\text{(7)}

Theorem. Proof. Only bilinear gates contribute to the residual curvature. At a product vertex vv, write the normalized gate coefficients as Bv,kijB_{v,kij}, and define a complex matrix (Cv)ij=∑k(r*)v,k¯Bv,kij. (C_v)_{ij}=\sum_k\overline{(r_*)_{v,k}} B_{v,kij}. Let C0C_0 be the complex symmetric matrix with blocks CvC_v between the left and right child variables and CvTC_v^T in the reverse positions, with every other block zero. Distinct parents have disjoint sibling pairs. Therefore ∥C0∥F2≤2σ2∥r*∥2.(10) \|C_0\|_{\mathrm{ F}}^2 \le2\sigma^2\|r_*\|^2. \qquad\text{(10)} The exact half-Hessian quadratic form is 12H*[h,h]=∥T*h∥2+|ch|2+Re⁡(hTC0h).(11) \tfrac12H_*[h,h] =\|T_*h\|^2+|ch|^2+\operatorname{Re}(h^TC_0h). \qquad\text{(11)} Make the complex-linear change u=T*hu=T_*h. Its real determinant is one by (4). Put λ=cT*−1=−d*\lambda=cT_*^{-1}=-d_*, and H0=I+λ*λH_0=I+\lambda^*\lambda as a complex Hermitian matrix. The first two terms of (11) become u*H0uu^*H_0u. 3. The exact integral and its Gaussian normalization

For a real inverse temperature τ>0\tau>0, define the normalized positive integral on the whole coordinate space ZN(τ)=(τπ)D∫ℂDe−τE(x)dx.(15) Z_N(\tau)=\left(\frac{\tau}{\pi}\right)^D \int_{\mathbb C^D}e^{-\tau E(x)}\,dx. \qquad\text{(15)} The change of variables r=G(x)r=G(x), with its exact Jacobian one, gives ZN(τ)=𝔼r∼𝒩ℂ(0,τ−1I)exp⁡{−τ|cG−1(r)|2},0<ZN(τ)≤1.(16) \boxed{\quad Z_N(\tau)= \mathbb E_{\,r\sim\mathcal N_{\mathbb C}(0,\tau^{-1}I)} \exp\{-\tau|cG^{-1}(r)|^2\},\qquad 0<Z_N(\tau)\le1 . \quad} \qquad\text{(16)} Here the complex Gaussian has density (τ/π)De−τ∥r∥2(\tau/\pi)^D e^{-\tau\|r\|^2}. The inverse G−1G^{-1} is the actual polynomial recursion from Section 1. The scalar cG−1(0)cG^{-1}(0) is the original normalized source kk; it is not replaced by an unsigned model.

The quadratic approximation has the exact integral (τπ)D∫e−τ{E(x*)+12H*[h,h]}dh=e−τE(x*)detℝ(H*/2).(17) \left(\frac{\tau}{\pi}\right)^D \int e^{-\tau\{E(x_*)+\tfrac12H_*[h,h]\}}\,dh =\frac{e^{-\tau E(x_*)}} {\sqrt{\det_{\mathbb R}(H_*/2)}}. \qquad\text{(17)} By (9), its prefactor lies in [196197,(1−2−22)−1/2].(18) \left[\frac{196}{197},\, (1-2^{-22})^{-1/2}\right]. \qquad\text{(18)} In particular the logarithm of this prefactor is uniformly bounded, despite the growing number of coordinates.

For each fixed NN, the same is the usual large-τ\tau prefactor of (15): ZN(τ)=e−τE(x*)detℝ(H*/2)(1+oN(1)),τ→∞.(19) Z_N(\tau)= \frac{e^{-\tau E(x_*)}}{\sqrt{\det_{\mathbb R}(H_*/2)}} (1+o_N(1)), \qquad \tau\longrightarrow\infty. \qquad\text{(19)} Indeed the unique global minimum is interior and nondegenerate. Taylor expansion and h=τ−1/2uh=\tau^{-1/2}u give (19) locally, dominated by a fixed positive quadratic form. On the complement of a fixed neighborhood there is a positive energy gap, by coercivity and uniqueness. Its integral is negligible: integrability of e−Ee^{-E} follows directly from the change of variables in (16), and e−τE≤e−(τ−1)(E(x*)+δ)e−Ee^{-\tau E}\le e^{-(\tau-1)(E(x_*)+\delta)}e^{-E} on a region with gap δ>0\delta>0.

No uniform error bound for (19) when NN also varies has been proved here. In particular neither a polynomial temperature nor an exponentially increasing temperature is substituted into it. The theorem removes a spurious determinant factor from the local Gaussian normalization; it does not determine E(x*)E(x_*). Its source-compatible geometric rate, and a nonzero geometric lower certificate after arbitrary fixed short fillings, remain necessary.
