The division-free braid formula and the partial-filling problem

Written comparison, 14 November 2026. The inspected external source is rivin-jones, commit e956fb919ac7d1bbc7cb3d3873fbac993feb94b7. The mathematical specification, sparse crossing implementation and tensor contraction description were read. No code from that repository was imported, executed, or used for a numerical calculation.

The useful contribution is a particularly explicit presentation of the reduced colored braid scalar. It does not itself prove a volume limit. The deductions below concern its displayed finite formulas. Identifying them with every marked free-boundary object in our construction is a separate task.

1. The exact reduced observation

The mathematical specification uses representation dimension NN, n=N−1n=N-1, v=A2v=A^2, and Jones parameter q=A−4q=A^{-4}. Its crossing entries are Laurent polynomials in AA: the symmetric Gaussian binomials are obtained by a polynomial recurrence, with no division by quantum factorials.

For a braid with knot closure, one strand is cut and its input and output are fixed to the highest-weight state. The other strands are traced with their quantum weights, and the writhe correction is applied. Thus the displayed scalar has a well-defined specialization at nonzero roots of unity. It is not obtained by dividing two values that both specialize to zero.

This is relevant to our prescribed closing functional. Any comparison must preserve that marked scalar, its framing and its boundary specializations. Scalar equality for closed knots alone does not identify an unreduced polynomial at several free boundaries or its normal derivatives.

2. Exact positive magnitudes and the remaining phase

For an integer N≥2N\ge2, take AN=e−πi/(2N),Sm=∏r=1m2sin⁡(πr/N),S0=1. A_N=e^{-\pi i/(2N)},\qquad S_m=\prod_{r=1}^{m}2\sin(\pi r/N),\qquad S_0=1. All SmS_m with 0≤m<N0\le m<N are strictly positive. At this root the symmetric Gaussian binomial is B(a,b)=SaSbSa−b(0≤b≤a<N). B(a,b)=\frac{S_a}{S_b S_{a-b}}\quad(0\le b\le a<N). Indeed the symmetric quantum integer becomes sin⁡(πm/N)/sin⁡(π/N)\sin(\pi m/N)/\sin(\pi/N); its factorial quotient gives the displayed expression. Equivalently this expression satisfies the finite Gaussian recurrence used in the implementation.

For a positive crossing, the allowed transition is (i,j)↦(j+k,i−k),0≤k≤min⁡(i,n−j). (i,j)\longmapsto(j+k,i-k),\qquad 0\le k\le\min(i,n-j). Its coefficient is exactly R+(i,j,k)=ANQ+(i,j,k)SiSj+kSi−kSjSk, R_+(i,j,k)=A_N^{Q_+(i,j,k)} \frac{S_i S_{j+k}}{S_{i-k}S_j S_k}, where Q+=(n−2i+2k)(n−2j−2k)+k(k−1)+Nk. Q_+=(n-2i+2k)(n-2j-2k)+k(k-1)+Nk. For a negative crossing the transition is (i,j)↦(j−k,i+k),0≤k≤min⁡(j,n−i), (i,j)\longmapsto(j-k,i+k),\qquad 0\le k\le\min(j,n-i), and its coefficient is R−(i,j,k)=ANQ−(i,j,k)SjSi+kSj−kSiSk, R_-(i,j,k)=A_N^{Q_-(i,j,k)} \frac{S_j S_{i+k}}{S_{j-k}S_i S_k}, where Q−=−(n−2i)(n−2j)−k(k−1)−Nk. Q_-=-(n-2i)(n-2j)-k(k-1)-Nk.

To check the extra phase in the positive formula, each factor in the implementation’s product is AN2(n−i+r)−AN−2(n−i+r)=−2isin⁡π(i+1−r)N. A_N^{2(n-i+r)}-A_N^{-2(n-i+r)} =-2i\sin\frac{\pi(i+1-r)}N . The product is therefore (−i)kSi/Si−k(-i)^kS_i/S_{i-k}, and (−i)k=ANNk(-i)^k=A_N^{Nk}. In the negative formula the corresponding product combines with its printed (−1)k(-1)^k to give ikSj/Sj−k=AN−NkSj/Sj−ki^k S_j/S_{j-k}=A_N^{-Nk}S_j/S_{j-k}. This proves both identities directly from the displayed coefficients.

In particular every allowed local transition has nonzero coefficient at the matched root. There is no further local zero rule that removes the allowed transitions.

Every crossing conserves i+ji+j. After the braid connections and highest-weight cut are imposed, the entire scalar is consequently a sum over integer points satisfying linear equalities and inequalities: IN=∑𝒎∈𝒫N∩ℤdaN(𝒎)exp⁡(−πi2NQN(𝒎)),aN(𝒎)>0. I_N=\sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathcal P_N\cap\mathbb Z^d} a_N(\mathbf m) \exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi i}{2N}Q_N(\mathbf m)\right), \qquad a_N(\mathbf m)>0. Here aNa_N is the product of the positive ratios above. The integer quadratic polynomial QNQ_N also contains the quantum trace and writhe phases. The braid is fixed; the bounds defining 𝒫N\mathcal P_N depend on NN.

This is an explicit oscillatory sum. An estimate of its positive absolute sum is not a lower bound for INI_N.

For example, in the charge-two block of R+R_+, N≥3N\ge3, the diagonal entries at (i,j,k)=(1,1,0)(i,j,k)=(1,1,0) and (2,0,2)(2,0,2) are both nonzero. Their phase exponents differ by 2n2n, so their phase ratio is AN2n=−eπi/N. A_N^{2n}=-e^{\pi i/N}. It is not positive real. Diagonal similarity leaves diagonal entries unchanged, and multiplication by one common scalar leaves their ratio unchanged. Hence even this block cannot be made entrywise nonnegative by a diagonal phase change and a common scalar. This does not exclude a different, non-diagonal or boundary-dependent construction.

3. What can be transferred to the general research programme

The planned contraction implementation keeps the same weights while changing contraction order. Slicing fixes selected internal indices and sums every resulting slice. Backward support propagation excludes paths that cannot reach the prescribed output. Those are exact operations. They supply no estimate on cancellation among paths that remain.

Our arbitrary-power compiler already contracts finite compositions of multiplication, turns and arbitrary fixed signed crossing powers, and retains their free ordinary boundary polynomials and normal data. Our full scalar-flow construction, Section 3, already retains a bounded polytope from weight conservation. Conservation and exact regrouping are therefore not new missing theorems in the project.

The useful comparison is more specific. Retain the boundary indices of an arbitrary short component in the division-free braid presentation, and prove the exact change from those coordinates to our Newton boundary polynomial, including the prescribed final observation and all repeated-root normals. Then retain the complete quadratic phase when deriving estimates. This could provide a simpler representation for a general composition proof and an independent check of the source. It has not yet supplied the required geometric coefficient.

Two distinctions must be maintained in such an argument. Laurent regularity in AA at fixed integer indices is not holomorphy in continued summation indices; rewriting the amplitudes as continued factorial ratios may introduce divisors again. Also the current external API computes closed knot scalars, whereas the partial-filling theorem needs boundary data before the final closure.

The repository’s finite-level enclosure routines and speed improvements are useful computational infrastructure. They do not bound the error to the large-NN limit. They have not been used as proof inputs here.
