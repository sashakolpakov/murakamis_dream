The actual closed Conway cut from its bowtie ribbon graph

29 October 2026. This supplies a direct CLOSED-diagram identification for the complete Conway geometry. It also corrects an overstatement in the earlier explanation of the open cells: the quadrilateral port of a single leaf pyramid must not simply be called half of the physical four-punctured tangle boundary. The closed identification can and should be proved without that assertion.

The proof below reconstructs the actual projection cut from literal main-wire connections and the original crossing disks. It compares EVERY colored face cycle and its ribbon order with the stationary polyhedron. Matching only component counts or volume is not used.

1. Why the open-port description needs qualification

For a leaf pyramid the port is Q=(C0,C1,C2,C3), while its shaded faces are (O,C1,C2) and (O,C3,C0). Restoring the crossing disk pairs C1 with C0 and C2 with C3. In particular it identifies the two CAP edges of Q within each copy. The two FREE edges are joined across copies through the white faces. The two remaining quadrilateral faces consequently have four paired edges and TWO ideal vertex classes. Their compactification has Euler characteristic 2-4+2=0; it is not the usual two-quad cellulation of a four-punctured sphere, whose four punctures remain four distinct vertices.

Thus an open metric port is useful auxiliary cut data. Its ordered cross ratio and its gluing identities are valid, but those facts do not identify it by itself with the physical four-punctured boundary or determine that boundary’s full holonomy representation. In particular one cannot infer a deformed peripheral representation from port cross-ratio matching alone.

This observation does NOT give a counterexample to the closed hyperbolic-volume theorem. The closed theorem has the following independent projection-cut proof, which avoids the open-port claim.

2. Compile the original main segments without using prisms

Expand the robust expression into h leaves, actual turns and stacks. At leaf i label its four main half-segments

C_i0 bottom-left, C_i1 bottom-right,
C_i2 top-right, C_i3 top-left.

These are the pieces from the leaf’s crossing-disk punctures to its four physical ports. Its two original main arcs join 0 to 1 and 2 to 3, each crossing its own disk once. The half-segments on opposite sides of that puncture are kept DISTINCT in the projection polyhedron; the shaded face pairing will restore the main arc.

An open expression has four exposed, still unpaired half-segments. The literal rules for these four labels are:

  • A turn replaces (C0,C1,C2,C3) by (C1,C2,C3,C0).
  • In a lower F, upper G stack, pair F2 with G1 and F3 with G0. The four exposed labels become (F0,F1,G2,G3).
  • The final cylindrical closure pairs C0 with C3 and C1 with C2.

Each pairing joins exactly the two ends of one actual main segment between successive crossing disks. A structural induction shows that each exposed label is still unpaired, and every removed label is paired exactly once. Therefore a closed expression with H leaves has 2H such segment pairs, not an unspecified equivalence relation deduced from a quantum polynomial.

Make one ideal vertex for each paired segment, and one further vertex O_i for each upper crossing-circle semicircle. At leaf i place the two SHADED triangles

(O_i,C_i1,C_i2),       (O_i,C_i3,C_i0).         (1)

Here each C now denotes its actual paired-segment vertex. These are the original two sides of that leaf’s half crossing disk. Their six edges are the complete bowtie at that leaf. All pairings are the physical identity wires, with no auxiliary circle omitted.

3. Ribbon order determines all white faces

Use the orientation of the original projection sphere. At O_i the cyclic neighbor order is (C_i0,C_i1,C_i2,C_i3). Both shaded triangles in (1) have their displayed positive cyclic order in the local projection chart.

At an outer corner of a shaded triangle, list its outgoing next neighbor followed by its incoming previous neighbor. This ordered pair records the shaded sector; the complementary sector is white. When an actual identity segment joins two such corners, concatenate their two ordered shaded sectors. This is the cyclic order at the resulting four-valent vertex. It follows by taking a thin planar regular neighborhood of the actual joining arc: the two shaded sectors remain on its two sides and the white sectors connect through the two sides of that neighborhood. No strand crosses another during this contraction.

Consequently the source’s literal planar matching gives a complete oriented ribbon graph, including every shaded sector. A facial walk arriving along the dart (u,v) leaves v along the neighbor preceding u in that cyclic order. The walks (1) are exactly the shaded faces; all the remaining walks are WHITE faces. Thus all white polygons are fixed by the actual diagram, not guessed from face counts.

The graph is embedded in the original projection sphere: quarter- turns rotate its charts, and products join the two matching ports by the two actual disjoint identity wires. This construction records nesting and cyclic order as well as abstract incidence. An unembedded graph or a linking matrix would not supply the same information.

4. This is the genuine cut of the closed link exterior

Put the compiled untwisted main diagram in its projection sphere. Every auxiliary circle is the original small perpendicular circle with its recorded twice-punctured spanning disk. Those disks lie in disjoint leaf boxes, and remain disjoint under every actual turn and stack.

In each projection ball, the upper or lower semicircle of an auxiliary component is boundary parallel by its literal half disk. Drill these disjoint semicircles and cut along those same half disks. This returns a ball: drilling one boundary-parallel arc introduces one handle, and opening its recorded bridge disk cancels that handle in the explicit collar of the arc. The collars are disjoint, so this cancellation can be performed for all arcs. The main segments lie on the projection boundary; removing their small neighborhoods makes ideal ends and does not add an interior handle to this cut ball.

The ideal vertices of the cut ball are precisely the upper semicircles O_i and the main segments between disk punctures. Flattening each half disk exposes exactly its two triangles (1). At its punctures, the main-segment sides occur in the order of section 3. The remaining boundary pieces are the original planar projection regions, whose cycles are exactly the white facial walks of that ribbon graph.

This gives a reversible cut with all faces labeled. The two balls glue across white faces by their projection labels. Within each ball, the two shaded faces of disk i are paired fixing O_i and matching C_i1 to C_i0 and C_i2 to C_i3. These restore the two original arcs through that disk. Reversing all cuts therefore recovers the ACTUAL original link exterior, with its retained auxiliary circles and component/mark paths.

5. The stationary polyhedron has exactly this ribbon boundary

The geometric construction uses the same local shaded triangles (1). At a product its explicit prism maps are

F:(C0,C1,C2,C3) -> (v0,v1,P,infinity),
G:(C0,C1,C2,C3) -> (infinity,P,v2,v3).

They make precisely the two segment pairings F2=G1 and F3=G0, and leave exposed corners (F0,F1,G2,G3). A turn cyclically rotates these exposed labels. The final face gluing j->3-j makes the remaining cylindrical pairings, exactly as in section 2.

The right/flat angle table proves that every edge not belonging to a shaded leaf triangle is a flat WHITE/WHITE seam and is removed when those two white pieces are merged. No edge of a shaded triangle is removed. Hence the final nonflat boundary graph is precisely the union of the leaf bowties (1) under the actual segment pairings. The local orientation check in the displayed prism maps concatenates the shaded sectors exactly as in section 3; the maps are the left/right identity stack, not a reflection of one selected child or an unrecorded mutation. Flat white merging does not change any remaining cyclic order.

Therefore the geometric polyhedron and the actual projection cut have the SAME oriented ribbon graph, the SAME two shaded triangles for every disk, and the SAME complete list of white face cycles. Their boundaries are labeled cellulations of a sphere. A map of the matched cells extends over the two balls by radial extension after choosing interior points. Together with the matched white and shaded gluing maps, this gives a homeomorphism of the assembled geometric manifold to the actual link exterior. This is the closed topological identification needed by the volume theorem.

The independent audit below implements section 2–3 directly from the original wires, WITHOUT constructing any prism. It then compares every colored face cycle with the separately implemented stationary cell compiler. This is a stronger check than component counts or the numbers 3H,6H,3H+2; the proof itself is the local ribbon-order argument and reversible disk cut above.

6. Every actual crossing-circle cusp is explicitly marked

The closed-cut identification also gives a general complete-cusp formula. The apex O_i retains the four original neighbors of its leaf pyramid, even after all white merges. In its original local upper-half-space coordinate they are

C0=1, C1=E, C2=-1, C3=-E,
E=exp(2pi i x_i), 0<x_i<1/2.

On a horosphere at O_i=infinity these form a rectangle. Its FREE (white) sides have length W=2sin(pi x_i); its CAP (shaded) sides have length H=2cos(pi x_i). The complete cusp has TWO rectangles, one from each projection ball. Their original disk and projection pairings give the primitive lattice

mu=E-1,     lambda=-2(E+1),
tau_geom=lambda/mu=2i cot(pi x_i).             (2)

One direct path check fixes the marking. The meridian is the inverse shaded map, fixing O and sending C3 to C2 and C0 to C1. Thus at completeness it is exactly z->z+(E-1). The longitude crosses the bottom white face to the other copy and returns through the top white face. Its translation is -2(E+1). This runs along the two sides of the original crossing disk, and is its disk-framed preferred longitude. It traverses those sides once, not twice. The parallelogram has area 2WH, the total area of the two rectangles, so these paths form a primitive peripheral basis.

The sign convention agrees with a positive imaginary cusp shape. Equation (2) is a statement about ACTUAL crossing-circle meridians and longitudes. The Newton wire x_i is used to compute their shape; it is not itself renamed as a meridian logarithm. In the general source filling convention phi_titi(0)=2pi i tau_packet,i, the packet parameter is HALF this geometric ratio:

tau_packet,i=i cot(pi x_i),
dV_i/dU_i=2 tau_packet,i=tau_geom,i.

Matching this Hessian is a useful necessary check, but a full deformed marked representation must still be supplied before the general quantum filling transfer can identify filled volumes.

Audit: literal bowtie ribbon faces and complete cusp paths and results. This repairs the closed cut interpretation; it does not assert that the single open quadrilateral port is a physical four-punctured boundary, or that arbitrary deformed port matches already solve the full peripheral gluing equations.

The audit additionally fixes one global sphere orientation and compares every oriented face cycle and every vertex cyclic order, rather than allowing independent reversal of faces.

The closed disk cut, segment matching and oriented ribbon induction determine the cusp marking. The correction of the former open-face statement does not construct a deformed representation.
