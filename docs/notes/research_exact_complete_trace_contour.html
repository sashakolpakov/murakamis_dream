An exact contour for the complete-meridian trace

Research note, 2 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The finite trace at the complete meridian contains first derivatives at double roots. Those derivatives make a direct Fourier expansion slightly different from an ordinary sampling formula: the coefficient of the positive Fourier mode of charge ℓ\ell is proportional to ℓ\ell. Summing these modes gives a rational kernel with double poles. This note derives that kernel directly from the original finite trace, including the vertical endpoint term. It also gives a version after removing outer grid points, and a criterion under which all charges higher than one are controlled on a single contour.

The contour identity is exact for every signed integer crossing power. The existence of a contour with the required global phase bound for all fixed small powers is a separate analytic problem.

1. The original finite trace at the complete meridian

Let N≥3N\geq3, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and η=(−1)d\eta=(-1)^d. For an even trigonometric polynomial f(t)=f♯(−2cos⁡t)f(t)=f^\sharp(-2\cos t), write

𝒟n,N[f]=ϕ(Rnf♯),CN,n=qnd2/4,τj=2πN(d2−j),0≤j<N.(1) \mathcal D_{n,N}[f]=\phi(R^n f^\sharp),\qquad C_{N,n}=q^{nd^2/4},\qquad \tau_j=\frac{2\pi}{N}\left(\frac d2-j\right), \quad 0\leq j<N. \qquad\text{(1)}

The polynomial f♯f^\sharp may have arbitrary degree. The functional ϕ\phi means the full closing functional in ℂ[z]/(PN)\mathbb C[z]/(P_N), where PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2 and TNT_N is the ordinary Chebyshev polynomial, including its first normal derivatives. Define the odd entire function

g(t)=sin⁡tf(t)exp⁡(inN4πt2).(2) g(t)=\sin t\,f(t)\exp\left(\frac{inN}{4\pi}t^2\right). \qquad\text{(2)}

Proposition 1. The exact complete-meridian trace is

𝒟n,N[f]=ηCN,n∑j=0N−1g′(τj).(3) \boxed{\displaystyle \mathcal D_{n,N}[f]=\eta C_{N,n}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}g'(\tau_j).} \qquad\text{(3)}

In particular, (3) is a derivative sampling formula, not a sum of function values at the distinct roots of the pair algebra.

Proof. The original moving-meridian trace, keeping f♯f^\sharp fixed, is

𝒟n,σ,N[f]=ηNCN,n(σ)sin⁡(2πσ)∑j=0N−1g(τj+2πσN),CN,n(σ)=qn(d2/4−σ2/2).(4) \mathcal D_{n,\sigma,N}[f] =\frac{\eta N C_{N,n}(\sigma)}{\sin(2\pi\sigma)} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} g\left(\tau_j+\frac{2\pi\sigma}{N}\right), \qquad C_{N,n}(\sigma)=q^{n(d^2/4-\sigma^2/2)}. \qquad\text{(4)}

This is the finite identity in the Fourier trace calculation. The grid in (1) is symmetric and gg is odd, so the numerator sum vanishes at σ=0\sigma=0. Its first derivative is (2π/N)∑jg′(τj)(2\pi/N)\sum_jg'(\tau_j). Since sin⁡(2πσ)=2πσ+O(σ3)\sin(2\pi\sigma)=2\pi\sigma+O(\sigma^3), the removable value of (4) is (3). The derivative of CN,n(σ)C_{N,n}(\sigma) gives no extra term. The removable value of the original finite trace is exactly its Hermite trace; no roots or normal directions have been dropped. ▫\square

For later estimates, the summand is explicitly

g′(t)={costf(t)+sintf′(t)+inN2πtsintf(t)}exp⁡(inN4πt2).(5) g'(t)=\left\{\cos t\,f(t)+\sin t\,f'(t) +\frac{inN}{2\pi}t\sin t\,f(t)\right\} \exp\left(\frac{inN}{4\pi}t^2\right). \qquad\text{(5)}

When NN is odd, the single grid point τ=0\tau=0 contributes g′(0)=f(0)g'(0)=f(0), with its original crossing scalar. The two points τ\tau and −τ-\tau associated with a double root give its full value-and-derivative contribution.

2. Summing every positive Fourier charge

For a positive real number yy, define

WN(t)=e−iNt(1−ηe−iNt)2.(6) W_N(t)=\frac{e^{-iNt}}{(1-\eta e^{-iNt})^2}. \qquad\text{(6)}

Its poles lie on the real physical grid. In the lower half-plane,

WN(t)=∑ℓ=1∞ℓηℓ−1e−iℓNt.(7) W_N(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty} \ell\eta^{\ell-1}e^{-i\ell Nt}. \qquad\text{(7)}

This series is absolutely and locally uniformly convergent there.

Proposition 2. For every y>0y>0, the complete trace is

𝒟n,N[f]=−N2CN,nπi∫−π−iyπ−iyg(t)WN(t)dt+ηN2CN,nπ∫−yyg(π+iv)4cosh⁡2(Nv/2)dv.(8) \boxed{\begin{aligned} \mathcal D_{n,N}[f] ={}&-\frac{N^2C_{N,n}}{\pi i} \int_{-\pi-iy}^{\pi-iy}g(t)W_N(t)\,dt\\ &+\frac{\eta N^2C_{N,n}}{\pi} \int_{-y}^{y} \frac{g(\pi+iv)}{4\cosh^2(Nv/2)}\,dv . \end{aligned}} \qquad\text{(8)}

The horizontal integral is directed from left to right. The second line is the exact contribution from the vertical sides of the original rectangle. It is not an error term until it has been estimated for the particular source.

Proof. The grid kernel

KN(t)=iNeiNteiNt−η K_N(t)=\frac{iNe^{iNt}}{e^{iNt}-\eta}

has residue one at every point of the grid. The positively oriented rectangle with corners ±π±iy\pm\pi\pm iy encloses exactly the NN points in (1). Applying the residue theorem to KNg′K_Ng' and integrating by parts on the closed contour gives

𝒟n,N[f]=−ηCN,n2πi∮KN′(t)g(t)dt,KN′(t)=N2ηeiNt(eiNt−η)2.(9) \mathcal D_{n,N}[f] =-\frac{\eta C_{N,n}}{2\pi i}\oint K_N'(t)g(t)\,dt, \qquad K_N'(t)=\frac{N^2\eta e^{iNt}}{(e^{iNt}-\eta)^2}. \qquad\text{(9)}

The derivative KN′K_N' is even, and gg is odd. Reflecting the upper horizontal side by t↦−tt\mapsto-t, with its boundary orientation, therefore makes it equal to the lower horizontal contribution. On the lower side,

KN′(t)=N2ηWN(t), K_N'(t)=N^2\eta W_N(t),

which gives the first line of (8).

On either vertical side t=±π+ivt=\pm\pi+iv, one has eiNt=−ηe−Nve^{iNt}=-\eta e^{-Nv} and hence

KN′(±π+iv)=−N24cosh⁡2(Nv/2).(10) K_N'(\pm\pi+iv)=-\frac{N^2}{4\cosh^2(Nv/2)}. \qquad\text{(10)}

The right side is directed upward and the left side downward. Oddness gives g(−π+iv)=−g(π−iv)g(-\pi+iv)=-g(\pi-iv). Combining both sides, and then replacing vv by −v-v in one term, gives the second line of (8), with the sign shown there. ▫\square

The factor ℓ\ell in (7) is essential: it is the Fourier expression of the first normal derivatives. Using a simple-pole kernel in its place would give a different finite functional.

3. Removing outer grid points before contour deformation

It is often easier to estimate the outer part of the original finite sum directly. This can be done without changing the contour identity for its remaining part.

Choose an integer mm with 0≤m<N/20\leq m<N/2, and set

aN=π−2πmN>0. a_N=\pi-\frac{2\pi m}{N}>0.

Then eiNaN=−ηe^{iNa_N}=-\eta, and ±aN\pm a_N lie halfway between consecutive physical grid points. Define the retained trace

𝒟n,NaN[f]=ηCN,n∑|τj|<aNg′(τj).(11) \mathcal D_{n,N}^{\,a_N}[f] =\eta C_{N,n}\sum_{|\tau_j|<a_N}g'(\tau_j). \qquad\text{(11)}

The removed contribution is literally the sum of (5) over the complementary grid points. In particular, normal derivatives are retained on both sides of this decomposition.

Corollary 3. For every y>0y>0,

𝒟n,NaN[f]=−N2CN,nπi∫−aN−iyaN−iyg(t)WN(t)dt+ηN2CN,nπ∫−yyg(aN+iv)4cosh⁡2(Nv/2)dv.(12) \begin{aligned} \mathcal D_{n,N}^{\,a_N}[f] ={}&-\frac{N^2C_{N,n}}{\pi i} \int_{-a_N-iy}^{a_N-iy}g(t)W_N(t)\,dt\\ &+\frac{\eta N^2C_{N,n}}{\pi} \int_{-y}^{y} \frac{g(a_N+iv)}{4\cosh^2(Nv/2)}\,dv . \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(12)}

Proof. Repeat the proof of Proposition 2 with vertical sides ±aN\pm a_N. The enclosed residues are exactly the terms in (11), and (10) still holds because eiNaN=−ηe^{iNa_N}=-\eta. ▫\square

Given a desired limiting cutoff 0<a<π0<a<\pi, one can choose these midpoint boundaries with aN=a+O(N−1)a_N=a+O(N^{-1}). There is no moving parameter or infinite family of endpoint connectors in (12).

For example, suppose the removed grid terms satisfy

|f(τj)|+|f′(τj)|≤CNAeN(M−δ)(|τj|≥aN)(13) |f(\tau_j)|+|f'(\tau_j)| \leq CN^A e^{N(M-\delta)} \qquad (|\tau_j|\geq a_N) \qquad\text{(13)}

for a fixed δ>0\delta>0. Equation (5) then bounds their total contribution by C(1+|n|)NA+2eN(M−δ)C(1+|n|)N^{A+2}e^{N(M-\delta)}. A bound on values alone is not substituted for (13).

4. A freely deformable lower contour

Let ΓN\Gamma_N be any piecewise smooth path from −aN−iy-a_N-iy to aN−iya_N-iy that stays in the open lower half-plane and is homotopic to the straight segment there. The integrand gWNgW_N is holomorphic throughout that half-plane. Therefore the horizontal integral in (12) can be replaced exactly by the integral over ΓN\Gamma_N. The vertical endpoint current remains unchanged.

This permits a useful separation of choices. The endpoint height yy can be small enough to preserve an already proved endpoint gap. Only the interior of ΓN\Gamma_N needs to descend to the critical point. All the deformations concern one holomorphic rational integrand, rather than an infinite list of Fourier modes.

If −Im⁡t≥h>0-\operatorname{Im}t\geq h>0, then

WN(t)=e−iNt(1+EN(t)),|EN(t)|≤2e−Nh+e−2Nh(1−e−Nh)2.(14) W_N(t)=e^{-iNt}\bigl(1+E_N(t)\bigr),\qquad |E_N(t)|\leq \frac{2e^{-Nh}+e^{-2Nh}}{(1-e^{-Nh})^2}. \qquad\text{(14)}

For all sufficiently large NN, the right side is at most 10e−Nh10e^{-Nh}. Thus, if ΓN\Gamma_N has length at most CNACN^A and

|g(t)e−iNt|≤CNAeNM(t∈ΓN),(15) |g(t)e^{-iNt}|\leq CN^A e^{NM}\qquad(t\in\Gamma_N), \qquad\text{(15)}

then the contribution from every charge ℓ≥2\ell\geq2, already summed together, is bounded by

CNA′eN(M−h).(16) CN^{A'}e^{N(M-h)}. \qquad\text{(16)}

The estimate is conditional on the bound along the entire contour. The existence of a local saddle expansion alone does not imply (15).

5. The actual endpoint current

The endpoint term in (12) has its own finite estimate. Suppose that for |v|≤y|v|\leq y,

|f(aN+iv)|≤CNAexp⁡N(E+Bv2),(17) |f(a_N+iv)|\leq CN^A\exp N(E+Bv^2), \qquad\text{(17)}

where E,BE,B are independent of NN. Since

|expinN4π(aN+iv)2|=exp⁡(−nNaN2πv),14cosh⁡2(Nv/2)≤e−N|v|, \left|\exp\frac{inN}{4\pi}(a_N+iv)^2\right| =\exp\left(-\frac{nNa_N}{2\pi}v\right),\qquad \frac1{4\cosh^2(Nv/2)}\leq e^{-N|v|},

the absolute value of the endpoint term is at most

CNA+2(1+y)exp⁡N{E+By2+(|n|aN2π−1)+y}.(18) CN^{A+2}(1+y) \exp N\left\{E+By^2+ \left(\frac{|n|a_N}{2\pi}-1\right)_+y\right\}. \qquad\text{(18)}

Here x+=max⁡{x,0}x_+=\max\{x,0\}. The factor sin⁡(aN+iv)\sin(a_N+iv) is uniformly bounded when yy stays in a fixed compact interval. If E<ME<M, then a sufficiently small fixed y>0y>0 makes the exponent in (18) strictly less than MM, for every fixed nn. The interior contour may subsequently be moved farther into the lower half-plane.

One can replace the quadratic term in (17) by any established local modulus of continuity. The quadratic form is convenient, but the endpoint argument needs only that its cost tends to zero with yy.

6. What is sufficient for a fixed small-power asymptotic

The exact identity reduces the analytic problem to ordinary steepest descent with two explicitly retained errors. To state this precisely, suppose a fixed crossing power nn and a family fNf_N satisfy the following conditions.

  1. The outer grid terms obey (13).

  2. The endpoint current has a strict gap as in (18).

  3. There is a family of lower contours ΓN\Gamma_N, of bounded length and a fixed positive distance from the real axis, on which

    1. holds and has a strict gap outside a neighborhood of one critical point t*t_*. In that neighborhood,

    fN(t)=Nαa(t)eNΨ(t)(1+O(N−1)), f_N(t)=N^\alpha a(t)e^{N\Psi(t)}(1+O(N^{-1})),

    with a uniform holomorphic error, a(t*)≠0a(t_*)\ne0, and

    Φ(t)=Ψ(t)+in4πt2−it,Φ′(t*)=0,Φ″(t*)≠0,Re⁡Φ(t*)=M. \Phi(t)=\Psi(t)+\frac{in}{4\pi}t^2-it, \qquad \Phi'(t_*)=0,\quad\Phi''(t_*)\ne0, \quad \operatorname{Re}\Phi(t_*)=M.

  4. The local contour crosses the saddle along a direction of strict quadratic decrease, and sin⁡t*≠0\sin t_*\ne0.

Then ordinary one-variable saddle integration applied to (12) gives

𝒟n,N[fN]=CN,nNα+3/2A*eNΦ(t*)(1+O(N−1)),A*≠0.(19) \mathcal D_{n,N}[f_N] =C_{N,n}N^{\alpha+3/2}A_* e^{N\Phi(t_*)}(1+O(N^{-1})),\qquad A_*\ne0. \qquad\text{(19)}

The contour orientation and the continued square root of the Hessian fix A*A_*. The rational correction in (14), the endpoint current, and the removed finite grid terms are exponentially smaller. For the one-edge source with α=−3/2\alpha=-3/2, the resulting terminal polynomial has a nonzero leading amplitude of order N0N^0.

The role of the rational kernel is precise. Once a contour with (15) exists, there is no additional infinite-charge theorem to prove. Constructing and estimating that contour uniformly in the free boundary variable remains necessary. This note does not infer it from the finite trace identity.

7. Finite numerical check of signs and normal terms

Run the finite verification with

python3 experiments/audit_complete_trace_rational_contour.py

The script requires mpmath. The identities (8) and (12) were compared directly with their derivative sums in 120 cases at 60 decimal digits for N∈{3,4,5,8}N\in\{3,4,5,8\}, n∈{−3,0,1,2,3}n\in\{-3,0,1,2,3\}, and f(t)∈{1,cos⁡(2t),cos⁡((N+2)t)}f(t)\in\{1,\cos(2t),\cos((N+2)t)\}, using y=0.17y=0.17, both for the full grid and after removing its outermost pair. The largest relative discrepancy, measured with denominator 1+|𝒟|1+|\mathcal D|, was 5.4×10−475.4\times10^{-47}. The high-frequency examples test the absence of a degree restriction. The case n=0n=0 also checks the cancellation between the horizontal and vertical parts when the exact trace vanishes.

These are numerical sign checks. The proof of the identity is the residue and integration-by-parts calculation in Section 2.
