The source-normalized Racah polynomial has all its zeros above two

30 October 2026. For EVERY folded original channel, the exact matching-edge Casimir polynomial has simple real zeros strictly greater than 2. A positive matrix factorization proves this, including maximal channels at both level parities. Its canonical logarithm and leading action consequently continue to the whole slit plane C minus [2,infinity), including every real geometric target below 2. No continuation of a finite target color g is required.

This theorem concerns the actual scalar polynomial. It does not prove a relative amplitude, control moving-q normal derivatives, estimate the complementary tensor contraction, or select a global source cycle.

1. The exact original polynomial at the root

Use the generic source identity proved in the geometric target polynomial theorem. Its normalization is

D_a G_ag D_g=p_a(lambda_g),
lambda_g=zeta^(2g+1)+zeta^(-2g-1),

with all generic source factors retained. Put q=exp(pi i/N), theta=pi/N, d=N-1, 0<=a<=M=floor(d/2), and specialize to q. Let s_k=product_(r=1)^k 2sin(r theta), and set

lambda_r=2cos((2r+1)theta),
phi_k(x)=product_(r=0)^(k-1)(lambda_r-x).

The generic terminating formula becomes EXACTLY

p_a(x)=sum_(k=0)^a c_(a,k) phi_k(x),
c_(a,k)=s_(a+k)/(s_(a-k)s_k^4).                 (1)

Indeed [d-r]=[r+1] and [d+r+2]=-[r+1] at q, so the generic alternating signs cancel. Also

[g-r][g+r+1]=(lambda_r-lambda_g)/(4sin^2 theta),
product_(r<k)(lambda_r-lambda_a)=s_(a+k)/s_(a-k).

These identities yield (1) without changing the original spectral variable. In particular p_a(lambda_0)=1 and its leading coefficient is

kappa_a=(-1)^a s_(2a)/s_a^4.                    (2)

All displayed denominators are nonzero for 2a<N. For a=0 the polynomial is identically one.

2. An own three-term recurrence

For 0<=n<M define

A_n=-4sin^4((n+1)theta)
                /[sin((2n+1)theta)sin((2n+2)theta)],
B_n=2/[cos(n theta)cos((n+1)theta)],
C_n=-4sin^4(n theta)/[sin(2n theta)sin((2n+1)theta)],
C_0=0.

Then the finite polynomials (1) satisfy

x p_n(x)=A_n p_(n+1)(x)+B_n p_n(x)+C_n p_(n-1)(x).          (3)

Here is a direct coefficient proof. Since x phi_k=lambda_k phi_k-phi_(k+1), the coefficient of phi_k on the left is lambda_k c_(n,k)-c_(n,k-1). The required ratios are

c_(n+1,k)/c_(n,k)=sin((n+k+1)theta)/sin((n-k+1)theta),
c_(n-1,k)/c_(n,k)=sin((n-k)theta)/sin((n+k)theta),
c_(n,k-1)/c_(n,k)
   =4sin^4(k theta)/[sin((n+k)theta)sin((n-k+1)theta)].       (4)

Substitution in (3), followed by the sine addition identities, gives equality. More explicitly the identity after division by c_(n,k) is

lambda_k-4sin^4(k theta)/[sin((n+k)theta)sin((n-k+1)theta)]
  =A_n sin((n+k+1)theta)/sin((n-k+1)theta)
     +B_n+C_n sin((n-k)theta)/sin((n+k)theta).

It is a rational identity in exp(i theta), exp(i n theta) and exp(i k theta); clearing its displayed denominators gives zero. The top coefficient k=n+1 is A_n=kappa_n/kappa_(n+1). The case n=0 follows directly from p_0 and p_1. Thus no presumed orthogonality or external q-Racah recurrence enters the proof.

Set P_n=p_n/kappa_n, the monic polynomials. Equation (3) becomes

P_(n+1)=(x-B_n)P_n-e_n^2 P_(n-1),
e_n^2=A_(n-1)C_n
   =4sin^6(n theta)
      /[cos^2(n theta)sin((2n-1)theta)sin((2n+1)theta)]>0.   (5)

Consequently P_a(x)=det(xI-J_a), where J_a is the real symmetric Jacobi matrix with diagonal B_0,…,B_(a-1) and adjacent entries e_1,…,e_(a-1)>0.

3. The positive shift by two

Let Dpos_a be the previously proved positive q-Hahn matrix; the distinct scalar face factor retains its original name D_a:

(Dpos_a)_(n,n)=1/[cos(n theta)cos((n+1)theta)],
(Dpos_a)_(n-1,n)=sqrt(beta_n),
beta_n=sin^2(n theta)
      /[cos^2(n theta)sin((2n-1)theta)sin((2n+1)theta)].

For completeness, its leading principal minors are

det Dpos_m=2^m product_(r=1)^m sin(r theta)
                    /product_(r=m+1)^(2m)sin(r theta)>0.

This finite formula was proved by the determinant recurrence in the original q-Hahn determinant theorem. Every factor is positive because 2m<N, so Dpos_a is positive definite also at a=M. Put

H=diag(sin((n+1/2)theta))_(n=0)^(a-1),
s0=sin(theta/2).

There is the exact matrix factorization

J_a-2I=2 H Dpos_a H+2s0^2 Dpos_a.              (6)

The diagonal identity is sin2((n+1/2)theta)+sin2(theta/2) =1-cos(n theta)cos((n+1)theta). The adjacent identity is sin((n-1/2)theta)sin((n+1/2)theta)+sin^2(theta/2) =sin^2(n theta). These are precisely the entries in (5),(6).

The right side of (6) is positive definite. Therefore EVERY eigenvalue of J_a is strictly greater than 2. The nonzero adjacent entries also imply simple spectrum: an eigenvector is determined by its first coordinate through the tridiagonal recurrence, and that coordinate cannot vanish. Hence

ALL zeros of p_a are simple, real, and strictly greater than 2. (7)

This is uniform in the folded channel, with no restriction to odd levels or to channels bounded away from the fold.

4. The canonical logarithm and its uniform derivative bounds

Write the zeros as r_1,…,r_a>2. Since p_a(lambda_0)=1,

p_a(x)=product_(h=1)^a (r_h-x)/(r_h-lambda_0).

On Omega=C minus [2,infinity), define

Log p_a(x)=sum_h Log((r_h-x)/(r_h-lambda_0)),     (8)

where each logarithm is continued from its positive real value at any x<2. Equivalently its argument has the principal logarithm in each individual factor. This is a single holomorphic branch on Omega, real on the whole interval x<2. For m>=1,

|partial_x^m Log p_a(x)|
   <=(m-1)! a/dist(x,[2,infinity))^m.           (9)

Thus the original scalar itself, rather than a complexly continued finite color g, supplies a canonical selected branch at every geometric real target x<2.

5. Leading source action continues through the physical endpoint

Here the scalar leading limit can also be proved directly. Let a_N/N tend to alpha in (0,1/2]. For a real x<-2, every term in (1) is positive. Uniform sine-factorial estimates and Riemann sums give

N^-1 log p_(a_N)(x) -> max_(0<=t<=alpha) E_alpha(t;x),
E_alpha(t;x)=J(alpha+t)-J(alpha-t)-4J(t)
                +integral_0^t log(2cos(2pi u)-x) du,
J(v)=integral_0^v log(2sin(pi u)) du.            (10)

The endpoint logarithmic singularities in the sine factorials cost O(log N), uniformly in their integer indices. The final integrand in (10) is smooth and positive-argument for x<-2. The number of summands is at most N, so maximum-term comparison proves (10), including sequences approaching the folded channel.

The maximum is unique and interior. Put A=sin^2(pi alpha), v=2-x and z=sin^2(pi t). Its derivative is

partial_t E=log[(A-z)(v-4z)/(4z^2)].

For x<-2 this strictly decreases from +infinity to -infinity. The critical equation is linear after cancellation of its z^2 terms, and gives

z_*=A v/(v+4A),
t_*=pi^-1 arcsin(sqrt(A v/(v+4A))).             (11)

Now fix an anchor x0<-2. The bounds (9), divided by N, make N^-1 Log p_(a_N) a locally bounded normal family on the connected slit domain Omega: its value at x0 is bounded by (10), and its derivative is uniformly bounded along any fixed compact path. Every subsequential holomorphic limit agrees with (10) on the real interval x<-2. The identity theorem therefore makes the limit unique. Consequently

N^-1 Log p_(a_N)(x) -> Psi_alpha(x)             (12)

locally uniformly on ALL of Omega, with every fixed x derivative. This is the actual source logarithm (8), not a formally chosen solution of a characteristic equation.

On the entire real interval x<2 the continuation is explicitly

Psi_alpha(x)=E_alpha(t_*(x);x),                 (13)

with (11). Indeed 0<z_<min(A,v/4), so all logarithms in the integral up to t_ have positive arguments. Formula (13) is real analytic on x<2 and agrees with (10) on x<-2, proving the claimed continuation. In particular the passage through x=-2 is regular. There is no claimed uniformity at the removed boundary x=2.

The result proves a leading selected SCALAR action. It does not turn the original tensor contraction into a scalar evaluation, or justify discarding its generic first normals. Those distinctions remain essential to the global volume problem.
