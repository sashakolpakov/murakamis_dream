The actual Newton source and its quantum canonical transform

This note gives a generic Newton-index recurrence for the actual knot source, a finite pointing that determines it, and an exact selected-period formula. It also proves an explicit quantum birational transform and its classical canonical map. The classical map resolves a signed complete boundary point into the cut curve. No geometric-support or volume asymptotic assumption enters these statements.

The final unresolved issue is selection and control of a geometric coefficient branch in the actual selected sum. An arbitrary root solution of the operator is insufficient.

1. Actual input and Newton interpolation

Use the conventions of the actual Fourier–Mellin source: QN=exp⁡(2πi/N)Q_N=\exp(2\pi i/N), Y=M2Y=M^2, and the actual reduced periods satisfy

FN(QNj)=Cj(QN)(1≤j≤N),FN(1)=CN(QN)=⟨K⟩N. F_N(Q_N^j)=C_j(Q_N)\quad(1\le j\le N),\qquad F_N(1)=C_N(Q_N)=\langle K\rangle_N. \tag{1.1}

Here Cj(Q)C_j(Q) are the actual finite-color Laurent polynomials over the recorded rational coefficient field, not a replacement state. Only fixed small colors will be needed as initial data. Whole reciprocity FN(Y)=FN(Y−1)F_N(Y)=F_N(Y^{-1}) is proved in actual Weyl descent. A nonzero all-level operator over ℚ(Q,Y)\mathbb Q(Q,Y) is proved in squared-meridian descent.

For generic QQ, put

aj=Qj+Q−j,tn(X)=∏r=1n(X−ar),vn(Y)=(1−Y)tn(Y+Y−1). a_j=Q^j+Q^{-j},\qquad t_n(X)=\prod_{r=1}^n(X-a_r),\qquad v_n(Y)=(1-Y)t_n(Y+Y^{-1}).

The nodes a1,a2,…a_1,a_2,\ldots are distinct in ℚ(Q)\mathbb Q(Q). Define the universal rational Newton coefficients by

cn(Q)=∑j=1n+1Cj(Q)∏1≤r≤n+1r≠j(aj−ar). \boxed{c_n(Q)= \sum_{j=1}^{n+1} \frac{C_j(Q)} {\prod_{\substack{1\le r\le n+1\\r\ne j}}(a_j-a_r)}.} \tag{1.2}

This is the divided difference at the first n+1n+1 nodes; c0=C1=1c_0=C_1=1. Newton interpolation proves the exact identity

Cm(Q)=∑n=0m−1cn(Q)tn(am)(m≥1). \boxed{C_m(Q)=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}c_n(Q)t_n(a_m)} \qquad(m\ge1). \tag{1.3}

For completeness, subtract the degree-(m−2)(m-2) interpolant at the first m−1m-1 nodes. Its difference from the degree-(m−1)(m-1) interpolant vanishes at these nodes, and hence is a scalar multiple of tm−1t_{m-1}. Evaluation at the last node gives its scalar, equivalently (1.2). Induction proves (1.3).

The formal series

ℱ(Q,Y)=∑n≥0cn(Q)tn(Y+Y−1) \mathscr F(Q,Y)=\sum_{n\ge0}c_n(Q)t_n(Y+Y^{-1}) \tag{1.4}

is interpreted in the Newton completion, not as an asserted analytic series. Evaluation at Y=QmY=Q^m is finite: all terms n≥mn\ge m vanish. Thus it has exactly the actual fixed-color values Cm(Q)C_m(Q).

3. The transformed recurrence is actual at generic QQ

As proved in whole-source Newton transport, section 6, the actual all-level annihilator may be made nonzero and invariant under

θ(Y)=Y−1,θ(T)=QT−1. \theta(Y)=Y^{-1},\qquad \theta(T)=QT^{-1}.

Its fixed algebra is generated by X,S,ZX,S,Z. Fix such an actual operator R=R(Q;X,S,Z)≠0 R=R(Q;X,S,Z)\ne0 annihilating fN=(1−Y)FNf_N=(1-Y)F_N, and form R̂=R(Q;X̂,Ŝ,Ẑ)=∑k=prbk(Q,u)Ek. \widehat R=R(Q;\widehat X,\widehat S,\widehat Z) =\sum_{k=p}^{r}b_k(Q,u)E^k. \tag{3.1}

∑k=prbk(Q,Qn)cn+k(Q)=0(n≥0),cj=0(j<0). \boxed{\sum_{k=p}^{r}b_k(Q,Q^n)c_{n+k}(Q)=0 \quad(n\ge0),\qquad c_j=0\ (j<0).} \tag{3.2}

Proof by the actual whole source, not an off-root lift

At a fixed root QNQ_N, write the actual reciprocal Laurent polynomial as a polynomial in XX:

FN(Y)=∑m=0DNcm(N)tm(Y+Y−1). F_N(Y)=\sum_{m=0}^{D_N}c_m^{(N)}t_m(Y+Y^{-1}).

The monic Newton polynomials remain a basis even if some nodes coincide. For a fixed mm, take N>2(m+1)N>2(m+1). The first m+1m+1 nodes are then distinct, so their actual values (1.1) give

cm(N)=cm(QN). c_m^{(N)}=c_m(Q_N). \tag{3.3}

Apply the exact root operator RR and extract a fixed output coefficient nn using (2.1). Only finitely many indices n+kn+k occur. For every sufficiently large NN, all their coefficients are given by (3.3), and there are no coefficient poles. Consequently the rational function

Hn(Q)=∑kbk(Q,Qn)cn+k(Q) H_n(Q)=\sum_k b_k(Q,Q^n)c_{n+k}(Q)

vanishes at infinitely many distinct QNQ_N. It is identically zero. This proves (3.2) for every fixed nn. No uniform limit in n,Nn,N and no claim of reciprocity for an arbitrary off-root matrix lift are used.

Exact finite-root folding and its corner

At Q=QNQ=Q_N, set U=YNU=Y^N, W=U+U−1−2W=U+U^{-1}-2. If the actual finite Newton coefficients are collected as

C(N)(z)=∑mcm(N)zm=∑s=0N−1zsCs(N)(zN), C^{(N)}(z)=\sum_m c_m^{(N)}z^m =\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}z^s C_s^{(N)}(z^N),

then the exact folded whole source is

This is vs+N=Wvsv_{s+N}=Wv_s; it retains the entire central corner. The finite matrices of X,S,ZX,S,Z, including Wv0Wv_0 in their last columns, are those of the whole-source transport note.

Equation (3.3) concerns fixed indices and sufficiently large levels. It does not assert cm(N)=cm(QN)c_m^{(N)}=c_m(Q_N) simultaneously for every m<Nm<N. At colliding nodes the latter expression can have poles, and whole parameter jets must be retained.

There is an exact confluent extraction rule for the actual root polynomial PN(X)=FN(Y)P_N(X)=F_N(Y): cm(N)=∑αRes⁡X=αPN(X)tm+1(X)dX, c_m^{(N)} =\sum_{\alpha} \operatorname{Res}_{X=\alpha} \frac{P_N(X)}{t_{m+1}(X)}\,dX, \tag{3.5} where the sum runs over the distinct first m+1m+1 nodes, with their multiplicities retained in the denominator. To verify it, expand PNP_N in the Newton basis. Terms of index below mm have no X−1X^{-1} coefficient at infinity; the term of index mm has coefficient 11; higher terms are divisible by tm+1t_{m+1} and have no finite residues. Repeated nodes therefore demand whole source jets rather than division by zero. For m<Nm<N their multiplicities are at most two, so values and first XX-derivatives suffice. The last node at m=N−1m=N-1 includes X=2X=2, whose value is the selected period; this rule does not compute that missing value from nonidentity ordinary-root values.

4. Finite pointing and the selected Newton period

The generic recurrence (3.2) determines its actual solution from finitely many fixed colors. Here is a direct bound.

Left-multiply R̂\widehat R by E−pE^{-p}, so its shifts are 0,…,d0,\ldots,d, where d=r−pd=r-p. Clear a common coefficient denominator in QQ and write

R̃=∑j=0dBj(Q,u)Ej,Bj∈ℚ[Q±1,u±1],Bd≠0. \widetilde R=\sum_{j=0}^d B_j(Q,u)E^j,\qquad B_j\in\mathbb Q[Q^{\pm1},u^{\pm1}],\quad B_d\ne0. \tag{4.1}

The resulting recurrence is valid when n≥max⁡(0,p)n\ge\max(0,p). Let A*A_* be the QQ-exponent span in the collected support of BdB_d. For every integer n>A*n>A_*, Bd(Q,Qn)≠0B_d(Q,Q^n)\ne0 identically: a collision between monomials QaubQ^a u^b, Qa′ub′Q^{a'}u^{b'}, with b≠b′b\ne b', would require n=|a−a′|/|b−b′|≤A*n=|a-a'|/|b-b'|\le A_*. Terms with equal bb do not collide after collection.

Choose n*≥max⁡(0,p,A*+1)n_*\ge\max(0,p,A_*+1). The finitely many coefficients c0,…,cn*+d−1 c_0,\ldots,c_{n_*+d-1} propagate rationally to all later indices. They require only the fixed ordinary colors C1,…,Cn*+dC_1,\ldots,C_{n_*+d} through (1.2). For d=0d=0, the recurrence instead forces the tail cn=0c_n=0 for n≥n*n\ge n_*. Thus no assumed order or regular-singular condition at n=0n=0 is needed.

The selected period is now the following whole regular evaluation:

⟨K⟩N=[∑n=0N−1cn(Q)∏j=1n(QN+Q−N−Qj−Q−j)]Q=QN. \boxed{\langle K\rangle_N= \left[ \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}c_n(Q) \prod_{j=1}^{n} \big(Q^N+Q^{-N}-Q^j-Q^{-j}\big) \right]_{Q=Q_N}.} \tag{4.2}

Indeed the bracketed rational expression is exactly CN(Q)C_N(Q) by (1.3), and CN(Q)C_N(Q) is Laurent polynomial. It is regular at QNQ_N, with value (1.1). This formula uses the actual finite pointing and actual operator. No new levelwise normalization is free.

One must not replace (4.2) by ∑n<Ncn(QN)tn(2)\sum_{n<N}c_n(Q_N)t_n(2) unless the individual cnc_n are known regular there. Equation (4.2) first combines the whole germ, including all coefficient poles and cancellations.

Uniformly bounded parameter jets

Let D*D_* be the uu-exponent span and s*s_* the number of monomials of BdB_d. For all sufficiently large NN, the nonzero Laurent polynomial Bd(QN,u)B_d(Q_N,u) has at most D*D_* zeros among a segment of fewer than NN distinct orbit points u=QNnu=Q_N^n. Each nonzero substituted Laurent polynomial Bd(Q,Qn)B_d(Q,Q^n) has at most s*s_* monomials. Its vanishing multiplicity at any nonzero QQ is at most s*−1s_*-1: otherwise its first s*s_* logarithmic derivatives give a Vandermonde system in its distinct integer exponents.

The recurrence propagation needed to produce c0,…,cN−1c_0,\ldots,c_{N-1} therefore has total pole budget at QNQ_N bounded by

B*=D*(s*−1), \boxed{B_*=D_*(s_*-1),} \tag{4.3}

outside finitely many levels where a fixed coefficient or initial denominator has a pole. Multiplication by the analytic weights in (4.2) does not increase it. A common propagation denominator gives a numerator whose prescribed vanishing cancels these poles, so a QQ-jet of order at most B*B_* of the fixed initial data suffices for the regular value. This includes tangencies of the moving orbit u=Qnu=Q^n, not merely zeros of Bd(QN,u)B_d(Q_N,u).

5. Exact quantum birationality

Let 𝒬\mathcal Q be the generic quantum torus generated by Y±1Y^{\pm1} and T±1T^{\pm1}, TY=QYTTY=QYT, and let 𝒜=𝒬θ\mathcal A=\mathcal Q^\theta. The assignments (2.2)–(2.4) give an injective homomorphism

ψ:𝒜→k⟨u±1,E±1⟩/(Eu=QuE). \psi:\mathcal A\longrightarrow k\langle u^{\pm1},E^{\pm1}\rangle/(Eu=QuE). \tag{5.1}

To see that it is well defined, a relation in X,S,ZX,S,Z acts as zero on every vnv_n, hence its coefficient action acts as zero on every finite sequence supported on n≥0n\ge0. A finite difference operator ∑aj(u)Ej\sum a_j(u)E^j doing so has aj(Qm)=0a_j(Q^m)=0 for infinitely many mm; each rational function aja_j is zero. Thus the coefficient formulas respect all relations.

For injectivity, if a quantum-torus element annihilates all vnv_n, write it in normal form ∑a,bra,bYaTb\sum_{a,b}r_{a,b}Y^aT^b. Since vn=−Yn+1+⋯v_n=-Y^{n+1}+\cdots, the largest aa gives ∑bra,bQb(n+1)=0\sum_b r_{a,b}Q^{b(n+1)}=0 for all nn. The Vandermonde system in the distinct QbQ^b makes these coefficients zero. Descend through aa. The element is zero.

Most importantly, the coefficient formulas obey the exact identity

Ẑ=u(X̂+QŜ)−(Q+Q−1). \boxed{\widehat Z=u(\widehat X+Q\widehat S)-(Q+Q^{-1}).} \tag{5.2}

Therefore in the quantum division ring

u=(Ẑ+Q+Q−1)(X̂+QŜ)−1,E−1=X̂−Qu−Q−1u−1. \boxed{u=(\widehat Z+Q+Q^{-1}) (\widehat X+Q\widehat S)^{-1},\qquad E^{-1}=\widehat X-Qu-Q^{-1}u^{-1}.} \tag{5.3}

The order of the inverse in (5.3) is essential. Its denominator is nonzero since the E−1E^{-1} coefficient in X̂+QŜ\widehat X+Q\widehat S is 11.

These formulas prove an isomorphism of quantum fraction fields, not just a representation on special states. Here is a short justification that the fractions exist without importing a structural theorem. The quantum torus is an Ore domain: to find a common right multiple of nonzero a,ba,b, choose unknown multipliers in a large monomial square of side mm. There are 2(m+1)22(m+1)^2 unknown scalar coefficients and only (m+O(1))2(m+O(1))^2 output coefficients in ar−bsar-bs, so a nonzero solution exists for large mm. The domain property ensures both multipliers are nonzero. The left argument is identical.

Its fixed subalgebra is Ore too. For ar=bsar=bs, with a,ba,b invariant, average r,sr,s with their images under θ\theta. If the average is zero, both are anti-invariant; multiply both on the right by the nonzero anti-invariant Y−Y−1Y-Y^{-1}. The resulting common multipliers are invariant and nonzero. The left version is analogous. Thus (5.1) extends to fractions, and (5.3) shows its image contains the entire u,Eu,E quantum division ring.

6. Classical characteristic and the canonical chart

At Q=1Q=1, write ee for the commuting symbol of EE. The transform is

x=e−1+u+u−1,s=u+u−1+(u−u−1)2e,z=u(x+s)−2. \boxed{ x=e^{-1}+u+u^{-1},\quad s=u+u^{-1}+(u-u^{-1})^2e,\quad z=u(x+s)-2.} \tag{6.1}

These obey

x2+s2+z2−xsz−4=0. x^2+s^2+z^2-xsz-4=0. \tag{6.2}

They are birational coordinates on this Weyl-quotient boundary surface, with rational inverse

u=z+2x+s,e=1x−u−u−1. \boxed{u=\frac{z+2}{x+s},\qquad e=\frac1{x-u-u^{-1}}.} \tag{6.3}

For a direct converse calculation, put u=(z+2)/(x+s)u=(z+2)/(x+s); then

(x−u−u−1)(s−u−u−1)−(u−u−1)2=−x2+s2+z2−xsz−4z+2. (x-u-u^{-1})(s-u-u^{-1})-(u-u^{-1})^2 =-\frac{x^2+s^2+z^2-xsz-4}{z+2}.

This proves the inverse on the displayed open chart.

On the full boundary torus Y,LY,L, the same chart is

u=YL+1Y+L,e=Y(Y+L)(YL+1)L(Y2−1)2. \boxed{ u=\frac{YL+1}{Y+L},\qquad e=\frac{Y(Y+L)(YL+1)}{L(Y^2-1)^2}.} \tag{6.4}

Both functions are invariant under (Y,L)↦(Y−1,L−1)(Y,L)\mapsto(Y^{-1},L^{-1}). They generate its invariant function field, and direct differentiation gives

{log⁡e,log⁡u}=1when{log⁡L,log⁡Y}=1. \{\log e,\log u\}=1 \quad\text{when}\quad\{\log L,\log Y\}=1. \tag{6.5}

Thus this is a canonical chart on the quotient. The full boundary torus generically double-covers it; claiming birationality to the full Y,LY,L curve would omit this Weyl cover.

If RR is normalized to have a nonzero classical symbol r(x,s,z)r(x,s,z), then the classical characteristic of R̂\widehat R is precisely rr after (6.1). Specialization is valid term by term; the map on classical function fields is injective, so no nonzero leading symbol is silently lost. Every curve component meeting the common open chart is carried birationally to its transformed component. This statement concerns the symbol of the actual specified invariant operator. It does not assume that its zero set has already been proved to be the geometric AA-component.

7. The kernel action and the cut curve

The exact Newton kernel is

8. What root operator data alone do not select

There is an explicit counterexample to selection from only a root operator, reciprocity, degree, and nonidentity ordinary-root values. At each level let
