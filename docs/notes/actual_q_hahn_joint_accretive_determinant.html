An exact accretive determinant for the joint magnetic source

30 October 2026. The finite rational continuation specified in the magnetic corridor note, section 6, has an exact tridiagonal determinant. Its Hermitian part is positive on a joint complex domain in the pair degree and the magnetic coordinate. This proves joint zero exclusion and uniform logarithmic derivative bounds for the ORIGINAL finite source, including both parities, composite levels and the folded channel endpoint. The domain is a central wedge; it is smaller than the fixed-real-degree strip. No global source cycle, magnetic stationary point, or volume limit is selected by this theorem.

1. The exact finite source, including its scalar

Put d=N-1, theta=pi/N, q=exp(i theta), Q=q^2, x=q^d and 0<=j<=floor(d/2). Gaussian binomials below have base Q. The source is

fhat_j(u,v)=x^u H_j(U,V)/D_j(UV),
U=Q^u, V=Q^v, T=UV=Q^t, t=u+v,
D_j(T)=product_(r=0)^(j-1)(T-Q^r),

H_j(U,V)=sum_(a=0)^j [j choose a]_Q^2 q^[a(a-d)] V^a
    product_(r=0)^(a-1)(U-Q^r)
    product_(r=0)^(j-a-1)(V-Q^r).                  (1)

All exponential powers use these displayed logarithms. At physical integer u,v this is the original normalized complete-circle column; (1) also specifies its continuation, without interpolating samples. Set

S_j(U,T)=U^j H_j(U,T/U).

Since q1=(-1)^a Q2, this is the polynomial

S_j(U,T)=sum_(a=0)^j (-1)^a Q^[a(a+1)/2]
  [j choose a]_Q^2 T^a
  product_(r=0)^(a-1)(U-Q^r)
  product_(r=0)^(j-a-1)(T-UQ^r).                  (2)

It has degree at most j separately in U and T. Two exact evaluations are

S_j(U,0)=(-1)^j Q^[j(j-1)/2] U^j,
S_j(0,T)=T^j [2j choose j]_Q.                    (3)

For the second, the finite coefficient identity sum_a Q(a2)[j choose a]_Q^2=[2j choose j]Q follows by comparing z^j in product(r=0)(2j-1)(1+Qr z), split into its two j-factor blocks. All scalar factors in (3) are nonzero: 2j<N.

2. A joint Casimir identity, proved before continuation

The exact polynomial identity is

-Q^(-j)(U-T)(1-QU) S_j(QU,T)
-Q^j(U-QT)(1-U) S_j(U/Q,T)
+[2(1+QT)U-(1+Q)U^2
                  -T(Q^(j+1)+Q^(-j))] S_j(U,T)=0.              (4)

Here is a finite proof which does not extend an integer-t recurrence without justification. For the j+1 original complete slices T=Q(d-j),…,Qd, the exact physical Casimir equation gives (4). The entire-U equation on each slice is already proved in section 4 of the magnetic corridor note: its leading U^(j+2) coefficient cancels, and its U=0 value and all original t+1 column nodes make the remaining polynomial of degree at most j+1 vanish. This includes t=d-j=j when N is odd and the channel is maximal.

The left side of (4) has degree at most j+1 in T. At T=0, substitute the first identity in (3); the remaining factor is

-U(1-QU)-U(1-U)+2U-(1+Q)U^2=0.

The j+1 distinct nonzero complete slices and T=0 are j+2 distinct zeros, so (4) holds identically in T and U. This argument proves the complex-degree source identity at every admissible N directly. The focused audit also verifies (4) coefficientwise at formal Q for j=2 through 6; the proof does not depend on those checks.

Write

c=-cos((t+1)theta),
P_j(z;c)=S_j(q^t z,Q^t)/[Q^(jt) [2j choose j]_Q].                (5)

The constant coefficient is 1. Substitution into (4), followed by coefficient comparison, gives, with p_(j,-1)=0 and p_(j,0)=1,

sin(k theta) sin((2j+1-k)theta) p_(j,k)
  =2c sin^2((j+1-k)theta) p_(j,k-1)
   +sin((j+2-k)theta) sin((j+1-k)theta) p_(j,k-2).               (6)

All denominators for 1<=k<=j are positive. Thus this identity determines P_j for every complex t, not only for physical slices. Define the monic reverse polynomial R_j(z;c)=z^j P_j(1/z;c). Equation (1) is exactly

fhat_j(u,t-u)=x^u q^(jt) [2j choose j]_Q
               R_j(q^t/U;c)/D_j(Q^t).                         (7)

In particular no root-dependent normalization is discarded.

3. The ordinary three-term recurrence

For 1<=n<floor(d/2), put

B_n=sin^2(n theta)/[cos^2(n theta)
                     sin((2n-1)theta)sin((2n+1)theta)].         (8)

The recurrence is

R_0=1, R_1=z+c/cos(theta),
R_(n+1)=[z+c/(cos(n theta)cos((n+1)theta))]R_n
            +B_n[cos^2(n theta)-c^2]R_(n-1).                  (9)

We prove that no lower-degree terms are hidden in (9). Set [r]=sin(r theta)/sin(theta) and D_q zr=[r]z(r-1). Equation (6) is equivalent to

L_c R_n=[n][n+1]R_n,
L_c=D_q ((z^2+2cz+1)D_q).                                    (10)

Indeed L_c zr=[r][r+1]zr+2c[r]2z(r-1)+[r][r-1]z^(r-2). The eigenvalues for n=0,…,J, J=floor(d/2), are distinct: their successive differences are [2n]>0.

Define finite moments mu_0=1, mu_(-1)=0, and

[r+1]mu_r+2c[r]mu_(r-1)+[r-1]mu_(r-2)=0
                       (1<=r<=2J-1).                         (11)

No denominator vanishes, since 2J<N. On available degrees let <zm,zn>=mu_(m+n). The difference <z^m,L_c z^n>-<L_c zm,zn> is [n-m] times the left side of (11) at r=m+n, by the elementary sine addition identities. Thus the R_n are pairwise orthogonal whenever the requisite total degree is at most 2J-1.

Inductively assume the lower norms are nonzero for generic c. In the expansion of zR_n-R_(n+1), pairing with R_k, k<=n-2, kills every coefficient below R_(n-1), since multiplication by z is symmetric. The two remaining coefficients are read from the top powers. Equation (6) gives

p_(n,1)=c tan(n theta)/sin(theta),
p_(n,2)=[2c sin^2((n-1)theta)p_(n,1)
               +sin(n theta)sin((n-1)theta)]
                    /[sin(2theta)sin((2n-1)theta)],            (12)

where p_(1,2)=0. Subtracting these coefficients at n and n+1 gives exactly (9). The norm identity is

<R_n,R_n>=-B_n[cos^2(n theta)-c^2]<R_(n-1),R_(n-1)>.

It proves the generic nonvanishing needed for the next induction step, starting at norm 1; hence there is no assumption of positivity of this auxiliary moment functional. Polynomial continuation in c then gives (9) for every c. Only moments through 2J-1 are used, including for the maximal channel. The moment functional is a proof device, not a new quantum inner product.

4. A positive definite real part

For a chosen size j, define real j-by-j matrices D and K, with indices 0,…,j-1. Their diagonal and adjacent entries are

D_(n,n)=1/[cos(n theta)cos((n+1)theta)],
D_(n-1,n)=D_(n,n-1)=sqrt(B_n),
K_(n-1,n)=sqrt(B_n)cos(n theta),
K_(n,n-1)=-sqrt(B_n)cos(n theta), K_(n,n)=0.                    (13)

All other entries are zero. Thus D is symmetric and K is skew-symmetric. The tridiagonal determinant recurrence and (9) give

R_j(z;c)=det(zI+cD+K).                                        (14)

Every leading principal minor of D is explicitly

det D_m=2^m product_(r=1)^m sin(r theta)
                 /product_(r=m+1)^(2m) sin(r theta)>0,
              1<=m<=j.                                     (15)

To see the formula without solving a separate determinant problem, compare the leading c^m coefficient of R_m(0;c)=p_(m,m)(c) in (14). Iteration of the highest-c terms of (6) gives precisely (15). All factors are positive because 2m<N. Sylvester’s criterion gives D positive definite, including when j=floor(d/2).

If Re c>0, the Hermitian part of cD+K is (Re c)D>0. For any eigenvector, its eigenvalue therefore has positive real part. The zeros of R_j all lie strictly in Re z<0; reversal gives the same statement for P_j. No real-rootedness assertion is needed or made for noninteger t. For positive real c the coefficients in (6) are positive, so R_j(1;c)>0.

More generally A=zI+cD+K is invertible whenever Re z>=0 and Re c>0. In the open product of right half-planes its logarithmic derivatives satisfy

|partial_z log det A|<=j/Re z,
|partial_c log det A|<=j/Re c.                                (16)

The first follows from Re A>=(Re z)I and the inverse operator norm. For the second conjugate by D^(-1/2):

D^(-1/2) A D^(-1/2)
    =zD^(-1)+cI+D^(-1/2)KD^(-1/2).

Its Hermitian part is at least (Re c)I, and its inverse has trace partial_c log det A. Thus (16) uses no uniform lower eigenvalue bound for D, which could degenerate as the channel approaches 1/2.

5. An N-independent joint central wedge

Use scaled variables tau=t/N and upsilon=u/N, retaining j as its actual integer channel. Consider the convex domain

W={ (tau,upsilon) in C^2:
         1/2<Re tau<1,
         |2Re upsilon-Re tau|<1/2 }.                         (17)

For every admissible N and j,

c=-cos(pi(tau+1/N)),
z=exp(pi i(tau-2upsilon)).                                    (18)

The first has positive real part on (17): for N>=2 its cosine argument has real part strictly between pi/2 and 3pi/2. The second has positive real part by the second inequality. Also D_j(Q^t) has no zero on W. Such a zero would require tau=r/N plus an integer, with 0<=r<=j-1<N/2, contrary to 1/2<Re tau<1. Consequently the exact original finite function (7) is holomorphic and nowhere zero throughout W, simultaneously in tau and upsilon. The zero channel j=0 is the explicit nonzero exponential and needs no matrix.

On every compact subset of W the quantities Re c, Re z and the separation from the denominator zeros have positive lower bounds uniformly for sufficiently large N. Equations (7), (16), and the product formula for D_j show that both first logarithmic derivatives of N^(-1)log fhat are uniformly bounded. Higher derivatives on smaller compacts follow from Cauchy’s formula. Since W is convex, a logarithm is fixed by one marked base value, and normalized logarithmic ratios are locally bounded uniformly in N and j.

There is a convenient exact phase normalization at a REAL center upsilon=tau/2 with 1/2<tau<1, even when t=N tau is noninteger. Here c>0 and R_j(1;c)>0. Writing each D_j factor as 2i q^(t+r)sin((t-r)theta), and using [2j choose j]_Q=q(j2) times a positive sine-binomial, gives

Arg fhat_j(t/2,t/2)=pi[d(t-j)+j^2]/(2N).                       (19)

This is a specified continuous argument, rather than its value modulo 2pi. Thus the joint continuation carries the same marked center phase as the original real-degree corridor.

6. What this removes, and what it leaves

The pair degree is now genuinely complex in an exact source-defined zero-free domain. Neither integer sampling nor a formal WKB solution is used to declare that continuation. The logarithmic estimates are uniform at the folded endpoint and can support a subsequent normal family or finite-shift argument for a joint selected action.

The present theorem does not assert a joint amplitude asymptotic, a globally admissible frame for an arbitrary plat, or intersection of its original cycle with the geometric critical point. In particular W is a central wedge, not the whole fixed-t magnetic strip, and simultaneous membership of all source coupons in W is an additional global question. These distinctions remain even though (14) is an exact algebraic identity.
