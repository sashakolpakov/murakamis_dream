Positive boundary pressures for Conway product trees

A Conway tangle is built by stacking ordered two-string tangles and applying quarter-turns. Group consecutive products that have no turn between them into a single product junction. A connected region with no crossing-circle operator is then a tree of product junctions; its internal edges are the physical quarter-turns. This note studies the positive action obtained from the complete Newton contraction of such a region. The underlying ordered constructions and their strict angle domains are defined in the strict-tree theorem.

The results compare entire coupled boundary pressures. Deleting a turn and fusing its two product junctions cannot increase the complete positive maximum. Several prescribed high boundary labels can also be moved simultaneously to one half without decreasing the pressure which bounds them. An exact half-label identity then reassembles whole junctions with several high incidences. These comparisons are used by the general component estimate and the Fourier reflection bound.

The comparison trees need not define the same link as the starting diagram. Their positive maxima are bounded by the starting action. The marked representation, filling curves and final knot-complement volume remain those of the starting construction.

2. Strict resolutions and uniqueness of the positive maximum

2.1. A prescribed association has the same strict output interval

For positive input labels a1,…,aka_1,\ldots,a_k, the exact product interval is ai+aj<1(i≠j),maxiai<b<min⁡(1,∑iai). a_i+a_j<1\quad(i\ne j),\qquad \max_i a_i<b<\min\left(1,\sum_i a_i\right). \tag{5} These conditions are necessary and sufficient for strict intermediate gates at output bb, in every prescribed binary association. They are the fixed-label form of the ordered product lemma, Section 2. Here is its constructive proof.

For a child set SS, put LS=max⁡i∈SaiL_S=\max_{i\in S}a_i and US=min⁡(1,∑i∈Sai)U_S=\min(1,\sum_{i\in S}a_i). A composite child has available outputs (LS,US)(L_S,U_S), by induction; a singleton has its fixed label. At a parent with target bb, restrict both child values below bb. Their lower sum is less than one by the pair condition, and their upper sum exceeds bb by the total condition. Choose their sum in (b,1)(b,1). If both are singletons, their fixed sum is already in that interval. The two inputs are less than bb, so the parent gate is strict. Repeat down the fixed binary tree.

Conversely, each gate output exceeds both inputs and is less than their sum. The gate at which any two original inputs first meet also forces their pair sum below one. These observations prove the necessity in (5). Exposing the last incidence of a whole junction makes its ordinary output b=1−aDb=1-a_D; (5) then becomes precisely (4). The closed statement follows either from the same finite interval rules or by taking limits. No hidden covariant edge is treated as a physical equal-label edge in this argument.

Assigning 3/83/8 to all physical edges and ordinary boundaries satisfies (4) at every unmarked hub. It gives a strict witness after the hidden unturned labels are constructed by (5). If at most one boundary at each hub is fixed at half, assign 3/83/8 to all other physical labels. A marked hub then has largest pair sum 7/87/8 and total at least 5/45/4; an unmarked hub has largest pair sum 3/43/4 and total at least 9/89/8. The same construction gives a strict witness. These margins persist when all fixed half labels are changed slightly and independently.

8. Reassembling whole junctions with several high incidences

This final comparison is useful when an absolute tensor bound has the following local action: each low incidence contributes g(x)g(x), and each high incidence contributes zero. For the moving Newton tensor this follows from the high basis polynomial’s polynomial full spectral-jet norm, the low norm rate gg, and finite-degree Chebyshev folding. The source bounds, Section 3.4, retain those full norms and show that trace extraction costs only a polynomial factor. No same-index moving/complete comparison is assumed.

Choose a nonempty set of pairwise nonadjacent whole junctions, each with at least two high incidences. Bound them by the local action just described. All other junctions must already have their complete positive tensors or a justified positive majorant at the same labels. In particular an uncontrolled moving junction with exactly one high incidence cannot be left in these old regions. Actual equal-color moving junctions are nonadjacent across physical turns, but constant junctions need not alternate with them globally.

Remove the selected junctions. A retained complete hub has at most one strictly high incidence, since its complete pairwise support forbids two indices whose sum exceeds dd. At every high interface to a removed junction mark its adjoining old-region boundary. Mark an original external high input as well, retaining its constant cjc_j and its zero variable cone action.
