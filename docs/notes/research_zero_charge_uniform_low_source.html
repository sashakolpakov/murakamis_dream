The zero-charge transition of the original low Newton source

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The original all-low zero-power source has a uniform nonzero formula down to the first nonzero lattice charge. Its endpoint prefactor is the product of the charges. This comes from the antisymmetric Fourier differences in the full Hermite trace, not from replacing those differences by Fourier coefficients. The proof also allows some charges to stay in the interior while others tend to zero.

The archived regular low-source theorem gives the local edge symbol and the interior Gaussian. The additional points here are compact control on partially zero-charge faces, a fixed gap for higher aliases without a positive lower bound on the heights, and analytic division of the principal Fourier remainder. The interpolants used for the last step agree with the actual source only on its lattice; they do not assert an off-lattice cosine initializer.

2. Geometry on all partially zero faces

The convex potential source proves continuity and convexity of VV, its regular positive Hessian, and V(h,g)≥(h+g)/2V(h,g)\ge(h+g)/2. Thus the functional in (5) is uniformly coercive on the closed cube: ℒ𝝌(𝑯)≥∑v(dv/2−χv)Hv≥(3/2−c*)∑vHv.(11) \mathcal L_{\boldsymbol\chi}(\mathbf H) \ge\sum_v(d_v/2-\chi_v)H_v \ge(3/2-c_*)\sum_vH_v. \qquad\text{(11)} If χv=0\chi_v=0, a minimum has Hv=0H_v=0: every incident potential is strictly increasing in its own positive height, whereas there is no linear reward at that coordinate. The remaining positive coordinates separate into selected components with the zero-height vertices treated as complete ends. Their original degrees are unchanged. The charge-cube theorem applies to each such component. Its only exceptional saturated component requires two charges equal to one, which is excluded by c*<1c_*<1. Therefore every edge is regular, including edges between two zero-height vertices.

The minimizer is unique: the positive components have their unique minima, and the other heights have just been forced to zero. Uniform coercivity and uniqueness imply continuity on the closed cube by the convergent-subsequence argument for minimizers. Compactness now gives a uniform strict regularity margin and uniform interior wire indices 0<xe<1/20<x_e<1/2.

On this compact regular set the edge potential extends analytically and evenly in each signed height. Its Hessian is positive definite after assembly, including at zero heights. The analytic implicit function theorem therefore gives the continued minimizing branch near every charge vector on the closed cube. The branches agree on their real overlaps and hence on sufficiently small complex overlaps. Reflection of one charge changes the sign of its own height and leaves the other heights unchanged. In particular Hv=0⇔χv=0,Hv/χv extends to a positive analytic unit.(12) H_v=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \chi_v=0, \qquad H_v/\chi_v\text{ extends to a positive analytic unit.} \qquad\text{(12)} For the last assertion at a zero coordinate, the off-diagonal entries in its Hessian row vanish by separate evenness. Its positive diagonal entry gives ∂Hv/∂χv>0\partial H_v/\partial\chi_v>0.

5. The transition and its quantitative scope

At 𝝌=𝟎\boldsymbol\chi=\mathbf0, every height is zero, every wire maximum is xe=1/4x_e=1/4, and direct differentiation of (25) gives Qxx=−8π,ae=1/4,K=diag⁡(dv/(2π)),Hv/χv=2π/dv.(30) Q_{xx}=-8\pi,\quad a_e=1/4,\quad K=\operatorname{diag}(d_v/(2\pi)),\quad H_v/\chi_v=2\pi/d_v. \qquad\text{(30)} Substitution in (14) proves (9). Since ∂χvM=−Hv\partial_{\chi_v}M=-H_v and MM is separately even, M(𝝌)=eM∞−π∑vχv2/dv+O(|𝝌|4),β(𝝌,𝟎)=β(𝟎,𝟎)(1+O(|𝝌|2)).(31) M(\boldsymbol\chi)=eM_\infty -\pi\sum_v\chi_v^2/d_v+O(|\boldsymbol\chi|^4), \qquad \beta(\boldsymbol\chi,\mathbf0)=\beta(\mathbf0,\mathbf0) (1+O(|\boldsymbol\chi|^2)). \qquad\text{(31)} Equations (8) and (31) give (10), including its uniform error on the stated N\sqrt N scale. For mixed faces, (8) instead retains the exact action of the remaining interior charges and the analytic coefficient (14); no simultaneous smallness of those charges is needed.

The nonzero lattice qualification is essential. At Jv=0J_v=0 the principal difference in that coordinate is exactly zero. Moreover 𝒞N,0=η1\mathcal C_{N,0}=\eta\,1 in the full physical algebra: its values are η\eta and all double-node normals vanish. Thus the actual zero-charge trace in that coordinate is η\eta times the uncharged trace, supplied by the remaining aliases. Formula (8) is not asserted at that exact endpoint for the full low source.

On the whole nonzero lower half-charge block, combine (8) with the adopted uniform high-sector gap to obtain the same formula for the full original zero-power source, with unchanged coefficient and ordinary boundary derivatives. On larger charge sets this additional step requires the corresponding actual high-sector estimate; none is inferred from the low calculation alone.

The quantitative error proved here is relative O(N−1)O(N^{-1}), with the explicitly retained factor ∏χv\prod\chi_v. Repeating the local factorial and Gaussian expansions to any fixed order and the same odd division gives the corresponding fixed-order version whenever those fixed-order symbols are retained. No uniform optimal-truncation or exponentially accurate sampling theorem is claimed. In particular, this relative estimate alone does not meet an exponentially amplified finite-reconstruction error budget. Nor does it supply a common off-lattice complex-charge initializer, a heat-path coverage theorem, or a nonzero coefficient after arbitrary further fillings.
