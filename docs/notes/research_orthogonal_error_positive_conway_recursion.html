A positive matrix recursion for the original Conway source

22 November 2026. Research note. Frozen manuscripts and earlier final notes remain unchanged.

The full Conway source has a positive representation which requires no minimization. At each proper vertex of its expression tree, add a small orthogonal error space. Contract the original maps and their adjoints, keeping their complete Gram matrices. The resulting scalar differs from the squared original invariant by a prescribed, uniformly bounded additive error.

This construction uses the actual closing functional and the full repeated-root boundary algebra. It gives local positive-matrix lower and upper certificates. Their hyperbolic-volume rate is not evaluated here.

1. The normalized original tree

Use the complete normalized compiler in the convex source theorem. There is a fixed finite rooted expression tree. Its proper vertices are leaves, unary linear maps, or binary complex-bilinear maps. The root is the prescribed original complex-linear closing row.

At level N≥2N\ge2, each proper boundary space is the entire HN=ℂ[ζ]/(2TN(ζ/2)+2)≅ℂN,(1) H_N=\mathbb C[\zeta]/(2T_N(\zeta/2)+2)\cong\mathbb C^N, \qquad\text{(1)} with its declared Newton coefficient Hermitian metric. Here TNT_N is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. Value and derivative directions at repeated roots both remain in this space. All adjoints below are in this fixed metric.

Let bv=16max⁡(1,∥Tv∥HS)b_v=16\max(1,\|T_v\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}) be the known local budgets, including the root budget, and put BN=∏vbv,σ=116.(2) B_N=\prod_v b_v,\qquad \sigma=\frac1{16}. \qquad\text{(2)} The coefficients of every local tensor TvT_v are prescribed by the original construction. In particular BN≥1B_N\ge1 is known without evaluating the closed invariant. After dividing each tensor by its own budget, write its leaf vector as ava_v, its unary map as AvA_v, its bilinear map as MvM_v, and its original closing row as LL. Each has norm at most σ\sigma; for a bilinear map this means ∥Mv(u,w)∥≤σ∥u∥∥w∥.(3) \|M_v(u,w)\|\le\sigma\|u\|\,\|w\|. \qquad\text{(3)} The normalized true packets, also denoted ava_v, obey av=Avacorav=Mv(al,ar),∥av∥≤σ,Latop=KN/BN.(4) a_v=A_va_c\quad\text{or}\quad a_v=M_v(a_l,a_r),\qquad \|a_v\|\le\sigma,\qquad La_{\mathrm{ top}}=K_N/B_N. \qquad\text{(4)} At a leaf ava_v is prescribed; at other vertices (4) defines it. The invariant KNK_N is the original reduced Kashaev invariant with the recorded marking, phases and framing normalization. It is not an input to the construction below.

An expression is a literal tree: distinct child occurrences have disjoint sets of descendants. Repeated equal-looking subexpressions are kept as separate occurrences.

For a bilinear map, the same letter MvM_v denotes its linearization from HN⊗HNH_N\otimes H_N to HNH_N. For Hermitian matrices, U≼VU\preceq V means that V−UV-U is positive semidefinite.

2. The deterministic matrix recursion

For any real ε>0\varepsilon>0, define matrices from the leaves upward: Sv=avav*+εI,va leaf,Sv=AvScAv*+εI,vunary,Sv=Mv(Sl⊗Sr)Mv*+εI,vbinary.(5) \begin{array}{ll} S_v=a_va_v^*+\varepsilon I, &v\ \text{a leaf},\\[2mm] S_v=A_vS_cA_v^*+\varepsilon I, &v\ \text{unary},\\[2mm] S_v=M_v(S_l\otimes S_r)M_v^*+\varepsilon I, &v\ \text{binary}. \end{array} \qquad\text{(5)} All matrices in (5) are NN-by-NN and strictly positive definite. The binary operation is positive and order preserving in either matrix argument with the other held positive. Equivalently, one applies the positive linear map U↦MvUMv*U\mapsto M_vUM_v^* to the tensor-product input. Joint linearity in the two matrices is not asserted.

Retain the actual root row and set FN(ε)=BN2LStop(ε)L*.(6) F_N(\varepsilon)=B_N^2 L S_{\mathrm{ top}}(\varepsilon)L^*. \qquad\text{(6)} Equations (2), (5), and (6) prescribe this number entirely by local source data. No true internal packet from (4), except the prescribed leaf vectors, is needed to evaluate (5).

Theorem. Fix 0<θ≤10<\theta\le1, and choose εN=θNBN2,PN=1+FN(εN).(7) \varepsilon_N=\frac{\theta}{N B_N^2}, \qquad P_N=1+F_N(\varepsilon_N). \qquad\text{(7)} For every level and every such normalized tree, 0≤FN(εN)−|KN|2≤θ128,1+|KN|2≤PN≤1+|KN|2+θ128.(8) \boxed{\qquad 0\le F_N(\varepsilon_N)-|K_N|^2\le\frac{\theta}{128}, \qquad 1+|K_N|^2\le P_N\le1+|K_N|^2+\frac{\theta}{128}. \qquad} \qquad\text{(8)} The bounds include exact zeros. 3. Orthogonality and the error bound

Here is a finite Fourier interpretation and proof. At each proper vertex take an independent variable ωv\omega_v, uniform on ℤ/(N+1)\mathbb Z/(N+1). Let χj(ω)=exp⁡(2πijω/(N+1))\chi_j(\omega)=\exp(2\pi i j\omega/(N+1)), and choose an orthonormal basis e1,…,eNe_1,\ldots,e_N of HNH_N. Define ξv=ε∑j=1Nχj(ωv)ej.(9) \xi_v=\sqrt{\varepsilon} \sum_{j=1}^N\chi_j(\omega_v)e_j. \qquad\text{(9)} Character orthogonality gives 𝔼ξv=0,𝔼(ξvξv*)=εI,𝔼∥ξv∥2=Nε.(10) \mathbb E\xi_v=0,\qquad \mathbb E(\xi_v\xi_v^*)=\varepsilon I,\qquad \mathbb E\|\xi_v\|^2=N\varepsilon. \qquad\text{(10)} Define random packets by the original maps: Xv=av+ξvat a leaf,Xv=AvXc+ξv,Xv=Mv(Xl,Xr)+ξv.(11) X_v=a_v+\xi_v\quad\text{at a leaf},\qquad X_v=A_vX_c+\xi_v,\qquad X_v=M_v(X_l,X_r)+\xi_v. \qquad\text{(11)} Child subtrees are independent, and the fresh error at their parent is independent of both. Induction therefore gives 𝔼Xv=av,𝔼(XvXv*)=Sv.(12) \mathbb E X_v=a_v,\qquad \mathbb E(X_vX_v^*)=S_v. \qquad\text{(12)} For the second identity at a binary vertex, use the exact equality 𝔼[(XlXl*)⊗(XrXr*)]=Sl⊗Sr\mathbb E[(X_lX_l^*)\otimes(X_rX_r^*)]=S_l\otimes S_r. The adjoint in (5) conjugates the entire original map, including its phases. Its entries are not squared separately.

Put Vv=𝔼[(Xv−av)(Xv−av)*]≽0,tv=tr⁡Vv,h=Nε.(13) V_v=\mathbb E[(X_v-a_v)(X_v-a_v)^*]\succeq0,\qquad t_v=\operatorname{tr}V_v,\qquad h=N\varepsilon. \qquad\text{(13)} Thus Sv=avav*+VvS_v=a_va_v^*+V_v. At a binary vertex, writing δc=Xc−ac\delta_c=X_c-a_c, the three propagated fluctuations are Mv(al,δr),Mv(δl,ar),Mv(δl,δr).(14) M_v(a_l,\delta_r),\quad M_v(\delta_l,a_r), \quad M_v(\delta_l,\delta_r). \qquad\text{(14)} Their pairwise L2L^2 inner products vanish: in each cross term one independent centered child fluctuation occurs only once. The fresh error is orthogonal to all three. In matrix form, Vv=Mv(alal*⊗Vr+Vl⊗arar*+Vl⊗Vr)Mv*+εI.(15) \begin{split} V_v={}&M_v\bigl(a_la_l^*\otimes V_r+ V_l\otimes a_ra_r^*+V_l\otimes V_r\bigr)M_v^* +\varepsilon I . \end{split} \qquad\text{(15)} Using (3), (4), independence and (10) consequently gives tv=h,va leaf,tv≤σ2tc+h,vunary,tv≤σ4(tl+tr)+σ2tltr+h,vbinary.(16) \begin{array}{ll} t_v=h,&v\ \text{a leaf},\\ t_v\le\sigma^2t_c+h,&v\ \text{unary},\\ t_v\le\sigma^4(t_l+t_r)+\sigma^2t_lt_r+h, &v\ \text{binary}. \end{array} \qquad\text{(16)} If h≤1h\le1, induction shows tv≤2ht_v\le2h at every vertex. For a unary vertex use 1+2σ2<21+2\sigma^2<2. For a binary vertex, σ4(2h+2h)+σ2(2h)2+h=h(1+4σ4+4σ2h)<2h.(17) \sigma^4(2h+2h)+\sigma^2(2h)^2+h =h(1+4\sigma^4+4\sigma^2h)<2h. \qquad\text{(17)} These estimates do not accumulate a factor for the number of vertices.

At the unchanged original closing, FN(ε)=|KN|2+BN2LVtopL*,0≤BN2LVtopL*≤2σ2BN2Nε.(18) F_N(\varepsilon)=|K_N|^2+B_N^2 L V_{\mathrm{ top}}L^*,\qquad 0\le B_N^2 L V_{\mathrm{ top}}L^* \le2\sigma^2B_N^2N\varepsilon. \qquad\text{(18)} The choice (7) has h=θ/BN2≤1h=\theta/B_N^2\le1, and 2σ2=1/1282\sigma^2=1/128. 4. A scalar polynomial of degree bounded by the number of leaves

There is a second fixed-degree simplification. If the original tree has ll leaf occurrences, then FN(ε)=|KN|2+∑j=1lcN,jεj,cN,j≥0.(19) F_N(\varepsilon)=|K_N|^2+ \sum_{j=1}^{l}c_{N,j}\varepsilon^j, \qquad c_{N,j}\ge0. \qquad\text{(19)} Indeed each Sv(ε)S_v(\varepsilon) is a matrix polynomial with positive semidefinite coefficient matrices. This holds at a leaf, is preserved by AvSAv*A_v S A_v^*, and is preserved by tensoring the two polynomial matrices and applying Mv(⋅)Mv*M_v(\,\cdot\,)M_v^*. Adding εI\varepsilon I preserves the assertion. The degree is at most one at a leaf, unchanged along a unary corridor, and at most the sum of the child degrees at a binary vertex. The constant coefficient is avav*a_va_v^* by (4). Applying LL proves (19).

More explicitly, an antichain is a set of proper vertices no one of which is an ancestor of another. Let CAC_A be the original normalized scalar expression with the subtrees at the vertices of an antichain AA replaced by free HNH_N inputs. All other subtrees and the original closing remain as prescribed. Then FN(ε)=|KN|2+BN2∑A≠⌀Aantichainε|A|∥CA∥HS2.(20) F_N(\varepsilon)=|K_N|^2+ B_N^2\sum_{\substack{A\ne\varnothing\\A\ {\mathrm{ antichain}}}} \varepsilon^{|A|}\|C_A\|_{\mathrm{ HS}}^2. \qquad\text{(20)} To see this, expand (11) using bilinearity. Choosing a fresh ξv\xi_v replaces its entire subtree, so a surviving term uses an antichain of error vertices. Different antichains are orthogonal: an error variable present in just one term has mean zero. For the same antichain, (10) contracts the full coefficient tensor with its adjoint, giving its Hilbert–Schmidt norm. An antichain has at most ll vertices, since their descendant leaf sets are disjoint. This also proves the degree bound.

The nonnegative coefficients in (19) are squared norms of complete exterior contexts. Signed terms within a context are still assembled before taking its norm. Formula (19) does not make the original scalar state-sum coefficients nonnegative.

Errors only at the original leaves

The internal additions in (5) can also be omitted. Define S̃v=avav*+εI\widetilde S_v=a_va_v^*+\varepsilon I at each original leaf, and propagate by S̃v=AvS̃cAv*,S̃v=Mv(S̃l⊗S̃r)Mv*(20a) \widetilde S_v=A_v\widetilde S_cA_v^*,\qquad \widetilde S_v=M_v(\widetilde S_l\otimes\widetilde S_r)M_v^* \qquad\text{(20a)} at internal vertices. These matrices are positive semidefinite; strict definiteness for an arbitrary unary map is not required. The same independent-error proof applies, with no fresh internal error. Now the covariance trace is at most h=Nε≤1h=N\varepsilon\le1: it equals hh at leaves, is at most σ2h\sigma^2h after a unary map, and at a binary map is at most σ2(2σ2h+h2)≤h\sigma^2(2\sigma^2h+h^2)\le h. Consequently, at the same choice (7), 0≤BN2LS̃topL*−|KN|2≤θ256.(20b) 0\le B_N^2 L\widetilde S_{\mathrm{ top}}L^*-|K_N|^2 \le\frac{\theta}{256}. \qquad\text{(20b)}

Writing ℬE:HN⊗l→HN\mathcal B_E:H_N^{\otimes l}\to H_N for the normalized synthesis map of the original expression, the exact formula is simply S̃top=ℬE[⨂ℓleaf(aℓaℓ*+εI)]ℬE*.(20c) \widetilde S_{\mathrm{ top}} =\mathcal B_E\! \left[\bigotimes_{\ell\ {\mathrm{ leaf}}} (a_\ell a_\ell^*+\varepsilon I)\right] \mathcal B_E^* . \qquad\text{(20c)} The nonconstant coefficients now correspond to subsets of leaves. Equation (20c) is the usual weighted synthesis covariance; the additional assertion here is its calibrated additive comparison with the same filled source. This version needs orthogonal error labels only at the original leaves. The all-vertex version gives a strict positive floor at every intermediate packet independently of the ranks of the maps.

5. Local matrix certificates

One need not evaluate the exact messages to obtain rigorous bounds. Suppose positive semidefinite matrices Qv−Q_v^- satisfy the following local inequalities, with the same εN\varepsilon_N: Qv−≼avav*+εNI,va leaf,Qv−≼AvQc−Av*+εNI,vunary,Qv−≼Mv(Ql−⊗Qr−)Mv*+εNI,vbinary.(21) \begin{array}{ll} Q_v^-\preceq a_va_v^*+\varepsilon_N I,&v\ \text{a leaf},\\ Q_v^-\preceq A_vQ_c^-A_v^*+\varepsilon_N I,&v\ \text{unary},\\ Q_v^-\preceq M_v(Q_l^-\otimes Q_r^-)M_v^*+\varepsilon_N I, &v\ \text{binary}. \end{array} \qquad\text{(21)} Likewise let positive semidefinite Qv+Q_v^+ satisfy the reversed inequalities. Then Qv−≼Sv≼Qv+(22) Q_v^-\preceq S_v\preceq Q_v^+ \qquad\text{(22)} by induction. The tensor-product step follows from Sl⊗Sr−Ql−⊗Qr−=(Sl−Ql−)⊗Sr+Ql−⊗(Sr−Qr−)≽0,(23) S_l\otimes S_r-Q_l^-\otimes Q_r^- =(S_l-Q_l^-)\otimes S_r+ Q_l^-\otimes(S_r-Q_r^-)\succeq0, \qquad\text{(23)} and the analogous identity for the upper comparison. Combining (8), (18), and (22) gives max⁡(0,BN2LQtop−L*−θ128)≤|KN|2≤BN2LQtop+L*.(24) \boxed{\quad \max\!\left(0,B_N^2LQ_{\mathrm{ top}}^-L^*-\frac{\theta}{128}\right) \le |K_N|^2\le B_N^2LQ_{\mathrm{ top}}^+L^*. \quad} \qquad\text{(24)} The matrices can have off-diagonal entries. Replacing them by their diagonals is not justified by these inequalities.

For a hyperbolic knot complement of volume V>0V>0, one sufficient geometric target is a family of such messages for which, eventually, the two root observations are positive and liminfN→∞πNlog⁡(BN2LQtop−L*)≥V,limsupN→∞πNlog⁡(BN2LQtop+L*)≤V.(25) \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\pi}{N} \log(B_N^2LQ_{\mathrm{ top}}^-L^*)\ge V,\qquad \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\pi}{N} \log(B_N^2LQ_{\mathrm{ top}}^+L^*)\le V. \qquad\text{(25)} The lower observation then grows exponentially; subtracting the fixed constant in (24) does not change its rate. The original volume identity follows by squeezing. No such geometric message family is constructed in this note.

6. Scope and sources

At ε=0\varepsilon=0, (5) is simply the exact rank-one contraction of the original source with its conjugate. The additional result is the explicit strictly positive regularization and its uniform unnormalized error bound. It avoids a variational solve but retains the full matrix size, precision requirements and coherent phases. The chosen εN\varepsilon_N can be exponentially small because the known budget BNB_N can grow exponentially.

The two sides of (8) imply, for every V>0V>0, πNlog⁡PN→V⇔2πNlog⁡|KN|→V.(26) \frac{\pi}{N}\log P_N\longrightarrow V \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N|\longrightarrow V. \qquad\text{(26)} Indeed PNP_N differs from 1+|KN|21+|K_N|^2 by a bounded additive quantity. A positive limiting rate implies eventual nonvanishing of KNK_N. This is a finite-level comparison, not a proof of either limit.

Every fixed short partial filling can be inserted into its actual leaf data, or contracted as a full open subtree before recompiling. The theorem therefore has no restriction on short words. The finite-exception program still needs (25), or the equivalent geometric rate of (5), for every required fixed short partial filling and sufficiently long remaining hyperbolic completions. Local saddle existence and strict angle-volume concavity alone do not supply the full-matrix inequalities (21).
