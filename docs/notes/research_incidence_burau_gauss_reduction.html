The integral clock coupling and its finite Gaussian transform

Written deduction, 15 November 2026. We use the complete original singleton incidence sum, including every closing degree and pivotal phase. For a braid whose closure is a knot, its quadratic phase has an integral normal form governed by the Alexander polynomial at −1-1. This gives an exact Fourier reduction at every level. No coprimality assumption, numerical calculation, or change of the closing functional is used.

The nonzero Gaussian coefficients obtained below have polynomial size. This controls those coefficients, not the coherent sum in which they occur. In particular it supplies no volume lower bound.

1. The original lattice and its midpoint map

Let the signed braid have bb heads and cc crossings, with knot closure KK. Put d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and Sa=∏r=1a2sin⁡(πr/N)S_a=\prod_{r=1}^a2\sin(\pi r/N), with S0=1S_0=1. Use the physical signs σj\sigma_j of the joint-phase source. At crossing jj, the overstrand goes from uj+vju_j+v_j to vjv_j, and the understrand goes from JjJ_j to Jj+ujJ_j+u_j. Its four incidences are (d−uj−vj,uj−hj+vj,hj−vj,vj).(1) (d-u_j-v_j,\ u_j-h_j+v_j,\ h_j-v_j,\ v_j). \qquad\text{(1)} The closing row on head ii is (d−ai,ai)(d-a_i,a_i). The linear wire conservation equations are precisely those in the joint-phase note, Section 2. Let PP be their integral solution lattice in the physical variables u,v,au,v,a, without the inequalities. Every JjJ_j is its actual integral prefix function. Define ℳ:P→ℤc,ℳj(p)=uj+2Jj.(2) \mathcal M:P\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^c, \qquad \mathcal M_j(p)=u_j+2J_j. \qquad\text{(2)} Thus ℳj\mathcal M_j is the sum of the two understrand degrees.

The homogeneous incidence lattice is integrally isomorphic to P⊕ℤcP\oplus\mathbb Z^c, the second factor consisting of the clocks hjh_j. Indeed (1) and the closing pairs reconstruct all incidence variations; conversely vj=nj3v_j=n_{j3}, hj=nj2+nj3h_j=n_{j2}+n_{j3}, uj=nj1+nj2u_j=n_{j1}+n_{j2}, and the closing indices recover the inverse. There is no divisibility condition in this change of coordinates.

The affine source fiber has the integral base point with all physical degrees and clocks zero. Its first incidence in each event is dd, and all its other incidences are zero. Consequently every source state is this base point plus a unique element of P⊕ℤcP\oplus\mathbb Z^c. The inequalities 0≤ne≤d0\le n_e\le d still specify exactly which such elements occur.

The full variable-dependent phase is ΦN(p,h)=∑jσjhjℳj(p)+∑j(N+σj)hj−d∑jσjJj(p)−2∑i=2bai(p).(3) \Phi_N(p,h)= \sum_j\sigma_jh_j\mathcal M_j(p) +\sum_j(N+\sigma_j)h_j -d\sum_j\sigma_jJ_j(p)-2\sum_{i=2}^b a_i(p). \qquad\text{(3)} In particular its quadratic part is a pairing of clocks with physical flows. It has no clock–clock or physical–physical term.

2. An integral exact sequence

Write y∈ℤby\in\mathbb Z^b for the initial head degrees and tj=ℳj(p)t_j=\mathcal M_j(p). At a positive crossing on adjacent heads, the checked output degrees are (t−JI+2J−t)=M+(IJ)+v+t,M+=(0−112),v+=(1−1).(4) \binom{t-J}{I+2J-t} =M_+\binom I J+v_+t, \quad M_+=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}, \quad v_+=\binom1{-1}. \qquad\text{(4)} For a negative crossing the input order is (J,I)(J,I), and the output is (I+2J−tt−J)=M−(JI)+v−t,M−=(21−10)=M+−1,v−=−v+.(5) \binom{I+2J-t}{t-J} =M_-\binom J I+v_-t, \quad M_-=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}=M_+^{-1}, \quad v_-=-v_+. \qquad\text{(5)} These are direct consequences of transfer conservation and u=t−2Ju=t-2J; they do not impose a new coloring on the source. Embed these matrices and vectors at each crossing’s two heads and put B=Mc⋯M1,Dj=Mc⋯Mj+1vj.(6) B=M_c\cdots M_1,\qquad D_j=M_c\cdots M_{j+1}v_j. \qquad\text{(6)} Every matrix preserves the sum of the head degrees. Write ℤ0b={y:∑iyi=0}\mathbb Z_0^b=\{y:\sum_i y_i=0\}. Closing the braid gives the integral identification P={(y,t)∈ℤb⊕ℤc:(I−B)y=Dt},ℳ(y,t)=t.(7) P=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb Z^b\oplus\mathbb Z^c: (I-B)y=Dt\}, \qquad \mathcal M(y,t)=t. \qquad\text{(7)} The inverse follows the crossings in order, using u=t−2Ju=t-2J.

The columns of DD span ℤ0b\mathbb Z_0^b. To check this integrally, im⁡(Mj−I)⊂ℤvj\operatorname{im}(M_j-I)\subset\mathbb Z v_j. It follows that Dj−vjD_j-v_j is an integral combination of the later raw vectors vj+1,…,vcv_{j+1},\ldots,v_c. Descending induction therefore proves that the propagated and raw columns have the same integral span. The raw vectors are signed adjacent simple roots. The graph of the adjacent heads occurring in a knot braid is connected: otherwise its braid permutation would preserve two nonempty sets, whereas that permutation is one cycle. The simple roots on this connected graph span ℤ0b\mathbb Z_0^b.

Consequently (7) gives the exact sequence 0→ker⁡(I−B)→P→ℳℤc→t↦[Dt]ℤ0b/(I−B)ℤb→0.(8) 0\longrightarrow\ker(I-B)\longrightarrow P \xrightarrow{\mathcal M}\mathbb Z^c \xrightarrow{\ t\mapsto[Dt]\ } \mathbb Z_0^b/(I-B)\mathbb Z^b\longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(8)} This also proves rank⁡P=c+1\operatorname{rank}P=c+1: the map (I−B,−D):ℤb+c→ℤ0b(I-B,-D):\mathbb Z^{b+c}\to\mathbb Z_0^b is surjective.

3. The Alexander group in the cokernel

Conjugate the head coordinates by E=diag⁡(1,−1,1,−1,…)E=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots). The local matrices in (4)–(5) become, respectively, the inverse and the value at t=−1t=-1 of the unreduced Burau block T(t)=(1−tt10).(9) T(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1-t&t\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(9)} Thus EBE−1EBE^{-1} is the Burau matrix at −1-1 for the braid with crossing signs reversed, using the same product convention. Its closure is the mirror of KK; this does not change |ΔK(−1)||\Delta_K(-1)|.

Here is the minor argument, including the issue at even braid width. Over R=ℤ[t,t−1]R=\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}], the unreduced braid matrix ℬ(t)\mathcal B(t) fixes the column 𝟏\mathbf1 and the row v(t)=(1,t,…,tb−1)v(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^{b-1}). At t=1t=1 it is the permutation of a single cycle, so I−ℬ(t)I-\mathcal B(t) has rank b−1b-1 over ℚ(t)\mathbb Q(t). Its adjugate is therefore adj⁡(I−ℬ(t))=f(t)𝟏v(t),f(t)∈R.(10) \operatorname{adj}(I-\mathcal B(t)) =f(t)\,\mathbf1\,v(t),\qquad f(t)\in R. \qquad\text{(10)} The coefficient is Laurent polynomial because the first entry of both null vectors is one. All deleted minors are consequently f(t)f(t) times a signed Laurent unit.

To identify ff, use the braid presentation ⟨x1,…,xb:β(xi)=xi⟩\langle x_1,\ldots,x_b:\beta(x_i)=x_i\rangle of the mirror knot group. The Artin automorphism preserves x1⋯xbx_1\cdots x_b; one of these relations follows from the other b−1b-1. Abelian Fox differentiation gives ℬ(t)−I\mathcal B(t)-I, up to the transpose and Laurent basis changes fixed by the action convention. The boundary map of the infinite cyclic cover is (t−1)𝟏T(t-1)\mathbf1^T. Its kernel is free with basis ei−ebe_i-e_b, i<bi<b. Expressing the remaining relation boundaries in this basis amounts to deleting one generator coordinate from the Fox matrix. The resulting square matrix presents the Alexander module; its determinant is the Alexander polynomial up to a Laurent unit. This is one of the minors in (10). Hence f(t)f(t) is ΔK(t)\Delta_K(t), with the mirror convention, up to such a unit.

At t=1t=1 a deleted minor of the single-cycle permutation matrix has value ±1\pm1. Thus f(−1)≡f(1)(mod⁡2)f(-1)\equiv f(1)\pmod2 is odd and nonzero. In particular I−BI-B has rank b−1b-1. Its right kernel is the primitive alternating vector ε=(1,−1,…)T\varepsilon=(1,-1,\ldots)^T, and its left kernel is 𝟏T\mathbf1^T. Deleting one output coordinate identifies ℤ0b\mathbb Z_0^b with ℤb−1\mathbb Z^{b-1}. The maximal minors of the resulting (b−1)(b-1)-by-bb matrix are precisely the specialized cofactors; each has absolute value |f(−1)||f(-1)|. Their greatest common divisor is the index of its image. Therefore |ℤ0b/(I−B)ℤb|=|ΔK(−1)|.(11) \left|\mathbb Z_0^b/(I-B)\mathbb Z^b\right| =|\Delta_K(-1)|. \qquad\text{(11)} No division by 1+t+⋯+tb−11+t+\cdots+t^{b-1} occurs. In particular the argument remains valid when bb is even. This is an integral cokernel statement, rather than a specialization of a rational rank computation.

The finite group, not only its order, has an Alexander description. The column dependence of I−BI-B has coefficients εi=±1\varepsilon_i=\pm1. After deleting one output coordinate, any one input column is therefore an integral combination of the other columns. Deleting it leaves exactly the same image lattice. The resulting square matrix is the specialized Alexander presentation above, up to integral basis changes and possibly a transpose. A square matrix and its transpose have the same integer Smith factors. Consequently, as an abstract finite abelian group, coker⁡ℳ≅𝒜K/(t+1)𝒜K,(11a) \operatorname{coker}\mathcal M\cong \mathcal A_K/(t+1)\mathcal A_K, \qquad\text{(11a)} where 𝒜K\mathcal A_K is the Alexander module. The mirror convention replaces tt by t−1t^{-1}, which has the same specialization at −1-1. This is an abstract isomorphism; no canonical identification with a transposed presentation is asserted.

Combining (8)–(11), the midpoint map has rank cc, a primitive rank-one kernel, and a finite cokernel of odd order. Let its nonzero Smith factors be s1∣⋯∣scs_1\mid\cdots\mid s_c, all positive. Then ∏j=1csj=|ΔK(−1)|.(12) \prod_{j=1}^c s_j=|\Delta_K(-1)|. \qquad\text{(12)} Their nonunit members are independent of the braid presentation by (11a); the number of unit factors changes with the number of crossings. For b=1,c=0b=1,c=0, the braid is empty. The same assertions use the empty product one, P=ℤP=\mathbb Z, and the zero midpoint map.

4. Integral hyperbolic form of the actual phase

The kernel generator with initial heads ε\varepsilon has a1=1a_1=1. Therefore P=ker⁡(a1)⊕ℤεP=\ker(a_1)\oplus\mathbb Z\varepsilon, and the image of ℳ|ker⁡(a1)\mathcal M|_{\ker(a_1)} is the full image of ℳ\mathcal M. Choose its Smith bases on this complement. Changing the physical basis and making the dual integral change in the clocks, with their signs σj\sigma_j absorbed, transforms the quadratic term in (3) into ∑jsjxjyj\sum_j s_j x_jy_j. Choose the remaining physical coordinate zz to be the primitive kernel direction with initial heads ε\varepsilon; with this choice, z=a1z=a_1 exactly. All these changes are unimodular on the integral incidence lattice. Thus the complete phase, including its linear terms, is exactly ΦN=∑j=1c(sjxjyj+αj,Nxj+βj,Nyj)+γNz.(13) \Phi_N=\sum_{j=1}^c (s_jx_jy_j+\alpha_{j,N}x_j+\beta_{j,N}y_j) +\gamma_Nz. \qquad\text{(13)} The displayed coefficients are the transformed integer linear coefficients of (3), and are affine functions of NN. No phase has been absorbed into an unspecified normalization. Equivalently, the symmetric quadratic matrix is integrally congruent to ⨁j(0sjsj0)⊕(0)\bigoplus_j\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&s_j\\s_j&0\end{smallmatrix}\right) \oplus(0). Its rank is 2c2c.

The linear term along the free direction can be read without finding the other Smith coordinates: γN=−d∑jσjJj(ε)−2∑i=2bεi.(14) \gamma_N=-d\sum_j\sigma_jJ_j(\varepsilon) -2\sum_{i=2}^b\varepsilon_i. \qquad\text{(14)} Indeed the zero-midpoint transport fixes ε\varepsilon at every horizontal slice, and its clocks do not vary. If crossing jj uses heads ij,ij+1i_j,i_j+1, then σjJj(ε)=−(−1)ij−1\sigma_jJ_j(\varepsilon)=-(-1)^{i_j-1}. Consequently γN=(N−1)∑j(−1)ij−1+2𝟏beven.(15) \gamma_N=(N-1)\sum_j(-1)^{i_j-1} +2\mathbf1_{b\ {\mathrm{ even}}}. \qquad\text{(15)} This is one explicit place where the prescribed closing pivots enter the Fourier support. Dropping them changes the finite transform.

5. The complete finite Gaussian transform

Put m=2Nm=2N, em(t)=e2πit/me_m(t)=e^{2\pi it/m}, and Gm=(ℤ/m)2c+1G_m=(\mathbb Z/m)^{2c+1}, in the coordinates (x,y,z)(x,y,z) of (13). This phase is periodic modulo mm. Define its normalized Fourier coefficients by gN(k)=m−(2c+1)∑w∈Gmem(ΦN(w)−k⋅w).(16) g_N(k)=m^{-(2c+1)}\sum_{w\in G_m} e_m(\Phi_N(w)-k\cdot w). \qquad\text{(16)} For rj=gcd⁡(m,sj)=gcd⁡(N,sj)r_j=\gcd(m,s_j)=\gcd(N,s_j), these coefficients vanish unless kz≡γN(mod⁡m),kxj≡αj,N(mod⁡rj),kyj≡βj,N(mod⁡rj).(17) k_z\equiv\gamma_N\pmod m,\qquad k_{x_j}\equiv\alpha_{j,N}\pmod{r_j},\qquad k_{y_j}\equiv\beta_{j,N}\pmod{r_j}. \qquad\text{(17)} On exactly this affine set, choose any solution yj*y_j^* of sjyj*≡kxj−αj,N(mod⁡m)s_jy_j^*\equiv k_{x_j}-\alpha_{j,N}\pmod m. Then the exact coefficient is gN(k)=∏j=1crjmem(∑j(βj,N−kyj)yj*).(18) g_N(k)=\prod_{j=1}^c\frac{r_j}{m}\, e_m\!\left(\sum_j(\beta_{j,N}-k_{y_j})y_j^*\right). \qquad\text{(18)} The expression is independent of the choices of yj*y_j^*.

For proof, the sum in zz gives the first condition in (17). Summing in xjx_j gives zero unless sjyj+αj,N−kxj≡0s_jy_j+\alpha_{j,N}-k_{x_j}\equiv0. That congruence has rjr_j solutions when the second condition holds. They differ by m/rjm/r_j; summing their remaining characters gives zero unless the third condition holds, and otherwise gives (rj/m)em((βj,N−kyj)yj*)(r_j/m)e_m((\beta_{j,N}-k_{y_j})y_j^*). Multiplication proves (18). This argument also covers even and composite NN.

In particular, every admitted coefficient is nonzero, with |gN(k)|=∏jgcd⁡(N,sj)(2N)c,(2N)−c≤|gN(k)|≤|ΔK(−1)|(2N)−c.(19) |g_N(k)|=\frac{\prod_j\gcd(N,s_j)}{(2N)^c},\qquad (2N)^{-c}\le |g_N(k)|\le |\Delta_K(-1)|(2N)^{-c}. \qquad\text{(19)} There are m2c/∏jrj2m^{2c}/\prod_j r_j^2 admitted frequencies. No assertion of positivity of its phase is made.

6. Exact reduction of the original closing evaluation

Let 𝒲N⊂ℤ2c+1\mathcal W_N\subset\mathbb Z^{2c+1} be the coordinates of the actual feasible incidence states and define aN(w)=∏eSne(w)−1(w∈𝒲N),ZN(k)=∑w∈𝒲NaN(w)em(k⋅w).(20) a_N(w)=\prod_eS_{n_e(w)}^{-1}\quad(w\in\mathcal W_N), \qquad Z_N(k)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_N}a_N(w)e_m(k\cdot w). \qquad\text{(20)} Every aN(w)a_N(w) is positive. Distinct feasible states stay distinct modulo mm: if their coordinate difference were mvm v, their incidence difference would be mm times a nonzero integral vector, whereas each incidence lies between zero and N−1<mN-1<m. Thus zero extension to GmG_m introduces no aliases.

Fourier inversion of (16) now gives the original source exactly: KN=𝒞N∑k satisfying (17)gN(k)ZN(k),𝒞N=χNκ∑jσjηb−1Nc+b−1.(21) \boxed{\quad K_N=\mathcal C_N\sum_{k\text{ satisfying }(17)}g_N(k)Z_N(k), \qquad \mathcal C_N=\chi_N\kappa^{\sum_j\sigma_j} \eta^{b-1}N^{c+b-1}. \quad} \qquad\text{(21)} Here κ=qd2/2\kappa=q^{d^2/2}, η=q−d2\eta=q^{-d^2}, and χN\chi_N are the original factors. The positive value ZN(0)Z_N(0) is the absolute incidence partition whose exponent is the proved concave angle optimization. It is not substituted for the right side of (21). Its Fourier evaluations at other frequencies can have phases and zeros, and the coefficients (18) also have their specified phases.

Removing the free direction by the actual marked cut

The adopted marked-cut theorem gives a further exact reduction. If the incidence sum is weighted by ta1t^{a_1}, its value is FN(𝟏)1+t+⋯+tN−1N.(22) F_N(\mathbf1)\,\frac{1+t+\cdots+t^{N-1}}N. \qquad\text{(22)} This follows from scalarity of the complete pivotal partial trace. In particular, for every 0≤r<N0\le r<N, the sum over all remaining coordinates at a1=ra_1=r equals FN(𝟏)/NF_N(\mathbf1)/N. This is an identity of complete sums; it is not applied to a fixed clock sector or to a multivariable fugacity deformation of the exterior.

Our chosen Smith complement makes a1=za_1=z. For any prescribed integer rr in this range, define the positive slice and its Fourier evaluations by ZNr(kx,ky)=∑(x,y,r)∈𝒲NaN(x,y,r)em(kx⋅x+ky⋅y).(23) Z_N^r(k_x,k_y)= \sum_{(x,y,r)\in\mathcal W_N} a_N(x,y,r)e_m(k_x\cdot x+k_y\cdot y). \qquad\text{(23)} Let gN0(kx,ky)g_N^0(k_x,k_y) be the normalized Fourier transform on (ℤ/m)2c(\mathbb Z/m)^{2c} of (13) restricted to z=0z=0. Its coefficients are exactly (18), subject only to the two congruences for each pair in (17). The radical congruence is absent. At z=rz=r the additional phase is exactly em(γNr)e_m(\gamma_Nr); the pair phase is unchanged. Fourier inversion on this slice, followed by (22), gives KN=N𝒞Nem(γNr)∑kx,kygN0(kx,ky)ZNr(kx,ky).(24) \boxed{\quad K_N=N\mathcal C_N e_m(\gamma_Nr) \sum_{k_x,k_y}g_N^0(k_x,k_y)Z_N^r(k_x,k_y). \quad} \qquad\text{(24)} All original inequalities and every closing pivot remain in this formula. The same injection modulo mm proves that the slice has no aliases. No unknown scalar was divided out, so (24) also holds when the original invariant vanishes. The choice r=rNr=r_N may depend on the level; in particular one may choose a central physical degree instead of the boundary degree zero. No contributing geometric saddle is inferred for such a chosen slice.

The unweighted ZNr(0)Z_N^r(0) is a different positive partition from ZN(0)Z_N(0). For r=0r=0, the condition pins a boundary face and can force further zero coordinates. The number 2c2c is the rank of the ambient integral and Fourier lattice; a feasible slice need not have that dimension. The unsliced angle maximum cannot be assigned to an arbitrary prescribed central or boundary slice.

There is, however, an exact choice preserving that positive exponent. Since ZN(0)=∑r=0N−1ZNr(0)Z_N(0)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}Z_N^r(0), choose rNr_N attaining the largest positive slice. Then ZN(0)N≤ZNrN(0)≤ZN(0).(25) \frac{Z_N(0)}N\le Z_N^{r_N}(0)\le Z_N(0). \qquad\text{(25)} The already proved exponent of ZN(0)Z_N(0), namely the maximum of the concave absolute incidence action, is therefore also the exponent of this adaptively chosen positive slice. The choice uses positive weights alone, not the unknown invariant. It need not be a prescribed central slice or contain the selected complex geometric point. Equation (25) is not a lower bound for the coherent sum in (24). What (24) removes exactly is the permanent free direction of the quadratic phase, using the actual marked cut at any allowed degree.

The older feedback theorem already gives a finite Fourier realization of the understrand coupling. The additional content here is its integral normal form on the complete conserved-flow lattice, the Alexander-controlled torsion, the explicit closing character, and the nonzero polynomial size of every admitted Gaussian coefficient. Smith reduction, the Burau–Alexander relation, and finite Fourier orthogonality themselves are classical methods, not novelty claims. For coverage of the Burau–Alexander relation see Anthony Conway, Burau maps and twisted Alexander polynomials. Its abstract was checked for coverage; none of its proofs is a premise of the integral argument above.

The result applies to every fixed knot braid, hence also to a braid obtained after any fixed short partial filling that gives a knot. It proves no uniform asymptotic stability under that operation. The closed Markov identity in the direct source shows why the phases in (21) remain necessary: complete nonzero-clock sectors can cancel exactly even though their absolute weights have positive exponential rate. The Gaussian factors cannot be exponentially small on their support; the remaining question is the noncancellation of their prescribed coherent contraction. No nonzero geometric coefficient or volume limit is inferred here.
