The geometric longitude for strict Conway trees

A product junction without a crossing circle can be treated geometrically by inserting a circle around a zero-crossing two-strand box. Filling that new circle along its meridian removes it and restores the same ordered tangle. The new circle supplies an angular variable whose stationary elimination recovers the original product action exactly.

This note uses that construction to identify the full preferred longitude selected by the strict-tree action, including junctions of arbitrary valence and adjacency. The proof keeps the original peripheral paths and also covers the possible physical edge label x=1/2x=1/2, where an individual edge quadratic has a double root.

The theorem and its geometric conventions

Use a fixed strict ordered Conway construction from the strict-tree theorem. The elementary twist pieces are joined by their prescribed ordered products and physical quarter-turns, then closed by the original cylindrical matching. After the forced final product is removed, strictness means that the real angle domain contains a point with 0<ai<12,max⁡(u,v)<w<u+v<1 0<a_i<\tfrac12,\qquad \max(u,v)<w<u+v<1 \tag{T1} at each original leaf and each retained binary product. A bare junction is a maximal group of consecutive products that contains no crossing-circle operator. Any binary association used to calculate it is retained only as an internal resolution; it does not create additional physical turns.

Require the original strand pairing to give one knot, counting any circles already closed inside intermediate tangles. Keep all original parity qualifications. In the two-leaf case retain the original pure-leaf qualification; the two-odd pairing is a link and is excluded by the knot test. The construction and the independently signed twist powers nin_i are fixed before the quantum level varies. Assume those powers are sufficiently long for the existing marked strict-tree filling theorem.

A meridian goes once around a strand. The preferred longitude follows the knot once and has zero linking number with it. Holonomy assigns a Möbius transformation to each based closed path in the complement. We use determinant-one matrix lifts; MM is the eigenvalue of the positively oriented main meridian, and LL is the eigenvalue of the preferred longitude on the same line. The source cut selects the other, M−1M^{-1}, eigenline, so LL is the inverse longitude eigenvalue on that selected line.

Let ℰT\mathcal E_T be the original physical quarter-turn edges of the reduced junction tree. On edge ee, xex_e is the normalized polynomial index shared by its two ends after the trace pairing. When that incidence is exposed as the ordinary output of the rooted branch, the output angle is 1−xe1-x_e. The original ordered construction fixes this convention. Put Xe=e2πixeX_e=e^{2\pi ix_e}.

1. The literal insertion

Use the reduced tree of original product junctions, with consecutive unturned products already grouped. A bare vertex has full valence at least three. Its incident edges are the original quarter-turns; a binary resolution used to describe its positive kernel does not add physical edges.

At a bare vertex insert the literal unturned box R0=IR^0=I, at the distinguished position immediately before the original ordered child product. Encircle its two strands by a new crossing circle. Keep every child in its original order. Put ϵv=pv=nv=0 \epsilon_v=p_v=n_v=0 \tag{1} at this new circle. Filling its original primitive meridian removes the auxiliary circle and restores the literal identity box. Thus the filled tangle, its endpoints, its framed main strands and its original peripheral curves are unchanged. No new quarter-turn is inserted. Repeating the operation at all bare vertices produces a literal all-circle tree on the same physical edge set.

The fixed access paths make these ordered equalities precise. They are constructed in section 5; the figure records the collar without choosing additional paths.

For a holonomy representation, the added meridian must be projectively identity: its matrix must be a scalar. An eigenvalue ratio of one would also permit a nontrivial parabolic matrix. Section 2 excludes this ambiguity by exhibiting an auxiliary longitude with distinct eigenvalues.

The original crossing powers, writhe and integer a(D)=−w(D)−∑vnv. a(D)=-w(D)-\sum_v n_v. \tag{2} are unchanged. The added circles have filling parameter zero, so their preferred-longitude surgery correction pvℓv2p_v\ell_v^2 also vanishes.

After the zero-power circles have been inserted, the ordered all-circle cut uses this attachment at every tree edge. The labels fix the page order. The representation comparison below also permits a flat added subdivision; it uses the original strict geometry to identify the knot holonomy.

8. Transfer to presentations with net twists and arbitrary closures

The conclusion is intrinsic to the zero-framed knot with its marked peripheral curves. Suppose the ordered net-twist reduction gives an actual ambient isotopy from the input knot to a strict knot presentation covered by Section 7. The isotopy carries the knot orientation, meridian, preferred zero-linking longitude and cut mark. Correcting any transported ribbon framing to zero gives exactly the same reduced ADO function FN(M)F_N(M) at every level and every meridian value where it is defined. Its rational continuation is therefore the same function as well.

The geometric eigenvalue germ is also the same under this marked isotopy. An actual all-level operator for the input family thus satisfies the hypotheses of Section 7 for that same intrinsic family. Its first classical symbol contains the selected geometric factor. This transfers the factor statement without identifying presentation-specific recurrence formulas or their unreduced polynomial representatives.

The same argument applies to the arbitrary-closure reduction when its hypotheses yield one covered strict knot factor and only unknot factors. In particular, under its long surviving-factor hypotheses, a hyperbolic knot input has exactly that form. Removing the exhibited unknot summands is an actual orientation-preserving ambient isotopy. The preferred peripheral pair is carried by the induced map of exteriors. No multiplicativity assertion for quantum growth over connected sums is required.

For a fixed original expression there are finitely many recorded reduction and parity branches. If a uniform net-weight statement is desired, take the maximum of the strict source and geometric thresholds over those finitely many possible strict outputs. The threshold concerns their surviving signed net powers. Large individual powers that cancel to a short surviving power do not satisfy it. This does not assert that an earlier, smaller complete-value threshold automatically supplies the stronger moving-meridian hypotheses.

The integer a(D)a(D) and the individual edge factors in (O9) are computed on the covered strict diagram. They are not claimed to equal those read from an unreduced input presentation term by term. The preferred geometric longitude and its selected irreducible factor are the intrinsic objects that transfer.

The conclusions concern one irreducible geometric factor, not the full A-polynomial, its multiplicity, or an equality of quantum operator ideals. They do not provide a fixed-MM large-level WKB theorem, nonvanishing through ordinary-color resonances, or a general short-twist theorem. The wider quantitative all-circle alternatives remain available under their own source and geometric hypotheses; this section neither narrows those results nor extends them to arbitrary short bare-junction fillings.
