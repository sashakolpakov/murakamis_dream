The two-ended microscopic kernel with its original normal channels

Private written proof, 23 November 2026.

The original Newton kernel has an entire limit when both colors lie at distance of order N−2N^{-2} from 22. Both ends of its finite Newton sum survive. The low end gives the Wilson polynomial at a fixed physical index; the complementary end vanishes at that value but contributes to its true first normal. The error is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}), with every fixed holomorphic parameter derivative.

This concerns the canonical ordinary kernel, without replacing it by a moderate representative. It supplies neither an orthogonality transform with the original cap nor an estimate of a closed knot source.

1. The original kernel and the limiting series

At odd levels the additional root −2-2 is simple. Theorem. The series 𝒦(α,β)=∑k=0∞𝖤k(α)𝖤k(β)+∑m=1∞𝖧m(α)𝖧m(β)(3) \boxed{\quad \mathcal K(\alpha,\beta)= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathsf E_k(\alpha)\mathsf E_k(\beta) +\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathsf H_m(\alpha)\mathsf H_m(\beta) \quad} \qquad\text{(3)} converge normally on every compact complex bidisk. 4. Summing the two ends and retaining derivatives

Let L=⌊N/4⌋L=\lfloor N/4\rfloor. Split the canonical finite sum into 0≤i≤L0\le i\le L, i=N−mi=N-m with 1≤m≤L1\le m\le L, and the remaining middle. There is no overlap for large NN. Since the two signs (−1)i(-1)^i cancel in every same-index product, these are products of the Ei,NE_{i,N} defined above.

For 1≤i≤L1\le i\le L, (9)–(10) and (13) give |Ei,N(α)Ei,N(β)−𝖤i(α)𝖤i(β)|≤CAi2i2N2=CAN2.(14) |E_{i,N}(\alpha)E_{i,N}(\beta) -\mathsf E_i(\alpha)\mathsf E_i(\beta)| \le \frac{C_A}{i^2}\frac{i^2}{N^2}=\frac{C_A}{N^2}. \qquad\text{(14)} The index-zero terms agree exactly. 6. Source scope and complete coverage

For the original ordinary Askey–Wilson operator, write (ℒf)(cN(r))=(c0+AN(r)+CN(r))f(cN(r))−AN(r)f(cN(r+1))−CN(r)f(cN(r−1)), (\mathcal L f)(c_N(r))=(c_0+A_N(r)+C_N(r))f(c_N(r)) -A_N(r)f(c_N(r+1))-C_N(r)f(c_N(r-1)), where AN(r)=sr+14/(s2r+1s2r+2)A_N(r)=s_{r+1}^4/(s_{2r+1}s_{2r+2}), CN(r)=sr4/(s2rs2r+1)C_N(r)=s_r^4/(s_{2r}s_{2r+1}), and sr=2sin⁡(πr/N)s_r=2\sin(\pi r/N). This source is retained. We have not identified (3) with a standard homogeneous Wilson function or imported a Wilson orthogonality theorem. The Wilson name in (15) uses only its defining terminating formula.

The estimates concern bounded microscopic parameters and fixed physical indices. They are not uniform over all jj of order NN, arbitrary outer turns, or original cap contractions after a general short word. Neither Gram positivity nor the nonzero endpoint normal changes the original closing into an adjoint norm or proves a geometric coefficient.
