Original coefficient blocks, stationary touching, and the absolute cap budget

31 October 2026. analytical research lemma. Unpublished. No full-source or filling theorem is asserted. The purpose is to identify an exact usable closure statement and the two missing steps between the proved whole-star estimate and an absolute Phi_D bound.

Inputs are the whole-star spectral gap, cap-leaf structural theorem, original stationary classification, uniform sharp coefficient upper, closed endpoint coefficient entropy, and unique endpoint maximum. Their local estimates, endpoint theorem and original source identities are inputs. The unproved prescribed-chart full-source theorem is not an input.

1. Exact contraction before localization

Fix the original marked diagram. Every coupon has degree d=N-1; ordinary targets are d and protected targets are zero. Keep the actual finite LEFT/CROSS/RIGHT restrictions whenever a sector is specified. Let B be a collection of coupons and I a set of ordinary faces all of whose incidences belong to B. Define the original contracted polynomial

B_N(Y;theta)=[prod_(a in I) A_a^d] prod_(e in B) P_e,N(A,Y;theta_e).

The exterior G_N has no A_a incidence. Consequently

[original target] prod_e P_e,N
  =[Y^t] B_N(Y) G_N(Y).                              (1)

All shared boundary variables remain identified. The degree of B_N is d(|B|-|I|); no new target or normalization occurs.

Expand the EXTERIOR into its original local coefficient arrays k. Its boundary demand left for B is

a_N(k)_f=t_f-sum_(e outside B) k_(e,f).

Only nonnegative a_N contribute. The exact identity is

[Y^t]B_N G_N
  =sum_k [Y^(a_N(k))]B_N prod_(e outside B) p_e,N(k_e). (2)

This is a sum over polynomially many arrays at a fixed diagram. In (2) all internal B allocations have ALREADY been summed with their original signs. In particular no restriction on one internal cap index is introduced by restricting k.

Let a=a_N/d. Suppose, uniformly on a spectral compact K, the actual boundary coefficients have an upper rate b(a,theta), in the sequential sense that every convergent admissible (a_N/d,theta_N) has limsup N^-1 log |[Y^a_N]B_N| at most b(a,theta). Take b upper semicontinuous. The established uniform local coefficient upper for the exterior then gives

limsup N^-1 log sup_(theta in K) |[Y^t]B_N G_N|
   <= max_(q_out,theta in K)
      {b(tbar-A_out q_out,theta)+sum_(e outside B) H_e(q_e,theta_e)}.
                                                           (3)

The maximum ranges only over limits of exact exterior arrays for which the boundary degree is supported. Enlarging this to the corresponding closed original incidence image is safe for an upper bound. If that image is empty, there is no term. Formula (3) uses compactness and the polynomial array count, not lower rounding or a minimax interchange.

In particular a torus estimate

sup_(|Y|=L) |B_N(Y;theta)| <= exp(N(W(L)-delta(L)+o(1)))

implies the genuine boundary coefficient estimate

b_L(a)=W(L)-delta(L)-a dot log L.                    (4)

Any finite number of such bounds can be MINIMIZED for the SAME boundary coefficient. One may also include its direct sharp block upper, obtained by summing its actual internal arrays. Thus

b(a,theta)=min{b_direct(a,theta),b_L1(a),...,b_Ls(a)} (5)

is legitimate. For a local spectral domain, include b_L only there; one can work with closed smaller domains or continuous majorants to avoid a discontinuous convention at its boundary.

The same proof works for a FIXED partition into port-disjoint blocks with disjoint exclusive internal faces. Boundary variables of distinct blocks may coincide. Sum their b’s and the remaining local H’s on the reduced original flow. This gives an explicit finite-dimensional upper bound for the same source, retaining all targets.

2. What a finite cover is allowed to cover

A spectral cover is harmless: at a fixed theta, estimates from different block decompositions bound the SAME full coefficient. Thus one may take the minimum of their full capacities from (3), and use a finite spectral cover on which this minimum is below Phi_D by a uniform positive margin. That is the same logical operation as the mark-spectral cover.

A cover in INDIVIDUAL INTERNAL INCIDENCE ARRAYS is different. A mask cutting (2) preserves B_N exactly if it is constant on each fiber of the map from full arrays to exterior arrays k. Equivalently the mask must depend only on data left outside the contracted block. This is an exact sufficient condition. It is also necessary for the proposed factorization with unchanged B_N and a mask depending only on exterior data, as a formal identity for arbitrary local coefficients. Assign an independent symbol to each local monomial coefficient of each coupon. Distinct original incidence arrays give distinct products of these symbols. For fixed exterior array k, comparison of these independent monomials forces the full-array mask to have the same value on every internal array over k. No cancellation between different symbolic products can repair a nonconstant mask.

This formal coefficient test is a logical obstruction to an inference, not an actual-link counterexample. A special identity for the actual coupons could give additional cancellations, but it would be a new input beyond the unchanged whole-star theorem.

For overlapping stars a priority partition into neighborhoods need not have this fiber property for the star assigned to each piece. For example, the exterior degrees of one star may include a coupon inside the other. A common-exterior degree partition is safe, as is a fixed disjoint-block contraction. Otherwise an additional projection or boundary-tensor identity is required. One cannot reuse the unchanged whole-star bound for an arbitrarily cut internal sum.

Consequently (3)-(5), rather than a cover of individual saddle arrays, are a rigorous quantitative extraction theorem. An absolute criterion is that the right side of (3), or the minimum over finitely many valid full capacities, is less than Phi_D. This criterion still has a variational comparison to prove on a general prescribed diagram.

3. The star envelope actually touches the stationary sharp action

There is a useful precise connection between the existing weighted star bound and the sharp maximum; the two are not unrelated envelopes.

Take a classified stationary residual cap F=(u,A,v,C), u=v=0, q_F,A=1-nu, q_F,C=nu, 0<nu<1/2. Suppose every other A-incident coupon is CROSS. Let B consist of F and ALL these neighbors, and I={A}. The common positive multipliers give L_A=L_C. Let q* be the classified stationary assignment and let a* be its total B-boundary loads, with A omitted. Write

W(L)=sum_(e in B minus F) X_e(L),
X_e(L)=Q_e,input(L)+Q_e,output(L).

At each stationary CROSS neighbor, the same ordinary positive weights attain its triangle Legendre formulas, including its protected zeros. Therefore

X_e(L)=h_e(q*_e)+q*_e dot log L.

This follows alternatively from concavity of the complete two-triangle entropy and the common first-order equations; it is not a weighted maximality assertion about a Green coupon.

The total B load at A is one, and its cap weighted load equals

(1-nu)log L_A+nu log L_C=log L_A.

Since the cap sharp action is zero, exact cancellation of these two log L_A terms gives

W(L)-a* dot log L = sum_(e in B) H_e(q*_e).          (6)

Thus the proved spectral-star estimate, with its actual gap delta>0, gives a boundary-coefficient upper at a* equal to the original sharp block action MINUS delta. After adjoining the unchanged exterior at q*_out, the expression in (3) is

total sharp action(q*)-delta.                      (7)

Continuity of the exterior H and of the affine term in (4) preserves a strict improvement in a neighborhood of the EXTERIOR data and spectra. The whole cap spectral disk and CROSS strips are genuine fixed neighborhoods, as required by the star theorem. Equation (7) also holds for a maximum restricted to closed NC once the independently proved strict overload margin permits the original stationary equations.

Notice what is and is not localized: (7) bounds every internal star allocation compatible with that exterior coefficient, not just those near q*. This is exactly why (2) is the correct decomposition.

As a useful conditional consequence, if all maximizers of a specified sharp sector capacity M are covered by exterior-fiber neighborhoods admitting (7) for ONE FIXED block, and the block direct sharp upper is at most M elsewhere, compactness of (3)-(5) gives an actual strict upper M-delta_0 for some delta_0>0. A compatible finite partition into blocks has the same consequence. This is a strict improvement relative to M; it does not assert M-delta_0<Phi_D.

4. The absolute cap deficit is exactly 2 f(nu)

Let

f(t)=-integral_0^t log tan(pi s) ds,
f(0)=f(1/2)=0, f(t)>0 for 0<t<1/2.

Use h_0 for the actual positive endpoint coefficient entropy (both parities, with the prescribed odd regularizer), and H for the sharp real spectral upper. The closed monomer formula proves on the cap degrees just used

H_cap=0,       h_0,cap=-2f(nu).                    (8)

At a classified CROSS neighbor at spectrum -2, H=h_0. Hence for a cap-only classified datum, with caps indexed by F,

F_sharp(q)=H_endpoint(q)+2 sum_F f(nu_F).          (9)

This is an identity on the original flow, not a smoothing or a changed triangle allocation. The endpoint theorem and unique geometric maximum give H_endpoint<=Phi_D. More quantitatively let

g_D=Phi_D-max_(q in original closed NC) H_endpoint(q)>0. (10)

The maximum exists on the compact original flow; it is strictly below Phi_D because the unique geometric optimizer is strictly CROSS. Empty NC is vacuous. Thus on the cap-only data

F_sharp(q)-Phi_D <= 2 sum_F f(nu_F)-g_D.            (11)

In particular a cap-only datum of value at least Phi_D must satisfy 2 sum f(nu_F)>=g_D. With one cap its nu is in a compact subinterval of (0,1/2). With two caps at least one has a definite positive f; this does not place BOTH parameters away from their endpoints.

Formula (11) identifies a quantitative sufficient budget for a VALID localization or disjoint-block extraction: a cancellation loss exceeding 2 sum f(nu_F)-g_D gives an absolute improvement below Phi_D at that datum. Loss at least 2 sum f(nu_F) is a simpler conservative sufficient budget, leaving the original endpoint gap untouched. A potentially sharper budget uses the actual endpoint value H_endpoint(q), not g_D.

The star theorem currently provides delta>0, not an inequality between delta and this deficit. Equations (6)-(7) explain how to compare the quantities without confusing different weighted envelopes. They do not prove the missing comparison.

For the actual contracted star an even more intrinsic target is its positive ENDPOINT REFERENCE with the SAME A^d and boundary degrees, including the actual odd regularizer and the same sector projections. Let its coefficients be r_N(a)>0 on the relevant support. An estimate

|[Y^a]B_N| <= exp(o(N)) r_N(a)                    (12)

uniformly over original admissible a, and in an appropriate spectral neighborhood, pays the entire boundary-optimized endpoint deficit. The two-spectrum degree-two theorem proves the endpoint version with exp(o(N)); on a fixed nearby spectral polydisk it gives exp(N omega(epsilon)+o(N)), with omega(epsilon) tending to zero, for its typed pair. The arbitrary valence star theorem has not proved (12).

5. The exact minimal replacement test for arbitrary exteriors

Fix any contracted B_N and positive comparison R_N with the same boundary degree. Restrict to boundary exponents a which are compatible with the specified remaining original target t, namely 0<=a<=t and any additional genuine exterior support restrictions. For a family of arbitrary exterior coefficients supported on all such complements, the following are equivalent:

  1. |[Y^a]B_N| <= kappa_N [Y^a]R_N for every such a;

  2. |[Y^t]B_N G_N| <= kappa_N [Y^t]R_N |G_N|_coeff for every polynomial G_N with that allowed exterior support.

The implication (i)->(ii) is exact convolution and the triangle inequality. For (ii)->(i), choose the single monomial G_N=Y^(t-a). One may include its harmless unit phase. Unsupported comparison coefficients must then force the corresponding B coefficient to vanish. No statement about inadmissible exponents is needed.

For the actual fixed exterior, (i) is a sufficient condition, not a claimed necessary condition: its special source correlations can provide more cancellation. This distinction leaves room for a genuine larger-source identity beyond (12).

For port-disjoint exclusive blocks, (12) at every block combines by ordinary positive convolution while retaining shared boundary labels. For overlapping blocks it does not automatically iterate. Taking an absolute value after the first contraction can erase the second block’s internal signs, and its factors are no longer the unchanged original block. Either a single larger-block comparison or a proved compatible replacement operator is needed.

6. Why strict loss at old maxima is not an iteration theorem

Two logically distinct claims are sometimes conflated:

A. every high maximum of the original sharp functional has a cancellation block giving some positive loss; B. the actual source has rate at most Phi_D.

Even with completely compatible localization, A gives a strict reduction from the original maximum M. It does not say the reduction exceeds M-Phi_D. A one-dimensional logical test is H(x)=Phi_D+a-x^2 and an exactly known improved bound H(x)-b, where 0<b<a. The maximum is strictly reduced, all original maxima are covered, but the resulting upper remains Phi_D+a-b>Phi_D. This is not a diagram counterexample; it disproves the purported abstract implication.

Nor can the SAME estimate simply be applied again to subtract another b. After replacing the original upper by the minimum of new bounds, its new maxima can occur on a switch between bounds and need not satisfy the original sharp stationary equations. The boundary activation and cap-leaf theorems classify maxima of the original functional on its original fiber. They have not been proved for these new contracted, often nonsmooth upper functionals. Reusing their conclusion there would be circular.

A valid finite iteration must therefore supply, at each stage, an exact coefficient-preserving replacement and a proved decreasing potential with a quantitative budget, together with either termination at the positive endpoint source or an absolute terminal bound. Current cap-leaf selection proves existence of an applicable relative star at certain original maxima; it does not supply these iteration data.

The stellar identity reinforces the distinction: it preserves M_cap-Phi while increasing the cap incidence degree. It transports a CLASSICAL sector optimization, not the finite signed boundary polynomial or its needed compensation (12).

7. Endpoint resolvent: exact identity, but no free positivity

For the full original Casimir quotient, chi_N(Omega)=0 and chi_N(2)=4 give the exact polynomial resolvent identity

(2-Omega)V_N(2)=4I.                              (13)

Every unchanged linear original source functional Lambda therefore satisfies 2Lambda(V_N(2))-Lambda(Omega V_N(2))=4Lambda(1). The extra Omega coupon has not disappeared. Projecting to the cap range produces the already known boundary currents of the Newton contiguity; omitting them would alter the source.

An ordinary self-adjoint Casimir model would bound the scalar inverse by its spectral distance, but cannot be assumed to preserve the complete-colored quotient. The even coupon L_N=(-1)^d U_d(z/2) has L_N^2=0 modulo chi_N and vanishes at every distinct spectral node, whereas the actual conditioned coefficients Lambda_n(1,L_N) are strictly positive for every 0<=n<=M, where M=N/2-1; in particular Lambda_0(1,L_N)=N^2. Thus a coupon-uniform semisimple adapter preserving these observables is impossible. This does not exclude a special identity for the fixed resolvent, an explicit treatment of its confluent corrections, or a nonlinear bound not intertwining arbitrary coupons.

8. Current concrete missing lemma

The precise new candidate is a cancellation-aware boundary comparison for a whole cap/CROSS star at original A^d, either (12), or a weaker weighted coefficient bound whose loss satisfies the explicit budget in (11) after a legitimate reduced-flow maximization (3). It must retain every boundary face degree and the actual parity reference.

Such a theorem would be a real absolute input. A finite collection of compatible bounds satisfying (3)<Phi_D uniformly off the geometric spectral cube would feed the existing entire-period criterion; its nonzero geometric packet and primitive filling action are separate already proved inputs. Neither a positive local gap by itself nor a classification of old maxima supplies that strict absolute premise.

No global counterexample has been proved here. The existing shared-star and structural notes state their limitations correctly; the gap is in the unproved bridge one might try to infer from them.

scope

Block localization uses independent formal local coefficients. Its stationary touching identity retains the negative endpoint cap entropy and the non-iteration limitation; a relative block loss alone does not pay an absolute volume deficit.

The all-parity original target, actual sector projections, protected zeros, and distinction between endpoint compensation and a positive relative gap are retained. No source lower bound, universal cap elimination, absolute prescribed-chart comparison, or filling theorem is inferred.
