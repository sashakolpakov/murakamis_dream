Actual complete geometry for all strict Conway source trees

31 October 2026. complete-geometry theorem. This note extends the original ideal-cell construction to every literal h/T/producth/T/\mathrm{product} expression with a strict reduced cylindrical state. Four individual edge masks replace the insufficient pair-symmetric port pattern. The proof retains the actual colored boundary, terminal identifications, reversible link cut and cusp cycles.

The conclusion concerns the complete augmented-link complement and its original positive source. It is not a signed-source or long-filling theorem for the enlarged syntax.

1. Statement and the strengthened induction invariant

Let F,GF,G be arbitrary finite expressions in the original leaf hh, literal ordered turn TT, and ordered product. Require the original reduced wire polytope of C(FG)C(FG), after deleting only its forced final gate, to have a strict state: all leaf labels belong to (0,1/2)(0,1/2), and every retained product obeys max⁡(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \max(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \tag{1} The exact strict-output compiler, §2, assigns each open expression one of the types L=(0,1/2),H=(1/2,1),A=(0,1),⌀. L=(0,1/2),\qquad H=(1/2,1),\qquad A=(0,1),\qquad\varnothing. Here hh has type LL; TT exchanges L,HL,H and fixes AA; the product table is LL,LA,AA↦A,HL,HA↦H,HH↦⌀, LL,LA,AA\longmapsto A,\qquad HL,HA\longmapsto H,\qquad HH\longmapsto\varnothing, \tag{2} with reversed input orders included. An empty type remains empty. Every stated interval is actually attained. Thus the strict cylindrical root pairs are AA,AL,AH,LHAA,AL,AH,LH, in either order; LLLL and HHHH fail. The two children need not belong to the earlier completed grammar.

Theorem. The unique complete positive optimizer builds an actual convex ideal right-angled polyhedron with the original colored bowtie ribbon boundary. Its two projection copies, with the original labeled face pairings, give the actual complete finite-volume augmented-link complement. All auxiliary components are retained, and the volume is twice the sum of the retained source-cell volumes.

The open-cell induction keeps more than its angles:

  1. An actual three-dimensional convex ideal polyhedron has a supported, nondegenerate quadrilateral output Q=(C0,C1,C2,C3)Q=(C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3).
  2. Each original leaf retains its two distinct shaded ideal triangles. Its apex and four corners remain distinct; those two triangles meet only at their original apex.
  3. Every nonport edge is right angled and separates WHITE from SHADED. Each port edge has one adjacent nonport face, recorded individually.
  4. Each port corner is an active original terminal singleton. A consumed terminal pair and an auxiliary apex never occur on a later port.
  5. Each active corner carries one original shaded sector; an internal main vertex carries the two sectors of its paired terminals in their original ribbon order.

These statements, including vertex identifications and cyclic orders, are proved below.

3. Convexity, ideal coincidences and all port corners

Position two convex ideal cells on opposite sides of their matching supported face. In hyperbolic space their intersection is exactly that face. The finite union is closed, connected and locally convex at every finite boundary point by the preceding angles. Any two finite points have a shortest path in the union: a length-bounded minimizing sequence stays in a compact ambient ball, and constant-speed compactness gives a limit. Local convexity makes it an ambient geodesic locally, hence the unique whole geodesic segment. The union is convex. This is the original supported-face proof, applied to the refined angles.

The output remains a genuine supported quadrilateral. Its four edge angles are strictly positive and nonflat, so no other boundary piece extends its supporting face through an output edge.

This also excludes accidental ideal coincidences, which an argument inside hyperbolic space alone would miss. Cells on opposite supporting sides can have common ideal points only on the ideal circle of the support plane. A cell has no ideal vertex on that circle other than its matching quadrilateral’s corners: an additional vertex would enlarge the supporting face past a quadrilateral edge, contradicting its strict supporting angle. The ideal closure of the quadrilateral itself has only its four ideal vertices. Therefore only the prescribed face-corner identifications occur. This applies to the first attachment and to the second; its remaining input face stays supported and nondegenerate.

Every shaded triangle remains an entire unchanged face. It is never an attaching port. Its seams are always right, never flat, by the CAP rule and (9)–(10). Only WHITE pieces merge. No shaded edge is deleted, subdivided or merged into another shaded face. No leaf apex is ever on an attaching port, so it stays a separate ideal vertex with its four original right edges and two shaded sectors.

The output-corner patterns are explicit. At CjC_j, the adjacent bits ηj−1,ηj\eta_{j-1},\eta_j have sum at most one by (7). The two port-edge angles sum to 1−(ηj−1+ηj)/21-(\eta_{j-1}+\eta_j)/2; every other angle is right. The Euclidean ideal-link angle sum gives ηj−1+ηjvalencecyclic faces04PORT,WHITE,SHADED,WHITE13PORT,WHITE,SHADEDin the recorded cyclic order. \begin{array}{c|c|c} \eta_{j-1}+\eta_j&\text{valence}&\text{cyclic faces}\\ 0&4&\mathrm{PORT,WHITE,SHADED,WHITE}\\ 1&3&\mathrm{PORT,WHITE,SHADED} \ \text{in the recorded cyclic order}. \end{array} \tag{11} Each exposed corner has one original shaded sector. Under (8), an output corner inherits one child terminal; only the two internal interface corners combine terminals. A turn rotates their sectors. This proves the full corner invariant, including the asymmetric cases.

4. Exact terminal accounting: no transitive corner collapse

Give each leaf the four original symbols (i,0),(i,1),(i,2),(i,3)(i,0),(i,1),(i,2),(i,3). An open expression has exactly four active singleton symbols in its ordered port. All other terminals are partitioned into disjoint two-element pairs. A paired symbol is permanently consumed: its vertex is internal and never appears on a later attaching or output port. Apices OiO_i are separate symbols and never enter this matching.

This holds for a leaf. A turn permutes the active singletons. For a product of disjoint child trees, (8) consumes exactly F2∼G1,F3∼G0, F_2\sim G_1,\qquad F_3\sim G_0, and leaves F0,F1,G2,G3F_0,F_1,G_2,G_3 active. It never acts on a previously consumed pair. Each new pair contains terminals from different child leaf sets. Thus there are no chains of pair equalities and no classes with more than two terminals. The six prism vertices implement exactly these identifications; their fresh names create no additional relation among old internal vertices. Section 3 excludes extra geometric identifications.

For C(FG)C(FG), either retain these two root interface pairs and close the remaining four singletons, or delete the forced root prism and use the entire original closing map F:Cj↦G:C3−j. F:C_j\longmapsto G:C_{3-j}. \tag{12} This forms all four pairs between the distinct root-child leaf sets. Both descriptions give the same matching. Every original terminal is used once in one pair; no pair contains two terminals of one leaf. Consequently every leaf has four distinct main corner vertices after closure. Its two original shaded triangles are nondegenerate, distinct, and meet only at their original apex. This rules out transitive identification, rather than merely checking each individual gluing.

5. The closed sphere and the exact oriented ribbon cut

Root stationarity at complementary labels b,1−bb,1-b gives equal ordered quadrilateral metrics, so (12) is the actual closing isometry. Paired FREE or CAP angles sum to one minus half their two mask bits. Two nonzero bits would require HHHH on a FREE edge or LLLL on a CAP edge. Neither root pair is strict. All closing seams are therefore flat WHITE/WHITE or right SHADED/WHITE.

The supported-face argument makes the union an actual convex ideal polyhedron PP. Its boundary is a sphere, every surviving edge is right, and every such edge belongs to an original shaded triangle. All other seams merge only white pieces. There is no duplicate shaded edge or white bigon: distinct shaded faces cannot share an edge because each such edge has a WHITE side. White faces are actual maximal convex ideal polygons, with no zero-width or two-edge region.

To identify the original link, use the independent literal-wire compiler. At leaf ii, put shaded triangles (Oi,Ci1,Ci2)(O_i,C_{i1},C_{i2}), (Oi,Ci3,Ci0)(O_i,C_{i3},C_{i0}) in the original oriented chart. At each terminal record the outgoing-next and incoming-previous shaded neighbors. Product (8) concatenates the two recorded sectors at each consumed terminal pair; turn rotates the ports; closure (12) makes the original cylindrical pairs.

These are precisely the metric cell maps and cyclic orders. The refined masks change which seams remain right edges, without changing terminal pairings or orientations. Every shaded edge survives and every remaining seam is WHITE/WHITE flat. The resulting metric boundary is exactly the original bowtie ribbon graph. All white facial cycles are its complementary oriented facial walks. One global orientation is retained throughout.

The projection cut is reversible for arbitrary literal syntax here. Each auxiliary circle remains in its original disjoint leaf box with its recorded twice-punctured disk. In either projection ball, drilling its boundary-parallel semicircle and cutting along the same half disk cancel one handle in that collar. The collars are disjoint. This leaves a ball with the original ideal main-segment vertices and disk triangles. Its oriented boundary is the ribbon cellulation just proved.

Match the labeled sphere cellulations and extend over the two balls. Restoring the original white projection pairings and shaded disk pairings recovers the actual augmented-link exterior. The construction has degree one and retains every component. It does not interpret an auxiliary open quadrilateral port as a physical four-punctured boundary.

6. Nondegenerate cusp rectangles and four-step edge cycles

Every ideal vertex of PP has only right incident edges. Its sufficiently small horosphere section is a finite convex Euclidean polygon with all angles π/2\pi/2; the angle sum forces four sides. It is a nondegenerate rectangle. Its supporting faces are distinct and PP has three-dimensional interior; coincident opposite supporting lines would put PP into a plane. Every side length is positive. Colors alternate WHITE and SHADED.

Take two oppositely oriented projection copies. A white pairing matches identically labeled faces across copies. The shaded pair for leaf ii is glued within each copy by the original rule fixing OiO_i, mapping Ci1C_{i1} to Ci0C_{i0}, and Ci2C_{i2} to Ci3C_{i3}. These ideal-triangle maps are isometries.

For an edge ee of one shaded triangle, its partner e′e' belongs to the other shaded triangle of the same leaf. They are distinct: those triangles share only the apex, not an edge. The complete edge cycle has the four occurrences e(0),e′(0),e′(1),e(1) e^{(0)},\quad e'^{(0)},\quad e'^{(1)},\quad e^{(1)} \tag{13} up to cyclic order. There is no shorter self-gluing return. The shaded maps are inverse labeled triangle maps, and white maps are the identity between labeled copies. The cycle returns pointwise on its edge, with total angle 2π2\pi and no residual longitudinal translation.

At each ideal vertex, scale its horosphere so the two equal positive SHADED side lengths are one. Each shaded triangle now has its canonical three corner segments of length one. All shaded maps preserve these horospheres; white pairings join identical decorations. Shrink them uniformly to disjoint cusp neighborhoods. Their rectangles glue without dilation into compact nonsingular Euclidean surfaces, identified by the actual link cut as the original cusp tori. The truncated core is compact. Thus the assembled metric is complete and has finite volume.

Each leaf apex retains its exact local rectangle. The original primitive crossing-circle paths, §6, therefore remain the actual complete meridian and disk-framed longitude, with ratio 2icot⁡(πai)2i\cot(\pi a_i). This complete marking alone does not establish a deformed signed-source representation.

7. Volume, connected-sum scope and the quantum limitation

Each local cell is attached on the opposite supported side of its face. The cell adjacency graph is a tree and every cell has positive volume. With 𝒜E\mathcal A_E the original dimensionless sum of leaf and retained gate actions, Vol⁡(P)=πmax⁡𝒜E,Vol⁡(YE0)=2πmax⁡𝒜E. \operatorname{Vol}(P)=\pi\max\mathcal A_E,\qquad \operatorname{Vol}(Y_E^0)=2\pi\max\mathcal A_E. \tag{14} The original positive finite source ZE,N0Z^0_{E,N} has the same rate. Uniform factorial upper bounds and polynomial state counting give the upper bound; primitive every-level rounding of the strict optimizer gives a positive lower term at both parities. Thus limN→∞2πNlog⁡ZE,N0=Vol⁡(YE0). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log Z^0_{E,N} =\operatorname{Vol}(Y_E^0). \tag{15} All original normal directions and finite source support remain present.

This does not discard the cap-connected-sum obstruction. A cap product D(FG)=C(T(FG))D(FG)=C(T(FG)) forces its final product output to zero, both inputs to zero, and that gate to be flat. Such a diagram has not been exhibited as a strict root C(F′G′)C(F'G') of the present kind. The boundary cap reduction retains its two-punctured sum sphere and gives the sum of piece volumes.

Here the additional information is the explicit complete metric. A nontrivial sum of already complete cusp pieces would give two noncommuting longitudes centralizing their common meridian, contrary to the abelian parabolic centralizer in this metric. Hyperbolicity has not been inferred merely from positivity of a source rate. Likewise the rigid L/HL/H constants in (5) are not interpreted as finite scalar factors or automatic connected-sum factors.

The theorem above is for the original untwisted complete source. No extension to arbitrary retained half-twist patterns is claimed by this note. Their local pairing construction is a separate input. Nor is a signed packet, global alias bound, or long-filling volume theorem inferred for the whole enlarged syntax. Strict L/HL/H expressions can contain quarter-index leaves; the analytic treatment of those leaves and the required global source estimates are separate from this complete geometry.
