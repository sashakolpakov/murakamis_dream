The actual primitive gauge has a Gaussian resonant endpoint

27 October 2026. The canonical finite Hilbert-90 gauge from the original source has an exactly computable resonant endpoint. Its polynomial Ward identity contains two retained highest-degree terms. The endpoint is a quadratic Gauss period, of size N^(-1/2), despite the gauge’s exponential operator norm on the flat relative module.

This settles the endpoint of THIS specified source gauge. It does not evaluate the knot-dependent endpoint sum or identify the marked geometric A-period. In particular it supplies no additional knot-volume conjecture proof.

1. Canonical polynomial and exact unreduced Ward equation

Use Q=exp(2pi*i/N), N>=2, F_r=(Q;Q)_r, and

v_N(z)=sum_(r=0)^(N-1) (-1)^r Q^(r(r-1)/2)z^r/F_r.

Let g_N(z)=sum_(r=0)^(N-1)c_r z^r be the CANONICAL degree-<N representative of v_N(z)v_N(Qz) modulo z^N. It is the actual primitive source gauge in the Fourier transport theorem, whose regular-disk action was proved in the gauge-action note.

As an ordinary polynomial, before setting z^N=0, it obeys

(1-z)(1-Qz)g_N(Qz)-g_N(z)
             =-(2/N)z^N+c_(N-1)z^(N+1).       (1)

To prove this, first keep the full ordinary product w_N(z)=v_N(z)v_N(Qz), including its degrees N,…,2N-2. The exact Euler Ward equation is

(1-z)v_N(Qz)=v_N(z)-z^N/N.

Multiplying its versions at z and Qz gives

(1-z)(1-Qz)w_N(Qz)-w_N(z)
  =-z^N[v_N(z)+v_N(Qz)]/N+z^(2N)/N^2.          (2)

Hence the coefficient of z^N is -2/N, and coefficients below N vanish. Removing the high tail of w_N does not alter that z^N coefficient: its own degree-N contribution to the left side is (QN-1)[zN]w_N=0. The left side for g_N has degree at most N+1; its top coefficient is Q^N c_(N-1)=c_(N-1). This proves (1). Equivalently it gives the exact coefficient relation

c_(N-2)=(1+Q)c_(N-1)-2Q/N.

Setting the polynomial argument z=1 in (1) now yields

g_N(1)=2/N-c_(N-1).                            (3)

Both source terms in (1) are required. If (1) were replaced by its homogeneous identity in z^N=0 before evaluation, the selected endpoint would be changed.

2. The retained coefficient is an exact Gauss period

The root factorial identity F_(N-1)=N gives, coefficient by coefficient,

[z^(N-1-r)]v_N(z)=Q^(r+1)F_r/N, 0<=r<N.

For example this follows from F_(N-1-r)=N(-1)^r Q^(r(r+1)/2)/F_r. Substituting it into the convolution for c_(N-1) cancels every factorial and gives

c_(N-1)=(1/N)sum_(r=0)^(N-1)
                        (-1)^r Q^(r(r-1)/2).   (4)

Thus (3)-(4) are an exact algebraic period evaluation of the canonical endpoint at every level, including composite N. They are not an identification of the nilpotent algebra with scalar points. The representative and the degree-N source were fixed before evaluation.

3. Direct Gaussian asymptotic, with the finite boundary retained

Put

T_N=sum_(r=0)^(N-1)(-1)^r Q^(r(r-1)/2),
omega_N=exp[-pi*i*(N+1)/4-pi*i/(4N)].

Then

T_N=sqrt(N) omega_N+O(1),                     (5)
c_(N-1)=omega_N/sqrt(N)+O(1/N),
g_N(1)=-omega_N/sqrt(N)+O(1/N).               (6)

Here is a proof of (5) without importing an incomplete-Gauss-sum estimate. On integer r the summand equals exp(2piif(r)), where

f(x)=[x^2-(N+1)x]/(2N).

Periodize exp(2piif(x)) times the indicator of [-1/2,N-1/2]. This is a piecewise smooth periodic function, and zero is away from its discontinuities. Its symmetrically summed Fourier series therefore gives the finite Poisson identity

T_N=sum_(m in Z)^sym I_m,
I_m=integral_(-1/2)^(N-1/2)
                   exp(2pi*i*(f(x)-m*x)) dx.  (7)

For N>=4, f’ lies in [-3/4,1/2]. For every nonzero m, integration by parts expresses I_m as

[exp(2pi*i*(f(x)-m*x))/(2pi*i*(f'(x)-m))]_a^b
   +(1/(2pi*i)) integral_a^b
      f''(x) exp(2pi*i*(f(x)-m*x))/(f'(x)-m)^2 dx,

with a=-1/2, b=N-1/2. The second term is O(m^-2), uniformly in N: f’‘=1/N, the interval has length N, and |f’(x)-m|>=|m|/4. The boundary terms also have an absolutely summable PAIRED bound. Both endpoints have exp(-2piim*x)=(-1)^m, and

1/(t-m)+1/(t+m)=2t/(t^2-m^2)=O(m^-2)

uniformly for -3/4<=t<=1/2. Hence the sum of all m!=0 terms in (7) is O(1). No separate divergent boundary sums have been discarded.

For m=0, complete the square around a_0=(N+1)/2. This gives

I_0=exp[-pi*i*(N+1)^2/(4N)] sqrt(N)
      *integral_(-(N+2)/(2sqrt(N)))^((N-2)/(2sqrt(N)))
                                 exp(pi*i*y^2) dy.

The complete Fresnel integral is exp(pi*i/4). It follows by Gaussian regularization, and its two tails are O(1/R) by one integration by parts beyond |y|=R. The displayed endpoints have size comparable to sqrt(N), so replacing them by infinity changes I_0 by O(1). Its complete value is sqrt(N) omega_N. Together with the nonzero Fourier modes this proves (5). The finitely many smaller levels do not affect the asymptotic. Equations (3)-(4) finish (6).

In particular the exact resonant source gauge satisfies

|g_N(1)|=N^(-1/2)[1+O(N^(-1/2))],
log|g_N(1)|=-(1/2)log N+O(N^(-1/2)),
(1/N)log|g_N(1)| -> 0.                       (8)

It is nonzero for all sufficiently large N. No all-level nonvanishing claim beyond this is needed for (8).

4. Consequence for the actual source comparison

The operator gauge has a positive exponential norm on a regular flat-metric chart, as already proved, while its canonical resonant endpoint (8) has no positive exponential rate. Neither its operator size nor a regular-chart dilogarithm value determines this selected endpoint. The high coefficient in (1) becomes the leading term of the endpoint; the source carry must be retained.

This endpoint is not the original figure-eight invariant. Already at N=2, g_2(1)=1 whereas the original source identity for 4_1 gives Kashaev_2(4_1)=1+4=5. The whole-source proof for that knot uses a separate positive product sum, not this universal gauge. No equality with that invariant or a general knot period is introduced here.

Thus this is a usable fully evaluated local source period, but it does not close the selected geometric-period or volume-norm gate for a general actual word. It specifies what has to be composed with that word and its other source sectors before any volume conclusion can follow.

The audit and saved results check the canonical polynomial coefficients, the FULL unreduced Ward equation and Gauss-period identity exactly at finite-field roots. High-precision samples separately diagnose (5)-(8); the uniform proof above establishes their asymptotic scope.
