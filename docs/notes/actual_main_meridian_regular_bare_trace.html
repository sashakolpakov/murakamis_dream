The trace at a product junction on a regular index window

A product junction without a crossing-circle operator contributes a polynomial trace to the Conway source. When its incident Newton indices lie in a compact part of the strict low-index region, that trace changes by an exponentially small relative amount under a bounded main-meridian deformation. The exact Fourier expansion groups Laurent coefficients whose indices differ by multiples of the level; these groups are called aliases below. Its first alias is independent of the meridian parameter, and strict log-concavity makes every higher alias exponentially smaller.

The source-bound theorem, Section 2, supplies the exact Fourier trace and the finite interval-tail inequality used here. The grouped wire packet applies the result at the regular maximizing state. For signed coefficient arrays the conclusion is first an error relative to a positive absolute majorant; control of the whole signed contraction is a separate step.

3. Higher aliases are exponentially small relative to the first difference

For ℓ≥2\ell\ge2 with ℓN−1≤D\ell N-1\le D, apply (11) with t1=1−1/Nt_1=1-1/N, t2=ℓ−1/Nt_2=\ell-1/N. Since t22−t12=(ℓ−1)(ℓ+1−2/N)≥2, t_2^2-t_1^2=(\ell-1)(\ell+1-2/N)\ge2, equation (12) gives aℓN−1aN−1≤NCexp⁡(−λδN/B). \frac{a_{\ell N-1}}{a_{N-1}} \le N^C\exp(-\lambda_\delta N/B). \tag{13} The denominator exists because (4) implies D>N−1D>N-1. If the numerator lies outside the support, set it to zero.

It remains to compare a coefficient to its first discrete difference. The elementary interval-tail inequality for a convolution of BB symmetric unimodal sequences supported in [−M,M][-M,M] states ak≤(2M+1)B(ak−ak+2)(k≥M). a_k\le(2M+1)^B(a_k-a_{k+2})\qquad(k\ge M). \tag{14} Its proof is finite: decompose each sequence into a nonnegative sum of interval indicators. For a product of such indicators, the convolution count strictly drops by step two beyond the largest interval radius whenever the first count is nonzero; that positive integer difference is at least one, whereas the count is at most (2M+1)B(2M+1)^B. Summing the interval decompositions proves (14). This is the same all-index lemma used in the adjacent-level source comparison.

Applying (14) at k=N−1k=N-1 gives d1≥N−BaN−1>0. d_1\ge N^{-B}a_{N-1}>0. \tag{15} Combining 0≤dℓ≤aℓN−10\le d_\ell\le a_{\ell N-1}, (13), and (15) proves ∑ℓ=2Ldℓd1≤NC′exp⁡(−λδN/B). \sum_{\ell=2}^{L}\frac{d_\ell}{d_1} \le N^{C'}\exp(-\lambda_\delta N/B). \tag{16} Here LL is bounded in terms of BB. The conclusion is uniform throughout (4).

4. The local deformed trace has the same leading kernel

For every fixed complex disk |s|≤R|s|\le R, |Uℓ−1(cos⁡(2πs))|≤ℓexp⁡(2π(ℓ−1)R). |U_{\ell-1}(\cos(2\pi s))| \le\ell\exp(2\pi(\ell-1)R). \tag{17} This follows from its finite exponential sum and holds through all sine resonances. At s=0s=0, the value is ℓ\ell. Equation (16) therefore shows, for all sufficiently large NN, ϕ0(FN)=N2d1(1+O(e−κN))>0,ϕs(FN)ϕ0(FN)=1+OB,δ,R(e−κN). \phi_0(F_N)=N^2d_1\bigl(1+O(e^{-\kappa N})\bigr)>0, \qquad \boxed{\quad \frac{\phi_s(F_N)}{\phi_0(F_N)} =1+O_{B,\delta,R}(e^{-\kappa N}).\quad} \tag{18} One can take any fixed κ<λδ/B\kappa<\lambda_\delta/B, after absorbing the polynomial factors. The sign alternation at even levels is included in (5) and is harmless once (16) is proved. The opposite-color relation remains constant, so its corresponding statement is exact without a deformation.

For fixed B≤4B\le4, there are no higher aliases for any low indices: D≤4M≤2N−2D\le4M\le2N-2, so L≤1L\le1. Then ϕs(FN)=ϕ0(FN) \phi_s(F_N)=\phi_0(F_N) \tag{19} for every ss, with no regular-window hypothesis; a zero trace remains zero. This also follows by the one-division degree bound.

The ratio in (18) is entire in ss, with a constant positive denominator. Applying (18) on a slightly larger fixed disk and using Cauchy’s estimate gives the same exponential error for every fixed derivative in ss. No contour avoiding a resonance is needed for this local trace statement.

5. Applying the local estimate to a source

The result concerns the exact raw polynomial trace of the prescribed fixed-coordinate factors. It has not replaced a physical auxiliary color by a coefficient index. Any original color dependence remains in the coefficients multiplying those factors. The typed turn, circle pivot, crossing scalar and primitive main-cut normalization are separate parts of the compiler.

On a compact bulk index window, the proved paired-turn formula also gives Wn(s)/HN(s)=1+OR(N−1)W_n(s)/H_N(s)=1+O_R(N^{-1}), and the six type scalars q±s2/2q^{\pm s^2/2} are 1+OR(N−1)1+O_R(N^{-1}). Thus a fixed product of the local trace kernels in (18) and those specified bulk turn factors has the same leading fixed-state weight, with the appropriate O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) turn error. This statement retains the actual types; it is not a replacement of the moving multiplication law by the complete one globally.

For arbitrary signed coefficient arrays supported in a common window satisfying (4), (18) implies an error bounded by e−κNe^{-\kappa N} times the corresponding complete positive absolute contraction. It does not imply a relative error with respect to a possibly canceling signed contraction. Similarly the turn errors have an O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) absolute-majorant bound. To conclude a whole-source microscopic profile, one must also control the literal main-meridian deformation of the physical input coefficients, establish suitable conditioning or stationary cancellation for their contraction, and bound the complement of the regular window. None of these follows just from (18).

The regular circle trace gives the physical input deformation and its canonical analytic interpolation. The component extension controls grouped high-index errors, while the grouped wire packet proves the finite-window and contour statements needed to use these local estimates. The filled-volume theorem combines those inputs with a joint charge comparison. The present result is the regular-window trace estimate; it does not obtain a whole-source relative error from a potentially cancelling coefficient sum.
