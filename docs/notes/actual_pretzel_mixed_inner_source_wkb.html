Mixed cusp-color inner packets: exact source and uniform holomorphic WKB

29 October 2026. The low core of the ACTUAL rotated circle of color j=N-h has a second-order selected recurrence at every integer h. Near the complete color it admits a source-fixed, nonzero analytic amplitude on any fixed high-index interval r/N>1/4. A literal zero in the endpoint recurrence also provides a uniformly holomorphic ratio interpolant which is EXACT at the physical r-grid. Optimal analytic Euler–Maclaurin gives an exponentially accurate action interpolant, with its residual source explicitly retained.

This is a theorem about the low core. The original upper band R P is the separate, nonzero term in the exact two-band theorem. It is not discarded or incorporated into the following amplitude. No mixed-color period, fixed filling, or knot-volume theorem follows without the remaining outer-source comparison.

1. Literal finite packets and their fixed factorial continuation

Use N>=2, d=N-1, q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, M=floor(d/2), F_a=(Q;Q)_a, s_a=|F_a|, and the original continuants f_v. For an INTEGER 0<=h<=M the low core is exactly

c_(N,h)=kappa^(-1)sum_(v=h)^M a_(h,v)f_v,
a_(h,v)=N^2 s_(2v)/[s_v^2 s_(v-h)s_(v+h)].

Its marked phase is not optional:

a_(h,v)x^v=N^2 q^(h^2)
                     F_(2v)/[F_v^2F_(v-h)F_(v+h)].

Interchanging the actual two finite sums therefore gives

kappa c_(N,h)(z)/N^2
   =q^(h^2)sum_(r=0)^M
     (-1)^r Q^(r(r+1)/2)P_r(z) A_(h,r)/F_r^3,

A_(h,r)=sum_(v=max(h,r))^M
   Q^(-vr)F_(2v)/[F_vF_(v-h)F_(v+h)F_(v-r)].       (1)

All original normalizations remain. For r>=h define

D_(h,r)=Q^(-r^2)F_(2r)/[F_rF_(r-h)F_(r+h)],
U_(h,r)=A_(h,r)/D_(h,r).

On a high interval r/N>=tau_0>1/4 and |chi| small, put h=N chi/pi. The source’s complex continuation means replacing ONLY F_(v-h),F_(v+h) in (1), with v=r,…,M, by the canonical Fhat_N(v-h),Fhat_N(v+h) of the exact original factorial interpolation. All these arguments stay in regular compact factorial substrips. The same replacement defines D_(h,r). This is holomorphic in complex chi on a fixed disk and agrees with every physical integer h there. No assertion about the off-grid continuation of the omitted low indices r<h is needed for this high-index theorem.

2. Exact finite transformation and the all-index Ward equation

For r>=h, n=M-r, the exact normalized finite sum is

U_(h,r)=sum_(k=0)^n
  (qQ^r;Q)_k(-qQ^r;Q)_k(-Q^(r+1);Q)_k Q^(-rk)
       /[F_k(Q^(r+1-h);Q)_k(Q^(r+1+h);Q)_k].       (2)

It is balanced because the product of its two lower parameters is independent of h. The OWN finite transformation (3) in the coherent inner proof applies exactly, with no absent amplitude. For example at odd N, choosing its lower parameters in the safe order gives

U_(h,r)=(qQ^h;Q)_n/(Q^(r+1+h);Q)_n
  *sum_(j=0)^n
   (Q^(2r+1);Q^2)_j(-Q^(-h);Q)_j Q^j
                /[(Q^(2r+2-2h);Q^2)_jF_j].        (3)

For physical integers r>=h all magnitudes on the second line are positive after its exact term phase (i qh)j is removed. The monotonicity used at h=0 is NOT asserted when h is macroscopic.

There is a stronger exact source equation. Write H=Q^h and T_h=H+H^(-1). For EVERY physical 0<=h<=M and 0<=r<=M,

QY^2(1-Q^2Y)A_(h,r+2)
  +Y(T_h Q^3Y^2+Q+1)A_(h,r+1)
  -Q(QY+1)(QY^2-1)A_(h,r)=0, Y=Q^r,            (4)

A_(h,M)=D_(h,M), A_(h,M+1)=0.

The equation includes r<h. For its direct proof use the summand in (1), z=Q^v and the rational certificate

g_h(z)=(1-z/H)(1-Hz)(1-z/Y)/z^2.

The v-step ratio is Y(-1)(1-Qz2)(1+Qz)/[(1-Qz/H)(1-QHz)(1-Qz/Y)]. Consequently the telescoping difference is

(1/(YQ^2)-1)z^(-2)
  +(1/(YQ)+H+H^(-1)+1/Y)z^(-1)
                              -1/(YQ)-1-(H+H^(-1))/Y.

The lower endpoint vanishes at z=H or z=Y, according to whether the actual sum starts at h or r. Its upper endpoint has F_(2M+2)=0. The r-step ratio is z(-1)-Y(-1), so the first two moments yield (4). On the high interval r>|Re h| the SAME proof holds for the specified complex-h factorial completion: the lower endpoint is z=Y and all shifted-factorial identities are exact. Thus this is the continuation of the original finite source, not a selected formal solution of an annihilator.

Normalize (4) by its actual D_(h,r). With

den_r=(1-QY/H)(1-QYH),

one obtains exactly

U_r=p_N U_(r+1)+q_N U_(r+2),
p_N=-Q^(-2)Y^(-1)(T_hQ^3Y^2+Q+1)/den_r,
q_N=-Q^(-4)Y^(-2)(1-Q^2Y)(1-Q^3Y^2)(1+Q^2Y)
                                         /(den_r den_(r+1)). (5)

Here den_(r+1)=(1-Q2Y/H)(1-Q2YH). At r=M-1, q_N=0 at BOTH parities, independently of H. Thus the selected source is U_M=1 and U_(M-1)=p_N(M-1); no value of D_(h,M+1) is divided out.

The recurrence is used where its displayed denominators are nonzero; this holds uniformly in the small complex cusp corridor below. At extreme colors outside that corridor one uses the uncanceled exact equation (4), rather than evaluating a spurious 0/0 at r=M.

3. A uniform dominant channel, with its endpoint included

Fix tau_0>1/4, tau_0<1/2. Put epsilon=1/N, tau=r/N, Y=exp(2pi i tau), H=exp(2i chi), and Q=exp(2pi i epsilon). Regard the rational coefficients (5) as analytic functions p(tau,chi,epsilon),q(tau,chi,epsilon). Let p_0,q_0 denote their values at epsilon=0. Explicitly,

p_0=-Y^(-1)(2cos(2chi)Y^2+2)
                          /[(1-Y/H)(1-YH)],
q_0=-Y^(-2)(1-Y^2)^2/[(1-Y/H)^2(1-YH)^2].

The roots of mu^2-p_0 mu-q_0=0 at chi=0 are

mu_1=-Y^(-1), mu_2=Y^(-1)cot(pi tau)^2.

On every compact real interval I contained in (1/4,3/4), there is a fixed complex neighborhood and a fixed chi disk on which the continuation mu=mu_1 is analytic, nonzero, and satisfies

|mu_2/mu|<=kappa<1, Re mu>=m>0.                   (6)

These hypotheses are CHECKABLE from the displayed rational quadratic: at chi=0 the ratio is cot(pi tau)^2<1 and Re(-Y^-1)>0; compactness and sufficiently small complex neighborhoods preserve both strict inequalities. All coefficient denominators stay nonzero. No eigenvalue selection from unknown period data occurs.

In particular tau=1/2 is INCLUDED: mu(1/2,chi)=1, q_0 has a double zero there, and the epsilon derivative q_1 also vanishes there. The eigenvalue and source frame do not become singular at the physical terminal point.

4. A holomorphic ratio interpolant which matches the source exactly

Choose sigma>0 small enough that [tau_0-sigma,1-tau_0+4sigma] lies strictly inside (1/4,3/4), and shrink its complex tube and the chi disk until (6) holds. Let

L_N=ceil((1/2-tau_0+2sigma)N),
F_tau(z)=p(tau,chi,epsilon)+q(tau,chi,epsilon)/z.

For tau in a fixed complex neighborhood of [tau_0,1/2], define

v_N(tau)=F_tau o F_(tau+epsilon) o ...
         o F_(tau+(L_N-1)epsilon)
                   (mu(tau+L_N epsilon)).          (7)

Every iterate is within C epsilon of its corresponding nonzero mu. Indeed F_tau(mu(tau+epsilon))-mu(tau)=O(epsilon), while its z derivative has modulus at most kappa+O(epsilon)<kappa’<1 on those disks. A sufficiently large C gives an invariant disk at each backwards step. This proves that (7) is uniformly holomorphic, nonzero and bounded, with a uniform bounded inverse.

At EVERY physical tau=r/N with tau_0N<=r<M, the continued fraction reaches r=M-1. There q_N=0 EXACTLY, so it forgets its entire remaining analytic tail and takes the value p_N(M-1). Hence

v_N(r/N)=U_r/U_(r+1) EXACTLY,                     (8)

and every physical high-index U_r is nonzero. This works also for complex chi in the fixed disk because the wall zero is independent of H. There is no assertion that v_N(M/N) equals a nonexistent physical ratio U_M/U_(M+1); the source product stops at M-1.

Moreover the Riccati defect of (7) is O((kappa’)^L_N): adding one future step changes its terminal value by O(epsilon), which is contracted by the preceding L_N steps. Thus the chosen terminal function cannot hide a macroscopic source change.

5. The selected action and a nonzero joint amplitude

All derivatives in the following formula are derivatives of the EXPLICIT rational functions (5). Let

p_1=partial_epsilon p|_0, q_1=partial_epsilon q|_0,
nu=[p_1+q_1/mu-q_0(partial_tau mu)/mu^2]
                                         /[1+q_0/mu^2].

The denominator equals 1-mu_2/mu and is bounded away from zero. The analytic trial ratio mu+epsilon nu has Riccati residual O(epsilon^2), obtained by Taylor expansion at tau+epsilon. Applying the same strict contraction as in section4 gives uniformly on a fixed complex neighborhood

v_N=mu+epsilon nu+O(epsilon^2).                   (9)

The principal log is well defined by Re v_N>0. Define

S(tau,chi)=integral_tau^(1/2) Log mu(s,chi) ds,
C(tau,chi)=sqrt(mu(tau,chi))
                  *exp[integral_tau^(1/2)nu(s,chi)/mu(s,chi)ds]. (10)

The square root is its branch equal to one at tau=1/2. Both are holomorphic on fixed neighborhoods, and C has a uniform nonzero lower bound on compact subsets. The physical selected packet is

U_(h,r)=C(r/N,chi)exp[N S(r/N,chi)](1+O(1/N)),     (11)

uniformly in physical r/N>=tau_0 and complex chi in a fixed small disk. The proof is to sum Log v_N, use (9) and one-step Euler–Maclaurin. Since Log mu(1/2,chi)=0, replacing the true endpoint M/N by 1/2 introduces O(1/N), at BOTH parities. There is no extra unknown endpoint matching constant. In particular U_M=1 is its original source normalization, not an imposed WKB scale.

At chi=0, the formulas calibrate against the independent finite transformation:

S(tau,0)=i pi(1/2-tau)^2,
C(tau,0)=sqrt(sin(pi tau))
                          *exp[-3pi i(1/2-tau)/2]. (12)

This calibration also follows directly without that transformation: substitution in the rational derivatives gives nu/mu=-(pi/2)cot(pi tau)-(5pi i/2), and Log mu=i pi-2pi i tau. Integrating these two expressions in (10) proves (12), with its constant fixed by C(1/2,0)=1.

Thus the joint amplitude is an analytic nonzero deformation of the already proved complete-color amplitude. This is not a formal normal-jet assertion: it is uniform for h up to delta N with a fixed sufficiently small delta, and for the displayed complex factorial completion.

6. Exponentially accurate analytic action, with residual source kept

This strengthening matters before taking an oscillatory outer sum. An O(1/N) error on individual real-grid terms alone cannot justify a complex saddle at a fixed nonzero chi.

The functions f_N=Log v_N are uniformly bounded and holomorphic on a FIXED larger complex neighborhood of [tau_0,1/2]. On an inner neighborhood there are constants B,a>0, independent of N, such that

|f_N^(k)|<=B k! a^(-k).

Put b_N=M/N and, for K_N=floor(pi a N/4), define the analytic Euler–Maclaurin expression

E_N(tau)=N integral_tau^b_N f_N(s)ds
    +(f_N(tau)-f_N(b_N))/2
    +sum_(k=1)^K_N B_(2k)/(2k)! N^(1-2k)
              [f_N^(2k-1)(b_N)-f_N^(2k-1)(tau)]. (13)

The Bernoulli polynomial remainder is bounded by C N (K_N/(pi a N))^(2K_N), after shrinking a if necessary. This follows from |B_(2k)(x)|<=4(2k)!/(2pi)^(2k) and the displayed Cauchy derivative bound. Hence, for some c>0,

U_(h,r)=exp[E_N(r/N)](1+R_(N,r)),
|R_(N,r)|<=C exp(-cN),                            (14)

uniformly on the physical high grid and the complex chi disk. There is NO approximation of source values prior to this bound: the product of the exact grid ratios (8) is precisely U_r. Its endpoint product is empty and equals one at r=M.

The interpolant E_N is holomorphic in complex tau,chi. Equations (9) and (13) also give, uniformly on an inner complex neighborhood,

exp(E_N(tau))=C(tau,chi)exp[N S(tau,chi)](1+O(1/N)). (15)

Indeed the higher Bernoulli terms in (13) sum to O(1/N), and f_N=Log mu+N(-1)nu/mu+O(N-2) there. The grid residual R is retained explicitly; it is not relabeled a homogeneous solution or zero current. One may use exp(E_N) in an outer analytic estimate ONLY after bounding that residual in the whole original sum.

For example on a fixed negative-w neighborhood and |chi| small, its total high-packet contribution is at most

poly(N)exp{N[j_0+C|chi|^2-c]},                    (16)

where j_0 is the complete-color real action envelope on that neighborhood. This follows by the original factorial estimates, the even analytic perturbation S(tau,chi)-S(tau,0)=O(chi^2), and (14); no unrestricted exterior operator norm is inserted. For a fixed small enough chi disk the error barrier survives any O(|chi|^2) action perturbation, in particular a smaller O(|chi|^4) loss from a nearby complex index saddle.

This supplies the quantitative source input for a finite-Poisson or cotangent contour argument in the outer r variable. Its endpoint currents, the lower r packets, and the R P upper color band still require their own estimates. No such current is erased by (14).

7. A full real-color upper envelope retains the lower indices

For physical integer h define V_(h,r)=q(h2)A_(h,r)/D_(0,r). Its normalized recurrence has EXACTLY the complete-color q_N and in the shifted variable Z=Q^(r+1) its other coefficient changes by

p_(h,N)-p_(0,N)=-Z(T_h-2)/(1-Z)^2.               (17)

For 0<=r<=tau_0N, the old full branch envelope rho=max(1,cot(pi(r+1)/N)^2) gives |p_h-p_0|/rho<=C|chi|^2, uniformly even at r=0. The globally conditioned frame of the all-index source bound therefore changes its one-step bound only by the factor 1+C|chi|^2. The selected high-index anchor (11), together with s_r^2/(s_(r-h)s_(r+h))<=1 for real physical h<=r<=M, is at most exp(CN chi^2) times a fixed polynomial. Iteration proves

|V_(h,r)|<=poly(N)exp{N[E(pi r/N)+C chi^2]},
E(u)=(2/pi)integral_u^(pi/4)log cot v dv for u<pi/4,
E(u)=0 for u>=pi/4.                              (18)

The exact mixed Ward (4) proves this also for r<h; its lower telescoping endpoint is then v=h. Thus no low-index terms have been omitted. At a fixed complete-color outer radius the strict old lower-index profile gap persists for sufficiently small |chi|. This is a controlled continuity estimate, not a claim that the second recurrence branch disappears.

Audit checks the original finite mixed coefficients, balanced transform, all-r Ward and normalized endpoint wall at both parities; verifies the holomorphic source ratios and the calibrated selected amplitudes.

The finite audit checks 17 complete finite-field families N=2,…,18, with EVERY admissible integer color and inner index, the original low-core marked phases and odd mixed finite transformation; four independent canonical complex-Fhat source cases; and six full selected analytic-WKB families at N=32,33,64,65,128,129. The last families include complex chi and compare the literal finite source, its exact continued-fraction ratios and its normalized nonzero amplitude. The exponential Euler–Maclaurin barrier is proved analytically above; finite tests are supplementary, not an inference of that barrier.

8. The actual analytic Newton factor has prefactor one

Let w=-exp eta, initially eta>0 real, and set

alpha_+=(N+1)/2-iN eta/(2pi),
alpha_-=(N+1)/2+iN eta/(2pi).

Then Q^(alpha_+)=qw and Q^(alpha_-)=q/w EXACTLY, with the negative-argument logarithmic marks retained. Define

Bhat_N(r,eta)=q^(-r^2)
   Fhat(alpha_++r-1)Fhat(alpha_-+r-1)
                 /[Fhat(alpha_+-1)Fhat(alpha_--1)]. (19)

At physical integer r it is EXACTLY the original Newton product product_(a<r)(c_a+2cosh eta). The claim below holds uniformly on fixed complex neighborhoods with tau=r/N in a compact subinterval of (0,1/2), eta near a fixed positive real value, and all four scaled factorial arguments in compact subsets of 0<Re a<1:

Bhat_N(N tau,eta)
  =exp{N/(2pi) integral_0^(2pi tau)
                          Log(2cosh eta+2cos v)dv}
                                                *[1+O(1/N)]. (20)

There is no missing periodic multiplier or square-root factor. Here is the branch calculation. Put a_+=1/2-i eta/(2pi), a_-=1/2+i eta/(2pi), and f(a)=[Li2(1)-Li2(exp(2pi i a))]/(2pi i). The canonical factorial expansion on its marked strip gives

Fhat_N(Na-1/2)=sqrt(iN) exp[Nf(a)] [1+O(1/N)].

Indeed the displacement contributes -f’(a)/2, where f’(a)=Log(1-exp(2pi i a)); it cancels the variable part of the factorial square-root prefactor. The remaining sqrt(iN) has the same fixed mark exp(i pi/4)sqrt N at all four arguments, so it cancels between numerator and denominator. In particular one must NOT reset Log[i(1-X)] to an unrelated principal logarithm.

The exponent of (19) divided by N is

f(a_++tau)+f(a_-+tau)-f(a_+)-f(a_-)-i pi tau^2.

Its tau derivative is

Log(1+exp(eta)Y)+Log(1+exp(-eta)Y)-2pi i tau
                           =Log(2cosh eta+2cos(2pi tau)).

At real 0<tau<1/2 the two arguments on the left have positive imaginary parts, their arguments sum to 2pi tau, and the argument on the right is positive. Thus the integer logarithmic ambiguity is ZERO. Holomorphic continuation gives the same identity on the specified complex neighborhood. Both primitives vanish at tau=0, which proves (20) with its constant.

This is sufficient for a fixed high-index Poisson window whose upper cutoff is STRICTLY below 1/2. It is not a uniform bulk asymptotic through tau=1/2: there an upper scaled factorial reaches the edge of its strip, and using a reset dilogarithm could lose a second-shift current. Physical indices beyond such a fixed cutoff must first be bounded by their strict profile gap, or their upper continuation treated separately. Formula (20) makes no such endpoint current vanish.
