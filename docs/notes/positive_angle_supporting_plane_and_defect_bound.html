A geometric supporting-plane bound for positive factorial energies with charge defects

28 October 2026. Complete positive geometry gives a quantitative upper certificate for the angle-factorial model even when the edge charges are not exact. Bounded integer edge defects cost only a polynomial factor. Sublinear defects preserve the sharp volume exponent. There is also a uniform Gaussian deficit away from the complete angle point.

This is a statement about the positive angle energy, with its specified normalization. It does not identify that energy with the original Kashaev period. In particular, the exact scalar in the original star–triangle identity is a FULL binomial, whereas the one-tetrahedron energy amplitude is its square root after division by N. Section 5 records that compulsory normalization distinction.

1. The complete geometric supporting plane

Fix a finite complete positive ideal triangulation T with t tetrahedra. Let x* be its angle vector in units of pi, and put

mu=min_i x*_i>0,
V(x)=sum_i Lambda(pi*x_i),
Lambda'(theta)=-log(2sin theta).

Let A be the tetrahedral sum matrix and E the global edge-angle incidence matrix. Thus

A x*=1, E x*=2*1, V(x*)=Vol(M).                (1)

No peripheral equation is required for the following upper bound. One may impose additional exact peripheral restrictions on the same angle variables; that only restricts the set being bounded. There is no claim that a modular peripheral condition becomes exact.

Define the full coordinate gradient

g_i=-pi*log(2sin(pi*x*_i)).

There are FIXED real vectors lambda,eta such that

g=A^t lambda+E^t eta.                         (2)

Indeed, complete geometric gluing is stationary for volume on the angle slice Ax=1,Ex=2. One direct check uses fixed cusp horospheres and ideal Schlaefli: a tangent v with Av=Ev=0 has dV(v)=-(pi/2)sum_e ell_e(Ev)_e=0, with the SAME truncated global edge length ell_e on all incident tetrahedra. Hence g annihilates ker(A,E), and elementary real linear algebra proves (2). Equivalently one can fix a row basis of (A,E), solve (2) once, and retain that certificate. Row dependencies make the certificate nonunique but do not change its pairing with a consistent charge defect.

For EVERY weak local angle point x>=0 with Ax=1,

V(x)<=Vol(M)+eta dot(Ex-2)
                  -c_*||x-x*||_2²,
c_*=pi³*mu/64>0.                             (3)

Thus the supporting-plane linear terms telescope exactly into the integral edge defects. They are not independent per-tetrahedron errors and do not require estimating a different critical branch.

2. An explicit global quadratic deficit

Here is a proof of the constant in(3), including boundary points. For one tetrahedron use independent coordinates x1,x2 and x3=1-x1-x2. Its negative Hessian is

pi² [[cot(pi*x1)+cot(pi*x3), cot(pi*x3)],
     [cot(pi*x3), cot(pi*x2)+cot(pi*x3)]].       (4)

Its determinant is pi^4, by the cotangent addition identity, and it is positive definite in the strict simplex. Along the FIRST HALF of the segment x_s=(1-s)x+s x, every coordinate is at least mu/2 and at most1-mu. Consequently each cotangent has absolute value at most2/(pimu), so the trace of(4) is at most 8pi/mu. Its least eigenvalue is therefore at least pi³*mu/8.

For a tetrahedral tangent v=(v1,v2,-v1-v2), ||v||_3²<=3(v1²+v2²). Thus its second derivative on that half-segment is at most -(pi³*mu/24)||v||_3². Integrate the Taylor remainder with weight1-s over0<=s<=1/2. Since the remaining part of the segment has nonpositive second derivative,

V(x*)+g dot(x-x*)-V(x)
             >=(pi³*mu/64)||x-x*||².          (5)

For a boundary endpoint, apply the argument before reaching the endpoint and then use continuity of Lambda. Summing over tetrahedra gives(5) for the whole vector. Substitution of(2) and Ax=Ax=1 proves(3). Global uniform strong concavity of the Hessian at every boundary point is NOT assumed; integrating its controlled first half is what gives the global deficit from the fixed interior x.

3. The actual factorial weights and bounded edge-charge errors

Use precisely

s_N(k)=product_(r=1)^k 2sin(pi*r/N),
w_N(k)=product_i s_N(k_i)^(-1), 0<=k_i<N.

The uniform estimate, including k=0 and N-1, is

log s_N(k)=-(N/pi)Lambda(pi*k/N)+O(log N).      (6)

For an explicit elementary error bound write log(2sin pi*x)=log x+log(1-x)+h(x), where h is smooth on[0,1]. The increasing log term has sum-integral error between0 and logN+1, the decreasing log term has absolute error at mostlogN, and h contributes at most its fixed total variation. Thus the constant in(6) is universal. In a fixed triangulation all weight errors are bounded by C_t logN+C’_t.

Suppose A k=N*1 and let

delta=E k-2N*1.

Combining(3),(6) gives the finite-N Gaussian majorant

w_N(k)<=C N^C_t exp[
   N Vol(M)/pi+eta dot(delta)/pi
      -c_*||k-Nx*||²/(pi*N)].                 (7)

All original zero-angle weights are retained. In particular, for ANY set of such configurations with ||delta||_infty<=R_N,

sum w_N(k)<=C N^C_t
   exp[N Vol(M)/pi+||eta||_1 R_N/pi].          (8)

The number of configurations is at most N^(3t) and has been absorbed in the fixed polynomial. Thus O(1) edge defects cost only a polynomial factor; R_N=o(N) preserves the sharp geometric upper exponent. Exact full2N edge charge is sufficient but is NOT necessary for this positive-energy estimate.

Here delta is the TRUE INTEGER difference E k-2N, not a distance between residues modulo2N. A bounded change of a residue need not give a bounded integer defect: for example the affine residue 2d changes to0 after adding only2, and0 permits zero or multiple edge windings. Thus robustness under bounded integer defects does not justify replacing the actual compatible quantum edge character by a nearby one. The source still has to supply the stated lift or an independent bound on its true supported defects.

If ||k/N-x*||>=epsilon, the same sum has the extra factor exp[-N c_*epsilon²/pi]. More quantitatively a radius squared larger than a fixed multiple of(R_N+logN)/N controls the Gaussian tail relative to the complete geometric exponential rate. This is a consequence of the support-plane certificate, not a phase selection or an assertion about the original quantum cycle.

The useful condition is even weaker than small defects in every edge: equation(7) controls any supported defect set for which sup eta dot(delta)=o(N), with the Gaussian deficit still retained.

4. Affine charge d=N-1 and further bounded local offsets

For the source-compatible tetrahedral charge A k=d1, set y=k/d and delta_d=E k-2d1. Then Ay=1 and Ey-2=delta_d/d. Equation(3) applies exactly to y. The arguments k_i/N and k_i/d differ by at most1/N. Integrating the two logarithmic endpoint singularities of Lambda gives the uniform modulus

|Lambda(u)-Lambda(v)|
  <=C |u-v|(1+|log|u-v||).

It contributes only O(logN) to the logarithm of the weight. Therefore the affine version of(7) is

w_N(k)<=C N^C_t exp[
   N Vol(M)/pi+(N/(pi*d)) eta dot(delta_d)
     -(c_*N/(pi*d²))||k-dx*||²].               (9)

Bounded or sublinear edge defects have the same conclusions as in(8), since N/d<=2 for N>=2. This retains flat tetrahedra and does not rely on endpoint vanishing.

More generally let the local sums be N+u_j, |u_j|<=U_N<N/2, and normalize each tetrahedral triple by its own local sum. The normalized edge defects differ from delta/N by O(U_N/N), with constants determined by the fixed incidence matrix. The factorial action changes by at most

C_t U_N[1+log(N/max(1,U_N))].                 (10)

Hence bounded local AND edge charge offsets still cost only a polynomial, and sublinear offsets still cost exp(o(N)). The Gaussian deficit in the normalized local angles remains valid. This extension requires the specified nonnegative angle support; it does not extend a factorial through a forbidden index N.

On a common allowed nonempty progression, the strict geometric partition satisfies Z_N(T)>=N^(-C)exp[N Vol(M)/pi] by rounding the complete strict point in its integer kernel. Consequently the bounded-defect positive energies above are at most a fixed polynomial times Z_N(T); the sublinear-defect energies are at most exp(o(N))Z_N(T).

4.1 A bounded-defect comparison exists at EVERY level

There is no lattice-progression obstruction for a sufficiently wide FIXED integer defect window. Put B=2 max_e sum_i E_(e,i), determined solely by the fixed edge incidence matrix, and define

Z_N^B=sum_{k>=0, A k=d*1, ||E k-2d*1||_infty<=B} w_N(k).

Then this sum is nonempty for every N>=2, and fixed constants give

C^-1 N^-C exp[N Vol(M)/pi]
  <= Z_N^B <= C N^C exp[N Vol(M)/pi].          (10a)

For nonemptiness, in each complete tetrahedral triple set k_1=floor(d x*_1), k_2=floor(d x*_2), k_3=d-k_1-k_2. All entries lie in[0,d]. Their errors relative to d x* lie in (-1,0] for the first two and [0,2) for the third. Hence every edge defect has absolute value at most B. This constructs an admissible state at each N, without a rationality assumption on x. For large N it is strictly positive and k/N=x+O(1/N). Smoothness of the coordinate Lobachevsky sum near x* and (6) give a single weight exp[N Vol(M)/pi+O(logN)], proving the lower bound. The upper bound is (9) summed with the fixed defect bound B. Increasing C handles the finitely many remaining levels.

Thus this bounded-defect positive model has the desired energy rate at all levels, independently of any exact-fiber progression. It may be used as an upper comparison target. Its polynomial two-sided estimate still proves nothing about K_N until an exact original-source comparison is established; neither nonemptiness nor rounding constructs that comparison or enforces the true edge lift.

5. The exact affine local weight is not its amplitude square root

There is a particularly direct connection to the original charged star. If a+b+c=d, root reflection gives

w_N(a,b,c)=1/[s_N(a)s_N(b)s_N(c)]
            =|[a+b choose a]_Q|/N.           (11)

The exact ORIGINAL source-preserving balanced star–triangle identity in the general fusion theorem has scalar modulus |[a+b choose a]_Q|, with its full phase and typed outer factors retained. It is the FULL binomial, namely N w_N, not the normalized energy amplitude sqrt(w_N).

For example at a=b=c=d/3, N=1 modulo3, the scalar divided by sqrt(w_N) has modulus

sqrt(N*|[a+b choose a]_Q|),
log of this ratio=N v_3/(2pi)+O(logN).          (12)

Here v_3=3Lambda(pi/3) is the regular ideal tetrahedron volume. Thus a uniform local one-tetrahedron comparison which simply replaces this scalar by sqrt(w_N) pays an exponential cost. Two-tetrahedron assignments, a genuinely different doubled contraction, or the exact exponentially varying internal gauges could compensate it. Their compensation at the ORIGINAL full source has to be proved; it cannot be inferred from the factorial identity(11) or absorbed into an unknown knot normalization.

This warning does not disprove the requested upper comparison. It keeps its amplitude/energy and source normalizations explicit. The subsequent exact length-frame calculation now supplies a constructive compensation: b_L=N^(1/4)B_L/sqrt(s_L) makes a balanced star scalar N^(-1/2) times its retained phase, and parallel fusion produces a polynomial times sqrt(w_N). The original singleton clock co-source also has polynomial norm in that frame. Thus the flat-frame ratio in (12) is not an obstruction to a properly transported metric. The compulsory dual anchor weights and the coherent global state norm remain; every diagonal clock metric fails the sharp budget in an actual complete figure-eight sector. The supporting-plane theorem supplies a general geometric bound once a suitable positive angle energy with small charge defects has actually been obtained from the source. It does not construct that source map or bound the norm of an unproved one.

6. Reproduction and scope

python3 experiments/audit_positive_angle_supporting_plane.py

The audit uses the irregular complete three-tetrahedron shape point, solves the nontrivial edge supporting-plane certificate, tests the explicit global quadratic deficit including boundary triples, checks factorial estimates with charge defects, and verifies(11). The uniform proof is(2)–(10), not the numerical tests. No broad original Kashaev upper bound or geometric survival is asserted.

For coverage only, the classical optimization framework is recorded in Rivin, Combinatorial optimization in geometry and Futer–Gueritaud, From angled triangulations to hyperbolic structures. The latter abstract explicitly describes the volume critical-point method on an angle polytope. Only those abstracts were consulted for coverage here; no proof or technique from either paper is an input to the supporting-plane, deficit, or finite-factorial arguments.
