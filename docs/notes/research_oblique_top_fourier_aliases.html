A convergent Fourier expansion on the translated state domains

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The exact oblique contour decomposition can be written as a sum over the integer Fourier charges, each occurring once with coefficient one, together with its retained relative boundary currents. On each translated top domain the factorial amplitude is uniformly nonzero on the entire domain. Charges sufficiently far out in their respective orthants have an arbitrarily small absolute exponential bound.

The Fourier integrals are complex and can cancel. The result supplies neither a nonzero geometric integral nor cancellation bounds for the relative boundary currents.

The complete proof sources are R, the exact oblique decomposition, and A, the literal factorial amplitude. Their pins appear below.

1. Keep the exact domains and boundary terms

Use R’s primitive cut-and-transfer coordinates in dimension m=c+1m=c+1, original literal interpolant IN(z)I_N(z), and bulk real chain ΩN\Omega_N. Let x*x^* be its geometric point, x0x^0 its strict real interior point, and ϵj=sgn⁡Im⁡xj*\epsilon_j=\operatorname{sgn}\operatorname{Im}x^*_j. The positive vector aa is R’s generic height vector.

For σ∈{±1}m\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^m, denote the translated top by Tσ,N={ΩN+N(x*−x0),σ=ϵ,ΩN+iNσa,σ≠ϵ.(1) T_{\sigma,N}= \begin{cases} \Omega_N+N(x^*-x^0),&\sigma=\epsilon,\\ \Omega_N+iN\sigma a,&\sigma\ne\epsilon . \end{cases} \qquad\text{(1)} Every top has the coordinate orientation inherited from ΩN\Omega_N, before multiplication by cσ=∏j(−σj)c_\sigma=\prod_j(-\sigma_j). Fix the geometric correcting charge q∈ℤmq\in\mathbb Z^m, and put JN(z)=e−2πiq⋅zIN(z),Kϵj(zj)=πcot⁡(πzj)−iπϵj. J_N(z)=e^{-2\pi i q\cdot z}I_N(z),\qquad K_{\epsilon_j}(z_j)=\pi\cot(\pi z_j)-i\pi\epsilon_j . Write ℬN\mathcal B_N for the sum of the three retained boundary contributions in R’s equation (24): the original boundary-band tori, the initial mixed rectangle current, and the signed translated side currents. This is their actual sum of integrals, not an estimated error. The exact identity is KN=∑σ∫Tσ,Ncσ(2πi)−mJN(z)∏jKϵj(zj)dz+ℬN.(2) K_N=\sum_\sigma\int_{T_{\sigma,N}} c_\sigma(2\pi i)^{-m}J_N(z) \prod_jK_{\epsilon_j}(z_j)\,dz+\mathcal B_N . \qquad\text{(2)} All fixed framing, pivotal and external normalization lines in R remain unchanged.

2. Exact Fourier charges of the polarized kernels

Set dσ=ϵ−σ2∈ℤm,qσ=q+dσ.(3) d_\sigma=\frac{\epsilon-\sigma}{2}\in\mathbb Z^m, \qquad q_\sigma=q+d_\sigma . \qquad\text{(3)} The elementary formula for cotangent proves the meromorphic identity cσ(2πi)−m∏jKϵj(zj)=exp⁡(−2πidσ⋅z)∏j(1−exp⁡(2πiσjzj)).(4) c_\sigma(2\pi i)^{-m}\prod_jK_{\epsilon_j}(z_j) =\frac{\exp(-2\pi i d_\sigma\cdot z)} {\prod_j(1-\exp(2\pi i\sigma_jz_j))}. \qquad\text{(4)} For one coordinate, when σ=ϵ\sigma=\epsilon its normalized factor is 1/(1−e2πiϵz)1/(1-e^{2\pi i\epsilon z}). When σ=−ϵ\sigma=-\epsilon, it is e−2πiϵz/(1−e−2πiϵz)e^{-2\pi i\epsilon z}/(1-e^{-2\pi i\epsilon z}). These are the four possible sign cases and prove (4), including the rectangle orientations.

On each top, σjIm⁡zj>0\sigma_j\operatorname{Im}z_j>0 with a fixed lower bound proportional to NN. The denominator of (4) therefore has its absolutely and uniformly convergent geometric expansion. Consequently (2) becomes KN=∑σ∈{±1}m∑r∈ℤ≥0m∫Tσ,NIN(z)exp⁡{−2πi(qσ−σr)⋅z}dz+ℬN.(5) K_N= \sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^m}\ \sum_{r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m} \int_{T_{\sigma,N}} I_N(z)\exp\{-2\pi i(q_\sigma-\sigma r)\cdot z\}\,dz +\mathcal B_N . \qquad\text{(5)} Products such as σr\sigma r are coordinatewise. Uniform absolute convergence on the compact chain at each fixed NN justifies termwise integration.

The charges in (5) form a disjoint partition of ℤm\mathbb Z^m. In one coordinate, the two sets are {qj−ϵjr:r≥0},{qj+ϵj(1+r):r≥0}.(6) \{q_j-\epsilon_jr:r\ge0\}, \qquad \{q_j+\epsilon_j(1+r):r\ge0\}. \qquad\text{(6)} They partition the integers. Taking products proves the claim. Thus (5) is a genuine complete integer Fourier expansion, with a specified translated integration domain for each charge. It is not a collection of independent unknown lattice-period coefficients.

The geometric charge qq occurs at σ=ϵ,r=0\sigma=\epsilon,\ r=0, with coefficient exactly one. This statement concerns its explicit Fourier integral. It does not say that a local geometric steepest-descent cycle occurs in that integral with coefficient one.

3. Uniform amplitudes on the whole translated tops

All normalized real domains ΩN/N\Omega_N/N lie in one fixed bounded set. On any chosen top, every normalized factorial length has a fixed nonzero imaginary part, independent of the real point of that domain. The same holds for the cut coordinate. Hence the union, over all sufficiently large NN, of the normalized tops has a compact neighborhood on which every such length lies in 𝒟=ℂ\((−∞,0]∪[1,∞)).(7) \mathcal D=\mathbb C\setminus((-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty)). \qquad\text{(7)} There are only finitely many tops and factor forms. This gives simultaneous compact sets, with a separate neighborhood component when necessary.

A supplies its uniform shifted factorial expansion on these compact sets. Its chosen branches are fixed on 𝒟\mathcal D. On every compact subset of (7), both arguments remain in fixed closed sectors away from the negative real axis. The remainder is uniform. The identity (N−1Ny)=(1−y)(NNy)\binom{N-1}{Ny}=(1-y)\binom N{Ny} gives the displayed finite correction. None of its amplitude factors vanish on the compact sets under consideration.

Multiplying (8), the cc actual crossing expansions, and their retained monomial and pivotal factors proves IN(Nx)=N−(c+1)/2A0(x)eNϕ(x)(1+O(N−1)),A0(x)≠0.(9) I_N(Nx)=N^{-(c+1)/2}A_0(x)e^{N\phi(x)} (1+O(N^{-1})), \qquad A_0(x)\ne0 . \qquad\text{(9)} It holds uniformly on the entire normalized top domains and on fixed neighborhoods of them, with every fixed holomorphic derivative on smaller neighborhoods. Every signed top has leading normalized kernel coefficient +1+1; its differing exponential penalty is part of the explicit integer-alias phase.

4. A uniform tail bound for the Fourier charges

Choose v*>0v_*>0 such that σjIm⁡zj≥Nv*\sigma_j\operatorname{Im}z_j\ge Nv_* on every top and every coordinate. Put Cσ,N=∫Tσ,N|IN(z)e−2πiqσ⋅z||dz|. C_{\sigma,N}= \int_{T_{\sigma,N}} |I_N(z)e^{-2\pi iq_\sigma\cdot z}|\,|dz|. Therefore ∑|r|1≥R|∫Tσ,NIN(z)e−2πi(qσ−σr)⋅zdz|≤Cσ,Ne−γNR/2(1−e−γN/2)m.(12) \sum_{|r|_1\ge R} \left|\int_{T_{\sigma,N}} I_N(z)e^{-2\pi i(q_\sigma-\sigma r)\cdot z}\,dz\right| \le C_{\sigma,N} \frac{e^{-\gamma NR/2}}{(1-e^{-\gamma N/2})^m}. \qquad\text{(12)} For any fixed Λ>0\Lambda>0, choose a fixed integer RR with γR/2>C*+Λ\gamma R/2>C_*+\Lambda. Summing (12) over the finitely many tops then gives O(e−ΛN)O(e^{-\Lambda N}), after absorbing the polynomial factor using the strict inequality. Thus only finitely many Fourier integrals are needed at any prescribed exponential accuracy. This is a finite-charge reduction, not a theorem of finitely many exceptional Dehn fillings.

The finitely many remaining integrals still require their genuine contour analysis. Their coefficients being one does not make the integrals nonnegative. The geometric charge retains its centralizer direction. Nor has (12) estimated any term in ℬN\mathcal B_N. Those distinctions are essential to the outstanding volume-conjecture lower bound.
