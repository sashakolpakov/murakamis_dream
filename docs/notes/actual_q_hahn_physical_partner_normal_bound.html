A polynomial bound for the actual partner normal on the physical grid

Subsequent complex estimate: the near-fold theorem controls the full paired normal on a fixed complex compact when (l-j) log N=o(N). It is a relative upper bound in a shrinking scaled channel window; fixed nonzero scaled shifts and a leading normal amplitude remain open. The theorem below retains its original scope.

30 October 2026. Every original physical magnetic entry of a complete double Casimir channel has a polynomially bounded confluent normal relative to its nonzero ordinary value. This includes both halves of every physical degree block, both parities, and every complete double channel. The estimate is for the ACTUAL generic divided difference. It is not an estimate obtained by differentiating a root-only formula.

The argument is a positive-imaginary-part estimate for a half-prefix of the original complex symmetric Casimir matrix, followed by its exact reflection symmetry through first order at a double spectral node. No complex magnetic continuation, global magnetic contour, or whole marked-source estimate follows from this physical-grid statement.

1. The source and the quantitative statement

Put theta=pi/N, q=exp(i theta), Q=q^2, d=N-1, x=q^d. Let

0<=j<l, j+l=d, l<=t<=d, 0<=u<=t,
c_j=q^(2j+1)+q^(-2j-1),
Delta_j=q^(2j+1)-q^(-2j-1).

All these indices are integers. Let C_(j,u) be the original root column normalized by C_(j,0)=1. Let w_(j,u) be its actual confluent normal as specified by the mixed compiler:

w_(j,u)=[D C_(j,u)-D C_(l,u)]/[-2N Delta_j],
D=zeta d/dzeta, evaluated at zeta=q.                (1)

Both generic columns in (1) use the original highest weight d and its full factorial ratio. Then EVERY C_(j,u) is nonzero and

|w_(j,u)/C_(j,u)|<=N^6/256+N^2/8.                 (2)

If t/N stays in a fixed compact subset of (0,1), the right side can be replaced by C_K N^4. In the present complete range t>=l this only requires a fixed upper separation from t/N=1. No separation from the folded channel is required.

2. The actual prefix has positive imaginary part

The original degree-t Casimir block J_t is complex symmetric tridiagonal, as proved directly from the balanced tensor operators in the complete Hermite packet. For r=0,…,t put U_r=Q^r, T=Q^t. Its entries simplify exactly to

D_r=U_r/T [2(q^(-1)+qT)-(q+q^(-1))U_r],
A_r=(U_r-T)(1-QU_r)/T,             0<=r<t.          (3)

Every A_r is nonzero. For 1<=m<=floor(t/2), let J_[m] be its leading m-by-m principal submatrix. Because the matrix is symmetric, its Hermitian imaginary part is the entrywise imaginary-part matrix. Set h_r=(t-2r)theta and c=-cos((t+1)theta). Its diagonal and upper offdiagonal imaginary parts, including the next boundary coefficient, are

d_r=4 sin(h_r)[c+cos(theta)cos(h_r)],
o_r=4 sin(h_r-theta)sin((t-r)theta)sin((r+1)theta).
                                                           (4)

For 0<=r<m these o_r are positive. Introduce positive weights

a_r=1/sin((t-r)theta),          0<=r<=m.

A direct sine expansion gives the EXACT weighted row identity

d_r-o_(r-1)a_(r-1)/a_r-o_r a_(r+1)/a_r
  =4 sin(r theta)sin^2(theta)sin(t theta)
                     /sin((t-r-1)theta) >=0,       (5)

where the r=0 term involving o_(-1) is absent. The right side is zero at r=0. All denominators in (5) are positive in the stated range. One elementary verification expands sin(b-a) and cos(a+b+theta), with a=r theta and b=(t-r)theta. After multiplying by sin(b-theta)/4, the residual is precisely sin(a)sin^2(theta)sin(a+b).

Conjugate Im J_[m] by diag(1,-1,1,…), so its offdiagonals are -o_r. For a vector v write v_r=a_r y_r, and set y_m=0. Equation (5) expresses its quadratic form as

sum_(r=0)^(m-1) k_r |y_r-y_(r+1)|^2
       +sum_(r=0)^(m-1) V_r a_r^2 |y_r|^2,

k_r=o_r a_r a_(r+1)
   =4 sin((t-2r-1)theta)sin((r+1)theta)
                              /sin((t-r-1)theta),
V_r=the nonnegative right side of (5).             (6)

There is a genuine Dirichlet term at the right endpoint; it is not discarded when the prefix is taken. The conductances obey

k_r>=4 sin(theta).                                (7)

Indeed the two numerator sine arguments alpha,beta are at least theta, with alpha+beta=(t-r)theta<pi. On the interval theta<=alpha<=alpha+beta-theta, their product is minimized at an endpoint, so sin(alpha)sin(beta)>=sin(theta) sin(alpha+beta-theta), which is (7).

Since a_r<=1/sin(theta) and sum|y_r|2<=m2 sum|y_r-y_(r+1)|^2, equations (6),(7) prove

Im J_[m] >= [4 sin^3(theta)/m^2] I.               (8)

The sign-conjugation is unitary and does not change this lower bound. For t/N in a fixed compact inside (0,1), all a_r in this half-prefix are uniformly bounded; the stronger lower bound is c_K/N^3.

3. Lower-half spectral derivatives

Let f_r(z) be the original normalized forward column,

f_0=1, f_r(z)=det(zI-J_[r])/(A_0 ... A_(r-1)).      (9)

The real point c_j is a double eigenvalue of J_t. The actual source and normal are exactly

C_(j,r)=f_r(c_j),             w_(j,r)=f_r'(c_j).    (10)

This is the generic-confluence identity in the complete Hermite packet and the mixed compiler: differentiating the two normalized generic columns cancels the common q derivative and retains the original eigenvalue-gap derivative -2N Delta_j.

For ANY real z, (8) gives

||(zI-J_[m])^(-1)||<=m^2/[4 sin^3(theta)].

In particular the determinant in (9) is nonzero there. Taking its logarithmic derivative gives

|f_m'(z)/f_m(z)|
   =|tr((zI-J_[m])^(-1))|
   <=m^3/[4 sin^3(theta)].                        (11)

Since m<=N/2 and sin(theta)>=2/N, (11) is at most N^6/256. The compact-degree improvement of (8) gives C_K N^4 instead. For m=0 the normal is zero and the value is 1.

4. Reflection extends the bound through the full physical block

Let P reverse the indices 0,…,t. Formula (3) gives exactly

P J_t P=conjugate(J_t).                           (12)

The characteristic polynomial is

p_(t+1)(z)=product_(m=0)^t(z-c_m),

and the forward column satisfies

(J_t-zI)f(z)=-p_(t+1)(z)e_t/(A_0 ... A_(t-1)).     (13)

At a double node c_j, both the residual and its first derivative vanish. Reflecting and conjugating (13), and using uniqueness of the forward recurrence with its first component fixed, therefore gives, at that node,

f_(t-r)=f_t conjugate(f_r),
f'_(t-r)=f'_t conjugate(f_r)+f_t conjugate(f'_r).    (14)

This is a first-order identity at a DOUBLE node. It is not inferred by differentiating a value identity known only at isolated points. One may justify (14) as an identity modulo (z-c_j)^2: the reflected column has first entry conjugate(f_t(conjugate z)); its first-row error is a multiple of p_(t+1)(z), so its forward recurrence agrees to this order. Setting r=t in the value identity gives |f_t|=1.

The endpoint normal has a particularly simple original generic calculation. At u=t only a=m survives in the generic Clebsch sum, and its highest-vector ratio R_(m,m) is identically 1. Thus

C_(m,t)(zeta)=(-1)^m
          zeta^[d(t-2m)+m(m-1)].                 (15)

The two exponents for j,l differ by N(l-j). Combining (15) with the actual gap in (1) gives

f'_t(c_j)/f_t(c_j)=-(l-j)/(2 Delta_j),
|f'_t/f_t|<N/[4 sin(theta)]<=N^2/8.               (16)

For r>t/2 apply (14) with t-r in the lower half. Its value is nonzero and

f'_r/f_r=f'_t/f_t+conjugate(f'_(t-r)/f_(t-r)).       (17)

Equations (11),(16),(17) prove (2) for every physical entry.

5. Scope of the estimate

The paired normal, including its formerly hidden large-channel contribution, is controlled on the entire physical grid. Its exponential rate there cannot exceed that of its ordinary column by more than O(log N)/N. This is a relative entrywise statement; it does not control cancellations after a whole graph is sewn.

The high-total-degree blocks are transported by the constant Chevalley-plus-flip adapter in the complete Hermite packet. Thus the same root spectral-column estimate applies after the stated physical index translation. It is the root confluent column being transported here, not a new generic-q adapter assumed to be flat.

The estimate does NOT extend by continuity to a fixed complex magnetic neighborhood. The canonical large-partner continuation has the additional degree poles proved in the generic collision note, and its finite tail requires a separate complex estimate. A bound on integer magnetic samples, even at all levels, supplies no such analytic continuation theorem.

Focused audit: original normal, positive prefix and reflection, with saved results. The symbolic weighted-row identity passed. Exact arithmetic checked 131 original Casimir blocks, 726 weighted-row identities, 8,578 actual generic paired normals and their first-order reflections, and 571 original endpoint normals. At 90 digits, 10,164 actual relative-normal bounds passed; the audit also checked 288 positive prefix matrices and 2,384 conductance inequalities. These finite checks supplement the uniform proof above.

The high-degree adapter transports the root spectral column without assuming that an individual generic derivative is flat. The theorem is restricted to the physical grid.

No complex magnetic continuation or whole-source bound is inferred.
