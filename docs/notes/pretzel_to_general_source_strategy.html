Extending the proved pretzel argument to general source networks

29 October 2026. The main route is the proved pretzel mechanism: estimate the whole original source, prove survival of its geometric contribution, and identify that contribution with the actual complete or filled hyperbolic metric. The cube calculations and alternative positive circuits are retained as tests and possible ingredients. They do not replace the general objective.

What is already proved in the pretzel case

The complete augmented pretzel theorem covers every fixed odd width s>=3, both level parities and all original normal terms. Its explicitly weighted Fourier energy has the sharp volume rate. The original marked row has polynomial dual cost, and a separate coherent calculation proves a nonzero period with only a polynomial overlap loss. Weighted Cauchy gives an upper bound; that last calculation supplies the matching lower bound.

The filled-source proof, with the whole-color width improvement, covers fixed odd widths s>=9 with independently sufficiently long fixed signed fillings. The geometric part is the Casson–Rivin equality proof on the actual filled-slope angle slice, including smooth end completion. It does not maximize the unfilled volume instead.

The Karamata suggestion led to useful positive-energy and transport questions. The separate Tauberian criterion is not a missing hypothesis silently assumed in the pretzel proof: the latter establishes its whole-source estimates and nonzero asymptotic directly. A cumulative Tauberian law alone would still need cross-level control to give a limit at every N.

A general part of the weighted-Cauchy construction is now complete

On every supported six-head source cell the prescribed positive correlated Gram H has actual costs

||v_j||_H^2=K_j,
||lambda_j||_(H^-1)^2=tau_j^2 K_j.

Here tau_j is the explicitly evaluated positive marked top moment. The whole-support theorem now proves K_j<=poly(N) uniformly over EVERY supported folded triple, including saturation boundaries, high partners, simple axes, and both parities. This removes the former central-corridor restriction. The Gram is determined by the original local source and marked row, before choosing an exterior graph.

Its proof compares the entire finite Racah sum, including its second-root-zero terms, to a positive midpoint term. A global sine-product inequality replaces the need for a uniform tail gap. Thus this step transfers beyond the one-dimensional pretzel corridor.

The exact three-port sewing identity also already holds for the whole complete-circle source:

Z_new=sum_j Z_A(j) Z_B(j)/Z_theta(j).

The sum is over supported triples and Z_theta(j)>0. Defining F_A(j)=Z_A(j)/sqrt(Z_theta(j)) and similarly F_B gives the actual bilinear contraction sum_j F_A(j)F_B(j), and therefore

|Z_new|^2 <= (sum_j |F_A(j)|^2)(sum_j |F_B(j)|^2).

All shared colors remain correlated. This algebraic rewrite neither computes the two energies nor ensures that their bilinear overlap is nonzero. After the three complete circles act, each regular cell is observed through its top monomial; local Gram conditioning must not be presented as a bound on the exterior’s unknown scalar response.

The remaining general theorem must control the transported energy

For an original graph source written as K_N=b_N v_N, a useful metric must establish both

b_N G_N^-1 b_N^* <= exp(o(N)),
v_N^* G_N v_N <= exp[N Vol(M)/pi+o(N)].

It must also prove geometric survival, equivalently no exponential loss in the original source pairing at the geometric contribution. The pretzel proof does all three jobs in its explicit Fourier frame. For branching, the unresolved operation is transport between different pair decompositions while retaining the correlated metric, phases, boundary currents and common physical normal parameter.

Polynomial local costs alone do not make this transport isometric. Nor can one multiply local theta rates and omit a global contribution: the actual graph geometry has volume 4b v8+2 Vol(Q). The positive cube period explicitly detects that extra core term in a specified original sector. It is a useful test of any proposed sector-wise energy bound, while its full color sum remains an independent task.

The geometric conclusion has a general finite certificate

The source-volume certificate applies beyond pretzels once the actual source supplies a positive angle point on the actual triangulation, an absolute equality of its action with tetrahedral volume, and stationarity on the FULL edge and peripheral angle slice. Strict concavity then gives its unique global maximum; the decoration and end checks give the actual hyperbolic metric. A stationary point on a smaller source slice is insufficient.

The next decisive estimate is therefore the sharp energy of the coherently transported source, with a nonzero geometric overlap. The full-support boundary theorem is a completed input to that work; it is not yet the general volume equality.

New local transport and geometry inputs

The general actual inverse kernel now has a source-normalized one-sum expression and an exact finite conversion to the seven-factorial convention. Its row/column gauge to the four-face scalar normalization is explicit. The supported scalar theorem keeps the common root phase and bounds every fixed generic normal of the low-fold scalar at polynomial relative cost.

The local hyperideal geometry proves the actual volume and all six length derivatives of that normalized action. This supplies a sharp local candidate density for weighted Cauchy. It is not the full confluent operator metric: paired coefficients have genuine poles before their partners are combined, and the high-partner [2N] terms have nonzero normal currents.

The four supported faces alone also fail to determine a global geometric saddle: every interior color derivative of the normalized action is positive. The missing global incidence/boundary constraints are therefore substantive, not just a volume normalization constant.

The positive Conway variational theorem retains a complementary route with exact global support. It proves an all-level whole-source limit and a concave angle problem for every fixed robust expression. Its integer-semigroup argument gives every-level survival directly, but its angle problem still needs an actual geometric gluing certificate.
