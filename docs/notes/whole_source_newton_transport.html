Whole-source Newton transport and the finite-root normal lattice

This note proves a boundary quantum-torus calculation, retaining the whole meridian variable and its normal directions. It does not yet assert that an arbitrary marked crossing or bridge recoupling has been realized by these operators. In particular, it does not prove the remaining triple-to-cyclotomic transform in the 525_2 source reduction.

No literature proof is used. Comparisons with torus skeins or rank-one Askey–Wilson algebras supply background terminology only.

The independent lattice/cut audit gives a second ring proof and checks the physical derivative convention.

1. A source basis and three banded operators

Initially let QQ be an indeterminate and work in k[Y,Y−1]k[Y,Y^{-1}], where k=ℚ(Q)k=\mathbb Q(Q). Set

X=Y+Y−1,tn(Y)=∏r=1n(X−Qr−Q−r),vn(Y)=(1−Y)tn(Y). X=Y+Y^{-1},\qquad t_n(Y)=\prod_{r=1}^n(X-Q^r-Q^{-r}),\qquad v_n(Y)=(1-Y)t_n(Y).

The lower-case tnt_n is a Newton polynomial. The upper-case TT denotes the shift

(Tf)(Y)=f(QY),TY=QYT. (Tf)(Y)=f(QY),\qquad TY=QYT.

The following product fixes every factor and power:

vn(Y)=−Yn+1(Q−n/Y;Q)2n+1. v_n(Y)=-Y^{n+1}(Q^{-n}/Y;Q)_{2n+1}. \tag{1.1}

Define

S=T+QT−1,Z=YT+QY−1T−1, S=T+QT^{-1},\qquad Z=YT+QY^{-1}T^{-1},

and

an=Qn+Q−n,γn=Qn+1+Q−n,δn=Q−2n(1−Q2n)(1−Q2n+1). a_n=Q^n+Q^{-n},\quad \gamma_n=Q^{n+1}+Q^{-n},\quad \delta_n=Q^{-2n}(1-Q^{2n})(1-Q^{2n+1}).

With v−1=0v_{-1}=0, the exact Laurent-polynomial identities are

Xvn=vn+1+an+1vn, \boxed{Xv_n=v_{n+1}+a_{n+1}v_n,} \tag{1.2}

Svn=γnvn+δnvn−1, \boxed{Sv_n=\gamma_n v_n+\delta_n v_{n-1},} \tag{1.3}

Zvn=Qn+1vn+1+(1+Q)Q2n+1vn+Qnδnvn−1. \boxed{Zv_n=Q^{n+1}v_{n+1} +(1+Q)Q^{2n+1}v_n+Q^n\delta_n v_{n-1}.} \tag{1.4}

These hold over ℤ[Q,Q−1]\mathbb Z[Q,Q^{-1}]. They therefore specialize at every nonzero QQ, including Q=−1Q=-1, without a 1−Q21-Q^2 denominator.

Proof

The first identity is the defining Newton recurrence. Let

(Jf)(Y)=−Yf(Y−1). (Jf)(Y)=-Yf(Y^{-1}).

Then J2=1J^2=1, Jvn=vnJv_n=v_n, and

JTJ=QT−1,JYJ=Y−1. JTJ=QT^{-1},\qquad JYJ=Y^{-1}.

Thus S,ZS,Z preserve the JJ-fixed Laurent polynomials. These are exactly (1−Y)k[X](1-Y)k[X]: evaluation at Y=1Y=1 gives divisibility by 1−Y1-Y, and after division the quotient is invariant under Y↔Y−1Y\leftrightarrow Y^{-1}.

For generic QQ, (1.1) shows that SvnSv_n is divisible by vn−1v_{n-1}. The quotient is invariant under inversion and has Laurent degree at most one, so it is affine in XX. Comparing the top coefficient vn=−Yn+1+⋯v_n=-Y^{n+1}+\cdots gives the coefficient γn\gamma_n of vnv_n. Evaluation at Y=QnY=Q^n then gives

δn=vn(Qn+1)vn−1(Qn)=Q2n+1(1−Q−2n)(1−Q−2n−1)=Q−2n(1−Q2n)(1−Q2n+1). \delta_n =\frac{v_n(Q^{n+1})}{v_{n-1}(Q^n)} =Q^{2n+1}(1-Q^{-2n})(1-Q^{-2n-1}) =Q^{-2n}(1-Q^{2n})(1-Q^{2n+1}).

For n=0n=0, (1.3) is directly S(1−Y)=(1+Q)(1−Y)S(1-Y)=(1+Q)(1-Y). This proves (1.3) generically and hence as an integral Laurent identity. Finally,

(1−Q2)Z=XS−QSX. (1-Q^2)Z=XS-QSX. \tag{1.5}

Substituting (1.2)–(1.3) and cancelling this polynomial factor before specialization gives (1.4). It does not divide by 1−Q21-Q^2 after setting Q=−1Q=-1.

2. The exact finite-root corner

Let QQ now be a primitive NN-th root, N≥2N\ge2, in a characteristic-zero field kk. Introduce

A=k[U,U−1],B=A[Y]/(YN−U)=k[Y,Y−1]. A=k[U,U^{-1}],\qquad B=A[Y]/(Y^N-U)=k[Y,Y^{-1}]. \tag{2.1}

The last equality specifies the AA-algebra structure U=YNU=Y^N. The power basis is 1,Y,…,YN−11,Y,\ldots,Y^{N-1}. The shift TT is AA-linear and TN=1T^N=1. Put

W=U+U−1−2=(U−1)2U. W=U+U^{-1}-2=\frac{(U-1)^2}{U}.

There is the exact product identity

tN=W,tn+N=Wtn,vn+N=Wvn. t_N=W,\qquad t_{n+N}=Wt_n,\qquad v_{n+N}=Wv_n. \tag{2.2}

Indeed

YNtN(Y)=∏r=1N(Y−Qr)(Y−Q−r)=(YN−1)2, Y^Nt_N(Y)=\prod_{r=1}^N(Y-Q^r)(Y-Q^{-r}) =(Y^N-1)^2,

and the factors repeat with period NN. Define

L=⨁n=0N−1Avn⊂B. L=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{N-1}A v_n\subset B. \tag{2.3}

Independence is proved in section 3. Equations (1.2)–(1.4), with vN=Wv0v_N=Wv_0, give banded N×NN\times N matrices on this whole-source lattice. In column convention, the columns 0≤n<N−10\le n<N-1 are exactly (1.2)–(1.4), while the last columns are

XvN−1=Wv0+2vN−1, Xv_{N-1}=Wv_0+2v_{N-1}, \tag{2.4}

SvN−1=γN−1vN−1+δN−1vN−2, Sv_{N-1}=\gamma_{N-1}v_{N-1} +\delta_{N-1}v_{N-2}, \tag{2.5}

ZvN−1=Wv0+(1+Q)Q−1vN−1+Q−1δN−1vN−2. Zv_{N-1}=Wv_0+(1+Q)Q^{-1}v_{N-1} +Q^{-1}\delta_{N-1}v_{N-2}. \tag{2.6}

At n=0n=0, the lower term is zero since δ0=0\delta_0=0. For N=2N=2, contributions in the same row are added.

The corner Wv0Wv_0 is retained. It vanishes to order two at U=1U=1, rather than being identically zero; deleting it changes second and higher normal jets.

3. The normal lattice and its saturation

The involution JJ acts on B=k[Y,Y−1]B=k[Y,Y^{-1}], but is semilinear over AA:

J(a(U)f)=a(U−1)Jf. J(a(U)f)=a(U^{-1})Jf. \tag{3.1}

It becomes AA-linear modulo U2−1U^2-1. There is the exact characterization

L={f∈B:Jf−f∈(U2−1)B}. \boxed{L=\{f\in B:Jf-f\in(U^2-1)B\}.} \tag{3.2}

In particular,

(U2−1)B⊂L⊂B. \boxed{(U^2-1)B\subset L\subset B.} \tag{3.3}

Since the Newton polynomials are monic, this AA-span is independent of the choice of primitive QQ:

L=(1−Y)span⁡A{1,X,…,XN−1}. L=(1-Y)\operatorname{span}_A\{1,X,\ldots,X^{N-1}\}. \tag{3.4}

Proof of (3.2)–(3.3)

Every JJ-fixed Laurent polynomial is (1−Y)(1-Y) times a polynomial in XX. Expand in the monic Newton basis and fold using (2.2); it belongs to LL. The inclusion from left to right in (3.2) follows from (3.1) and a(U−1)−a(U)∈(U2−1)Aa(U^{-1})-a(U)\in(U^2-1)A.

For any h∈Bh\in B, use its kk-linear JJ-eigenspace decomposition h=h++h−h=h_++h_-. We have h+∈Lh_+\in L, and

J((U−U−1)h−)=(U−U−1)h−. J\big((U-U^{-1})h_-\big)=(U-U^{-1})h_-.

Thus (U−U−1)h−∈L(U-U^{-1})h_-\in L; multiplication by the unit UU shows (U2−1)h−∈L(U^2-1)h_-\in L. This proves (3.3). If Jf−f∈(U2−1)BJf-f\in(U^2-1)B, then f−=(f−Jf)/2∈(U2−1)B⊂Lf_-=(f-Jf)/2\in(U^2-1)B\subset L, and f+∈Lf_+\in L. This proves (3.2).

Independence and determinant

Over an algebraic closure of k(U)k(U), write yj=Qjyy_j=Q^j y, yN=Uy^N=U, and xj=yj+yj−1x_j=y_j+y_j^{-1}. The xjx_j are distinct away from U2=1U^2=1: equality for distinct i,ji,j would force yiyj=1y_i y_j=1, hence U2=1U^2=1. Also 1−yj≠01-y_j\ne0 away from U=1U=1. The monic Newton evaluation matrix multiplied by diag⁡(1−yj)\operatorname{diag}(1-y_j) is therefore invertible generically. This proves independence over AA and spanning over k(U)k(U).

Let MNM_N be the coefficient matrix of v0,…,vN−1v_0,\ldots,v_{N-1} in 1,Y,…,YN−11,Y,\ldots,Y^{N-1}, with increasing columns. Then

det⁡MN=∏j(1−yj)∏i<jxj−xiyj−yi=(1−U)∏i<j(1−(yiyj)−1)={(1−U)(1−U−2)(N−1)/2,N odd,−U(1−U−2)N/2,N even. \begin{aligned} \det M_N &=\prod_j(1-y_j)\prod_{i<j}\frac{x_j-x_i}{y_j-y_i}\\ &=(1-U)\prod_{i<j}(1-(y_i y_j)^{-1})\\ &=\boxed{\begin{cases} (1-U)(1-U^{-2})^{(N-1)/2},&N\text{ odd},\\ -U(1-U^{-2})^{N/2},&N\text{ even}. \end{cases}} \tag{3.5} \end{aligned}

For odd NN, each residue i+j(mod⁡N)i+j\pmod N occurs (N−1)/2(N-1)/2 times among unordered distinct pairs. For N=2mN=2m, even residues occur m−1m-1 times and odd residues mm times. Their products are 1−U−11-U^{-1} and 1+U−11+U^{-1}, respectively, giving the displayed even sign.

The Smith factors over the PID AA, up to units, are

N=2m1(m times),U2−1(m times),N=2m+11(m times),U−1,U2−1(m times). \begin{array}{c|c} N=2m&1\ (m\text{ times}),\quad U^2-1\ (m\text{ times}),\\ N=2m+1&1\ (m\text{ times}),\quad U-1,\quad U^2-1\ (m\text{ times}). \end{array} \tag{3.6}

To see the multiplicities, reduce (3.2) at U=±1U=\pm1, where JJ is an ordinary involution. Evaluation on reciprocal pairs of roots gives

dim⁡im⁡(L→B/(U−1)B)={(N−1)/2,N odd,N/2,N even. \dim\operatorname{im}(L\longrightarrow B/(U-1)B) =\begin{cases}(N-1)/2,&N\text{ odd},\\N/2,&N\text{ even}.\end{cases} \tag{3.7}

At Y=1Y=1, a fixed function must vanish; at Y=−1Y=-1, there is no condition. At U=−1U=-1, the rank is (N+1)/2(N+1)/2 for odd NN, and N/2N/2 for even NN. Inclusion (3.3), or these ranks together with (3.5), shows that all nontrivial local Smith exponents are one.

Crucially, L/(U−1)LL/(U-1)L has dimension NN, whereas its image in B/(U−1)BB/(U-1)B has only the dimension in (3.7). The lost directions are retained by first normal jets of the embedding L⊂BL\subset B.

For another description, let Cn(Y+Y−1)=Yn+Y−nC_n(Y+Y^{-1})=Y^n+Y^{-n}. On the abstract fiber L/(U−1)LL/(U-1)L, both the characteristic and minimal polynomials of the XX matrix equal CN(x)−2C_N(x)-2. Indeed v0v_0 is a cyclic vector by the Newton recurrence. The repeated roots give size-two Jordan blocks. On B/(U−1)BB/(U-1)B, multiplication by XX is diagonalizable by evaluation at the distinct NN-th roots. The two fibers must not be identified.

4. Cleared recovery of the clock and shift

Define integer polynomials by

C0(x)=2,C1(x)=x,Cn+1(x)=xCn(x)−Cn−1(x), C_0(x)=2,\quad C_1(x)=x,\quad C_{n+1}(x)=xC_n(x)-C_{n-1}(x),

P−1(x)=0,P0(x)=1,Pn+1(x)=xPn(x)−Pn−1(x). P_{-1}(x)=0,\quad P_0(x)=1,\quad P_{n+1}(x)=xP_n(x)-P_{n-1}(x).

Thus Pj(x)=𝒰j(x/2)P_j(x)=\mathcal U_j(x/2), and

PN−1(X)(Y−Y−1)=U−U−1,PN−1(X)Y−1=PN−2(X)+U−1. P_{N-1}(X)(Y-Y^{-1})=U-U^{-1},\qquad P_{N-1}(X)Y^{-1}=P_{N-2}(X)+U^{-1}. \tag{4.1}

Write D=U−U−1D=U-U^{-1}. On the original regular power lattice BB, the following are exact cleared operator identities:

DY=CN+1(X)−U−1X, \boxed{DY=C_{N+1}(X)-U^{-1}X,} \tag{4.2}

DY−1=CN−1(X)−U−1X, \boxed{DY^{-1}=C_{N-1}(X)-U^{-1}X,} \tag{4.3}

DT=PN−1(X)Z−(PN−2(X)+U−1)S, \boxed{DT=P_{N-1}(X)Z -(P_{N-2}(X)+U^{-1})S,} \tag{4.4}

QDT−1=(PN−2(X)+U)S−PN−1(X)Z. \boxed{QD T^{-1}=(P_{N-2}(X)+U)S-P_{N-1}(X)Z.} \tag{4.5}

For (4.2), expand CN+1(X)=UY+U−1Y−1C_{N+1}(X)=UY+U^{-1}Y^{-1}; (4.3) is analogous. For (4.4), use Z−Y−1S=(Y−Y−1)TZ-Y^{-1}S=(Y-Y^{-1})T and (4.1). Subtracting (4.4) from DSDS gives (4.5). Operator order is as displayed; the polynomials in XX multiply on the left.

Thus X,S,ZX,S,Z recover the full quantum-torus action over k(U)k(U), and recover its cleared equations over AA. Dividing by DD and then substituting U=1U=1 is not a recovery rule. Use the regular action on BB, or retain the compatible lattice and normal jets, before specialization.

There is a uniform denominator bound. For any AA-linear endomorphism RR of BB,

DR(L)⊂L, DR(L)\subset L, \tag{4.6}

since R(L)⊂BR(L)\subset B and DB=(U2−1)BDB=(U^2-1)B up to a unit. Its matrix in the vnv_n basis therefore has at most a simple pole at each of U=1,−1U=1,-1, independently of its clock/shift word length. Higher poles appearing in products of (4.2)–(4.5) cancel. This applies to an operator already proved regular and AA-linear on BB; it supplies neither hypothesis for an uncomputed crossing.

For reference, clock and shift generate all AA-linear endomorphisms of BB. The projectors

Ej=1N∑ℓ=0N−1Q−jℓTℓ E_j=\frac1N\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}Q^{-j\ell}T^\ell

project onto AYjAY^j, and Yi−jEjY^{i-j}E_j are matrix units.

5. Reciprocal sources and the marked cut

If an actual marked source calculation gives a reciprocal Laurent polynomial F(Y)=F(Y−1)F(Y)=F(Y^{-1}), then

f(Y)=(1−Y)F(Y)∈L. f(Y)=(1-Y)F(Y)\in L. \tag{5.1}

This follows from whole reciprocity, rather than ordinary-color interpolation. For a finite Newton expansion F=∑ncntnF=\sum_n c_n t_n, every tail folds exactly:

f=∑r=0N−1(∑a≥0cr+aNWa)vr. f=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1} \left(\sum_{a\ge0}c_{r+aN}W^a\right)v_r. \tag{5.2}

Terms with index r+aNr+aN start at normal order 2a2a at U=1U=1; vanishing at the point does not discard them as whole functions.

Define the marked cut on L⊂k[Y,Y−1]L\subset k[Y,Y^{-1}] by

cut⁡(f)=−dfdY|Y=1. \operatorname{cut}(f)=-\left.\frac{df}{dY}\right|_{Y=1}. \tag{5.3}

This derivative follows U=YNU=Y^N. It is not the derivative at fixed UU of a chosen power-basis representative. Every f∈Lf\in L vanishes at Y=1Y=1, so

cut⁡((U−1)f)=0,cut⁡(vn)=tn(1)=∏r=1n(2−Qr−Q−r). \operatorname{cut}((U-1)f)=0,\qquad \operatorname{cut}(v_n)=t_n(1) =\prod_{r=1}^n(2-Q^r-Q^{-r}). \tag{5.4}

Thus cut factors through the full abstract fiber L/(U−1)LL/(U-1)L. If f=∑n<Nbn(U)vnf=\sum_{n<N}b_n(U)v_n, then

F(1)=cut⁡(f)=∑n=0N−1bn(1)∏r=1n(2−Qr−Q−r). \boxed{F(1)=\operatorname{cut}(f) =\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_n(1) \prod_{r=1}^n(2-Q^r-Q^{-r}).} \tag{5.5}

Cut need not factor through the image in B/(U−1)BB/(U-1)B. For N=3N=3, Q+Q−1=−1Q+Q^{-1}=-1, and

v1=(1−Y)(Y+Y−1+1)=1−UY. v_1=(1-Y)(Y+Y^{-1}+1)=\frac{1-U}{Y}.

Its image in B/(U−1)BB/(U-1)B is zero, but its cut is 33. This is an exact example of the normal data required by the selected period.

More generally, v⌊N/2⌋v_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor} has zero image in that power fiber: the factors t⌊N/2⌋t_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor} cover every nontrivial reciprocal pair of roots, and 1−Y1-Y kills Y=1Y=1. Its cut is nonzero, and is positive at the principal complex root. Thus this phenomenon already occurs among exactly JJ-fixed whole sources at every level.

Equations (5.1)–(5.5) do not determine uncomputed Newton coefficients. For the actual figure-eight source proved in the figure-eight source identity, the coefficients are bn=1b_n=1. For the actual 525_2 triple sum, the proposed coefficient transform remains unproved.

6. A whole annihilator can be put in the banded algebra

This section is conditional on an actual source annihilator, not on a conjectured recurrence or its geometric interpretation. Let 𝒬=k⟨Y±1,T±1⟩/(TY−QYT)\mathcal Q=k\langle Y^{\pm1},T^{\pm1}\rangle/(TY-QYT). Conjugation by JJ induces the involution

θ(Y)=Y−1,θ(T)=QT−1. \theta(Y)=Y^{-1},\qquad\theta(T)=QT^{-1}.

Its fixed algebra is exactly the algebra generated by X,S,ZX,S,Z. Here is an elementary proof. Its fixed vector space is spanned by 11 and the orbit sums

Ea,b=YaTb+QbY−aT−b. E_{a,b}=Y^aT^b+Q^bY^{-a}T^{-b}.

They obey

XEa,b=Ea+1,b+Ea−1,b,Ea,bS=Ea,b+1+QEa,b−1, XE_{a,b}=E_{a+1,b}+E_{a-1,b},\qquad E_{a,b}S=E_{a,b+1}+QE_{a,b-1},

E1,−1=Q−1(XS−Z),E−a,b=QbEa,−b. E_{1,-1}=Q^{-1}(XS-Z),\qquad E_{-a,b}=Q^bE_{a,-b}.

Starting from E0,0=2E_{0,0}=2, E1,0=XE_{1,0}=X, E0,1=SE_{0,1}=S, E1,1=ZE_{1,1}=Z, these recurrences generate every orbit sum. The proof works before or after root specialization in characteristic zero.

At Q=1Q=1, the commuting symbols obey x2+s2+z2−xsz−4=0x^2+s^2+z^2-xsz-4=0, as direct substitution of x=Y+Y−1x=Y+Y^{-1}, s=T+T−1s=T+T^{-1}, z=YT+Y−1T−1z=YT+Y^{-1}T^{-1} shows. This identifies the classical fixed algebra with the Weyl quotient of the boundary torus; it does not choose a longitude convention for TT.

Suppose FF is reciprocal and an actual nonzero quantum-torus operator PP annihilates it. For f=(1−Y)Ff=(1-Y)F, conjugate to (1−Y)P(1−Y)−1(1-Y)P(1-Y)^{-1} in the rational-coefficient shift algebra and clear its finitely many YY-denominators by left multiplication. This gives a nonzero Laurent operator R∈𝒬R\in\mathcal Q with Rf=0Rf=0. Since Jf=fJf=f, also θ(R)f=0\theta(R)f=0.

If R+θ(R)≠0R+\theta(R)\ne0, this is a nonzero invariant annihilator. Otherwise RR is anti-invariant and

(Y−Y−1)R (Y-Y^{-1})R

is a nonzero invariant annihilator. Nonzeroness uses the fact that the abstract quantum torus is a domain, also at roots of unity; no quotient by TN−1T^N-1 is taken in this domain assertion. By the fixed-algebra result, the resulting annihilator is a noncommutative polynomial in X,S,ZX,S,Z. It therefore acts by a finite-band expression in the whole source basis, with the exact corner in section 2.

This proves an operator-existence statement whenever the actual annihilator and reciprocity have been established. It does not select a particular operator, prove that its classical symbol is the geometric AA-component, or prove a regulator/volume statement.

For the actual reduced knot family these hypotheses have already been established in the repository: actual Weyl descent, section 1 proves reciprocity at every level for every knot, and squared-meridian descent, section 2, using the finite-sum proof, supplies a nonzero all-level annihilator over ℚ(Q,Y)\mathbb Q(Q,Y). Clearing its finitely many denominators gives the Laurent input used here. The argument applies levelwise to fN=(1−Y)FNf_N=(1-Y)F_N with this same operator and therefore gives a nonzero all-level annihilator in the algebra generated by X,S,ZX,S,Z. This corollary has no hyperbolicity, alternatingness, or bridge-number restriction. It remains an existence statement, without a selected geometric symbol or a volume estimate.
