Full cyclotomic transport removes the limiting-root nonresonance condition

Independent extension, 27 October 2026. The coefficient field and the quantum parameter matter. For a rational coefficient in the true parameter Q, all-level principal-root transport identities propagate to all primitive roots by Galois conjugacy. Repeated shorter orbits then supply transport at scaled roots approaching Q=1. This removes the limiting-root nonresonance assumption in the unramified rank-one descent argument.

The proof below is elementary and independent of any general published q-curvature theorem. It concerns a specified comparison line. It does not construct that line for the actual selected knot period, identify its marked source, or prove the volume conjecture.

1. The actual reduced state admits a Q-only annihilator

I checked the new construction in the even-meridian note against the exact crossing formulas and the finite-sum annihilator proof. There is a useful stronger identity for its integer-exponent step.

At a crossing write a_t for the first input index and c_t for the first output. The x-power contributed by a positive crossing is -(a_t+c_t), and by a negative crossing it is a_t+c_t. The remaining pivot/framing contribution is (N-1)kappa_D, where kappa_D=w-b+1 is even for a knot braid. Consequently

E=(N-1)kappa_D
       +2 sum_negative a_t-2 sum_positive c_t
       +sum_all(c_t-a_t).                    (1.1)

The last sum is zero on each closed state, as the exact integer position coboundary. Thus

E/2=(N-1)kappa_D/2
                  +sum_negative a_t-sum_positive c_t. (1.2)

This is manifestly integral affine-linear on the closed-flow lattice. No additional parity saturation or half-lattice assertion is needed. Replacing x^E by (Y/Q)^(E/2) therefore leaves a proper Q-product sum with integer-valued quadratic Q exponents and step-one Pochhammers.

The filtered dimension bound, boundary strata and finite-moment repair of the existing proof apply with base Q, shift Y->QY and Z=Q^N. Stripping the common central factor (Z-1)^h before specialization gives a nonzero operator over the rational field in Q,Y. Factorials have lengths below N, so their root specializations are regular. This verifies the claimed existence result, conditional only on that already supplied finite-sum proof. It does not prove q-sign symmetry of a different minimal generator defined on half-roots. The full ADO sign and modified-dimension line remain exactly those of the original state.

2. Whole-root transport and the scaled-root lemma

Let sigma_Q(Y)=QY and let a(Q,Y) be a nonzero rational function. For any complex Q where its coefficients are defined put

C_n(Q,Y)=product_(j=0)^(n-1) a(Q,Q^jY).       (2.1)

Assume first a belongs to ℚ(Q,Y), and that for every sufficiently large n,

C_n(zeta_n,Y)=1 in C(Y),
zeta_n=exp(2pi*i/n).                         (2.2)

The fixed coefficient denominators exclude only finitely many root orders. Galois conjugation over ℚ takes (2.2) to

C_n(zeta_n^r,Y)=1 whenever gcd(r,n)=1.       (2.3)

Fix a positive integer k, and set

xi_N=exp(2pi*i*k/N), g=gcd(k,N), m=N/g.

Its order is m and it is a primitive m-th root. For all sufficiently large N, m is above the finite exceptional range, so (2.3) gives C_m(xi_N,Y)=1. The N-step orbit consists of g identical m-step orbits, hence C_N(xi_N,Y)=C_m(xi_N,Y)^g=1.

One must not replace the nonprimitive orbit product by the primitive formula 1-alpha^N Y^N. Instead use its actual order m. The identity at that order gives equality of the multisets of m-th powers of the numerator and denominator roots. Raising those multisets to the g-th power gives equality of their N-th powers. This is the only root-power implication used below.

The scaled-root lemma requires all sufficiently large orders, not merely primes or one residue class. The half-root alias in the divisor note is excluded by using the true shift parameter Q and the full cyclotomic family.

More generally the argument applies over any subfield K of C provided (2.3) is supplied for the fixed coefficient embedding. A sufficient condition for a number field K is that (2.2) is known for every coefficient conjugate of a in a finite normal closure of K. Indeed, apply a cyclotomic automorphism to the appropriate inverse coefficient conjugate. A statement at one chosen geometric embedding alone is not silently promoted to this stronger Galois marking.

3. Unramified full-curvature descent

Assume a(Q,Y) belongs to ℚ(Q,Y) and has the full-order norm property (2.2). Write over its splitting field

a(Q,Y)=r(Q) Y^s
       product_(i=1)^d(1-alpha_i(Q)Y)
         /product_(j=1)^e(1-beta_j(Q)Y).     (3.1)

Assume all alpha_i and beta_j admit holomorphic nonzero branches near Q=1. Coincident limiting roots and root-of-unity ratios between different limiting roots are allowed. There is no nonresonance assumption on those limiting values.

Theorem. One has s=0, d=e and r(Q)=Q^(k_0). The branches can be paired so that

alpha_i(Q)=Q^(k_i) beta_(pi(i))(Q),
                          k_i in Z.         (3.2)

There is a rational gauge f in ℚ(Q,Y)^* with

a(Q,Y)=f(Q,QY)/f(Q,Y).                      (3.3)

Its numerator and denominator Y-degrees can each be bounded by |k_0|+sum_i |k_i|. In particular the degree bound is deduced from fixed branch data, rather than imposed on levelwise Hilbert-90 gauges. The shifts are

k_i=alpha_i'(1)/alpha_i(1)
                     -beta_(pi(i))'(1)/beta_(pi(i))(1). (3.4)

If a has coefficients in K and the whole-root hypothesis (2.3) holds, the same proof gives f in K(Q,Y).

Preliminary scalar and exponential-sequence facts

At a primitive root the product formula gives

r(zeta_N)^N (-1)^(s(N-1)) U^s
   product_i(1-alpha_i(zeta_N)^N U)
      /product_j(1-beta_j(zeta_N)^N U)=1,
U=Y^N.

Its U-valuation gives s=0, its value at U=0 gives r(zeta_N)^N=1, and its factorization gives d=e and the equality of the indicated root multisets. The scalar discreteness argument of the divisor note, now with Q_N=exp(2pii/N), gives r(Q)=Q^(k_0): choose a logarithm ell at Q=1, use N ell(exp(2pii/N)) in 2pi*i Z, first take consecutive differences to get r(1)=1, then take the limit to make these integers constant. The rational identity follows from infinitely many root values.

We also use the following directly proved uniqueness fact. For distinct nonzero constants c_1,…,c_h and meromorphic germs F_i(t),

sum_i c_i^N F_i(1/N)=0 for all large N       (3.5)

implies every F_i=0. Among nonzero germs choose the largest modulus R of the c_i, then the smallest Laurent order of those germs. After division by R^N and that common power of 1/N, the identity is a finite sum sum a_i omega_i^N tending to zero, with distinct unit complex omega_i and at least one nonzero a_i. Multiplying by omega_j^(-N) and taking Cesaro means gives a_j=0, a contradiction. The lower-modulus terms vanish exponentially and the remaining errors tend to zero, so the averaging is justified. Root-of-unity ratios between distinct c_i cause no problem in this lemma.

Separation at scaled roots, without separating c^k

Let C be the finite set of distinct limiting branch values at Q=1. Use logarithms vanishing at Q=1 and define the holomorphic units

A_i(t)=exp(Log(alpha_i(exp(2pi*i*t))/alpha_i(1))/t),
B_j(t)=exp(Log(beta_j(exp(2pi*i*t))/beta_j(1))/t). (3.6)

Fix an integer k between 1 and d. The scaled-root lemma implies equality of the N-th root-power multisets at xi_N=exp(2piik/N). Their first power sums therefore give

sum_(c in C) c^N
   [sum_(alpha_i(1)=c) A_i(k/N)^k
                -sum_(beta_j(1)=c) B_j(k/N)^k]=0. (3.7)

The bases here are the distinct c, not c^k. Apply (3.5) to the holomorphic bracketed functions of 1/N, then rescale their argument by k. It follows that for each c and every k=1,…,d,

sum_(alpha_i(1)=c) A_i(t)^k
                  =sum_(beta_j(1)=c) B_j(t)^k. (3.8)

Newton identities, with zeros padding each multiset to length d, give equality of these multisets over the field of meromorphic germs. Their entries are units, so the cardinalities at each c agree. A fixed permutation pairs A_i identically with B_(pi(i)).

The quotient of the logarithms in (3.6) has exponential one and is holomorphic. It is therefore a constant 2pi*i k_i. This gives Log(alpha_i/beta_(pi(i)))=k_i Log Q, proving (3.2) and (3.4).

Rational descent and the degree bound

For a pair with k_i>0 use the gauge

product_(u=0)^(k_i-1)(1-Q^u beta_(pi(i))(Q)Y).

For k_i<0 use the inverse product over k_i<=u<0. The product of all these gauges, times Y^(k_0), proves (3.3) over the splitting field and gives the stated degree bound.

The finite divisor flow solving div(a)=sigma(div f)-div f is unique on each infinite Q-shift orbit. Because a is rational over ℚ(Q), its flow divisor is invariant under the Galois group of a finite splitting field. The product part is normalized to one at Y=0, so its multiplicative scalar is invariant as well. Thus the gauge descends to ℚ(Q,Y). The same argument works over K(Q). This completes the proof.

4. What the theorem does not remove

The unramified branch hypothesis remains. It concerns zeros and poles as functions of the quantum parameter Q, not merely classical shape etaleness over M. No claim is made here that every rational comparison has that property, or that the actual unknown comparison line has been constructed.

Nor does (3.3) itself preserve the ideal-meridian lattice. The gauge can carry a zero or pole on that divisor. The source, cut functional, and full ADO sign line must be transported in their specified lattices. A rational generic comparison fixes neither an arbitrary root-central scalar nor the nonzero coefficient of the selected regulator period. Those require the original pointing. This theorem is a new sufficient descent tool for that comparison problem, not the volume theorem.
