The scalar prefix kernel and the original first-meridian-jet source

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The universal kernel in C loses its remaining-head Casimir dependence exactly. At a fixed primitive quantum root it is 𝔥N(k,ω;M)=DN(M)DN(Mk),DN(z)=zN−z−Nz−z−1.(1) \boxed{\qquad \mathfrak h_N(k,\omega;M)=\frac{D_N(M)}{D_N(Mk)},\qquad D_N(z)=\frac{z^N-z^{-N}}{z-z^{-1}}. \qquad} \qquad\text{(1)} Here DND_N is its Laurent-polynomial continuation at z=±1z=\pm1, and (1) is a rational identity, not a prescription to evaluate an uncancelled denominator at a zero.

For the actual common-meridian family, this gives an explicit first-jet formula for the original highest-cut, pivotal source. Its functional cost in the actual occupation basis is polynomial in NN at fixed tensor width. Neither the constant coefficient nor its derivative is estimated here. The proof uses finite matrix paths, a finite product telescope, and polynomial interpolation; it imports no modified-trace theorem.

The sole complete direct is C, pinned at the end. The moment argument and finite telescope were independently developed with Early; all calculations needed for the conclusion are included.

1. The universal kernel and its two-head realization

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, Q=q2Q=q^2. Keep qq fixed throughout. A head with meridian MiM_i has Kiwa=xiQ−awa,xi=−Mi/q,Fiwa=(1−Qa+1)wa+1,Eiwa=(MiQ−a−Mi−1)wa−1.(2) K_iw_a=x_iQ^{-a}w_a,\quad x_i=-M_i/q,\qquad F_iw_a=(1-Q^{a+1})w_{a+1},\qquad E_iw_a=(M_iQ^{-a}-M_i^{-1})w_{a-1}. \qquad\text{(2)} The outward endpoint arrows vanish. We write EiE_i for C’s scaled lowering operator ℰi\mathcal E_i. The head Casimir is ωi=qxi+q−1xi−1=−Mi−Mi−1\omega_i=q x_i+q^{-1}x_i^{-1}=-M_i-M_i^{-1}.

For an ordered tensor prefix, C gives the analytic triangular operator SS, preserving the total Cartan, such that S−1FΣS=F1,S−1EΣS=Er+L1bA,bj=MKrQ−j−M−1Kr−1,(3) S^{-1}F_\Sigma S=F_1,\qquad S^{-1}E_\Sigma S=E_{\mathrm{ r}}+L_1b_A,\qquad b_j=MK_{\mathrm{ r}}Q^{-j}-M^{-1}K_{\mathrm{ r}}^{-1}, \qquad\text{(3)} where M=M1M=M_1. For reference, the scalar kernel of C is defined as follows: u(k,ω)=ω−qk−q−1k−1,bj(k)=MkQ−j−M−1k−1,an,n(k,ω)=Q−nk1−N,(6) u(k,\omega)=\omega-qk-q^{-1}k^{-1},\qquad b_j(k)=MkQ^{-j}-M^{-1}k^{-1},\qquad a_{n,n}(k,\omega)=Q^{-n}k^{1-N}, \qquad\text{(6)} an,j−1(k,ω)=11−Qj{u(Q−jk,ω)an,j(k,ω)bj(k)−u(k,ω)an,j(Qk,ω)bj(Qk)},j=n,…,1,(7) a_{n,j-1}(k,\omega)=\frac1{1-Q^j} \left\{ \frac{u(Q^{-j}k,\omega)a_{n,j}(k,\omega)}{b_j(k)} -\frac{u(k,\omega)a_{n,j}(Qk,\omega)}{b_j(Qk)} \right\},\quad j=n,\ldots,1, \qquad\text{(7)} 𝔥N(k,ω;M)=∑n=0dx1−nan,0(k,ω).(8) \mathfrak h_N(k,\omega;M)=\sum_{n=0}^d x_1^{-n}a_{n,0}(k,\omega). \qquad\text{(8)} It is rational in k,Mk,M and polynomial of degree at most dd in ω\omega. With the full remaining Casimir Ωr=FrEr+qKr+q−1Kr−1\Omega_{\mathrm{ r}}=F_{\mathrm{ r}}E_{\mathrm{ r}}+qK_{\mathrm{ r}}+q^{-1}K_{\mathrm{ r}}^{-1}, the exact source functional is Φ0(W)=Tr⁡r(𝔥N(Kr,Ωr;M)B0).(9) \Phi_0(W)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ r}} \big(\mathfrak h_N(K_{\mathrm{ r}},\Omega_{\mathrm{ r}};M)B_0\big). \qquad\text{(9)}

We will use (9) on two heads with independent generic meridians. This does not require extending C’s common-meridian pole claim. Indeed (3)–(5) hold with independent meridians, and the adjoint of the recursion in (4), for the ordinary trace pairing, is A↦[Er,Abj−1]1−Qj.(10) A\longmapsto\frac{[E_{\mathrm{ r}},A b_j^{-1}]}{1-Q^j}. \qquad\text{(10)} Using FE=u(K,Ω)FE=u(K,\Omega), EF=u(Q−1K,Ω)EF=u(Q^{-1}K,\Omega), and the weight relations in this adjoint gives precisely (6)–(9). These are finite rational matrix identities in independent nonzero M1,M2M_1,M_2, so they extend algebraically beyond an initial neighborhood of one. No Borel continuation or source-cycle transport is used when an auxiliary second color is moved around its root orbit.

2. Closed first-head paths and the pivotal moments

Take a second head whose highest Cartan value is an independent k0≠0k_0\ne0, so M2=−qk0M_2=-qk_0 and ω2=qk0+q−1k0−1\omega_2=qk_0+q^{-1}k_0^{-1}. Write K=K2K=K_2. The ordered coproducts are FΣ=F1+K1−1F2,EΣ=E1K2+E2,KΣ=K1K2. F_\Sigma=F_1+K_1^{-1}F_2,\qquad E_\Sigma=E_1K_2+E_2,\qquad K_\Sigma=K_1K_2. Expanding the actual total Casimir and collecting only commuting head factors gives ΩΣ=K1−1ω2+(ω1−(q+q−1)K1−1)K+F1E2+K1−1E1F2K.(11) \Omega_\Sigma =K_1^{-1}\omega_2+ \big(\omega_1-(q+q^{-1})K_1^{-1}\big)K +F_1E_2+K_1^{-1}E_1F_2K. \qquad\text{(11)} All endpoint rows remain those of (2).

For every 0≤p≤d0\le p\le d, the first-head highest block ⟨0|ΩΣp|0⟩\langle0|\Omega_\Sigma^p|0\rangle is a polynomial in KK of degrees 0,…,p0,\ldots,p. Its coefficient of KpK^p is independent of ω2\omega_2 and k0k_0.

Here is a finite path proof, including the degree assertion. Set X=E2X=E_2, Y=F2KY=F_2K. The tail letters satisfy KX=QXK,KY=Q−1YK, KX=QXK,\qquad KY=Q^{-1}YK, XY=u(Q−1K,ω2)K,YX=Qu(K,ω2)K.(12) XY=u(Q^{-1}K,\omega_2)K,\qquad YX=Q\,u(K,\omega_2)K. \qquad\text{(12)} Both products in (12) are polynomials in KK of degrees zero through two, with their degree-two coefficient independent of ω2\omega_2. A nonzero first-head path from occupation zero back to zero has equally many upward and downward steps, say rr of each. Its remaining p−2rp-2r steps are diagonal. Move their tail polynomials through X,YX,Y by the weight relations. Every balanced word in X,YX,Y has an adjacent opposite pair. Removing such pairs with (12) inductively gives a polynomial of degree at most 2r2r, with no negative powers. The diagonal factors add degree at most p−2rp-2r. To reach degree pp, every factor must contribute its top degree, whose coefficient contains no ω2\omega_2. All first-head path coefficients depend only on M,qM,q. Forbidden paths are simply zero, so no endpoint correction is removed in this argument.

Since the second-head eigenvalues are k0Q−ak_0Q^{-a}, Tr⁡(K1−N+j)=0(0≤j<d),Tr⁡(K1−N+d)=N.(13) \operatorname{Tr}(K^{1-N+j})=0\quad(0\le j<d),\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(K^{1-N+d})=N. \qquad\text{(13)} Consequently the original source moments satisfy Φ0(ΩΣp)=0(0≤p<d),Φ0(ΩΣd)=CN(M),(14) \Phi_0(\Omega_\Sigma^p)=0\quad(0\le p<d),\qquad \Phi_0(\Omega_\Sigma^d)=C_N(M), \qquad\text{(14)} where CN(M)C_N(M) is independent of the second-head meridian. No positive or cyclic replacement of the original source was used: (13) is the literal pivotal trace of the highest block.

3. Lagrange coupons and the exact phase factor

Put ka=k0Q−a,za=Mka,λa=−za−za−1,0≤a<N, k_a=k_0Q^{-a},\quad z_a=Mk_a,\quad \lambda_a=-z_a-z_a^{-1},\qquad 0\le a<N, P(t)=∏a=0d(t−λa).(15) P(t)=\prod_{a=0}^d(t-\lambda_a). \qquad\text{(15)} Work first where these NN values are distinct. Let fa(t)=∏b≠a(t−λb)P′(λa).(16) f_a(t)=\frac{\prod_{b\ne a}(t-\lambda_b)}{P'(\lambda_a)}. \qquad\text{(16)} The actual coupon Wa=fa(ΩΣ)W_a=f_a(\Omega_\Sigma) is an intertwiner. In the SS coordinates the Casimir is S−1ΩΣS=F1E2−I⊗(MK+M−1K−1).(17) S^{-1}\Omega_\Sigma S =F_1E_2-I\otimes(MK+M^{-1}K^{-1}). \qquad\text{(17)} Therefore its constant coefficient is B0=fa(−MK−M−1K−1)=|a⟩⟨a|B_0=f_a(-MK-M^{-1}K^{-1})=|a\rangle\langle a|. Equation (9), and separately the leading coefficient of (16) with (14), give 𝔥N(ka,ω2;M)=Φ0(Wa)=CN(M)P′(λa).(18) \mathfrak h_N(k_a,\omega_2;M) =\Phi_0(W_a)=\frac{C_N(M)}{P'(\lambda_a)}. \qquad\text{(18)} The remaining-head projector has ordinary trace one; there is no additional factor NN in (18).

The derivative in (18) has a direct product evaluation. Since the zbz_b’s run around the roots with common NN-th power, P′(λa)=(−1)d∏b≠a(za−zb)∏b≠a(1−za−1zb−1)=(−1)dNzaN−11−za−2N1−za−2=(−1)dNDN(za).(19) \begin{split} P'(\lambda_a) &=(-1)^d\prod_{b\ne a}(z_a-z_b) \prod_{b\ne a}(1-z_a^{-1}z_b^{-1})\\ &=(-1)^d N z_a^{N-1} \frac{1-z_a^{-2N}}{1-z_a^{-2}} =(-1)^d N D_N(z_a). \end{split} \qquad\text{(19)} The calculation is initially on its generic domain and hence holds by rational continuation. It fixes the phase as well as the magnitude.

4. The finite telescope and polynomial interpolation

Evaluate (7) at ω0=qk+q−1k−1\omega_0=qk+q^{-1}k^{-1}. Then u(k,ω0)=0u(k,\omega_0)=0, so every second term in the downward recursion at that fixed kk vanishes. Direct factorization gives u(Q−jk,ω0)(1−Qj)bj(k)=x11−k2Q1−j1−M2k2Q−j.(20) \frac{u(Q^{-j}k,\omega_0)}{(1-Q^j)b_j(k)} =x_1\,\frac{1-k^2Q^{1-j}}{1-M^2k^2Q^{-j}}. \qquad\text{(20)} Put r=Q−1r=Q^{-1}, a=k2a=k^2, b=M2k2rb=M^2k^2r, and use (a;r)n=∏j=0n−1(1−arj)(a;r)_n=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-ar^j). Equations (6)–(8) become 𝔥N(k,ω0;M)=k1−N∑n=0N−1rn(a;r)n(b;r)n.(21) \mathfrak h_N(k,\omega_0;M) =k^{1-N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r^n \frac{(a;r)_n}{(b;r)_n}. \qquad\text{(21)} This sum telescopes without an infinite-series identity. For Tn=(a;r)n/(b;r)nT_n=(a;r)_n/(b;r)_n and Un=(1−brn−1)TnU_n=(1-br^{n-1})T_n, including U0=1−b/rU_0=1-b/r, Un+1−Un=rn(b/r−a)Tn,TN=1−aN1−bN.(22) U_{n+1}-U_n=r^n(b/r-a)T_n,\qquad T_N=\frac{1-a^N}{1-b^N}. \qquad\text{(22)} As rN=1r^N=1, UN=(1−b/r)TNU_N=(1-b/r)T_N. Summing (22) and substituting the parameters in (21) gives 𝔥N(k,ω0;M)=k1−N(1−b/r)(TN−1)b/r−a=DN(M)DN(Mk).(23) \mathfrak h_N(k,\omega_0;M) =k^{1-N}\frac{(1-b/r)(T_N-1)}{b/r-a} =\frac{D_N(M)}{D_N(Mk)}. \qquad\text{(23)} All intermediate divisions are made generically; the resulting identity is rational, including the removable parameter values.

Apply (23) to (18) with a=0a=0, so k=k0k=k_0, and use (19). It follows that CN(M)=(−1)dNDN(M).(24) C_N(M)=(-1)^d N D_N(M). \qquad\text{(24)} Thus (18) proves the desired value for every weight of every generic second head.

Finally fix independent generic k,Mk,M. For each aa choose the second-head highest value k0=Qakk_0=Q^a k. Its weight aa is kk, and its Casimir is ωa=qQak+q−1Q−ak−1.(25) \omega_a=qQ^a k+q^{-1}Q^{-a}k^{-1}. \qquad\text{(25)} These NN numbers are distinct for generic kk: equality for different labels requires a fixed exceptional value of k2k^2. The polynomial in ω\omega in (8) has degree at most N−1N-1, and (18),(24) give the same claimed value at all NN nodes (25). Polynomial interpolation proves (1) over the rational function field in k,Mk,M, hence identically. There is no assumption about a scalar or semisimple remaining Casimir in this conclusion. Evaluating this rational-polynomial identity at the commuting matrices Kr,ΩrK_{\mathrm{ r}},\Omega_{\mathrm{ r}} retains all their weight multiplicities and Casimir Jordan blocks.

5. The common-meridian first-jet row

Return to m≥1m\ge1 actual heads with common meridian MM, and an analytic family W(M)W(M) of total intertwiners. Set K0=Kr(1),Kr(M)=Mm−1K0,ε=(−1)(N+1)(m−1).(26) K_0=K_{\mathrm{ r}}(1),\qquad K_{\mathrm{ r}}(M)=M^{m-1}K_0,\qquad \varepsilon=(-1)^{(N+1)(m-1)}. \qquad\text{(26)} The empty remaining tensor has K0=IK_0=I and ε=1\varepsilon=1. The actual root weights give (MKr)N=εMmNI(MK_{\mathrm{ r}})^N=\varepsilon M^{mN}I. Combining (1) and (9) gives, on a punctured meridian disk, Φ0(W(M))=DN(M)ε(MmN−M−mN)Tr⁡r((MKr−M−1Kr−1)B0(M)).(27) \boxed{\quad \Phi_0(W(M))= \frac{D_N(M)} {\varepsilon(M^{mN}-M^{-mN})} \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ r}} \big((MK_{\mathrm{ r}}-M^{-1}K_{\mathrm{ r}}^{-1})B_0(M)\big). \quad} \qquad\text{(27)} This is the original source, not a new normalization. Its left side is analytic. The denominator has a simple zero at one, where DN(1)=ND_N(1)=N, so admissibility forces the exact residue row Tr⁡r((K0−K0−1)B0(1))=0.(28) \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ r}} ((K_0-K_0^{-1})B_0(1))=0. \qquad\text{(28)} The removable value of (27) is consequently Φ0(W(1))=12εTr⁡r((K0+K0−1)B0(1))+12εmTr⁡r((K0−K0−1)B0′(1)).(29) \boxed{\quad \Phi_0(W(1))= \frac1{2\varepsilon} \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ r}}((K_0+K_0^{-1})B_0(1)) +\frac1{2\varepsilon m} \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ r}}((K_0-K_0^{-1})B_0'(1)). \quad} \qquad\text{(29)} Indeed the derivative of the denominator in (27) is 2εmN2\varepsilon mN; differentiate its trace numerator using (26). Equation (28) removes every term involving the derivative of DND_N or the regular part of the scalar prefactor. Both the sign ε\varepsilon and the factor mm are retained. For m=1m=1, (29) reduces to the scalar value of B0B_0, as it should.

The derivative in (29) is that of the actual constant coefficient of S(M)−1W(M)S(M)S(M)^{-1}W(M)S(M). It includes the meridian dependence of the entire triangular transform. An arbitrary pair of matrices called B0,B0′B_0,B_0' need not satisfy (28) or the other exact intertwiner conditions in C.

Equip the actual remaining occupation basis in (2) with its standard ℓ2\ell^2 norm. At the complete root K0K_0 is diagonal unitary, and the remaining dimension is Nm−1N^{m-1}. The elementary bound |Tr⁡(AB)|≤Nm−1∥A∥∥B∥|\operatorname{Tr}(AB)|\le N^{m-1}\|A\|\|B\| and ∥K0±K0−1∥≤2\|K_0\pm K_0^{-1}\|\le2 give |Φ0(W(1))|≤Nm−1(∥B0(1)∥+m−1∥B0′(1)∥).(30) \boxed{\quad |\Phi_0(W(1))| \le N^{m-1}\big(\|B_0(1)\|+m^{-1}\|B_0'(1)\|\big). \quad} \qquad\text{(30)} This controls the cost of the explicit functional in that original basis. It bounds neither these two source matrices nor the condition number of SS. Any separately prescribed external framing or peripheral scalar multiplies the original functional unchanged and must also be retained in its bound.

6. Source and normal scope

The collapse (1) is a universal rational identity for C’s kernel. Equations (27)–(30) apply to its actual analytic common-meridian intertwiner families at fixed qNq_N. Inserted marks that fail to commute with the total generators retain their Ward defects; they are not declared homogeneous intertwiners to use this reconstruction.

On a whole quantum-normal germ, C instead retains S−1FΣS=F1+JNS^{-1}F_\Sigma S=F_1+J_N, whose qq-normal need not vanish. The whole source, cut, pivotal and framing adapters must be differentiated before any such specialization. The root-meridian identities above are not ordinary-color closed-source quantum-normal identities.

No knot-word estimate, source noncancellation, framing-cycle pushforward, or nonzero geometric coefficient follows from (29) or (30). The new conclusion is the exact Casimir-free kernel and its explicitly controlled first-meridian-jet functional for the original pointed source.
