Actual first-normal normalization of the physical row interpolant

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The analytic interpolation of physical row values has a scalar first-normal normalization, determined by the original source. On every compact spectral slit chart that normalization is exponentially accurate. Its proof uses the common-shift Jacobi derivative, exact kernel symmetry at the physical grid, and analytic propagation of smallness. It does not assume a source coefficient from a formal characteristic equation.

The degree variable remains in a fixed bulk interval. The result continues the second spectral variable through arbitrary compact subsets of its slit plane; it does not continue the first degree variable to an arbitrary height. An exact scalar full-jet interpolant on a thin degree strip follows, with the original row action. Equality at its bulk subgrid is not equality in the whole original trace.

5. Dense physical symmetry determines the constant

We use two elementary analytic observations. First, suppose holomorphic functions on a fixed rectangle are bounded by a fixed power of NN, and their values at the equally spaced nodes j/Nj/N of a fixed interior interval are O(e−κN)O(e^{-\kappa N}). On a smaller neighborhood of a shorter interior interval they and their fixed derivatives are O(e−κ′N)O(e^{-\kappa' N}), for some κ′>0\kappa'>0.

Here is a proof that also handles approximate zeros. Subtract the finite cardinal sum of the nodal errors using sinc⁡(Nx−j)\operatorname{sinc}(Nx-j). On a sufficiently thin fixed strip its size is at most CNMe−κN+πN|ℑx|CN^M e^{-\kappa N+\pi N|\Im x|}, hence exponentially small. The remainder has exact zeros at all the nodes. Choose a fixed disk in the rectangle and a smaller concentric disk containing a positive fraction of those nodes. Factor the corresponding disk Blaschke factors. On a still smaller disk each factor has modulus at most a fixed number less than one, uniformly in the chosen nodes. The maximum principle then bounds the remainder by CNMe−cNCN^M e^{-cN}. A finite covering and Cauchy’s formula prove the observation.

Second, an exponentially small holomorphic family on one fixed open subset, polynomially bounded on a connected larger domain, is exponentially small on each compact subset of that domain. A finite chain of overlapping buffered disks and the three-circles inequality proves this: at each step the exponent is multiplied by a fixed positive number, while polynomial losses remain eo(N)e^{o(N)}. This assertion concerns compact paths; it has no uniform boundary or shrinking-buffer content.

At a physical second endpoint, (39) contains the actual value, both independent first normals, and their mixed normal. At the odd simple second endpoint only its value is prescribed; the spectral formulas remain holomorphic through w=−2w=-2. There is no degree-fold or degree-endpoint assertion.

One may symmetrize (38) and its transposed construction on an overlapping thin two-degree bulk rectangle. This observation does not extend the domain of either construction.
