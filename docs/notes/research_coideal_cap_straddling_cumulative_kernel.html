A crossing between an ordinary and a marked cap gives a cumulative sum

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

The original last marked cap, an adjacent ordinary cap, and a crossing between their two pairs give an explicit two-height boundary tensor. Its exact zero pattern is triangular after a known permutation. The surviving coefficients factor into a row weight and a column weight. Removing their specified phases gives a positive weighted cumulative-sum matrix, with an explicit first-difference inverse and polynomial conditioning. Both original crossing signs are retained.

Both heights are input boundary coordinates before a matrix reshaping is chosen. The singular-value bounds concern that reshaping and the associated bilinear pairing. They do not give a lower bound for its contraction against an arbitrary coherent exterior.

5. Arbitrary fixed width and the meaning of the lower bound

Nor is the full NbN^b-dimensional compiler silently replaced by an NN-dimensional one. The N×NN\times N matrix is only the stated two-index reshaping.
