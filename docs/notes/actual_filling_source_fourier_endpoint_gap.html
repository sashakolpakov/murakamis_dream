A source-specific Fourier gap for the exact filling endpoints

29 October 2026. The full finite filling kernel, including its linear normal weight, has exponentially small endpoint-frequency strips when paired with an analytic source having a strict complete-color maximum. This is an estimate on a truncated original color corridor. It does not bound colors outside that corridor or prove survival of the remaining filled saddle. No Fresnel endpoint remainder is replaced by a polynomial error.

1. An exact finite Fourier lemma with its source hypotheses

Let H=floor(delta N), d_N=(H+1/2)/N. Suppose a physical packet c_h, -H<=h<=H, has a holomorphic interpolation f_N(z), for |Re z|<=2delta and |Im z|<=sigma_0, satisfying

∥fN(x+iy)∥≤CNBexp⁡{N(F−ax2+C0y2)},a>0, \|f_N(x+iy)\|\le C N^B \exp\{N(F-a x^2+C_0 y^2)\},\qquad a>0, \tag{1} ∥ch−fN(h/N)∥≤CNBeN(F−γ),γ>0. \|c_h-f_N(h/N)\|\le C N^B e^{N(F-\gamma)},\qquad\gamma>0. \tag{2}

These are hypotheses on the specified source, not on arbitrary holomorphic test functions. Scalar or finite-dimensional vector/operator packets with the displayed norm bounds are allowed. All constants are independent of N. For any fixed phi_0>0 and fixed nonnegative integer r, there are g>0 and C’,B’ such that

∥∑h=−HHhrcheiϕh∥≤C′NB′eN(F−g),dist⁡(ϕ,2πℤ)≥ϕ0. \boxed{\left\|\sum_{h=-H}^Hh^r c_h e^{i\phi h}\right\| \le C'N^{B'}e^{N(F-g)},\quad \operatorname{dist}(\phi,2\pi\mathbb Z)\ge\phi_0.} \tag{3}

The distance is at most pi; the assertion is empty if phi_0>pi. In particular no number-theoretic restriction on N is involved.

Here is an exact contour proof retaining the finite endpoints. Reduce phi modulo2pi into [phi_0,2pi-phi_0], which changes no physical value. For a fixed small sigma>0 use the rectangle in the UNWRAPPED variable z whose vertical sides have Re z=+/-(H+1/2) and whose horizontal sides have Im z=+/-sigma N. Then

∑h=−HHhrfN(h/N)eiϕh=12πi∮zrfN(z/N)eiϕz(πcot⁡πz−iπ)dz. \sum_{h=-H}^H h^r f_N(h/N)e^{i\phi h} =\frac1{2\pi i}\oint z^r f_N(z/N)e^{i\phi z} (\pi\cot\pi z-i\pi)\,dz. \tag{4}

Every enclosed integer has residue one. The subtracted entire term -i pi changes no residue and is essential for simultaneous upper and lower decay. Indeed

pi cot(pi z)-i pi=2pi i/(exp(2pi i z)-1).

On the upper horizontal side its product with exp(i phi z) is at most 4pi exp(-phi sigma N); on the lower side it is at most 4pi exp(-(2pi-phi)sigma N), for all sufficiently large N. On either vertical half-integer line the sharper exact bound is

|exp(i phi z)(pi cot(pi z)-i pi)|
    <=2pi exp[-phi_0 |Im z|]<=2pi.

Choose sigma<=sigma_0 so that C_0 sigma^2<=min(a delta^2/8,phi_0 sigma/2). For large N, d_N>=delta/2. The horizontal exponent in (4) is at most F-phi_0 sigma/2, and the vertical exponent is at most F-a delta^2/8. Its contour length and z^r cost only powers of N. Equation (2) contributes at most C N^(B+r+1)exp[N(F-gamma)] on the physical grid. Thus one may take

g=min(gamma,a delta^2/8,phi_0 sigma/2)>0.             (5)

This proof neither discards a vertical current nor assumes that a universal residue rewrite lowers the maximum of arbitrary source values. Its input is precisely the state-specific analytic bound (1).

2. Apply the lemma BEFORE any Gaussian approximation to the filling

Use the original q,Q,kappa and the exact full-jet kernel from the coherent filling theorem. For t_k=(k+1/2)/N and y_k=2pi i(t_k-1/2), write

KP(h)=2(−1)N−1N2∑k=0N−1hkehyk[(h+2Pyk/ℏ)sinhyk+coshyk]. K_P(h)=\frac{2(-1)^{N-1}}{N^2} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}h_k e^{hy_k} \left[(h+2Py_k/\hbar)\sinh y_k+\cosh y_k\right]. \tag{6}

Here |h_k|=1 for every integer P and real physical data. The original normal derivative has not been dropped: 2Py_k/hbar is its unwrapped linear weight, and the h term comes from the same exact circle difference. Every factor outside exp(hy_k) is at most polynomial in N for fixed P, uniformly over k.

Here beta and K_P are the coefficients of the NORMALIZED power f_P=r_N^(2P), exactly as in the filling theorem. The actual box R(-1)R(2P) multiplies every color pairing below by the known unit phase kappa^(2P). In particular the actual prefactor corresponding to kappa^(-P) in (10)–(11) is kappa^P. This original phase is kept; it does not change any norm estimate.

Let K_P^end retain any subset of the original k values with |t_k-1/2|>=eta_0>0. For a physical EVEN core c_h=c_(-h), the original half-color convention gives the exact truncated pairing

sum_(h=0)^H beta_(N-h-1)c_h
         =(1/2)sum_(h=-H)^H K_P(h)c_h.             (7)

In (6), phi_k=2pi(t_k-1/2) belongs to [-pi,pi], and dist(phi_k,2pi Z)>=2pi eta_0 on the retained endpoint strips. Apply (3) for r=0,1 and sum the N original k values. It follows that

∥12∑h=−HHKPend(h)ch∥≤CPNBPeN(F−g). \boxed{\left\|\frac12\sum_{h=-H}^H K_P^{\mathrm{end}}(h)c_h\right\| \le C_P N^{B_P}e^{N(F-g)}.} \tag{8}

This is a bound on the EXACT finite endpoint part, with both parities, all gcd cases and the normal source retained. There is no infinite charge tail to estimate, and no O(1/N) Fresnel error against an exponential target. The bounded current of section8 in the coherent filling note is consistent with (8), but (8) does not assume that every endpoint remainder equals that one Ward forcing.

3. The actual mixed-source input and its precise remaining scope

The EXACT finite packet in the analytic original-source theorem provides (1)–(2) for a mixed pretzel period at fixed odd s>=5, on a sufficiently small complete-color corridor. Here the other physical cusp colors may be fixed in a sufficiently small corridor. To specify the interpolation, retain a fixed regular high Newton-index window in each box, use its canonical paired factorial continuation Fhat(v-h)Fhat(v+h) and q(h2), and apply the UNCHANGED Hermite functional Phi_2. The lower index windows and the full nonempty alias sets are their proved exponentially smaller physical source errors. Its separate exact-interpolation bridge must be retained; a pointwise relative O(1/N) approximation on the color lattice alone does NOT imply (2) with an exponentially small Fourier error. This construction is even in the continued color, rather than an arbitrary exp(pi i h) extension.

The resulting nonzero analytic leading phase, composed with chi=pi z, is even in the color variable z=h/N. Its real second derivative at zero is strictly negative, uniformly for the other sufficiently small physical colors. Taylor’s formula and the elementary inequality 2|xy|<=epsilon x2+y2/epsilon give

Re Phi(x+iy)<=Re Phi(0)-a x^2+C_0 y^2.

The analytic amplitude is uniformly bounded by a polynomial in N. The exact high-window finite-source interpolation error and the alias bound supply (2), after shrinking the corridor so that its height stays within gamma/2 of the complete height. Thus (8) removes a genuine class of endpoints of this original mixed-source corridor.

Aliases need not be even and are estimated before folding. Directly from the original finite H,D data, |a_n|<=1+|P| for 0<=n<N and |beta_j|<=2(1+|P|). Their entire corridor pairing therefore costs only another factor 2N(1+|P|) times the proved alias error. The exact normal forcing and the vertical color sides in (4) are both retained.

This does not estimate physical colors h>H. It does not discard the central k strip, establish a nonzero filled critical pairing, or identify its action with a preferred topological longitude. A fixed-large-filling proof still requires those three steps, including the peripheral sign convention. The present source-conditioned estimate is not an all-input operator norm bound for a free exterior.

4. The combined source is a total derivative, with exact paired charges

There is a useful exact representation for the remaining coherent packet, avoiding the separate interpolation of any a_n. For ANY physical even core in (7), set

ℱN(z)=∑h=−HHche2πihz,UN(z)=e2πiPNz2sinh⁡(2πiz)ℱN(z),zk=(k+1/2)/N−1/2. \mathcal F_N(z)=\sum_{h=-H}^Hc_h e^{2\pi i h z},\qquad U_N(z)=e^{2\pi iPNz^2}\sinh(2\pi i z)\mathcal F_N(z), \qquad z_k=(k+1/2)/N-1/2. \tag{9}

The literal entire-normal filling kernel (6) gives EXACTLY

∑h=0HβN−h−1ch=(−1)N−1κ−P2πiN2∑k=0N−1UN′(zk). \boxed{\sum_{h=0}^H\beta_{N-h-1}c_h =\frac{(-1)^{N-1}\kappa^{-P}}{2\pi iN^2} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}U_N'(z_k).} \tag{10}

Indeed differentiating U multiplies its sinh term by 2pi i(2PNz+h) and differentiating sinh supplies 2pi i cosh. These are precisely BOTH linear normal contributions in (6). This holds with operator-valued c_h in their unchanged exterior order.

In particular U_N(1/2)=U_N(-1/2)=0, and

U_N'(1/2)=U_N'(-1/2)
   =-2pi i exp(pi iPN/2)sum_h(-1)^h c_h.

The periodic extension of U_N’ is continuous, with piecewise smooth derivative. Its Fourier series is absolutely convergent (two integrations by parts bound its nonzero coefficients by a constant times frequency^(-2)). Exact midpoint Fourier selection and one integration by parts therefore prove

∑h=0HβN−h−1ch=(−1)N−1κ−P∑m∈ℤ\{0}(−1)m(N−1)m∫−1/21/2e2πiN(Pz2−mz)sinh⁡(2πiz)ℱN(z)dz. \boxed{\sum_{h=0}^H\beta_{N-h-1}c_h =(-1)^{N-1}\kappa^{-P} \sum_{m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}} (-1)^{m(N-1)}m\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} e^{2\pi iN(Pz^2-mz)}\sinh(2\pi i z) \mathcal F_N(z)\,dz.} \tag{11}

The series is absolutely convergent for each N. Its constants can depend on the actual source; no uniform asymptotic tail estimate is silently inferred. The zero charge vanishes EXACTLY from the two original endpoint zeros of U_N, not by discarding a Fresnel current. Because the core is even, mathcalF_N is even and U_N is odd. The integrals at -m and m are opposites, so their weighted contributions in (11) are equal. Thus the +/-m channels ADD exactly, at every parity and level, before any saddle approximation.

For the source obeying (1)–(2), mathcalF_N and its fixed derivatives are exponentially small on any fixed real strip eta_0<=|z|<=1/2, by (3). Equations (10)–(11) therefore provide a source-faithful central phase problem with exact normal charge m. The finite-k endpoint bound (8) is already quantitative. Passing between a restricted central integral and (11) must still retain the new side currents at its artificial cutoff; absolute convergence at fixed N alone is not a uniform exponential bound on all charges. The uniform infinite-tail bound is proved separately below; a central-saddle conclusion is not inferred from fixed-N convergence.

5. A uniform exponential high-charge tail, with its paired current

Under (1)–(2), the large-|m| part of (11) is exponentially smaller than exp(NF). This is a UNIFORM estimate; absolute convergence at each fixed N alone would not suffice. For fixed integer P there are M_P>0 and finite B_P,C_P, independent of N, such that

∑|m|>MP∥m∫−1/21/2UN(z)e−2πimNzdz∥≤CPNBPeN(F−g*). \boxed{\sum_{|m|>M_P} \left\|m\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}U_N(z)e^{-2\pi imNz}\,dz\right\| \le C_P N^{B_P}e^{N(F-g_*)}.} \tag{12}

The positive gap g_* and the contour width below depend ONLY on the source hypotheses, not on P. This matters for independently large fillings with no comparability between their lengths.

First, (3), for a small real neighborhood of +/-1/2 in z, extends to a FIXED small complex strip. In its exact rectangle proof, an extra Im z=v in the Fourier variable costs at most exp(2pi delta |v|N) on the physical source support. The same argument applies to fixed derivatives of mathcalF_N, by taking r larger in (3). Thus for some fixed sigma_1,g_1>0,

||mathcalF_N^(j)(+/-1/2+iv)||
    <=poly(N)exp[N(F-g_1)], |v|<=sigma_1, j=0,1,2.

Fix a smaller sigma>0 using ONLY this source strip. On either endpoint line, at height -v, the chirp costs at most exp(2pi|P|Nv), and its first two derivatives cost powers of N and P. Keep that v-dependent cost; do not bound it by exp(2pi|P|sigma N) and then shrink sigma as the filling length grows.

For m>0 shift its Fourier integral DOWN by sigma. On the lower horizontal side the elementary physical-grid estimate

||mathcalF_N(x-i sigma)||
    <=poly(N)exp[N(F+2pi delta sigma)]

gives an upper bound

poly(N)exp{N[F-2pi sigma(m-|P|-delta)]}.            (13)

Crucially the two vertical currents must be paired. Their common Fourier endpoint phase is (-1)^(mN), and their difference is

V_N(v)=U_N(1/2-iv)-U_N(-1/2-iv), 0<=v<=sigma.

The original integer Fourier polynomial mathcalF_N is periodic. Together with the sinh zeros this gives V_N(0)=V_N’(0)=0 exactly, as already checked before (11). The source derivative bound on the two vertical strips, with the chirp cost explicitly retained, gives

||V_N(v)||<=poly(N,P)v^2
                      exp[N(F-g_1)+2pi|P|Nv].

Consequently the paired vertical contribution obeys

∥m∫0σe−2πmNvVN(v)dv∥≤poly⁡(N,P)m(m−|P|)3N3eN(F−g1),m>|P|. \left\|m\int_0^\sigma e^{-2\pi mNv}V_N(v)\,dv\right\| \le\frac{\operatorname{poly}(N,P)m}{(m-|P|)^3N^3} e^{N(F-g_1)},\quad m>|P|. \tag{14}

For m>M_P>=2|P|+2delta+2 this is at most a fixed multiple of poly(N,P)m(-2)N(-3)exp[N(F-g_1)], which is summable. The horizontal bounds (13) have an exponentially convergent geometric sum with gap at least 2pi sigma, independent of P. Take g_*=min(g_1,2pi sigma)>0. The upper shift for negative m is identical; for an even core it also follows from the exact +/-m identity. This proves (12). Neither individual vertical current is set to zero.

Here is the precise multiport justification. Leave all remaining z_j variables REAL until their tails have been removed. On the entire source U=exp(2pi iN sum P_i z_i^2) product_i sinh(2pi i z_i) mathcalF_N(z), define the commuting COLOR-register functionals

D_i U=(2pi iN^2)^(-1)sum_k partial_i U(z_(i,k)),
I_(i,m)U=(-1)^(m(N-1))m integral_[-1/2,1/2] U dz_i,
D_i^lo=sum_(0<|m|<=M_i) I_(i,m), D_i^tail=D_i-D_i^lo.

Midpoint Poisson and the matched endpoint values prove D_i=sum_m I_(i,m) exactly. These operations act linearly on scalar color registers; they do NOT commute or reorder physical Hopf boxes or matrix-valued exterior coefficients. Exact telescoping gives

∏iDi−∏iDilo=∑i(∏j<iDjlo)Ditail(∏j>iDj). \prod_iD_i-\prod_iD_i^{\mathrm{lo}} =\sum_i\left(\prod_{j<i}D_j^{\mathrm{lo}}\right) D_i^{\mathrm{tail}}\left(\prod_{j>i}D_j\right). \tag{14a}

For fixed M_j, the real-integral functional D_j^lo has supremum norm at most sum_(0<|m|<=M_j)|m|=M_j(M_j+1). The original D_j uses N samples and one derivative. Its mixed derivatives on U cost powers of N and P_j: all remaining chirps have modulus ONE at real z_j, while differentiating them supplies only P_j N. Differentiating mathcalF_N supplies integer powers of h_j, bounded by powers of N.

The endpoint Fourier bound and (14) are uniform in these remaining real spectators AND in every mixed derivative just described. To see this directly, sum the other physical h_j in absolute value using the original source bound exp[N(F_0-a sum x_j^2+C sum y_j^2)]. Their sums and fixed powers of h_j cost only powers of N; no positive exponential is introduced. The contour shift and paired vertical current occur ONLY in the tail coordinate i. Thus each term of (14a) is bounded by poly_(s,p)(N)exp[N(F_0-g_*)], with g_* fixed by the source and independent of the largest slope. There is no uncontrolled infinite charge sum in a spectator coordinate. Later central saddle-contour motions are made only AFTER (14a) has removed the tails.

Equations (8),(11),(12) leave a FINITE set of nonzero coherent central charges for every fixed collection of integer fillings. Their source Fourier saddle is made explicit next. Comparing the remaining charge actions and bounding colors outside the chosen corridor are separate tasks.

6. A nonzero central Fourier packet from the EXACT analytic source

This step uses the stronger, proved input from the exact color packet: on a fixed complex polydisk, with r color ports,

C_N(pi x)=N^(3/2)e^(-i s pi d/4) A(pi x)
                              exp[N phi(x)](1+O(1/N)),
phi(x)=Phi_s(pi x), phi(0)=F_0,
phi''(0)=2pi i tau_s I,
tau_s=(1+exp(2iu_s))/2, Im tau_s>0.                 (15)

The exact function C_N, not just this leading expression, has the complex Gaussian bound (1) in all continued coordinates. The case needed for filling all boxes is r=s. Define the ENTIRE finite packet

mathcalF_N(z)=sum_(|h_i|<=H) C_N(pi h/N)
                                     exp(2pi i h dot z). (16)

It is exactly even in every z_i. For a fixed sufficiently small complex z-polydisk, the holomorphic implicit-function theorem defines

grad phi(xi(z))+2pi i z=0,
Psi(z)=phi(xi(z))+2pi i xi(z) dot z.

Then, UNIFORMLY on a smaller fixed complex polydisk,

ℱN(z)=N(r+3)/2e−isπd/4B(z)eNΨ(z)(1+O(1/N)),B(z)=(2π)r/2A(πξ(z))det⁡[−ϕ″(ξ(z))]. \boxed{\mathcal F_N(z)=N^{(r+3)/2}e^{-i s\pi d/4} B(z)e^{N\Psi(z)}(1+O(1/N)),\quad B(z)=\frac{(2\pi)^{r/2}A(\pi\xi(z))} {\sqrt{\det[-\phi''(\xi(z))]}}.} \tag{17}

The Gaussian square root is fixed at zero by the positive real part of -phi’’(0). In particular

B(0)=A(0)(-i tau_s)^(-r/2) !=0,
xi(z)=-z/tau_s+O(||z||^3),
Psi(z)=F_0-i pi sum_i z_i^2/tau_s+O(||z||^4).        (18)

Both Psi and B are even in each z_i and B is nonzero on a smaller polydisk. All phases in (15) remain in (17).

Here is the finite-source proof of (17). Apply exact half-integer cotangent rectangles in the h coordinates to (16). The two constant cotangent parts give the ordinary integral with density N^r. The nonconstant parts cost exp(-2pi sigma N) on its horizontal sides; the extra factor exp(2pi iN x dot z), the quadratic complex-source growth and the small complex z displacement cost strictly less after choosing sigma first and then the z-polydisk. The vertical h sides keep their strict endpoint gap from (1). This proves

mathcalF_N(z)=N^r integral_[-d_N,d_N]^r
            C_N(pi x)exp(2pi iN x dot z)dx
                      +poly(N)exp[N(F_0-g)], g>0. (19)

Slightly changing the half-integer endpoints of the integral is exponentially harmless, and all corresponding side currents are in the displayed error. This is a statement about the EXACT holomorphic source; a pointwise real-grid O(1/N) error would not imply (19).

Deform the ordinary integral locally to the parallel real contour through xi(z). Its end connectors retain their strict gap for small z. The real part of phi’’ remains negative definite, uniformly near zero. The elementary multivariate Gaussian integral and the uniform analytic amplitude in (15) therefore give (17); its exponentially small cotangent remainder stays below the saddle after shrinking the z-polydisk. This also proves the required uniformity BEFORE any later z-contour motion. No original-cycle coefficient has been assigned.

For completeness, apply (11) successively in all r filled ports. The actual R powers restore their phases as explained above. The exact corridor source is

(−1)r(N−1)κ∑iPi∑mi≠0(−1)(N−1)∑imi∏imi∫[−1/2,1/2]re2πiN∑i(Pizi2−mizi)∏isinh⁡(2πizi)ℱN(z)dz. (-1)^{r(N-1)}\kappa^{\sum_iP_i} \sum_{m_i\ne0}(-1)^{(N-1)\sum_i m_i}\prod_i m_i \int_{[-1/2,1/2]^r} e^{2\pi iN\sum_i(P_i z_i^2-m_i z_i)} \prod_i\sinh(2\pi i z_i)\mathcal F_N(z)\,dz. \tag{20}

All original normal charges and the full original source are kept. Every coordinate reflection (m_i,z_i)->(-m_i,-z_i) preserves its term, so the 2^r all-unit charges have exactly equal contributions, including the even-level signs. There is no charge with m_i=0. The large-charge remainder is controlled by (12) before any Gaussian approximation; the real endpoint-frequency strips are controlled by (8). Equations (17),(20) therefore give an actual nonzero analytic source amplitude for the remaining finite central phase problem

Psi(z)+2pi i sum_i(P_i z_i^2-m_i z_i).               (21)

The comparison of its finite charges, its legitimate local saddle contours, and the physical color complement are not replaced by formal stationarity. In particular (20) alone is not a theorem for an entire filled knot.
