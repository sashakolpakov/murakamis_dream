Finite trace observables for the opposite-polarization characteristic

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

A finite explicit family of actual transported Casimirs separates the ordered irreducible SL2SL_2 tuple up to one common conjugation. Both the full operators and their first-head quotients have Laurent degree bounded independently of the color NN. Their complete-root coherent symbols can therefore be computed directly, without a symbol theorem for a full crossing or the prefix change of basis.

Applied to the actual root kernel T(1)T(1) and the Laurent residue of JJ, these observables give explicit homogeneous characteristic equations. They recover the turned ordered character and reverse the selected regular total-product eigenvalue wherever such a characteristic relation exists. The finite part of JJ instead retains an inhomogeneous normal row. No nonzero kernel amplitude, integration cycle, or geometric contribution is constructed.

The complete directs are M, the actual classical meridians; Q, the literal rational quantum conjugation; R, the actual prefix quotient; and J, the actual opposite-polarization kernel. All trace separation, four-entry transport, and bounded-degree calculations are below.

5. The finite trace family separates irreducible tuples

We prove this algebraically, including its local scheme meaning. For A,B,C∈SL2A,B,C\in SL_2, Cayley–Hamilton and trace give AB+BA=(tr⁡A)B+(tr⁡B)A+(tr⁡(AB)−tr⁡Atr⁡B)I,(25) AB+BA=(\operatorname{tr}A)B+(\operatorname{tr}B)A+ \bigl(\operatorname{tr}(AB)-\operatorname{tr}A\operatorname{tr}B\bigr)I, \qquad\text{(25)} tr⁡(ABC)+tr⁡(ACB)=tr⁡(AB)tr⁡C+tr⁡(AC)tr⁡B+tr⁡(BC)tr⁡A−tr⁡Atr⁡Btr⁡C.(26) \begin{split} \operatorname{tr}(ABC)+\operatorname{tr}(ACB) ={}&\operatorname{tr}(AB)\operatorname{tr}C+ \operatorname{tr}(AC)\operatorname{tr}B+ \operatorname{tr}(BC)\operatorname{tr}A\\ &-\operatorname{tr}A\operatorname{tr}B\operatorname{tr}C. \end{split}\qquad\text{(26)} For (25), apply the squared traceless-matrix identity to A−tr⁡(A)I/2A-\operatorname{tr}(A)I/2 and B−tr⁡(B)I/2B-\operatorname{tr}(B)I/2. Indeed for u=A−aI/2u=A-aI/2, v=B−bI/2v=B-bI/2, write u2=αIu^2=\alpha I, v2=βIv^2=\beta I, uv+vu=γIuv+vu=\gamma I. Then γ2−4αβ=Δ\gamma^2-4\alpha\beta=\Delta. When Δ\Delta is a unit these four matrices are a basis of Mat⁡2\operatorname{Mat}_2. The traces against them recover the coordinates of every HjH_j by this explicit Gram inverse. They use only single, pair, and triple traces. The multiplication table is fixed by A2=aA−IA^2=aA-I, B2=bB−IB^2=bB-I, and (25).

Second, choose three generators A,B,CA,B,C for which the trace Gram matrix of I,A,B,CI,A,B,C is invertible. Again pair traces recover all generator coordinates. Every multiplication constant of this basis is recovered by taking the traces of a product of two basis members against a third and applying that Gram inverse. These are precisely traces of length at most three, already supplied by (24),(26).

These finitely many charts cover every irreducible complex tuple. Let LL be the span of its traceless generator parts in 𝔰𝔩2\mathfrak{sl}_2. If dim⁡L=3\dim L=3, the second chart applies. If dim⁡L≤1\dim L\le1, all generators have a common eigenline. If dim⁡L=2\dim L=2 and the trace form on it is degenerate, its radical contains a nonzero isotropic traceless matrix, hence a nilpotent. Conjugate it to a nonzero multiple of e12e_{12}. Orthogonality forces the lower-left entry of every member of LL to vanish, again giving a common eigenline. In the remaining two-dimensional case the trace form is nondegenerate; any two generator parts forming a basis have Δ≠0\Delta\ne0, giving the first chart. For a complete irreducible parabolic tuple one can simply choose two distinct parabolic flags. Their nilpotent parts have nonzero product trace, so (27) is nonzero.

Suppose two tuples on one of these charts have the same data (24). The corresponding basis map preserves every multiplication constant just computed. It is therefore an algebra isomorphism between their full matrix algebras, sending every ordered generator to its mate. This isomorphism is inner. Here is also the local Artin-ring proof of that last assertion. Let FijF_{ij} be the images of ordinary matrix units. Choose vv in the image of F11F_{11} lifting a nonzero vector of its residue line, and put w=F21vw=F_{21}v. The relations of the FijF_{ij} show that (v,w)(v,w) is a basis after reduction, hence over the local ring. In this basis all FijF_{ij} are ordinary matrix units. The change-of-basis determinant is a unit; a square root of it exists locally over a complex Artin ring if an SL2SL_2 representative is desired. Uniqueness is up to scalars, since a matrix commuting with the full matrix algebra is scalar.

This proves equality of the local ordered-character relations, not only a count of dimensions or separation of closed points. The Gram denominators are the specified chart units. All conjugations are one common conjugation of the whole tuple.
