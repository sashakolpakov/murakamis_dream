Radial charge compactness and the sharp corner alternative

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

A positive unit-charge solution with complete outside angles gives uniform bounds on every height, real return and square covector at every positive solution with common charge 0<χ≤10<\chi\le1. The bounds do not assume that those positive solutions form a connected charge interval. Any first loss of positivity at a positive charge must therefore have a finite corner limit satisfying the sharp gradient-limit equality, rather than only the weaker corner subgradient inequality.

This does not exclude that corner event. In particular it does not prove that existence at charge one implies existence at every smaller charge. The original finite quantum cycle is not changed or estimated.

1. Original graph, branches and constants

4. Every limiting alternative, including the zero endpoint

Theorem 2. Let χj∈I∩(0,1]\chi_j\in I\cap(0,1] tend to χ*∈[0,1]\chi_*\in[0,1].

  1. The primal assignments converge to the unique closed minimizer zχ*z_{\chi_*}. The finitely many heights, returns and square covectors admit a jointly convergent subsequence with the bounds (16).
  2. If χ*=0\chi_*=0, all these coordinates tend to their complete values. In particular tv,rv,e,xP,BP,xQ,BQ→0t_v,r_{v,e},x_P,B_P,x_Q,B_Q\to0.
  3. If χ*>0\chi_*>0 and zχ*∈Xz_{\chi_*}\in X, it is the original positive solution at χ*\chi_*, and the period implicit-function theorem extends it through that parameter.
  4. Otherwise at least one square is a corner and no square is on an open side. At every corner (A,y)=(εAπ/4,εyπ/4)(A,y)=(\varepsilon_A\pi/4,\varepsilon_y\pi/4), each limiting covector satisfies U=εAx>0,V=εyB>0,sinh⁡(2U)sinh⁡(2V)=1.(29) U=\varepsilon_Ax>0,\qquad V=\varepsilon_yB>0,\qquad \sinh(2U)\sinh(2V)=1. \qquad\text{(29)} All original affine rows, endpoint matching relations and real period equations remain valid in the limit, with those covectors.

Proof. Primal convergence follows from Section 2, and Theorem 1 gives the stated subsequential compactness. An interior limit reconstructs the original matched positive solution by the convex variation theorem. Its nonsingular complex period Jacobian gives the extension in part 3.

The lower bound prevents these sine and half-angle observation factors from vanishing even along a corner approach at positive charge. It does not prevent square corners or make their Hessians nonsingular, and it is not a lower bound on the original scalar.

The general corner KKT condition permits sinh⁡(2U)sinh⁡(2V)≥1\sinh(2U)\sinh(2V)\ge1. Theorem 2 requires equality at a corner reached from actual positive radial solutions. An arbitrary corner certificate with strict inequality is therefore not a counterexample to radial continuation from a positive unit point. Conversely, equality does not itself exclude a corner. The square Hessian can have a collapsing and a diverging eigenvalue there, so interior nonsingularity does not justify continuation through it.

Thus the component of II containing one can fail to reach zero only through the specific finite boundary system (29), (32), (33). The component containing zero also exists by (15). This note does not prove that the two components coincide, or that any intervening corner event actually occurs. It gives a necessary boundary system, not a sufficient criterion for such an event.
