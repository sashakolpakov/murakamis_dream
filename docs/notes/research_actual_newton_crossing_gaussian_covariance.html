Gaussian and endpoint kernels for the actual Newton crossing

20 November 2026. The full original crossing has a positive Hermitian row covariance, even though its phases cannot all be removed by a diagonal change of coordinates. This note calculates that covariance in two regimes. In the interior of the Newton index interval its normalized kernel is Gaussian at scale N\sqrt N. At either endpoint, any fixed number of rows has the same explicit Pascal moment kernel after the stated polynomial row rescaling. These are asymptotics of the actual finite crossing, with its Cartan scalar and both closing indices retained.

The results do not give a norm estimate for an arbitrary short tangle. They do not identify a reflected norm with the original scalar closure, or show that its geometric contribution survives arbitrary gluing.

5. Geometric meaning and the remaining gluing step

The positive row amplitude in (1) is already an exact angle weight: βik=Nwd(k,i−k,d−i),wd(a,b,c)=1sasbsc,a+b+c=d. \beta_{ik}=N\,w_d(k,i-k,d-i),\qquad w_d(a,b,c)=\frac1{s_as_bs_c},\qquad a+b+c=d. The symmetric interior maximum is the ideal angle triple (x/2,x/2,1−x)(x/2,x/2,1-x), at the continuum scale. With Λ(θ)=−∫0θlog⁡(2sin⁡u)du\Lambda(\theta)=-\int_0^\theta\log(2\sin u)du, πB(x)=2Λ(πx/2)+Λ(π(1−x)) \pi B(x)=2\Lambda(\pi x/2)+\Lambda(\pi(1-x)) is its ideal tetrahedron volume. Formula (6) quantifies the actual overlap of neighboring row packets, including the Fourier phase. It is a nonzero Gaussian kernel after one explicit diagonal chirp; it is not obtained by replacing those phases with their magnitudes.

The coefficient sin⁡(πa)\sin(\pi a) in (6) is unchanged at leading order by the genuine turn’s reflection a↦1−aa\mapsto1-a. The two fixed endpoint limits likewise have the same moment matrix after their stated row weights. These facts describe local compatibility of the kernel. They do not give a global operator inequality for arbitrarily many rows or an arbitrary sequence of crossings and turns.

In particular no statement here identifies this row covariance with the full main-color Plancherel energy, the quotient covariance CEC_E of a whole Conway expression, or the original scalar closing after an unspecified short component. Such an identity or a controlled composition theorem is still needed before these local orthogonal frames can prove the finite-exception volume statement.
