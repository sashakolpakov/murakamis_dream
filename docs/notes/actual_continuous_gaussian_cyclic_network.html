The original source has an exact cyclic-dilogarithm and ordinary-Gaussian period

28 October 2026. The remaining finite marked Gaussian in the exact cyclic/Hardy network can also be removed. The original knot period is an exact integral of the literature cyclic dilogarithm and its inverse, ordinary Cauchy kernels, and ordinary continuous Gaussian weights. There are no finite Dirichlet, finite Euler, or finite Gaussian scalar kernels in this presentation.

The cycle is a specified fibered torus over real Gaussian variables, with Gaussian-dominated absolute convergence. It is not a fixed product of circles interchanged formally with an unbounded Gaussian integral. All original source, peripheral, Cartan and framing factors are retained.

The audit and results check 660 exact both-sign phase identities, eight whole original pivotal word matrices with joint Gaussian moments and no finite Gaussian kernel, ten real-Gaussian quadratures, and the fibered radius bounds.

1. The exact Gaussian moment, with both signs fixed

Put h=2πi/Nh=2\pi i/N, Q=ehQ=e^h. For the original equal-color Gaussian,

da=(−1)ax−2aexp⁡{h(a2−a)/2}. d_a=(-1)^a x^{-2a}\exp\{h(a^2-a)/2\}.

For s∈{+1,−1}s\in\{+1,-1\} set

cs=−x−2se−sh/2,αs=π(1+is),αs2=2πis. c_s=-x^{-2s}e^{-sh/2},\qquad \alpha_s=\sqrt{\pi}(1+is),\qquad \alpha_s^2=2\pi is. \tag{1}

Then exactly

das=N2π∫ℝe−Nξ2/2(cseαsξ)adξ. \boxed{\quad d_a^s= \sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb R} e^{-N\xi^2/2} (c_se^{\alpha_s\xi})^a\,d\xi. \quad} \tag{2}

Indeed the centered Gaussian of variance 1/N1/N has complex moment exp⁡(αs2a2/(2N))=exp⁡(sha2/2)\exp(\alpha_s^2a^2/(2N))=\exp(sh a^2/2). Multiplication by csac_s^a gives the original linear phase and meridian powers. For s=−1s=-1, the sign is still (−1)a(-1)^a, as required by the inverse Gaussian.

For a typed unequal-color crossing replace x−2sx^{-2s} by (xy)−s(xy)^{-s} in (1). Nothing else changes. No common Cartan factor is incorporated into this replacement.

For every polynomial ff, (2) gives an exact operator identity

𝖣sf(z)=N2π∫ℝe−Nξ2/2f(cseαsξz)dξ. \mathsf D_sf(z)= \sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb R} e^{-N\xi^2/2} f(c_se^{\alpha_s\xi}z)\,d\xi. \tag{3}

Each integrand is a dilation, so it preserves polynomial degree. Before every original Gaussian its active head lies below NN. Thus (3) equals the original finite marked Gaussian on every actual input. This is an equality of whole operators on those inputs, not an assumed selected Gaussian branch.

The active scalar Cauchy kernel for the dilation is

11−cseαsξz/w. \frac1{1-c_se^{\alpha_s\xi}z/w}. \tag{4}

All spectator heads retain their ordinary Hardy kernels. There is one independent ξ\xi for each original Gaussian factor, hence one for each signed crossing in the specified strict presentation.

2. A common fibered cycle

Retain the norm-one literature functions

𝒱N(t)=(1−tN)(N−1)/(2N)∏ℓ=1N−1(1−Qℓt)−ℓ/N,𝒰N(t)=𝒱N(t)−1, \mathcal V_N(t)=(1-t^N)^{(N-1)/(2N)} \prod_{\ell=1}^{N-1}(1-Q^\ell t)^{-\ell/N}, \qquad \mathcal U_N(t)=\mathcal V_N(t)^{-1}, \tag{5}

on their branches equal to one at zero. All non-Gaussian kernels and reflected monomials are exactly those in the previous theorem.

Choose ρ=1/16\rho=1/16. On a fixed color chart choose 0<λ≤10<\lambda\le1 with λ≤|xj|\lambda\le|x_j| and choose

Cc≥max⁡(1,sup⁡|xj|2,sup⁡|xj|−2). C_c\ge\max(1,\sup|x_j|^2,\sup|x_j|^{-2}).

The same bound covers the typed products (xjxj+1)±1(x_jx_{j+1})^{\pm1}. Insert a Hardy identity-reset layer before every elementary factor. Given preceding output radii RjR_j, first put rj=ρRj/2r_j=\rho R_j/2. Before a transfer decrease its numerator radius, if needed, so rnum/rden≤ρλ/2r_{\mathrm{num}}/r_{\mathrm{den}} \le\rho\lambda/2.

For the factor’s output radii use

sj=ρrj/2 s_j=\rho r_j/2

except at a positive factorial, where si=2ri/ρs_i=2r_i/\rho, and at a Gaussian, where use

si=ρ2Cce−2|ξ|ri. \boxed{\quad s_i=\frac{\rho}{2C_c}e^{-2|\xi|}r_i. \quad} \tag{6}

Since Re⁡αs=π<2\operatorname{Re}\alpha_s=\sqrt\pi<2,

|cseαsξ|si/ri≤ρ2eπξ−2|ξ|≤ρ/2. |c_se^{\alpha_s\xi}|\,s_i/r_i \le\frac\rho2e^{\sqrt\pi\,\xi-2|\xi|} \le\rho/2. \tag{7}

Thus every cyclic atom argument and every Cauchy-kernel argument has modulus at most ρ/2\rho/2. At a positive factorial the reflected argument has modulus |Qwi/zi|=ρ/2|Qw_i/z_i|=\rho/2; its full scalar kernel is still N(zi/wi)N−1𝒱N(Qwi/zi)N(z_i/w_i)^{N-1}\mathcal V_N(Qw_i/z_i).

These inequalities hold simultaneously for every 𝝃∈ℝm\boldsymbol\xi\in\mathbb R^m. The number of layers is fixed by the word. There is no inequality closing terminal radii back to initial radii, because the fused source is monomial. The construction uses no NN-dependent radius constant on the fixed color chart.

For each 𝝃\boldsymbol\xi, take the product of the positively oriented circles of these radii and its uniquely anchored lift through (5). Denote it by Γ(𝝃)\Gamma(\boldsymbol\xi). The full integration chain is the union of these tori over the oriented base ℝm\mathbb R^m.

The radii are continuous and piecewise smooth because of the minima and absolute values. Their strata glue without additional internal boundary. When the logarithmic torus volume form is wedged with all dξjd\xi_j, its dlog⁡r(𝝃)d\log r(\boldsymbol\xi) terms vanish. Equivalently one integrates the fibers with their usual dz/(2πiz)dz/(2\pi iz) measure. There is no extra radius Jacobian.

3. Absolute convergence on the full fibered chain

The following bounds are uniform in NN and in the real Gaussian variables; constants depend on the fixed word and color chart.

First, all radii can be chosen at most one. A positive factorial’s increase after its reset never exceeds its preceding radius. If LL is the number of elementary factors, put

creset=min⁡(ρ/2,ρ2λ/4),c*=cresetρ/(2Cc)>0. c_{\mathrm{reset}}=\min(\rho/2,\rho^2\lambda/4),\qquad c_* =c_{\mathrm{reset}}\rho/(2C_c)>0.

An induction gives, at every layer,

−log⁡rj≤Cword+2∑ℓ=1m|ξℓ|. -\log r_j\le C_{\mathrm{word}} +2\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}|\xi_\ell|. \tag{8}

One may take CwordC_{\mathrm{word}} to be a fixed multiple of L|log⁡c*|L|\log c_*|. Minima in the radius construction only replace one radius by a fixed constant times another; they do not introduce products of previously accumulated logarithms.

Second, the exact logarithm of (5) gives on |t|≤ρ|t|\le\rho

|log⁡𝒱N(t)|,|log⁡𝒰N(t)|≤−N4log⁡(1−ρ). |\log\mathcal V_N(t)|,\ |\log\mathcal U_N(t)| \le-\frac N4\log(1-\rho). \tag{9}

Here |Qr−1|≥4/N|Q^r-1|\ge4/N for every nontrivial root, and the resonant logarithmic coefficients cancel exactly. All Hardy and Gaussian-dilation kernels are bounded by (1−ρ)−1(1-\rho)^{-1}.

Every reflected factorial contributes its original factor NN and a monomial whose radius ratio is fixed at 2/ρ2/\rho. Every source exponent has magnitude at most N−1N-1. Consequently the absolute scalar density, apart from the unchanged external line χβ,N\chi_{\beta,N} and the normalized Gaussian measure, is bounded by

CNBexp⁡{C0N+C1N∑ℓ|ξℓ|}. C N^B \exp\left\{C_0N+C_1N\sum_\ell|\xi_\ell|\right\}. \tag{10}

Thus the full pullback is dominated by

This is integrable for every NN. With the normalized Gaussian measures its integral is at most C′NBeC2NC'N^B e^{C_2N}. We make no additional growth assertion about the retained external χβ,N\chi_{\beta,N}. These estimates prove Fubini and the disappearance of Gaussian boundary terms at real infinity.

4. Exact original-source identity, with no finite scalar kernels

Let ℋℓ,N(𝝃)\mathcal H_{\ell,N}(\boldsymbol\xi) be the kernels of the cyclic/Hardy network with each finite Gaussian replaced by (4). Let mm be their Gaussian count. The original full fused-plat period is exactly

Pβ,N=χβ,NN1−k(N2π)m/2∫ℝme−N∥𝝃∥2/2⋅∫Γ(𝝃)(z0)ain(zL)−aout∏ℓ=1Lℋℓ,N(𝝃)∏ℓ,jdzjℓ2πizjℓd𝝃. \begin{split} P_{\beta,N} ={}&\chi_{\beta,N}N^{1-k} \left(\frac N{2\pi}\right)^{m/2} \int_{\mathbb R^m} e^{-N\|\boldsymbol\xi\|^2/2}\\ &\quad\cdot \int_{\Gamma(\boldsymbol\xi)} (z^0)^{a_{\mathrm{in}}}(z^L)^{-a_{\mathrm{out}}} \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \mathcal H_{\ell,N}(\boldsymbol\xi) \prod_{\ell,j}\frac{dz_j^\ell}{2\pi iz_j^\ell} \,d\boldsymbol\xi . \end{split} \tag{12}

Every source exponent, factor NN, monomial, norm root, half-character and original external scalar is retained. No finite Γ±,N\Gamma_{\pm,N}, finite Euler polynomial, or finite Dirichlet numerator occurs in its scalar integrand.

For a fixed 𝝃\boldsymbol\xi, the torus contraction is the original completed polynomial word with its Gaussian factors replaced by independent dilations. Each head is still polynomial and all previous support/barrier arguments remain valid. The contraction is a finite sum of products of the dilation moments. Integration of each independent variable gives (2), in its original operator order, and therefore the exact original word.

Estimate (11) also justifies this as an equality of absolutely convergent multiple integrals, rather than only an iterated polynomial calculation.

The full original Ward corner is fixed BEFORE expanding the Gaussian factors. A fixed-𝝃\boldsymbol\xi word need not be a Hopf intertwiner. We do not reapply the Ward corner reduction to such an individual word or replace its transported co-source by a newly chosen cap. Linearity of the already proved full-source functional is what is used in (12).

Any specified root-specialized quantum A-annihilator of the original meridian function therefore annihilates (12) as well. No identification of a classical symbol or selected geometric component is inferred.

5. Uniform leading symbols on the INITIAL cycle

There is now a direct uniform phase comparison for the actual scalar functions on this cycle, without a finite Taylor-remainder sector. For every fixed ρ<1\rho<1,

𝒱N(t)=(1−t)1/2exp⁡{Li⁡2(t)/h}(1+Oρ(N−1)),𝒰N(t)=(1−t)−1/2exp⁡{−Li⁡2(t)/h}(1+Oρ(N−1)). \begin{split} \mathcal V_N(t) &=(1-t)^{1/2} \exp\{\operatorname{Li}_2(t)/h\} (1+O_\rho(N^{-1})),\\ \mathcal U_N(t) &=(1-t)^{-1/2} \exp\{-\operatorname{Li}_2(t)/h\} (1+O_\rho(N^{-1})). \end{split} \tag{13}

The normalized errors are analytic and admit the same bound with any fixed number of derivatives on smaller disks. All logarithms are the anchored branches.

For a direct proof split the logarithmic series log⁡𝒱N(t)=∑N∤mtm/[m(Qm−1)]\log\mathcal V_N(t)=\sum_{N\nmid m}t^m/[m(Q^m-1)] at m=N/2m=N/2. On the lower range,

1ehm−1=1hm−12+O(|h|m). \frac1{e^{hm}-1}=\frac1{hm}-\frac12+O(|h|m).

For example the remainder bound on |hm|≤π|hm|\le\pi follows from the elementary sine/cotangent formula, with its removable value at zero. Summation gives Oρ(1/N)O_\rho(1/N). The upper range is exponentially small in NN, using |Qm−1|−1≤N/4|Q^m-1|^{-1}\le N/4. The omitted resonant coefficients are exponentially small too. Thus

log⁡𝒱N(t)=Li⁡2(t)/h+12Log⁡(1−t)+Oρ(N−1), \log\mathcal V_N(t) =\operatorname{Li}_2(t)/h+\tfrac12\operatorname{Log}(1-t) +O_\rho(N^{-1}),

and Cauchy estimates give the derivative version.

The exact Gaussian weight in (12) is e−Nξ2/2e^{-N\xi^2/2}. On the original color charts, the remaining qq phases shift the small cyclic arguments by eβhe^{\beta h} for fixed half-integer or integer β\beta. Their change is an explicit nonzero amplitude:

𝒱N(eβht)=(1−t)1/2−βeLi⁡2(t)/h(1+Oρ(N−1)), \mathcal V_N(e^{\beta h}t) =(1-t)^{1/2-\beta}e^{\operatorname{Li}_2(t)/h} (1+O_\rho(N^{-1})), \tag{14}

with the inverse formula for 𝒰N\mathcal U_N. The dilation Cauchy kernel has the same type of uniform nonzero density correction, since its argument stays below ρ\rho.

Thus (12) has actual dilogarithm/Gaussian leading symbols and a specified nonzero leading density on its INITIAL fibered cycle. This is a pointwise integrand comparison. It does not imply a relative asymptotic for a cancelling total period, geometric dominance, or a nonzero intersection with a Rivin critical cycle.

6. The remaining global theorem

The finite scalar endpoints have been replaced by global anchored functions and a specified convergent cycle, with exact equality to the original invariant. Their boundary information has not been discarded: it is carried by that cycle, the norm-root branches, the original affine charges and the Cauchy pole data.

To prove a broad volume theorem one must still transport this cycle to the geometric ideal-angle chain, retaining norm-divisor residues, compatible root frames, Cauchy-pole contributions and the original pointed source. The oversized weak alias example shows why an unmarked choice of every nonnegative angle locus would be incorrect. No global transport or volume limit is claimed here.
