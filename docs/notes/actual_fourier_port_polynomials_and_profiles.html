Exact physical port polynomials and global conditional angular profiles

28 October 2026. The ORIGINAL four-full-Psi crossing has exact finite-band Laurent-polynomial interpolants in each port, while the other three ports stay on their specified correlated grids. These are the actual finite Fourier interpolants, with their entire source normalization fixed by a known local kernel entry. Their apparent full-Psi poles cancel after the exact fixed-grid period factors and integer aliases are retained.

This proves a nonzero analytic symbol for a single actual finite Fourier adapter on a compact positive crossing chart. It also gives the unique GLOBAL angular modulus maximum on the whole conditional port circle. It does not justify moving several unrelated ports simultaneously or suppressing a whole knot’s boundary sectors.

1. Actual kernel, grids, and the two integer chamber labels

Use the EXACT original operator and source of the balanced Fourier theorem. Write q=exp(pii/N), Q=q^2, h=2pii/N, d=N-1, x=-q^(-1), X=pi*i-h/2. The marked grids have

z0=r0 Q^i, z1=r1 Q^j,
z6=x r1 Q^k6, z7=x r0 Q^l,
r0^N=A, r1^N=B, A B(B-x^N A)!=0.

Retain a,b,c,d0 for their logarithms and define

k=the representative of l-i modulo N in {1,...,N},
m=the representative of k6-j modulo N in {1-N,...,0}.

Choose the corresponding matched logarithmic lifts so that d0-a-X=k h and c-b-X=m h. The whole-turn characters are one at the original balanced grids, so this does not change M or discard its compensating central factors.

The positive octahedral chamber has

1<=k<=N-1, 2-N<=m<=0.                              (1)

Indeed its positive angle sums are, up to the recorded half-step, theta1+theta3=2pik/N and theta2+theta3=2pi(N+m-1)/N. These are both between zero and 2pi. The EXACT polynomial statements hold for all those cyclic representatives, including k=N and m=1-N. Only the positive-chart estimates need the strict ranges in (1).

Let M be the actual normalized Fourier matrix entry, including its original kappa and every fixed central factor. It is explicitly the four-full-Psi expression (14) of the linked theorem; every grid entry is nonzero on its regular torus.

2. The exact ORIGINAL finite-band interpolants

Write (v;Q)L=product(r=0)(L-1)(1-Qr v). For a single variable Z and all other ports fixed on the ORIGINAL grids, define the following Laurent polynomials:

P0(Z)=Z^m (z1/(xZ);Q)_(N+m-1),
P1(Z)=(Q^(k+1)x z0/Z;Q)_(N-k),
P6(Z)=Z^(N-k)(Q^(1-k)Z/z0;Q)_(k-1),
P7(Z)=(Q^m Z/(x z6);Q)_(-m).                       (2)

P0 and P7 use m in {1-N,…,0}; P1 and P6 use k in {1,…,N}. They have the exact physical spectra

Port Powers Physical frequency indices
input z0 negative -m,…,N-1
input z1 negative 0,…,N-k
output z6 positive N-k,…,N-1
output z7 positive 0,…,-m

Every coefficient inside the stated interval is nonzero. Each interval is contained in the ORIGINAL head band 0,…,N-1.

Choose any fixed grid value Z_* for the port under consideration. Then its exact original Fourier interpolation is

g_port(Z)=M(Z_*) P_port(Z)/P_port(Z_*).              (3)

This preserves the full ORIGINAL scalar. M(Z_) is a known explicit local four-full-Psi value, not an unknown knot period. P_port(Z_) is nonzero since its root central argument is not one. No new normalization is introduced by (3).

Equation (3) agrees with M at EVERY old grid point. Since its physical frequency interval lies in 0,…,N-1, finite Fourier uniqueness makes it the actual original finite-band interpolation. It therefore holds for every complex nonzero Z, with no omitted residues or approximate projection.

At the two cyclic endpoint cases the table is literal: k=N gives P1=1 and P6 of degree N-1; m=1-N gives the input monomial P0=Z^(1-N) and P7 of degree N-1. There is no degree-N norm polynomial substituted for a constant grid row.

3. Full-function proof, including every period and alias factor

In the exact kernel use

beta=b-a-X, f=d0-c-X, rho=c-a,
lambda=d0-b+2pi*i, G=d0-a-X, J=b-c+X.

Its full-function part is Psi(beta)Psi(h-f)Psi(lambda+2pi*i+h)/Psi(rho). First use the EXACT whole-turn identity

Psi(lambda+2pi*i+h)
   =Psi(h+d0-b)/(1-exp[N(d0-b)])^2.                 (4)

On the old grids exp[N(d0-b)]=x^N A/B, and R=exp(Nrho)=x^N B/A. Thus the central density R(1-R)^2 in kernel (14), divided by (1-1/R)^2 from (4), is exactly R^3. For interpolation, these are the fixed old-grid constants. They are retained in the normalizer in (3).

This qualification matters: substituting a moving Z into the RAW four-Psi expression changes those Nth powers. That meromorphic continuation is generally NOT the finite Fourier interpolation. For example (4) creates moving double poles at (z7/Z)^N=1; they are absent from the polynomial (3). No contour is moved across such artificial poles by the present theorem.

For input z0, rho-beta=c-b+X=(N+m-1)h. Hence

Psi(beta)/Psi(rho)=(exp beta;Q)_(N+m-1).

The exact Gaussian in kernel (14) has a-dependent power exp(m a). This gives P0 after the fixed central factors are retained.

For input z1, beta+(d0-b)=G=kh. Full inversion gives

Psi(beta)Psi((k+1)h-beta)
   =zeta exp[pi*i*(sqrt(N)beta/(2pi)-c_b)^2]
          *(Q^(k+1)exp(-beta);Q)_(N-k).

Combining its Gaussian with the ORIGINAL kernel Gaussian leaves exp(-N beta), times a factor independent of b. On the old grid exp(-N beta) is a known fixed central scalar. Retaining that scalar gives precisely P1. This is the integer alias +N b; it is compensated by its known old-grid value.

For output z6, h-f=rho+(1-k)h, so

Psi(h-f)/Psi(rho)=(Q^(1-k)exp rho;Q)_(k-1).

The original Gaussian power is exp[-(N+k)c]. Its fixed-grid alias exp(-2N c) is retained as a central scalar, leaving exp[(N-k)c] and giving P6.

For output z7 put t=h-f. Then

t+(h+d0-b)=(N+m+1)h.

The same full inversion gives a polynomial of length -m, with argument Q^m z7/(xz6). Its Gaussian combines with the original one to give exp(3N d0), independent of the remaining polynomial. This is again a known old-grid central scalar and gives P7.

All displayed Gaussian cancellations are exact, including the affine logarithmic marks and half-step in X. Thus the integer aliases +N b,+2N c,-3N d0 are NOT discarded phases: their known central values are in (3). Nonzero coefficients in (2) follow from the finite q-binomial theorem, since all factorial indices in its lengths are less than N.

There is also a genuine TWO-port result. With b,c fixed on their original grids, the physical interpolant factors exactly:

M_interp(Z0,Z7)=M(z0_*,z7_*)
      *P0(Z0)P7(Z7)/[P0(z0_*)P7(z7_*)].            (3a)

With a,d0 fixed, the analogous formula uses P1(Z1)P6(Z6). To prove this, kernel (14) on the original grids separates into an a-only factor times a d0-only factor when b,c are fixed: its Gaussian has no a*d0 term, and its full functions and central factors split. The other pair splits likewise. Every two-by-two conditional minor is zero. Tensor-product finite Fourier uniqueness then proves (3a) for BOTH free complex variables, with the complete original scalar. This is a paired polynomial identity, not the assertion that the raw four-Psi formula is the same off-grid function.

The pair scalar is explicit and tame

The reference value in (3a) can be eliminated altogether. Use the uniform signed role table and write the paired kernel as c_sigma Pout Pin, with the physical over/under clock h in 0,…,d used there. Then

c_(+,over)=kappa/N *(-1)^h Q^(h(h+1)/2) z6^h,
c_(+,under)=kappa/N *(-1)^h Q^(h(h+1)/2) z0^(-h),
c_(-,over)=kappa^(-1)/N
                     *(-1)^h Q^(-h(h+1)/2) z7^h,
c_(-,under)=kappa^(-1)/N
                     *(-1)^h Q^(-h(h+1)/2) z1^(-h). (3b)

These retain the ENTIRE original crossing scalar and phase. Their magnitudes lie between c_K/N and C_K/N on fixed nonzero central compacta, uniformly in every spectator label and h. Their explicit Gaussian and monomial phases can still carry a leading oscillatory action; tameness is not positivity.

Here is an independent all-level proof directly from ORIGINAL coefficients. For the positive over-pair extract over-input physical frequency h and over-output frequency zero. Since the chosen Pin starts with Z^(-h) and Pout with 1, this coefficient is exactly c. The original sparse crossing gives

c=kappa/N^2 z6^h F_h
      sum_(t=0)^(d-h) [h+t choose t]_Q Q^(-ht).

Root complementation turns its summand into (-1)^t Q^(t(t+1)/2)[d-h choose t]Q. The finite q-binomial theorem makes that sum F(d-h). Finally

F_h F_(d-h)=N(-1)^h Q^(h(h+1)/2).

This proves the first line. For the positive under-pair extract input frequency zero and output frequency h; the same original coefficient sum appears, multiplied by z0^(-h). The exact negative mirror conjugates kappa and Q and swaps ports, proving the other lines. Reversing a Q^(-1) product only reorders its factors and introduces no further scalar. All steps hold for even and composite levels as well.

This improves merely specifying the scalar by a known local value: no hidden exponential magnitude is left in the pair normalization. It also fixes the normalization of every source-preserving bridge contraction in the linked theorem.

4. Actual adapter action and a nonzero analytic symbol

For an outgoing positive-band port the exact finite adapter F(r’)F(r)^(-1) acts on the sampled polynomial by

g(r Q^j) -> g(r' Q^j).                             (5)

For an incoming port the inverse/contravariant adapter has the same evaluation statement with the matching direction and the negative powers in (2). This is finite Fourier interpolation, not a formal pseudodifferential assertion.

Take r’/r=exp(delta/N) with delta in a fixed compact set. On a compact positive octahedral chart every factor in the corresponding Pochhammer arc stays uniformly away from zero. The arc connects two nonreal positive-orientation endpoints without crossing 1. Its length is at most N. Thus

log[g(Z exp(delta/N))/g(Z)]
   =delta p_port(Z)+O_K(1/N),
p_port=(1/N) Z partial_Z log P_port.                (6)

The estimate is uniform, with derivatives: the second logarithmic derivative of each factor is bounded, so the sum of N such derivatives is O_K(N). Equation (6) follows by Taylor expansion through a displacement delta/N. Ordinary Riemann sums on the zero-free arc give p_port’s explicit classical limit with another O_K(1/N) error. The resulting adapter symbol is exp(delta p_port), nonzero and bounded above and below on compact data. The sign convention in (6) is the Laurent power of the kernel: physical incoming momentum is -p_port, whereas physical outgoing momentum is +p_port.

The REAL parts of its physical incoming/outgoing momenta, after the just-proved fixed aliases, are

p_in,0=1-(theta2+theta3)/(4pi),
p_in,1=1/2-(theta1+theta3)/(4pi),
p_out,0=1-(theta1+theta3)/(4pi),
p_out,1=1/2-(theta2+theta3)/(4pi).                  (7)

Every one belongs to (0,1), uniformly away from its ends on every compact positive chamber. At the regular octahedron they are (3/4,1/4;3/4,1/4). This agrees with the ORIGINAL finite-head indices, not an independently selected wave packet.

Equivalently, the exact global linear alias of the original action is 2pi*i(b+2c-3d0), with inverse scalar compensation by exp[-N(b+2c-3d0)]. On the original grids this scalar is known and bounded for fixed central data. Its derivative puts all four momenta in the physical band without changing any actual matrix value or original source.

5. Unique GLOBAL fixed-port angular maxima

Assume additionally |A|!=|B|, as in the fixed regular choice A=2,B=3i. Along each original port circle all Pochhammer roots are radially separated by a constant times 1/N. Uniformly on the ENTIRE conditional angular circle,

sum_(j=0)^(L-1) log|1-e^((gamma+i phi)/N)
                                e^(i theta)Q^j|
   =N integral_0^(L/N) log|1-e^(i(theta+2pi t))|dt
                                                 +O_K(log N). (8)

Here gamma ranges in a compact set bounded away from zero, L<=N, and phi is bounded. To prove (8), regularize the periodic logarithmic singularity at radial distance |gamma|/N. Its total variation is O_K(log N), giving that Riemann-sum error; its integrated change from the boundary logarithm is O_K(log N/N). This also proves the endpoint uniformity. Known monomial and source constants do not depend on theta.

Let L(theta)=D(exp(i theta)). On a fixed positive crossing chart the four angles sum to 2pi. A conditional port varies only one of the following two FULL-circle profiles, up to constant and reversal of its angular coordinate:

H_delta(theta)=L(theta)-L(theta+delta),
J_delta(theta)=L(theta)+L(delta-theta),
                              0<delta<2pi.          (9)

For input z0 and output z6 use H with the sum of the two other relevant positive angles; for input z1 and output z7 use J with the fixed sum of their two varying positive angles. By (8) these are the true leading logarithmic modulus profiles of the ORIGINAL finite polynomials on the whole circle. Explicitly, (2pi/N)log|g_port| equals its H_delta or J_delta profile, plus an angularly constant normalization and O_K(log N/N). The profile itself is not divided by 2pi. All quantum whole-turn factors are already in the fixed source normalization from Section 3.

The maxima in (9) are UNIQUE and GLOBAL:

max H_delta=2L(pi-delta/2), theta_*=pi-delta/2,
max J_delta=2L(delta/2), theta_*=delta/2.            (10)

For H, differentiation gives

H'=log|sin((theta+delta)/2)/sin(theta/2)|.

Its only stationary points are theta_* and theta_+pi. The former is the strict global maximum; the latter is the strict minimum. At shape-1 points the derivative is logarithmically infinite with the same monotone direction on both sides, so no extra endpoint maximum is concealed. For J the same computation gives its maximum theta_ and minimum theta_*+pi. The curvatures at the maxima are -tan(delta/4) and -cot(delta/4), respectively.

At these maxima the two varying positively oriented tetrahedron angles are equal. If the other pair was positive, the maximum lies in the positive chamber. Every other sheet or endpoint on this conditional circle is strictly lower. On compact delta ranges, excluding a fixed neighborhood of theta_* gives a UNIFORM gap.

This is a conditional statement. The other ports are held fixed. Its maximum is not automatically the complete global geometric point after sewing the original knot.

6. Composition scope and the remaining global source issue

The exact formulas may collapse ONE adapter, or the paired adapters of (3a), while the untouched opposite ports remain on their correlated original grids. Nothing here permits applying the other pair’s formula after arbitrary movements of the first pair. In particular a general geometric complex deformation can destroy its integer relation.

The exact deck-character calculation makes the obstruction explicit. If the first pair moves so that D!=sA, then the other input port has mu_b-1=(sD-A)/(A-sB), generically nonzero. The physical FOUR-variable Laurent polynomial remains single-valued; the raw four-Psi continuation does not. Known fixed-grid constants and integer alias multipliers alone cannot turn that raw continuation into a simultaneous equality. An asymptotic comparison in a complex neighborhood may still be possible, but it needs the additional seam terms or their actual bounds, beyond the one-pair theorem.

Known phase oscillation is not removed by the modulus maxima. On the positive real-angle chamber the mixed input-output Hessian of the retained leading REAL action is

partial_(theta_out) partial_(theta_in) Re W
                                  =-(1/2) I_2.

Head-only or input/output-only gauges do not remove it. The exact one-port adapter result (5)–(7) controls its local Fourier action; it does not make the entire sewn original scalar a positive Laplace integral. Global cycle selection, compatible elimination order, and the retained cap/boundary source still need a proof.
