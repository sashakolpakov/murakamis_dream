The actual quantum source at every bridge

Research note, 27 October 2026. This continues the arbitrary-bridge classical contraction using the original finite quantum matrices. It computes all four balanced turns, a literal marked closure, their cyclic boundary sources and a flat completion under the actual meridian half-step. All formulas hold for arbitrary N and arbitrary bridge count.

The completion needs two bulk channels: a single cap’s bulk norm changes sign under the half-step. Keeping only the squared step would miss this. The explicit three-dimensional system below keeps that sign and the boundary source, including at the Kashaev color. This is finite quantum boundary transport. The selected knot period’s comparison with the geometric regulator remains unproved.

1. The original caps lose their factorials exactly

Fix q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, N>=2 and x=-M/q. Write F_a=(Q;Q)_a and w_a=v_a/F_a, both on V(x) and its standard dual color V(x(-1)Q(-1)). No unrolled weight lift is changed. The existing actual turn matrices have

c_a=(-x)^a Q^(a(a+1)/2),
g_b=x^(1-N) Q^(-b).                                  (1.1)

For 0<=a<N, cancellation in the full product F_(N-1)=N gives

F_a F_(N-1-a)=N (-1)^a Q^(a(a+1)/2).                  (1.2)

Indeed F_(N-1)/F_(N-1-a)=product_(r=1)a(1-Q(-r)) =(-1)^a Q^(-a(a+1)/2)F_a. Thus the actual balanced left cap and cup are simply

ev_L(w_a^dual tensor w_b)
                       =x^a/N delta_(a+b,N-1),
coev_L(1)=sum_a N x^(-a) w_(N-1-a) tensor w_a^dual.   (1.3)

The actual right cap and cup are

ev_R(w_b tensor w_a^dual)
            =x^a g_b/N delta_(a+b,N-1),
coev_R(1)=sum_a N x^(-a)g_(N-1-a)^(-1)
                        w_a^dual tensor w_(N-1-a).  (1.4)

On their support the right-cap coefficient is

(Q x^(1-N)/N)(Qx)^a.                                (1.5)

The coefficients (1.3)–(1.4) are whole identities in the original finite matrices. The unbalanced J_x, its exact double dual g and all four snake identities were independently proved in the pivotal duality note. Equations (1.2)–(1.5) do not change their normalization. In particular, every cup contributes exactly N and every cap 1/N, in addition to the displayed monomials and pivots.

2. A marked nilpotent defect gives the reduced closure

In this same balanced basis the actual cut source and co-source are

psi_a=1, phi_a=1/N, e=psi phi, e^2=e, Tr(e)=1.

If the cut operator is C=F_N(M) I, the original reduced evaluation is Tr(e C)=F_N(M). A fully closed ordinary pivotal network instead has Tr(g C)=0. To implement the original selected cut as a marked closed network, insert the specified operator

D=g^(-1)e,      D_(a,b)=x^(N-1)Q^a/N.                (2.1)

Then, for every matrix C, not just scalar ones,

Tr(g D C)=Tr(e C).                                  (2.2)

Moreover

rank D=1, D^2=0, Tr(gD)=1, D g D=D.                 (2.3)

The nilpotence follows from sum_a Q^a=0. This defect is completely fixed by the original pivot and boundary projector. It is not an ordinary Hopf-intertwining local coupon, and its transport through a crossing must carry the actual operator, not replace it by the same local matrix on another strand.

The marked right cap is particularly simple. Insert D on its primal input. Its array, with primal index b and dual index a, is

ev_R^D(w_b tensor w_a^dual)=x^a/N^2,                 (2.4)

with no diagonal-support constraint. Directly, the only summand in ev_R(D w_b tensor w_a^dual) has c=N-1-a, and g_c D_(c,b)=1/N. Contracting (2.4) with coev_L in (1.3) gives the unknot value one, whereas the unmarked cap gives Tr g=0.

Consequently any k-bridge plat can express the original reduced knot evaluation with k cups, k-1 ordinary caps and one marked cap. The exact scalar from these turns is

N^k (1/N)^(k-1) (1/N^2)=1/N.                       (2.5)

All monomials, dual colors, crossing matrices and zero-framing corrections are still present. Formula (2.5) explains the same cut average 1/N for arbitrary k. It does not divide a zero quantum dimension or introduce one modified trace for each bridge. If the unreduced ADO evaluation is used, its whole d_N(M) remains the same separate factor.

3. A bridge has an explicit cyclic boundary source

On a cap’s support use a as the independent index; b=N-1-a. Let T f(a)=f(a+1 mod N) and let e_partial be the delta function at a=N-1. Each of (1.3)–(1.5), and the corresponding cup, has the form

v_a=kappa lambda^a,                                  (3.1)

with kappa a specified nonzero monomial times N or 1/N and lambda a specified Q-power times x or x^(-1). There are exact identities

(T-lambda)v=kappa(1-lambda^N)e_partial,
v=kappa sum_(j=0)^(N-1)lambda^(N-1-j)T^j e_partial.  (3.2)

The second identity follows by inspecting its a-th coefficient. Equivalently (T-lambda)sum lambda(N-1-j)Tj=1-lambda^N. There is no division by 1-lambda^N. The vector remains nonzero and has prescribed anchor v_0=kappa on that divisor as well.

For a left cap lambda=x. At x=-M/q its source factor is

1-x^N=1+(-1)^N M^N.                                (3.3)

At M=1 this is 2 at even N and 0 at odd N. Those values do not allow its whole meridian function to be discarded. In the odd case (3.1) becomes a pointed homogeneous eigenvector; it does not become the zero vector. The inverse-lambda cups retain their corresponding 1-x^(-N) factors.

For disjoint bridges all T_i commute. Applying any subset of the operators T_i-lambda_i to their tensor source gives exactly the product of the displayed boundary factors and the corresponding corner deltas, with all other bridge factors unchanged. This is the entire cubical family of boundary intersections, not only the single-corner terms.

For the marked cap (2.4), the primal index b is free. Its shift in b has multiplier one, and the dual-index shift has

(T_a-x)ev_R^D=(1-x^N)delta_(a,N-1) tensor 1_b/N^2.   (3.4)

Thus its removal of the ordinary cap relation is part of the actual selected source. It cannot be replaced by an unmarked inverse-meridian cap when comparing period complexes.

The principal cap equations are the inverse-meridian equations

This paragraph concerns the q->1 principal symbol, not a fixed root’s center. Let U be the primal position and V the dual position. The support in (1.3) gives UV=1 at the symbol. Write p_P,p_D for their balanced momenta. The cup coefficient x^(-a) gives p_D/p_P=x^(-1). A cap is a co-source, so its phase has the opposite momentum sign; the leading x^a factor gives the same relation. The right pivotal monomials and their base-only meridian phases are still retained, as in the existing actual action calculation.

This has a direct matrix verification. For one balanced meridian use

K=x/U, e=(1-x^2/U)p_P/x, f=(1-U)/p_P,
b_+=[K e;0 1], b_-=[1 0;-Kf K].                     (3.5)

For its dual use x_D=x^(-1), position V and momentum p_D. Substituting V=U^(-1),p_D=p_P/x gives exactly

b_(+,D)b_(+,P)=I,
b_(-,D)b_(-,P)=I.                                  (3.6)

Indeed K_D=K(-1),e_D=-K(-1)e,f_D=-Kf. Under the already proved prefix transformation H_j=B_(-,j-1)^(-1)G_j B_(-,j-1), equation (3.6) is precisely the inverse-meridian cap in the arbitrary-plat representation scheme. This checks the cap from the actual quantum coefficients, rather than assigning a classical inverse relation independently.

The marked cap (2.4) instead imposes p_P=1,p_D=x^(-1), with no position constraint of its own. At every bottom cup both total Borel products are I. Each crossing preserves them. All ordinary top caps also have product I. Therefore the two products at the remaining marked pair must be I; on the regular chart this restores V=U^(-1). The extra marked equation p_P=1 is exactly the fixed inverse-eigenflag: direct substitution gives

G_P(1,1)^t=x^(-1)(1,1)^t.                            (3.7)

Conversely (3.7) forces p_P=1 when its stated shape factors are units. It follows that the regular principal critical scheme of the actual pointed plat is the knot representation scheme with this selected peripheral flag fixed to 1. The inverse maps are the same rational prefix/Gauss maps already proved for crossings. The identities hold over Artin rings on that chart, since every inverse used is a unit there. The retained meridian is M=-x at the symbol, so the selected actual eigenvalue is M^(-1), consistent with the original cut convention.

Thus every bridge’s quantum turn, boundary source and principal holonomy constraint now come from the same finite matrices. This does not perform the remaining selected global period pairing.

4. The actual meridian half-step forces a second bulk channel

Put A=Q^a and let delta x=qx, delta A=A. This is the actual M->qM step, not its square. First normalize kappa=1 and lambda=x. The internal bulk monodromy of T-lambda is X=x^N. The half-step sends X to -X. Therefore an invertible commuting meridian transport cannot preserve a two-dimensional affine system whose internal monodromy has only eigenvalues X and 1: its characteristic polynomial would have to be both (z-X)(z-1) and (z+X)(z-1). This fails over the generic coefficient field.

There is an explicit minimal three-dimensional completion. Put

v_0(a,x)=x^a, v_1(a,x)=(qx)^a, w=(v_0,v_1,1)^t,
B_T=[x 0 (1-X)e_partial;
     0 qx (1+X)e_partial;
     0 0 1],
B_delta=[0 1 0; A 0 0; 0 0 1].                    (4.1)

Then

T w=B_T w, delta w=B_delta w,
T(B_delta) B_T=delta(B_T) B_delta.                  (4.2)

For the last identity use T(A)=QA and QA e_partial=e_partial. The first bulk channel goes to the second, and delta v_1=A v_0. The two boundary factors 1-X and 1+X are swapped. Both matrices are invertible for x!=0, even at X=1 or X=-1; their determinants are qx^2 and -A.

The internal N-fold monodromy has eigenvalues X,-X,1. Hence three is the least possible generic rank for a flat completion retaining this bulk sector and its trivial source. The preceding spectral argument is a rank obstruction, not a choice of an extra physical state. The selected cap is still its specified first component v_0 with its anchor. There are two bulk channels because the meridian transports their central characters.

For a turn with lambda=q^s x^epsilon, epsilon=+1 or -1, and kappa=c x^r (c fixed under delta), replace x,qx by lambda,q^epsilon lambda, replace A by A^epsilon, and multiply B_delta by q^r. The third selected component is then kappa, and the first two are kappa lambda^a and kappa(q^epsilon lambda)^a. The same direct calculation proves flatness. This covers the actual cups, caps and marked cap, with their full normalizations. No inverse of 1+X or 1-X occurs.

The equivalent pointed norm-one frame

The three-dimensional assertion keeps the unmodified bulk norm and its source as separate data. It is not a claim that a known cap vector intrinsically needs three components. There is also an explicit rank-one norm torsor which includes the last transition in its multiplier. On the supported orbit put

h(A)=sum_(a=0)^(N-1) q^a e_a(A), h^2=A,
T(h)=q h (1-2e_partial),
a_T=lambda[1+(lambda^(-N)-1)e_partial],
a_delta=q^r h^epsilon.                              (4.3)

Every displayed multiplier is a unit when lambda!=0. The last internal multiplier is lambda^(1-N), so

Norm_T(a_T)=1, T(v)=a_T v, delta(v)=a_delta v,
T(a_delta) a_T=delta(a_T) a_delta.                   (4.4)

To check the mixed square, use the ordinary q^epsilon factor off the cut and T(h)(N-1)=1 at the cut. Equations (4.3)–(4.4) also follow from a_T=T(v)/v and a_delta=delta(v)/v with the explicit anchored unit v=kappa lambda^a. Thus this cap’s Hilbert-90 frame and full half-step transport are solved exactly, including at lambda^N=1. The boundary defect is recoverable as (a_T-lambda)v=kappa(1-lambda^N)e_partial. It is encoded in the retained cut multiplier, rather than discarded.

For fixed q, h is a specified polynomial in A of degree <N; its two square-root choices are not interchangeable. Its cut sign is exactly what the two bulk channels in (4.1) record. The meridian transport has 2N-fold norm one, and the entire frame is fixed by v_0=kappa. This gives the explicit torsor description of every turn alongside its affine-source presentation.

5. All bridges share one half-step, rather than independent parity choices

The construction has a uniform simultaneous version. For k bridges let lambda_i=q(s_i)x(epsilon_i), epsilon_i in {+1,-1}, A_i=Q^(a_i), X_i=lambda_i^N and kappa=c x^r the full retained monomial normalization. Use a component u_empty=kappa, and for each nonempty subset I of {1,…,k} use two components

u_(I,b)=kappa product_(i in I)
                       (q^(b epsilon_i)lambda_i)^(a_i),
b=0,1.                                             (5.1)

There are 2^(k+1)-1 components, independent of N. The equations retain two synchronized half-step channels for each nonempty subset, sharing one empty-subset source. Set u_(empty,0)=u_(empty,1)=u_empty. Their internal equations are

T_i u_(I,b)=u_(I,b),                    i notin I,
T_i u_(I,b)=q^(b epsilon_i)lambda_i u_(I,b)
      +(1-(-1)^b X_i)e_(partial,i)u_(I\{i},b), i in I.
                                                    (5.2)

The full meridian step is

delta u_empty=q^r u_empty,
delta u_(I,0)=q^r u_(I,1),
delta u_(I,1)=q^r product_(i in I)A_i^epsilon_i
                                         u_(I,0).  (5.3)

These are commuting flat transport matrices. For distinct internal directions, lowering subsets commutes and their coefficients depend only on their own clock. To check a mixed square with delta, use T_i(A_i)=QA_i and QA_i e_(partial,i)=e_(partial,i), just as in (4.2); after lowering I the remaining product is exactly that in (5.3) for I{i}. The empty subset has the same retained scalar character q^r. This proves flatness for every k and N.

The actual simultaneous bridge source is the component u_({1,…,k},0), with the known diagonal-support embeddings and, at the distinguished cap, its extra constant primal factor. Thus the source intersections and actual half-step transport have a finite explicit description for unbounded bridge number. The exponential dependence here is on k, not on the root level N.

Their determinant line has no boundary denominator. With R_k=2^(k+1)-1 the explicit matrices give

det B_(T_i)=(q^epsilon_i lambda_i^2)^(2^(k-1)),
det B_delta=-q^(r R_k)
                product_i A_i^(epsilon_i 2^(k-1)).  (5.4)

The first identity counts the 2^(k-1) subsets containing i. For the second, each nonempty-subset block has determinant -product_(i in I)A_i^epsilon_i and every i occurs 2^(k-1) times. All are units. In fact B_delta^(2N)=I, and its determinant has meridian norm one. These statements retain the actual half-step characters, with no division by a factor that becomes 0 or 2 at completeness.

6. Exact propagation through the actual crossing word

Let B_N(x) be any actual oriented braid operator in the plat, using the original R matrices and the literal turned matrices J_V(-1)Ct J_W where appropriate. They are invertible; same-color formulas cannot be substituted on a dual-colored turn.

For the cup vector U and any finite boundary equation L U=b, the transported equations and source are exactly

(B_N L B_N^(-1))(B_N U)=B_N b.                     (6.1)

For several source channels apply B_N to all components. If delta U=C_delta U, the meridian transport becomes

delta(B_N U)=delta(B_N) C_delta B_N^(-1)(B_N U).     (6.2)

For an internal cyclic shift replace delta(B_N) by T_i B_N T_i^(-1). Consequently all commuting squares in Sections 4–5 survive; this is an identity in the finite matrix algebra, including the corner projectors. The co-source is transported by B_N^(-t). Its pairing with the source is unchanged. No central character, corner source, pivot or selected normalization is replaced by a scalar chosen after specialization.

Equations (1.3)–(6.2), together with the marked cap, specify the original quantum boundary data for arbitrary k. They go beyond assigning unnamed b_s to the classical contraction. They do not yet prove that those full finite data descend to its geometric normal deformation complex, or that their selected scalar has the regulator’s norm.

7. Boundary norms at completeness and the remaining volume problem

At M=1 the balanced g is unitary, e is an orthogonal projector and D=g^(-1)e has operator norm one. The actual cups have norm N^(3/2), ordinary caps N^(-1/2), and the marked cap N^(-1). For a fixed k-bridge presentation their product has norm

N^(k-1/2).                                         (7.1)

The zero-framing character has modulus one. In these specified balanced bases, Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives

|F_N(1)| <= N^(k-1/2) ||B_N(1)||.                   (7.2)

The same control retains bounded meridian jets. Write u=Log M. Every coefficient in the tensor of all turns is a monomial in M; its total exponent has absolute value at most 2kN. Consequently, for each fixed j, its j-th u derivative has norm at most

(2kN)^j N^(k-1/2).                                 (7.3)

This estimates the actual whole boundary germ, rather than only its value at M=1. The unchanged unreduced peripheral factor is

d_N(e^u)=i^(N+3) sinh(u)/sinh(Nu).                   (7.4)

The fixed-order Taylor coefficients of N i(-N-3)d_N(eu) are polynomials in N of degree at most their order. The j-th derivative of d_N at zero is therefore O_j(N^(j-1)); the odd derivatives vanish. Thus retaining that whole germ also preserves polynomial boundary-jet control. No factor 1+M^N or 1-M^N has been evaluated and canceled separately in deriving these bounds.

If the zero-framing monomial is included with the boundary data, replace 2k in (7.3) by 2k+|w|, where w is the fixed diagram’s writhe. Its value at completeness has modulus one.

This is a uniform polynomial boundary bound. It does not give a lower bound on the selected matrix coefficient or rule out an exponentially small overlap. Any volume proof through the A-torsor must still control that coupling and compare the resulting pointed period with the calibrated geometric action. The nonzero boundary vectors, flat completion and fixed normalization proved here do not supply that remaining theorem by themselves.
