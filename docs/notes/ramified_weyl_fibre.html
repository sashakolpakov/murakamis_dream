The complete quantum period as a marked fibre after ramified Weyl descent

Research note, 26 October 2026. The complete Kashaev value, previously written as a first normal derivative, is an ordinary linear evaluation on a specified descended lattice. This is an exact algebraic construction at each level, with the first-order information retained in the lattice. It is not an asymptotic argument or an identification with the Bloch regulator. A comparison that fixes only the ordinary root fibre can still change the period; an explicit example below exhibits the missing marking.

The input is the actual Weyl theorem, including the primitive normal operator’s half-root and sign. The actual peripheral calculation fixes its boundary lattice. No different state or quantization is introduced.

1. The actual orbit and its two involutions

Fix N>=2, q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2, Y=M^2, and the central coordinate U=Y^N. The actual normal state is v_N(Y)=(Y-1)F_N(Y). At a regular operator specialization write

B=sum_j b_j(q,Y)T^j, T Y=QY T,
B J=J' B,
J=-Y R, J'=sigma*q^(dm)*Y^m*T^d R,
R h(Y)=h(Y^(-1)), sigma in {1,-1}, m in Z.     (1.1)

These quantum symmetries are proved for the actual primitive minimal normal operator. The state satisfies J v_N=v_N. All statements below also apply to a specified nonminimal operator with the checked same kind of intertwiner; their rank conclusions must be checked separately.

Near U=1 choose the algebraic roots

Y_r(U)=Q^r U^(1/N), 0<=r<N, U^(1/N)|_(U=1)=1.

Let B_N(U) be the matrix acting on the orbit values. Its entries are

(B_N(U))_(r,r+j)=b_j(q,Q^r U^(1/N)),          (1.2)

with coincident columns added modulo N. The quantum involutions on orbit vectors act semilinearly over U->U^(-1):

(J_U h)(U)=U^(1/N)*J_0*h(U^(-1)),
(J'_U h)(U)=U^(m/N)*J'_0*h(U^(-1)),
(J_0)_(r,-r)=-Q^r,
(J'_0)_(r,-r-d)=sigma*q^(dm)*Q^(mr).          (1.3)

All indices are modulo N. Direct multiplication gives J_02=J’_02=I. They are unitary in the orbit metric. This includes even levels; the q^(dm) in the second involution has not been replaced by a guessed Q-power.

The semilinear identity corresponding to (1.1) is

B_N(U) U^(1/N) J_0
      =U^(m/N) J'_0 B_N(U^(-1)).             (1.4)

2. Linearizing the ramification, with explicit algebraic frames

Use the marked rational parameter

z=(U-1)/(U+1), U=(1+z)/(1-z), s=z^2.

Then U inversion is exactly z->-z. The completed local rings are R_z=C[[z]] and R_s=C[[s]]. Choose the units

Gamma_in=U^(1/(2N)), Gamma_out=U^(m/(2N)),

both equal to one at z=0. They are algebraic roots, unramified there. Thus the formal calculation may equally be performed on an etale local algebraic cover with these specified roots. Set

x_N=Gamma_in^(-1)*v_N,
B_tilde=Gamma_out^(-1)*B_N*Gamma_in
       =U^((1-m)/(2N))*B_N.

Equations (1.3)–(1.4) become

x_N(z)=J_0 x_N(-z),
B_tilde(z)J_0=J'_0 B_tilde(-z).              (2.1)

This is an explicit linearization, rather than a presumed unramified descent theorem. Write E_+,E_- for the two eigenspaces of J_0 and E’+,E’- for those of J’_0.

3. The descended lattice retains the negative first coefficient

An invariant vector has a unique form

x(z)=a(s)+z b(s), a(s) in R_s E_+,
                        b(s) in R_s E_-.

Consequently its invariant lattice is the free R_s-module

Lambda_in=R_s E_+ direct-sum z R_s E_-.       (3.1)

The symbol z e_- is a generator of the second summand as an R_s-module. Its class is nonzero in Lambda_in/s Lambda_in, even though its value as a function at z=0 is zero. There is the exact special-fibre sequence

0 -> E_- -> Lambda_in/s Lambda_in -> E_+ -> 0. (3.2)

The last map records the ordinary value a(0); the kernel records the coefficient b(0). Evaluating first at z=0 and then taking the J_0 invariants retains only E_+ and discards that kernel. The two operations are therefore different.

There is also an intrinsic filtration. The invariants of z times the original lattice are

F_(1/2)Lambda_in=s R_s E_+ direct-sum z R_s E_-.

Its special fibre is the subspace E_- in (3.2). This is the elementary weight-one-half parabolic structure of the ramified double cover. The output lattice is constructed identically using E’+,E’-.

The descended actual operator

Use constant eigenframes for the four eigenspaces. Equation (2.1) forces the exact block form

B_tilde(z)=[ A(s)    z C(s) ]
           [ z D(s)    E(s) ].

Therefore its map on the invariant lattices is

B_desc(s)=[ A(s)    s C(s) ]
          [ D(s)      E(s) ],
B_desc(0)=[ A(0)       0  ]
          [ D(0)    E(0) ].                  (3.3)

The blocks may be rectangular; the total matrix is N by N. If B_N(U)=B_0+(U-1)B_1+…, then

B_tilde_z(0)=2B_1+(1-m)B_0/N.               (3.4)

Thus D(0) is its block from E_+ to E’-. The negative block E(0) is exactly B_0 restricted from E- to E’-, the operator previously called B-.

This construction is functorial. If an equivariant matrix G(z) has the analogous blocks, its descended matrix is obtained by the same replacement of z C by s C and z D by D. Substitution shows (G_1 G_2)_desc=(G_1)_desc (G_2)_desc. In particular an invertible equivariant lattice comparison descends to an invertible comparison preserving the filtration in (3.2). This proves the needed elementary descent directly.

4. The original Kashaev value is a special-fibre evaluation

For the actual state, let (a_0,b_0) be the class of x_N in the fibre of (3.1). The ordinary part is already specified:

a_0=v_0,
(v_0)_r=(Q^r-1)C_r(q), 1<=r<N,
(v_0)_0=0,
(a_0)_1/(Q-1)=1.                             (4.1)

The last equality is the fixed ordinary-color anchor. It shows that the actual section is primitive at the branch point; it has not been multiplied by a vanishing central factor.

Let e_0 be the complete-coordinate vector. It lies in E_-. Since dU/dz=2 at zero and Gamma_in,z(0)=1/N, differentiating the explicit algebraic frame gives

x_N,z(0)=2v_N,U(1)-v_0/N=b_0,
K_N=F_N(1)=(N/2)*e_0^* b_0,
N_N(1)=(eta_N/2)*e_0^* b_0, eta_N=i^(N+3).    (4.2)

The final equation is the complete full ADO period. Thus its value is an ordinary linear functional on a specified algebraic fibre. The derivative notation is no longer needed to evaluate it once the descended lattice is retained. Its first-order data have not been removed; they are the second summand in that fibre.

This statement uses no kernel-rank or sector-injectivity hypothesis. It applies to the actual state for every knot and N. At fixed N, the state, its frames, a(s), b(s), and this evaluation are algebraic. Their presentations vary with N; this is not a fixed-degree algebraic normalization across all levels or a growth bound.

The affine source is a lifting equation in the same fibre

The actual equation descends to

A(0)a_0=0, E(0)b_0=-D(0)a_0.                (4.3)

For this particular a_0, (3.4) and B_0 a_0=0 give D(0)a_0=2B_1 a_0. Thus the second equation is exactly the preceding normal-source equation, with its reflection sector built into the algebraic fibre. Its set of lifts b_0, if nonempty, is an affine torsor under ker E(0). The actual state supplies a point.

If E(0) is injective, this point and the period are determined by the source alone. More generally the scalar is unique when e_0^* annihilates ker E(0). These are the previous precise conditions; ramified descent does not prove them automatically.

When the generic kernel over C((s)) is a line, its saturated intersection with Lambda_in is a free rank-one R_s-module. The actual section (4.1) generates it, because its anchor is a unit. This supplies a pointed line whose fibre and (4.2) remain defined even if B_desc(0) itself has an extra kernel. One must specialize the saturated kernel lattice, rather than replace it by the possibly larger kernel of the special matrix. Generic rank one is an additional hypothesis for this operator-only description; the actual state-defined line does not require it.

The subsequent torsion initial-rank theorem supplies a finite sufficient condition for this generic line. A rational-coefficient actual annihilator with exactly one occupied binomial torsion curve in its initial form has generic rational rank one at all sufficiently large levels, even with non-toric remaining factors. Its primitive minimal actual right factor inherits the rank. This is an operator certificate, not a claim that every knot operator satisfies it; the special-sector rank and marked regulator norm remain additional questions.

5. Identity on the ordinary fibre does not fix this marking

There is a sharp two-dimensional example. Use J_0=diag(1,-1),

B_tilde(z)=[ 0  0 ], x(z)=(1,-z)^t.
           [ z  1 ]

Then B_tilde*x=0 and the equivariance (2.1) holds. The descended operator is [[0,0],[1,1]], and the descended fibre vector is (1,-1). Its second coordinate is the retained normal period.

For any c, the equivariant invertible frame change

G(z)=[ 1   0 ], G(0)=I,
     [ cz  1 ]

descends to G_desc(0)=[[1,0],[c,1]]. It sends that fibre vector to (1,c-1). Hence a frame change that is identity on the ordinary root fibre, and preserves the lattice and its filtration, can change the normal period by an arbitrary scalar. Transporting the period functional along with G restores its value; omitting that transport loses precisely the extra marking.

This is an algebraic example about comparisons, not a knot counterexample. It shows why ordinary-fibre agreement or a normalization of a determinant does not alone identify the selected source period.

6. A parity obstruction to the optional injectivity condition

The dimensions of the domain sectors are

dim E_-=(N+1)/2 if N is odd, N/2 if N is even,
tr J_0=-1 if N is odd, 0 if N is even.

The range trace, including the full half-root, is exactly

tr J'_0=sum_(0<=r<N, 2r+d=0 mod N)
             sigma*(-1)^(m*(d+2r)/N).        (6.1)

Only fixed coordinates contribute to the trace, and q^N=-1 gives the displayed sign. Consequently E(0):E_->E’_- cannot be injective whenever tr J’_0>tr J_0.

For even N this obstruction occurs precisely when d and m are even and sigma=+1. For odd N>d, it occurs precisely when sigma*(-1)^m=+1 for odd d, or sigma=+1 for even d. The general formula (6.1) also covers the finitely many smaller levels.

These are diagnostics for the proposed first-order inversion, not restrictions already proved for every primitive knot operator. If the obstruction occurs, one must keep the saturated kernel and the further source/lifting data; assuming sector injectivity would be false for that signature.

7. Metric and the remaining regulator comparison

Choose orthonormal constant frames of E_+,E_-. Along |U|=1 the specified Gamma_in is unitary. Orthogonality then gives exactly

||v_N(U)||^2=||a(s)||^2+|z|^2||b(s)||^2.      (7.1)

The regular metric on the descended fibre records the coefficients as ||a_0||2+||b_0||2. This is a renormalization of the vanishing negative-weight part; it is not the result of setting z=0 in the original orbit norm. The Kashaev functional has norm N/2, and the full ADO functional has norm 1/2. Neither gives a lower bound on its overlap with the actual vector. The specified functional remains essential.

The positive triangulation and actual classical action already supply the marked Bloch period with norm logarithm 2Vol/pi. The quantum object to compare now has an explicit algebraic special fibre, its filtration, ordinary anchor, and functional (4.2). A geometric comparison must carry that marked source period; matching only an unpointed module or its ordinary fibre does not suffice. The target becomes

(4/N)log|e_0^* b_0| -> 2Vol/pi,

equivalent to the original statement because e_0^*b_0=2K_N/N. No such norm comparison or nonzero geometric contribution is proved here. Ramified descent identifies the exact object to compare; it does not establish its volume growth.

The subsequent threaded-character theorem also fixes interior data on this marked fibre. Every 2N-threaded loop acts by two at U=1 on both a_0 and b_0, and null-homologous loops act by two on the full family. A commutator separates this character from fixed hyperbolic central data. An actual source extension carrying a compatible separating observable can select its horizontal source-one point by a spectral projector, without a further rank assumption; the extension and its marked regulator norm have not been constructed by that criterion alone.

8. Coverage and checks

The later Frobenius Verma-period construction gives an explicit alternate root realization F_N/Delta_K(U), retaining the entire Alexander factor. Reciprocity and Delta_K(1)=1 give 1/Delta_K(U)=1+O(z^2), z=(U-1)/(U+1). Consequently both marked fiber coordinates a_0,b_0 and the complete period functional above are unchanged. This is a specified central gauge, including its polar divisor away from the complete fiber; it is not an arbitrary normalization of the period.

This elementary ramified descent belongs to existing parabolic-bundle theory. Coverage includes Borne, Fibres paraboliques et champ des racines, and Biswas–Borne, Tamely ramified torsors and parabolic bundles. Only their abstracts and scope were consulted; no descent or torsor proof from either is used above, and no novelty claim is made for the general framework. The concrete contribution here is the explicit linearization and marked fibre for the fixed ADO state, including its half-root, source, metric and parity signatures.

The audit checks 18 actual knot cut polynomials through z-order five at levels 2 through 7, recovering their exact complete values without a rank assumption. Eleven tests of the previously certified finite cyclotomic operator check the descended equation through s-order two and its special rank, at even/composite/odd levels through 18. Equality with the actual figure-eight cut is tested only at the seven listed levels through 8; an all-level state equality is not inferred. Another 528 signatures check the trace and dimension formulas, and exact symbolic examples check functoriality and the change-of-frame obstruction in Section 5. Results are algebraic diagnostics, not an asymptotic proof.
