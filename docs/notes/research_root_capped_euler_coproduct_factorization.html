Exponentially small root carries in an actual Euler coproduct

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The finite Euler polynomial of the actual total lowering operator factors, up to an exponentially small operator carry, into a reversed ordered product of the finite one-head coproduct factors. The agreement below total degree NN is exact. The carry is bounded before any coherent evaluation and remains exponentially small after multiplication by either actual operator inverse.

The estimates hold in the original binomial metric, including a genuine complex meridian collar and its fixed derivatives. On any fixed compact coherent chart away from incidence, shrinking the activity disk makes the carry beat the exact conditioning cost of coherent evaluation. This does not prove a nonzero phase for the individual factors or for their product.

The complete directs C and J are pinned below. C supplies the finite logarithm estimate; J supplies the actual ordered coproduct and source conventions. The factorization, norm estimates, and coherent comparison are proved here.

1. Actual finite operators and the required order

Fix r≥1r\ge1 heads and N≥2,d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,fn=∏j=1n(1−Qj),f0=1.(1) N\ge2,\quad d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad f_n=\prod_{j=1}^n(1-Q^j),\quad f_0=1. \qquad\text{(1)} The quantum root remains fixed. Each head has the original metric ∥wa∥2=(da)−1.(2) \|w_a\|^2=\binom da^{-1}. \qquad\text{(2)} At common meridian M≠0M\ne0, the actual matrices are E(M)wa=(MQ−a−M−1)wa−1(a>0),E(M)w0=0,K(M)wa=−Mq−1Q−awa.(3) E(M)w_a=(MQ^{-a}-M^{-1})w_{a-1}\quad(a>0),\quad E(M)w_0=0, \qquad K(M)w_a=-M q^{-1}Q^{-a}w_a. \qquad\text{(3)} All tensor metrics are products of (2). Define Xi(M)=Ei(M)∏j>iKj(M),X(M)=∑i=1rXi(M)=Etot(M).(4) X_i(M)=E_i(M)\prod_{j>i}K_j(M),\qquad X(M)=\sum_{i=1}^rX_i(M)=E_{\mathrm{ tot}}(M). \qquad\text{(4)} These are exactly J’s ordered lowering coproduct terms, with all Cartan factors present.

Since KE=QEKKE=QEK, one has XiXj=QXjXi(i<j),XiN=0,XN=0.(5) X_iX_j=QX_jX_i\quad(i<j),\qquad X_i^N=0,\qquad X^N=0. \qquad\text{(5)} For the last identity, the finite binomial formula has zero intermediate coefficients in degree NN: its numerator contains 1−QN1-Q^N, whereas its denominators in the intermediate terms contain only 1−Qj1-Q^j, 0<j<N0<j<N. Induction over the heads proves the assertion. These are whole-meridian finite-matrix identities.

Write EN(t)=∑n=0dtnfn,𝒢N(t,M)=EN(tX(M)),𝒫N(t,M)=EN(tXr(M))⋯EN(tX1(M)).(6) E_N(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{d}\frac{t^n}{f_n},\qquad \mathcal G_N(t,M)=E_N(tX(M)),\qquad \mathcal P_N(t,M)=E_N(tX_r(M))\cdots E_N(tX_1(M)). \qquad\text{(6)} The order in 𝒫N\mathcal P_N is decreasing head index. For n<Nn<N, the finite binomial identity gives Xnfn=∑n1+⋯+nr=nXrnr⋯X1n1fnr⋯fn1.(7) \frac{X^n}{f_n} =\sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_r=n} \frac{X_r^{n_r}\cdots X_1^{n_1}} {f_{n_r}\cdots f_{n_1}}. \qquad\text{(7)} One elementary proof for two terms with AB=QBAAB=QBA is to expand (A+B)n(A+B)^n in the order BbAaB^bA^a. Multiplication by A+BA+B gives the finite Gaussian-binomial recurrence, with coefficient fn/(fafb)f_n/(f_af_b). All denominators are nonzero for n<Nn<N. Iteration gives (7).

Thus 𝒢N=[𝒫N]<N,𝒞N:=𝒫N−𝒢N=∑n=NrdPN,n(M)tn.(8) \boxed{\quad \mathcal G_N=[\mathcal P_N]_{<N},\qquad \mathcal C_N:=\mathcal P_N-\mathcal G_N =\sum_{n=N}^{rd}P_{N,n}(M)t^n . \quad}\qquad\text{(8)} For r=1r=1 this carry is zero. For more heads it is retained as the specified finite polynomial. No exact generic-parameter infinite-product factorization has been specialized through a root singularity.

2. Exact finite logarithms and both inverses

Set LN(t)=∑j=1dtjj(1−Qj).(9) L_N(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{t^j}{j(1-Q^j)}. \qquad\text{(9)} C proves directly from the coefficient recurrence that ENE_N is the degree-dd Taylor polynomial of exp⁡LN\exp L_N. It follows, for every actual matrix YY with YN=0Y^N=0, that EN(tY)=exp⁡LN(tY),EN(tY)−1=exp⁡[−LN(tY)].(10) \boxed{\quad E_N(tY)=\exp L_N(tY),\qquad E_N(tY)^{-1}=\exp[-L_N(tY)] . \quad}\qquad\text{(10)} These are exact matrix identities for every tt, not small-tt asymptotics. Terms of degree at least NN vanish because they contain YNY^N. In particular both matrices in (6) are invertible for all tt. The inverse of the product has the reversed factor order.

There is an absolute C>0C>0 such that ∥LN(tY)∥≤CN|t|∥Y∥,|t|∥Y∥≤1/4.(11) \|L_N(tY)\|\le CN|t|\,\|Y\|, \qquad |t|\,\|Y\|\le1/4. \qquad\text{(11)} For clarity this does not require a spectral norm equality. Bound the sum in (9) by ∑j(|t|∥Y∥)j/[j|1−Qj|]\sum_j(|t|\|Y\|)^j/[j|1-Q^j|]. The elementary sine inequality |1−Qj|≥4min⁡(j,N−j)/N |1-Q^j|\ge4\min(j,N-j)/N bounds the terms j≤N/2j\le N/2 by N(|t|∥Y∥)j/(4j2)N(|t|\|Y\|)^j/(4j^2). The upper half has a geometric factor (|t|∥Y∥)N/2(|t|\|Y\|)^{N/2} and a harmonic sum, uniformly bounded by the right side of (11). This proves the estimate. Consequently ∥EN(tY)∥,∥EN(tY)−1∥≤exp⁡(CN|t|∥Y∥).(12) \|E_N(tY)\|,\ \|E_N(tY)^{-1}\| \le \exp(CN|t|\,\|Y\|). \qquad\text{(12)} The inverse bound is essential to the relative carry estimate.

3. Original-metric bounds on a genuine meridian collar

At M=1M=1, the weighted lowering coefficient in (3) has norm 2sin⁡(πa/N)N−aa≤2πaN(1−a/N)≤π.(13) 2\sin(\pi a/N)\sqrt{\frac{N-a}{a}} \le 2\pi\sqrt{\frac aN(1-a/N)}\le\pi. \qquad\text{(13)} Here 1≤a≤d1\le a\le d; the forced bottom arrow is zero. If Lwa=wa−1Lw_a=w_{a-1} with its bottom zero, then ∥L∥=d,E(M)=ME(1)+(M−M−1)L,∥K(M)∥=|M|.(14) \|L\|=\sqrt d,\qquad E(M)=M E(1)+(M-M^{-1})L,\qquad \|K(M)\|=|M|. \qquad\text{(14)} Thus no unscaled meridian disk with a uniform small-activity domain is being assumed.

Fix η>0\eta>0 and use the actual complex collar M=exp⁡(u/N),|u|≤η.(15) M=\exp(u/\sqrt N),\qquad |u|\le\eta. \qquad\text{(15)} Equations (13)–(14) give ∥E(M)∥≤πeη+2ηeη,∥Xi(M)∥≤B,∥X(M)∥≤rB,(16) \|E(M)\|\le \pi e^\eta+2\eta e^\eta,\qquad \|X_i(M)\|\le B,\qquad \|X(M)\|\le rB, \qquad\text{(16)} where B=B(r,η)B=B(r,\eta) is independent of NN. For example the first bound follows from |M−M−1|≤2|u|e|u|/N/N|M-M^{-1}|\le2|u|e^{|u|/\sqrt N}/\sqrt N; the remaining Cartan factors in (4) give a bounded factor erηe^{r\eta}. Their exact values have not been dropped. The customary smaller collar M=exp⁡(v/N)M=\exp(v/N), with bounded vv, is contained in (15) for large NN.

Choose R>0R>0 with RrB≤1/4RrB\le1/4. Equations (12),(16) imply, for |t|≤R|t|\le R, ∥𝒢N(t,M)∥,∥𝒢N(t,M)−1∥≤eAN|t|,∥𝒫N(t,M)∥,∥𝒫N(t,M)−1∥≤eAN|t|,(17) \begin{split} \|\mathcal G_N(t,M)\|,\ \|\mathcal G_N(t,M)^{-1}\| &\le e^{A N|t|},\\ \|\mathcal P_N(t,M)\|,\ \|\mathcal P_N(t,M)^{-1}\| &\le e^{A N|t|}, \end{split}\qquad\text{(17)} with A=CrBA=CrB, enlarged if needed. All matrices are holomorphic in t,ut,u on a neighborhood of each smaller closed bidisk.

4. The retained carry is exponentially small

On |t|=R|t|=R, (17) and the operator-valued Cauchy estimate give ∥PN,n(M)∥≤eANRR−n. \|P_{N,n}(M)\|\le e^{ANR}R^{-n}. Apply this only to the actual high-degree coefficients in (8). For 0<ρ<10<\rho<1 and |t|≤ρR|t|\le\rho R, ∥𝒞N(t,M)∥≤ρN1−ρeANR.(18) \boxed{\quad \|\mathcal C_N(t,M)\| \le \frac{\rho^N}{1-\rho}e^{ANR}. \quad}\qquad\text{(18)} Multiplication by either actual inverse in (17) gives the two-sided relative bounds ∥𝒢N−1𝒫N−I∥,∥𝒫N𝒢N−1−I∥≤11−ρexp⁡{N[log⁡ρ+AR(1+ρ)]},∥𝒫N−1𝒢N−I∥,∥𝒢N𝒫N−1−I∥≤11−ρexp⁡{N[log⁡ρ+AR(1+ρ)]}.(19) \boxed{\begin{split} \|\mathcal G_N^{-1}\mathcal P_N-I\|,\ \|\mathcal P_N\mathcal G_N^{-1}-I\| &\le \frac1{1-\rho} \exp\{N[\log\rho+AR(1+\rho)]\},\\ \|\mathcal P_N^{-1}\mathcal G_N-I\|,\ \|\mathcal G_N\mathcal P_N^{-1}-I\| &\le \frac1{1-\rho} \exp\{N[\log\rho+AR(1+\rho)]\}. \end{split}}\qquad\text{(19)} No commuting of an inverse through the carry is used.

In particular, fixing a sufficiently small RR and ρ=1/2\rho=1/2 proves an exponentially small relative carry. More generally, for any prescribed Λ>0\Lambda>0, one can keep RR fixed and choose ρ>0\rho>0 sufficiently small so that all four relative errors and the absolute error in (18) are O(e−ΛN)O(e^{-\Lambda N}). This gives a nonempty activity disk, whose size is allowed to depend on Λ,r,η\Lambda,r,\eta. It does not assert that a given geometric source point lies inside that disk.

The same conclusions hold with all fixed tt- and uu-derivatives on smaller bidisks. To see this, first choose a slightly larger activity disk and a slightly larger collar on which the desired exponential rate holds, then apply Cauchy estimates to the exact holomorphic errors in (18)–(19). In the original meridian variable this gives, for each fixed jj, the corresponding bound with at most a factor O(Nj/2)O(N^{j/2}) for ∂Mj\partial_M^j. Indeed M∂M=N∂uM\partial_M=\sqrt N\,\partial_u, and MM stays bounded away from zero. These are genuine meridian derivatives of the actual fixed-root matrices, not quantum-parameter derivatives of a root identity.

5. Coherent evaluation and its explicit conditioning

Retain the exact original binomial kernels of J: c(𝒛)=⨂i∑a=0d(da)ziawa,v(𝒘)=⨂i∑a=0dwiawa*,σN(B)=v(𝒘)Bc(𝒛)∏i(1+wizi)d.(20) c(\mathbf z)=\bigotimes_i \sum_{a=0}^d\binom da z_i^aw_a,\qquad v(\mathbf w)=\bigotimes_i\sum_{a=0}^d w_i^aw_a^*,\qquad \sigma_N(B)= \frac{v(\mathbf w)B c(\mathbf z)} {\prod_i(1+w_i z_i)^d}. \qquad\text{(20)} There is no change of metric or of the finite caps. For any matrix BB, Cauchy–Schwarz in (2) gives |σN(B)|≤∥B∥∏i[(1+|wi|2)(1+|zi|2)|1+wizi|]d.(21) |\sigma_N(B)| \le \|B\|\prod_i \left[ \frac{\sqrt{(1+|w_i|^2)(1+|z_i|^2)}}{|1+w_i z_i|} \right]^d. \qquad\text{(21)} The bracket is projectively well defined and continuous off the incidence divisor. For each fixed compact 𝒦⋐((ℙ1×ℙ1)\{1+wz=0})r \mathcal K\Subset \bigl((\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1) \setminus\{1+wz=0\}\bigr)^r there is therefore a finite κ𝒦≥0\kappa_{\mathcal K}\ge0 with sup𝒦|σN(B)|≤eκ𝒦N∥B∥.(22) \sup_{\mathcal K}|\sigma_N(B)| \le e^{\kappa_{\mathcal K}N}\|B\|. \qquad\text{(22)} On the original real coherent cycle zi=w‾iz_i=\bar w_i the brackets are exactly one.

Given 𝒦\mathcal K and Λ>0\Lambda>0, choose the activity disk in (18)–(19) with rate larger than Λ+κ𝒦\Lambda+\kappa_{\mathcal K}. Then sup𝒦|σN(𝒫N−𝒢N)|=O(e−ΛN).(23) \boxed{\quad \sup_{\mathcal K} |\sigma_N(\mathcal P_N-\mathcal G_N)| =O(e^{-\Lambda N}). \quad}\qquad\text{(23)} The same holds for the normalized kernels of each relative operator error in (19). Every fixed coherent-coordinate derivative has the same conclusion on smaller charts: use (22) on slightly larger projective charts and apply Cauchy estimates to the holomorphic normalized kernel. The meridian-normal statement from §4 remains valid.

This is an absolute comparison after the stated identity-cap normalization. It is not a relative scalar estimate obtained by dividing by σN(𝒢N)\sigma_N(\mathcal G_N), which may vanish. In particular neither (19) nor (23) supplies a selected coherent phase or a nonzero amplitude for the product factors. A paired exterior remains an actual matrix or kernel; its norm or conditioning must be included before (18) is used inside the full source.

6. The fixed-denominator family and the original normal row

The theorem estimates precisely the family in (6): EN(tEtot(M))E_N(tE_{\mathrm{ tot}}(M)), with the fixed denominators fnf_n and all meridian dependence in the actual operators. It retains the complete cap and uses no continuation of an occupation index.

It does not replace a different coefficient family such as ∑n=0dtnEtot(M)n(M−2Q;Q)n.(24) \sum_{n=0}^d \frac{t^n E_{\mathrm{ tot}}(M)^n}{(M^{-2}Q;Q)_n}. \qquad\text{(24)} Although (24) agrees with 𝒢N\mathcal G_N at M=1M=1, its meridian derivative also differentiates the denominator. For example that extra coefficient at M=1M=1 is exactly ∂Mfn(M−2Q;Q)n|M=1=−2∑j=1nQj1−Qj.(25) \left.\partial_M \frac{f_n}{(M^{-2}Q;Q)_n}\right|_{M=1} =-2\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{Q^j}{1-Q^j}. \qquad\text{(25)} This finite normal insertion must be retained in any use of the theorem for such a contracted source. No bound or cancellation for its pairing with the quotient is supplied by silently replacing (24) by (6).

Likewise no ordinary quantum-parameter normal follows by differentiating (5), (7), or (10) away from the root. The result is a controlled factorization of an actual finite coproduct factor, with its carry and coherent conditioning quantified. The quotient, turnover, all source coupons, and relative-window defects of J remain in the full period.
