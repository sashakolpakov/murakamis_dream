Complete physical residue packets on compact positive contours

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The physical-row Jacobi formula already supplies an exact replacement for an independent first normal inside a subsequent original trace. Here its determinant form removes all apparent resolvent poles, and the all-row slit theorem bounds that replacement by the action of the original physical row. This gives a uniform estimate for complete physical residue packets of a fixed graph, including edges between frozen vertices and all mixed first normals.

The result controls the local cost of crossing original grid poles on compact positive-angle contours. It does not construct the remaining joint contour, bound its untouched kernel factors, or change an ordinary free-boundary polynomial.

2. Uniform slit bounds with the row’s own action

For all sufficiently large NN, uniformly in every eligible row and w∈𝒦w\in\mathcal K, |∂wkFj(w)|≤CkNk|Fj(w)|,|∂wkĜj(w)|≤CkN6+k(1+log⁡N)|Fj(w)|.(10) |\partial_w^k F_j(w)|\le C_k N^k|F_j(w)|,\qquad |\partial_w^k\widehat G_j(w)| \le C_k N^{6+k}(1+\log N)|F_j(w)|. \qquad\text{(10)} The second inequality concerns the double-row range only. 3. Complete frozen-vertex residue packets

Let Γ\Gamma be a fixed finite loopless graph, with the original same-index edge KNK_N on every edge and a fixed signed integer crossing power at each traced vertex. Repeated edges cause no change. Choose a set SS of traced vertices to freeze at one of the distinct physical spectral nodes for each vertex; different vertices may use the same node. No condition on the signs or nonvanishing of the powers is needed.

For a double node define the original local trace functional ℛj,Nf=η{(4−cj2)f′(cj)−cjf(cj)}.(20) \mathcal R_{j,N}f =\eta\{(4-c_j^2)f'(c_j)-c_jf(c_j)\}. \qquad\text{(20)} At the odd simple node define ℛJN,Nf=f(−2)\mathcal R_{J_N,N}f=f(-2). Their sum is the original ϕN\phi_N. In particular the simple node has one value and no independently prescribed spectral normal. Formula (20) includes the complete node, with its original normalization; when angular pole pairs are treated separately their original orientations and multiplicities are retained.

Apply the product of (20) over SS to the original source. Edges disjoint from SS, and every other factor independent of the frozen variables, remain outside this local operation. Let ESE_S denote the edges incident to SS. The resulting incident-edge packet is exactly AS,N(𝒘)=(∏v∈Sℛjv,N(zv))[∏v∈SR(zv)nv∏vw∈ESKN(zv,zw)],zv=wv(v∉S).(20a) A_{S,N}(\mathbf w)= \left(\prod_{v\in S}\mathcal R_{j_v,N}^{(z_v)}\right) \left[\prod_{v\in S}R(z_v)^{n_v} \prod_{vw\in E_S}K_N(z_v,z_w)\right], \qquad z_v=w_v\quad(v\notin S). \qquad\text{(20a)} The crossing powers in this formula retain their original value and first-normal data. Expanding (20a) is a finite sum of products of:

  • values and first or mixed first normals of KNK_N on edges with both endpoints in SS;
  • Fjv(w)F_{j_v}(w) or Gjv(w)G_{j_v}(w) on edges from v∈Sv\in S to a remaining endpoint;
  • the original physical crossing values and first normals, and the coefficients in (20).

This expansion is literal: each frozen variable is differentiated at most once, and an internal frozen edge can receive one derivative at each endpoint.

Theorem 3. Allow every frozen vertex to use any physical node, with no angle buffer or index restriction. Suppose the remaining traced angles range over a fixed compact positive-angle family. On every edge from SS to a remaining traced endpoint, replace GjG_j by (8), leaving FjF_j unchanged. Denote the resulting incident packet by ÂS,N\widehat A_{S,N}.

This packet has exactly the original full mixed physical data in every remaining traced variable. Its product with the untouched remainder has the same value under all remaining original traces, coefficientwise in any retained free boundary polynomial.

Let tvt_v be the actual real physical angle at v∈Sv\in S and the retained complex angle otherwise. For every ϵ>0\epsilon>0 and every fixed multi-index β\beta in the remaining spectral variables, there are C,MC,M such that |∂𝒘βÂS,N|≤CNMexp⁡N{∑vw∈ESℜψ(tv,tw)+ϵ}.(21) |\partial_{\mathbf w}^{\beta}\widehat A_{S,N}| \le C N^M \exp N\left\{ \sum_{vw\in E_S}\Re\psi(t_v,t_w)+\epsilon \right\}. \qquad\text{(21)} Only the incident-edge packet is bounded in (21). Untouched edge kernels and their original remaining currents are not included in this estimate. If their own bounds are available, (21) can be multiplied with them without altering the incident-edge action. If all remaining spectral arguments lie in a compact subset of ℜw<2\Re w<2, the fixed tolerance in (21) can instead be replaced by an additional polynomial factor.

If an incident endpoint is genuinely free and untraced, its GjG_j is not replaced. Keep that exact ordinary free-boundary factor in the packet. The corresponding bound contains its actual norm and derivatives as an additional supplied factor instead of an asserted ψ\psi-bound. The same convention applies to additional spectators whose source-specific norms have not been proved.

Proof. Proposition 1 applies at every remaining traced endpoint before it is contracted. Its product assertion retains all mixed normals, including when several frozen vertices meet the same remaining vertex. It is immaterial that the physical colors at different vertices coincide. The product rule gives the exact statement with all untouched spectators and free boundary parameters.

For a frozen internal edge, use (19a) for its value and each possible first or mixed first normal. This retains the original ordinary derivatives at both endpoints uniformly even when either spectral argument tends to 22; no fixed slit distance is claimed there. Equivalently, Proposition 1 identifies a first normal with Ĝj(ck)\widehat G_j(c_k) and a mixed normal with ∂wĜj(ck)\partial_w\widehat G_j(c_k), but the uniform bound at these moving real arguments is supplied by (19a). If an endpoint is the odd simple node it is never differentiated, and (19a) includes its value contribution.

Theorem 2 also applies to every edge going to a remaining traced endpoint and to its fixed derivatives, uniformly over every frozen row. Use the monotone upper comparison after (19) to replace its row profile by the action at the actual physical angle, without an endpoint error. Allocate a fixed small part of ϵ\epsilon to each edge in the finite graph. All crossing values have modulus one at physical nodes; their first normals are bounded by a fixed power of NN, for every fixed signed power, including zero. The factors in (20) are bounded. There are only finitely many product-rule terms, independently of NN. Multiplication proves (21). The stronger half-plane alternative follows from the last assertion of Theorem 2 and the polynomial bound (19a).

The proof never replaces a factor at an untraced endpoint, which gives the last assertion. It also proves the corresponding estimate for fixed spectral derivatives near the odd simple endpoint by holomorphic spectral descent, rather than division by sin⁡t\sin t. ▫\square

For a polynomially sized collection of crossed physical-node assignments, (21) bounds their sum by the supremum of the displayed action, with an additional polynomial factor. For example all assignments on a fixed frozen set have at most N|S|N^{|S|} members. Actual contour orientations, any different crossing multiplicities, and the untouched remaining integrals still belong to the original source.

4. Scope for contour transport

This theorem removes a specific local obstacle: crossing an original physical pole does not force an additional exponential cost from the independent first normal once the other endpoint is subsequently traced on a compact positive contour. The bound keeps the physical angle of that pole rather than replacing it by the complete-end maximum.

It is a relative-source assertion. The omitted term of (9) and the change Q0↦Q̃NQ_0\mapsto\widetilde Q_N are not discarded as separate functions on an arbitrary open current. Their full physical jets agree before the remaining original traces are represented by contours. Any later open contour decomposition must retain its own endpoints and seams. The logarithmic-factorial representative has poles outside the original angular window; this argument does not shift that window across them.

No global deformation, rate for the untouched remaining kernel product, bound on its joint wire connectors, or nonzero scalar coefficient follows. In particular a geometric action decrease along a proposed flow and (21) still require a source-preserving construction of that flow and control of its complete remaining boundary.
