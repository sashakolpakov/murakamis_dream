Equality audit: positive geometric core and primitive tube completion

29 October 2026. The whole-source theorem already fixes a nonzero limit equal to the regulator of an explicit filled representation. A sharp representation-volume bound does NOT make that representation geometric. This note isolates and proves the remaining local END-completion lemma. It uses a positive geometric deformation of the compact core, not an unfilled Casson–Rivin maximum and not convergence of volumes alone.

1. Exact inputs needed from the original source

For each fixed odd s>=25 the whole original source theorem provides a nonzero asymptotic at every independently sufficiently long fixed signed tuple p. Its constants may depend on s and that fixed tuple; there is no assertion uniform in slopes growing with N. All auxiliary colors, both root parities, the middle color, every normal jet, and the original single marked source are retained there.

The literal port theorem constructs rho_z of the ACTUAL augmented-link group. It includes the right-to-left cap order, rather than a permutation of arbitrary pretzel boxes. At z=0 its complete main and auxiliary generators are the oriented four-antiprism geometric holonomy. The preferred-peripheral certificate fixes M_i=H_i^(-1/2), lambda_i=-L_i^(-1), including the ordinary-circle spin. The regulator comparison then gives, at a selected filling point,

source rate = Vol(rho_p).

For equality with the manifold volume one needs more than the facts that rho_p kills the slope and Vol(rho_p) tends to the augmented volume. The two additional geometric inputs used below are precise:

  • A FIXED finite partially ideal geometric triangulation of the complete augmented manifold, every tetrahedron strictly positive. Finite face and cell centers are allowed. An arbitrary ideal topological triangulation is insufficient.
  • Transporting its vertex orbits by rho_z and the selected cusp flags gives a nearby positive developing triangulation with local degree one at its finite faces, edges and vertices. Equivariance and the local degree statement concern the SAME original marked manifold.

The finite-center construction of this input is proved in the geometric-core and filling theorem, sections2–4. It has been independently read in full: matched face-center fans and finite cell centers give24s positive tetrahedra, exact contractible edge transitions remove both screw and cone defects, and the finite spherical links have degree one. The result below is independent of whether an all-ideal positive subdivision is available.

2. A compact affine link becomes an honest translation torus

Consider one ideal end of such a positive developing triangulation. Its oriented link is a compact torus T. Sending the selected peripheral fixed flag to infinity identifies the link triangles with Euclidean triangles and their transitions with orientation-preserving complex similarities. Its developing map

d: universal_cover(T) -> C

is a local homeomorphism, including at the link vertices. Local degree one and the positive Euclidean angle sums are essential here.

Suppose the peripheral affine holonomies have a common finite fixed point q. Assume d avoids q. On the simply connected cover choose

f=Log(d-q).

The differential df is invariant under the deck group, because an affine holonomy has d-q -> a_gamma(d-q). It is everywhere nonsingular. Thus

|df|^2                                                   (1)

is a nonsingular Euclidean metric on the COMPACT torus T. It is complete. The lifted f is a local isometry from its complete simply connected Euclidean cover to C; it is a global isometry. To see this directly, lift every straight segment starting at f(x). The corresponding unit speed geodesic exists for the whole segment by completeness. This proves surjectivity and path lifting; since C is simply connected, the local isometry is a one-sheeted covering.

Consequently f gives a global coordinate on the link cover. Its deck translations form a rank-two lattice

Lambda={L_gamma : f(gamma x)=f(x)+L_gamma}.              (2)

The map pi_1(T)->Lambda is injective. Rank one or a nonzero deck element with zero period would contradict the compact flat-torus quotient. This argument controls the DEGREE of the logarithmic development, not just its local derivative.

Why q is avoided in the selected near-complete family

Choose the peripheral flag normalization continuously from the complete geometric cusp. A fixed fundamental union of link triangles has bounded developed image, uniformly near completeness. A meridian has affine form

d -> a d+b, a ->1, b -> b_0!=0.

When a!=1 its common finite fixed point is q=b/(1-a), so q goes to infinity. For sufficiently small deformation it misses that entire finite fundamental image. Every other developed triangle is its affine translate; the holonomy fixes q, so none of those triangles meets q either. This proves the hypothesis globally on the link cover.

The selected source family approaches the base uniformly as max_i |p_i|^(-1) goes to zero. Therefore one small geometric neighborhood works for independently unequal signed sufficiently long slopes. No comparison between the different |p_i| is required.

There is no genericity exception a_i=1 at a selected finite filling. Exact separate evenness gives V_i=tau_i(z)U_i holomorphically, with tau_i near its nonreal complete cusp value. If U_i=0 then V_i=0, contradicting U_i+p_iV_i=+/-2pi i. The selected logs are small, so exp(U_i)=1 also forces U_i=0. Thus every selected auxiliary meridian has a_i!=1 and the stated finite second fixed point exists.

3. The actual end contains a full punctured tube

Translate q to zero in upper half-space coordinates (z,y), y>0. The link development now has z=exp(f). Every incident geometric tetrahedron has its chosen ideal vertex at infinity. Its faces through infinity are vertical planes. Above a bounded lower height its part over its Euclidean link triangle is therefore a vertical product. The opposite face cannot be vertical: otherwise all four vertices would lie in one hyperbolic plane and the tetrahedron would have zero volume. This works with finite as well as ideal remaining vertices.

There are finitely many such pieces in a fundamental link domain and that domain avoids zero. It follows that for some finite A>0 the end contains the full equivariant epigraph

y>A|z|.                                                (3)

Indeed bound every lower cutoff y_0(z)/|z| on those finitely many compact pieces; affine deck similarities multiply numerator and denominator by the same positive number. Since f is globally one-to-one onto C, the different pieces meet coherently across their side faces and do not introduce an uncounted logarithmic covering.

Put r=y/|z|. Cylindrical coordinates about the vertical 0–infinity axis are

R=asinh(1/r), theta=Im f,
X=Re f+(1/2)log(1+r^2).

The hyperbolic metric is exactly

dR^2+cosh(R)^2 dX^2+sinh(R)^2 dtheta^2.                (4)

A link deck translation f->f+L_gamma acts by (X,theta)->(X+Re L_gamma,theta+Im L_gamma). Equation (3) says that the end contains the WHOLE region 0<R<R_0 of this tube, not merely a narrow subset tending toward its axis.

4. Primitive logarithmic angle is the decisive completion condition

Let alpha be a primitive filled slope and choose beta so (alpha,beta) is a basis of pi_1(T)=Z^2. Suppose its ACTUAL log-link period is

L_alpha=+2pi i or -2pi i.                              (5)

Because Lambda has real rank two, Re L_beta!=0. The quotient of (4) for 0<R<R_0 therefore has one angular period exactly 2pi and one loxodromic screw generator. Adding R=0 is the ordinary smooth hyperbolic solid torus, with core length |Re L_beta|. Its meridian is exactly alpha. Either sign in (5) gives a smooth tube; it chooses the orientation of its meridian parameter, not a mirror of the positive compact core.

This proves smooth filling and discreteness directly from the local metric. Killing alpha in PSL2 alone would NOT suffice. If instead L_alpha=2pi i m with |m|>1, the same construction has cone angle 2pi|m| at its core. All punctured local geometry and the killed PSL slope could still hold, but the completion would be branched rather than smooth. If L_alpha=0, injectivity of the logarithmic period map would already contradict a nonsingular compact link torus.

The primitive-angle proof identifies the actual periods with the source logarithms, including their integer windings. In the source conventions,

U_i=Log(M_i^2)=-z_i,
V_i=Log(lambda_i^2) centered at completeness=-2ell_i,
U_i+p_i V_i=-2pi i m_i.

The surviving charges satisfy |m_i|=1, precisely (5). The complete SL2 longitude sign is NOT used as an uncentered 2pi i contribution to V_i. The proof of the period identification must remain separate from merely checking exp(U_i+p_i V_i)=1.

At the unfilled main cusp all affine multipliers are one. Its positive compact link is an ordinary complete Euclidean torus by the same flat-metric argument, and its end contains a full horoball quotient. Thus the completed filled metric has finite volume and exactly the required complete main cusp.

5. What follows, without assuming a character-variety uniqueness theorem

Combining the positive-core input with sections2–4 yields a complete oriented hyperbolic metric on the ACTUAL filled knot complement. Its holonomy is rho_p. The developing map on its complete universal cover is a local isometry to H3. Lifting geodesic segments exactly as above makes it a covering of H3; since H3 is simply connected it is a global isometry. Hence rho_p is discrete and faithful.

One can compare its volume to any specified complete hyperbolic metric on the same oriented manifold without importing an additional volume- rigidity assertion. Apply the proved sharp representation-volume inequality once with each complete structure as domain and the other structure’s holonomy as target. The finite fundamental chain has the same oriented degree one in both cases. The two inequalities give

Vol(rho_p)=Vol(the filled hyperbolic manifold).         (6)

Together with the already proved whole-source asymptotic and marked regulator comparison, this is the required equality. The proof is conditional ONLY on the explicit positive-core input and the actual primitive-period certificate, both stated above; convergence of Vol(rho_p) to the augmented volume is not used as an equality argument.

6. Casson–Rivin and Gromov: the exact scope of the extremal argument

Strict concavity of the ideal-tetrahedron volume on an affine POSITIVE angle slice gives at most one interior critical point and makes that point its global maximum. It does not by itself prove:

  • that a supplied topological ideal triangulation has a positive geometric realization;
  • that the SOURCE point lies in that same angle slice;
  • that the correct filling slope and its integer log angle are used;
  • that a locally geometric but incomplete end completes without a branching multiplicity.

In particular, the UNFILLED complete-cusp angle constraints have a different maximum from a finite Dehn filling. For actual peripheral logarithms u,v the regulator variation is

dV=2 sum(Re v dIm u-Re u dIm v).

On a slice fixing only the rotational filling condition Im(u+p v)=constant, this becomes

dV=-2 sum(Re u+p Re v)dIm v.

The real filling condition Re(u+p v)=0 is therefore the relevant stationarity condition. Replacing this modified slice by the original complete-cusp slice would be incorrect. A positive all-ideal triangulation and the full-rank marked gluing rows can make this a strict-concavity proof of uniqueness; the finite-center completion above does not need that additional representation.

Likewise the Gromov/representation-volume norm inequality proves only |Vol(rho)|<=Vol(M). Equality needs an actual equality certificate. Here that certificate is the complete local isometry constructed from the positive core and the primitive tube, rather than proximity of two volumes or a formal maximum principle.

7. A precise local-uniqueness lemma when positive ideal rows are supplied

There is also a short independent local-rigidity statement. Suppose an ACTUAL positive all-ideal triangulation has t tetrahedra and c cusps, and its t-c independent edge equations together with the c specified meridian equations form a full-rank real isotropic row matrix (A B). Thus its row space is Lagrangian, not just an arbitrary selection of cleared polynomial equations. Write shape logs x_j=Log z_j and y_j=Log(1-z_j^(-1)). At the positive complete point,

dy_j=tau_j dx_j, tau_j=1/(z_j-1), Im tau_j<0.

The fixed-meridian gluing Jacobian A+B diag(tau_j) is invertible. If a vector a were in its kernel, (a,diag(tau)a) would lie in the complex Lagrangian kernel of (A B). Its symplectic pairing with its conjugate would be zero; multiplied by i/2 that pairing is

sum_j Im(tau_j)|a_j|^2,

which is strictly negative unless a=0. The holomorphic implicit-function theorem therefore gives a UNIQUE local positive shape solution for each set of small marked meridian logs.

Any sufficiently close framed representation whose equivariant ideal vertices give that same positive triangulation satisfies those gluing equations and is this shape germ, up to its already specified framing. Thus no additional near-complete branch can be substituted while keeping the SAME marked geometric gluing data. This lemma neither asserts full rank for unverified rows nor obtains the source’s geometric realization from an arbitrary topological triangulation. Even with this local uniqueness, the primitive-angle and end-completion arguments in sections2–4 remain necessary to identify a smooth filled manifold rather than a branched completion.

8. Final independent audit verdict

The primitive-angle proof and the positive-core construction have now both passed independent analytical reads, and two independent readers checked sections2–5 of this note. Thus the stated geometric inputs are supplied, rather than left as formal hypotheses. The completed metric and its degree-one fundamental chain identify the source regulator with the actual hyperbolic volume for the precise long-pretzel class.

The separate Casson–Rivin presentation also checks: its coisotropic first-variation formula applies to every tangent of the marked ANGLE slice, because both the actual real log vector and an arbitrary edge-angle tangent lie in E^perp. It does not assume that every such tangent solves nonlinear gluing. The factor1/2 for squared peripheral coordinates is correct, and the source’s real filling equations kill the boundary derivative on the filled rotational slice. Strict concavity then gives a unique maximum. The geometric completion remains in that proof’s dependency chain; the maximum alone does not identify a smooth filled end.

The conclusion remains restricted to every fixed odd s>=25 and all independently sufficiently long fixed signed p_i, with the original source and normalization. It is not a conclusion for all hyperbolic knots, short fillings, or slopes growing with the quantum level.
