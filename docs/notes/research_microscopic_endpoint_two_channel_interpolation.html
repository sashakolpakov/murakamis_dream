Moderate bulk interpolation of the actual endpoint value and normal channels

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

A uniformly analytic matrix recurrence whose step is I+O(N−1)I+O(N^{-1}) has an exact holomorphic interpolation on a fixed narrow strip, with the same polynomial size as its nodal data. A discrete Newton construction first gives bounded exact local interpolants. Their many common zeros make the overlap errors exponentially small. A Cauchy correction and a final sinc repair produce one exact global vector.

For the original microscopic endpoint kernel, the physical value and common-index normal form such a four-component recurrence. The result therefore retains both channels without dividing by the value, which can vanish. It does not identify the interpolated normal with the derivative of the interpolated value. This distinction is essential for placing the result in the original trace.

3. Polynomial initial size and exact two-channel interpolation

The pinned endpoint-product theorem, with one factor, gives supz∈[−2,2],|α|≤A|KN(z,wN(α))|≤CANpA.(28) \sup_{z\in[-2,2],\ |\alpha|\le A} |K_N(z,w_N(\alpha))|\le C_A N^{p_A}. \qquad\text{(28)} It holds on every bounded complex parameter disk, not only below the first endpoint zero. Its proof compares the physical values to one fixed low physical row and retains the whole high radical. Markov’s inequality for the ordinary degree-DD kernel gives |Gj(α)|≤CANpA+2.(29) |G_j(\alpha)|\le C_A N^{p_A+2}. \qquad\text{(29)} Since |c′(j)|≤4π/N|c'(j)|\le4\pi/N, (23) yields the sufficient bound ∥Zj∥≤CANpA+3(30) \|Z_j\|\le C_A N^{p_A+3} \qquad\text{(30)} on the entire buffered bulk node interval. Differences in (23) are bounded by the sum of their two actual endpoint norms; no smoothness of the unknown interpolation is assumed.

Apply Theorem 1 to (25). Denote its first and third components by ℱN(x,α)\mathcal F_N(x,\alpha) and 𝒰N(x,α)\mathcal U_N(x,\alpha). Shrinking its fixed strip if necessary, define 𝒢N(x,α)=𝒰N(x,α)Nc′(Nx)=−𝒰N(x,α)4πsin⁡(2πx+π/N).(31) \mathcal G_N(x,\alpha)= \frac{\mathcal U_N(x,\alpha)}{N c'(Nx)} =-\frac{\mathcal U_N(x,\alpha)} {4\pi\sin(2\pi x+\pi/N)}. \qquad\text{(31)} The denominator is bounded away from zero on this bulk strip. Thus for some fixed r>0,C,pr>0,C,p, at both parities and all sufficiently large NN, ℱN(j/N,α)=Fj(α),𝒢N(j/N,α)=Gj(α),∥ℱN∥+∥𝒢N∥≤CNp(32) \boxed{\quad \mathcal F_N(j/N,\alpha)=F_j(\alpha),\qquad \mathcal G_N(j/N,\alpha)=G_j(\alpha),\qquad \|\mathcal F_N\|+\|\mathcal G_N\|\le CN^p \quad} \qquad\text{(32)} on ℛr\mathcal R_r, uniformly for |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A. Both interpolants are jointly holomorphic. All fixed index-strip and exterior-parameter derivatives have polynomial bounds on smaller fixed domains. The other two vector components interpolate the differences in (23) and remain available.

This is a phase-free analytic interpolation of both separate physical channels: its norm has no extensive exponential rate. No scalar zero-freeness or separation of limiting modes is needed. Any NN-dependent oscillation in the actual vector initial data is retained rather than normalized away.

4. Original trace placement and its precise limitation

For any actual ordinary spectator f(z;𝒖)f(z;\mathbf u), the contribution of the bulk rows JI={j:j/N∈I}J_I=\{j:j/N\in I\} to the original full trace is exactly η∑j∈JI[(4−c(j)2){Gj(α)f(c(j);𝒖)+Fj(α)∂zf(c(j);𝒖)}−c(j)Fj(α)f(c(j);𝒖)].(33) \eta\sum_{j\in J_I} \left[ (4-c(j)^2) \{G_j(\alpha)f(c(j);\mathbf u) +F_j(\alpha)\partial_z f(c(j);\mathbf u)\} -c(j)F_j(\alpha)f(c(j);\mathbf u) \right]. \qquad\text{(33)} Any original crossing RnR^n, including its carrier and normal, can remain inside ff. Every other graph factor and remaining trace can remain jointly inside ff as well. The replacements Fj↦ℱN(j/N,α)F_j\mapsto\mathcal F_N(j/N,\alpha) and Gj↦𝒢N(j/N,α)G_j\mapsto\mathcal G_N(j/N,\alpha) leave (33) exactly unchanged. All ordinary exterior derivatives commute with these identities.

For fixed shared endpoint products, their value and normal in (33) are respectively ∏iℱN(x,αi),∑i𝒢N(x,αi)∏k≠iℱN(x,αk).(34) \prod_i\mathcal F_N(x,\alpha_i),\qquad \sum_i\mathcal G_N(x,\alpha_i) \prod_{k\ne i}\mathcal F_N(x,\alpha_k). \qquad\text{(34)} These have polynomial bounds on the common strip and reproduce the entire shared product rule. No quotient is imposed on a free outside variable, and no spectral normal of the spectator is removed.

The theorem does not assert ∂xℱN(x,α)=Nc′(Nx)𝒢N(x,α).(35) \partial_x\mathcal F_N(x,\alpha) =N c'(Nx)\mathcal G_N(x,\alpha). \qquad\text{(35)} Interpolating the two components separately is sufficient for the two-channel sampling formula (33), but does not create a single scalar Hermite representative. Accordingly (32) alone does not justify replacing a scalar double-pole contour amplitude by ℱN\mathcal F_N and differentiating it. Such a replacement must retain the difference between the two sides of (35), with its actual current or normal contribution.

The physical endpoints j/N=0,1/2j/N=0,1/2 are outside the fixed bulk hypothesis. The odd simple node is therefore not among (33); its separate original value remains untouched. Both even and odd bulk recurrences are covered by the same argument. No global periodicity, endpoint connector estimate, filled-action upper bound, or nonzero source coefficient follows from this result. In particular it does not contradict the full-period scalar Hermite obstruction.
