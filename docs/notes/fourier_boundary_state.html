Recovering the meridian state when the shape plane moves

Research note, 25 October 2026. This extends general_boundary_state_and_framing.md to the other possible boundary rank. We retain the original charged Andersen–Kashaev tensor and its existing level convention, rather than replacing its tetrahedral function. The new construction is inverse Fourier transformation of a specifically leveled tensor. For ordinary fibers it is exactly the state already constructed, including its Gaussian normalization.

Definitions used: Andersen–Kashaev, arXiv:1109.6295v2, equations (4) and (18), and the Fourier/Gaussian framework of Andersen–Kashaev, arXiv:1409.1208v1, Section 6. All Fourier identities, phases, and analytic arguments needed below are reconstructed here or in the preceding notes. We do not assume an analytic theorem from either source.

1. The two ranks and a rational angle path

Let a globally branched ideal triangulation of a knot exterior in S^3 have a complete positive geometric structure. Use the independently proved Fourier reduction and cochain identity:

W=im S=im B^T, dim W=d=N-2k,
G=-2Q+P_+, H=-G/2,
L={(t,s):t in W, s-Gt in W^perp}=ker(A|-B).

Here the full A,B use N-1 independent edge rows and the unhalved preferred-longitude row. The oriented meridian and longitude have intersection 1, so

A_m B_l^T-B_m A_l^T=-2.

Rank B_ed is either d-1 or d. The preceding ordinary-fiber theorem treats d-1. We call d the moving-plane case, without assuming that this is the same as any degeneration of the hyperbolic structure.

Choose a rational strict reference angle structure theta0, in units of pi, with lifted angular meridian and longitude both zero. Existence follows by rational density around the complete geometric angles. Below physical angles are in radians. Write a for the first shape angle and b’’ for the angle of the literature double-prime shape:

d0=pi*1-a0, W0=b0''-G d0.

The angular gluing equations are A_r a+B_r b’‘=nu_r. Their homogeneous edge block has rank N-1. Adding both peripheral rows has rank N+1: edge rows are symplectically orthogonal to both peripheral rows, while their mutual pairing is -2. Therefore a rational angle variation (a1,b1’’) exists with

A_ed a1+B_ed b1''=0,
A_m a1+B_m b1''=0,
A_l a1+B_l b1''=1.                                 (1.1)

Put d1=-a1, W1=b1’’-G d1 and theta(lambda)=theta0+lambda*theta1. For real lambda in a neighborhood of zero all physical angles remain strictly positive, mu=0 and the angular longitude equals lambda. The coefficients of theta1 are rational; lambda is measured in radians.

If rank B_ed=d-1, then d1 is in W, as proved in the ordinary-fiber note. If rank B_ed=d, then d1 is outside W. Indeed otherwise the projected edge identity gives B_ed W1=0, and B_l is in the row space of B_ed. The longitude variation would then also be zero, contrary to (1.1). Thus

S_ext=[S,-d1] has rank d+1                          (1.2)

in the moving-plane case.

2. The exact level and inverse Fourier definition

Let T_b(lambda) denote the phase-free reduced integral with all Jacobians, in the common-Phi convention:

T_b(lambda)=c_X*(2pi sqrt(h))^(-d)
   integral_R^d exp[(i y^T H y+(W0+lambda W1)^T y)/(2pi h)]
                 product_j Phi_b(y_j/(2pi sqrt(h))) dz,
y=i(d0+lambda d1)+S z,
h=b^2/(1+b^2)^2.

For real small lambda the exact charged-kernel calculation gives

Z_AK(theta(lambda);b)
   =exp(pi i N/12+i K0(theta(lambda))/h) T_b(lambda),

with the explicit K0 of the preceding boundary-state note. Define

A_ref(lambda)=lambda W1.d0+(lambda^2/2)W1.d1,
K(theta(lambda))=K0(theta(lambda))+A_ref(lambda)/(2pi),
L_b(lambda)=exp[-i A_ref(lambda)/(2pi h)] T_b(lambda). (2.1)

Consequently L_b is exactly exp(-pi i N/12) times the existing AK leveled tensor at level ell=-4K/pi. No scalar has been suppressed. For real lambda this is an allowed real b-independent level. Complex lambda below means analytic continuation of this precise normalization from its real interval.

Define the meridian state by Fourier inversion:

J_b(h,x)=1/(8pi^2 h) integral_R
            L_b(i tau) exp[-i tau x/(4pi h)] d tau. (2.2)

The factor 1/(8pi^2 h) follows from the Fourier frequency tau/(4pi h). It is essential for comparison of states, although its logarithm contributes no volume rate.

In the moving-plane case this is an ordinary absolutely convergent integral. Substituting (2.1), set y=i d0+S z-tau d1 and

I0_b(y)=exp[(i y^T H y+W0^T y)/(2pi h)]
                     product_j Phi_b(y_j/(2pi sqrt(h))).

Then the exact formula is

J_b(h,x)=c_X/(8pi^2 h)*(2pi sqrt(h))^(-d)
   integral_R^(d+1) I0_b(y)
    exp{[i tau W1^T S z-i tau^2 W1.d1/2-i tau x/2]/(2pi h)}
                                                    dz d tau. (2.3)

Every extra quadratic coefficient is real. For real z,tau,x its extra exponential has modulus one. On the horizontal plane Im y=d0,

Re(i y^T H y+W0^T y)=b0'' . Re y.

Strict angles give 0<b0’’_j<d0_j<pi. The exact Phi tails therefore bound the modulus by C exp(-c sum_j |Re y_j|), for fixed b; derivatives only add polynomial factors. By (1.2) this is an exponential bound in |z|+|tau|. It proves absolute convergence, Schwartz regularity, and holomorphy of (2.2) on a small horizontal x strip.

The same estimates on smaller real-lambda intervals give analytic continuation of (2.1) to a vertical lambda strip. Explicitly, for lambda=s+i tau, Im y=d0+s d1. The real terms from i tau W1.y and -i A_ref cancel. The remaining modulus is exactly the charged product for theta(s), and hence decreases exponentially in |z|+|tau|, uniformly on smaller s intervals. Fourier inversion, followed by a contour shift within that strip, proves exactly

L_b(lambda)=integral_R J_b(h,x) exp(lambda x/(4pi h)) dx. (2.4)

Equivalently, this recovers the state from the normalized tensor. Uniqueness is ordinary Fourier uniqueness. No imaginary-charge value is postulated independently of its analytic continuation.

This also gives the local factorization for general angles, including nonzero angular meridian mu. Let theta be near theta0, with angular longitude lambda, and set

d_bar=d0+lambda d1, W_bar=W0+lambda W1,
Delta W=W_theta-W_bar,
K(theta)=K0(theta)+[A_ref(lambda)+Delta W.d_bar]/(2pi). (2.5)

The fixed-longitude relation gives d_theta-d_bar in W and Delta W in W^perp. Contour translation through the convex positive angle segment changes T_b by precisely exp[i Delta W.d_bar/(2pi h)]. Thus

Z_AK(theta;b)=exp(pi i N/12+i K(theta)/h)
   integral_(R+i mu) J_b(h,x) exp(lambda*x/(4pi h)) dx. (2.6)

Locally the small mu contour lies in the holomorphic x strip above; its translation changes no integral. Formula (2.5) is an explicit real angle polynomial, and ell=-4K/pi is its existing AK level. This supplies the general peripheral factorization near completeness, rather than only the zero-meridian slice used to define the Fourier transform. Continuation beyond these strips needs its own contour control; no global continuation through singularities is asserted.

The factor two can also be checked directly for a variation at fixed longitude: its symplectic pairing with theta1 gives

Delta W.d1-W1.(d_theta-d_bar)=-mu/2.

The corresponding real tau term in the shifted z contour is -taumu/2; the x contour Im x=mu supplies +taumu/2. They cancel exactly. The audit verifies this identity on the entire balanced angle space, with both peripheral variations free, before any specialization.

3. Agreement with the ordinary-fiber construction

Suppose rank B_ed=d-1 and retain p,beta from the preceding note. Write d1=S eta1. The zero-meridian condition gives

S^T W1=p/2, p^T eta1=-beta, W1.d1=-beta/2.

In (2.3) change z to z+tau eta1. The extra classical phase becomes

i tau(p^T z-x)/2-i beta tau^2/4.

If beta is nonzero, its Fresnel integral gives precisely

J_b(h,x)=1/(2pi sqrt(h)*sqrt(2i beta))
     integral exp[i(x-x')^2/(8pi beta h)] J_old(h,x') dx'.

The square root is the branch recorded in the ordinary-fiber note. If beta=0, Fourier inversion gives delta(p^T z-x), recovering its positive coarea fiber exactly. These operations may be justified by a Gaussian cutoff and Schwartz Fourier inversion; no absolute Fubini assertion is made for (2.3) in this noninjective case.

Also (2.1) reduces exactly to

K=K_old-beta*lambda^2/(8pi).

Thus the inverse Fourier definition agrees with the previously normalized meridian state in every ordinary-fiber chart. It is an extension of the same construction, including its finite-b phase.

4. Independence of the auxiliary angle path

For vectors (a,c), where c denotes the double-prime angle, use the symplectic form

Omega((a,c),(a',c'))=a.c'-c.a'.

The kernel of the edge, meridian and longitude rows is the symplectic dual of the edge-row space. It is isotropic with every solution of the homogeneous edge equations. This follows directly by dimensions and the peripheral pairing -2; it is not an additional NZ theorem.

Consequently, if delta d,delta W are angle variations with zero meridian and longitude, then

delta d in W, delta W in W^perp,
delta W.d1=W1.delta d.                             (4.1)

Change theta1 by such a variation, keeping theta0 fixed. Contour translation in W changes T_b(lambda) by exactly

exp{i[lambda delta W.d0+lambda^2 delta W.d1]/(2pi h)}.

The change in A_ref is the same polynomial, by (4.1) and delta W.delta d=0. Hence L_b and J_b are unchanged, not merely equal in modulus. The argument first holds for small real lambda where both paths have positive angles and then continues analytically.

Changing the reference theta0 to another strict rational reference with mu=lambda=0 gives only

J_new=exp[i (W_new-W0).d0/(2pi h)] J_old.             (4.2)

This is the previously recorded central phase. It is retained in the level and leaves the boundary annihilator unchanged.

5. Exact classical equations and the extra saddle direction

The moving-plane action in (2.3) is

F_ext(z,tau;x)=F0(y)+i tau W1^T S z
                           -i tau^2 W1.d1/2-i tau x/2,
F0(y)=i y^T H y+W0^T y-i sum_j Li_2(-exp y_j),
y=i d0+r, r=S z-tau d1.

Use the lifted shape deviations

u=Log z_shape-i a0=-r,
v=Log z_shape''-i b0'', D=v-G r.

Differentiation gives

grad_z F_ext=i S^T(D+tau W1).

At a z-critical point rho=D+tau W1 is in W^perp. From (1.1) and the projected cochain identities one obtains, with full lifted holonomies H_r=A_r u+B_r v,

H_ed=0, H_l=-tau.                                  (5.1)

For the remaining derivative,

partial_tau F_ext
   =-i(b1'' . u-a1 . v)-i x/2.

The angle variation (a1,b1’’) differs from (B_mT,-A_mT)/2 by a symplectic dual edge vector, because its longitude is one and its meridian is zero. Thus on H_ed=0,

b1'' . u-a1 . v=-H_m/2,
partial_tau F_ext=i(H_m-x)/2.                       (5.2)

All reference constants cancel in these formulas; the holonomies are differences from their specified reference lifts. Hence the full saddle equations are the edge equations with H_m=x, and the boundary phase derivative is i H_l/2. The variable x really is the meridian coordinate, without a zero-shear assumption.

At the complete shapes H_m=H_l=0. They lie in the lambda=0 plane y=i d0+W by the preferred-longitude relation. Equations (5.1)–(5.2) put their saddle at tau=0, including the new transverse direction. Its phase value is the old F0 value, whose real part is -Vol(M).

6. Contour deformation and the volume theorem

Write d_geo-d0=S eta for the difference to the actual complete geometric angles. Move the z contour through z+i s eta, 0<=s<=1, keeping tau real. These are the convex strict angle structures with zero meridian and longitude, so (4.1) applies at each s.

There is a potentially dangerous new linear tau term. It cancels exactly: if delta W=W_s-W0 and delta d=S s eta, the real linear change from F0 is taudelta W.d1, while the cross term in (2.3) contributes -tauW1.delta d. Equation (4.1) makes their sum zero. The modulus on every intermediate plane is therefore the charged product for its positive angles. Its exponential tails are uniform on the compact angle segment. The Phi poles stay outside 0<Im y_j<pi, and Cauchy side faces vanish. This justifies the contour deformation for the complete d+1 dimensional integral.

On the geometric horizontal plane its real Hessian is

-S_ext^T diag[Im(e^y/(1+e^y))] S_ext.

All diagonal entries are positive and S_ext has full column rank. Thus this Hessian is negative definite. The added real quadratic chirp affects only the imaginary part. The geometric critical point is the unique maximum on this plane and has a nonzero complex Gaussian coefficient. The previously proved uniform Phi remainder and global linear tail estimates now give the ordinary multivariate complex saddle asymptotic, in dimension d+1.

The prefactor in (2.3) is h^(-d/2-1), and the saddle factor is h^((d+1)/2). The resulting polynomial scale is h^(-1/2), agreeing with the ordinary-fiber normalization. In particular the state is nonzero for all sufficiently small h and

lim_(h->0+) -2pi h log|J_b(h,0)|=Vol(M).             (6.1)

Together with the ordinary-fiber theorem this proves (6.1) for every globally branched complete positive geometric knot triangulation, with its specified normalized tensor-derived meridian state. The extra boundary-rank and zero-shear hypotheses have been removed.

7. Quantum operator and the limits of the result

Every Phi argument and Gaussian coefficient in (2.3) is rational, apart from the recorded pi offsets and powers of h. The independent finite-product, dimension-count and moment argument of general_annihilator_and_volume.md therefore applies to this state. It gives a nonzero annihilator and its primitive minimal generator in the existing Andersen–Malusa b-shift algebra, after the stated finite coefficient cover and descent. No Jones-polynomial input is used, and no preservation of minimal degree under a fractional Gaussian conjugation is assumed.

In its conventions

Q=exp(-2pi b t), E f(t)=f(t-i b), t=x/(2pi sqrt(h)),
EQ=qQE, q=exp(2pi i b^2).

Equations (5.1)–(5.2) give the classical branch

Q=exp(-H_m), E=exp(H_l/2).

The previously proved uniform saddle substitution makes its geometric peripheral factor divide the classical symbol of the minimal annihilator. All generic peripheral coefficients must still be retained. Equality to the entire A-polynomial, equality to a Jones AJ operator, and an explicit coefficient list do not follow from existence alone.

This is a theorem for the specified realizations. It does not construct complete positive triangulations for all alternating knots, treat flat tetrahedra, prove arbitrary global state identifications, or establish an H-triangulation limit. Those remain parts of the original objective. It also does not identify the positive factorial sum with this complex state.

The exact matrix, polynomial, branch and Fourier-normalization checks are in experiments/audit_fourier_boundary.py. They include a six-tetrahedron 5_2 chart with rank B_ed=rank B=4, so that its longitude really moves the shape plane. Its topology calibration uses three explicitly recorded 2–3 moves; the local quantum move identity is not inferred from this combinatorial calibration.

8. A certified moving-plane example

The fixture has ideal triangulation signature gLLPQcdefeffikdqhtk. Its stacked face kernel has dimension k=1, its reduced dimension is d=4, both B ranks are 4, c_X=1, and S_ext has rank 5. The rational zero-meridian longitude variation can be chosen with d1=(1/2,0,0,0,0,0). Thus this is an actual instance of (2.3), not just an abstract symplectic matrix example.

In its native positive convention the six shapes are

a, a, (a+2)/a^2, a-1,
a^2/((a-1)(a+2)), -(a+1)^2/a,
a^3+3a^2+2a+1=0.

Rouche’s inequality on the rational disc centered at (-337641021+562279512i)/10^9, of radius 10^(-8), has remainder-to-linear ratio less than 0.044. Rational images and argument intervals put every physical angle above 0.14 radians. All native edge and peripheral products reduce exactly modulo this cubic; the argument intervals fix the six edge branches at 2pi and both peripheral branches at zero. The script separately verifies the literature shape equations after the recorded vertex reorderings, without guessing a global conjugation.

Regina verifies each of the three legal 2–3 moves from the already audited three-tetrahedron twist chart. Its initial combinatorial isomorphism maps the independently marked twist meridian to minus the source meridian, with zero longitude shear and zero additive angle correction. The final preferred longitude also has a separate integral dual-boundary certificate. These are topology and marking checks; a complex quantum-state identity along this particular sequence is not asserted without its level and move-phase audit.

The resulting hyperbolic volume is 2.82812208833078316276… . The 70-digit numerical diagnostic of the full five-direction saddle is recorded separately from the rational geometry and matrix certificates.

9. A uniform boundary phase, independent of the angle path

For every balanced angle structure in the local factorization domain, let d=pi*1-a, W=b’’-Gd, with fixed zero-peripheral reference d0,W0. If r is the number of tetrahedra, set

L_tet(theta)=-pi*r/24+sum_j epsilon_j(c_j-b_j)/12,

where b_j denotes the middle physical angle. Then exactly

K(theta)=L_tet(theta)
    +[d0.W-W0.d-d0.W0]/(4pi)-lambda*mu/(8pi).        (9.1)

The first two terms are affine in the angles; the only quadratic peripheral term is universal. The auxiliary path theta1 has disappeared.

For the proof, the charged phase and W=2Qd-C reduce to K0=L_tet-d.W/(4pi). Write

d=d0+lambda*d1+delta d,
W=W0+lambda*W1+delta W.

At fixed longitude, delta d lies in the allowed shape subspace, delta W annihilates it, and the peripheral pairing gives

delta W.delta d=0,
delta W.d1-W1.delta d=-mu/2.

Expansion of (2.5) cancels lambda^2. Its remaining quadratic term is lambda*(delta W.d1-W1.delta d)/(4pi), giving (9.1). This is the boundary Heisenberg term hidden in the separate chart normalizations. It connects the continuous level with the structural period line of heisenberg_period_torsor.md. The identity retains the full angle polynomials before any peripheral specialization.
