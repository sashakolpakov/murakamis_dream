Whole algebraic comparisons and their cyclotomic jets

Research note, 27 October 2026. This follows the user’s proposal that Rivin’s optimizer and quantum restrictions should be treated as parts of a whole period object. It proves a finite reconstruction theorem for algebraic comparison maps, including a quantum-normal direction which the earlier root-only source base does not record. The argument is exponent separation, Vandermonde inversion and a finite-algebra norm. No published rigidity or arithmetic module theorem is assumed.

This does not construct the missing comparison of the actual selected Kashaev period with the geometric regulator period. The volume conjecture remains unproved. In particular, the theorem below requires a fixed algebraic presentation or a bounded space of possible maps; it does not assert algebraicity of a quantum period from its classical saddle.

1. What is algebraic about the Rivin optimum

Let X be the nondegenerate gluing variety of a positive complete ideal triangulation, with shapes z_j, marked meridian M and longitude L. Its equations are monomial equations in z_j and 1-z_j, with integer exponents and the recorded peripheral signs. They define an algebraic variety over Q after inverting the shape denominators. Use the normalized geometric component, with its selected complete lift.

The positive rigidity proof shows that its marked-meridian Jacobian is invertible at the complete geometric point. Consequently:

  • the complete shapes are algebraic numbers;
  • the local shape branch is algebraic over Q(M), and hence over the function field of its marked A-curve;
  • after shrinking a suitable algebraic neighborhood and retaining the chosen place, the branch is an etale algebraic germ over M;
  • every nilpotent thickening of that chosen place has a unique shape lift once its meridian lift is specified.

The last assertion follows directly by Newton lifting. At each square- zero extension the correction is the unique solution of J delta z=-r, where J is the invertible gluing Jacobian and r is the residual. Repeating this through powers of the nilpotent ideal proves existence and uniqueness, with no convergence assertion needed.

The Rivin calculation independently proves strict concavity and stationarity at this geometric point. Thus positivity selects the same branch among the algebraic solutions. At the complete lift M=+1 or -1; the specified longitude sign and fundamental-class mark are still retained. M and L are not independent parameters on this curve.

These statements concern shapes. Their arguments, logarithms and dilogarithm periods are not thereby algebraic functions. For example, the algebraic Gaussian coefficient and the action exp(S/h) are distinct parts of the period. The already marked action has

Im S_complete=Vol(X)/2,   h_N=pi*i/N.

This gives log|exp(S_complete/h_N)|=N Vol(X)/(2pi). Establishing its contribution to the actual selected finite period is the remaining quantum assertion.

For coverage, Futer–Gueritaud, 1004.0440 describes the Casson–Rivin angle polytope and its critical-point criterion. Our rigidity and concavity proofs are in the linked notes.

2. Retain both power coordinates before taking quantum jets

Work over any characteristic-zero coefficient field k. Put

B=Spec k[t,t^(-1),q^(+/-1),M^(+/-1),v^(+/-1),w^(+/-1)].

Here t=1/N on the N-th restriction, and the power graphs are

Gamma_N: t=1/N, v=q^N, w=M^N.                 (2.1)

The actual cyclotomic point on this graph is

q=q_N=exp(pi*i/N), M=1, v=-1, w=1.           (2.2)

The other meridian lift is handled in the same way. The finite phase cover of the source base can be retained component by component, using levels in its specified residue class modulo four. None of its phases is reset here.

The exact meridian shift on B is

sigma(q)=q, sigma(M)=qM, sigma(v)=v,
sigma(w)=vw, sigma(t)=t.                     (2.3)

It preserves every Gamma_N. The two relative logarithmic derivatives which preserve these graphs are

D_q=q partial_q+t^(-1)v partial_v,
D_M=M partial_M+t^(-1)w partial_w.           (2.4)

They commute and satisfy the exact identities

D_M sigma=sigma D_M,
D_q sigma=sigma(D_q+D_M).                    (2.5)

These follow by checking the five coordinate generators and extending by the product rule. Their pullbacks to Gamma_N are exactly q d/dq and M d/dM. Equivalently, the power coordinates are marked horizontal sections of the algebraic relative equations

t dlog v=dlog q,   t dlog w=dlog M,           (2.6)

with t fixed. Equations (2.6) specify a connection; their horizontal sections need not be rational functions for nonintegral 1/t.

Setting v=-1 recovers the earlier root-only action w->-w. That closed slice is not preserved by D_q: D_q(v+1)=-1/t on it. In particular, replacing q^N by -1 before differentiating destroys quantum-normal information. The root values of v+1 are zero at every level, while their first quantum derivatives are -N.

This is a refinement of the old source base, not an assertion that an N-dimensional cyclic Weyl representation extends off its root. The actual whole q-germs must still be supplied by the specified color/quantum-period construction.

3. Finite cyclotomic jets detect a whole bounded presentation

Write a Laurent polynomial on B in the form

P=sum_(a,b,c,d) p_(a,b,c,d)(t) q^a v^b M^c w^d,
deg_t p_(a,b,c,d)<=D.                        (3.1)

A Laurent power of t can be cleared first. Let S be the number of distinct pairs (a,b), R the number of distinct pairs (c,d), A the span of the a exponents, and C the span of the c exponents. These are bounds of the fixed presentation, independent of N.

Finite reconstruction theorem. Choose D+1 distinct positive integers N>max(A,C). At each chosen N, suppose

(D_q^i D_M^j P)(1/N,q_N,1,-1,1)=0
        for 0<=i<S, 0<=j<R.                 (3.2)

Then P=0 on B. The same conclusion holds if these jets vanish for infinitely many sufficiently large N. The number of derivatives is independent of N. No lower bound on the algebraic degree of q_N is needed, and k need not be a number field.

Proof. On Gamma_N the exponents are

lambda_(a,b)=a+Nb,   mu_(c,d)=c+Nd.

The lambda values are pairwise distinct when N>A: equality with b!=b’ would give N<=|a-a’|. The same argument separates the mu values when N>C. Fill missing pairs with zero coefficients and form the S by R matrix A_N of coefficients p_(a,b,c,d)(1/N).

Let V_lambda and V_mu be the Vandermonde matrices with rows indexed by derivative order, and put W=diag(q_N^lambda). The rectangular jet matrix in (3.2) is exactly

J_N=V_lambda W A_N V_mu^t.                   (3.3)

All three outer factors are invertible: characteristic is zero, the exponent lists are distinct, and q_N!=0. Hence J_N=0 implies A_N=0. Every p coefficient vanishes at D+1 distinct values 1/N; its degree bound makes it identically zero. This proves the theorem.

If the full meridian family rather than its M=1 jets is known, its distinct M exponents can be compared directly; the R meridian jets are then unnecessary. Sparse supports can also reduce the rectangular test, but (3.2) gives a simple uniform sufficient bound.

The theorem applies to rational residuals by clearing their fixed denominators. At a pole one must specify the regularized whole map and lattice before testing, rather than separately evaluating a vanishing numerator and denominator. Increasing the allowable support increases the required jet bound. For example (v+1)^s defeats all quantum jets of order less than s, and a polynomial in t vanishing at the tested levels defeats an insufficient number of level tests.

The same proof handles any fixed number of power coordinates, including q(N2) from Gaussian phases. Write v_j=q(Nj), w_j=M(Nj), and use D_q(v_j)=t^(-j)v_j, D_M(w_j)=t^(-j)w_j. Exponents are now fixed-degree integer polynomials in N. Distinct such polynomials have distinct values for all sufficiently large N. The same two Vandermonde matrices and interpolation in t apply, with a fixed bound on the excluded levels. This retains a whole chosen phase lift; a phase’s values at the roots alone do not determine its quantum-normal derivatives.

4. The same detection works on a fixed algebraic shape cover

Let A be a finite free integral algebra over a localization of the coordinate ring of B, with fraction field K. A fixed algebraic shape branch is of this form after suitable localization. Consider a regular element f of A. At each tested cyclotomic point, choose an etale place, so it lifts uniquely to its two-variable formal q,M neighborhood, as in Section 1.

Take the norm n(f), the determinant of multiplication by f on A. It has a fixed rational presentation on B. If the chosen lift of f vanishes in the rectangular jet ring, then the same is true of n(f). Indeed that etale place splits off a rank-one factor after formal base change, and multiplication determinants factor as

n(f)=f_selected * det(m_f on the complementary factor).

The complementary determinant is regular. Apply Section 3 with support and degree bounds of the cleared norm. It follows that n(f)=0 identically. Since K is a field, the norm of a nonzero element cannot be zero, and hence f=0.

For a finite algebra with several integral components, apply this argument on the marked component; one selected component does not test all the others. A rational f must first have its poles cleared in the prescribed lattice. A finite number of shifted algebraic expressions can be treated in their finite compositum. We do not assume that the classical shape cover itself admits a finite algebraic lift of every q-shift.

Thus algebraic shape coefficients do not require an independent unbounded list of Taylor coefficients. Nilpotent restrictions are faithful tests of a fixed whole comparison once its presentation and chosen algebraic place have been supplied.

5. Consequence for a pointed quantum comparison

Suppose two specified finite-rank quantum objects have algebraic coefficient matrices, with their actual sources, cuts and integral lattices. Suppose a proposed comparison C and proposed inverse E are given on a fixed algebraic presentation. The intertwining, inverse, source and cut identities have residuals such as

C B_1-B_2 sigma(C),   CE-I,   EC-I,
C s_1-s_2,   ell_2 C-ell_1.                  (5.1)

If differential period connections are part of the specified objects, their residuals must also be checked:

D_i C+C Omega_(1,i)-Omega_(2,i) C,
                  i=q,M.                    (5.2)

The maps C and E must be regular as maps of the prescribed lattices on their marked charts. This condition retains the peripheral zero and pole factors; it is not inferred from nonzero fiber values. Each cleared residual in (5.1)–(5.2) has a fixed algebraic norm. The finite jet test with the resulting bounds therefore proves all these identities for the whole objects. This is a finite certificate criterion for an already constructed comparison, not an existence theorem for C or E.

In particular, equality of all fixed-root connection restrictions would not test a term proportional to dq. The quantum-normal jets and D_q in (5.2) are what test that direction. A classical optimizer, or a levelwise Hilbert-90 trivialization without bounded presentation, does not supply (5.1)–(5.2).

A bounded family of jet intertwiners also proves generic existence

There is an existence consequence which does not require proposing C in advance. It has a substantial uniformity hypothesis, stated here. Let F=k(q,t,v), and fix a finite-dimensional F-space of matrix functions of M,w, with a fixed Laurent support and denominator. Use this as the space of possible comparisons. The matrix entries of the two objects, their sources and cuts have fixed algebraic presentations. Include the meridian differential equation if it is specified and its unknown coefficients are constant for D_M.

For rational coefficient objects, expanding the difference, source, cut and meridian identities in this fixed space gives a finite affine linear system

H(q,t,v) u=b(q,t,v).                          (5.3)

All unknown coefficients u are in F; sigma fixes F. For algebraic coefficient objects, a system of the form (5.3) is an additional descent requirement: testing one algebraic place alone does not imply coefficientwise equality in a generic algebraic basis. The theorem here assumes the displayed finite system and jet solutions to it. Clear the fixed denominators of (5.3). The assertion concerns regular charts of the prescribed lattices, so the candidate map denominators are units on the test germs. Other boundary factors need their stated lattice charts first.

Let r be the generic rank of H. Take bounds for the t degrees and q,v supports of every r-minor of H and every (r+1)-minor of [H|b]. Such bounds are finite and can be obtained before choosing a comparison. Use D+1 levels for their t-degree bound and B quantum jets for their support bound as in Section 3. Use enough meridian jets to recover the coefficients in (5.3).

Bounded-comparison existence theorem. Suppose at all these levels there are solutions in the fixed comparison space to order B in the quantum parameter, satisfying the pointing identities to that order. Suppose their leading matrices are invertible as rational meridian matrices. Then there is a generically invertible comparison in that space over F satisfying all of (5.3) exactly.

Here order B means equality modulo epsilon^B, where q=q_N(1+epsilon), v=-(1+epsilon)^N. B is the number of jets, so the orders tested are 0 through B-1. It is enough to take B at least the largest number of distinct q,v exponent pairs in the stated minors. The size of the bound depends on the fixed comparison space, not on N. Maps at different levels need not be interpolated or assumed coherent beforehand.

Proof. A jet solution makes every (r+1)-minor of [H|b] vanish modulo epsilon^B. Indeed its last column is a linear combination of the H columns plus a remainder of order B, and every (r+1)-minor of H is identically zero. Section 3, now with no meridian variables, makes these augmented minors identically zero. Therefore (5.3) is generically consistent and has a rational affine family of solutions, computable by Gaussian elimination.

Choose a nonzero r-minor Delta of H. At one of the tested levels its whole power-graph pullback is nonzero; otherwise reconstruction would give Delta=0. Its order k at q_N is at most S_Delta-1 by the same sparse Vandermonde argument. In particular k<B.

At that level retain the free coordinates of a jet solution and solve the pivot equations exactly over the Laurent-series field in epsilon. The pivot inverse has pole order at most k, so the correction is O(epsilon^(B-k)). It preserves the leading matrix. The exact specialized affine solution family consequently contains an invertible matrix. Its determinant is thus not the zero polynomial in its free parameters. The generic rational affine family has the same property. Since F is infinite, free parameters can be chosen in F outside that determinant’s proper zero locus. This gives the claimed invertible rational comparison.

For r=0 the same argument first gives b=0; an invertible matrix in the proposed space can be chosen directly. The zero-size pivot has order zero.

This is an existence theorem for the difference/meridian/pointing identities encoded in (5.3). A q-direction period-connection identity contains derivatives of the unknown coefficients and is not an affine algebraic system of this form. It must be established for the constructed comparison by (5.2), or by a separate flatness and uniqueness argument with its hypotheses proved. Nor does generic invertibility prove regularity of C and its inverse on every boundary chart.

There is one useful sufficient condition for the q-connection check. Suppose the two supplied period connections and semilinear shifts obey

nabla_M T=T nabla_M,
nabla_q T=T(nabla_q+nabla_M),                 (5.4)

and their marked sources satisfy nabla_q s_i=0. Suppose s_1 is cyclic for T_1. If C already intertwines T and nabla_M and sends s_1 to s_2, then it automatically intertwines nabla_q. Indeed

E=nabla_(q,2) C-C nabla_(q,1)

is linear over the coefficient field; (5.4) and the meridian identity give E T_1=T_2 E. Also E s_1=0. Hence E kills every T_1^j s_1, which span the module, and E=0. The same proof works if nabla_q s_i is a specified common expression in T_i,nabla_(M,i),s_i already respected by C. Existence of these connections, their compatibility and this source law are hypotheses; they are not inferred from an A-symbol.

The unresolved knot-specific input is a bounded comparison space and its sufficiently deep source-preserving jet intertwiners. Classical etaleness alone does not provide them. If that input is proved, however, generic algebraic existence follows from the finite rank argument above; a comparison matrix need not be guessed from a list of knot examples.

The subsequent divisor-flow descent theorem supplies such a bound for a stated rank-one class. If the comparison coefficient has a fixed product of linear factors in known q-shift orbits, equality of its full cyclic norm characters at every large level forces zero divisor charge in each orbit. A finite cumulative flow then constructs one rational generic gauge with an explicit degree bound. No bounds for the separate levelwise gauges are assumed. A second theorem treats moving algebraic zeros and poles under explicit unramified and nonresonance hypotheses, using uniqueness of exponential sequences with meromorphic coefficients. It also records an exact half-root alias counterexample and a direct construction of a nonzero actual annihilator over Q=q^2. Establishing the comparison line’s stated hypotheses and norm compatibility remains necessary.

6. Relation to the existing work and the volume target

The earlier uniform source base already proves whole-root-graph Zariski density for fixed rational functions of q,M,M^N and the finite phases. The all-level period proof already uses sparse Vandermonde multiplicity bounds for singular companion steps. The addition here is the simultaneous power-coordinate base for q^N and M^N, its differential/shift compatibility, and the finite reconstruction of whole algebraic maps from both quantum and meridian jets, extended to marked etale shape covers by a norm. The bounded-comparison theorem further gives generic existence from jet solutions in a fixed finite comparison space, by rank minors and a controlled pivot lift, without a precomputed matrix. No novelty claim is made for these elementary algebraic arguments.

This supplies a rigorous part of the proposed functions-before-jets approach. The actual missing construction is the whole pointed quantum comparison, with its period marking. Rivin optimization and algebraic gluing supply its geometric place; they do not by themselves algebraize the quantum period or choose its global source.

The central normalization theorem already proves that a nonzero factor from a fixed finite algebraic central presentation has log norm O(log N), after whole-germ specialization. Thus a comparison of that type would have controlled normalization. Neither its existence nor the actual period’s geometric exponential component is inferred here. The action and the nonzero algebraic principal density are established in the geometric notes; their selected finite-period contribution remains the outstanding theorem.

The subsequent full rank-one proof removes the earlier unramified and nonresonance restrictions for rational Q-coefficients. Weighted exponential–Puiseux uniqueness detects all moving divisor orbits, including poles tending to zero or infinity at Q=1. 7. Actual clock transport requires functions before finite evaluation

There is now an exact instance of this distinction on the ORIGINAL high-color source. Put C_Q=Q^S Z^R, Z=Q^N, with Frobenius index 0<=R<=N. Its actual whole divided matrices e_q,f_q obey

e_q C_Q=Z C_Q e_q,    f_q C_Q=Z^(-1) C_Q f_q.

Their nonzero interior arrows are units at the marked root. Let e_qdagger,f_qdagger be their finite inverse arrows, retaining the endpoint projections P_0,P_top. Then

e_q^dagger e_q=I-P_0,    f_q^dagger f_q=I-P_top.

For a retained rational function g whose denominators are invertible at BOTH C_Q and Z C_Q, the endpoint-completed current is exactly

[-e_q^dagger[e_q,g(Z C_Q)]
   +P_0 f_q^dagger[f_q,g(C_Q)]]/(1-Z)
              =[g(C_Q)-g(Z C_Q)]/(1-Z).             (7.1)

The proof and original-matrix checks are in the whole Jackson-current theorem. The quotient is regular at Z=1 and gives C g’(C). The shifted argument in the first commutator is essential. Keeping only g(C_Q) does not specify (7.1): the finite evaluation kernel need not be preserved by g(x)->g(Zx).

For example, on one head string set c=Q^s and

p_N(x)=product_(R=0)^N(x-c Z^R).

Then p_N(C_Q)=0 on that string, whereas

p_N(Z C_Q)=c^(N+1) Z^[N(N+1)/2]
                product_(k=1)^(N+1)(Z^k-1) P_top.    (7.2)

After division by 1-Z its first nonzero normal order is N. Consequently every fixed normal depth eventually misses this endpoint. The degree of p_N grows with N, so this example respects the FIXED-presentation hypothesis of Section 3. It does not contradict the independent bounded-lattice Mellin uniqueness theorem either.

The function-algebra audit also proves the general algebraic reason: for generic Z of infinite multiplicative order, the Laurent function algebra has no nonzero proper finite-codimension ideal preserved by x->Zx. Thus a whole function algebra with its relative endpoint data is the appropriate object for this current; a finite functional-calculus quotient loses part of its transport. At the cyclotomic fiber the finite matrix representation remains valid.

The same audit supplies an explicit positive construction. Over R=k[[delta]], Z=1-delta, take the monic spectral lattices

A_L=R[x,x^(-1)]/product_(r=0)^L(x-cZ^r).

They are free of rank L+1, and their whole Jackson maps give an exact primitive sequence

0 -> R*1 -> A_L -> A_(L-1) -> 0.

One extra endpoint per current suffices. In their Newton bases the evaluation lattice has exact Smith factors 1,delta,…,delta^L; the root algebra is the confluent (x-c_0)^(L+1) jet algebra. Thus finite ambient matrix evaluation and its integral function lattice carry different root data. This establishes flatness and solvability for this clock complex. The full pointed knot/geometric comparison is a further complex with its own flatness condition.

The source has a compatible global P1/Serre model and an actual relative interval. Its retained pairing density gives a rational whole comparison of the interval co-source with a normalized uniform row. These are explicit source constructions before taking any normal jets. The full braid coefficient currents and their Gaussian pushforward must still respect them. In particular an abstractly matching Borel action, or agreement of its root two-form, does not supply that last intertwining identity.

At the finite cut a further whole degree-elevation map now supplies such a current/co-source comparison for the actual two-row head K/F complex. Its explicit matrix formula and retained whole diagonal gauges preserve the image complex, while an explicit trace homotopy carries the original cut row. The map is linear and not multiplicative; equality of this cut complex does not assert transport of the full ordered crossing network or its geometric relative period.
