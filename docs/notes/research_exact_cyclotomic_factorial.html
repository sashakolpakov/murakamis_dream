An exact formula for the canonical factorial

This note evaluates the logarithmic derivative of the canonical factorial as a finite sum. Integrating that sum gives an exact formula in the lower half-plane, including its additive constant. In particular, its behavior at large negative imaginary part is determined by the original gamma product; no unspecified periodic factor remains.

The result concerns the interpolation used in papers/conway-asymptotics/factorial.tex. It is not by itself an estimate for a quantum sum or a proof that a correction contour can be discarded.

1. Definition and statement

Fix an integer N≥2N\geq2. For −1<ℜz<N-1<\Re z<N, define GN(z)=(2πN)zΓ(z+1)∏m=1∞Γ(Nm+z+1)Γ(Nm−z)(Nm)2z+1.(1) G_N(z)=\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^z\Gamma(z+1) \prod_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{\Gamma(Nm+z+1)}{\Gamma(Nm-z)(Nm)^{2z+1}}. \qquad\text{(1)} Powers of positive numbers use their real logarithms. The product is locally normally convergent, is nonzero in this strip, and has GN(0)=1G_N(0)=1. Its logarithm is chosen to be zero at z=0z=0. These facts also follow directly from the product: its mm-th logarithmic factor is −z(z+1)(2z+1)6N2m2+O(m−3).(2) -\frac{z(z+1)(2z+1)}{6N^2m^2}+O(m^{-3}). \qquad\text{(2)} The shift relation GN(z+1)=2sin⁡π(z+1)NGN(z)(3) G_N(z+1)=2\sin\frac{\pi(z+1)}N\,G_N(z) \qquad\text{(3)} continues GNG_N to a nonzero holomorphic function on ℑz<0\Im z<0. There is a unique logarithm there agreeing with the logarithm of (1) on the overlap. Write this logarithm as gNg_N.

Theorem. The logarithmic derivative is the finite sum gN′(z)=πN2∑k=0N−1(z−k)cot⁡π(z−k)N.(4) g_N'(z)=\frac{\pi}{N^2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (z-k)\cot\frac{\pi(z-k)}N. \qquad\text{(4)} Terms at z=kz=k have their removable values. Formula (4) holds on the original strip and by continuation wherever both sides are regular.

For ℑt<0\Im t<0, put L(t)=iπt22+tLog⁡(1−e−2πit)+i2πLi⁡2(e−2πit).(5) L(t)=\frac{i\pi t^2}{2} +t\operatorname{Log}(1-e^{-2\pi it}) +\frac{i}{2\pi}\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-2\pi it}). \qquad\text{(5)} Here Log⁡(1−w)=−∑j≥1wj/j\operatorname{Log}(1-w)=-\sum_{j\geq1}w^j/j and Li⁡2(w)=∑j≥1wj/j2\operatorname{Li}_2(w)=\sum_{j\geq1}w^j/j^2 for |w|<1|w|<1. Thus the branches in (5) are specified without analytic continuation across a cut. For every ℑx<0\Im x<0, gN(Nx)=12log⁡N−iπN12+∑k=0N−1L(x−kN).(6) \boxed{ g_N(Nx)=\frac12\log N-\frac{i\pi N}{12} +\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}L\left(x-\frac{k}{N}\right).} \qquad\text{(6)}

The remainder form of the same identity is gN(Nx)=iπNx22−iπ(N−1)x2+12log⁡N+iπ(N12−14+112N)+EN(x).(7) \begin{split} g_N(Nx)={}&\frac{i\pi N x^2}{2} -\frac{i\pi(N-1)x}{2}+\frac12\log N\\ &+i\pi\left(\frac{N}{12}-\frac14+\frac{1}{12N}\right) +E_N(x). \qquad\text{(7)} \end{split} Writing w=e−2πixw=e^{-2\pi ix} and ζN=e2πi/N\zeta_N=e^{2\pi i/N}, the remainder is exactly EN(x)=xLog⁡(1−wN)−1N∑k=0N−1kLog⁡(1−wζNk)+i2πNLi⁡2(wN).(8) \boxed{ E_N(x)=x\operatorname{Log}(1-w^N) -\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}k\operatorname{Log}(1-w\zeta_N^k) +\frac{i}{2\pi N}\operatorname{Li}_2(w^N).} \qquad\text{(8)} Every logarithm and dilogarithm on the right is analytic in the disk |w|<1|w|<1. The factor xLog⁡(1−wN)x\operatorname{Log}(1-w^N) is displayed separately; xx is still the coordinate on the covering half-plane.

If r=|w|<1r=|w|<1, one explicit bound is |EN(x)|≤(|x|+12πN)rN1−rN+N−12r1−r.(9) |E_N(x)|\leq \left(|x|+\frac{1}{2\pi N}\right)\frac{r^N}{1-r^N} +\frac{N-1}{2}\frac{r}{1-r}. \qquad\text{(9)} For fixed NN, (9) gives the stated quadratic asymptotic, with error ON((1+|x|)e2πℑx)O_N((1+|x|)e^{2\pi\Im x}), as ℑx→−∞\Im x\to-\infty in any fixed vertical strip. The explicit bound (9), rather than this shorthand, also applies when xx and NN vary simultaneously.

2. Finite evaluation of the logarithmic derivative

Let ψ=Γ′/Γ\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma. Logarithmic differentiation of the locally normally convergent product gives gN′(z)=log⁡2πN+ψ(z+1)+∑m≥1[ψ(Nm+z+1)+ψ(Nm−z)−2log⁡(Nm)].(10) g_N'(z)=\log\frac{2\pi}{N}+\psi(z+1) +\sum_{m\geq1}\bigl[ \psi(Nm+z+1)+\psi(Nm-z)-2\log(Nm)\bigr]. \qquad\text{(10)} All manipulations below may first be made away from the isolated poles of the individual digamma terms. The resulting identities extend across their removable singularities.

Set aj=z+1+jN,bj=−z+jN,0≤j<N.(11) a_j=\frac{z+1+j}{N},\qquad b_j=\frac{-z+j}{N}, \qquad 0\leq j<N. \qquad\text{(11)} The Gauss multiplication identity, differentiated once, says ψ(Nv)=log⁡N+1N∑j=0N−1ψ(v+j/N).(12) \psi(Nv)=\log N+\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\psi(v+j/N). \qquad\text{(12)} Consequently the part of the sum in (10) with 1≤m≤K1\leq m\leq K is 1N∑j=0N−1[TK(aj)+TK(bj)]−2log⁡Γ(K+1),(13) \frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\bigl[T_K(a_j)+T_K(b_j)\bigr] -2\log\Gamma(K+1), \qquad\text{(13)} where the functional equation of the digamma function gives the exact finite identity TK(a):=∑m=1Kψ(m+a)=(K+a)ψ(K+a+1)−aψ(a+1)−K.(14) T_K(a):=\sum_{m=1}^K\psi(m+a) =(K+a)\psi(K+a+1)-a\psi(a+1)-K. \qquad\text{(14)} For example, subtracting the expression at K−1K-1 from that at KK gives ψ(K+a)\psi(K+a), and the expression at K=0K=0 vanishes.

For aa in a compact set, TK(a)=Klog⁡K−K+alog⁡K+a+12−aψ(a+1)+O(K−1).(15) T_K(a)=K\log K-K+a\log K+a+\frac12-a\psi(a+1)+O(K^{-1}). \qquad\text{(15)} Also 1N∑j=0N−1(aj+bj)=1. \frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(a_j+b_j)=1. Substitution of (15) into (13), followed by Stirling’s formula for Γ(K+1)\Gamma(K+1), therefore gives the limit 2−log⁡(2π)−1N∑j[ajψ(aj+1)+bjψ(bj+1)]=−log⁡(2π)−1N∑j[ajψ(aj)+bjψ(bj)].(16) 2-\log(2\pi) -\frac1N\sum_j\bigl[a_j\psi(a_j+1)+b_j\psi(b_j+1)\bigr] =-\log(2\pi) -\frac1N\sum_j\bigl[a_j\psi(a_j)+b_j\psi(b_j)\bigr]. \qquad\text{(16)} The constants 22 cancel because aψ(a+1)=aψ(a)+1a\psi(a+1)=a\psi(a)+1. The error tends locally uniformly to zero. Equations (10) and (16) give gN′(z)=ψ(z+1)−log⁡N−1N∑j[ajψ(aj)+bjψ(bj)].(17) g_N'(z)=\psi(z+1)-\log N -\frac1N\sum_j\bigl[a_j\psi(a_j)+b_j\psi(b_j)\bigr]. \qquad\text{(17)}

The pairing bN−1−j=1−ajb_{N-1-j}=1-a_j and the reflection identity ψ(1−a)−ψ(a)=πcot⁡(πa) \psi(1-a)-\psi(a)=\pi\cot(\pi a) transform the summand into aψ(a)+(1−a)ψ(1−a)=ψ(a)+(1−a)πcot⁡(πa). a\psi(a)+(1-a)\psi(1-a) =\psi(a)+(1-a)\pi\cot(\pi a). Applying (12) once more cancels ψ(z+1)−log⁡N\psi(z+1)-\log N in (17). Thus gN′(z)=−πN∑j=0N−1(1−aj)cot⁡(πaj).(18) g_N'(z)=-\frac\pi N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-a_j)\cot(\pi a_j). \qquad\text{(18)} With k=N−1−jk=N-1-j, one has aj=1+(z−k)/Na_j=1+(z-k)/N; (18) is exactly (4). This argument starts from (1), rather than from a difference equation, so it determines the derivative of the specified interpolation.

3. Integration and the constant

Direct differentiation of the convergent series in (5) gives L′(t)=iπt1+e−2πit1−e−2πit=πtcot⁡(πt).(19) L'(t)=i\pi t\frac{1+e^{-2\pi it}}{1-e^{-2\pi it}} =\pi t\cot(\pi t). \qquad\text{(19)} The derivative with respect to xx of the sum in (6) is therefore NgN′(Nx)N g_N'(Nx). Their difference is constant on the lower half-plane. To determine it, take x→0x\to0 from below.

For 0<r<10<r<1, the boundary value from below has Log⁡(1−e2πir)=log⁡(2sin⁡πr)+iπ(r−1/2). \operatorname{Log}(1-e^{2\pi ir})=\log(2\sin\pi r)+i\pi(r-1/2). The elementary Fourier identity ℜLi⁡2(e2πir)=π2(r2−r+1/6)(20) \Re\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{2\pi ir}) =\pi^2(r^2-r+1/6) \qquad\text{(20)} then gives ℑL(−r)=π/12,ℜL(−r)=−rlog⁡(2sin⁡πr)−12πℑLi⁡2(e2πir).(21) \begin{split} \Im L(-r)&=\pi/12,\\ \Re L(-r)&=-r\log(2\sin\pi r) -\frac1{2\pi}\Im\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{2\pi ir}). \qquad\text{(21)} \end{split} Identity (20) can be obtained by integrating the imaginary part of −Log⁡(1−e2πir)=∑m≥1e2πimr/m-\operatorname{Log}(1-e^{2\pi ir})=\sum_{m\geq1}e^{2\pi imr}/m, first with an Abel factor less than one. The integration constant is ∑m≥1m−2=π2/6\sum_{m\geq1}m^{-2}=\pi^2/6. All dilogarithm boundary series converge absolutely. At r=0r=0, the term tLog⁡(1−e−2πit)t\operatorname{Log}(1-e^{-2\pi it}) tends to zero, so L(0)=iπ/12L(0)=i\pi/12 in this limiting sense.

In the sum over r=k/Nr=k/N, the imaginary parts of the dilogarithms in the second line of (21) cancel in the pairs k,N−kk,N-k. The same pairing gives ∑k=1N−1kNlog⁡(2sin⁡πk/N)=12∑k=1N−1log⁡(2sin⁡πk/N)=12log⁡N,(22) \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{k}{N}\log(2\sin\pi k/N) =\frac12\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\log(2\sin\pi k/N) =\frac12\log N, \qquad\text{(22)} where the last equality follows by evaluating ∏k=1N−1(1−ζNk)=N\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}(1-\zeta_N^k)=N and taking absolute values. It follows that ∑k=0N−1L(−k/N)=−12log⁡N+iπN12.(23) \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}L(-k/N)=-\frac12\log N+\frac{i\pi N}{12}. \qquad\text{(23)} Since gN(0)=0g_N(0)=0, equation (23) fixes the constant in (6).

4. Disk expression and the lower end

Summing the quadratic terms in (6) uses ∑k=0N−1kN=N−12,∑k=0N−1k2N2=(N−1)(2N−1)6N. \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{k}{N}=\frac{N-1}{2},\qquad \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{k^2}{N^2} =\frac{(N-1)(2N-1)}{6N}. These sums give the polynomial and constant in (7). The remainder before resummation is EN(x)=∑k=0N−1[(x−kN)Log(1−wζNk)+i2πLi⁡2(wζNk)].(24) E_N(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left[ \left(x-\frac{k}{N}\right)\operatorname{Log}(1-w\zeta_N^k) +\frac{i}{2\pi}\operatorname{Li}_2(w\zeta_N^k) \right]. \qquad\text{(24)} Absolute convergence in the disk proves the root-of-unity identities ∑kLog⁡(1−wζNk)=Log⁡(1−wN),∑kLi⁡2(wζNk)=1NLi⁡2(wN).(25) \sum_k\operatorname{Log}(1-w\zeta_N^k)=\operatorname{Log}(1-w^N),\qquad \sum_k\operatorname{Li}_2(w\zeta_N^k) =\frac1N\operatorname{Li}_2(w^N). \qquad\text{(25)} There is no additive logarithmic ambiguity: both sides of the first identity vanish at w=0w=0. Substitution gives (8).

The elementary series bounds |Log⁡(1−v)|≤|v|1−|v|,|Li⁡2(v)|≤|v|1−|v| |\operatorname{Log}(1-v)|\leq\frac{|v|}{1-|v|},\qquad |\operatorname{Li}_2(v)|\leq\frac{|v|}{1-|v|} and ∑k/N=(N−1)/2\sum k/N=(N-1)/2 prove (9).

One may equivalently exponentiate (7) and (8): GN(Nx)=Nexp⁡{iπNx22−iπ(N−1)x2+iπ(N12−14+112N)}⋅exp⁡{xLog⁡(1−wN)}∏k=0N−1exp⁡{−kNLog(1−wζNk)}exp⁡{i2πNLi⁡2(wN)}.(26) \begin{split} G_N(Nx)={}&\sqrt N\exp\left\{ \frac{i\pi N x^2}{2}-\frac{i\pi(N-1)x}{2} +i\pi\left(\frac{N}{12}-\frac14+\frac1{12N}\right)\right\}\\ &\cdot\exp\{x\operatorname{Log}(1-w^N)\} \prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{k}{N}\operatorname{Log}(1-w\zeta_N^k)\right\} \exp\left\{\frac{i}{2\pi N}\operatorname{Li}_2(w^N)\right\}. \qquad\text{(26)} \end{split} The finite product on the second line, excluding the displayed factor containing xx, is a nonzero holomorphic function of ww at w=0w=0, with value one. Thus (26) records exactly which factor still depends on the logarithmic covering coordinate. Applications to a source containing several factorials must combine these factors with the original quadratic phases before concluding that the source descends to a function of ww.

5. Numerical reproduction

The standalone script experiments/check_exact_cyclotomic_factorial.py checks the finite formula against truncated versions of the original gamma product and its logarithmic derivative. It also checks the lattice normalization, shift, reflection, equality of (6) and (8), and the explicit bound (9). Run

python3 experiments/check_exact_cyclotomic_factorial.py --output repro/results/research-exact-cyclotomic-factorial.json

The saved values are in repro/results/research-exact-cyclotomic-factorial.json. These finite-precision calculations are diagnostics; the proof is the finite digamma calculation and the determination of its constant above.

6. Relation to the existing cyclic dilogarithm

The weighted finite product in (26) is the inverse of the cyclic dilogarithm DN(w;ζN)=∏k=1N−1(1−wζNk)k/N,DN(0;ζN)=1, D_N(w;\zeta_N)=\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}(1-w\zeta_N^k)^{k/N}, \qquad D_N(0;\zeta_N)=1, with its local branches fixed at zero. This is an established function; see equation (9) and Section 1.2 of Garoufalidis and Kashaev, Evaluation of state integrals at rational points, version 2. That paper also treats formulas at roots of unity in Section 1.4.

This attribution concerns the known finite product. The proof above derives the specified Gamma interpolation, its constant and its logarithmic covering factor directly. Its application here is to the original finite source and the precise contour correction in the adjacent-pair translation calculation.
