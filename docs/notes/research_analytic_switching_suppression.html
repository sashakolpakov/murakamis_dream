Exponential suppression of an analytic discrete switch

31 October 2026. This is a finite difference normal-form theorem with an explicit error. It does not identify one eigenbranch as the growth rate of a general slowly varying recurrence. Its application to the original middle-row sums requires their literal transfer and boundary data.

1. Analytic triangular transfer and an exact finite construction

Let I=[a,b]I=[a,b], UR={z∈ℂ:dist⁡(z,I)<R}U_R=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{dist}(z,I)<R\}, and let the bounded analytic block matrix T(t)=(A(t)B(t)0C(t)) T(t)=\begin{pmatrix}A(t)&B(t)\\0&C(t)\end{pmatrix} \tag{1} be given on URU_R. Rectangular off-diagonal blocks are allowed. Assume the Sylvester operator 𝒮t(X)=XC(t)−A(t)X \mathcal S_t(X)=XC(t)-A(t)X has a bounded analytic inverse on URU_R. Fix compatible matrix norms. Put H0(t)=𝒮t−1B(t),ℒt(X)=𝒮t−1(A(t)X),K=supUR∥ℒt∥,M0=supUR∥H0(t)∥. H_0(t)=\mathcal S_t^{-1}B(t),\qquad \mathcal L_t(X)=\mathcal S_t^{-1}(A(t)X), \quad K=\sup_{U_R}\|\mathcal L_t\|, \quad M_0=\sup_{U_R}\|H_0(t)\|. For a positive mesh hh, define the EXACT difference operator (ℱhH)(t)=ℒt[H(t−h)−H(t)] (\mathcal F_h H)(t)=\mathcal L_t\,[H(t-h)-H(t)] and its finite sum H[m]=∑j=0m−1ℱhjH0,Ph(t)=(IH[m](t)0I). Direct multiplication gives Ph(t)−1T(t)Ph(t−h)=(A(t)Rh(t)0C(t)),Rh(t)=𝒮t(ℱhmH0(t)). 2. An exponentially small residual with a bounded gauge

Choose m=⌊R8(K+1)h⌋≥1,σ=R2m. m=\left\lfloor\frac{R}{8(K+1)h}\right\rfloor\ge1, \qquad \sigma=\frac{R}{2m}. \tag{4} For a function analytic on UrU_r, Cauchy’s derivative bound on the segment from t−ht-h to tt gives, when h≤σ/2h\le\sigma/2, ∥H(t−h)−H(t)∥Ur−σ≤2hσ∥H∥Ur. \|H(t-h)-H(t)\|_{U_{r-\sigma}} \le \frac{2h}{\sigma}\|H\|_{U_r}. There are mm nested domains between URU_R and UR/2U_{R/2}. Consequently each application of ℱh\mathcal F_h costs at most q=4KhmR≤K2(K+1)<12. q=\frac{4Khm}{R}\le\frac{K}{2(K+1)}<\frac12. The choice (4) also ensures h≤σ/2h\le\sigma/2. Thus on UR/2U_{R/2}, ∥H[m]∥≤2M0,∥Rh∥≤M0supUR∥𝒮t∥2−m≤C0e−c/h,c=Rlog⁡28(K+1). \|H^{[m]}\|\le2M_0,\qquad \|R_h\|\le M_0\sup_{U_R}\|\mathcal S_t\|\,2^{-m} \le C_0e^{-c/h}, \quad c=\frac{R\log2}{8(K+1)}. \tag{5} One may take C0=2M0sup⁡∥𝒮t∥C_0=2M_0\sup\|\mathcal S_t\|. 3. The full product retains both endpoint contributions

Take tj=t0+jht_j=t_0+jh, j=1,…,nj=1,\ldots,n, all in II, with nh≤Lnh\le L fixed. Write Aj=A(tj)A_j=A(t_j), Cj=C(tj)C_j=C(t_j), Hj=H[m](tj)H_j=H^{[m]}(t_j), and use identity matrices for empty products. Let SnS_n be the off-diagonal block of Tn⋯T1T_n\cdots T_1. The block triangular product has at most one off-diagonal transition, so (3) gives the exact identity Sn=Hn(Cn⋯C1)−(An⋯A1)H0+∑j=1nAn⋯Aj+1Rh(tj)Cj−1⋯C1. \boxed{\displaystyle S_n=H_n(C_n\cdots C_1)-(A_n\cdots A_1)H_0 +\sum_{j=1}^n A_n\cdots A_{j+1}\,R_h(t_j)\, C_{j-1}\cdots C_1.} \tag{6} In (6), H0H_0 denotes the gauge at t0t_0, not the zeroth term 𝒮−1B\mathcal S^{-1}B used in (2).

Define the possible switch amplification Ωn=max1≤j≤n∥An⋯Aj+1∥∥Cj−1⋯C1∥. \Omega_n=\max_{1\le j\le n} \|A_n\cdots A_{j+1}\|\, \|C_{j-1}\cdots C_1\|. Then ∥Sn∥≤2M0(∥An⋯A1∥+∥Cn⋯C1∥)+nC0e−c/hΩn. \|S_n\|\le2M_0\bigl(\|A_n\cdots A_1\| +\|C_n\cdots C_1\|\bigr) +nC_0e^{-c/h}\Omega_n. \tag{7} In particular, if the two pure endpoint products satisfy ∥An⋯A1∥+∥Cn⋯C1∥≤C1e−γ/hΩn,γ>0, \|A_n\cdots A_1\|+\|C_n\cdots C_1\| \le C_1e^{-\gamma/h}\Omega_n, \qquad\gamma>0, then ∥Sn∥≤C2(1+n)e−min⁡(c,γ)/hΩn\|S_n\|\le C_2(1+n)e^{-\min(c,\gamma)/h}\Omega_n. This is a strict exponential loss relative to the absolute interior-switch envelope. The residual term in (7) can still dominate both pure products. It would be false to infer from this theorem that the growth is always one diagonal branch’s action.

4. Scalar phase separation

For scalar blocks a,ca,c, the Sylvester hypothesis is just c−a≠0c-a\ne0 on a fixed complex neighborhood. If on the real interval a(t)=eiθaa+(t),c(t)=eiθcc+(t),m*≤a+,c+≤M*, a(t)=e^{i\theta_a}a_+(t),\qquad c(t)=e^{i\theta_c}c_+(t),\qquad m_*\le a_+,c_+\le M_*, and |sin⁡((θa−θc)/2)|≥ε>0|\sin((\theta_a-\theta_c)/2)|\ge\varepsilon>0, then |a(t)−c(t)|≥2m*ε. |a(t)-c(t)|\ge2m_*\varepsilon. Analyticity and uniform bounds extend this separation to a sufficiently small fixed complex neighborhood. Thus a nonzero relative phase gives the required analytic gap even when |a|=|c||a|=|c| on the real interval.

With a scalar positive coupling b+(t)b_+(t) bounded above and below, the corresponding positive switch sum has size between constant multiples of Ωn\Omega_n and nΩnn\Omega_n. Hence (7) proves exponential cancellation relative to that positive sum whenever its maximizing switch has a fixed rate gap from both endpoints. Constant phases on the coupling do not affect this comparison.

5. Interface with the original source

The positive tail transfer supplies an exact two-state active block for the original spectral continuant. Before and after that block, the source carries the actual middle powers, unused row factors, and the retained omitted-half scalar modes. The literal four-state dictionary now applies (3) directly to a mismatched middle phase. The regular-window theorem checks full block separation and both endpoint gaps, without a guessed analytic interpolation of discrete endpoints. In particular it excludes a collision with the secondary eigenvalue as well as the Perron eigenvalue.

Those regular windows include exact sampled zeros in common retained factors. Protected degeneracies, spectral caps and boundary maxima still require separate estimates. An inconsistent shared phase system gives an exponential improvement relative to its specified window envelope; no general comparison of that envelope with hyperbolic volume is claimed.
