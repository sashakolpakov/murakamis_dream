A specified generic exceptional-operator lift at nonreal deficient degree

30 October 2026. Independently reviewed. The original generic exceptional polynomial has a source-preserving meromorphic lift to a nonreal deficient-degree neighborhood. It uses the actual LARGE-channel white numerators and an explicitly chosen moving-period factorial. Every fixed genuine q derivative has subexponential relative cost in shrinking folded-channel windows. At maximal folded channels the cost is polynomial, at both parities.

The root value of this generic lift is exp(-2pi i v) times the earlier phase-canceled entire kernel, where v is the target input row. This integer character is one on the source lattice but is retained off it. Negative channel-row poles are genuine divisors of the generic lift. Neither a nonzero leading normal amplitude nor a nonzero transported global cycle is asserted.

1. Which generic operator and which source germ

Fix N>=3, d=N-1, 0<=a<=floor(d/2), m=d-a, s=d-2a, and zeta=q0 exp(epsilon), q0=exp(pi i/N), Q=zeta^2. All complex powers use this Log zeta and the specified logarithmic row lifts. Use the ORIGINAL generic identity

p_a(Omega)=D_a/(rho_a beta_N) B^(-1) R^(-1) S_(2a),
S_(2a)=W_a p_(2a) W_a^T,                             (1)

proved in the actual exceptional polynomial theorem. Here B is the actual nested cup bend and R the actual cyclic turn. In particular this is not the regular complete-circle lift W_a pi_a with a fixed root dual scalar. Its coefficient

c_a(zeta):=D_a/(rho_a beta_N)
  =(-1)^a N^2 zeta^(a^2-2d^2)
      [2a]! [m]! / product_(j=1,j!=N)^(d+a+1)[j]     (2)

has the factor [N] canceled as an EXACT generic quotient. The actual completed operator is E_a=D_a^(-1)p_a(Omega); dividing our lift below by the same moving D_a gives that very generic germ and its jets. All factors remaining in (2), D_a, and their inverses are root units.

For an original low-degree matrix entry put

0<=u,v<=t<=d, h=v-u, ell=t-v,
sigma=d-h, r=a-h.

The polynomial bandwidth gives zero identically in zeta when |h|>a. On the SUPPORTED grid |h|<=a, inverse bending and the original turn give the exact generic tensor entry

[p_a(J_t)]_(u,v)
  =c_a W_a(d-v,u;r) p_(2a)(r)
          W_a(t-u,d-t+v;2a-r)
    /[(-1)^d zeta^(-d+2v) pV(v) pV(ell)].            (3)

All positions and cup orientations in (3) are literal. They are the same ones used in the original physical-operator audit; no scalar symbol is substituted for an operator.

2. Put both actual white factors in the same deficient chart

The boundary/source theorem gives a finite continuation of the original white map:

mathscr W_a(zeta;sigma,i)
   =(-1)^m zeta^[-d(sigma-m)+di-m(m-1)]
      H_m^B(Q^i,Q^(sigma-i))/F_m,
B=Q^(-N), F_m=(Q;Q)_m.                              (4)

This is the actual LARGE generic numerator H_m. It agrees with every physical white coefficient in both total-degree halves, not just its root value.

The high factor in (3) can be reduced before any interpolation. For the original Chevalley maps J_L|i>=zeta1|L-i> and S=P(J_d tensor J_d), the generic intertwiner identity is

S W_a=kappa W_a J_(2a).

Its scalar is fixed without a square-root convention: at middle channel row a the physical total is d, and S acts by zeta^(-Nd) on that entire degree block. The column is nonzero, so

kappa=zeta^[-Nd+a(2a+1)].

Consequently the EXACT generic reversal is

W_a(d-j,d-i;2a-r)=zeta^[s(a-r)] W_a(i,j;r).           (5)

For (3), its second white factor is therefore zeta^(s h) mathscr W_a(zeta;sigma,ell), and its first is mathscr W_a(zeta;sigma,sigma-u). Both actual numerators have the same complex total sigma. Equation (5) is proved on the finite source; the displayed right side now specifies the continued high factor. No off-grid conclusion is inferred merely from physical jet bounds.

3. The factorial lift and its original support

Let

P=pi i/Log zeta,
G_P(z)=(2pi/P)^z Gamma(z+1)
   * product_(k>=1) Gamma(Pk+z+1)
                  /[Gamma(Pk-z)(Pk)^(2z+1)],
Fhat_P(z)=exp[(Log zeta)z(z+1)/2-pi i z/2] G_P(z).    (6)

The moving-period factorial lemma proves local convergence, meromorphic continuation, and

Fhat_P(z+1)=(1-zeta^(2z+2))Fhat_P(z),
Fhat_P(0)=1, Fhat_P(n)=(Q;Q)_n                       (7)

for every nonnegative integer n at which the finite product is regular. In particular (7) agrees with every supported generic factorial in (3), with highest weights d and 2a FIXED. They are not replaced by P-1 as zeta varies.

Define the specified meromorphic cups by

mathscr p_L(zeta;x)
  =exp(pi i x) zeta^[x(L-x-1)]
                   Fhat_P(x)Fhat_P(L-x)/F_L,
mathscr pV(zeta;x)=N zeta^(-d^2) mathscr p_d(zeta;x). (8)

The factor exp(pi i x) specifies the original (-1)^x mark. Equations (2), (4), (6), and (8) define the full local lift

mathscr K_a(zeta;t,u,v)
   =c_a zeta^(s h)
      mathscr W_a(zeta;sigma,sigma-u)
      mathscr W_a(zeta;sigma,ell) mathscr p_(2a)(zeta;r)
      /[(-1)^d zeta^(-d+2v)
                 mathscr pV(zeta;v) mathscr pV(zeta;ell)]. (9)

For every original SUPPORTED physical entry this equals (3) for generic zeta, hence matches all its genuine q jets. The lift of the completed operator is mathscr E_a=D_a^(-1) mathscr K_a.

The original finite sum may discard the identically zero entries |h|>a before its residue cycle is introduced. Small product circles around its remaining original lattice points then evaluate this same generic source exactly, with its other tensors and exposed mark retained. Formula (9) is not claimed to match zero entries on forbidden generic lattice slices: it is MEROMORPHIC there, and its poles are part of any subsequent cycle transport.

In particular Fhat_P(r) has a pole at each negative integer r=-k, k>=1. When 2a+k>=N, its complementary factor has a root zero at P=N, and the root pole may cancel. This root cancellation does not remove the generic divisor or its normal residue. The construction does not differentiate a reflection with d=P-1.

4. The exact root character

Put beta_a=s_(2a)/s_a^2 and

V_a(h)=product_(j=a+1)^m 2sin(pi(h+j)/N),
z1=q0^(2u-sigma), z2=q0^(sigma-2ell),
c=cos(pi h/N).

The previous entire ROOT kernel is

K_ent=beta_a^2/N * V_a(h) R_a(z1;c)R_a(z2;c).

The specified generic lift has the exact meromorphic root identity

mathscr K_a(q0;t,u,v)=exp(-2pi i v) K_ent.             (10)

Here is a phase certificate. The exact root reflection of (6) gives

mathscr pV(q0;x)=N q0^(-d^2+dx),
mathscr p_(2a)(q0;a-h)
   =N q0^[2a(a-h)-s h]/[F_(2a)(q0)V_a(h)].           (11)

The actual large/small root collision and Gaussian normalization give

H_m^1=P_s(T)q0^(a sigma+a^2) beta_a R_a,
P_s(T)=(-i)^s q0^[s((d-3)/2-h)] V_a(h),
c_a(q0)=N^2 q0^(a^2-2d^2) beta_a,
F_(2a)(q0) F_m(q0)^2
   =(-1)^d q0^[a(2a+1)+m(m+1)] N^2 beta_a.           (12)

Insert (11),(12) in (9). The nonconstant surviving exponent is q0^(-2Nv)=exp(-2pi i v). The remaining integer sign is (-1)^s q02=1. This proves (10) and fixes its character; matching the physical grid alone would not have fixed it.

In the original circle coordinates R=sigma-u, S=ell, the normalized entire circle coefficient in the regulator normalization satisfies

C_a(sigma;R,S)
   =k_a N q0^[d(ell-u)] exp(2pi i v)
                                  mathscr K_a(q0;t,u,v). (13)

Thus the character of this specified generic lift cancels the character from restoring the actual physical turn. The absolute circle normalization is the one already fixed in that theorem. The bare canceled-root K_ent and the root of (9) are different off-grid functions, although they agree on the original lattice.

5. Full local generic-normal upper bound

Take a fixed compact neighborhood on which BOTH numerator arguments in (9) belong to the joint deficient corridor, with all marked scaled coordinates bounded. Require also

|Im(sigma/N)|>=b>0,
0<Re(v/N)<1, 0<Re(ell/N)<1                         (14)

with fixed margins. At a sufficiently small nonreal perturbation of the actual deficient geometric frame these conditions hold. The two channel-cup arguments r/N and (2a-r)/N then stay in fixed compact sets with imaginary part bounded away from zero. The physical-cup arguments remain in their canonical interior strips.

This perturbation exists in the actual common frame, not just in formal source coordinates. In its disk chart the proved real frame has z-w=-1+i d0 and prefix trace A2=-1-2/(z-w), with d0=1/sqrt(3). Shift the real part of its global flag w0 by a small real e; the actual white reflection then changes z-w to -1-e+i d0. At e=0,

partial_e Log A2=-2/(1+d0^2),
partial_e Sigma=i/[pi(1+d0^2)] !=0.

Thus a sufficiently small fixed nonzero e makes the normalized degree nonreal. All the previous strict exterior, regular-chart, ordinary-complement and numerator-corridor conditions persist by openness. After choosing this frame, take its sufficiently small compact neighborhood so that the lower bound in (14) is fixed.

For s=d-2a satisfying 0<=s<=eta N, with eta sufficiently small, the root lift is nonzero on this compact and every fixed k>=0 obeys

|partial_epsilon^k mathscr E_a|_0
   <=C_k N^(10k)(C N^20)^(2s) |mathscr E_a(q0)|.      (15)

The same estimate holds for mathscr K_a. In particular it has subexponential relative cost when s log N=o(N), and polynomial relative cost for bounded s, including both maximal folded parities. It makes no claim at a fixed nonzero scaled separation s/N.

To prove (15), use the actual LARGE-numerator estimates of the boundary/source theorem separately for the two specified whites. On |epsilon|<=cN^(-10), each has ratio at most (C N20)s to its nonzero root value. The moving-period lemma bounds each cup and its inverse relative to its root value on this disk. For the negative and above-N channel arguments it uses a fixed O(N) integer shift into the canonical strip, whose sine factors avoid zero because of (14). The fixed original factorials F_d,F_(2a) and the scalar (2), D_a and their inverses have the usual harmonic root-unit bound. Their relative variations are bounded on this same disk. Every remaining monomial has exponent O(N^2) and hence bounded relative variation there. Thus (9) is holomorphic in epsilon throughout the disk and has the stated ratio bound on its boundary. Cauchy’s formula gives (15) for the LITERAL generic operator germ.

The nonreal-degree requirement in (14) is substantive. A claim covering a real negative-integer channel row would cross the divisors described in section 3 and is not a consequence of this theorem. Likewise, (15) is an upper bound, not a relative asymptotic formula with a nonzero leading normal coefficient. The original global cycle, its crossed residues, complementary-source estimates and its selected stationary coefficient remain necessary for a volume theorem.
