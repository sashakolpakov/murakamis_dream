Finite charge polarizations retaining the original Hermite trace

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The canonical positive physical angles define a square root of the full-Hermite squared-angle class. Its two algebra exponentials split the original cosine coupon exactly. Each polarized family has the same signed heat evolution and recovers the original source at unit charge. Its charge reflection exchanges the two families instead of forcing either family to have zero first derivative there.

This is a finite-source construction. A polarized initializer is not inferred from the already proved cosine initializer. The earlier one-sided angular representative changes representatives modulo the angular Hermite ideal; the polarization here instead changes the charge coupon in the original spectral algebra. These are different operations.

1. The positive-angle class and its exponentials

Use the original data of the entire charged-source heat theorem: D=N−1,η=(−1)D,N≥2,𝒜N=ℂ[z]/(PN(z)),PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2, D=N-1,\qquad \eta=(-1)^D,\qquad N\ge2,\qquad \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[z]/(P_N(z)),\qquad P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2, ϕN(f)=ηN2[zD]rem⁡PNf.(1) \phi_N(f)=\eta N^2[z^D]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}f. \qquad\text{(1)} The distinct double nodes have positive angular lifts ca=−2cos⁡τa,τa=π(D−2a)N>0,0≤a<⌊N/2⌋.(2) c_a=-2\cos\tau_a, \qquad \tau_a=\frac{\pi(D-2a)}N>0, \qquad 0\le a<\lfloor N/2\rfloor. \qquad\text{(2)} At odd levels the remaining node −2-2 is simple. Define the unique class LN∈𝒜NL_N\in\mathcal A_N by LN(ca)=τa,LN′(ca)=12sin⁡τa,LN(−2)=0(N odd).(3) L_N(c_a)=\tau_a,\qquad L_N'(c_a)=\frac1{2\sin\tau_a},\qquad L_N(-2)=0\quad(N\text{ odd}). \qquad\text{(3)} This is a choice of full finite jets, not an assertion that a single inverse cosine is holomorphic across −2-2. If 𝒯N\mathscr T_N is the squared-angle class in the heat theorem, then LN2=𝒯N.(4) L_N^2=\mathscr T_N. \qquad\text{(4)} Indeed its double-node values are τa2\tau_a^2, its derivatives are τa/sin⁡τa\tau_a/\sin\tau_a, and its odd simple value is zero. These are all the defining data of 𝒯N\mathscr T_N.

For σ∈{+1,−1}\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}, put QN,σ(χ)=exp⁡𝒜N(σiN(1−χ)LN).(5) Q_{N,\sigma}(\chi) =\exp_{\mathcal A_N}\bigl(\sigma iN(1-\chi)L_N\bigr). \qquad\text{(5)} Its complete data are QN,σ(χ)(ca)=eσiN(1−χ)τa,QN,σ(χ)′(ca)=σiN(1−χ)2sin⁡τaeσiN(1−χ)τa,QN,σ(χ)(−2)=1(N odd).(6) \begin{split} Q_{N,\sigma}(\chi)(c_a) &=e^{\sigma iN(1-\chi)\tau_a},\\ Q_{N,\sigma}(\chi)'(c_a) &=\frac{\sigma iN(1-\chi)}{2\sin\tau_a} e^{\sigma iN(1-\chi)\tau_a}, \end{split} \qquad Q_{N,\sigma}(\chi)(-2)=1\quad(N\text{ odd}). \qquad\text{(6)} Thus all coefficients are entire in χ\chi, and each coupon is an algebra unit at every finite charge. The original entire cosine coupon 𝒞N,χ\mathcal C_{N,\chi} satisfies the exact identities QN,+(χ)+QN,−(χ)2=𝒞N,χ,QN,σ(1)=1,QN,σ(2−χ)=QN,−σ(χ).(7) \frac{Q_{N,+}(\chi)+Q_{N,-}(\chi)}2=\mathcal C_{N,\chi}, \qquad Q_{N,\sigma}(1)=1, \qquad Q_{N,\sigma}(2-\chi)=Q_{N,-\sigma}(\chi). \qquad\text{(7)} The last reflection is a reflection of the charge. Each polynomial QN,σ(−2cos⁡t)Q_{N,\sigma}(-2\cos t) is individually even in tt; angular reflection does not exchange the two fixed spectral classes.

Differentiation in charge gives, in the full algebra, ∂χQN,σ=−σiNLNQN,σ,∂χ2QN,σ=−N2𝒯NQN,σ.(8) \partial_\chi Q_{N,\sigma} =-\sigma iN L_N Q_{N,\sigma},\qquad \partial_\chi^2 Q_{N,\sigma} =-N^2\mathscr T_NQ_{N,\sigma}. \qquad\text{(8)} In particular (8) keeps the first normals, rather than just the semisimple values. At χ=0\chi=0 the values in (6) are η\eta, but the double-node normals are generally nonzero. The polarized zero-charge coupon is therefore not silently identified with η1\eta\,1.

2. Faithful one-sided charge observations and heat transport

Write y=1−χy=1-\chi, λa=Nτa=π(D−2a)\lambda_a=N\tau_a=\pi(D-2a), and let ℰN,σ=span⁡{eσiλay,yeσiλay}a+span⁡{1}(N odd),(9) \mathcal E_{N,\sigma} =\operatorname{span}\{e^{\sigma i\lambda_a y}, y e^{\sigma i\lambda_a y}\}_a +\operatorname{span}\{1\}\quad(N\text{ odd}), \qquad\text{(9)} where the last summand is absent at even levels. Then f↦ϕN(fQN,σ(χ))is an isomorphism𝒜N→ℰN,σ.(10) f\longmapsto\phi_N(fQ_{N,\sigma}(\chi)) \quad\text{is an isomorphism}\quad \mathcal A_N\longrightarrow\mathcal E_{N,\sigma}. \qquad\text{(10)} To see this directly, the original trace formula gives ϕN(fQN,σ)=∑a{Aa(f)+σiBa(f)y}eσiλay+𝟏N oddf(−2),(11) \phi_N(fQ_{N,\sigma}) =\sum_a\{A_a(f)+\sigma i B_a(f)y\} e^{\sigma i\lambda_a y} +\mathbf1_{N\text{ odd}}f(-2), \qquad\text{(11)} Aa(f)=η{4sin⁡2τaf′(ca)−caf(ca)},Ba(f)=2ηNsin⁡τaf(ca).(12) A_a(f)=\eta\{4\sin^2\tau_a f'(c_a)-c_af(c_a)\}, \qquad B_a(f)=2\eta N\sin\tau_a f(c_a). \qquad\text{(12)} The distinct nonzero frequencies and their first polynomial factors are linearly independent, as is the optional constant. The coefficient of yy first recovers f(ca)f(c_a), and the constant at that frequency then recovers f′(ca)f'(c_a). Thus the dimension is exactly NN.

Let FNF_N be any original compiled numerator, with all kernels, spectators and ordinary free boundaries retained. Distinct crossing positions remain in their distinct contracted algebra copies. Define ZN,𝝈(𝒏,𝝌)=ϕN⊗m[FN∏vRnvQN,σv(χv)],Z̃N,𝝈=(∏vCN,nv−1)ZN,𝝈,(13) Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma}(\mathbf n,\boldsymbol\chi) =\phi_N^{\otimes m}\!\left[ F_N\prod_v R_{n_v}Q_{N,\sigma_v}(\chi_v)\right], \qquad \widetilde Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma} =\left(\prod_v C_{N,n_v}^{-1}\right)Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma}, \qquad\text{(13)} CN,n=eπinD2/(4N),Rn=CN,nexp⁡𝒜N(inN𝒯N4π).(14) C_{N,n}=e^{\pi i nD^2/(4N)},\qquad R_n=C_{N,n}\exp_{\mathcal A_N} \left(\frac{inN\mathscr T_N}{4\pi}\right). \qquad\text{(14)} For arbitrary fixed signed real powers, and also as entire identities in the powers, ∂nvZ̃N,𝝈=−i4πN∂χv2Z̃N,𝝈.(15) \partial_{n_v}\widetilde Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma} =-\frac{i}{4\pi N}\partial_{\chi_v}^2 \widetilde Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma}. \qquad\text{(15)} This follows from (8) and (14) inside the original trace. The constant-coefficient equations preserve (9), so their solution is unique in the tensor product of those finite spaces.

For a nonzero real step δ\delta, set vδ=e−isgn⁡(δ)π/4v_\delta=e^{-i\operatorname{sgn}(\delta)\pi/4}. The exact solution in that coordinate is Z̃N,𝝈(𝒏+δ𝒆v,𝝌)=N|δ|∫ℝe−πNr2/|δ|Z̃N,𝝈(𝒏,𝝌+vδr𝒆v)dr.(16) \widetilde Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma} (\mathbf n+\delta\mathbf e_v,\boldsymbol\chi) =\sqrt{\frac N{|\delta|}} \int_{\mathbb R}e^{-\pi Nr^2/|\delta|} \widetilde Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma} (\mathbf n,\boldsymbol\chi+v_\delta r\mathbf e_v)\,dr. \qquad\text{(16)} The Gaussian computation on eλχe^{\lambda\chi} and its λ\lambda-derivative proves (16), including every normal block. The corresponding oriented charge line may be translated by any finite complex amount, exactly as in the original heat theorem. The Gaussian beats the linear exponential type of (9), so all these integrals converge absolutely. Tensorization and ordinary free-boundary derivatives commute with the identities.

For the fixed same-index graph sources covered by the local heat theorem, the same physical-data argument gives |Z̃N,𝝈(𝒏,𝝌)|≤ANp∏v(1+|χv|)exp⁡N(C+π∑v|ℑχv|).(17) |\widetilde Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma}(\mathbf n,\boldsymbol\chi)| \le A N^p\prod_v(1+|\chi_v|) \exp N\left(C+\pi\sum_v|\Im\chi_v|\right). \qquad\text{(17)} Here powers range over a bounded real box and free boundaries over a fixed compact set. Indeed the values in (6) have modulus at most eπD|ℑχ|e^{\pi D|\Im\chi|}; their normals add only a polynomial factor, since the smallest nonzero physical sine is bounded below by a constant times N−1N^{-1}. The original kernel and mixed-normal envelopes are otherwise unchanged. No filled-action estimate is substituted for the constant CC.

Consequently the proved local heat estimate applies to (13) whenever an actual uniform holomorphic nonzero initializer for this polarized source is supplied. Equations (15)–(17) alone do not supply it.

3. Exact endpoint recovery and the Fourier projection

The symmetrization and endpoint recovery are literal source identities: ZN(𝒏,𝝌)=2−m∑𝝈∈{+,−}mZN,𝝈(𝒏,𝝌),ZN,𝝈(𝒏,𝟏)=ZN(𝒏,𝟏).(18) Z_N(\mathbf n,\boldsymbol\chi) =2^{-m}\sum_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{+,-\}^m} Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma}(\mathbf n,\boldsymbol\chi), \qquad Z_{N,\boldsymbol\sigma}(\mathbf n,\mathbf1) =Z_N(\mathbf n,\mathbf1). \qquad\text{(18)} Reflecting one charge exchanges the corresponding signs. If a polarized source has a uniquely specified holomorphic asymptotic branch eNS+(χ)a+(χ)e^{NS_+(\chi)}a_+(\chi), its reflected branch is marked by S−(χ)=S+(2−χ)S_-(\chi)=S_+(2-\chi), a−(χ)=a+(2−χ)a_-(\chi)=a_+(2-\chi). At unit charge their actions and coefficients agree with those marks. The factor 1/21/2 in their average then preserves the coefficient; it does not create cancellation. Integer 2πi2\pi i action shifts give the same exponential at integer levels but must not be inserted without their marks. This assertion presupposes the actual branch expansion; it is not its existence proof.

Unlike the cosine family, an individual family (13) is not forced by charge reflection to have zero first derivative at unit charge. From (8) that derivative is exactly the original trace with insertion −σiNLN-\sigma iN L_N. Thus the elementary single-branch symmetry obstruction is removed, while the prescribed endpoint scalar remains the same.

There is an exact way to obtain (13) from the whole cosine charge function. In one variable let Π+\Pi_+, Π−\Pi_-, Π0\Pi_0 retain respectively its generalized Fourier frequencies +λa+\lambda_a, −λa-\lambda_a, and zero as a function of y=1−χy=1-\chi. Then ϕN(fQN,σ)=(2Πσ+Π0)ϕN(f𝒞N,χ).(19) \phi_N(fQ_{N,\sigma}) =(2\Pi_\sigma+\Pi_0)\phi_N(f\mathcal C_{N,\chi}). \qquad\text{(19)} This follows by expanding the sine and cosine, including their linear-in-yy normal factors. At odd levels the constant must be retained once, not doubled. The analogous operators in distinct charges commute.

Projection (19) is a projection of the whole charge function. It can be encoded on its finite space by a differential polynomial whose order grows with NN, or by a one-sided Fourier projection. It is not a fixed-order differentiation of a local asymptotic expansion. The existing cosine initializer therefore does not automatically give a polarized initializer.

4. The correct angular realization

For one traced variable let A(t)A(t) be an even holomorphic spectator on neighborhoods of all physical angular nodes. Keep the canonical crossing representative and set WN(t)=e−iNt(1−ηe−iNt)2,hn(t)=sin⁡tA(t)einNt2/(4π).(20) W_N(t)=\frac{e^{-iNt}}{(1-\eta e^{-iNt})^2},\qquad h_n(t)=\sin t\,A(t)e^{inNt^2/(4\pi)}. \qquad\text{(20)} Let γ+\gamma_+ be the union of small positively oriented circles around precisely the positive physical angles τa\tau_a. Then ϕN(Rn[A]QN,σ)=−N2CN,nπi∫γ+hn(t)eσiN(1−χ)tWN(t)dt+𝟏N oddCN,nA(0).(21) \phi_N(R_n[A]Q_{N,\sigma}) =-\frac{N^2 C_{N,n}}{\pi i} \int_{\gamma_+}h_n(t)e^{\sigma iN(1-\chi)t}W_N(t)\,dt +\mathbf1_{N\text{ odd}}C_{N,n}A(0). \qquad\text{(21)} Near a physical angle, WN(t)W_N(t) has double principal part −η/[N2(t−τa)2]-\eta/[N^2(t-\tau_a)^2] and no simple-pole term. Its residue in (21) therefore gives twice η\eta times the derivative of hn(t)eσiN(1−χ)th_n(t)e^{\sigma iN(1-\chi)t}, exactly the double-node value and normal in the original trace. The remaining odd simple term is exactly the last term displayed. Equivalently it is obtained by adding one half of a small full angular circle around zero. Its residue uses only the constant term of the exponential; no spurious spectral derivative is prescribed there.

When the spectator is holomorphic on a symmetric lens neighborhood, a literal exponential on the full symmetric closed lens is different. With the original angular double-cover factor 1/21/2, its reflected positive and negative preimages average the two exponential jets. It consequently gives ϕN(Rn[A]𝒞N,χ)\phi_N(R_n[A]\mathcal C_{N,\chi}), not (21). A literal exponential on only a lower open arc defines yet another entire period. Its average with the opposite exponential is the original lower-lens cosine period, and each agrees with that period at χ=1\chi=1, but it retains separate open-arc connector data. Neither identification erases those connectors.

Formula (21) gives the concrete remaining initializer problem: the positive-half-grid cycle, its central connector and its outer connectors must be estimated with the entire original spectator and all normals. A nonzero initializer on a fixed complex charge domain would then activate (16) and the already proved quantitative local heat theorem, with its actual domain and tail conditions. No such new half-grid estimate, arbitrary-filling propagation, or geometric cycle coefficient is asserted here.
