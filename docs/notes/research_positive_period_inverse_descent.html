A compact descent contour from the original primitive periods

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

Suppose a fixed short partial filling has a positive solution in the original circle-tree edge decomposition. Its primitive-period map has a canonical inverse on a real slice. The entire real slice need not be in its image. Nevertheless the original convex angle energy constructs a bounded, star-shaped inverse domain around the solution, with a constant strict action loss on its boundary. This is a theorem about the actual classical source action and its peripheral marking. It does not identify this contour with the original finite quantum cycle.

1. Fixed original data and the compact angle problem

Fix an original ordered circle tree, a nonempty selected set SS of size kk, and every edge incident to SS, counted once. Write mm for the number of included edges. Every selected vertex has positive original degree. Fix the full complex angles at the outside endpoints, the original real signed crossing powers nvn_v, and the primitive unit charges. In a physical integral filling, nv=ηv−2pvn_v=\eta_v-2p_v, with the original carrier ηv\eta_v.

Use the canonical positive edge branches of the global monotonicity theorem. At an endpoint write tv=sv+ihv,rv,e=kv,e+igv,e.(1) t_v=s_v+i h_v,\qquad r_{v,e}=k_{v,e}+i g_{v,e}. \qquad\text{(1)} Before matching imaginary angles, use one hv,eh_{v,e} per incidence. For an oriented edge e=(v,w)e=(v,w), its two positive square factors have coordinates AP=sv/4−gw,e/2,AQ=sv/4+gw,e/2,yP=gv,e/2+sw/4,yQ=gv,e/2−sw/4.(2) \begin{aligned} A_P&=s_v/4-g_{w,e}/2,& A_Q&=s_v/4+g_{w,e}/2,\\ y_P&=g_{v,e}/2+s_w/4,& y_Q&=g_{v,e}/2-s_w/4. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(2)} Each coordinate lies in (−π/4,π/4)(-\pi/4,\pi/4). Equivalently, |sv|+2|gw,e|<π,|sw|+2|gv,e|<π.(3) |s_v|+2|g_{w,e}|<\pi,\qquad |s_w|+2|g_{v,e}|<\pi. \qquad\text{(3)} The outside sb,hbs_b,h_b are prescribed. The selected constraints are ∑e∋vgv,e−nv2sv=−π(v∈S).(4) \sum_{e\ni v}g_{v,e}-\frac{n_v}{2}s_v=-\pi \qquad(v\in S). \qquad\text{(4)} Let 𝒫\mathcal P be this open feasible polytope in the selected svs_v and all incidence gv,eg_{v,e}, and let 𝒫¯\overline{\mathcal P} be its closure. Boundaries and interiors below are relative to its affine constraint space. We assume that the original primitive equations have a positive solution t0t^0; in particular 𝒫\mathcal P is nonempty.

There is no zero-dimensional case under these hypotheses. The kk constraints (4) are independent, since each contains incidence variables belonging to its own selected vertex. The affine space has dimension (k+2m)−k=2m>0(k+2m)-k=2m>0. Its projection to the selected ss coordinates is surjective: any infinitesimal change of svs_v can be offset in one incidence variable at that vertex. Strict feasibility therefore gives the full local kk-dimensional period problem. The empty selected set is excluded throughout.

For one square let x(A,y)=12arsinh⁡(tan⁡(2A)cos⁡(2y)),B(A,y)=12arsinh⁡(tan⁡(2y)cos⁡(2A)),dE▫=xdA+Bdy,E▫(0,0)=0.(5) \begin{aligned} x(A,y)&=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(\tan(2A)\cos(2y)),\\ B(A,y)&=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(\tan(2y)\cos(2A)),\\ dE_\square&=x\,dA+B\,dy,\qquad E_\square(0,0)=0. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(5)} These are real single-valued functions on the open square. The existing variational theorem proves that E▫E_\square is strictly convex, extends continuously to its closed square, and has the edge identity 4dEe=kv,edsv+kw,edsw+hv,edgv,e+hw,edgw,e,Ee=E▫,P+E▫,Q.(6) 4\,dE_e=k_{v,e}\,ds_v+k_{w,e}\,ds_w +h_{v,e}\,dg_{v,e}+h_{w,e}\,dg_{w,e}, \qquad E_e=E_{\square,P}+E_{\square,Q}. \qquad\text{(6)} Define the original energy, with the outside imaginary angles retained, ℰ=4∑eEe−∑b∉Shb∑e∋bgb,e.(7) \mathcal E=4\sum_eE_e- \sum_{b\notin S}h_b\sum_{e\ni b}g_{b,e}. \qquad\text{(7)} It is continuous on the compact polytope, strictly convex in its interior. Let z0z_0 denote the angle assignment of t0t^0. The original variational theorem says that z0z_0 is its unique global minimum, including against boundary assignments.

2. The exact real-period image and its boundary completion

For ξ∈ℝk\xi\in\mathbb R^k, introduce only a linear tilt: ℰξ(z)=ℰ(z)−∑v∈Sξvsv(z),zξ=argmin𝒫¯ℰξ.(8) \mathcal E_\xi(z)=\mathcal E(z)-\sum_{v\in S}\xi_v s_v(z), \qquad z_\xi=\operatorname*{argmin}_{\overline{\mathcal P}} \mathcal E_\xi. \qquad\text{(8)} The minimizer is unique for every ξ\xi. Here the existing boundary proof applies unchanged: a square on an open side has inward energy derivative −∞-\infty, while an interior square or a corner has a finite derivative along a line to a strictly feasible assignment. Adding the linear term in (8) cannot remove that contradiction. Thus every square factor of a boundary minimizer is either interior or a corner. Uniqueness follows from strict convexity along a segment between distinct minimizers; if the only differing factors join adjacent corners, their midpoint would instead be an excluded open-side minimizer. Existence follows from compactness.

The map ξ↦zξ\xi\mapsto z_\xi is continuous on all of ℝk\mathbb R^k. Indeed, for ξj→ξ\xi_j\to\xi, any convergent subsequence of the compact minimizers minimizes ℰξ\mathcal E_\xi by passage to the minimum inequality. Uniqueness identifies its limit with zξz_\xi, proving continuity of the whole sequence.

Define the actual period map and its real image by Pv(t)=∑e∋vrv,e(t)−inv2tv,J=∂P∂t,Ξ={ξ∈ℝk:zξ∈𝒫}.(9) P_v(t)=\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}(t)-\frac{i n_v}{2}t_v, \qquad J=\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}, \qquad \Xi=\{\xi\in\mathbb R^k:z_\xi\in\mathcal P\}. \qquad\text{(9)}

Proposition 1. The set Ξ\Xi is open and contains zero. It is exactly the set of ξ\xi for which the original positive domain, with its fixed outside angles, contains a solution of P(t)=ξ−πi𝟏.(10) P(t)=\xi-\pi i\mathbf1. \qquad\text{(10)} That solution t(ξ)t(\xi) is unique and real analytic. At any finite boundary point ξ*∈∂Ξ\xi_*\in\partial\Xi, the completed assignment zξ*z_{\xi_*} has at least one corner factor and no open-side factor. Every approach from Ξ\Xi converges to that same angle assignment. The reconstructed complex angles can diverge at a corner; their boundedness is not asserted.

Proof. Attach multipliers hvh_v to (4). Stationarity of (8) in the incidence variables gives hv,e=hvh_{v,e}=h_v at selected vertices and hb,e=hbh_{b,e}=h_b at outside vertices. Stationarity in svs_v gives ∑e∋vkv,e+nvhv/2=ξv.(11) \sum_{e\ni v}k_{v,e}+n_vh_v/2=\xi_v. \qquad\text{(11)} Together with (4), these are exactly (10). Conversely a positive solution gives an interior stationary point and hence the unique global minimizer of (8).

The original edge monotonicity gives Re⁡J>0\operatorname{Re}J>0, with JJ complex symmetric. It is therefore invertible. The implicit-function theorem gives openness and real analyticity; global uniqueness also follows directly from strict monotonicity. If a finite boundary point had an interior minimizer, this same theorem would put a neighborhood in Ξ\Xi. It must therefore have a corner, by the preceding boundary exclusion. Continuity of the unique closed minimizer proves the asserted limit. ▫\square

Thus (8) gives an exact global description of the image slice by a fixed compact convex problem. It does not assert that Ξ\Xi is all of ℝk\mathbb R^k, convex, or star shaped. Finite image boundaries are possible corner degenerations of the original cells.

3. The calibrated action equals a constant minus the angle energy

Let M∞=ψ(0,0)M_\infty=\psi(0,0), with the original edge normalization, and retain the primitive-charge action F(t)=∑e=vwψ(tv,tw)+i4π∑v∈Snvtv2−i∑v∈Stv.(12) F(t)=\sum_{e=vw}\psi(t_v,t_w) +\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{v\in S}n_vt_v^2 -i\sum_{v\in S}t_v. \qquad\text{(12)} The outside arguments are the fixed original boundary angles. As in the original trace calculation, Ft=−P+πi𝟏π.(13) F_t=-\frac{P+\pi i\mathbf1}{\pi}. \qquad\text{(13)}

Proposition 2. At every point of Ξ\Xi, with the canonical positive logarithms, Re⁡F(t(ξ))=mM∞−ℰ(zξ)π.(14) \boxed{\quad \operatorname{Re}F(t(\xi)) =mM_\infty-\frac{\mathcal E(z_\xi)}\pi. \quad} \qquad\text{(14)} The constant is the same on all components of Ξ\Xi.

Proof. On one positive edge, ψtv=−rv,e/π\psi_{t_v}=-r_{v,e}/\pi. Hence d(πRe⁡ψ)=∑j=v,w(−kj,edsj+gj,edhj). d(\pi\operatorname{Re}\psi) =\sum_{j=v,w}(-k_{j,e}\,ds_j+g_{j,e}\,dh_j). Combining this with (6) gives the exact identity πRe⁡ψ+4Ee−gv,ehv−gw,ehw=πM∞.(15) \pi\operatorname{Re}\psi+4E_e -g_{v,e}h_v-g_{w,e}h_w=\pi M_\infty. \qquad\text{(15)} Its differential vanishes. The positive edge domain is the connected two-disk domain of the original coordinate theorem, and its complete value fixes the displayed constant. Thus this is a global identity on that positive domain, not a choice of a constant on an inverse period component.

At an interior minimizer, all imaginary angles match. Sum (15) and subtract the outside term in (7). Equation (4) gives ℰ=πmM∞−πRe⁡∑eψ+∑v∈Shv(nvsv/2−π). \mathcal E=\pi mM_\infty-\pi\operatorname{Re}\sum_e\psi +\sum_{v\in S}h_v(n_vs_v/2-\pi). Since Re⁡(invtv2/(4π))=−nvsvhv/(2π)\operatorname{Re}(i n_vt_v^2/(4\pi))=-n_vs_vh_v/(2\pi) and Re⁡(−itv)=hv\operatorname{Re}(-it_v)=h_v, this is exactly (14). ▫\square

In particular t0t^0 is the unique global maximum of Re⁡F\operatorname{Re}F on the entire inverse-period slice, not only on its component containing t0t^0.

For the differential form of the result, define the finite convex conjugate 𝒞(ξ)=maxz∈𝒫¯{ξ⋅s(z)−ℰ(z)}.(16) \mathcal C(\xi)=\max_{z\in\overline{\mathcal P}} \{\xi\cdot s(z)-\mathcal E(z)\}. \qquad\text{(16)} Uniqueness and continuity of the maximizer imply 𝒞∈C1(ℝk)\mathcal C\in C^1(\mathbb R^k), with ∇𝒞=s(zξ)\nabla\mathcal C=s(z_\xi). This follows directly by sandwiching its difference quotient between the two maximizing linear forms. On Ξ\Xi it is real analytic and ∇2𝒞=∂s∂ξ=Re⁡(J−1)>0,Re⁡(J−1)=J−*(Re⁡J)J−1.(17) \nabla^2\mathcal C=\frac{\partial s}{\partial\xi} =\operatorname{Re}(J^{-1})>0, \qquad \operatorname{Re}(J^{-1}) =J^{-*}(\operatorname{Re}J)J^{-1}. \qquad\text{(17)} Here the real part is the Hermitian part, equal to the entrywise real part for these symmetric matrices. Along a ray inside Ξ\Xi, ddrℰ(zrω)=rω𝖳Re⁡(J−1)ω>0(r>0,|ω|=1),(18) \frac{d}{dr}\mathcal E(z_{r\omega}) =r\,\omega^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Re}(J^{-1})\omega>0 \qquad(r>0,\ |\omega|=1), \qquad\text{(18)} and the action derivative is minus this expression divided by π\pi. Equivalently its exact loss is Re⁡F(t0)−Re⁡F(t(ξ))=𝒞(0)−𝒞(ξ)+ξ⋅∇𝒞(ξ)π.(19) \operatorname{Re}F(t^0)-\operatorname{Re}F(t(\xi)) =\frac{\mathcal C(0)-\mathcal C(\xi) +\xi\cdot\nabla\mathcal C(\xi)}\pi. \qquad\text{(19)}

4. A bounded star-shaped domain with a strict boundary gap

The compact angle problem supplies a positive number Δ=minz∈∂𝒫ℰ(z)−ℰ(z0)>0.(20) \Delta=\min_{z\in\partial\mathcal P}\mathcal E(z) -\mathcal E(z_0)>0. \qquad\text{(20)} Strict positivity follows from continuity and the unique interior minimum. This is a boundary-energy quantity in the specified original polytope; no computable lower bound is assumed.

Theorem 3. For every 0<δ<Δ0<\delta<\Delta, the set Ωδ={ξ∈ℝk:ℰ(zξ)<ℰ(z0)+δ}(21) \Omega_\delta= \{\xi\in\mathbb R^k: \mathcal E(z_\xi)<\mathcal E(z_0)+\delta\} \qquad\text{(21)} is a bounded open strictly star-shaped domain with smooth boundary. Its closure lies in Ξ\Xi. Every ray has a unique boundary radius ρδ(ω)>0\rho_\delta(\omega)>0, depending smoothly on its direction. Its inverse image Γδ=t(Ωδ)(22) \Gamma_\delta=t(\Omega_\delta) \qquad\text{(22)} is an embedded real kk-dimensional contour with compact closure in the original positive domain. It has its unique action maximum at t0t^0, and Re⁡F|∂Γδ=Re⁡F(t0)−δ/π.(23) \operatorname{Re}F\big|_{\partial\Gamma_\delta} =\operatorname{Re}F(t^0)-\delta/\pi. \qquad\text{(23)}

Proof. The closed angle sublevel Kδ={z:ℰ(z)≤ℰ(z0)+δ}K_\delta=\{z:\mathcal E(z)\le\mathcal E(z_0)+\delta\} is compact and contained in 𝒫\mathcal P. Thus every minimizer occurring in (21), or at equality, is interior and reconstructs a regular matched positive point.

The inverse-period parameters of those minimizers are bounded. To see this without assuming properness of the whole period map, choose one incidence at each selected vertex and reconstruct its imaginary angle using (5) and the original square formulas. These are smooth on the compact set KδK_\delta. At every interior minimizer all incidences give the same angle. Formula (11) then expresses its ξ\xi as bounded smooth functions of that compact angle data. Consequently the set defined by the weak inequality in (21) is bounded and closed, hence compact and contained in Ξ\Xi.

There is also global monotonicity along each parameter ray, including after a possible exit from Ξ\Xi. For 0≤r1<r20\le r_1<r_2, the two minimum inequalities from (8) imply ω⋅(s2−s1)≥0,r1ω⋅(s2−s1)≤ℰ(zr2ω)−ℰ(zr1ω)≤r2ω⋅(s2−s1).(24) \omega\cdot(s_2-s_1)\ge0,\qquad r_1\omega\cdot(s_2-s_1) \le\mathcal E(z_{r_2\omega})-\mathcal E(z_{r_1\omega}) \le r_2\omega\cdot(s_2-s_1). \qquad\text{(24)} Thus the energy cannot decrease along the ray. Before the level in (21) is reached, the minimizer is interior and (18) makes it strictly increasing for r>0r>0. The ray must reach that level at a finite radius: otherwise its parameters would lie in the bounded weak sublevel just proved. Continuity and strict increase give exactly one first radius. There is no later equality interval: the first level point lies in the open set Ξ\Xi, so (18) gives an immediate strict increase there, followed by the nondecrease in (24).

At that radius the radial derivative in (18) is positive. The implicit function theorem therefore makes ρδ\rho_\delta smooth; for k=1k=1 this means the two positive endpoint radii. This proves all assertions about Ωδ\Omega_\delta. Injectivity and nonsingularity of the period map prove the embedding in (22). Compactness of its closure follows from the same reconstruction on KδK_\delta. Finally (14) gives (23) and the unique maximum. ▫\square

At the center the real phase curvature in ξ\xi coordinates is −Re⁡(J(t0)−1)/π-\operatorname{Re}(J(t^0)^{-1})/\pi, which is negative definite. On each compact annulus of this contour the action has a positive gap. If the original core signs satisfy Im⁡tv0<0\operatorname{Im}t_v^0<0, then sufficiently small δ\delta makes the entire closed contour lie in the lower poly-half-plane: compact angle sublevels shrink to their unique minimizer as δ↓0\delta\downarrow0.

All these finite-contour assertions persist for sufficiently small complex changes of the prescribed outside angles. On a compact closed contour, the holomorphic inverse-function theorem applies uniformly to (10); the continued contour and critical point retain, for example, half of the strict boundary gap in (23). The statement uses the same continued primitive logarithms, not newly selected endpoint branches.

5. What this does not prove about the original finite source

Finite boundary points of the full image Ξ\Xi are accounted for by its unique corner minimizers. If |ξ|→∞|\xi|\to\infty, a sequence of interior minimizers cannot remain in a compact subset of 𝒫\mathcal P, by (11). Neither fact asserts that the full inverse slice is a proper integration cycle with integrable ends. Indeed ℰ\mathcal E is bounded on the closed polytope, so (14) by itself does not make the action tend to −∞-\infty along an infinite end.

Theorem 3 avoids this issue by constructing a finite compact collar. For the original finite source, one must still match its boundary to the actual trace endpoints, control the connecting faces and vertical currents, and justify the complete edge-kernel asymptotics on the deformation. Its being a descent contour for the calibrated classical phase does not supply those facts or its relative homology coefficient. The original lattice modes and peripheral scalar factors are unchanged.

This limitation is substantive even inside positive edge charts. The actual full-wire obstruction proves that on positive all-three path fillings the full individual wire kernel can have a strictly larger rate than its geometric packet. Its dominant endpoint products can then vanish after the original adjacent Hermite traces. Thus neither an edgewise smaller remainder nor a competing rate for the whole contraction follows from the positive classical contour; the full source must be treated coherently.

The local coefficient theorem supplies a nonzero primitive Gaussian coefficient when its regular chart conditions hold. Together, the two results give a local block and a compact classical contour with a strict boundary action gap. They still do not identify the entire selected scalar with that block, exclude cancellation from the remaining source, or cover a short hyperbolic filling outside the original positive decomposition.
