The literal closing cap with a prescribed observation

Private written proof, 17 November 2026. The unmarked closing cap agrees with the original fixed cut on the scalar open knot operator. After a nonintertwining insertion the open operator need not be scalar. We give a marked cap which retains any specified linear observation of that operator. Its boundary entries and norm are explicit. No scalarity or cancellation is used for the inserted operator.

1. The already evaluated open source and cap

Fix odd N>2N>2, q=e2πi/Nq=e^{2\pi i/N}, and the constructed braid source of [Q]. Write its two free physical indices as i,ji,j. Its boundary expansion, with both end-edge phases omitted from the physical array, has the form BG=Kopen∑i,j=0N−1G(i,j)𝒜a(i)𝒜b(j),Kopen=N2c−1∏v=1cCvqϕv.(1) B_G=K_{\mathrm{ open}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1} G(i,j)\mathcal A_a(i)\mathcal A_b(j), \qquad K_{\mathrm{ open}}=N^{2c-1}\prod_{v=1}^c C_vq^{\phi_v}. \qquad\text{(1)} For the uninserted source G=OχG=O_\chi. Formula (1) also defines its replacement by an arbitrary physical array GG; the resulting paired boundary tensor is unambiguous because the adapters have their specified inverses. Any actual insertion whose boundary array has been computed can be used for GG. This does not assert that two different insertion conventions give the same array.

The actual two-tetrahedron cap of [Q, Section 5], with its neutral phase choice, has physical row ℓcap(i,j)=N−2qi.(2) \ell_{\mathrm{ cap}}(i,j)=N^{-2}q^i. \qquad\text{(2)} It includes its four newly interior vertices and all its edge weights. Consequently its unmarked closing is, for arbitrary GG, Zcap(G)=N−2Kopen∑i,jqiG(i,j).(3) Z_{\mathrm{ cap}}(G)=N^{-2}K_{\mathrm{ open}}\sum_{i,j}q^iG(i,j). \qquad\text{(3)} The passage from (3) to a fixed-zero observation in [Q] uses the particular identity Oχ(i,j)=q−iδijOχ(0,0)O_\chi(i,j)=q^{-i}\delta_{ij}O_\chi(0,0). We do not apply that identity to a modified array GG.

Put ΔN=NKopen=∏v=1cN2Cvqϕv.(4) \Delta_N=NK_{\mathrm{ open}} =\prod_{v=1}^c N^2C_vq^{\phi_v}. \qquad\text{(4)} On the injective real decoration and compatible branches of [Q], |ΔN|=1|\Delta_N|=1. These are the original retained crossing factors.

2. An exact cap for any covector

Specify a complex array ν=(νij)\nu=(\nu_{ij}) and its bilinear observation ν(G)=∑i,jνijG(i,j).(5) \nu(G)=\sum_{i,j}\nu_{ij}G(i,j). \qquad\text{(5)} There is no complex conjugation in (5). On the two physical end spaces insert the diagonal operator Dν=∑i,jq−iνij|i,j⟩⟨i,j|.(6) D_\nu=\sum_{i,j}q^{-i}\nu_{ij} |i,j\rangle\langle i,j|. \qquad\text{(6)} This is an explicit boundary coupon; it need not be a Hopf morphism, a positive operator, or a product of two one-end operators. Multiplying the actual row (2) by (6) gives N−2νN^{-2}\nu. Thus the marked closed source ZνZ_\nu, with the same cap geometry, charges and vertex measures, satisfies for every array GG Zν(G)=N−2Kopenν(G),N3Zν(G)=ΔNν(G).(7) \boxed{ Z_\nu(G)=N^{-2}K_{\mathrm{ open}}\nu(G),\qquad N^3Z_\nu(G)=\Delta_N\,\nu(G). } \qquad\text{(7)} This is an identity of linear observations. It does not divide by ν(G)\nu(G), which may vanish. On the real branches in (4), |N3Zν(G)|=|ν(G)|.(8) |N^3Z_\nu(G)|=|\nu(G)|. \qquad\text{(8)} The power N3N^3 is the normalization for this marked comparison; it does not replace the unmarked closed normalization N2N^2.

For the fixed-zero planar cut take νij=δi0δj0\nu_{ij}=\delta_{i0}\delta_{j0}. Then Dν=E00⊗E00.(9) D_\nu=E_{00}\otimes E_{00}. \qquad\text{(9)} Both free indices are fixed, exactly as in the planar definition. For the uninserted scalar array only, N3Zδ00(Oχ)=N2Zcap(Oχ).(10) N^3Z_{\delta_{00}}(O_\chi) =N^2Z_{\mathrm{ cap}}(O_\chi). \qquad\text{(10)} For a general inserted array their difference is explicitly N2Zcap(G)−N3Zδ00(G)=ΔN{N−1∑i,jqiG(i,j)−G(0,0)}.(11) N^2Z_{\mathrm{ cap}}(G)-N^3Z_{\delta_{00}}(G) =\Delta_N\left\{N^{-1}\sum_{i,j}q^iG(i,j)-G(0,0)\right\}. \qquad\text{(11)} Equation (11) is the closing discrepancy that an argument using unmarked scalarity would otherwise lose.

An averaged observation from a different representation must be supplied as its actual covector ν\nu in these end coordinates. Equality with a fixed-zero observation on scalar operators alone does not identify their values on nonintertwining insertions. Formula (6) retains either specified covector without making such an identification.

3. The actual face-pair insertion and its bounds

Use the boundary adapters and inverse duals of [A], unchanged. For m=a,bm=a,b, write km=αm,−−αm,++hm,lm=βm,−−βm,++hm,fm(k)=am[k−1]bm[−k]. k_m=\alpha_{m,-}-\alpha_{m,+}+h_m,\quad l_m=\beta_{m,-}-\beta_{m,+}+h_m,\quad f_m(k)=a_m^{[k-1]}b_m^{[-k]} . Their entries are 𝒜m(α;i)=fm(km)qikm,ℒm(i;β)=q−ilmN2fm(lm).(12) \mathcal A_m(\alpha;i)=f_m(k_m)q^{ik_m},\qquad \mathcal L_m(i;\beta)=\frac{q^{-il_m}}{N^2f_m(l_m)}. \qquad\text{(12)} Here residues lie in 0,…,N−10,\ldots,N-1, and am,bma_m,b_m are the original nonzero pole-edge roots. The face realization of (6) is 𝒟ν=(𝒜a⊗𝒜b)Dν(ℒa⊗ℒb). \mathcal D_\nu=(\mathcal A_a\otimes\mathcal A_b) D_\nu(\mathcal L_a\otimes\mathcal L_b). In all four incoming and four outgoing face indices it is exactly (𝒟ν)α;β=fa(ka)fb(kb)N4fa(la)fb(lb)∑i,jνijqi(ka−la−1)+j(kb−lb).(13) \boxed{ (\mathcal D_\nu)_{\alpha;\beta} =\frac{f_a(k_a)f_b(k_b)} {N^4f_a(l_a)f_b(l_b)} \sum_{i,j}\nu_{ij} q^{i(k_a-l_a-1)+j(k_b-l_b)} . } \qquad\text{(13)} It acts on the two paired invariant images and kills their specified complementary kernels. The identities ℒm𝒜m=I\mathcal L_m\mathcal A_m=I show that inserting (13) into (1) has exactly the effect of (6). Every cyclic seam and inverse fm(lm)−1f_m(l_m)^{-1} remains; no Hermitian adjoint is substituted. For (9), formula (13) is the product of the two literal fixed-cut face projectors in [A].

In the standard Euclidean physical norm, ∥Dν∥2=maxi,j|νij|.(14) \|D_\nu\|_2=\max_{i,j}|\nu_{ij}| . \qquad\text{(14)} Let κm=max⁡k|fm(k)|/min⁡k|fm(k)|\kappa_m=\max_k|f_m(k)|/\min_k|f_m(k)|. The exact singular values in [A] and cancellation of the two N2N^2 normalization factors give ∥ν∥∞κaκb≤∥𝒟ν∥2≤κaκb∥ν∥∞.(15) \frac{\|\nu\|_\infty}{\kappa_a\kappa_b} \le \|\mathcal D_\nu\|_2 \le \kappa_a\kappa_b\|\nu\|_\infty. \qquad\text{(15)} The norm includes the full face-pair space; the operator is zero on the stated complementary kernel. To obtain the lower inequality, apply the inverse coordinate map on the image to 𝒟ν\mathcal D_\nu. On fixed compact sets of nonzero decoration differences the two κm\kappa_m are bounded independently of NN. Thus the cap transport itself adds no exponential norm loss to a supplied covector. It does not bound the covector if its own coefficients grow exponentially.

All formulas are exact at fixed odd level and preserve any further linear combinations of inserted arrays. Regular decoration derivatives retain the coupons, inverse rows, and scalar ΔN\Delta_N; they do not introduce an off-root level or physical meridian family. This supplies the full marked closing observation once the open array and its covector are specified. It does not identify a negative-ray current with a particular insertion, transport an analytic integration cycle, or prove a geometric lower bound.
