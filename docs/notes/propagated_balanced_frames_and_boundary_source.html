Regular propagated Fourier frames move every wire adapter to the boundary

28 October 2026. The exact balanced four-Psi crossing permits a useful GLOBAL choice of polarization. For an arbitrary open signed braid, all local Fermat blocks can remain uniformly regular while every INTERIOR Fourier adapter is exactly the identity. Only the original boundary closure/cap co-source needs a change of frame. The number of remaining wire adapters is therefore at most the braid width, independently of the crossing count.

This is an exact theorem for the original operator and source, not a selected-geometric-cycle theorem. It moves the explicit WIRE adapters to the boundary. The bulk full-Psi logarithmic seams during an off-grid contour deformation are a distinct issue and are not removed here.

1. A regular propagated multisection at arbitrary width

At complete color put x=-exp(-pi*i/N), s=xN=(-1)(N+1). For b heads choose pairwise distinct nonzero constants c1,…,cb and initial central coordinates

sigma_i(0)=s^(i-1)c_i.                            (1)

Choose one marked N-th root r_i(0) of each coordinate. At a positive crossing on heads i,i+1 propagate the actual roots by

(r_i,r_(i+1)) -> (x r_(i+1),x r_i).              (2)

At a negative crossing use the actual antiunitary mirror, hence the root propagation has x^(-1) in place of x. Untouched roots stay fixed. Their central propagation is in BOTH cases

(A,B) -> (sB,sA),                                (3)

because s^(-1)=s. All root phases and label permutations are retained.

Let D=diag(1,s,…,s^(b-1)), and let P_j be the ordinary permutation of strands after any prefix of the braid. Then the entire vector is

sigma(j)=D P_j c.                                (4)

Indeed the signed adjacent interchange (3) is D P_i D^(-1). At the next crossing the regularity condition in the exact local theorem is B!=sA. Equation (4) gives

B-sA=s^i(c_(pi(i+1))-c_(pi(i))) !=0.             (5)

The permutation never assigns the same initial strand to both heads. Thus EVERY local Fermat chart is regular, for every signed word and every prefix, without any knot census or restriction to twist knots.

For example c_i=i+1 gives an explicit choice for all words of width b. All central coordinates then have modulus between 2 and b+1. The local central shape ratios are ratios of two distinct c_i, so their distance from one is at least 1/(b+1). The internal norm T=BD/(D-B) and all other central arguments consequently stay in a fixed compact regular set depending only on b. No parameter depends on the unknown knot value.

2. All interior adapters telescope EXACTLY

Let F_j be the tensor product of the normalized one-head Fourier frames F(r_i(j)) after prefix j. For the next original crossing R_j, the exact local theorem gives its regular four-Psi kernel

M_j=F_j R_j F_(j-1)^(-1).                        (6)

Untouched tensor factors in (6) are identities. This is true for either sign, with the FULL original scalar and marked full-Psi functions. The outgoing frame on every physical wire is by construction precisely its next incoming frame. Hence

M_m ... M_1=F_m W F_0^(-1),
W=F_m^(-1)(M_m ... M_1)F_0,                      (7)

for the actual word W=R_m…R_1. There is no interior rational adapter, no deformation through a Cauchy pole, and no discarded current in this identity. The positive-degree intermediate allowance in the local construction has already been removed by its exact E-coproduct proof.

For an original endpoint vector p and co-source row m, the whole source is therefore

m W p=(m F_m^(-1))(M_m ... M_1)(F_0 p).           (8)

This includes all original pointed-plat cups, ordinary caps and the FULL marked row. For a trace presentation with source operator C, it is

Tr(C W)=Tr[(F_0 C F_m^(-1))(M_m ... M_1)].        (9)

Use the actual averaged first-cut matrix and original closed pivots in C; no scalarity or replacement of the full marked row is required. All original framing and external scalar factors remain outside the same exact equality. Already specified inserted operators can also be transported by the applicable prefix frames.

3. The surviving boundary source has no exponential frame cost

The normalized Fourier matrix is a unitary root Fourier matrix times diag(r^a). If r^N=sigma_i(j), then

||F(r)||<=max(1,|c_(pi(i))|),
||F(r)^(-1)||<=max(1,|c_(pi(i))|^(-1)).          (10)

These bounds are independent of crossing count and N. The phases accumulated by (2) have modulus one. With c_i=i+1, each individual frame and inverse has norm at most b+1.

For a one-cut braid closure, the boundary change on head i is the specified finite kernel F(r_i(0))F(r_i(m))^(-1), with central ratio

C_i=c_i/c_(pi_W(i)).                             (11)

Its entries are the finite-sum/removable values of

[1-C_i]/[N(1-(r_i(0)/r_i(m))Q^(j-k))].           (12)

The original pivot adds its retained scalar and label shift. At most b such boundary adapters remain. Each and its inverse have norm bounded by max(1,|C_i|) and max(1,|C_i|^(-1)), respectively. The actual cups, caps and marked source keep their original polynomial norms up to constants depending only on the fixed width and chosen c_i.

The rational off-diagonal tails in (12) are generally only O(1/N), not exponentially small. Their bounded operator norms do not prove that the boundary co-source is nonzero on a particular geometric sector.

4. Why closing the final adapters by bare diagonal residues fails

If the SAME grid were matched across every closure wire as well, the vector sigma(0) would be fixed by the full signed permutation. For a knot, pi_W is one cycle. Equations (1),(4) would then require

c1=...=cb.                                      (13)

But (13) makes B=sA at every crossing, and its internal sampling radius T=BD/(D-B) escapes to infinity. Thus the ALL-wire-diagonal stratum is absent from the regular balanced knot chart. Bare unit Cauchy residues cannot collapse both the interior and final boundary wires while remaining in the regular multisection.

Independent or propagated frames avoid that collision, but a nontrivial boundary source remains. The wire-source calculation identifies how a locally balanced section regenerates a Newton/Rogers current when pushed to a different endpoint center. Equations (7)–(12) concentrate that transport problem at the original boundary rather than creating one at every crossing.

The next geometric comparison can therefore start with regular pure four-Psi bulk blocks on a coherently matched open braid, and a specified bounded wire co-source on only b boundary heads. This describes the EXACT grid network. It does not say that only boundary seams survive after an off-grid analytic interpolation: the local full-Psi deck characters equal one on the balanced multisection, but their products at a shared bulk variable need not equal one away from it. Those bulk logarithmic cut lips remain part of any contour transport. See the exact port characters. Neither (7) nor boundedness computes the intersection with the positive constrained-lens cycle. That selected geometric coefficient remains to be proved.

5. Knot scalarity does not slide unequal radius gauges unchanged

There is also a direct obstruction to erasing the boundary co-source by moving each radius gauge along the knot to its marked cut. Put D(t)|a>=t^a|a>. In the original complete crossing a positive transfer k has input (a,b), output (b+k,a-k); a negative transfer has output (b-k,a+k). Consequently, EXACTLY,

R_epsilon (D(t1) tensor D(t2))
   =(D(t2) tensor D(t1)) R_epsilon^[t1/t2],

(R_epsilon^[lambda])_(transfer k)
                     =lambda^(epsilon*k) R_(transfer k). (14)

The extra factor is an actual transfer-clock insertion. It is not a scalar and is not eliminated by connectedness. To see that it is unavoidable, suppose an unchanged crossing allowed a product-diagonal slide with some other outgoing diagonal gauges. Its nonzero transfer-0 entries force those gauges to be the swapped ones in (14); a nonzero transfer-1 entry then forces t1=t2. Only the common total-degree gauge slides unchanged through an arbitrary original crossing.

This is visible in the actual connected hyperbolic-knot source, with no arbitrary replacement exterior. At N=2 use the normalized original positive crossing (its unit scalar cancels in the writhe-zero word)

R=[[1,0,0,0],[0,0,-i,0],[0,-i,2,0],[0,0,0,1]],
g=diag(-i,i), e=ones(2,2)/2,
W=R_2^(-1) R_1 R_2^(-1) R_1,
mu(X)=Tr[(e tensor g tensor g) W X].

Direct exact multiplication gives the original figure-eight value mu(1)=5 and, for the same radius gauge placed on the three heads,

mu(D(t) tensor I tensor I)=5(1+t)/2,
mu(I tensor D(t) tensor I)=(11-t)/2,
mu(I tensor I tensor D(t))=(-7+17t)/2.           (15)

Thus one-cut scalarity does not identify a radius factor on an ordinary closed head with the same factor at the marked cut. Sliding it requires the nontrivial current/transfer corrections (14). Applying scalarity AFTER the whole closure simply recovers the unknown knot scalar; it does not prove that a separately action-normalized geometric component has a nonzero source coefficient.

Audit: exact original signed kernels, regular propagated centers, identity interior adapters, full-word operator and pivotal-source equality, and uniform complex frame/boundary norms.
