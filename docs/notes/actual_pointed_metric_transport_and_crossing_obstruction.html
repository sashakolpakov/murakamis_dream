Pointed metric certificates and the cost of a modulus crossing

28 October 2026. A varying positive metric gives an exact transport certificate for the ORIGINAL source. It is useful only when its source cost and conditioning are bounded independently. A concrete two-channel calculation below shows that a full-space quadratic certificate with subexponential conditioning can still be forced to pay the pointwise spectral envelope, even for a specified single input and output. Thus source endpoint costs alone do not recover coherent phase cancellation.

This complements, and does not reprove, the exact Gaussian conversion example. The example is an analytic matrix cocycle, not an actual knot operator. The final section states a source-specific adjoint certificate whose geometric construction remains open.

1. Exact metric and cocycle statements

Let v_(j+1)=T_j v_j, j=0,…,n-1, with original input v_0=s and original output A=l^*v_n. For positive definite Hermitian H_j,

T_j^*H_(j+1)T_j <= exp(2h_j) H_j              (1)

is equivalent, by taking square roots, to

||H_(j+1)^(1/2) T_j H_j^(-1/2)||<=exp(h_j).

There is no scalarity or unitarity assumption. Iteration gives

|A| <= exp(sum h_j)
               sqrt[(s^*H_0s)(l^*H_n^(-1)l)]. (2)

All actual external source factors multiply (2) unchanged. Applied to the actual recurrence in the macroscopic source theorem, the certificate must be uniform on its specified tiny complex-q circle. Its entire original initial color packet is s; it cannot be replaced by a truncated normal Taylor series.

The usual finite-horizon Gramian clarifies what (1) proves. Normalize A_j=exp(-h_j)T_j and write P_(k:j)=A_(k-1)…A_j, P_(j:j)=I. If ||P_(k:j)||<=B_N for EVERY subinterval, then

H_j=sum_(k=j)^n P_(k:j)^*P_(k:j)
   =I+A_j^*H_(j+1)A_j,
I<=H_j<=(n-j+1)B_N^2 I.                       (3)

Conversely a certificate I<=H_j<=C_N I bounds every normalized subproduct by sqrt(C_N). Thus a uniformly tame full-space Gramian is essentially equivalent to a tame bound for ALL subproducts, not merely the selected full scalar. Formula (3), constructed from unknown products, is an equivalence and not a new geometric estimate.

Scalar rescalings of H_j move telescoping scalar terms into h_j. They do not remove anisotropic condition numbers; the endpoint source factor in (2) must always be retained.

There is an exact test using ANY fixed partition of the time interval. Let P_i be its subproducts and let J list the partition nodes, including both endpoints. The logarithmic right side B of (2) obeys

B>=sum_i log||P_i||+log(||s|| ||l||)
                          -1/2 sum_(j in J) log cond H_j.  (3a)

Indeed each interval costs at least log||P_i|| minus its endpoint metric ratio. Interior scalar ratios telescope, leaving the displayed condition numbers. The source costs satisfy s*H_0s>=lambda_min(H_0)||s||2 and l*H_n(-1)l>=||l||^2/lambda_max(H_n), proving (3a). Thus tame full metrics cannot hide known large subinterval norms. An actual source norm which is exponentially small is still retained in (3a); no lower bound on an unknown color packet is assumed.

2. An exact lower bound on every positive quadratic certificate

Suppose each T_j is upper triangular in one fixed orthonormal basis. Split at an arbitrary m and write P_pre=T_(m-1)…T_0 and P_post=T_(n-1)…T_m. Their diagonal entries have the form

e_2^*P_pre=D_pre e_2^*, P_post e_1=A_post e_1.

Keep the actual source s=e_2 and l=e_1, not an operator norm source. Define the full bound furnished by (2) as

B=sum_j h_j+1/2 log[(H_0)_(22)(H_n^(-1))_(11)].

Then EVERY positive definite certificate (1) satisfies exactly

B >= log|D_pre A_post|-1/2 log cond(H_m).       (4)

Proof. The pre-product inequality, in its dual norm, gives

|D_pre|^2 (H_0^(-1))_(22)
               <=exp(2h_pre)(H_m^(-1))_(22).

Since H_(22)(H^(-1))_(22)>=1, this implies

(H_0)_(22)>=exp(-2h_pre)|D_pre|^2/(H_m^(-1))_(22).

The post-product inequality on e_1 gives

(H_n^(-1))_(11)
               >=exp(-2h_post)|A_post|^2/(H_m)_(11).

Multiply, and use (H_m)(11)(H_m^(-1))(22)<=cond(H_m). This proves (4). Neither endpoint is required to be comparable to the identity; their EXACT actual-source costs are already included in B.

3. Distinct expanding roots still force the envelope cost

Use the analytic cocycle from the cited conversion proof, allowing a harmless common positive multiplier exp(c):

T_(N,j)=exp(c) [[exp(a(j/N-1/2)), N^(-p)],
                [0,exp(-a(j/N-1/2)+i theta)]],
0<theta<min(a,pi), p fixed, j=0,...,N-1.      (5)

Its two eigenvalues are distinct at every real x and their moduli cross at x=1/2. Choosing c>a/2 makes BOTH roots expanding everywhere. The separately proved exact Gaussian computation gives

lim (1/N)log|e_1^*P_N e_2|
   =c+Gamma, Gamma=a/4-theta^2/(4a)>0.       (6)

Each complete marked eigenbranch instead has rate c. The pointwise spectral envelope has rate c+a/4. No real eigenvalue collision is involved in these different quantities.

Take m=ceil(N/2). Directly summing the two diagonal logarithms gives

log|D_pre A_post|=N(c+a/4)+O(1/N).

Therefore (4) yields

B/N >=c+a/4-(log cond H_m)/(2N)+O(N^(-2)).    (7)

If log cond H_m=o(N), this FULL-SPACE quadratic certificate pays at least the scalar envelope, although the actual selected amplitude has the smaller rate (6). To reach its true rate with (1)–(2),

log cond H_m >= N theta^2/(2a)-o(N).          (8)

Exponential anisotropy is thus necessary for that improvement. This is not a zero-source example or an appeal to a backward metric. It applies to the nonzero, explicitly calculated source in (6). The same issue can occur at a modulus-only crossing such as the independently derived 6_1 quartic; this does NOT assert that the model cocycle (5) is its actual quantum recurrence.

4. What a source-restricted certificate would need

A semidefinite observable Gramian can exclude irrelevant directions. If H_n>=ll^* and T_j^*H_(j+1)T_j<=exp(2h_j)H_j, then

|A|^2<=exp(2 sum h_j) s^*H_0s.               (9)

The rank-one choice H_j=w_jw_j^* with the EXACT backward adjoint w_j=T_j^*w_(j+1), w_n=l satisfies equality. Its initial pairing is the UNKNOWN answer itself. Likewise restricting to the exact forward trajectory line gives condition number one but requires knowing that trajectory. Neither observation supplies the missing estimate.

There is, however, a useful independently checkable residual criterion. Give explicit candidate adjoint vectors w_j with w_n=l and defects

r_j=w_j-T_j^*w_(j+1).

For the ORIGINAL forward state one has the exact identity

A=w_0^*s-sum_(j=0)^(n-1) r_j^*v_j.           (10)

Thus any proved forward bounds ||v_j||<=B_j imply

|A|<=|w_0^*s|+sum_j B_j ||r_j||.             (11)

The safe spectral-envelope theorem already supplies forward bounds of this kind on its actual denominator-avoiding circle. To improve the final exponent, one must CONSTRUCT w_j whose original boundary pairing and weighted residual sum in (11) have the desired geometric rate. Algebraic WKB candidates and their finite-difference residuals can be tested directly; merely taking formal O(1/N) residuals does not suffice when B_j is exponentially large.

The equivalent positive defect estimate is also explicit: for eta>0,

T_j^*w_(j+1)w_(j+1)^*T_j
    <=(1+eta)w_jw_j^*+(1+eta^(-1))r_jr_j^*.

It retains the entire residual observable rather than inserting an uncontrolled positive completion of a rank-one metric. The Gaussian conversion example shows that those residuals may encode a genuine additional exponential branch action. Distinct roots and a real modulus gap alone cannot justify deleting it.

The unproved geometric gate is therefore explicit source-compatible adjoint/filtration transport, including conversion terms at complex turning points and the actual boundary source. Full-space positive metrics with subexponential conditioning cannot be presumed to provide that improvement. Source-dependent nonquadratic methods, explicitly controlled exponential metrics, and a genuinely verified semidefinite adjoint certificate remain possible.

Audit checks the exact metric inequalities, their pointed lower bound, the finite Gramian identity and the adjoint residual formula.
