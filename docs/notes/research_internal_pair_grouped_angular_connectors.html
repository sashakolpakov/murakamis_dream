Grouped angular connectors for every positive internal pair

Research proof, updated 4 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The folded angular endpoint terms become uniformly smaller than the geometric rate when paired with the whole other trace. This uses its exact half-band source, not an independent asymptotic or a worst-case bound for each horizontal trace piece. The result holds at complete outside colors, through both level parities, with every original physical first normal retained. Section 5 extends the grouped estimate to free outside colors, using actual physical values and normals on the whole contour. This supplies an upper bound throughout the real outside period and a fixed complex neighborhood of completeness; it does not assert decay as a real outside angle moves away from completeness.

2. The exact factorial exponent at the angular endpoint

The theorem is also compatible with a coupled lower-trace surface. For each fixed wire point, start with the product of the two constant-height lower segments, with endpoint heights ϵn,ϵp\epsilon_n,\epsilon_p. Replace it by a smooth graph (τ,ρ)↦(τ−ihn(τ,ρ),ρ−ihp(τ,ρ)),−TN≤τ,ρ≤TN,(25) (\tau,\rho)\longmapsto (\tau-ih_n(\tau,\rho),\rho-ih_p(\tau,\rho)), \qquad -T_N\le\tau,\rho\le T_N , \qquad\text{(25)} which agrees with that product on its entire boundary, has strictly positive heights, and stays in the regular domain of the finite factorial representatives. Suppose its homotopy to the product has these properties as well. The product of the two original lower-trace densities is a closed holomorphic two-form below the physical poles. Stokes’ theorem relative to the fixed boundary therefore identifies its integral on (25) with HnHpH_nH_p, with the literal scalar and pullback Jacobian retained. There is no additional boundary term from the interior coupling of the heights. Its existing folded endpoint curtains are exactly the ones already included in (19).

This equality may be applied pointwise along the wire contour. Height derivatives in the wire direction disappear after wedging with dxdx, or equivalently by the pointwise residue identity and Fubini. Thus (19)–(20) also control the difference between the original product and this coupled lower-trace integral. The statement does not bound that integral itself or prove a global pair coefficient. A whole-boundary decay estimate for the remaining main integral, required for further composition, remains separate from the upper bounds proved here.
