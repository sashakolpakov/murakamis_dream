The actual period from an all-level A-operator and finite pointing

Research note, 25 October 2026. Starting from any specified nonzero all-level annihilator of the actual reduced ADO state, the Kashaev value is recovered from that operator and finitely many fixed small colors, without a rank-one or regular-orbit assumption. Singular steps require a uniformly bounded quantum-parameter jet. Those initial jets are bounded because the colors are fixed. This gives an exact operator period; its volume growth remains unproved.

The definitions and independently checked normalization are those of the ADO note. In particular

F_N(-q_N^j)=C_j(q_N), 1<=j<=N,
F_N(1)=<K>_N, q_N=exp(pi*i/N),                   (0.1)

where C_j(q) is the fixed finite-dimensional reduced period, with unknot normalization one. It is Laurent polynomial in the recorded quantum parameter, analytic at q=1, and C_j(1)=1. Only finitely many fixed colors will be input. No large-color Jones estimate or unverified holonomicity theorem is used.

1. Cross-level rigidity gives the generic trace recurrence

Fix a specified nonzero all-level reduced ADO annihilator

P=sum_(i=0)^d a_i(q,M)T^i, TM=qMT, d>=1,         (1.1)

with rational coefficients, annihilating F_N for all but finitely many N. Clear denominators once so that a_i are Laurent polynomials. This changes no state or peripheral normalization. A possible order-zero annihilator of this nonzero state is necessarily zero.

For any fixed positive integer j form the Laurent polynomial

H_j(q)=sum_(i=0)^d a_i(q,-q^j) C_(j+i)(q).        (1.2)

At every sufficiently large N with N>=j+d, (0.1) and the actual ADO equation give H_j(q_N)=0. Infinitely many distinct q_N occur. A nonzero Laurent polynomial has only finitely many nonzero roots, so

sum_(i=0)^d a_i(q,-q^j) C_(j+i)(q)=0             (1.3)

identically in q, for every j>=1. This is an elementary Zariski rigidity argument. It requires neither an equality of annihilator ideals nor an identification of the root central curve with A_K.

The inclusion of the ADO recursion ideal in a suitably normalized colored-Jones recursion ideal is already stated in Brown–Dimofte–Garoufalidis–Geer, 2005.08176. Its proof is not a premise: (1.2)–(1.3) follow directly from the quotient-color identity already independently proved here. Reduced meridian convention M=-q^j includes the needed sign and shift; it must not be replaced silently by a different paper’s x-variable.

2. A fixed initial window avoids identically singular steps

Write the support of the leading coefficient as

a_d(q,M)=sum_(a,b) c_(a,b) q^a M^b.

Let A be its q-exponent span, D its M-exponent span, and s its number of monomials after collecting terms. Choose

j_0=A+1.                                        (2.1)

For j>=j_0 the substituted Laurent polynomial a_d(q,-q^j) is nonzero identically. In fact its exponents are a+bj. A collision between distinct b’s would imply |j|=|a-a’|/|b-b’|<=A, and terms with the same b do not collide.

Thus (1.3) propagates the fixed initial vector

c_0(q)=(C_(j_0)(q),...,C_(j_0+d-1)(q))^t        (2.2)

as rational functions in q to every later color. Let B_j(q) be the companion matrix, now evaluated at M=-q^j with q variable. For N>=j_0+d put

r_N(q)=e_d^t B_(N-d)(q)...B_(j_0)(q).

Then the exact period formula is

<K>_N=[r_N(q)c_0(q)]_(q=q_N).                   (2.3)

The right side is the value of the whole regular germ. Its product equals the Laurent polynomial C_N(q) identically, so all apparent poles cancel before evaluation. The operator and the fixed initial periods specify this value uniquely. There is no free levelwise central multiplier and no choice of a favorable root solution.

The use of small colors here is finite pointing of the A-module. Equation (2.3) does not substitute a large-color Jones asymptotic for the requested A-operator analysis.

3. A uniform bound on the required q-jets

For all sufficiently large N, a_d(q_N,M) is a nonzero Laurent polynomial of M-span at most D. The points -q_N^j appearing in the product are distinct. Therefore at most D steps satisfy a_d(q_N,-q_N^j)=0.

Each such step has q-multiplicity at most s-1. To see this, any nonzero Laurent polynomial with t<=s monomials has the form

h(q)=sum_(r=1)^t c_r q^e_r, e_r distinct.

If h and its first t-1 logarithmic derivatives vanished at a nonzero q_0, then

sum_r e_r^k c_r q_0^e_r=0, k=0,...,t-1.

The Vandermonde determinant in the distinct integers e_r is nonzero in characteristic zero. All c_r would be zero, a contradiction. Ordinary and logarithmic vanishing multiplicities coincide at q_0!=0.

Consequently the total pole budget of the companion product obeys

B_N=sum_(j=j_0)^(N-d) ord_(q=q_N) a_d(q,-q^j)
    <=D(s-1).                                  (3.1)

This counts tangencies of the moving curve M=-q^j as well as ordinary root-orbit zeros. The simpler meridian bound D from the preceding note cannot just be reused for q-jets, because M moves with q.

Use t=q/q_N-1 and expand

r_N(q_N(1+t))=sum_(k=-B_N)^infinity R_(N,k)t^k,
J_k(N)=[t^k]c_0(q_N(1+t)).

The whole-germ formula becomes

<K>_N=sum_(k=0)^B_N R_(N,-k) J_k(N).             (3.2)

For every fixed k<=D(s-1), J_k(N) is bounded as N tends to infinity: its entries are q_N^k C_j^(k)(q_N)/k! for a fixed finite set of colors, and these functions are analytic near q=1. Thus initial pointing introduces no unknown exponential level normalization. Every growing part of (3.2) is in the specified operator’s long companion product and its bounded-order germ.

As in the meridian calculation, regular numerator matrices and the full denominator product compute (3.2) using a fixed truncation. No division by a zero coefficient at q_N is performed.

4. Why levelwise Hilbert 90 alone would miss this coherence

There are fixed operators with extra root-level solutions that cannot be the coherent knot family. For example take

Q=(1+q^2 M^2)T^2-(1+q^2)T+q^2(1-M^2)
  =(T-q^2)[(1+M^2)T-(1-M^2)].                  (4.1)

For even N put

f_N(M)=product_(r=N/2)^(N-1)(1-q_N^(2r) M^2).

Telescoping gives (1+M^2)f_N(q_N M)=(1-M^2)f_N(M), so Q annihilates both 1 and f_N. Moreover

f_N(-q_N^j)=0 for 1<=j<=N/2,
|f_N(1)|=sqrt(2N).                              (4.2)

For the modulus, pair the conjugate factors in product_(r=1)^(N-1)(1-exp(2piir/N))=N and retain the middle factor 2. This gives the stated square root exactly.

Hence h_N=1+a^N f_N, a>1, has value one at every fixed small color for all sufficiently large even N, yet its target has logarithmic rate log(a). Take h_N=1 at odd levels. The same fixed Q annihilates the whole manufactured root family.

This family cannot satisfy (0.1) with fixed Laurent polynomials C_j at every permitted level. For fixed j its values are one at infinitely many levels, so polynomial rigidity forces C_j=1 identically. But at a single even level, j=N/2+1 has a nonzero f_N value and contradicts that identity. Equivalently the generic recurrence with initial C_1=C_2=1 forces C_j=1 for all j, since 1+q^(2j+2) is never identically zero.

Thus the cross-level condition used in Sections 1–3 is essential. The example is not a counterexample to any knot invariant or to the volume conjecture. It distinguishes an arbitrary norm-torsor section at each root from the actual coherent pointed period.

5. Peripheral factors and the exact scope of the theorem

If the specified literature operator is in full ADO normalization, first transport it by the fixed rational gauge already checked in the pointing note. Retain the actual whole factor d_N in comparing states. Its exact evaluation is N_N(1)=eta_N <K>_N/N; its reference residue and target jet are those in the preceding note. No generic factor such as 1+M^N is being replaced by one.

The theorem has these inputs: a specified actual nonzero all-level annihilator, and the independently established fixed-color identities. It has no root rank, semisimplicity, primeness, regular-orbit, or alternatingness assumption. It removes the extra rank/regularity conditions of the earlier exact cofactor and value-propagation formulas.

The subsequent finite cut-state proof now independently supplies a nonzero actual all-level meridian annihilator for every knot, so this theorem’s existence input is available. It does not prove the classical-symbol identification with the geometric A-curve. Most importantly it does not estimate the logarithmic growth of the actual matrix-period expression (3.2). Comparison with the marked regulator period remains the volume theorem. Bounded jet order and bounded initial jets alone do not rule out cancellation in a long matrix product or prove its geometric norm.

The exact diagnostics test moving-q germs against known polynomial solutions, including singular steps of higher multiplicity and even/composite levels. They also check the coherence obstruction (4.1)–(4.2) algebraically. The results are diagnostics, not evidence that an unaudited knot operator has the required symbol or volume growth. No novelty claim is made for the standard recurrence or sparse-polynomial arguments.

The subsequent affine source construction retains a specified inhomogeneous source as an extension class, with a cyclic resolvent when its homogeneous block is nonresonant. The adequacy argument proves that fixed algebraic meridian sections cannot reproduce the actual adequate color periods. It supplies nonsplitting tests for this extension, without inferring the missing volume norm.

The cyclotomic Mellin uniqueness theorem extends the same bounded-jet argument to the whole rational root generating function. The actual source window through orders zero to D(s-1), together with the exact cyclotomic numerator lattice, determines every competing section and its Kashaev residue. This does not impose a rank or semisimplicity condition, and does not estimate that residue’s growth.
