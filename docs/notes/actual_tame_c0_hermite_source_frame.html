A tame Hermite frame for the original source channel

29 October 2026. The colliding c_0 Casimir channel containing the ORIGINAL cup has an explicit generalized vector with harmonic-sum coefficients. Its source-adapted Hermite frame has UNIFORMLY bounded physical condition number after a common N^(3/2) scale. In that frame the original marked and ordinary caps are exactly the two coordinate rows, and the full crossing is a unit scalar times a Jordan matrix with uniformly bounded off-diagonal coefficient.

This proves an actual source-controlled Hermite Gram for the selected T=N-1 channel, including its derivative direction. It does not estimate recoupling from this channel into all other channels on overlapping pairs. No full-channel q-Racah norm or sharp knot bound follows yet.

1. The original physical source and Casimir

Fix q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2, d=N-1, rho=q-q^(-1), x=q^d=-q^(-1), N>=2. On balanced heads use exactly

mathsfF|a>=(1-Q^(a+1))|a+1>,
Ecal|a>=(Q^(-a)-1)|a-1>,
K|a>=xQ^(-a)|a>,
F_tot=mathsfF_1+K_1^(-1)mathsfF_2,
E_tot=Ecal_1 K_2+Ecal_2,
Omega=F_tot E_tot+qK_tot+q^(-1)K_tot^(-1).

Missing endpoint arrows are zero. Work on the physical integer-degree d subspace with basis |u,d-u>, 0<=u<=d. Let c_*=q+q^(-1). The ORIGINAL cup, ordinary pivotal cap and marked row restrict here to

p_u=N x^(u-d), ell_u=x^(-u)Q^(-u)/N,
m_u=x^(d-u)/N^2.                               (1)

The unrestricted marked row is not replaced by this restricted row on other degrees; every vector constructed below has degree d. These are the fixed actual turns, with no invented scalar frame.

Define finite harmonic sums

H_0=0, H_u=sum_(j=1)^u 1/(1-Q^j),
g_u=x^(u-d)=p_u/N,
v_u=-g_u H_u/rho.                              (2)

Then the exact Jordan equations are

(Omega-c_*)g=0, (Omega-c_*)v=g.                 (3)

In particular g,v are independent, and their span is the actual size-two c_* block inside integer degree d. This block, rather than the entire 2N-dimensional c_* generalized eigenspace across degrees, is the source channel treated in this note.

2. Derivation from generic q with actual cup duality retained

Use a variable z near q and the ordinary highest-weight representation of highest weight d=N-1. Put Q_z=z^2, x(z)=z^d, F_a(z)=product_(j=1)^a(1-z^(2j)). Keep the standard highest-weight endpoint, including mathsfF|d>=0. All factorials with a<N are nonzero near q.

The top-channel descendant and spin-zero invariant cup may be normalized as

g_u(z)=x(z)^(u-d),
h_u(z)=g_u(z) R_u(z),
R_u(z)=(-1)^u z^[-u(u+1)] F_u(z)F_(d-u)(z)/F_d(z).
                                                           (4)

Indeed g(z)=F_tot(z)^d|0,0>/F_d(z). The finite coproduct binomial proves its displayed components. The E equation for an invariant degree-d vector gives the successive ratio

h_(u+1)/h_u=-z^[d-2u-2]
                     (1-z^(2u+2))/(1-z^(2d-2u)),

which is (4); direct F and K equations give F_tot h=0, K_tot h=h. Thus h is the ACTUAL self-duality cup up to the stated analytic unit normalization. Its paired evaluation is transported reciprocally. One does not extend the root-only expression K^(1-N) as a generic-q pivot or assume a root cyclic endpoint away from q.

The exact Casimir eigenvalues are

Omega(z)g(z)=c_top(z)g(z),
Omega(z)h(z)=c_triv(z)h(z),
c_top(z)=z^(2N-1)+z^(-2N+1),
c_triv(z)=z+z^(-1).                             (5)

These follow from the defining coproduct: the top highest vector has K-root z^(2d), while h is invariant. They can also be checked directly from the entries above. Root complementation gives R_u(q)=1, so h(q)=g(q). Meanwhile

D_logz(c_triv-c_top)|q=2N rho,
D_logz R_u|q=-2N H_u.                           (6)

For the second equation, differentiate (4) and pair its last u factorial terms with indices N-j. Their sum reduces to -2N sum_(j<=u)(1-Q^j)^(-1); the quadratic integer terms cancel. All derivatives are at fixed N and the actual root, at either parity.

Consequently

lim_(z->q) [h(z)-g(z)]/[c_triv(z)-c_top(z)]=v.   (7)

Applying (5) before the limit proves (3). This is a normalized divided difference of actual Clebsch channels, with the original cup kept. It does not use the fixed-q common-meridian splitting at a dual cap; that cap’s exact dual-color product keeps its Casimir nodes collided.

An analytic rescaling of h or g, equal to one at q, can add a multiple of g to v. The source-adapted vector in Section3 removes precisely that ambiguity. Thus the final Hermite frame does not depend on the unphysical first derivative of an eigenvector normalization.

3. Exact original co-sources and the canonical Hermite lift

The finite sums in (1)–(2) give

ell g=0, m g=1/N,
gamma_N:=ell v=q x^(-d)/(N rho^2)
               =(-1)^N/(N rho^2),
mu_N:=m v=-(sum_(u=0)^d H_u)/(N^2 rho).          (8)

In particular gamma_N is NONZERO at every level. To check its value, interchange the two finite sums:

sum_(u=0)^d Q^(-u)H_u
  =sum_(j=1)^d [sum_(u=j)^d Q^(-u)]/(1-Q^j)
  =-1/(1-Q^(-1)).

Also q x^(-d)=(-1)^N, by x=q^d and the actual half-period q. This accounts for the full parity sign.

Define the SOURCE-ADAPTED generalized vector

t_N=(v-mu_N p)/gamma_N.                         (9)

Then all original source pairings are exactly

m p=1, m t_N=0, ell p=0, ell t_N=1,
(Omega-c_*)t_N=theta_N p,
theta_N=1/(N gamma_N)=(-1)^N rho^2.              (10)

Thus the two original co-sources, not newly chosen dual rows, are the inverse frame on this actual Jordan channel. In particular (Omega-c_*)|span(p,t_N)=theta_N p ell, agreeing with the retained rank-one nilpotent source. No ordinary quantum dimension is divided out. The generalized direction and the marked co-source remain.

The replacement v->v+lambda g changes mu_N by lambda/N, so the numerator in (9) is unchanged. This proves the normalization independence asserted after (7).

4. Fourier–Plancherel gives uniform physical conditioning

For 1<=j<N,

|1-Q^j|>=4min(j,N-j)/N, |rho|>=4/N.

Hence, with Harm_N=1+log N,

max_u |H_u| <=(N/2)Harm_N,
||v||_2 <=(N^(5/2)/8)Harm_N,
|mu_N| <=(N/8)Harm_N,
|gamma_N|^(-1)=N|rho|^2<=4N.

These estimates already give polynomial frame bounds without a spectral projector. A stronger exact Fourier calculation removes their condition growth.

On degree d the original marked row is m=p^*/N^3, so (10) gives p^*t_N=0. If Hbar=(1/N)sum_u H_u, (9) becomes

(t_N)_u=(-1)^(N+1)N rho g_u(H_u-Hbar).           (11)

Use the unnormalized cyclic Fourier transform Hhat_k=sum_(u=0)^d H_u Q^(-ku). Pairing j with N-j gives H_d=d/2. The cyclic difference of H therefore has value -d/2 at zero and (1-Q^u)^(-1) at u=1,...,d. Its Fourier transform is EXACTLY -k, for 1<=k<=d: successive transforms differ by sum_(j=1)^d Q^(-kj)=-1, starting at zero for k=0. Consequently

Hhat_k=-k/(1-Q^(-k)), 1<=k<=d,
||t_N||_2^2=N|rho|^2 sum_(k=1)^d k^2/|1-Q^k|^2. (12)

This is ordinary finite Plancherel on the FULL physical N-window; no Fourier channel is omitted or independently dephased.

The elementary root identity is

sum_(k=1)^d |1-Q^k|^(-2)=(N^2-1)/12.

For a direct proof differentiate P(z)=(z^N-1)/(z-1) twice at one. The first reciprocal sum is d/2 and the sum of squared reciprocals is d(5-N)/12; their difference is the displayed absolute sum. Pairing k and N-k then gives

N^2(N^2-1)/48
   <=sum_(k=1)^d k^2/|1-Q^k|^2
   <=N^2(N^2-1)/24.

Since 4/N<=|rho|<=2pi/N, it follows that

N^3/4<=||t_N||_2^2<=pi^2 N^3/6,
p^*t_N=0, ||p||_2^2=N^3.                       (13)

For the two physical columns V=[p,t_N] this proves

sigma_min(V)>=N^(3/2)/2,
sigma_max(V)<=pi N^(3/2)/sqrt(6),
kappa(V)<=2, uniformly EVERY N>=2.              (14)

For the last bound use the known first column norm N^(3/2), not just the two extreme singular-value bounds: the other column’s relative norm lies in [1/2,pi/sqrt(6)], and pi/sqrt(6)<2. The condition number is therefore at most two; N=2 attains two.

The exact inverse on its range is still the two ORIGINAL rows m,ell by (10). The ordinary row on the full physical degree-d space has a larger norm because it also sees other channel directions; its restriction here is precisely the coordinate dual of t_N. No inverse of a possibly ill-conditioned whole Casimir projector was used.

5. An actual positive Hermite Gram and crossing bound

On S_N=span(p,t_N) define the positive inner product

||w||_(Herm)^2=|m w|^2+|ell w|^2.                (15)

It makes p,t_N orthonormal. It has rank two on S_N, not rank one, and retains BOTH the value and derivative channels. Both original co-sources have dual norm one. Equation (14) compares this Gram uniformly with N^(-3) times the physical metric. This is an explicit source-controlled Hermite Gram, not a metric defined from an unknown knot scalar or terminal word.

Keep the original crossing’s common Cartan scalar kappa. The independently derived Casimir polynomial has

r(c_*)=1, r'(c_*)=-d/(2rho).

Therefore in the p,t_N frame the EXACT original signed crossing is

R_+=kappa [[1,tau_N],[0,1]],
R_-=kappa^(-1) [[1,-tau_N],[0,1]],
tau_N=(-1)^(N+1)d rho/2, |tau_N|<=pi.            (16)

Thus, for every signed integer l,

||R_+^l||_(Herm)<=1+pi|l|                        (17)

at the original complete root, since |kappa|=1. The original full Cartan, pivot, framing and modified-dimension line have not been renormalized; (16) is a basis expression for that same operator. The PHYSICAL operator norm on this SAME invariant source channel is consequently at most 2(1+pi|l|), uniformly in N.

The Gram (15) is asserted on the SELECTED TWO-DIMENSIONAL invariant channel only. Adjacent crossings may take it into other intermediate Casimir channels. Extending it through the full q-Racah transform, with the source, is the remaining problem. Declaring the rest of the physical tensor space orthogonal would not control that leakage, and no such full-channel estimate is claimed here.
