An exact two-mode Gaussian source for the binomial cut

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The binomial cut has a finite Gaussian presentation at a bulk coherent occupation. Two oscillator modes with fixed total degree d=N−1d=N-1 realize the original finite head. Composition of the literal local kernels is exact, and the binomial closing is a fixed-radius latitude evaluation with a normalization bounded above and below by constants times d\sqrt d.

This construction does not continue the old small-clock Borel germ to the bulk. It gives a finite-level integral before an asymptotic expansion, retains the full occupation projector, and transports finite matrix currents. It is not identified with the previous finite-RR Borel coarea chain.

The complete proof directs are C, the actual compact coherent source, and S, the binomial cut and normal-residue density. Their pins appear at the end. The Gaussian and factorial calculations needed here are proved directly.

1. The finite head in a two-mode Gaussian space

Fix N≥2N\ge2 and d=N−1≥1d=N-1\ge1. Use the original head basis e0,…,ede_0,\ldots,e_d, with all matrix entries in the conventions of C and S. On ℂ2\mathbb C^2, write Z=(Z0,Z1)Z=(Z_0,Z_1) and use dγ(Z)=π−2e−|Z0|2−|Z1|2d2Z0d2Z1.(1) d\gamma(Z)=\pi^{-2}e^{-|Z_0|^2-|Z_1|^2}\,d^2Z_0\,d^2Z_1. \qquad\text{(1)} Angular integration and one-dimensional integration by parts give ∫ℂ2ZαZ¯βdγ(Z)=𝟏α=βα!.(2) \int_{\mathbb C^2}Z^\alpha\overline Z^\beta\,d\gamma(Z) =\mathbf1_{\alpha=\beta}\alpha!. \qquad\text{(2)} Indeed each radial factor is ∫0∞tae−tdt=a!\int_0^\infty t^a e^{-t}dt=a!, starting with the unit integral and integrating by parts aa times.

Embed the finite head as the homogeneous degree-dd subspace by ej↦uj(Z)=Z1jZ0d−jd!,0≤j≤d.(3) e_j\longmapsto u_j(Z) =\frac{Z_1^jZ_0^{d-j}}{\sqrt{d!}}, \qquad 0\le j\le d. \qquad\text{(3)} Then ⟨ui,uj⟩γ=δijj!(d−j)!d!=δij(dj).(4) \langle u_i,u_j\rangle_\gamma =\delta_{ij}\frac{j!(d-j)!}{d!} =\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\binom dj}. \qquad\text{(4)} This is exactly C’s coherent head metric. No claim that the literal braiding is unitary in this metric is made.

For an arbitrary finite matrix A=(Aij)A=(A_{ij}), define its kernel 𝒦A(Z,W¯)=∑i,j=0dAij(dj)ui(Z)uj(W)¯=1d!∑i,j=0dAij(dj)Z1iZ0d−iW¯1jW¯0d−j.(5) \mathcal K_A(Z,\overline W) =\sum_{i,j=0}^d A_{ij}\binom dj\, u_i(Z)\overline{u_j(W)} =\frac1{d!}\sum_{i,j=0}^d A_{ij}\binom dj Z_1^iZ_0^{d-i} \overline W_1^{\,j}\overline W_0^{\,d-j}. \qquad\text{(5)} The binomial belongs to the input index. 2. Exact local composition, trace and occupation projector

Using (2)–(4) on the middle index proves ∫ℂ2𝒦A(Z,X¯)𝒦B(X,W¯)dγ(X)=𝒦AB(Z,W¯),∫𝒦A(Z,Z¯)dγ(Z)=Tr⁡A.(7) \boxed{\quad \int_{\mathbb C^2} \mathcal K_A(Z,\overline X)\mathcal K_B(X,\overline W) \,d\gamma(X)=\mathcal K_{AB}(Z,\overline W),\qquad \int\mathcal K_A(Z,\overline Z)\,d\gamma(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}A. \quad} \qquad\text{(7)} All integrands here are finite polynomials times a Gaussian, so these are absolutely convergent integrals. Tensor products use one pair of modes for each physical head and product measures. Rectangular maps between tensor products use the same output monomials and dual input monomials; their composition proof is the identical index contraction.

In particular the identity head has kernel Πd(Z,W¯)=(Z0W¯0+Z1W¯1)dd!=[td]exp⁡(t(Z0W¯0+Z1W¯1)).(8) \Pi_d(Z,\overline W) =\frac{(Z_0\overline W_0+Z_1\overline W_1)^d}{d!} =[t^d]\exp\!\left(t(Z_0\overline W_0+Z_1\overline W_1)\right). \qquad\text{(8)} It is the orthogonal projection onto total degree dd, rather than the identity on the full oscillator space. This can be checked on every monomial using (2): all total degrees other than dd are annihilated. The coefficient in (8) can equivalently be retained as a Cauchy integral on any fixed small tt-circle. Neither a degree tail nor a boundary coefficient is discarded.

Both mode occupations are nonnegative. Their fixed sum is dd; hence the physical occupation jj lies exactly in {0,…,d}\{0,\ldots,d\}. This is a coupled sector condition, not two independent approximations to a cutoff.

To display the extreme physical walls explicitly, 𝒦T0(Z,W¯)=(Z0W¯0)dd!,𝒦Td(Z,W¯)=(Z1W¯1)dd!.(9) \mathcal K_{T_0}(Z,\overline W) =\frac{(Z_0\overline W_0)^d}{d!},\qquad \mathcal K_{T_d}(Z,\overline W) =\frac{(Z_1\overline W_1)^d}{d!}. \qquad\text{(9)} Any actual finite generator relation with lower or upper projector terms therefore has its exact kernel counterpart under (7). The finite pivots remain their actual matrices inside the same formula. The map does not replace them by an oscillator trace or a classical value.

5. A finite-word integral before asymptotic expansion

Formula (14) need not be applied after computing the unknown whole cut matrix. Factor that matrix by its given finite diagram: literal signed crossings, turn maps, pivots, marks, and the remaining finite partial traces. Apply (5) to each of those known local arrays, and (7) to every contraction. For example, for a one-head ordered product C=Am⋯A1C=A_m\cdots A_1, Tr⁡(PNcutC)=Ade−d2πi∮dvv∫𝒦Am(Zd(v),X¯m−1)⋅𝒦Am−1(Xm−1,X¯m−2)⋯𝒦A1(X1,Zd(v)¯)∏ℓ=1m−1dγ(Xℓ).(19) \begin{split} \operatorname{Tr}(P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}C) ={}&\frac{A_d e^{-d}}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{dv}{v} \int\mathcal K_{A_m}(Z_d(v),\overline X_{m-1})\\ &\quad\cdot \mathcal K_{A_{m-1}}(X_{m-1},\overline X_{m-2}) \cdots \mathcal K_{A_1}(X_1,\overline{Z_d(v)}) \prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}d\gamma(X_\ell). \end{split} \qquad\text{(19)} The case m=1m=1 means no internal integral. Multi-head words and the actual partially traced knot network use the tensor and rectangular versions of the same identity. Their number of Gaussian mode pairs depends on the fixed diagram, not on NN. All matrix coefficients are the original finite ones; no unknown scalar defines a kernel or its normalization.

All inserted identity wires use (8). Thus the formula retains every physical input/output occupation restriction, including restrictions shared by two legs of one crossing. Reordering integrations is legitimate because at fixed NN their integrands are finite polynomials against product Gaussian measures. The only new outer cut normalization is (13). The factor e−de^{-d} in (19) is the recorded Gaussian weight at its fixed boundary radius, not an omitted scalar.

For comparison with the compact source, ordinary radial coordinates give dγ(Z)=e−ttdtdσ(u),Z=tu,|u|=1,(20) d\gamma(Z)=e^{-t}t\,dt\,d\sigma(u),\qquad Z=\sqrt t\,u,\quad |u|=1, \qquad\text{(20)} where dσd\sigma is normalized measure on S3S^3. Since the trace kernel is homogeneous of bidegree (d,d)(d,d), its radial integral is (d+1)!/d!=N(d+1)!/d!=N. The residual angular integral, after the scalar U(1)U(1) phase is removed, is exactly C’s compact coherent trace with its factor NN. This verifies that (19) is a Gaussian presentation of the existing finite source, rather than a different quantum model.

6. Currents and the boundary of the conclusion

The injective map (5) and product identity (7) transport every finite matrix identity, including its marked defects. In particular, S’s exact equation Tr⁡((PNcut−T0)C)=Tr⁡(BNcut[C,ℰ])(21) \operatorname{Tr}((P_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}-T_0)C) =\operatorname{Tr}(B_N^{\mathrm{ cut}}[C,\mathcal E]) \qquad\text{(21)} is still the equality of the corresponding Gaussian kernel contractions. Commutators are Gaussian-composed kernels, not pointwise products of coherent symbols. Lower and upper projector terms remain the kernels (9). Their possibly small values at one latitude do not bound their composition with a growing source.

At every fixed NN, differentiation in meridian or marking parameters commutes with these identities on compact subsets where the literal coefficient matrices are holomorphic: the integrands remain finite polynomial Gaussian moments. Consequently all finite meridian normals and marked endpoint currents are retained. This assertion gives no uniform large-NN estimate for the differentiated whole word.

The coefficient degree and literal entries in (19) grow with the level. A uniform analytic phase, amplitude or Gevrey bound on the bulk fields has not been obtained by Gaussian composition alone. In particular the old highest-cut small-clock germ cannot be evaluated at a singular bulk parameter merely because (19) exists.

No Borel cutoff RR was introduced in this construction. The earlier highest-cut coarea formula has a different relative chain and explicit upper-RR derivative currents. Identifying that family with (19), including those currents, would require an additional source-preserving map; none is claimed here. The present gain is an exact finite Gaussian and conditioned-Poisson realization of the bulk cut, with polynomial outer normalization and complete finite walls. It supplies neither geometric-cycle occurrence nor a volume-conjecture lower bound.
