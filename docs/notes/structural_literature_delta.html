What the present notes add, and what they do not

Research comparison, 26 October 2026. The user’s requested direction is to identify the intrinsic period torsor and prove a uniform structural statement. Individual knot certificates are validation, not the main mechanism. This comparison distinguishes independent verification from a new mathematical construction; no novelty claim is made.

Latest target clarification. The intended route proves Kashaev’s volume growth through the quantum A-module or torsor attached to the actual invariant. The classical A-polynomial should emerge as its appropriate geometric support, with source and peripheral factors retained. Relating it to Jones is separate. The comparison with the previous work in this repo is in the primary status, Section 0. The auxiliary direct classical A-period construction now supplies such a marked-root limit under a connectedness criterion, independently verified for the hyperbolic twist family. Its finite samples are not canonical; it is not presented as a standard quantum A-state or a new classical regulator theorem. Coverage for exact polynomials and volume functions includes Guilloux–Marche, 1804.01395. The integer-norm construction, rounding estimate, cover product law and twist-curve algebra are independently proved in the note.

The later doubled-phase proof works directly with the normalized geometric and conjugate places. It makes the unique constrained Rivin optimizer algebraic in its two multiplicative boundary phases and writes its marked volume potential as the central period of one fixed two-step Heisenberg connection. This is a refinement of the repo’s earlier meridian etaleness and boundary Monge–Ampere calculation. It is not a new claim that classical volume potentials exist, and no Ronkin, Harnack or dimer equivalence is inferred. All formulas are derived in the note without using the coverage papers’ proofs.

The critical-source trace theorem adds a finite nonvanishing criterion through irreducible descent and exact source moments. It also proves polynomial norm bounds for a fixed whole algebraic density along the actual root-source arc. The whole unit/level-weight theorem separately records why finite type of a connection does not bound its amplified horizontal periods. These are elementary trace and function estimates, not claims that the actual quantum source has already descended to the geometric critical field.

The subsequent source work now gives actual maps: centralizer normal moments and endpoints, an exact dual whole clock, and a degree-N divided source in an actual high integral color. Quantum Frobenius and Steinberg tensor-product constructions are established representation theory; the high-color note uses their standard integral divided-power definitions and proves every needed coefficient and even/odd sign directly. Its specific role here is the literal wall row, first-normal block arrows, original knot-period adapter and source germ. The whole-clock proof similarly derives its dual Toeplitz polynomial and affine normal clutch explicitly. No general quantum source/geometry equivalence is inferred from these maps; the inserted-network and Gaussian naturality remain the parts to prove.

The intrinsic diagonal wall-source theorem subsequently constructs its finite Fourier constraint, exact endpoint-completed ladder and regular whole-source gauge. The first-normal crossing theorem supplies the universal resonant factor and quadratic divided degree, with a general braid-word extension. These are actual matrix/source identities, not a proposed positive replacement state. The memory-clock refinement retains first-normal block carries while explicitly reducing its root clock identity to the earlier position-clock factorization. The clock-dependent source gauge retains whole dilations; no bounded differential degree after that gauge or geometric Gaussian/current equivalence is inferred.

The later all-normal filtration proof extends the actual matrix bound to every finite order: normal depth j uses degree at most 2j and at most 2j+1 rotated source descendants. The unprojectivized actual source then uses the earlier standard Borel formulas on a marked actual Sym^N source/dual cover. Its additional content is the retained weight-N scale, its literal old-clock normal detector, and the framing two-form recovered from scale plus an actual descendant. It is not a novelty claim for Borel groups, pure-power cones or Kummer covers. The network Gaussian/current intertwiner remains to be constructed.

The original-Kashaev torsor work remains the main construction. The later AK meridian state studied in several notes is another comparison object. The claimed AK results here do not prove the original conjecture without a comparison of its distinguished period. The subsequent coverage audit records current statements. The user also separately authorized a proof audit of Ge’s geometric existence argument; its core is now checked in the geometric audit. The principal direction remains an intrinsic A-module/period-torsor construction. The subsequent intrinsic norm-torsor note constructs that module-comparison line directly from root-of-unity monodromy, proves its cyclic norm equation, and extends it by a finite cyclic lattice sum. This is standard semilinear descent proved explicitly, not a claim that the selected Kashaev period has now been identified. Subsequently, the crossing proof independently verifies the known enhanced Kashaev/quantum-group comparison at every level. The ADO pointing proof identifies its actual meridian normalization line, including the peripheral factor and regular specialization. This supplies a literature pointing; it does not yet identify its norm with the geometric regulator. In particular, root-level central support and the classical geometric A-curve must be distinguished.

The regular cyclic/Steinberg calculation subsequently identifies an all-level regular quantum-group family, the nilpotent Kashaev vacuum, matched endpoint rescalings for the actual Borel coproduct, and the two-step recoupling lattice at simultaneous degeneration. The normalization tower proves all-level distribution, a local Kummer certificate, and a local regulator estimate without a Habiro-ring theorem. This is independent verification of local representation and finite-product identities, not a new global quantum invariant or a knot volume theorem.

The Casimir comparison subsequently proves that the product-coordinate injection intertwines E as well as the Borel action. It derives the actual crossing as a confluent Casimir polynomial, with its inverse and writhe phase, and matches it on the integral recoupling lattice. The pivotal closing trace is an explicit spectral-jet functional. The relative spectral algebra is nonreduced: its units and normalization torsor retain nilpotent jets. A finite logarithmic Hilbert-90 argument extends descent to that Artin base. These are independent local calculations within existing projective braid theory, not a new volume theorem.

Following the user’s return to the A-polynomial direction, the formal A-curve construction now derives a branch of the specified operator by noncommutative Hensel lifting, its gauge-covariant period differential, and its leading regulator action with peripheral conversions explicit. Formal q-difference solutions already occur in Garoufalidis–Geronimo; their analytic theorems are not used. The ordinary-double-point calculation retains the colliding pair as a regular rank-two formal connection. The central normalization theorem also proves polynomial bounds for whole-germ values from a fixed finite algebraic central presentation. These are algebraic formal and normalization results, not an identification of the actual Kashaev period or a new knot-family volume theorem.

The root-orbit pointing calculation subsequently proves the nonzero anchor F_N(-q)=1 and the fixed integer-color specializations by a filtered cut-trace argument. These specializations overlap existing ADO/Jones unification coverage; no completion theorem is adopted. It then derives an operator-only cofactor expression on a one-dimensional rational solution line, using the saturated generic kernel and its actual reference pointing. The full-state formula retains the central residue and first meridian jet. This removes an unfixed central scalar at the Kashaev fiber once the common line is known; it does not prove the geometric identification or a volume growth theorem.

The all-level A-period theorem now gives a rank-independent exact presentation from any specified actual annihilator and finitely many fixed small colors. The ADO-to-trace recursion inclusion is already stated in Brown–Dimofte–Garoufalidis–Geer; it is reconstructed here from the independently proved color identity and infinitely many polynomial zeroes. Sparse-polynomial multiplicity bounds control the whole-q-germ jet order uniformly. The meridian version derives the Smith bound by elementary degree counting and integral companion propagation. These overlap existing q-difference desingularization theory; no novelty or volume theorem is inferred. They remove the earlier rank/regularity restriction from exact period recovery, while geometric growth still needs proof. The subsequent finite cut-state argument independently supplies the missing nonzero actual all-level meridian annihilator for every knot. It proves the needed proper-product shift bound on boundary strata as well as in the interior, repairs zero direct summation by finite moments, and strips a common central operator factor before q^N=-1. This reconstructs existing recursion coverage; it is not a new invariant or a general classical-symbol theorem.

The affine source construction now uses the literature inhomogeneous A-equation, retaining its source as an extension class modulo sigma-C. Cyclic affine averaging and the nonresonant resolvent are direct linear descent. Normalized trace proves that a finite stable algebraic cover cannot kill this class. These are standard affine difference-module constructions, not a newly defined quantum knot invariant. The adequacy proof reconstructs the known quadratic degree span by extreme states, cabling and Chebyshev threading. A valuation argument excludes a fixed algebraic meridian trace. With the stated actual-source and either cyclotomic or formal nonresonance test this forces nonsplitting, including for the proposed adequate knot class. None of this identifies the source class or its norm with the marked regulator. The two equal spectral-radius lobes of the classical figure-eight curve show why a maximum-eigenvalue norm calculation would not supply that identification.

The QHI link and geometric-character patterns are kept distinct by their definitions; no unaudited comparison theorem is imported.

This concretely supplies local relative data for the proposed comparison. The local octahedral growth bound overlaps Garoufalidis–Le and is not a volume-conjecture theorem. These are independently reconstructed with the actual source conventions, with no novelty claim. It reconstructs the classical shadow-volume coverage of Inoue–Kabaya and Cho in the fixed actual ADO conventions, with an explicit three-five-term certificate, four-tetrahedron face pairing, orientation, and peripheral degree. The generic meridian defect is retained. This closes the complete classical regulator comparison in these notes, with no novelty claim or imported proof. It does not prove quantum source transport, integral flattenings, a minimal-operator classical factor, or the original selected volume limit.

The new calculation removes the extra quantum-Weyl hypothesis from the canonical normal-period formulation, describes the bounded source by sine moments, and checks generator-metric invariance. It does not prove the root-sector inverse norm or the geometric factor’s survival.

The structural change made here

heisenberg_period_torsor.md presents the local normalization as an explicit H_n-torsor over the classical central shape t. The norm relation is the Steinberg relation, and the third root makes the first two deck transformations fail to commute by a central phase. This identifies the same finite Heisenberg structure behind the cyclic operator and its cover.

The coordinate-free finite face contraction is proved for every finite level, including composite levels and nonfree support groups. Its measure is exactly |W|^(-1/2), and its support and quadratic form follow from one ideal-cohomology vanishing. This replaces separate rational nullspace cases by a single finite-character argument. It is an application of finite Fourier duality, not a claim to have invented that theory.

The boundary module is obtained by primitive isotropic edge reduction and is irreducible of dimension n, independent of face projection rank. Its unpointed intertwiner line is rigid. The selected intrinsic boundary period remains to be identified; the subsequent local pointed results below do not already supply that global identification.

The pointed recoupling note goes further at the local algebraic level: the quantum tetrahedron has the canonical vacuum A/A(X+Y-1). The audited Faddeev shift equation identifies its root-of-unity central fibre with this pointed module exactly, including the choice X=-q^(-1/2)exp(2pi b Z). Thus the algebraic vacuum is a representation of the existing local difference object, rather than a separate quantum definition selected for its asymptotic behavior. Its factorization preserves the vacuum by two exact left-ideal identities at generic quantum parameter. The associated q-factorial kernel has determinant [t(1-st)/(1-t)^2]^(n(n-1)/2), so its normalization unit is explicit before any peripheral specialization. Coassociativity proves the basis-free pentagon uniformly. This does not yet identify all global period sections or their growth with the classical regulator.

The exact rational Faddeev formula also exhibits a continuous Heisenberg dilogarithm period in addition to the finite root cover. Thus the correct comparison object needs its flat connection and period/cycle data. A plain algebraic G_m-torsor does not retain all of those data.

pointed_period_descent.md explicitly identifies the Racah operator with the same q-factorial factorization, fixes the permutation sign, and makes the scalar normalization a shared-face coboundary. This proves selected-vacuum coherence through every finite recoupling diagram.

affine_frame_period_transport.md proves a necessary correction to the proposed pointing: unit vacua in two endpoint frames are generally different vectors under the central intertwiner. It constructs their exact norm-one transport and proves full affine covariance. projective_frame_period_torsor.md then proves that a fractional linear change rescales triangle generators by D_j/D_i and D_k/D_i. These rescalings commute with all four ordered Racah operator embeddings. The remaining comparison is consequently a scalar; determinant roots make it mu_(n^2)-valued and an identity-frame anchor fixes it on its irreducible lifted component. Shared-face roots preserve the pentagon. This supplies a local projective period system with its actual vector transport, uniformly at all odd levels. The proof uses precisely stated Hilbert-90 and Zariski-rigidity hypotheses and retains the deck phases.

These results reconstruct and clarify part of the existing cyclic matrix framework. The new global question for this project is the exact identification of its selected period with the prescribed literature quantum-A state, followed by compatibility across levels with the Bloch regulator. Constructing another algebraic character cover would not alone establish either comparison.

There is also an exact parameter condition on that last comparison. Section 5.1 of the rational Faddeev note substitutes the actual state argument at b^2=1/n and obtains t_n=exp((n+1)y), with a recorded omega deck shift of its chosen n-th root. Thus a fixed semiclassical shape does not mean a fixed finite central character. This distinction must be preserved in any claimed compatibility between the two period models.

bloch_lift_and_boundary_torsor.md identifies a further missing datum: the tetrahedral symbol is a lift of the boundary wedge, with delta beta=-(1/2)m wedge l in the recorded unhalved conventions. Its Bloch-Wigner regulator is the volume exactly. Thus the target includes the Bloch-period lift, rather than only an unpointed split boundary algebra. The wedge and five-term arguments are uniform.

The actual peripheral-fusion calculation now reconstructs the fundamental-color boundary action directly from the fixed unrolled matrices. Explicit adjacent-color projectors give the full modified-dimension ratios; heterogeneous double crossing fixes the meridian sign. The complete fusion wall retains a square-zero Casimir residue of rank N-1 and pivotal trace identity. Its actual cabled-knot coefficient and the normal Kashaev functional are specified derivatives. General unrolled/projective representation coverage is in Costantino–Geer–Patureau-Mirand, 1406.0410, and the torus skein/quantum-torus framework is covered by Frohman–Gelca, math/9806107. Derivative/radical-center knot invariants also have coverage in Murakami, 1406.1287. Their abstracts establish coverage only; no proof is imported. The normalization-matched formulas give a direct implication from peripheral word relations to actual ADO annihilators. They do not prove equality of the ideals, survival of a geometric classical factor, or the selected period’s regulator norm.

The ramified Weyl-fibre calculation uses the existing parabolic/ramified-descent framework, with coverage in Borne, math/0604458 and Biswas–Borne, 1706.06733. Only the abstracts were consulted; the involution linearization and the invariant lattice are derived directly. Its parity traces also give a necessary dimension test for sector injectivity. No new general descent theorem or volume growth result is claimed.

The torsion initial-rank theorem uses the existing root-of-unity relation framework covered by Mann, Mathematika 12 (1965). The later integral half-longitude proof specializes the existing integral/mod-two boundary compatibility to the knot’s preferred longitude. Neumann’s Theorems 4.1 and 5.1 already cover the general structural setting; only statement coverage was checked. Our own local flow identity and dual-face potentials prove that the longitude class is even in the saturated edge quotient. Its unique half and the actual marked meridian form an integral symplectic basis. The resulting Weyl algebra has the actual Q=q^2 shift at every root level, with explicit edge character and square-root lift. This is an independent marking and parity calculation, not a new general triangulation-complex theorem or an identification of the selected knot period.

The factorial source-torsor calculation now makes the actual boundary’s relation to the standard cyclic dilogarithm exact: p_a=nu^a phi_a on nuN=1-hN. This is a singular factorial frame of an existing local quantum function, not a new definition. Its two-direction affine extension has a source line of meridian norm (1-UC)/(1-U), fixed by flatness when the internal center is retained. The complete raw cut loop is non-scalar; explicit source-channel normalization gives strict cyclic descent on the Fermat cover. The full modified dimension and the earlier Verma pole character are recovered as endpoint and block transports in that frame. These are independently proved local source, frame and boundary identities. They do not repair the positive scalar model’s failed move by fiat or establish global geometric source transport and regulator growth. The abstract of Ishibashi’s phase correction, 2610.23572v1 records additional lifting data and a general pentagon phase; it is coverage only, not a proof premise or a claimed global phase audit here.

The common carry-lattice theorem now gives an explicit whole-tree integral statement beyond the earlier two-factor calculation. The existing pointed factorial kernel has node poles bounded by binary residue carries. They sum to floor(sum i_j/N), and its determinant proves that these are exactly the Smith poles and that every tree has the same image lattice. Thus recoupling loops preserve both the basic source and its actual integral sublattice through degeneration, at every parity. The wrap sources of an auxiliary actual crossing lift give strictly degree-lowering blocks; they cannot close a diagonal cut trace, so the selected recoupled period is exactly the original meridian family with all peripheral data. This reconstructs a coherent integral source presentation within the existing cyclic recoupling framework. No novelty claim, generic braid theorem, arbitrary colored triangulation comparison or selected volume-norm theorem is inferred. Kashaev’s 6j origin and Suzuki’s colored-ideal-triangulation framework were checked for statement coverage only; their proofs are not premises.
