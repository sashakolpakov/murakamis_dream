The complete knot source has no regular real interior saddle

Private written deduction, 15 November 2026. No mathematical code, numerical calculation, test, or saved numerical evidence is used. Frozen notes and public manuscripts are unchanged.

The selected local clock amplitude does not initialize a positive real saddle of the complete closed source. For every nonempty braid whose closure is a knot, the full incidence action has no real interior stationary point, even after any integer Fourier character is retained. The same statement holds with the original cut degree fixed. This is an exact consequence of the source’s clock equations and integral transfer lattice; it is not an abstract cancellation example.

For alternating generator signs, the central incidence packet also has an actual midpoint-rectangle deformation with a strict exponential loss, including both cotangent lips. This local contour statement is recorded separately from the stronger scalar upper bound already available by cut peeling.

The statement does not exclude a complex geometric saddle or a source-preserving deformation to it. It identifies where such a deformation must do more than multiply the positive local clock initializers.

1. The complete source and its real interior

Use the physical sign convention of the joint clock phase. At crossing jj, let its sign be σj\sigma_j, its adjacent head index be iji_j, and its normalized physical coordinates be Uj,Vj,JjU_j,V_j,J_j. The overstrand goes from Uj+VjU_j+V_j to VjV_j, and the understrand goes from JjJ_j to Jj+UjJ_j+U_j. The clock is HjH_j. Its four normalized incidences are (1−Uj−Vj,Uj−Hj+Vj,Hj−Vj,Vj).(1) (1-U_j-V_j,\ U_j-H_j+V_j,\ H_j-V_j,\ V_j). \qquad\text{(1)} The closing row on head ii is (1−Ai,Ai)(1-A_i,A_i). All wire conservation and the original closing equations remain imposed. The real regular interior means all incidences in (1) and all closing incidences are positive.

In this interior every physical head degree lies strictly between zero and one. To check the under degrees as well as the over degrees, follow a segment from one over-output or closing event to the next over-input or closing event. Its intermediate under degrees increase by the positive transfers UjU_j. Both endpoint degrees lie in (0,1)(0,1), hence so do the intermediate ones. In particular 0<Uj<1,0<Jj<Jj+Uj<1.(2) 0<U_j<1,\qquad 0<J_j<J_j+U_j<1 . \qquad\text{(2)}

Let 𝒥(x)=∫0xLog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt(3) \mathcal J(x)=\int_0^x\operatorname{Log}(2\sin\pi t)\,dt \qquad\text{(3)} with its branch real on (0,1)(0,1). The complete leading action on the specified source lift is 𝒮=−∑all incidences e𝒥(xe)+πiφ,φ=∑j{Hj+σjHj(Uj+2Jj)−σjJj}.(4) \begin{split} \mathscr S&=-\sum_{\text{all incidences }e}\mathcal J(x_e) +\pi i\,\varphi,\\ \varphi&=\sum_j \{H_j+\sigma_jH_j(U_j+2J_j)-\sigma_jJ_j\}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(4)} This follows from the full phase (3) of the integral midpoint theorem. The exact N−1N-1 shifts and the closing pivots contribute the already specified finite amplitudes; none changes (4). The closing rows have zero entropy by 𝒥(1−A)=−𝒥(A)\mathcal J(1-A)=-\mathcal J(A).

Let LL be the homogeneous integral incidence lattice. As in the source, it is the direct sum of the closed physical-flow lattice and the independent integral clock coordinates. A real point is stationary in some integer Fourier lift only if Dℓ𝒮∈2πiℤfor every ℓ∈L.(5) D_\ell\mathscr S\in2\pi i\mathbb Z \quad\text{for every }\ell\in L. \qquad\text{(5)} For the fixed-cut version use the sublattice with dA1=0dA_1=0. Condition (5) is necessary independently of the choice of lattice basis. Subtracting any integral Fourier character changes its left side by a multiple of 2πi2\pi i.

2. Transfers are independent integral coordinates after fixing the cut

Let bb be the braid width and c>0c>0 its crossing count. Write y∈ℤby\in\mathbb Z^b for its initial head degrees and u∈ℤcu\in\mathbb Z^c for its transfers, with inequalities temporarily removed. Let PjP_j be the transposition of the two heads at crossing jj, and put ej=eij−eij+1e_j=e_{i_j}-e_{i_j+1}. The exact checked degree dynamics are yout=Pjyin+σjujej.(6) y^{\mathrm{out}}=P_jy^{\mathrm{in}} +\sigma_j u_j e_j . \qquad\text{(6)} For a positive crossing this says (I,J)↦(J+u,I−u)(I,J)\mapsto(J+u,I-u); for a negative crossing it says (J,I)↦(I−u,J+u)(J,I)\mapsto(I-u,J+u). Thus the original role conventions give (6) at both signs.

Compose these equations. With P=Pc⋯P1P=P_c\cdots P_1 and integer columns Ej=σjPc⋯Pj+1ejE_j=\sigma_jP_c\cdots P_{j+1}e_j, the closed physical-flow lattice is (I−P)y=Eu.(7) (I-P)y=Eu . \qquad\text{(7)} Every column of EE has coordinate sum zero. Because the closure is a knot, PP is a single cycle, and ker⁡ℤ(I−P)=ℤ𝟏,(I−P)ℤb=ℤ0b.(8) \ker_{\mathbb Z}(I-P)=\mathbb Z\mathbf1,\qquad (I-P)\mathbb Z^b=\mathbb Z_0^b . \qquad\text{(8)} For completeness, writing the coordinates in the order of the cycle turns (I−P)y=z(I-P)y=z into successive integral differences. They can be solved by cumulative sums exactly when ∑zi=0\sum z_i=0; their sole free value adds a constant to every coordinate. This proves both assertions in (8) without division.

Consequently every u∈ℤcu\in\mathbb Z^c has a closed integral physical flow. There is a unique one with any prescribed initial degree y1y_1. In particular, on the homogeneous fixed-cut lattice, the transfer map is an isomorphism {closed integral physical variations,dA1=0}≃ℤc.(9) \{ \text{closed integral physical variations}, \ dA_1=0\}\ \simeq\ \mathbb Z^c . \qquad\text{(9)} Every single-transfer variation duj=1,duk=0du_j=1,\ du_k=0 for k≠jk\ne j is therefore a legitimate integral tangent vector. At a strictly interior real point, small real multiples of each such vector stay feasible. The clocks can be held fixed.

3. The only possible simultaneous positive clock base slice

At a real point put Rj=Hj−VjR_j=H_j-V_j. Differentiating (4) in its independent clock direction gives ∂Hj𝒮=log⁡sin⁡π(Uj−Rj)sin⁡πRj+πi{1+σj(Uj+2Jj)}.(10) \partial_{H_j}\mathscr S =\log\frac{\sin\pi(U_j-R_j)}{\sin\pi R_j} +\pi i\{1+\sigma_j(U_j+2J_j)\}. \qquad\text{(10)} Here 0<Rj<Uj<10<R_j<U_j<1. If (5) holds in this direction, its real part is zero. The identity sin⁡π(U−R)−sin⁡πR=2cos⁡(πU/2)sin⁡π(U/2−R)(11) \sin\pi(U-R)-\sin\pi R =2\cos(\pi U/2)\sin\pi(U/2-R) \qquad\text{(11)} then gives Rj=Uj/2R_j=U_j/2. By (2), the midpoint Mj=Uj+2JjM_j=U_j+2J_j belongs to (0,2)(0,2). The imaginary part of (10) is an integral multiple of 2π2\pi only when Mj=1,Hj=Vj+Uj/2.(12) M_j=1,\qquad H_j=V_j+U_j/2 . \qquad\text{(12)} These are exactly the central local clock slices, with their Fourier characters retained.

The integral midpoint theorem identifies the kernel of p↦(Uj+2Jj)jp\mapsto(U_j+2J_j)_j for a knot braid. Its rank is one, and its initial-head vector is ε=(1,−1,1,−1,…).(13) \varepsilon=(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots). \qquad\text{(13)} This uses the independently proved Burau–Alexander minor calculation at −1-1, including even braid width. The constant flow with every head degree 1/21/2 and all transfers zero has every midpoint one. Hence every real closed physical flow with all midpoints one is that flow plus τε\tau\varepsilon. The homogeneous zero-midpoint dynamics fix ε\varepsilon at every horizontal slice.

At crossing jj the over-head component of ε\varepsilon is σjεij\sigma_j\varepsilon_{i_j}, and its under-head component is −σjεij-\sigma_j\varepsilon_{i_j}. Therefore Uj=2τσjεij,Jj=Vj=12−τσjεij,Hj=12.(14) \begin{split} U_j&=2\tau\sigma_j\varepsilon_{i_j},\\ J_j=V_j&=\frac12-\tau\sigma_j\varepsilon_{i_j},\\ H_j&=\frac12 . \end{split} \qquad\text{(14)} The last identity uses (12).

In particular the simultaneous central slice meets the strict real interior if and only if all signs σjεij\sigma_j\varepsilon_{i_j} are the same. This is precisely the alternating generator-sign pattern σj=ϵ(−1)ij−1,ϵ∈{1,−1}.(15) \sigma_j=\epsilon(-1)^{i_j-1}, \qquad \epsilon\in\{1,-1\}. \qquad\text{(15)} In this case choose τ\tau with 0<τϵ<1/20<\tau\epsilon<1/2. All transfers have one common value U=2|τ|U=2|\tau|, and every crossing row is ((1−U)/2,U/2,U/2,(1−U)/2).(16) ((1-U)/2,\ U/2,\ U/2,\ (1-U)/2). \qquad\text{(16)} The closing degrees are 1/2+τεi1/2+\tau\varepsilon_i. Thus this is a one-dimensional affine slice, not an open family of physical flows. Fixing the first cut degree fixes τ\tau as well.

4. No Fourier lift makes it a full real saddle

Theorem. For every nonempty braid with knot closure, the action (4) has no real stationary point in its regular interior in any integer Fourier lift. The same assertion holds on every fixed-cut interior slice.

Proof. A purported point must satisfy (12), and hence (14). Vary only the physical flow, holding all HjH_j fixed. Since Hj=1/2H_j=1/2, the differential of the complete phase is dφ=∑jσj{HjdUj+(2Hj−1)dJj}=12∑jσjdUj.(17) \begin{split} d\varphi &=\sum_j\sigma_j \{H_j\,dU_j+(2H_j-1)\,dJ_j\}\\ &=\frac12\sum_j\sigma_j\,dU_j . \end{split} \qquad\text{(17)} Choose the integral fixed-cut tangent in (9) with a single duj=1du_j=1. The entropy differential is real at the real point, while (17) gives Im⁡Dℓ𝒮=σjπ/2.(18) \operatorname{Im}D_\ell\mathscr S =\sigma_j\pi/2 . \qquad\text{(18)} This is not in 2πℤ2\pi\mathbb Z. Subtracting an integer Fourier character cannot change that fact. It contradicts (5), proving both versions. ▫\square

The fixed-cut qualification uses the actual operator scalarity before insertion, as in marked-cut observability. It does not replace the prescribed cut in a separate clock sector. The proof of (18) is on the whole conserved lattice and keeps the original closing equations.

The earlier alternating real-phase obstruction already excludes a real saddle through a particular consecutive opposite-sign variation of the cyclic source. The theorem above is a different, general calculation on the full singleton incidence lattice: it covers every nonempty knot braid and every regular real interior point, including every fixed cut. It does not make a claim about boundary strata.

5. The actual complex chart still available

This exclusion does not remove the complex interpolation. Let GNG_N be the exact sine-factorial function of the factorial source, with GN(a)=SaG_N(a)=S_a at the original integers. The selected clock sum has the following specified extension in all its physical variables: C̃σ,N(U,V,j)=GN(N(j+U))GN(Nj)GN(NU)exp⁡[Nσπi{V(U+2j−1)+jU+U2/2−U/2}+σπi(V+U/2)].(19) \widetilde C_{\sigma,N}(U,V,j) =\frac{G_N(N(j+U))} {G_N(Nj)G_N(NU)} \exp\left[ N\sigma\pi i\{V(U+2j-1)+jU+U^2/2-U/2\} +\sigma\pi i(V+U/2)\right]. \qquad\text{(19)} For NU=u,NV=vNU=u,\ NV=v integral, this is exactly the finite clock sum of the selected-amplitude theorem, for every complex jj in its domain. Indeed the exact functional equation of GNG_N turns its first ratio into ∏r=1u2sin⁡π(j+r/N)\prod_{r=1}^u2\sin\pi(j+r/N); the displayed phase is exactly the marked Pochhammer phase and aσ,Nva_{\sigma,N}^v. Thus the equality fixes the continuation and its marks rather than choosing a new saddle normalization.

Multiplying (19) by the remaining exact row factors gives ℛ̃σ,N=κσGN(N(U+V))GN(NV)e−σπi(N−1)jC̃σ,N.(20) \widetilde{\mathcal R}_{\sigma,N} =\kappa^\sigma \frac{G_N(N(U+V))}{G_N(NV)} e^{-\sigma\pi i(N-1)j}\, \widetilde C_{\sigma,N}. \qquad\text{(20)} This is holomorphic and nonzero on the convex tube 0<ℜU<1,0<ℜV<ℜ(U+V)<1,0<ℜj<ℜ(j+U)<1.(21) \begin{gathered} 0<\Re U<1,\qquad 0<\Re V<\Re(U+V)<1,\\ 0<\Re j<\Re(j+U)<1 . \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(21)} The exact factorial expansion supplies its nonzero local complex symbol uniformly on compact subsets, with the original finite Cartan and framing phases. Products of these row charts retain the literal physical wire equations. This is the existing factorial interpolation expressed in the coordinates of the new exact clock contraction.

The original cycle is still the sum of residue tori around the actual allowed physical lattice states, with every endpoint state included. Points on its boundary are not contained in fixed compact subsets of (21). The theorem does not transform that cycle into a geometric descending cycle or bound the side currents of such a transformation. The raw positive Fourier charts also have the independently proved sewing obstruction; switching to them does not supply the missing transport.

The conclusion for the proposed route is precise. The local clock contraction has a selected nonzero amplitude, but its positive real initializer cannot be made a full interior saddle by the actual conserved-flow equations or by a Fourier relabeling. A geometric contribution must be located on a genuinely complex chart or after further source contractions or boundary reductions. No nonzero intersection with the original residue cycle has been proved here. The additional global conditions in dominant-saddle transport remain to be established for that actual complex source.

6. Exponential cancellation of the actual central packet

For the alternating sign class (15), the source obstruction has an explicit contour realization. This gives a cancellation estimate in the singleton incidence presentation. It is not a new claim of a strict crossing-count bound: the older cyclic cut peeling theorem, Section 2, already gives a stronger scalar loss by making the first and last cut-crossing weights have modulus one. They are the same crossing if the marked position occurs only once.

Fix a nonempty knot braid satisfying (15), put a*=1/2+ϵ/4a_*=1/2+\epsilon/4, and choose rN∈{0,…,N−1}r_N\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\} with |rN−Na*|≤1/2|r_N-Na_*|\le1/2. Fix the literal first cut degree to rNr_N. The exact marked-cut theorem permits this choice for the complete sum with its original normalization. By (9), the remaining (u,h)(u,h) are independent integral coordinates, with their original inequalities.

At the limiting point z*=(U,H)=(𝟏/2,𝟏/2)z_*=(U,H)=(\mathbf{1}/2,\mathbf{1}/2), the horizontal heads are 1/2+ϵεi/41/2+\epsilon\varepsilon_i/4. Every crossing has over-input 3/43/4, under-input and over-output 1/41/4, and transfer 1/21/2. Its four incidences are all 1/41/4. The closing incidences are 1/41/4 or 3/43/4.

The strict row concavity in the angle optimization makes this the unique absolute-action maximum on the fixed-cut limiting fiber, with value Mc=−4c𝒥(1/4)=cv82π.(22) M_c=-4c\,\mathcal J(1/4) =\frac{c\,v_8}{2\pi}. \qquad\text{(22)} Here v8v_8 is the regular ideal octahedron volume. Uniqueness follows because the crossing incidences recover all Uj,HjU_j,H_j, and (9) then recovers the physical flow. Closing rows have zero entropy.

Choose a sufficiently small fixed ρ>0\rho>0 and retain the actual rectangular packet ℬN={(u,h) in the allowed fixed-cut fiber:|uj/N−1/2|≤ρ,|hj/N−1/2|≤ρ for every j}.(23) \mathcal B_N=\{(u,h)\text{ in the allowed fixed-cut fiber}: |u_j/N-1/2|\le\rho,\quad |h_j/N-1/2|\le\rho \text{ for every }j\}. \qquad\text{(23)} For large NN, the entire box is feasible, since all incidence forms are affine and strictly interior at z*z_*. Let wN=qΦN/∏eSnew_N=q^{\Phi_N}/\prod_eS_{n_e} be the complete incidence weight on this fixed-cut slice, including its original closing pivots.

Packet theorem. There are C,B,δ>0C,B,\delta>0, depending only on this fixed braid and packet, such that |∑ℬNwN|≤CNBeN(Mc−δ)(24) \left|\sum_{\mathcal B_N}w_N\right| \le C N^B e^{N(M_c-\delta)} \qquad\text{(24)} for every sufficiently large NN, with no parity or coprimality restriction. Enlarging CC covers the finitely many remaining levels as well.

Proof. Extend the exact degree forms affinely and use the specified factorial function GNG_N. Its exact reflection contracts each closing denominator: ℐN(u,h)=N−bexp⁡{πiΦN(u,h)/N}∏j a crossing0≤k≤3GN(njk(u,h))−1.(25) \mathcal I_N(u,h) =N^{-b}\exp\{\pi i\Phi_N(u,h)/N\} \prod_{\substack{j\text{ a crossing}\\0\le k\le3}} G_N(n_{jk}(u,h))^{-1}. \qquad\text{(25)} This is the original weight at all integer points. All N−1N-1 shifts, the difference rN−Na*r_N-Na_*, and the pivotal phases remain in (25). On a fixed small complex neighborhood of z*z_*, its scaled factorial arguments have real parts strictly between zero and one. The exact compact complex expansion therefore gives |ℐN(Nz)|≤CNB0eNℜ𝒮*(z),(26) |\mathcal I_N(Nz)| \le C N^{B_0}e^{N\Re\mathscr S_*(z)}, \qquad\text{(26)} where 𝒮*\mathscr S_* is (4) with cut A1=a*A_1=a_*. The bounded finite offsets change the exponent by O(1)O(1) on this compact chart. The exact finite phase shifts are also retained in the bounded factor. No leading state-dependent phase is removed.

Choose one crossing jj, set t=σjujt=\sigma_j u_j, and write x=t/Nx=t/N, centered at x*=σj/2x_*=\sigma_j/2. All other 2c−12c-1 coordinates remain real and fixed. By (17), ℑ∂x𝒮*(z*)=π/2.(27) \Im\partial_x\mathscr S_*(z_*)=\pi/2. \qquad\text{(27)} Shrink ρ\rho and choose a small fixed height β>0\beta>0 so that throughout the resulting complex box π/4≤ℑ∂x𝒮*≤3π/4.(28) \pi/4\le\Im\partial_x\mathscr S_*\le3\pi/4. \qquad\text{(28)} The two real faces |x−x*|=ρ|x-x_*|=\rho have a uniform entropy gap η>0\eta>0 below McM_c, by uniqueness and compactness. Shrink it once to include the O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) endpoint changes below, and choose (3π/4)β≤η/2(3\pi/4)\beta\le\eta/2.

If the integers tt in the packet run from ANA_N to BNB_N, take the positively oriented rectangle with vertical sides AN−1/2,BN+1/2A_N-1/2,\ B_N+1/2 and horizontal sides ℑt=±Nβ\Im t=\pm N\beta. On the upper lip the cotangent factor is bounded, and (28) lowers ℜ𝒮*\Re\mathscr S_* by at least πβ/4\pi\beta/4. On the lower lip the density increases by at most 3πβ/43\pi\beta/4, but its exact kernel obeys |cot⁡(πt)−i|≤Ce−2πNβ.(30) |\cot(\pi t)-i| \le C e^{-2\pi N\beta}. \qquad\text{(30)} The combined exponent there is at most Mc−5πβ/4M_c-5\pi\beta/4. On a half-integer vertical side, |cot⁡(πt)−i|≤2|\cot(\pi t)-i|\le2, and the entropy gap with (28) bounds the exponent by Mc−η/2M_c-\eta/2. The contour length is O(N)O(N). Equations (26)–(30) therefore bound this one-variable sum by a polynomial times eN(Mc−δ)e^{N(M_c-\delta)}, uniformly in the other coordinates, with δ=min⁡(πβ/4,η/2)>0\delta=\min(\pi\beta/4,\eta/2)>0. There are only polynomially many choices of those real integer coordinates. Summing proves (24). ▫\square

The complement of (23) has a strict absolute entropy gap below McM_c: otherwise compactness gives another maximum with some transfer or clock separated from 1/21/2. The endpoint-uniform factorial estimate and the exact margin N−1N-1 control this complement with a polynomial prefactor. Combining it with (24) recovers a strict scalar gap after multiplication by the known original external source factor. As already noted, cyclic cut peeling gives a stronger scalar bound; the additional assertion here is the explicit complex deformation and loss of the central incidence packet itself.

The cotangent kernel is essential. Its lower density may grow while its exact source factor decays. Thus the proof does not give a low-modulus deformation for all individual lattice evaluations. It retains the prescribed packet period and does not identify the surviving geometric contour. In particular δ\delta is not compared with the difference between the crossing envelope and hyperbolic volume.
