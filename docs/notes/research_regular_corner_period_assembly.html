Primitive periods through regular square corners

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

A finite regular corner can flatten four of an original edge’s eight tetrahedra without making the coupled primitive filling equations singular. In particular, for a proper selected set in a connected original graph, the Hermitian part of the complete selected period Jacobian remains positive definite when every edge is positive or has only one regular corner square. The conclusion holds for arbitrary real signed powers. This note proves the exact kernel transport when there is no fixed outside vertex, and extends the existing exact wire contour construction to these corners.

All periods, face maps, wire kernels and logarithmic lifts are the original ones. A continuation of the analytic equations through a corner is not a positive-cell decomposition or a completed hyperbolic metric beyond it. No noncancellation assertion for the quantum source is made.

1. The original edge at a regular corner

For an ordered edge with angular coordinates (t,u)(t,u), put

a=tan⁡(t/2),b=tan⁡(u/2),q2=1+a2+b2,Je=12q(1+a2−ab−ab1+b2).(1) a=\tan(t/2),\quad b=\tan(u/2),\quad q^2=1+a^2+b^2, \qquad J_e=\frac1{2q} \begin{pmatrix}1+a^2&-ab\\-ab&1+b^2\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(1)}

Here JeJ_e is the derivative of the two original meridian returns, not the Hessian of a substituted positive invariant. Its determinant is 1/41/4, and Je=−πψe″J_e=-\pi\psi_e'', with the continued original edge phase ψe\psi_e. For a symmetric complex matrix, ℜJe\Re J_e denotes its Hermitian part, equivalently its entrywise real part.

Use the original disk and strip coordinates

ζ=a+ibq+1=tanh⁡P,ξ=a−ibq+1=tanh⁡Q,q=1+ζξ1−ζξ,a=ζ+ξ1−ζξ,b=ζ−ξi(1−ζξ).(2) \zeta=\frac{a+ib}{q+1}=\tanh P,\qquad \xi=\frac{a-ib}{q+1}=\tanh Q, \quad q=\frac{1+\zeta\xi}{1-\zeta\xi},\quad a=\frac{\zeta+\xi}{1-\zeta\xi},\quad b=\frac{\zeta-\xi}{i(1-\zeta\xi)}. \qquad\text{(2)}

A positive edge has |ζ|,|ξ|<1|\zeta|,|\xi|<1. A regular single-square corner has exactly one disk coordinate on the unit circle, away from {1,−1,i,−i}\{1,-1,i,-i\}, and the other strictly inside. The pinned corner classification proves that its face chart is regular: ℜq>0\Re q>0, and all a±i,b±i,q±a,q±b,q±ia\pm i,b\pm i,q\pm a,q\pm b,q\pm i are nonzero. Four original tetrahedra are flat with distinct vertices; the other four are positive. This excludes two-square corners and all ramified or infinite limits.

Lemma 1. At a PP-corner ζ=eiθ\zeta=e^{i\theta},

ℜJe=κP(cos⁡θ−sin⁡θ)(cos⁡θ,−sin⁡θ),κP=1−|ξ|4|1−ζ2ξ2|2>0,(3) \Re J_e=\kappa_P \binom{\cos\theta}{-\sin\theta} (\cos\theta,-\sin\theta),\qquad \kappa_P=\frac{1-|\xi|^4}{|1-\zeta^2\xi^2|^2}>0, \qquad\text{(3)} kP=(sin⁡θcos⁡θ),JekP=i2(cos⁡θ−sin⁡θ).(4) k_P=\binom{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},\qquad J_e k_P=\frac i2\binom{\cos\theta}{-\sin\theta}. \qquad\text{(4)}

At a QQ-corner ξ=eiφ\xi=e^{i\varphi},

ℜJe=κQ(cos⁡φsin⁡φ)(cos⁡φ,sin⁡φ),κQ=1−|ζ|4|1−ζ2ξ2|2>0,(5) \Re J_e=\kappa_Q \binom{\cos\varphi}{\sin\varphi} (\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi),\qquad \kappa_Q=\frac{1-|\zeta|^4}{|1-\zeta^2\xi^2|^2}>0, \qquad\text{(5)} kQ=(−sin⁡φcos⁡φ),JekQ=−i2(cos⁡φsin⁡φ).(6) k_Q=\binom{-\sin\varphi}{\cos\varphi},\qquad J_e k_Q=-\frac i2\binom{\cos\varphi}{\sin\varphi}. \qquad\text{(6)}

In particular every corner kernel has two nonzero coordinates.

Proof. Substitution in (1) gives

Je=12(1−ζ2ξ2)((1+ζ2)(1+ξ2)i(ζ2−ξ2)i(ζ2−ξ2)(1−ζ2)(1−ξ2)).(7) J_e=\frac1{2(1-\zeta^2\xi^2)} \begin{pmatrix} (1+\zeta^2)(1+\xi^2)&i(\zeta^2-\xi^2)\\ i(\zeta^2-\xi^2)&(1-\zeta^2)(1-\xi^2) \end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(7)}

One can read its kernel and imaginary action without taking lengthy real parts. The original strip dictionary, with f(P)=arctan⁡tanh⁡Pf(P)=\arctan\tanh P, is

t=2(f(P)+f(Q)),u=−2i(P−Q),rt=P+Q,ru=i(f(Q)−f(P)).(8) t=2(f(P)+f(Q)),\quad u=-2i(P-Q),\quad r_t=P+Q,\quad r_u=i(f(Q)-f(P)). \qquad\text{(8)}

Consequently the real energy of a complex variation is

ℜ(δrtδt¯+δruδu¯)=4ℜf′(P)|δP|2+4ℜf′(Q)|δQ|2.(9) \Re(\delta r_t\overline{\delta t} +\delta r_u\overline{\delta u}) =4\Re f'(P)|\delta P|^2+4\Re f'(Q)|\delta Q|^2. \qquad\text{(9)}

At the PP-corner, f′(P)=−itan⁡θf'(P)=-i\tan\theta, while ℜf′(Q)>0\Re f'(Q)>0. The coordinate Jacobian in (8) has determinant 4i(f′(P)+f′(Q))≠04i(f'(P)+f'(Q))\ne0. Thus (9) is positive semidefinite of rank one. Set δQ=0\delta Q=0 and δP=icos⁡θ/2\delta P=i\cos\theta/2; (8) gives (4). At the QQ-corner set δP=0\delta P=0 and δQ=−icos⁡φ/2\delta Q=-i\cos\varphi/2, obtaining (6). The real kernel therefore determines the rank-one directions in (3) and (5). Their coefficients equal the trace, which is

tr⁡ℜJe=12ℜ(q+q−1)=ℜ1+ζ2ξ21−ζ2ξ2.(10) \operatorname{tr}\Re J_e =\tfrac12\Re(q+q^{-1}) =\Re\frac{1+\zeta^2\xi^2}{1-\zeta^2\xi^2}. \qquad\text{(10)}

At the two respective walls this is exactly κP\kappa_P or κQ\kappa_Q. The excluded four disk points make every displayed sine and cosine nonzero. ▫\square

2. The assembled kernel and the anchored theorem

Let GG be a finite graph with the original endpoint incidence, and let SS be its selected vertices. Outside angular coordinates are fixed. Each selected vertex is incident to an edge. Keep all edges incident to SS, each once, with both occurrences of a loop. Assume every such edge is positive or a regular single-square corner. Write ιew\iota_e w for its ordered endpoint vector, putting zero at an outside endpoint. The actual period and its derivative are

𝒫v=∑e∋vrv,e−invtv2,JS=D𝒫=∑eιeTJeιe−i2diag⁡(nv),nv∈ℝ.(11) \mathcal P_v=\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}-\frac{i n_vt_v}{2},\qquad J_S=D\mathcal P =\sum_e\iota_e^T J_e\iota_e-\frac i2\operatorname{diag}(n_v), \quad n_v\in\mathbb R. \qquad\text{(11)}

Form the graph of corner edges. Mark as pinned every outside vertex and every selected vertex incident to a positive edge. Orient each corner edge temporarily. Its nonzero real gain is

ge=cot⁡θ(P-corner),ge=−cot⁡φ(Q-corner).(12) g_e=\cot\theta\quad(P\text{-corner}),\qquad g_e=-\cot\varphi\quad(Q\text{-corner}). \qquad\text{(12)}

Reversing traversal replaces the gain by its reciprocal.

Theorem 2. The real kernel of ℜJS\Re J_S is exactly the space of assignments satisfying zero at all pins and whead(e)=gewtail(e)w_{\mathrm{head}(e)}=g_e w_{\mathrm{tail}(e)} on every corner edge. Each connected corner component meeting a pin has zero kernel. An unpinned connected component has a one-dimensional kernel precisely when every oriented cycle has gain product one, and has zero kernel otherwise. Its nonzero kernel generator has no zero coordinate. These statements include parallel edges; a loop imposes (1−ge)wv=0(1-g_e)w_v=0.

In particular, if GG is connected and ⌀≠S≠V(G)\varnothing\ne S\ne V(G), then

ℜJS>0.(13) \Re J_S>0. \qquad\text{(13)}

Proof. Every edge Hermitian form is nonnegative. A positive edge has zero form only when its full endpoint variation is zero. By Lemma 1 a corner has zero form exactly when its endpoint variation obeys (12). The filling term contributes no Hermitian part. Thus the kernel is exactly the stated simultaneous constraint space. Propagation from one vertex determines all values on a connected corner component. A pin forces them all to vanish. Otherwise the path values are consistent precisely when all cycle products are one, proving the dimension statement.

For (13), extend a kernel assignment by zero outside SS. Along any original graph path, a positive edge makes both endpoint values zero, and a corner propagates a zero in either direction. Every selected vertex has a path to an outside vertex. Thus the whole assignment is zero. Positive semidefiniteness now gives positive definiteness. The same reasoning applies to complex vectors by complexification. ▫\square

When the fixed outside angles are zero, a boundary edge in fact cannot be a single-square corner: (2) gives ζ=ξ\zeta=\xi if its second endpoint has b=0b=0. Under our alternatives that edge must therefore be positive. The pin proof does not require this extra observation and also allows other fixed regular angles.

On a compact family with these hypotheses, fixed graph and selected set, continuity and (13) give a uniform constant ℜJS≥γI\Re J_S\ge\gamma I, γ>0\gamma>0. This assertion excludes approach to two-square corners or any of the specified singular limits. No constant uniform over arbitrary graphs is claimed.

3. The exact imaginary obstruction when there is no anchor

The gain criterion also determines when a null direction of the Hermitian part can be a null direction of the complex Jacobian. Let 𝒦=ker⁡ℝℜJS\mathcal K=\ker_{\mathbb R}\Re J_S. Define a real skew matrix KK on selected vertices by adding, for each oriented corner edge v→wv\to w,

Kvw=+1,Kwv=−1(P-corner),Kvw=−1,Kwv=+1(Q-corner).(14) K_{vw}=+1,\ K_{wv}=-1\quad(P\text{-corner}),\qquad K_{vw}=-1,\ K_{wv}=+1\quad(Q\text{-corner}). \qquad\text{(14)}

Contributions are summed; the two entries of a loop cancel. The ordered edge chart fixes whether (14) is the P or Q case.

Proposition 3. For w∈𝒦w\in\mathcal K,

JSw=i2(K−diag⁡n)w,(15) J_Sw=\frac i2(K-\operatorname{diag}n)w, \qquad\text{(15)} ker⁡ℂJS={w∈𝒦⊗ℂ:(K−diag⁡n)w=0}.(16) \ker_{\mathbb C}J_S= \{w\in\mathcal K\otimes\mathbb C: (K-\operatorname{diag}n)w=0\}. \qquad\text{(16)}

Consequently each unpinned gain-balanced component can be tested using its single real generator hh. A necessary condition for singularity on that component is

∑vnvhv2=0.(17) \sum_v n_vh_v^2=0. \qquad\text{(17)}

If on each such component all powers have one weak sign and at least one is nonzero, JSJ_S is nonsingular.

Proof. Equations (4) and (6) give (15) edge by edge; positive edges vanish on 𝒦\mathcal K. If JSz=0J_Sz=0, then z*(ℜJS)z=0z^*(\Re J_S)z=0, whence z∈𝒦⊗ℂz\in\mathcal K\otimes\mathbb C. Equation (15) proves (16). The residual matrix is real, so a complex kernel exists precisely when a real one exists. Distinct unpinned corner components do not couple in (15). On one such component a nonzero vector is a multiple of hh; multiply its residual equation by hTh^T and use hTKh=0h^TKh=0 to obtain (17). All entries of hh are nonzero, giving the sign conclusion. ▫\square

This last conclusion gives a nonsingular complex Jacobian. It does not give strict horizontal descent when 𝒦≠0\mathcal K\ne0.

4. Marked continuation and local descent

Retain the original logarithms from a supplied positive germ up to the regular corner, and their local continuation there. The original face denominators and (1) are regular, so this continuation exists without resetting any logarithmic period. With cone charges cvc_v, the original reduced phase is

F=∑eψe(tv,tw)+i4π∑v∈Snvtv2−i∑v∈Scvtv,F′=−𝒫/π−ic,−F″=JS/π.(18) F=\sum_e\psi_e(t_v,t_w) +\frac i{4\pi}\sum_{v\in S}n_vt_v^2 -i\sum_{v\in S}c_vt_v, \qquad F'=-\mathcal P/\pi-ic, \qquad -F''=J_S/\pi. \qquad\text{(18)}

At a supplied solution 𝒫=−πic\mathcal P=-\pi ic, Theorem 2 for a proper selected set gives a unique local holomorphic solution of the primitive equations as their parameters vary through the wall. In a sufficiently small convex neighborhood the Hermitian part remains positive definite, even on the side where some original tetrahedra have become negatively oriented. If ℜJS≥γI\Re J_S\ge\gamma I there, then for real hh in that neighborhood,

ℜF(t*+h)≤ℜF(t*)−γ2π|h|2.(19) \Re F(t_*+h)\le\Re F(t_*)-\frac\gamma{2\pi}|h|^2. \qquad\text{(19)}

This follows by integrating (18) twice on the horizontal segment. The same positivity proves local injectivity of the period map by integrating its Jacobian along complex straight segments. The compact-family bound after Theorem 2 supplies uniform local coercivity; the usual finite subcover argument supplies local continuation neighborhoods on a compact family of marked solutions.

For actual integral fillings, the original carrier ηv\eta_v and coefficient pvp_v give nv=ηv−2pvn_v=\eta_v-2p_v, and

Uv=2∑e∋vrv,e−iηvtv,Vv=2itv,Uv+pvVv=−2πicv.(20) U_v=2\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}-i\eta_vt_v,\qquad V_v=2it_v,\qquad U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi ic_v. \qquad\text{(20)}

Thus the continuation is of the marked primitive period, including the carrier. It is not merely an exponentiated slope equation. It does not assert that an arbitrary short hyperbolic filling reaches this configuration, or that the continued representation is discrete and faithful beyond it.

5. An exact wire contour through the regular corner

The product-contour theorem has a useful extension at these same points. This statement uses the original same-index wire phase; reversed wires require their own retained phase dictionary.

Lemma 4. If (a,b)(a,b) is a regular single-square corner, every proper independent contraction (λa,μb)(\lambda a,\mu b), 0≤λ,μ≤10\le\lambda,\mu\le1, is positive. The unchanged endpoint retains ℜq>0\Re q>0.

Proof. The canonical angles still obey |ℜt|,|ℜu|<π|\Re t|,|\Re u|<\pi, by (8). Thus a,b∈𝒜=ℂ\(i[1,∞)∪−i[1,∞))a,b\in\mathcal A=\mathbb C\setminus(i[1,\infty)\cup-i[1,\infty)). Write a=A+iα,b=B+iβa=A+i\alpha,b=B+i\beta, and X=A2+B2,C=Aβ−BαX=A^2+B^2,\ C=A\beta-B\alpha. The boundary of the positive-edge criterion at this regular point is C2=XC^2=X. Here X>0X>0: otherwise both tangents are purely imaginary and the boundary would have q=0q=0, contrary to ℜq>0\Re q>0. Moreover A,B≠0A,B\ne0. For example, if A=0A=0 and B≠0B\ne0, equality implies α2=1\alpha^2=1, excluded by a∈𝒜a\in\mathcal A.

For λμ>0\lambda\mu>0,

λ2A2+μ2B2−λ2μ2C2=λ2(1−μ2)A2+μ2(1−λ2)B2>0(21) \lambda^2A^2+\mu^2B^2-\lambda^2\mu^2C^2 =\lambda^2(1-\mu^2)A^2+\mu^2(1-\lambda^2)B^2>0 \qquad\text{(21)}

unless λ=μ=1\lambda=\mu=1. The strict positive-edge criterion therefore holds at every such proper contraction. If one factor is zero, the other tangent stays in 𝒜\mathcal A; the complete-end edge is positive there. ▫\square

For a target graph made of positive edges and regular single-square corners, Lemma 4 and the existing positive contraction lemma show that every pair on the finite arms [−av*,av*]×[−aw*,aw*][-a_v^*,a_v^*]\times[-a_w^*,a_w^*] has discriminant 1+a2+b21+a^2+b^2 off (−∞,0](-\infty,0]. At unchanged corner pairs this follows from ℜq>0\Re q>0; sign changes preserve the square. Pairs on (ℝ∪[−av*,av*])×(ℝ∪[−aw*,aw*])(\mathbb R\cup[-a_v^*,a_v^*])\times (\mathbb R\cup[-a_w^*,a_w^*]) have the same property: if one tangent is real, the other belongs to 𝒜\mathcal A, and a real nonpositive discriminant is impossible.

The explicit compact-and-tail neighborhood proof in the pinned product-contour theorem now applies without change to this skeleton. Namely, take the union of a sufficiently thin horizontal strip and a sufficiently thin neighborhood of each finite arm. On real tails the real part of the discriminant is positive; on the remaining compact pairs its distance from the excluded ray is positive. These give symmetric star-shaped tangent domains and simply connected angular domains VvV_v, chosen independently of NN, containing the full intervals (−π,π)(-\pi,\pi) and the corner target. On their product,

ℜqe>0,cot⁡(πxe*)=qe,0<ℜxe*<12,He=2πqe(1+qe2)2(1+av2)(1+aw2)≠0.(22) \Re q_e>0,\qquad \cot(\pi x_e^*)=q_e, \quad 0<\Re x_e^*<\tfrac12, \qquad H_e=\frac{2\pi q_e(1+q_e^2)^2} {(1+a_v^2)(1+a_w^2)}\ne0. \qquad\text{(22)}

The branches are even in each angle and agree with the original ones along the contraction to completeness.

Corollary 5. Suppose the traced target angles have negative imaginary parts. The exact full-Hermite joint factorial-lens source has a representative whose fixed-endpoint wire paths pass through the stationary graph (22), including this regular corner. Every original coupon, normal, free boundary polynomial and framing factor is retained. No wire seam current is introduced.

Proof. Put bN=−1/(2N)b_N=-1/(2N), N≥2N\ge2. The pinned exact factorial theorem gives holomorphy of both direct and reflected halfwires on compact subsets of ℜx∈[bN,bN+1],|ℜt|<π\Re x\in[b_N,b_N+1],\ |\Re t|<\pi, without a bound on their finite imaginary heights. Choose full lower angular arcs in VvV_v through the targets and retain the exact lens trace. For each angular tuple replace the horizontal wire interval by

bN+ihe→xe*(tv,tw)→bN+1+ihe.(23) b_N+ih_e\longrightarrow x_e^*(t_v,t_w) \longrightarrow b_N+1+ih_e. \qquad\text{(23)}

The real parts stay inside the same convex interval. Straight interpolation gives a compact holomorphic homotopy fixing both endpoints. Cauchy’s theorem on each fiber, followed by the retained angular integrals, proves exact equality. Angular derivatives of the wire paths wedge to zero against the angular volume form. This is the proof of the pinned exact source theorem, with the extended product domain now justified by Lemma 4. In particular it retains the odd simple node and all double-node normals. ▫\square

At a marked primitive critical point covered by Theorem 2, these paths can include coherent local wire coordinates with Fe=ψe−ξe2/2F_e=\psi_e-\xi_e^2/2, since He≠0H_e\ne0. Real ξe\xi_e and horizontal selected angular coordinates give, by (18)–(19), a strictly descending local contour for the continued original selected phase. The square-root construction of those coordinates is exactly the one in the pinned unsummed-source proof. It remains valid at the corner because every factor in (22) is a unit. Small connectors join this local segment to (23) without moving its endpoints.

This last stationary interpretation requires the same-index phase at each endpoint; the exact identity with a true free boundary polynomial does not identify its asymptotic phase with a factorial phase. Nor does holomorphy of the exact reciprocal factorials imply their nonvanishing. All factorial zeros, connectors, other angular sectors and coherent trace terms remain present. No estimate here shows they are smaller or prevents cancellation of the local term.

Direct sources and scope of the extension

The earlier positive-domain theorems establish strict positivity before a corner. The new assertions are the rank-one boundary identities, their exact graph kernel and skew-action tests, the automatic anchored coercivity, and the extension of the existing exact contour construction to regular single-square corners.
