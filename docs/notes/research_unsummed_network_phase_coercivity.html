Coercivity of the network phase with its wire variables retained

Research proof, 3 November 2026. Frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The complete-end contribution used in the earlier mixed-phase calculation is absent at a general interior vertex. The appropriate object is therefore the Hessian of each original incidence, followed by the Hessian of their actual sum. This gives an arbitrary-graph concavity theorem on an explicit contour domain. Outside that domain there is an exact network test, and positive geometry still gives an adapted local contour. None of these phase statements chooses a coefficient in the original finite quantum cycle.

1. The original incidence matrix

Let GG be a finite graph. Every edge ee has one retained wire variable xex_e. A selected vertex vv has an angular variable tvt_v, a real filling power nvn_v, and the original unit charge. An outside vertex has a fixed angle uvu_v. Count an edge at each of its endpoints. Throughout this note an edge has the same-index kernel KT(z,w)=∑j=0N−1ej(z)ej(w). K_T(z,w)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e_j(z)e_j(w). This is the kernel on the original circle-tree paths. The theorems below do not silently replace those edges by KTK_T. Such a replacement would need a separate exact source reduction. 3. Assembly on a finite graph

Fix Ye,Sv≥0Y_e,S_v\ge0. Require (8) at each incidence, interpreting the coordinates of an outside vertex as fixed. In particular a complete outside vertex has bv=1/2,Sv=0b_v=1/2,S_v=0 and is allowed. Proof. Extend each incidence matrix by zero in all other coordinates and sum. Its quadratic form is at least πmQ[(δae)2+(δbv)2]\pi m_Q[(\delta a_e)^2+(\delta b_v)^2]; a fixed outside coordinate has δbv=0\delta b_v=0. This is a phase theorem for graphs of arbitrary size. It does not assert that a geometric point for every filling power lies in (17). For example, the homogeneous positive degree-two power-three point has ℜt>π/2\Re t>\pi/2, hence b<1/4b<1/4. Nor does a statement valid for every real nvn_v on this fixed contour say that the selected geometry of mixed signs has these same heights.

4. The exact network test outside this chamber

The full matrix is also explicit without (8). Thus a further general extension has a precise target: prove (22), or construct weights in (23), on a specified connected family of original contour domains. Positive geometry after wire elimination alone does not prove (22).

There is an explicit regular positive edge where (22) fails. Take the exact angles t=2110−3i4,r=34−3i20.(24) t=\frac{21}{10}-\frac{3i}{4},\qquad r=\frac34-\frac{3i}{20}. \qquad\text{(24)} At its stationary wire, all original factorial arguments have real parts in (0,1)(0,1), and all eight original tetrahedra have positive imaginary parts. The exact-interval checker and saved bounds verify these strict inequalities, the original logarithmic stationary equation, and positivity of the reduced edge Hessian. This is a counterexample to an implication from positive edge geometry to fixed-horizontal concavity. It is not asserted to satisfy any integer unit-filling equation.

Proposition 4. At any regular positive geometric critical point of a finite graph, there is a local contour retaining every wire on which the real phase has a strictly negative Hessian. The contour keeps the imaginary angular coordinates fixed and takes all the coordinates ξe\xi_e in (29) real.

Proof. The coordinate changes in (29) have nonzero holomorphic Jacobians. Accordingly a nonzero original analytic amplitude remains nonzero under this local change of variables. This does not establish that the original finite integration cycle has a nonzero coefficient on this contour, nor that its other pieces are smaller.

The distinction between (22) and (30) matters. The former tests a fixed horizontal contour for the unsummed phase. The latter changes the wire directions with the angular variables. A complex holomorphic Schur complement does not imply the real-matrix inequality (22). The adapted contour proves local coercivity without making that invalid inference.
