Positive path solutions throughout the fractional-charge cube

Research note, 3 November 2026.

Fix a finite path and its original integer crossing powers, all at least three. The unit-charge positive solution extends uniquely when each filling charge is allowed to vary independently between zero and one. A zero charge makes that vertex complete and separates the positive-charge components. No lower bound on a nonzero charge is required in the statement.

The proof has two inputs already established in the repository. The integer corridor theorem, Theorem 15 and its mirror, supplies the unit-charge solutions. The fractional-charge height bound bounds every positive solution which already exists. That bound alone does not give existence. Here we prove the missing closedness argument: no angle endpoint, zero height, open square side, or square corner can be reached while all charges stay positive.

The integer-power hypothesis is retained. The proof does not interpolate the crossing powers through nonintegers, and it does not assert a quantum asymptotic.

1. The path, equations and conclusion

Retain every original edge, including its prescribed carrier and primitive marking. At a selected vertex fix an integer nv≥3n_v\ge3, and let its charge be λv∈[0,1]\lambda_v\in[0,1]. At an outside vertex set t=0t=0.

At a zero charge the unique solution has tv=0t_v=0 and rv,e=0r_{v,e}=0 at every incidence of that vertex. The remaining positive-charge components have the solutions just described, with these zero-charge vertices treated as complete boundaries. Every original edge remains present.

This theorem concerns the positive logarithmic equations and their convex minima. At unit charges, or at a Boolean face where every nonzero charge is one, the existing pointwise marked-completion theorem applies. No additional topological completion statement for fractional charges is needed or claimed here.

2. Strict feasibility and local continuation

For every Boolean vector ϵ∈{0,1}V\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{V}, make its zero-charge vertices complete. Each remaining selected component is a path with a complete outside end. Solve it by the unit-charge corridor theorem and its mirror. Joining these solutions retains every original edge. At a zero-charge vertex, arsinh⁡(tan⁡(0)cos⁡(tw/2))=0\operatorname{arsinh}(\tan(0)\cos(t_w/2))=0, so both equations in (3) hold there. A complete edge has two interior squares. Thus these finitely many assignments XϵX^\epsilon are strictly feasible in the common real angle domain.

Their convex combination with weights pϵ(λ)=∏vλvϵv(1−λv)1−ϵv(9) p_\epsilon(\lambda)= \prod_v\lambda_v^{\epsilon_v}(1-\lambda_v)^{1-\epsilon_v} \qquad\text{(9)} is strictly feasible at the charge λ\lambda. Indeed the constraints in (3) are affine in the real coordinates and in λ\lambda, and the marginal mean of ϵv\epsilon_v is λv\lambda_v. The edge inequalities define a convex domain. Since the finite list XϵX^\epsilon lies in its interior, all these convex combinations stay a fixed positive distance from every square side. The closed feasible sets are compact and nonempty, so a constrained minimum exists at every charge.

At an interior critical point this minimum is unique and locally real analytic in λ\lambda. One can see the required nondegeneracy directly from (7). With Δ=1−sin⁡2(2A)sin⁡2(2y)>0\Delta=1-\sin^2(2A)\sin^2(2y)>0, the square Hessian is ∇2E=1Δ(cos⁡(2y)/cos⁡(2A)−sin⁡(2A)sin⁡(2y)−sin⁡(2A)sin⁡(2y)cos⁡(2A)/cos⁡(2y)).(10) \nabla^2E= \frac1{\sqrt\Delta} \begin{pmatrix} \cos(2y)/\cos(2A)&-\sin(2A)\sin(2y)\\ -\sin(2A)\sin(2y)&\cos(2A)/\cos(2y) \end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(10)} It has determinant one and positive diagonal entries. The map from vertex/incidence coordinates to all square coordinates is injective. Therefore the total Hessian is positive definite on the real variable space, and in particular on the tangent space of the constraints. Those constraints have independent rows: the variable ava_v has coefficient −nv/2-n_v/2 in its own row and occurs in no other constraint. The bordered critical-point system is consequently invertible. The implicit function theorem gives local analytic continuation.

The unit-charge solution has the signs in (8). They persist locally. To prove existence for an arbitrary positive target charge it remains to show that this positive solution cannot reach a boundary along the segment joining the unit vector to that target.

4. Neither an angle endpoint nor a zero height can occur

The arguments in this section concern a possible limit from Section 3. The complete outside vertices remain fixed.

First, the pair (av,Hv)=(π,0)(a_v,H_v)=(\pi,0) is impossible. If it occurred, |tan⁡(tv/2)||\tan(t_v/2)| would tend to infinity. 6. Completing the continuation and adding zero charges

Fix a target vector with every charge positive, and join it to the unit vector by a straight segment. Let 𝒰\mathcal U be the set of segment parameters admitting a positive solution with the strict signs in (8). It contains the unit endpoint. Section 2 makes it relatively open.

Take any convergent sequence of parameters in 𝒰\mathcal U. All their charges have a common positive lower bound. Section 3 gives a compact limiting assignment with bounded square subgradients. Section 4 excludes every angle endpoint and zero height. There are no open square sides, and Section 5 excludes all corners. Thus the limit is interior and has the required strict signs. Its stationarity equations pass to the limit, so it is a positive solution at the limiting parameter. The height theorem applies again and gives the strict bound Hv<23/20H_v<23/20. Hence 𝒰\mathcal U is also relatively closed. Connectedness of the segment proves existence at its target.

The interior stationary solution is the global constrained minimum by convexity. It is unique: any distinct closed feasible point can be joined to it by a segment whose interior is in the open angle domain, where the energy is strictly convex. The stationary first variation is zero along that segment, so the other point cannot have the same minimum energy. This also proves uniqueness of the continued branch.

Now allow zero charges. Make every zero-charge vertex complete and solve each remaining positive-charge component by the result just proved. Each component has at least one complete boundary vertex. Join them with the original edges. At a zero-charge vertex all its returns are zero, so (2), the matching conditions, and the stationarity equations are exact. Every square is interior, including squares on complete edges. The same convex argument identifies this assignment as the unique closed minimum.

Finally, continuity at the cube faces follows directly from the variational problem. For a sequence λj→λ\lambda_j\to\lambda, take a convergent subsequence of its minimizing real coordinates. Its limit is feasible at λ\lambda. If ZZ is any feasible point there and YY is the strictly feasible point from (9), then Zϵ=(1−ϵ)Z+ϵYZ_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)Z+\epsilon Y is interior. The coordinate correction δav=2πnv(λj,v−λv),δgv,e=0(24) \delta a_v=\frac{2\pi}{n_v} (\lambda_{j,v}-\lambda_v), \qquad \delta g_{v,e}=0 \qquad\text{(24)} makes its constraints exactly those at λj\lambda_j. For fixed ϵ>0\epsilon>0 this correction stays in the open angle domain for all sufficiently large jj. The minimum at λj\lambda_j has no larger energy than this competitor. Pass first to the limit in jj, then let ϵ↓0\epsilon\downarrow0. The limiting point minimizes at λ\lambda, and hence equals its unique minimum. Every subsequence has that limit, proving continuity on the full cube. The interior analytic dependence was established in Section 2. This completes the proof of Theorem 1.

The result supplies a genuine family of positive stationary solutions for varying charges, with the original powers fixed. It does not prove that a corresponding quantum saddle has a nonzero coefficient. That remains an analytic question about the original finite source.

7. Differentiating the energy along the actual charge family

Let F(λ)F(\lambda) be the minimum in Theorem 1. The new existence statement justifies integration along actual stationary solutions, without assuming that intermediate charges remain geometric. That assumption has now been proved.

Corollary 2.
