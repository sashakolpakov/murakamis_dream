The literal charged tetrahedron inverse and three-face bubble

Private written proof, 17 November 2026. We reconstruct the inversion and bubble in Kashaev’s 1994 definitions [K], including their finite normalization sums. Neither the printed inversion identity nor CG duality is used as a proof premise. This is the charged face-state identity at odd level, not yet the original marked-source adapter for deleting a free vertex.

1. The specified functions and regular data

Let N>2N>2 be odd, ω\omega a primitive NN-th root, and interpret 1/21/2 modulo NN, as in [K]. All finite indices are in ℤ/Nℤ\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z. Choose nonzero roots

P=xp,Q=xq,R=xr,B=xpq,C=xqr,H=xpqr, P=x_p,\quad Q=x_q,\quad R=x_r,\quad B=x_{pq},\quad C=x_{qr},\quad H=x_{pqr}, BN=PN+QN,CN=QN+RN,HN=PN+QN+RN.(1) B^N=P^N+Q^N,\qquad C^N=Q^N+R^N,\qquad H^N=P^N+Q^N+R^N. \qquad\text{(1)}

Thus the three individual central parameters and all the partial sums displayed in (1) are nonzero. We work on a lifted regular parameter domain with fixed choices of these roots. Put

⟨U,V⟩=UN−VNN(U−V),⟨U⟩=UN−1, \langle U,V\rangle=\frac{U^N-V^N}{N(U-V)},\qquad \langle U\rangle=U^{N-1}, κ*=⟨B,Q⟩1/2⟨H,R⟩1/2⟨C,R⟩1/2⟨H,C⟩1/2.(2) \kappa_*= \frac{\langle B,Q\rangle^{1/2}\langle H,R\rangle^{1/2} \langle C,R\rangle^{1/2}} {\langle H,C\rangle^{1/2}}. \qquad\text{(2)}

The same square-root choices in (2) are used in both normalizations below. None of its brackets vanishes under (1). Independently changing the sign of only one normalization is not permitted.

For xN+yN=zNx^N+y^N=z^N with xyz≠0xyz\ne0, define the periodic sequence

W(x,y,z;k)=∏j=1[k]yz−xωj,w(x,y,z;i,j)=W(x,y,z;i−j)ωj2/2.(3) W(x,y,z;k)=\prod_{j=1}^{[k]}\frac{y}{z-x\omega^j}, \qquad w(x,y,z;i,j)=W(x,y,z;i-j)\omega^{j^2/2}. \qquad\text{(3)}

The cyclic product is one, so the recurrence extends (3) consistently to every integer. Also write

v(u;k)=∏j=1k(1−uωj)−1(0≤k<N),f(u,v∣z)=∑k=0N−1v(u;k)v(v;k)zk,zN=1−uN1−vN.(4) v(u;k)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1-u\omega^j)^{-1}\quad(0\le k<N),\qquad f(u,v\mid z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{v(u;k)}{v(v;k)}z^k, \qquad z^N=\frac{1-u^N}{1-v^N}. \qquad\text{(4)}

In (4) the letter vv inside ff is a parameter; v(u;k)v(u;k) is the displayed one-argument product. Extend that product to all integers by v(u;k+1)=v(u;k)/(1−uωk+1)v(u;k+1)=v(u;k)/(1-u\omega^{k+1}). Then v(u;k+N)=v(u;k)/(1−uN)v(u;k+N)=v(u;k)/(1-u^N); the summand in (4), rather than either product separately, is cyclic.

Set

u=RH,v=RωC,z=BCHQ,ρ=κ*f(u,v∣z),ρ‾=κ*f(ωv,u/ω∣z−1).(5) u=\frac RH,\quad v=\frac{R}{\omega C},\quad z=\frac{BC}{HQ},\qquad \rho=\kappa_* f(u,v\mid z),\qquad \bar\rho=\kappa_* f(\omega v,u/\omega\mid z^{-1}). \qquad\text{(5)}

These are exactly [K, (1.25),(1.29)], including the three numerator square roots in (2). The bar is a label, not complex conjugation. The literal two matrices are [K, (1.24),(1.28)]:

ℛα,βγ,δ=ρCN−1ωαδw(HQ,PR,BC;γ,α)δβ,γ+δ, \mathcal R^{\gamma,\delta}_{\alpha,\beta} =\frac{\rho}{C^{N-1}}\, \omega^{\alpha\delta} w(HQ,PR,BC;\gamma,\alpha)\, \delta_{\beta,\gamma+\delta}, ℛ¯γ,δα,β=ρ‾BN−1δβ,γ+δωαδw(HQ/ω,PR,BC;γ,α).(6) \overline{\mathcal R}^{\alpha,\beta}_{\gamma,\delta} =\frac{\bar\rho}{B^{N-1}}\, \frac{\delta_{\beta,\gamma+\delta}} {\omega^{\alpha\delta} w(HQ/\omega,PR,BC;\gamma,\alpha)}. \qquad\text{(6)}

Every denominator in these finite definitions is nonzero on (1). The apparently additional denominators used in the calculations below are nonzero there as well: in particular (HQ)N−(BC)N=−PNRN(HQ)^N-(BC)^N=-P^NR^N, and HN−CN=PNH^N-C^N=P^N.

2. The complete finite normalization product

We prove the identity needed for (5) without importing a transformation formula for ff. Temporarily use arbitrary regular u,v,zu,v,z satisfying (4), and put

ak=v(u;k)v(v;k)zk,bk=v(ωv;k)v(u/ω;k)z−k,C0=(1−ωv)(1−u). a_k=\frac{v(u;k)}{v(v;k)}z^k,\qquad b_k=\frac{v(\omega v;k)}{v(u/\omega;k)}z^{-k},\qquad C_0=(1-\omega v)(1-u).

The two sequences are periodic. Direct cancellation of their products gives, including the cyclic seam,

bk=gkak,gk=C0(1−vωk+1)(1−uωk).(7) b_k=\frac{g_k}{a_k},\qquad g_k=\frac{C_0}{(1-v\omega^{k+1})(1-u\omega^k)}. \qquad\text{(7)}

Regroup the product of their two sums by n=j−kn=j-k. For 1≤n<N1\le n<N, their recurrence gives

ak+nakgk=C0zn∏j=2n(1−vωk+j)∏j=0n(1−uωk+j).(8) \frac{a_{k+n}}{a_k}g_k =C_0z^n \frac{\prod_{j=2}^{n}(1-v\omega^{k+j})} {\prod_{j=0}^{n}(1-u\omega^{k+j})}. \qquad\text{(8)}

Multiply numerator and denominator by ∏j=n+1N−1(1−uωk+j)\prod_{j=n+1}^{N-1}(1-u\omega^{k+j}). The denominator becomes 1−uN1-u^N. The numerator is a polynomial in ωk\omega^k of degree N−2N-2, with constant coefficient one. Summing over kk kills every nonconstant monomial. Thus

∑kak+nakgk=NC0zn1−uN(1≤n<N).(9) \sum_k\frac{a_{k+n}}{a_k}g_k =\frac{NC_0z^n}{1-u^N}\qquad(1\le n<N). \qquad\text{(9)}

For n=0n=0, partial fractions and the finite geometric-root sum give

∑kgk=NC0ωv−u(ωv1−vN−u1−uN).(10) \sum_k g_k =\frac{NC_0}{\omega v-u} \left(\frac{\omega v}{1-v^N} -\frac{u}{1-u^N}\right). \qquad\text{(10)}

Here ∑k(1−tωk)−1=N/(1−tN)\sum_k(1-t\omega^k)^{-1}=N/(1-t^N), obtained by multiplying by 1−tN1-t^N and summing the finite geometric series. Combining (9),(10), and using (4), proves

f(u,v∣z)f(ωv,u/ω∣z−1)=NC01−uN{ωvzN−uωv−u+z−zN1−z}=N(1−ωv)(1−u)(1−zN)(ωvz−u)(1−uN)(1−z)(ωv−u).(11) \begin{split} f(u,v\mid z)f(\omega v,u/\omega\mid z^{-1}) &=\frac{NC_0}{1-u^N} \left\{\frac{\omega v z^N-u}{\omega v-u} +\frac{z-z^N}{1-z}\right\}\\ &=\frac{N(1-\omega v)(1-u)(1-z^N)(\omega vz-u)} {(1-u^N)(1-z)(\omega v-u)}. \qquad\text{(11)} \end{split}

This proof first uses the displayed nonzero denominators; the identity also extends wherever its literal finite sides are regular. No such extension is needed for the parameters in (5).

For those parameters, substitution of (1) gives explicitly

κ*2=BNQN(H−C)N2(B−Q)(H−R)(C−R), \kappa_*^2= \frac{B^NQ^N(H-C)} {N^2(B-Q)(H-R)(C-R)}, f(u,v∣z)f(ωv,u/ω∣z−1)=−NPNRN(C−R)(H−R)(B−Q)BNQN(HQ−BC)(H−C). f(u,v\mid z)f(\omega v,u/\omega\mid z^{-1}) = -\frac{NP^NR^N(C-R)(H-R)(B-Q)} {B^NQ^N(HQ-BC)(H-C)}.

Consequently the full normalization, including its sign, is

ρρ‾=(PR)NN(BC−HQ).(12) \rho\bar\rho =\frac{(PR)^N}{N(BC-HQ)}. \qquad\text{(12)}

3. Raw ww orthogonality and the actual inverse

We now multiply the two matrices (6). Their Kronecker symbols first give β=β′\beta=\beta'. In (ℛ¯ℛ)α′,β′α,β(\overline{\mathcal R}\mathcal R)^{\alpha,\beta} _{\alpha',\beta'}, put n=[α′−α]n=[\alpha'-\alpha], k=γ−αk=\gamma-\alpha, and, for this calculation only, x=HQ,y=PR,z=BCx=HQ,y=PR,z=BC. Apart from an index-dependent phase independent of kk, the remaining sum is

∑k=0N−1ω−nkW(x,y,z;k−n)W(x/ω,y,z;k).(13) \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\omega^{-nk} \frac{W(x,y,z;k-n)}{W(x/\omega,y,z;k)}. \qquad\text{(13)}

For 1≤n<N1\le n<N, the ratio in (13) is exactly

z−xyn∏j=1n−1(z−xωk−j).(14) \frac{z-x}{y^n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(z-x\omega^{k-j}). \qquad\text{(14)}

It is a polynomial in ωk\omega^k of degree n−1n-1. After the factor ω−nk\omega^{-nk}, none of its powers is divisible by NN; hence (13) is zero. For n=0n=0, the ratio is (z−x)/(z−xωk)(z-x)/(z-x\omega^k), so (13) equals

NzN−1(z−x)yN.(15) \frac{Nz^{N-1}(z-x)}{y^N}. \qquad\text{(15)}

The phase omitted in describing (13) is one when n=0n=0. Multiplying (15) by the prefactor ρρ‾/(BN−1CN−1)\rho\bar\rho/(B^{N-1}C^{N-1}), and using (12), gives one. Thus

ℛ¯ℛ=I,ℛℛ¯=I.(16) \overline{\mathcal R}\mathcal R=I,\qquad \mathcal R\overline{\mathcal R}=I. \qquad\text{(16)}

The second equality follows because the matrices are finite square matrices. This establishes the actual inverse with its primary normalization, rather than naming a matrix “dual” and assuming (16).

4. Charged tensors, the face contraction, and the third trace

On the face-index space let Z|j⟩=ωj|j⟩Z|j\rangle=\omega^j|j\rangle, X|j⟩=|j+1⟩X|j\rangle=|j+1\rangle, and Y=ω1/2XZY=\omega^{1/2}XZ. In the same branches as above, the primary charged tensor and its opposite-orientation tensor are

T(a,c)=ωac/2CN−1Y1−aZ1−cℛZ1cZ2−a, T(a,c)= \omega^{ac/2}C^{N-1} Y_1^{-a}Z_1^{-c}\mathcal R Z_1^cZ_2^{-a}, T‾(a,c)=ω−ac/2BN−1Z2−aZ1cℛ¯Z1−cY1−a.(17) \bar T(a,c)= \omega^{-ac/2}B^{N-1} Z_2^{-a}Z_1^c\overline{\mathcal R}Z_1^{-c}Y_1^{-a}. \qquad\text{(17)}

The first line is [K, (3.1)]. To check the second against the conjugation definition (3.2), let 𝖢|j⟩=|−j⟩\mathsf C|j\rangle=|-j\rangle. Then 𝖢Z𝖢=Z−1\mathsf C Z\mathsf C=Z^{-1} and 𝖢Y𝖢=Y−1\mathsf C Y\mathsf C=Y^{-1}. Conjugating the finite product at indices (−i,−j)(-i,-j) changes w(x,y,z;−i,−j)w(x,y,z;-i,-j) to 1/w(x/ω,y,z;i,j)1/w(x/\omega,y,z;i,j), with the roots of the conjugate parameters chosen compatibly. For its scalar, replacing kk by −k-k in the periodic summand gives

fω−1(u,v∣z)=fω(v/ω,u/ω∣z−1). f_{\omega^{-1}}(u,v\mid z) =f_\omega(v/\omega,u/\omega\mid z^{-1}).

Applied to (5) with the conjugated root of unity, this is precisely ρ‾\bar\rho. The same square-root choices are continued from a compatible real parameter anchor. These finite identities give (17), including its scalar BN−1B^{N-1}, from the primary definition. They do not choose independent normalization roots after contraction.

Write the tetrahedral edge charges as cijc_{ij}. The four vertex sums 1/21/2 imply

c01=c23=a,c12=c03=c,c02=c13=12−a−c.(18) c_{01}=c_{23}=a,\qquad c_{12}=c_{03}=c,\qquad c_{02}=c_{13}=\tfrac12-a-c. \qquad\text{(18)}

Since NN is odd these deductions and halves are legitimate. The primary inverse-pair conditions are

c13+c13′=1,c01+c01′=0. c_{13}+c'_{13}=1,\qquad c_{01}+c'_{01}=0.

By (18) they are exactly a′=−a,c′=−ca'=-a,c'=-c. Now (17),(16) cancel without commuting any unpaired clock past a shear:

T(a,c)T‾(−a,−c)=BN−1CN−1I=⟨s02⟩⟨s13⟩I.(19) T(a,c)\bar T(-a,-c) =B^{N-1}C^{N-1}I =\langle s_{02}\rangle\langle s_{13}\rangle I. \qquad\text{(19)}

The reverse product has the same value. In the primary face ordering, the inputs are (α3,α1)(\alpha_3,\alpha_1), and the outputs are (α2,α0)(\alpha_2,\alpha_0). Thus gluing faces 0,20,2 is exactly (19), leaving identity factors on faces 1,31,3. Gluing the two copies of face 11 is the ordinary coordinate pairing δα1,α1′\delta_{\alpha_1,\alpha'_1}. Its partial trace is therefore

∑α0,α1,α2Tu(s,c,α)T‾u(s,c′,α′)=N⟨s13⟩⟨s02⟩δα3,α3′.(20) \sum_{\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2}T_u(s,c,\alpha) \bar T_u(s,c',\alpha') =N\langle s_{13}\rangle\langle s_{02}\rangle \delta_{\alpha_3,\alpha'_3}. \qquad\text{(20)}

There is no additional pivot in this face-index trace. This is a conclusion from the literal charges and matrices, not an assertion about a physical quantum-group cap.

5. The retained edge weights and the marked-link route

The three glued faces in (20) meet at vertex 33. Equation (18) gives total charges

c03+c03′=0,c23+c23′=0,c13+c13′=1. c_{03}+c'_{03}=0,\qquad c_{23}+c'_{23}=0,\qquad c_{13}+c'_{13}=1.

Thus the charged-link rule in [K, (4.3)] routes the link through 0−3−20\!-\!3\!-\!2; it does not erase an unmarked isolated vertex. For the corresponding link move, the boundary edge 0202 is required to be initially non-link, and the detour is replaced by 0−20\!-\!2. This is a boundary routing condition, not a conclusion from local charges in an arbitrary exterior. The removed internal non-link edge 1313 has weight ⟨s13⟩−1\langle s_{13}\rangle^{-1}. The local total on 0202 is one before removal, so its exterior charge is zero and it becomes a link edge afterwards; its old non-link weight is ⟨s02⟩−1\langle s_{02}\rangle^{-1}. These two factors cancel the decoration factors in (20). The factor NN is then canceled by the relative vertex measure: the primary normalization N2−KN^{2-K} has one extra factor N−1N^{-1} before removal of this vertex. All other boundary data are retained.

Accordingly the normalized local identity is valid with arbitrary exterior tensors under exactly this routing and these charge data. It can be differentiated in regular continuous decoration parameters while retaining the displayed factors. This does not differentiate the level off its root or specify an original meridian-normal family.

6. Scope and proof inputs

This reconstructs the primary charged face-state bubble by finite algebra. It proves neither that the original P,SP,S free vertices already carry the required charged link detours nor that their local tetrahedra have this face-module assignment.

In particular, a physical wire represented by a pair of face indices is not the single face traced in (20). The original marked quantum-group cap, its pivot and its cut matrix cannot be inserted or removed by reinterpreting that trace. A global adapter must transport the actual boundary state, all charge and root data, and the retained source currents. Singular decoration limits are not covered by the regular calculation above.

The sole direct source is R. M. Kashaev, Quantum Dilogarithm as a 6j-Symbol, hep-th/9411147v2. Only its explicit finite definitions and charge/edge-weight conventions are inputs to the argument above. Its pentagon, inversion, bubble and global invariance assertions are not assumed. Equations (1.25) and (1.29) specify the relevant finite conventions.
