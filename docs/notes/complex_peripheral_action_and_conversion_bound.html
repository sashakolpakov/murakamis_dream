Complex peripheral paths retain the absolute regulator bound

28 October 2026. Complex meridian excursions do not by themselves invalidate the representation-volume bound on a holomorphic action. There is an exact endpoint correction, which vanishes when BOTH meridian endpoints are unitary. Consequently a continuously lifted path of genuine representations, starting at regulator zero, has the desired geometric exponential upper bound even if it changes sheets at complex ramification points or winds logarithms. A bare spectral node is a different matter. This note proves that distinction; it does not identify the conversion paths of an actual quantum recurrence.

1. Fixed peripheral marking and the exact complex-path identity

Use the actual preferred SL2 peripheral eigenvalues (M,ell) of the longitude/action calculation. Let beta be its absolutely marked fundamental Bloch chain and R=D(beta)=Vol(rho). The independently proved general peripheral representation-volume bound gives |R|<=V, where V is the hyperbolic manifold volume.

On any regular representation curve, the actual boundary identity is

delta beta=-2 M wedge ell.

Write compatible continuously lifted logarithms as

u=Log M=a+i theta, v=Log ell=b+i phi,
y=Log Y=2u, Y=M^2.

Here phi is an unwrapped longitude argument, not an independently reset principal argument. The Bloch–Wigner differential gives

dR=2(b dtheta-a dphi).                        (1)

Define the holomorphic action increment along a path gamma by

A_gamma=integral_gamma v dy=2integral_gamma v du.            (2)

Direct real and imaginary differentiation gives

Im(v dy)=2(b dtheta+phi da)
        =dR+2d(a phi).

Thus the complete endpoint formula is

Im A_gamma=R(end)-R(start)
                +2[log|M| Arg(ell)]_(start)^(end).          (3)

Equivalently, for the M-step action S_gamma=integral v du, Im S_gamma=(R(end)-R(start))/2+[log|M| Arg(ell)]_start^end. No integral along the complex excursion remains in the correction.

These are the actual root-step constants. For h_Y=2pi i/N,

(1/N)log|exp(A_gamma/h_Y)|=Im A_gamma/(2pi).                 (4)

The M-step convention h_M=pi i/N gives exactly the same number using S_gamma. In particular the actual previously derived identity dS_flat=2v du and Im S_flat=R+2Re(u)Im(v) is precisely (3), with its absolute constant already fixed by the original Bloch chain.

If both endpoints have |M|=1 and the initial regulator is zero,

Im A_gamma=R(end),
(1/N)log|exp(A_gamma/h_Y)|<=V/(2pi).           (5)

This holds for an arbitrary finite complex path of the stated type, not only a path staying on the meridian unit circle. Without the zero anchor, the bound from two absolute regulator values is 2V, not V. Without unit meridian endpoints the term in (3) must be retained.

2. Finite character covers and ramification

There is a useful descent statement. Let pi:C->B be a connected finite cover of normalized peripheral curves on a regular algebraic open set. On C suppose the actual, single-valued regulator satisfies dR=pi^*eta, where eta is the differential (1) determined by M,ell. Then R descends to B.

Proof. On the unramified open set, average R over the d points of each fiber. The average Rbar is globally defined there, since monodromy only permutes the summands, and dRbar=eta. Therefore R-pi^Rbar is locally constant on C. Removing finitely many points from the connected normalization leaves it connected, so this difference is one constant c. Its fiber average is zero, hence c=0. Thus R=pi^Rbar. This proof uses the absolute, single-valued R; it is not a choice of unrelated local action constants.

For a map of irreducible algebraic character/peripheral curves, remove the finitely many branch and nonproper points to obtain the finite cover used here. Additional algebraic flag or shape choices may be included in C. The result does not assert finiteness for a positive-dimensional family mapping to one peripheral point. Different disconnected character components over the same B can still have different additive constants. A common independently proved zero anchor fixes those constants if its limits are controlled.

At a point of B where M and ell are finite and nonzero, eta is smooth and closed, so its descended primitive extends across the finite excluded points. Its absolute volume bound extends by continuity. In particular, ordinary ramification of the meridian projection on the NORMALIZED curve creates no jump. In a local coordinate t, M=M_0+O(t^e), ell=ell_0+O(t), and

v dy=2 Log(ell(t)) M'(t)/M(t) dt

is holomorphic there. A path entering one meridian sheet and exiting another through this point is a continuous path on the normalization; its regulator and endpoint formula remain the same.

Consequently (5) also applies on a genuinely identified normalized peripheral component with such a finite character/Bloch cover. A particular character lift need not return to itself after a loop; the regulator is nevertheless unchanged on the same peripheral fiber within this connected cover.

3. Loops, logarithmic marks, and different kinds of collision

A closed path on the normalized lifted curve based at |M|=1 has zero imaginary action, even if u or v acquire integer 2pi i shifts. More generally a meridian loop ending on a different longitude sheet is NOT a closed path on that curve; its imaginary action is the regulator difference and still satisfies (5) from a zero anchor. Repeating such continuations cannot accumulate arbitrary positive growth, because their regulator increments telescope.

Changing the initial longitude lift by 2pi i n changes A_gamma by 2pi i n(y_end-y_start). Its imaginary part is 2pi n log|Y_end/Y_start|, which vanishes for unit meridian endpoints. A longitude sign adds i pi(y_end-y_start) and likewise changes only the real action there. A fixed integral framing ell->M^w ell adds w(u_end2-u_start2), also real at unit meridian endpoints. These explicit phase changes must be retained in an exact quantum formula, but they create no exponential norm in (5).

At a nonunit basepoint even a closed representation loop can have nonzero imaginary action on the logarithmic cover: if the longitude log winds by 2pi i n, (3) gives 4pi n log|M_base|. Thus a statement about all loops without the unit-endpoint qualification would be false.

A node of a plane A-polynomial is not the same as ramification of its normalization. Its two branches can correspond to two DIFFERENT points of the normalization; they cannot be exchanged by declaring their identical (M,ell) values to be one point. They can also belong to different character components or represent different limiting characters. The normalization and the actual Bloch lift matter. A true common representation, or matching regulator limits, would make a passage regulator-continuous; equality of peripheral eigenvalues alone does not prove that condition.

For a piecewise lifted path whose branches are spliced at nodes, choose matching longitude logs at each splice and write J_j=R_after-R_before. Applying (3) on each genuine piece gives

Im A_total=R_final-R_initial-sum_j J_j
                +2[log|M| Arg(ell)]_initial^final.          (6)

Thus an unsupported node switch carries exactly the missing regulator jump. If the logs themselves are reset at a nonunit node, its extra 2log|M| times the argument jump must be included as well. Neither operation can be omitted by treating a spectral discriminant zero as a smooth character ramification point.

4. The Gaussian conversion has precisely such a node mismatch

For the independently proved analytic two-channel model, the eigenvalues are alpha(x)=exp(a(x-1/2)) and beta(x)=exp(-a(x-1/2)+i theta). Give both branches their calibrated natural primitives at x=0:

sigma_1(x)=a(x^2-x)/2,
sigma_2(x)=-a(x^2-x)/2+i theta x.

Both complete marked actions have real part zero. Their logarithmic eigenvalues coincide at the complex point

x_*=1/2+i theta/(2a).

However their calibrated primitives do not agree there:

sigma_2(x_*)-sigma_1(x_*)
   =a/4-theta^2/(4a)+i theta/2
   =Gamma+i theta/2.                         (7)

Following beta from 0 to x_* and then alpha to 1 has exactly this total action. It is precisely the exponent computed by the exact Gaussian conversion sum, including its phase.

To compare constants with (2), set Y=exp(2pi i x), M=exp(pi i x) and A_i=2pi i sigma_i. At x_* the peripheral logs and hence the boundary term 2log|M| Arg(ell) agree on the two branches. Their artificial regulator lifts therefore differ by 2pi Gamma. The local spectral curve is the reducible nodal union (L-alpha(x))(L-beta(x))=0; its normalization keeps those branches separate. The conversion is not a continuation through a common smooth normalized point with one calibrated regulator. This explains exactly why the general analytic counterexample does not contradict the genuine geometric path bound.

5. Consequence for the actual upper-bound attack

Representation geometry supplies a sharp bound for complex action paths with a genuine calibrated lift, unit meridian endpoints, and a zero regulator anchor. Finite covers, ordinary meridian ramification, and logarithmic winding do not defeat it. This is stronger than bounding only real meridian arcs.

The unresolved quantum statement is specific: show that every exponentially relevant sourced conversion is represented by such a lifted path, or bound its additional node/connection jump by the corresponding calibrated action difference. The original annihilator’s actual character components, its full source and its subprincipal couplings must enter that proof. No assertion that a generic analytic recurrence has this property is made here. Lower noncancellation remains separate.

Audit checks the differential constants, the Gaussian collision mismatch, and complex Bloch-chart integrals with unit endpoints, nonunit endpoints, and logarithm-winding loops. These integrals test the identity, not the existence of a knot representation for an arbitrary one-shape chart.
