A boundary-response criterion for a protected source term

Removing one crossing-region factor from the marked source leaves a polynomial in two face variables. Its positive logarithmic growth records how the remaining triangles distribute their loads between those faces. The first derivative is the load at one boundary face; the second is an effective conductance obtained from the triangle Hessians. This note shows how those two quantities can pay for restoring the two removed triangles.

More precisely, let F(z)F(z) be the exterior pressure when the boundary weights are A=ez,C=1A=e^z,C=1, and put ν=F′(0)\nu=F'(0), κ=F″(0)\kappa=F''(0). Under the strict feasibility and connectivity hypotheses below, the condition 0<ν≤1/20<\nu\le1/2, ν+κ≥1/2\nu+\kappa\ge1/2 gives a strict exponential bound for the original signed left contribution, uniformly over its real spectral variable σ∈[−2,2]\sigma\in[-2,2], at odd levels. Every other crossing region keeps its central numerator at spectrum −2-2. The comparison is with the unchanged diagram’s complete action Φ=Vol⁡(Y)/(2π)\Phi=\operatorname{Vol}(Y)/(2\pi), where YY is its augmented link exterior. The precise bound and proof appear in section 9.

The argument allows arbitrary fixed matched simple spheres satisfying these hypotheses, with no restriction on their number of vertices or cycles. It estimates one source contribution. It does not estimate several noncentral regions, varying neighboring spectra, even levels, or the full filling period. The recorded 54-region example does not satisfy this response condition; its separate tilted certificate is proved in the uniform cap estimate. No strict enlargement of a known knot-volume class is asserted here.

2. The positive exterior exists for every finite real tilt

The strictly feasible table in (2) proves coercivity in the extracted variables for every fixed finite zz. Indeed the recession function is the sum of the triangle maxima minus the prescribed target pairing. Equality with the strictly positive table forces a recession direction to be constant on every supported triangle. Connectedness and the two fixed boundary coordinates then make that direction zero. Compactness of the unit sphere gives a positive recession margin. Changing finite boundary values changes only bounded terms in this argument.

The triangle Hessians are graph Laplacians. After grounding CC, their sum is positive definite. Thus the minimum exists uniquely for every real zz, and the implicit function theorem gives a real analytic interior solution wI(z)w_I(z). This argument does not assume that the tilted weights give a complete circle packing or a hyperbolic link exterior.

For supported triangle weights a,b,ca,b,c, its conductance between two distinct supported corners is

gab=ab2π(a+b)ab+ac+bc. g_{ab}=\frac{ab}{2\pi(a+b)\sqrt{ab+ac+bc}}. \tag{7}

The formula includes a protected third corner by setting c=0c=0. A singleton triangle has zero Hessian. Parallel contributions from distinct original triangles are all retained.

0≤wi′(z)≤1(i∈I). 0\le w_i'(z)\le1\qquad(i\in I). \tag{8}

Consequently, between zz and z+sz+s, s≥0s\ge0, every positive weight is multiplied by a factor in [1,es][1,e^s]. The two positive degree-one expressions ab/(a+b)ab/(a+b) and ab+ac+bc\sqrt{ab+ac+bc} are coordinatewise increasing and homogeneous. Each therefore increases by a factor in that same interval. Their quotient in (7) yields

These are geometric-network inequalities for the original triangle pressure. The root factorization of the odd polynomial gives the same lower bound in the second line, but is not needed for its derivation.

7. Why stability and two moments alone cannot settle every geometry

There is an elementary obstruction to deducing a universal result from only an abstract stable binary exterior and the formal restoration formula. For 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<1, take the root measure with mass 2/52/5 at 7/107/10 and mass 3/53/5 at ε\varepsilon. Its normalized pressure is

Fε(z)−M=25log⁡(310+710ez)+35log⁡(1−ε+εez). F_\varepsilon(z)-M =\frac25\log\left(\frac3{10}+\frac7{10}e^z\right) +\frac35\log(1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon e^z). \tag{32}

It is realized by genuine stable binary polynomials of degrees divisible by five, has full coefficient support, and has positive response. As ε↓0\varepsilon\downarrow0, its initial mean and response tend to 7/257/25 and 21/25021/250. Nevertheless, evaluating the formal restoration at z=log⁡(1/ε)z=\log(1/\varepsilon) gives

The actual negative-axis supremum has a finite lower rate from its endpoint PN(0,1)P_N(0,1):

M+25log⁡310+35log⁡(1−ε). M+\frac25\log\frac3{10} +\frac35\log(1-\varepsilon). \tag{34}

Hence no correct positive-tilt upper bound for that entire supremum can pay the restoration excess for all these abstract polynomials. This does not construct a matched-sphere counterexample. In fact the unbounded excess is incompatible with the existing positive one-cap geometric pyramid budget in its stated strict range. The example isolates the additional geometric restriction needed by any universal argument; real stability, complete support and positive response do not supply it alone.

8. Proof inputs and limits

The common-axis theorem supplies the original extraction, moment weights, odd stable factorization, squared-factor estimate and omitted-tail bound. The exact restoration of the two complete triangles is recorded in the cap-capacity comparison and the algebraic boundary pressure. The effective-response theorem gives the original triangle conductance and positive coefficient packet. The limitation discussed in section 7 is compared with the original one-cap geometric volume budget.

Sections 2–6 keep these original loads and the same complete action.
