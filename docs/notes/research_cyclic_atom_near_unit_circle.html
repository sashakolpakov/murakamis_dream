The original cyclic dilogarithm near the unit circle

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

The norm-one cyclic dilogarithm in the original knot period has a uniform logarithmic estimate through the unit circle. Polynomial separation from its root divisors gives a logarithmic error. At closer approaches an explicit root factor gives the same error, even when the distance is superexponentially small in the level. All factors belong to the actual function; no closed-period lower bound is inferred.

1. The actual atom and the estimate

Put N≥2N\ge2, Q=e2πi/NQ=e^{2\pi i/N}, and μN={Qj:0≤j<N}\mu_N=\{Q^j:0\le j<N\}. Use C’s unchanged functions DN(t)=∏j=1N−1(1−Qjt)j/N,VN(t)=(1−tN)(N−1)/(2N)DN(t),UN(t)=VN(t)−1.(1) D_N(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-Q^jt)^{j/N},\qquad V_N(t)=\frac{(1-t^N)^{(N-1)/(2N)}}{D_N(t)},\qquad U_N(t)=V_N(t)^{-1}. \qquad\text{(1)} All branches start at one at zero and are continued along the specified path avoiding μN\mu_N. Their moduli are independent of that path, since every exponent in (1) is real. Define the continuous function F(t)={Im⁡Li⁡2(t)/(2π),0<|t|≤1,−Im⁡Li⁡2(1/t)/(2π),|t|>1.(2) F(t)=\begin{cases} \operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(t)/(2\pi),&0<|t|\le1,\\ -\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(1/t)/(2\pi),&|t|>1. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(2)} Here Li⁡2(z)=∑m≥1zm/m2\operatorname{Li}_2(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}z^m/m^2 on the closed unit disk. Its continuity and conjugation symmetry show that the two boundary values in (2) agree.

Write ζj=Q−j\zeta_j=Q^{-j}, βj=(N−1−2j)/(2N)\beta_j=(N-1-2j)/(2N), and δ=dist⁡(t,μN)\delta=\operatorname{dist}(t,\mu_N). For t∉μNt\notin\mu_N, put BN(t)={βjlog⁡(N|t−ζj|),|t−ζj|<1/N,0,δ≥1/N. Theorem. Fix R≥1R\ge1. Uniformly for all N≥2N\ge2 and R−1≤|t|≤RR^{-1}\le|t|\le R, t∉μNt\notin\mu_N, |log|VN(t)|−NF(t)−BN(t)|≤CRlog⁡N,|log|UN(t)|+NF(t)+BN(t)|≤CRlog⁡N.(3) \left|\log|V_N(t)|-NF(t)-B_N(t)\right|\le C_R\log N, \quad \left|\log|U_N(t)|+NF(t)+B_N(t)\right|\le C_R\log N. \qquad\text{(3)} Consequently, for every fixed A>0A>0, dist⁡(t,μN)≥N−A⇒|log|VN(t)|−NF(t)|≤CA,Rlog⁡N.(4) \operatorname{dist}(t,\mu_N)\ge N^{-A} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left|\log|V_N(t)|-NF(t)\right|\le C_{A,R}\log N. \qquad\text{(4)} The inverse has the same assertion with leading term −NF-NF. These statements allow arbitrary level-dependent arguments satisfying the displayed conditions.

3. The central norm and the two chambers

For every nonzero tt, with δ=dist⁡(t,μN)\delta=\operatorname{dist}(t,\mu_N), there is an absolute c>0c>0 such that cmin⁡(1,Nδ)≤|1−tN|max⁡(1,|t|N)≤2.(8) c\min(1,N\delta)\le \frac{|1-t^N|}{\max(1,|t|^N)}\le2. \qquad\text{(8)} Choose the nearest root in angle and write t=rQkeiφt=rQ^ke^{i\varphi}, |φ|≤π/N|\varphi|\le\pi/N. Put a=N|log⁡r|a=N|\log r|, b=Nφb=N\varphi. The squared ratio in (8) is (1−e−a)2+4e−asin⁡2(b/2)(1-e^{-a})^2+4e^{-a}\sin^2(b/2). If a≥1a\ge1, it has a fixed positive lower bound. If a≤1a\le1, its square root is at least ca2+b2c\sqrt{a^2+b^2}, while |reiφ−1|≤Ca2+b2/N|r e^{i\varphi}-1|\le C\sqrt{a^2+b^2}/N. The chosen root is also nearest in Euclidean distance. This proves the lower bound; the upper bound is the triangle inequality.

When δ≥1/N\delta\ge1/N, (8) gives log⁡|1−tN|=Nlog⁡+|t|+O(1)\log|1-t^N|=N\log^+|t|+O(1). Thus (1),(7) imply log⁡|VN(t)|=N(12log⁡+|t|−I(t))+OR(log⁡N).(9) \log|V_N(t)|= N\left(\tfrac12\log^+|t|-I(t)\right)+O_R(\log N). \qquad\text{(9)} The remaining term −12log⁡+|t|-\tfrac12\log^+|t| is bounded on the annulus. The central norm factor itself has not been omitted.

For |t|<1|t|<1, integrating the absolutely convergent logarithmic series, with ∫01ue2πimudu=1/(2πim)\int_0^1u e^{2\pi imu}du=1/(2\pi im), gives I(t)=−Im⁡Li⁡2(t)/(2π)I(t)=-\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(t)/(2\pi). For |t|>1|t|>1, write gt(u)=log⁡|t|+log⁡|1−t−1e−2πiu|g_t(u)=\log|t|+\log|1-t^{-1}e^{-2\pi iu}|; then I(t)=12log⁡|t|+Im⁡Li⁡2(1/t)/(2π)I(t)=\tfrac12\log|t|+\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(1/t)/(2\pi). The uniform small-interval integral bound following (5), together with continuity away from the singular angle, proves continuity of these integrals at the unit circle. Equation (9) therefore proves (3) outside the root disks.

There is also an exact modulus identity: |VN(t)VN(1/t)|=|1−t||1−tN|1/N.(10) |V_N(t)V_N(1/t)|=\frac{|1-t|}{|1-t^N|^{1/N}}. \qquad\text{(10)} Indeed reindexing j↦N−jj\mapsto N-j gives |DN(t)DN(1/t)|=|t|−(N−1)/2|1−tN|/|1−t||D_N(t)D_N(1/t)|=|t|^{-(N-1)/2}|1-t^N|/|1-t|. The numerator norm factors in (1) give (10). This identity is understood off μN\mu_N, without resetting any branch.

4. Uniform estimates inside every root disk

Factoring 1−tN1-t^N, with the same anchor at zero, gives exactly VN(t)=∏k=0N−1(1−Qkt)βk,WN,j(t):=VN(t)(1−Qjt)−βj=∏k≠j(1−Qkt)βk.(11) V_N(t)=\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}(1-Q^kt)^{\beta_k},\qquad W_{N,j}(t):=V_N(t)(1-Q^jt)^{-\beta_j} =\prod_{k\ne j}(1-Q^kt)^{\beta_k}. \qquad\text{(11)} Every continued branch of WN,jW_{N,j} extends holomorphically and without zeros to |t−ζj|<1/N|t-\zeta_j|<1/N. Its modulus at the center is |WN,j(ζj)|=|(Q;Q)j|N−(j+1/2)/N,(Q;Q)0=1.(12) |W_{N,j}(\zeta_j)|=|(Q;Q)_j|\,N^{-(j+1/2)/N}, \qquad (Q;Q)_0=1. \qquad\text{(12)} For a direct verification put Ll=log⁡|1−Ql|L_l=\log|1-Q^l|. The product of the nontrivial roots gives ∑l=1N−1Ll=log⁡N\sum_{l=1}^{N-1}L_l=\log N, and symmetry gives ∑lLl=(N/2)log⁡N\sum lL_l=(N/2)\log N. Reindexing k=j+lk=j+l modulo NN in (11) yields log⁡|WN,j(ζj)|=(N−12N−jN−12)log⁡N+∑l=N−jN−1Ll, \log|W_{N,j}(\zeta_j)| =\left(\frac{N-1}{2N}-\frac jN-\frac12\right)\log N +\sum_{l=N-j}^{N-1}L_l, which is (12), including the empty sum at j=0j=0.

Let s=min⁡(|k−j|,N−|k−j|)≥1s=\min(|k-j|,N-|k-j|)\ge1. On the disk, |t−ζk|≥(4s−1)/N|t-\zeta_k|\ge(4s-1)/N, because 2sin⁡(πs/N)≥4s/N2\sin(\pi s/N)\ge4s/N. Since |βk|≤1/2|\beta_k|\le1/2, |(log⁡WN,j)′(t)|≤CNlog⁡N|(\log W_{N,j})'(t)|\le CN\log N. Integrating along the straight segment from the center changes log⁡|WN,j|\log|W_{N,j}| by at most Clog⁡NC\log N.

Here is the endpoint-aware factorial estimate needed in (12). Set J(v)=∫0vlog⁡(2sin⁡πu)duJ(v)=\int_0^v\log(2\sin\pi u)\,du. Uniformly for 0≤j<N0\le j<N, log⁡|(Q;Q)j|=NJ(j/N)+O(log⁡N)\log|(Q;Q)_j|=NJ(j/N)+O(\log N). For |z|,|w|≤1|z|,|w|\le1, d=|z−w|≤1/2d=|z-w|\le1/2, the series estimate |zm−wm|≤min⁡(md,2)|z^m-w^m|\le\min(md,2) gives |Li⁡2(z)−Li⁡2(w)|≤Cdlog⁡(e/d)|\operatorname{Li}_2(z)-\operatorname{Li}_2(w)| \le Cd\log(e/d). 5. Sharp envelope and scope

Put v3=Im⁡Li⁡2(eπi/3)v_3=\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{\pi i/3}), the volume of a regular ideal tetrahedron. Harmonicity and boundary continuity reduce the disk extrema to the unit circle. There the angular derivative is −log⁡|2sin⁡(θ/2)|-\log|2\sin(\theta/2)|, so the maximum is v3v_3 at θ=π/3\theta=\pi/3, and the minimum is −v3-v_3 at 5π/35\pi/3. Formula (2) gives the same bounds outside. Consequently |VN(t)|≤NCReNv3/(2π)+BN(t),|UN(t)|≤NCReNv3/(2π)−BN(t).(14) |V_N(t)|\le N^{C_R}e^{Nv_3/(2\pi)+B_N(t)},\qquad |U_N(t)|\le N^{C_R}e^{Nv_3/(2\pi)-B_N(t)}. \qquad\text{(14)} Under polynomial root separation, the correction is absorbed in a power of NN. At closer approaches its original exponent is mandatory.

H already gives the sharp per-atom constant v3/(2π)v_3/(2\pi), and S gives the two chamber actions and exact resonant source values. The refinement is uniform control near the circle, including the regularized functions throughout every root disk. It does not reduce the number of atoms in an actual word or replace H’s endpoint integrals. No unrestricted finite value of the unregularized atom is assigned at its roots. C’s source, inverse atoms, reflected monomials, Gaussian weights, Cauchy factors and external scalar remain unchanged. These function estimates neither bound a coherent contraction below nor transport its selected geometric cycle.
