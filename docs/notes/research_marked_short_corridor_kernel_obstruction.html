A marked short corridor outside the three/four reduction

Private written deduction, 10 November 2026. No mathematical numerical evaluation, test, import or compilation is used. The existing rational normal form is not reproved here. Its uniqueness gives an exact test for a prescribed coefficient alphabet, and already excludes a universal reduction to positive three/four paths by end twists and proper half-turns. The obstruction concerns that marked reduction, not the volume conjecture or the exponential rate of the unreduced source.

The second distinction is analytic: the actual joining of two proved single-power kernels retains a turn. Omitting it merges their powers and changes the finite source. The existing concentrated boundary class is not closed under arbitrary turns, even on actual tangle sources.

1. A coefficient-alphabet test with both boundary frames fixed

Use the physical crossing RR, turn TT, and affine pillowcase maps of the marked move theorem: R̃n(x,y)=(x+ny+n/2,y),T̃(x,y)=(−y,x+1/2).(1) \widetilde R^{\,n}(x,y)=(x+ny+n/2,y),\qquad \widetilde T(x,y)=(-y,x+1/2). \qquad\text{(1)} Their linear parts are An=(1n01),S=(0−110).(2) A_n=\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(2)} The symbols AnA_n in this section are two-dimensional integral matrices; the original quantum matrices will have a different symbol in Section 3.

Let a fixed corridor have projective linear matrix GG. Choose its deck sign so that its lower-left entry is C>0C>0, and suppose C≥2C\ge2. Integral twists on the left and right leave CC and the two diagonal residues modulo CC unchanged. The determinant-one condition makes both residues nonzero. There is consequently a unique normalization by these two end twists of the form G0=(ABCD),0<A,D<C,B=(AD−1)/C.(3) G_0=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}, \qquad 0<A,D<C,\qquad B=(AD-1)/C. \qquad\text{(3)} All physical half-turns retained in the marked normal form have identity linear part on the pillowcase, up to the deck sign. They do not change (3).

Fix any alphabet ℬ⊆{2,3,…}\mathcal B\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots\}. A replacement in this section must preserve the two outside frames and arbitrary outside tangles. It may add fixed integral end twists and the specified proper half-turn, but may not add an outside quarter-turn or a new long filling.

Proposition 1. Such a uniform-negative replacement RrTR−b1T⋯TR−bkTRsH,bj∈ℬ,(4) R^r T R^{-b_1}T\cdots T R^{-b_k}T R^s H, \qquad b_j\in\mathcal B, \qquad\text{(4)} exists if and only if the unique negative continued fraction of C/AC/A, with all coefficients at least two, has every coefficient in ℬ\mathcal B. A uniform-positive replacement of the same form with RbjR^{b_j} instead exists if and only if the corresponding continued fraction of C/(C−A)C/(C-A) has every coefficient in ℬ\mathcal B.

Proof. The definite corridor normal form, Sections 2–3, proves that a negative word with all bj≥2b_j\ge2 has normalized ratio C/A=[b1,…,bk]−.(5) C/A=[b_1,\ldots,b_k]_-. \qquad\text{(5)} It also proves uniqueness by the ceiling Euclidean algorithm: the next coefficient is the ceiling of the current complete quotient, and its remainder determines the next quotient. Thus (5) is necessary. Conversely, the same theorem realizes that continued fraction with the unique full matrix (3), and then restores both end twists and the actual affine half-turn. This gives the marked identity for arbitrary attached tangles.

For the positive statement, conjugate a negative-word matrix by diag⁡(1,−1)\operatorname{diag}(1,-1). On projective linear parts this changes every negative crossing to a positive one and preserves the turn. If the negative normalized matrix is (A′B′CD′)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A'&B'\\C&D'\end{smallmatrix}\right), the positive matrix, after choosing lower-left entry C>0C>0, is (−A′B′C−D′).(6) \begin{pmatrix}-A'&B'\\C&-D'\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(6)} Its normalized first diagonal residue is C−A′C-A'. Consequently matching (3) requires A′=C−AA'=C-A, and (5) becomes the second continued fraction in the proposition. The full affine correction is again the prescribed proper half-turn. This argument proves a boundary-map identity; it does not replace only one attachment by its mirror. ▫\square

This is a test for reductions of the specified marked corridor form. Special fixed attachments can have further stabilizers. The proposition does not exclude identities exploiting such an attachment or an unrelated finite-level quantum relation.

2. An explicit adjacent pair

Consider the fixed corridor W=TR3TR5T.(7) W=T R^3 T R^5 T. \qquad\text{(7)} Direct integer multiplication gives SA3SA5S=(−5114−3),A1(SA3SA5S)A1=(971411).(8) S A_3 S A_5 S =\begin{pmatrix}-5&1\\14&-3\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_1(S A_3 S A_5 S)A_1 =\begin{pmatrix}9&7\\14&11\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(8)} Its two ratios in Proposition 1 are exactly 149=[2,3,2,2,2]−,145=[3,5]−.(9) \frac{14}{9}=[2,3,2,2,2]_-, \qquad \frac{14}{5}=[3,5]_-. \qquad\text{(9)} For the first equality the successive tails from the right are 2,3/2,4/3,9/4,14/92,3/2,4/3,9/4,14/9; the second is 3−1/53-1/5. Thus neither sign has a replacement whose coefficients all belong to {3,4}\{3,4\}, even allowing arbitrary length and fixed integral twists at both ends. Reversing the corridor reverses the continued-fraction lists and does not change this conclusion.

Nor can (7) be replaced by one arbitrary nonzero-power kernel with only those end adapters. The necessary and sufficient test in single-corridor compression, Proposition 1, requires the two normalized diagonal residues to be the same 11 or −1-1 modulo CC. Here they are 9,119,11 modulo 1414, neither 11 nor 1313.

The same obstruction excludes reducing (7) to a same-sign run of twos with only those end adapters: every such run has the marked one-vertex form proved by the repeated-two reduction, Proposition 2. Composing its end twists and proper rotation with the proposed adapters would give the excluded one-vertex form for (7).

These are exact restrictions even under the stronger hypothetical assumption that every positive three/four path already had a complete boundary theorem. They do not assert that the pair (3,5)(3,5) has no other useful presentation or cannot be estimated directly. In particular (9) is itself a valid uniform-sign presentation; its new adjacent powers still require their actual coherent contraction. No added separator has been used to turn them into a covered long-separated family.

3. The turn in the exact finite composition

Now return to the original full algebra and use the quantum notation 𝒜N=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2),DN=N−1,ϕN(eiej)=δi+j,DN,𝖩ij=δi+j,DN.(10) \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2),\quad D_N=N-1, \quad \phi_N(e_i e_j)=\delta_{i+j,D_N},\quad \mathsf J_{ij}=\delta_{i+j,D_N}. \qquad\text{(10)} Let MM be multiplication by the original crossing, including κN=exp⁡(πiDN2/(2N))\kappa_N=\exp(\pi iD_N^2/(2N)), and put 𝖠n=𝖩Mn,v(w)=(e0(w),…,eDN(w))T,Pn(w,z)=v(w)T𝖠nv(z).(11) \mathsf A_n=\mathsf J M^n,\qquad v(w)=(e_0(w),\ldots,e_{D_N}(w))^T,\qquad P_n(w,z)=v(w)^T\mathsf A_n v(z). \qquad\text{(11)} The identity MT𝖩=𝖩MM^T\mathsf J=\mathsf J M makes every 𝖠n\mathsf A_n symmetric. The full single-power theorem for every nonzero degree-two filling estimates precisely PnP_n, with both free ordinary variables and their original normal data.

Two different contractions of these same polynomials must be distinguished. In contrast, the actual two-adjacent-vertex path is Qa,b(w,u)=v(w)T𝖠a𝖠bv(u)=ϕxϕy[Pa(w,x)KT(x,y)Pb(y,u)],KT(x,y)=∑j=0DNej(x)ej(y).(13) Q_{a,b}(w,u)=v(w)^T\mathsf A_a\mathsf A_bv(u) =\phi_x\phi_y[ P_a(w,x)K_T(x,y)P_b(y,u)], \qquad K_T(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{D_N}e_j(x)e_j(y). \qquad\text{(13)} Indeed the two pairings contribute 𝖠a𝖩I𝖩𝖠b\mathsf A_a\mathsf J I\mathsf J\mathsf A_b. Equivalently, (13) is ϕx[Pa(w,x)(𝖳xPb)(x,u)]\phi_x[P_a(w,x)(\mathsf T_xP_b)(x,u)], where 𝖳ej=eDN−j\mathsf T e_j=e_{D_N-j}. There is no extra scalar in either identity: the original crossing factors already occur in Ma,MbM^a,M^b. All products are taken before the full traces, so (12)–(13) retain both parities, every first normal at a double root, and the odd simple root.

Thus replacing (13) by the easier single-trace composition (12) deletes the turn and changes the source to power a+ba+b. Single-power upper bounds do not make that deletion legitimate. The exact ordinary adapters and their potentially growing-order Fourier observations are also explicit in the adjacent-kernel observation theorem, Sections 1–3.

End crossing adapters are different: if a complete concentrated boundary theorem is already known, canonical multiplication by a fixed crossing preserves it under boundary crossing preservation. For a rational normal-form identity the exact quantum transformation is 𝒬N(W)=κNℓMr[𝖩M−b1𝖩⋯𝖩M−bk𝖩]Ms,ℓ=e(W)−r−s+∑jbj3∈ℤ.(14) \mathcal Q_N(W)=\kappa_N^\ell M^r[\mathsf J M^{-b_1}\mathsf J\cdots \mathsf J M^{-b_k}\mathsf J]M^s, \qquad \ell=\frac{e(W)-r-s+\sum_jb_j}{3}\in\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(14)} Here e(W)e(W) is the original signed crossing sum. Formula (14) does not erase the separately specified physical half-turn.

4. Why the known analytic class cannot supply automatic turns

There is already an actual-source obstruction, not merely a formal possibility. For every fixed |p|≥2|p|\ge2, the terminal source Hp,N=𝖳(Rp)H_{p,N}=\mathsf T(R^p) has the complete nonzero local packet and a strict whole-real angular gap. But 𝖳Hp,N=can⁡(Rp).(15) \mathsf T H_{p,N}=\operatorname{can}(R^p). \qquad\text{(15)} At every physical angle τj=π(DN−2j)/N\tau_j=\pi(D_N-2j)/N, the latter has the exact value can⁡(Rp)(−2cos⁡τj)=exp⁡(πipDN24N+ipNτj24π),|can⁡(Rp)(−2cos⁡τj)|=1.(16) \operatorname{can}(R^p)(-2\cos\tau_j) =\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi i pD_N^2}{4N} +\frac{ipN\tau_j^2}{4\pi}\right), \qquad |\operatorname{can}(R^p)(-2\cos\tau_j)|=1. \qquad\text{(16)} As proved in Section 7 of boundary crossing preservation, a nonzero even local packet at the nodes tending to zero forces its real complete rate to be zero. At physical nodes tending to any fixed nonzero real angle, a strict angular bound at rate zero would then force the modulus to tend to zero, contradicting (16). This holds at both parities.

Consequently a theorem asserting closure of the existing concentrated class under every short turn is false. This does not disprove a source-specific theorem for (13), whose geometric action and contributing cycle can change. The existing finite-kernel composition theorem proves such preservation with a sufficiently long intervening power, and explicitly retains that hypothesis.

5. What remains for arbitrary partial fillings

The exact source compiler does cover every finite Conway expression. At a branch it keeps the multiplication tensor μijr=ϕN(eiejeDN−r),(17) \mu_{ijr}=\phi_N(e_i e_j e_{D_N-r}), \qquad\text{(17)} and every still-free ordinary boundary variable. The internal-wire averaging theorem then integrates every paired index exactly, with factor NN per internal wire. It retains full Hermite data and all signed powers. That identity removes internal-index alias errors in its stated representation; it does not remove the original angle traces or provide their calibrated competing-current bounds.

Thus the obstruction is already present for the rational two-port example (7), before branching is considered. General arborescent components additionally retain (17) and more outside boundaries. The rational-corridor theorem does not replace those tensors by a two-boundary path.

A useful sufficient next theorem is therefore a complete boundary estimate for the actual uniform-sign chains with arbitrary magnitudes at least two, stable under the particular turns in (13), or a direct estimate for the fixed short component after its actual surrounding contraction. The marked rational normal form would transfer such a theorem with its end crossings and scalar (14) retained. No such general analytic conclusion follows here, and no closed-knot or finite-exception statement is inferred.
