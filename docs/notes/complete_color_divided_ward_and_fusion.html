Complete-color divided Ward operators and the exact fusion ghost

Research theorem, 27 October 2026. At the complete integral color, the first normal of a vanishing N-th quantum power gives a nonzero divided Ward action. Its tensor fusion correction is one finite unipotent exponential, with nilpotence bounded by strand number. Ordinary closure kills this correction. A specified one-wrap, relative cut-current test can detect it, but no identification of that test with the selected global geometric class is asserted.

All formulas below are proved from finite matrices. In particular no extension of a generic typical meridian color to a divided-power quantum group is presumed.

1. The genuine complete integral color

Fix N>=2 and m=N-1. Initially q is generic, Q=q^2, rho=q-q^(-1). On the ordinary integral Weyl color D_m(q) use the divided-F basis v_a=F^(a)v_0, 0<=a<=m. Its literal matrices are

K v_a=q^(m-2a)v_a,
E v_a=[m-a+1]_q v_(a-1),
F v_a=[a+1]_q v_(a+1),                      (1.1)

with arrows outside the range absent. For r>=0,

E^(r)v_a={m-a+r choose r}_q v_(a-r),
F^(r)v_a={a+r choose r}_q v_(a+r),            (1.2)

when the target exists, and zero otherwise. Braces denote symmetric Gaussian polynomials. Formula (1.2) follows by multiplying adjacent entries and dividing by [r]_q! over the generic field; its resulting Gaussian polynomial is integral. Thus E(N)=F(N)=0 on each color identically in q.

The finite generic-q R matrix of this actual color is

R_uncheck=D sum_(r=0)^m
      q^(r(r-1)/2) rho^r E^r tensor F^r/[r]_q!,
D=q^(H tensor H/2).                         (1.3)

It is regular near every prescribed q_N=exp(pi*i/N). Indeed its r-th nilpotent coefficient can also be written q(r(r-1)/2)rhor[r]_q! E^(r) tensor F^(r). The recorded Cartan half-character remains on its chosen cover.

For completeness, its E intertwining does not need a literature proof. From the matrices one gets

[E,F^r]=[r]_q F^{r-1}
             (q^(1-r)K-q^(r-1)K^(-1))/rho.

Likewise [E^r,F]=[r]_q E^{r-1} (q(r-1)K-q(1-r)K^(-1))/rho; these are ordinary powers in both commutator formulas and follow by induction from the generator matrices.

Conjugating by D reduces the R intertwining equation to the recursion c_(r+1)=c_r q^r rho/[r+1]_q for the coefficients of E^r tensor F^r. This is exactly (1.3). The last unmatched term has E^(m+1)=0. Consequently checked R commutes with Delta E and Delta K. The F calculation uses the second displayed commutator and the same coefficient recursion. It also commutes with the iterated total actions on any tensor power. Inverses have the same property. All identities are generic q matrix identities before root specialization.

2. The tensor divided-power formula, including its order

On b tensor factors put

C_E=Delta^(b-1)E=sum_(i=1)^b A_i,
A_i=E_i K_(i+1)...K_b.

For i<j one has A_i A_j=Q A_j A_i. The ordinary q-multinomial formula, proved by multiplying one additional factor, therefore gives the following integral matrix formula:

C_E^n/[n]_q!
  =sum_(n_1+...+n_b=n)
      q^(sum_(i<j)n_i n_j)
      tensor_i [E_i^(n_i)K_i^(n_1+...+n_(i-1))]. (2.1)

Terms with n_i>N-1 are zero. The order E^(n_i) before K powers is part of the formula. On two factors this reads

Delta E^(n)=sum_(r=0)^n q^(r(n-r))
                   E^(r) tensor E^(n-r)K^r.  (2.2)

Thus the tensor divided power is regular at q_N even when [n]q! vanishes. For b>=2 it is nonzero at n=N. For example on v(N-1) tensor v_1 only the r=N-1 term of (2.2) survives; its output v_0 tensor v_0 has nonzero coefficient.

Every actual braid word commutes with (2.1): this holds generically by commutation with C_E, and extends through the root because both matrices are regular. This is a genuine complete-color root Ward action invisible in the equality C_E(q_N)^N=0.

3. Exact first-normal normalization and the scaled matrix

Set Ecal=rho^2 E, C=rho^2 C_E. The polynomial matrix

D_n(q)=C(q)^n/(Q;Q)_n

is regular near q_N for every n. Formula (2.1) proves this because

D_n=rho^(2n)q^(-n(n-1)/2)(1-Q)^(-n) C_E^n/[n]_q!.

The entire divided matrix C_E^n/[n]_q! is interpreted by (2.1). No zero factorial is separately evaluated.

In particular define

J_b=[C(q)^N/(1-Q^N)]_(q=q_N).

Since (Q;Q)_(N-1)=N at the root,

D_N(q_N)=J_b/N.                              (3.1)

The corresponding unscaled conversion is

[C_E^N/(1-Q^N)]root
  =q_N^(-N(N-1)/2)N/(1-Q_N)^N
                      *[C_E^N/[N]_q!]root.  (3.2)

For Q=Q_N(1+epsilon), along the complete integral color,

C(Q)^N=-N epsilon J_b+O(epsilon^2).           (3.3)

For q=q_N(1+h) the coefficient is -2N h J_b instead. These are specified normalizations, not unnamed scalar multiples.

In the balanced basis used by the actual cut, at the root

Ecal_(a,a+1)=Q_N^(-a-1)-1,
K_aa=x Q_N^(-a), x=q_N^(N-1).

The unique mixed term on w_(N-1) tensor w_1 gives the useful normalization check

J_2(w_(N-1) tensor w_1)=-N q_N w_0 tensor w_0. (3.4)

It is nonzero at every level, including even and composite N.

4. The general-b fusion correction is one finite exponential

For any argument z define the positive-argument finite polynomial

f_N(zX)=sum_(r=0)^(N-1) z^r X^r/(Q_N;Q_N)_r.

The actual negative factorial convention is obtained by z=-t. Order the tensor factors as f_N(zA_b)…f_N(zA_1). The finite q-multinomial identity before specializing gives exactly

product_(i=b,...,1) f_N(zA_i)
    =sum_(n=0)^(b(N-1)) z^n D_n(q_N).        (4.1)

One way to see regularity on its right is its identical generic-q ordered product on the left: each A_i^N=0 identically.

For 0<=r<N, Gaussian multiplication gives

D_(kN) D_r={kN+r choose r}_Q D_(kN+r).

The scalar Gaussian tends to one, directly from its r product factors. Thus D_(kN+r)=D_(kN)D_r at the root. Also

D_N^k=k! D_(kN),                            (4.2)

because {kN choose N}Q tends to k: all nonmultiple product ratios tend to one, and the final ratio (1-Q(kN))/(1-QN) tends to k. Equivalently (Q;Q)(kN)/(1-QN)k tends to N^k k!.

Combining these exact identities gives the full factorization

product_(i=b,...,1) f_N(zA_i)
    =exp(z^N J_b/N) f_N(zC).                 (4.3)

The exponential is finite. For the literal negative series this is exp((-t)^N J_b/N), so an odd-N sign is not suppressed.

Let position mean the ordinary integer sum a_1+…+a_b. Then J_b lowers position by exactly N. Hence J_b^b=0, more precisely its nilpotence index is at most floor(b(N-1)/N)+1. The defect exp(z^N J_b/N)-I has only lowering degrees kN. Actual checked R preserves position exactly, so its conjugates preserve this bounded lowering filtration. The group generated using only such positive lowering defects is unipotent, independently of N for fixed b. No analogous claim for both raising directions is made.

5. Ordinary closure kills the ghost, while one normal wrap can pair

Let P lower total position by at least N, and let R_left,R_right preserve total position. Weighted pivotal partial trace over all but one strand kills R_left P R_right. Every closed-strand input index equals its output index, so the remaining cut would have to lower its index by at least N; its index range has only N values. This is a term-by-term zero, independent of the diagonal pivotal weights. Thus every positive term of (4.3) is invisible to ordinary one-cut closure, including after actual braid transport.

A single missing edge W=E_(N-1,0) raises position by N-1. A single such normal insertion can therefore leave a lowering-one cut current from J_b. All higher powers J_b^k, k>=2, are still killed by one-cut partial trace after one wrap. Pairing with a specified cut cochain that raises one can then have degree zero. This is a permission by degree, not a nonvanishing theorem for an arbitrary global source.

There is a universal literal nonzero example. For b=2 let W_tot=W tensor K+I tensor W and take the actual pivotal trace over the second color. Then

[ptr_2(J_2 W_tot)]_(0,1)=delta N x^(2-N),
delta=(-1)^(N-1), x=q_N^(N-1).              (5.1)

Only the closed input index zero with its second-leg wrap contributes. The coefficient follows from the unique mixed divided term. It is nonzero for every N.

An even simpler formulation uses the true divided Hopf adjoint, which is described next. Both are local normal-cochain tests, not identification with the selected global relative class.

6. The true divided Hopf-adjoint Ward action and its SL2 block

On End(D_m(q)) put

A_E(X)=ad_Ecal(X)=[Ecal,X]K^(-1),
A_F(X)=ad_F(X)=F X-K^(-1)X K F.

These are generic-q Hopf actions on matrices. The correct divided operators are

J_E^ad=[A_E^N/(1-Q^N)]root,
J_F^ad=[A_F^N/(1-Q^N)]root.                 (6.1)

They are not commutators with the individual divided matrix, which is zero. For example write A_E=U+V, U=L_Ecal R_(K^-1), V=-R_(Ecal K^-1). Then UV=Q VU and UN=VN=0 identically. The same divided-binomial calculation proves the regularity in (6.1). The F version uses the inverse parameter Q^(-1).

Let W=E_(N-1,0) and U_0=sum_(a=0)^(N-2)E_(a,a+1). Directly from these mixed terms, in the actual balanced root basis,

J_E^ad(W)=-N U_0, J_E^ad(U_0)=0,
J_F^ad(U_0)=N W, J_F^ad(W)=0.               (6.2)

For the first equation, the term with r=N-1-a left E actions and s=a+1 right E actions is the only one contributing to E_(a,a+1). Root product cancellation gives coefficient -N for each a. Similarly

J_F^ad(E_(a,a+1))=-N Q_N^(-a-1)W,

whose sum gives N W. Thus the two-dimensional span of W,U_0 is invariant, and -J_Ead/N,J_Fad/N are opposite standard nilpotents generating SL2 on it. Both divided directions do not generate a nilpotent group. The bounded unipotence in Section 4 is specifically the one-direction relative defect.

The actual E-Ward cut primitive B=E_(1,0)/(Q_N^(-1)-1) satisfies

[Ecal,B]=E_(0,0)-E_(1,1),
Tr(B J_E^ad(W))=-N/(Q_N^(-1)-1)!=0.         (6.3)

This is a specified nonzero pairing of a divided normal current, the actual missing edge, and a relative cut cochain. It does not prove that the original global selected source has this class.

Ordinary pivotal partial trace with pivot K intertwines these divided Hopf adjoints before specializing: it intertwines A_E, A_F, their powers and scalar divisions, all by the actual Hopf cap identities. Regularity then gives the root divided map. At the complete root, the ADO pivot K^(1-N) differs on each individual color by the scalar delta, so it has the same root equivariance. This is the correct divided Ward naturality; replacing it by ad_(J_b) would lose the mixed terms just computed.

7. The two normal directions and the actual auxiliary family

On the generic-M balanced auxiliary array the last raising formula before root specialization is

w(Q,M)=M Q^(-N)-M^(-1)
      =(Y-Z)/(M Z), Y=M^2, Z=Q^N.          (7.1)

It equals kappa=M-M^(-1) only on the root wall Z=1. The genuine complete integral color is instead

x=q^(N-1), M=-q^N, Y=Z,

and (7.1) vanishes identically on that path. At the root crossing of the two walls,

w=(Y-1)-(Z-1)+higher order.

For Q=Q_N(1+epsilon), fixed Y gives dw=-N epsilon; the integral path has dlog Y=N epsilon and gives dw=0. Thus its normal derivative is partial_epsilon+N partial_(log Y), not the prior fixed-meridian q-normal derivative.

The precise failure of the generic auxiliary matrices to be a genuine off-root Hopf module is visible without any deformation theory. With actual truncated matrices one computes

[Ecal,F]-rho(K-K^(-1))
   =-(1-Q^N) w(Q,M) E_(N-1,N-1).           (7.2)

It vanishes on either the root wall Z=1 or the complete integral color wall Y=Z. This keeps the coupled boundary source explicitly; it does not extend generic typical colors to the divided-power theory by assumption.

The off-root boundary closure also has an adapter. On complete integral color the genuine ordinary pivot is K. The auxiliary ADO pivot is K^(1-N), with ratio

K^(-N)v_a=q^(-Nm+2Na)v_a.

At q_N it is delta I. Its q-logarithmic first derivative is the nonconstant diagonal N(2a-m), multiplied by delta. The ordinary and ADO twists have ratio q^(-Nm); this too must be differentiated if the closed q-normal sources are compared. For a zero-framed b-strand knot of writhe ell, the exact routewise adapter is

q^[Nm(ell-b+1)+2N sum_(closed legs) a_j].      (7.3)

It is one at the root by ell=b-1 modulo two, but its derivative contains the recorded closed-position moment. Therefore equality of root invariants does not identify their off-root source jets.

8. Scope

The complete integral-color divided Ward action, finite fusion exponential, bounded positive lowering filtration, ordinary closure cancellation, and explicit relative normal tests are proved here. The true divided Hopf-adjoint diagram is also an exact actual matrix construction.

The remaining source-survival theorem still requires a map from the partially contracted actual geometric current module to this marked relative cut module, preserving (7.1)–(7.3). The nonzero local tests (5.1), (6.3) do not supply that map or its global coefficient. No global cycle, Stokes, regulator norm, or volume limit is inferred.
