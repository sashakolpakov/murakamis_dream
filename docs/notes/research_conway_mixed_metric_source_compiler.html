A mixed physical and Newton metric for the original Conway source

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

Multiplication takes one uniform physical packet and one Newton packet to a Newton packet with bilinear norm at most one. This follows from the actual adjoint formulas, without comparing the condition numbers of the two metrics. A fixed Conway tree can consequently retain one Newton leaf, or two leaves meeting at its highest product, while all other branches use the physical metric. The structural normalization cost is constant except for a possible factor N\sqrt N at the root. The resulting compact reciprocal integral keeps the original closing. No geometric lower bound is proved.

1. The two complete packet metrics

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, Q=q2Q=q^2, and x=qd=−q−1x=q^d=-q^{-1}. Use the actual physical pair algebra of [P]: 𝒜N=ℂ[Ω]≃ℂ[z]/PN,PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,dim⁡𝒜N=N.(1) \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[\Omega]\simeq\mathbb C[z]/P_N,\qquad P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\qquad \dim\mathcal A_N=N . \qquad\text{(1)} Here TNT_N is the Chebyshev polynomial. Every double-root first normal and the odd simple-root value remain in (1). Give the original balanced head basis w0,…,wdw_0,\ldots,w_d its uniform orthonormal metric. For f∈𝒜Nf\in\mathcal A_N, write ι(f)=f(Ω),∥f∥ph=∥ι(f)∥HS,∥f∥mat=∥ι(f)∥op.(2) \iota(f)=f(\Omega),\qquad \|f\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}=\|\iota(f)\|_{\mathrm{ HS}},\qquad \|f\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}=\|\iota(f)\|_{\mathrm{ op}}. \qquad\text{(2)} The Hilbert–Schmidt norm is unnormalized, so ∥1∥ph=N\|1\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}=N.

The other Hilbert norm makes the full Newton basis orthonormal: cj=q2j+1+q−2j−1,Sr=∏h=1r2sin⁡(πh/N),S0=1,br(z)=Sr−2∏j<r(cj−z),0≤r≤d.(3) c_j=q^{2j+1}+q^{-2j-1},\qquad S_r=\prod_{h=1}^r2\sin(\pi h/N),\quad S_0=1,\qquad b_r(z)=S_r^{-2}\prod_{j<r}(c_j-z),\quad 0\le r\le d . \qquad\text{(3)} Write ∥⋅∥Newt\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}} for this norm and f♯(z)=f(z‾)¯f^\sharp(z)=\overline{f(\bar z)} for coefficient reflection. All brb_r have real coefficients. The original unscaled turn 𝒯\mathcal T, multiplication Mf:g↦fgM_f:g\mapsto fg, and both original closing rows obey [A,P] 𝒯br=bd−r,𝒯2=I,∥𝒯f∥Newt=∥f∥Newt,∥𝒯f∥ph=∥f∥ph,Mf†Newt=𝒯Mf♯𝒯,λ0(f)=f(c0),λd(f)=ϕ(f),∥λ0∥Newt,*=∥λd∥Newt,*=1,λ0𝒯=λd.(4) \begin{gathered} \mathcal T b_r=b_{d-r},\qquad \mathcal T^2=I,\\ \|\mathcal T f\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}},\qquad \|\mathcal T f\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{ ph}},\\ M_f^{\dagger_{\mathrm{ Newt}}}=\mathcal T M_{f^\sharp}\mathcal T,\\ \lambda_0(f)=f(c_0),\qquad \lambda_d(f)=\phi(f),\qquad \|\lambda_0\|_{{\mathrm{ Newt}},*} =\|\lambda_d\|_{{\mathrm{ Newt}},*}=1,\qquad \lambda_0\mathcal T=\lambda_d . \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(4)} The physical dual norms of these two rows are at most one. The ordinary cups, cap, unrestricted mark, pivot and framing are exactly those fixing (4); no new closure is introduced here.

2. A constant-one bridge, including matrix coefficients

Let 𝖲\mathsf S exchange the two physical heads. In the balanced metric the literal generators satisfy E*=−F,F*=−E,K*=K−1.(5) E^*=-F,\qquad F^*=-E,\qquad K^*=K^{-1}. \qquad\text{(5)} Using EΣ=E1K2+E2E_\Sigma=E_1K_2+E_2, FΣ=F1+K1−1F2F_\Sigma=F_1+K_1^{-1}F_2, and the original Ω=FΣEΣ+qK1K2+q−1K1−1K2−1\Omega=F_\Sigma E_\Sigma+qK_1K_2+q^{-1}K_1^{-1}K_2^{-1}, this gives EΣ*=−𝖲FΣ𝖲,FΣ*=−𝖲EΣ𝖲,Ω*=𝖲Ω𝖲.(6) E_\Sigma^*=-\mathsf S F_\Sigma\mathsf S,\quad F_\Sigma^*=-\mathsf S E_\Sigma\mathsf S,\quad \Omega^*=\mathsf S\Omega\mathsf S . \qquad\text{(6)} Indeed adjoint reverses FΣEΣF_\Sigma E_\Sigma, and the first two identities restore that order after the exchange; the two Cartan terms are exchanged by conjugating their scalars. Polynomial functional calculus therefore proves ι(f)*=𝖲ι(f♯)𝖲,∥f♯∥mat=∥f∥mat.(7) \iota(f)^*=\mathsf S\iota(f^\sharp)\mathsf S,\qquad \|f^\sharp\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}. \qquad\text{(7)}

Theorem 1. For every two complete packets, ∥Mf∥Newt→Newt≤∥f∥mat,∥fg∥Newt≤∥f∥ph∥g∥Newt.(8) \boxed{\quad \|M_f\|_{{\mathrm{ Newt}}\to{\mathrm{ Newt}}}\le\|f\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}, \qquad \|fg\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}\le\|f\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}. \quad} \qquad\text{(8)} Commutativity permits exchange of the two inputs.

Proof. The same finite linear map B=(𝒯Mf♯𝒯)MfB=(\mathcal T M_{f^\sharp}\mathcal T)M_f is positive in the Newton metric, by (4). Its spectral radius is thus ∥Mf∥Newt2\|M_f\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}^2. Compute its radius instead using its operator norm in the physical packet metric. Actual composition of pair matrices gives ∥Mh∥ph→ph≤∥h∥mat\|M_h\|_{{\mathrm{ ph}}\to{\mathrm{ ph}}}\le\|h\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}. The physical unitarity of the genuine turn and (7) imply ∥Mf∥Newt2=rad⁡(B)≤∥B∥ph→ph≤∥f♯∥mat∥f∥mat=∥f∥mat2.(9) \|M_f\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}^2 =\operatorname{rad}(B) \le\|B\|_{{\mathrm{ ph}}\to{\mathrm{ ph}}} \le\|f^\sharp\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{ mat}} =\|f\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}^2. \qquad\text{(9)} Finally ∥f∥mat≤∥f∥ph\|f\|_{\mathrm{ mat}}\le\|f\|_{\mathrm{ ph}} proves (8). No change-of-frame bound between the metrics was used. ▫\square

There is also a full matrix amplification. Let 𝒇=(fij)∈Ma,b(𝒜N)\mathbf f=(f_{ij})\in M_{a,b}(\mathcal A_N), let 𝑴𝒇\mathbf M_{\mathbf f} act on bb Newton packets by (gj)j↦(∑jfijgj)i(g_j)_j\mapsto(\sum_j f_{ij}g_j)_i, and put 𝑭=(ι(fij))\mathbf F=(\iota(f_{ij})), acting on bb physical pairs. Then ∥𝑴𝒇∥Newtb→Newta≤∥𝑭∥op.(10) \boxed{\qquad \|\mathbf M_{\mathbf f}\|_{{\mathrm{ Newt}}^b\to{\mathrm{ Newt}}^a} \le\|\mathbf F\|_{\mathrm{ op}}. \qquad} \qquad\text{(10)} For completeness, set 𝒇‡=(fji♯)\mathbf f^\ddagger=(f_{ji}^\sharp) and 𝒯b=Ib⊗𝒯\mathcal T_b=I_b\otimes\mathcal T. Equation (4) gives 𝑴𝒇†=𝒯b𝑴𝒇‡𝒯a\mathbf M_{\mathbf f}^\dagger =\mathcal T_b\mathbf M_{\mathbf f^\ddagger}\mathcal T_a. The coefficient block matrix (ι(fji♯))(\iota(f_{ji}^\sharp)) is (Ib⊗𝖲)𝑭*(Ia⊗𝖲)(I_b\otimes\mathsf S)\mathbf F^*(I_a\otimes\mathsf S). Physical left multiplication by a block matrix has norm at most that block matrix’s operator norm on the direct sum of pair spaces. The outer packet turns also have physical norm one. Applying (9) to 𝑴𝒇†𝑴𝒇\mathbf M_{\mathbf f}^\dagger\mathbf M_{\mathbf f} proves (10), also for rectangular sizes. The bound is independent of a,ba,b. It is not a claim that the Hilbert tensor flattening of the bilinear map in (8) has norm one.

Taking g=1g=1 in the first inequality of (8) gives the useful same-packet comparison ∥f∥Newt≤∥f∥mat≤∥f∥ph.(11) \|f\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}\le\|f\|_{\mathrm{ mat}} \le\|f\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}. \qquad\text{(11)}

3. One Newton path through an arbitrary literal tree

Fix a literal tree with LL prescribed leaf occurrences, ss proper vertices, products and genuine turns. Its original scalar is KN=ηNλ(Atop)K_N=\eta_N\lambda(A_{\mathrm{ top}}), where λ∈{λ0,λd}\lambda\in\{\lambda_0,\lambda_d\} and ηN\eta_N is its recorded known external factor. Every prescribed leaf is its entire actual packet AℓA_\ell, not its distinct-root values.

Choose any leaf ℓ*\ell_*. Give every vertex on its path to the root the Newton metric and every off-path vertex the physical metric. At a product on the path, exactly one child has the Newton metric. Equation (8) bounds that bilinear map by one. Off-path products have physical bilinear norm at most one by matrix composition. Turns have norm one in either metric, and the root row has dual norm one. These assertions hold on all packet inputs, not only on the prescribed forward field.

For each proper vertex and the scalar root choose the known budgets bv={16max⁡(1,∥Aℓ∥Newt),v=ℓ=ℓ*,16max⁡(1,∥Aℓ∥ph),v=ℓ≠ℓ*,16,v is a product or turn,broot=16max⁡(1,|ηN|).(12) b_v= \begin{cases} 16\max(1,\|A_\ell\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}),&v=\ell=\ell_*,\\ 16\max(1,\|A_\ell\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}),&v=\ell\ne\ell_*,\\ 16,&v\text{ is a product or turn}, \end{cases} \qquad b_{\mathrm{ root}}=16\max(1,|\eta_N|). \qquad\text{(12)} Thus every normalized map, leaf and closing has its respective bilinear, linear or vector norm at most 1/161/16. There is no exponential Newton multiplication budget. Relative to the physical budgets of [P], only one leaf factor changes: BoneBph=max⁡(1,∥Aℓ*∥Newt)max⁡(1,∥Aℓ*∥ph)≤1,Bone=∏vbv.(13) {\frac{B_{\mathrm{ one}}}{ B_{\mathrm{ ph}}}} ={\frac{\max(1,\|A_{\ell_*}\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}) }{\max(1,\|A_{\ell_*}\|_{\mathrm{ ph}})}} \le1,\qquad B_{\mathrm{ one}}=\prod_v b_v . \qquad\text{(13)} This is a comparison of known normalization products, not of the numerical minima or integrals in different packet metrics.

4. Two Newton paths and one terminal quotient metric

Suppose L≥2L\ge2. Let v*v_* be the highest binary product; above it there are only genuine turns before the scalar closing. Choose one leaf in each child subtree of v*v_*. Use the one-path assignment inside each child subtree, making both packets entering v*v_* Newton packets. All remaining vertices below it are physical.

At v*v_* use the already constructed multiplication quotient of [Q]. In fixed Newton coordinates let M:ℋNewt⊗2ℋNewt→𝒜N,C=MM†,∥y∥quot2=y†C−1y.(14) M:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{ Newt}}\otimes_2\mathcal H_{\mathrm{ Newt}} \longrightarrow\mathcal A_N,\qquad C=MM^\dagger,\qquad \|y\|_{\mathrm{ quot}}^2=y^\dagger C^{-1}y . \qquad\text{(14)} Multiplication is surjective since M(y⊗1)=yM(y\otimes1)=y; hence C>0C>0, with every boundary coordinate retained. The map C−1/2MC^{-1/2}M is a coisometry, so the product into this quotient metric has bilinear norm at most one. Transport the quotient metric through each turn above v*v_*: define ∥𝒯y∥next=∥y∥previous\|\mathcal T y\|_{\mathrm{ next}}=\|y\|_{\mathrm{ previous}}. Every such turn has norm one by definition, with its literal unscaled matrix retained.

If there are hh turns above v*v_*, put λ′=λ𝒯h\lambda'=\lambda\mathcal T^h. This is either λ0\lambda_0 or λd\lambda_d. The exact original rows give [A,Q] ∥λ0M∥⊗2,*=1,∥λdM∥⊗2,*=N.(15) \|\lambda_0M\|_{\otimes_2,*}=1,\qquad \|\lambda_dM\|_{\otimes_2,*}=\sqrt N . \qquad\text{(15)} Indeed λ0(ab)=a0b0\lambda_0(ab)=a_0b_0, while λd(ab)=a𝖳Jb\lambda_d(ab)=a^{\mathsf T}Jb with JJ the reversal matrix. Their flattened rows have respectively one and NN unit entries. Consequently the final transported quotient has root dual cost γN={1,λ′=λ0,N,λ′=λd.(16) \gamma_N= \begin{cases}1,&\lambda'=\lambda_0,\\ \sqrt N,&\lambda'=\lambda_d. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(16)} Use Newton leaf budgets at the two selected leaves, physical budgets at all others, budget 1616 at every product and turn, and broot=16max⁡(1,|ηN|γN).(17) b_{\mathrm{ root}}=16\max(1,|\eta_N|\gamma_N). \qquad\text{(17)} This gives a second full-dimensional compiler with every normalized local norm at most 1/161/16. Its budget product satisfies BtwoBph=max⁡(1,|ηN|γN)max⁡(1,|ηN|)∏ℓselectedmax⁡(1,∥Aℓ∥Newt)max⁡(1,∥Aℓ∥ph).(18) {\frac{B_{\mathrm{ two}}}{ B_{\mathrm{ ph}}}} ={\frac{\max(1,|\eta_N|\gamma_N)}{\max(1,|\eta_N|)}} \prod_{\ell\ {\mathrm{ selected}}} {\frac{\max(1,\|A_\ell\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}) }{\max(1,\|A_\ell\|_{\mathrm{ ph}})}}. \qquad\text{(18)} The quotient and its transport depend only on the original operations, not on the selected leaf values or the unknown scalar. No comparison of its condition number with the physical metric is used.

The direct scalar upper bound is slightly sharper than the flattened root budget: both bilinear rows λ0(ab)\lambda_0(ab) and λd(ab)\lambda_d(ab) have norm one on separately unit Newton inputs. Inducting below v*v_* therefore gives |KN|≤|ηN|∏ℓselected∥Aℓ∥Newt∏ℓother∥Aℓ∥ph.(19) |K_N|\le|\eta_N| \prod_{\ell\ {\mathrm{ selected}}}\|A_\ell\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}} \prod_{\ell\ {\mathrm{ other}}}\|A_\ell\|_{\mathrm{ ph}}. \qquad\text{(19)} There is no factor N\sqrt N in (19). That factor in (16) is the linear dual norm required for the full quotient packet compiler.

5. The same original scalar in a compact reciprocal integral

Apply [C] to either assignment above, with its actual packet metrics and budget product BmixB_{\mathrm{ mix}}. Multiply all ss proper budgets and the root budget by NN: B̂mix=Ns+1Bmix,σN=(16N)−1.(20) \widehat B_{\mathrm{ mix}}=N^{s+1}B_{\mathrm{ mix}},\qquad \sigma_N=(16N)^{-1}. \qquad\text{(20)} In orthonormal coordinates for these metrics the residual map still has own-output coefficient −I-I, polynomial triangular inverse, and real Jacobian one. Its energy, including the original scalar row, has degree at most four. Keep one child per binary product and solve the other packet equations exactly. Write Fmix(u)F_{\mathrm{ mix}}(u) for the remaining residuals and scalar row. Then, with n=N(L−1)n=N(L-1) and the product 𝒰N\mathcal U_N of retained unit balls, 𝒥mix=n!πn∫𝒰N∥Fmix(u)∥−2(n+1)du.(21) \mathcal J_{\mathrm{ mix}}={\frac{n!}{\pi^n}} \int_{\mathcal U_N}\|F_{\mathrm{ mix}}(u)\|^{-2(n+1)}du . \qquad\text{(21)} The measure is rebuilt in the stated orthonormal coordinates. For a fixed tree, [C] proves uniformly over its normalized leaf choices |KN|2=B̂mix2𝒥mix(1+O𝒯(N−1))(KN≠0).(22) \boxed{\qquad |K_N|^2={\frac{\widehat B_{\mathrm{ mix}}^{\,2}}{\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ mix}}}} \bigl(1+O_{\mathscr T}(N^{-1})\bigr) \qquad(K_N\ne0). \qquad} \qquad\text{(22)} At a source zero the integral is infinite and its reciprocal is zero. The one-point convention covers L=1L=1. All phases and full Hermite directions survive these metric choices and forward substitutions.

The actual single signed crossing has, with κ=qd2/2\kappa=q^{d^2/2}, [A] R±1=κ±1∑r=0d(−q)±rbr,∥R±1∥Newt=N.(23) R^{\pm1}=\kappa^{\pm1}\sum_{r=0}^d(-q)^{\pm r}b_r,\qquad \|R^{\pm1}\|_{\mathrm{ Newt}}=\sqrt N . \qquad\text{(23)} Thus one or two such selected leaves have this explicit polynomial leaf budget. An arbitrary fixed RpR^p must retain its actual Newton norm; no polynomial bound for all powers is asserted.

The gain is a source-exact choice of packet metrics with smaller known leaf factors and no large Newton structural multiplication budget. Equations (21)–(22) define new normalized integrals, not a free change in the value of an earlier integral. The original cap and the new budgets must both remain. The dimension and denominator power grow with NN when L>1L>1. A geometric upper estimate on 𝒥mix/B̂mix2\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ mix}}/\widehat B_{\mathrm{ mix}}^{\,2}, or an original closing lower certificate, is still required. There is no new volume-conjecture case or finite-exception theorem.
