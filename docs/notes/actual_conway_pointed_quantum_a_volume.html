The pointed quantum AA-period has the strict Conway knot volume

31 October 2026. Internally reviewed research corollary. This gives an operator-side expression for the original invariant and proves its volume limit for the fixed long-twist knots of the strict Conway tree theorem. The operator belongs to the actual literature ADO annihilator ideal. Its finite pointing is fixed by ordinary small-color periods; it is not chosen separately at each root of unity.

The conclusion concerns the selected complete period and its norm. Identification of the selected classical main-meridian geometric factor or of an entire quantum module is a separate stronger problem.

1. The knot, the literature family and the coordinates

Fix a strict literal Conway expression τ=C(FG)\tau=C(FG) in the sense of the linked theorem. Fix all integer twist weights 𝒏\mathbf n beyond that theorem’s threshold, with the original endpoint pairing a knot and with no internal closed component. Put K=Kτ(𝒏),V=Vol⁡(S3\K)>0,qN=exp⁡(πi/N). K=K_\tau(\mathbf n),\qquad V=\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K)>0,\qquad q_N=\exp(\pi i/N). \tag{1} The expression and weights are fixed before NN tends to infinity. Only the auxiliary crossing-circle cusps are filled; the main knot cusp stays unfilled. The limit below runs through all sufficiently large integer levels, with both parities and composite levels retained.

Use the zero-framed reduced ADO family defined by the original unrolled crossings and pivotal trace. Its conventions and its comparison with the original reduced enhanced Kashaev evaluation are proved in the ADO pointing note. There are two different coordinates: x=qNα,M=−qNx,FN(M)=fN(−qN−1M), x=q_N^\alpha,\qquad M=-q_Nx,\qquad F_N(M)=f_N(-q_N^{-1}M), \tag{2} where fN(x)f_N(x) denotes the raw unrolled family and FNF_N the marked meridian family. At the complete color α=N−1\alpha=N-1, the raw coordinate is x=−qN−1x=-q_N^{-1}, and the marked coordinate is M=1M=1. At the quotient color α=j−1\alpha=j-1, they are x=qNj−1x=q_N^{j-1} and M=−qNjM=-q_N^j.

The single-component quotient-color proof gives the exact identities FN(−qNj)=Cj(qN)(1≤j≤N),FN(1)=CN(qN)=KN(K). F_N(-q_N^j)=C_j(q_N)\quad(1\le j\le N),\qquad F_N(1)=C_N(q_N)=K_N(K). \tag{3} Here Cj(q)C_j(q) is the actual zero-framed reduced jj-dimensional period, with unknot value one. Each fixed CjC_j is a Laurent polynomial in the recorded quantum parameter, analytic at q=1q=1, and Cj(1)=1C_j(1)=1. The proof uses the invariant quotient of the original color and the single-cycle cut trace; it is not an extrapolation from a link component.

The literature all-level ideal is defined by annihilation of the actual ADO family at all but finitely many levels, in the qq-Weyl algebra. This is the convention of Brown–Dimofte–Garoufalidis–Geer, equation (1.5). Their holonomicity and ideal-comparison theorems are not proof inputs here. The repository’s finite cut-state argument supplies existence directly, as recalled next.

2. A genuine operator exists before the period is evaluated

The finite cut-state annihilator theorem constructs a nonzero level-independent operator for every knot. Its auxiliary generic-qq, NN-dimensional cut matrices specialize to the actual root state; they are not asserted to define a topological invariant away from the root. Their finite summands are aligned proper qq-hypergeometric products on the original integral support polytope.

The essential boundary safeguards are part of that proof. With s0s_0 internal summation variables, the span of the actions xazcTbS1β1⋯Ss0βs0,0≤a,c,b,βi≤R,z=qN, x^a z^c T^b S_1^{\beta_1}\cdots S_{s_0}^{\beta_{s_0}}, \qquad 0\le a,c,b,\beta_i\le R,\qquad z=q^N, \tag{4} has dimension O(Rs0+2)O(R^{s_0+2}), including the zero-extended boundaries. A rank-rr boundary stratum has O(Rr)O(R^r) possible labels and a remaining monomial span O(Rs0+2−r)O(R^{s_0+2-r}). The O(Rs0+3)O(R^{s_0+3}) formal operators therefore give a nonzero relation without internal multiplication variables. Taking the first nonzero coefficient in its expansion at Si=1S_i=1 via finite exponential moments produces an actual relation for the sum, even when direct summation of the first relation vanishes. Finally, any common factor (z+1)h(z+1)^h is canceled over generic qq before setting z=−1z=-1. The lift is regular at qNq_N. These steps preserve the finite support and do not divide by a zero root-specialized factor.

Transport the resulting raw-xx operator by the exact substitution x=−q−1Mx=-q^{-1}M. In marked reduced coordinates, choose any fixed nonzero actual annihilator and clear its rational denominators: P(q,M,T)=∑i=0dai(q,M)Ti,TM=qMT,ai∈ℂ[q±1,M±1],d≥1. P(q,M,T)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_i(q,M)T^i,\qquad TM=qMT,\qquad a_i\in\mathbb C[q^{\pm1},M^{\pm1}],\qquad d\ge1. \tag{5} Remove a common shift if necessary, so a0ad≠0a_0a_d\ne0. For all but finitely many NN, the actual equation is ∑i=0dai(qN,M)FN(qNiM)=0. \sum_{i=0}^{d}a_i(q_N,M)F_N(q_N^iM)=0. \tag{6} An order-zero nonzero multiplication operator cannot annihilate the nonzero actual family. Thus the positive order in (5) is automatic.

The localized skew polynomial ring over ℂ(q,M)\mathbb C(q,M) is left Euclidean. The actual nonzero annihilator ideal consequently has a unique monic element of least order. One may use that generator, or any other specified genuine annihilator, in everything below. The finite existence proof does not claim an efficient algorithm for the minimal generator or identify its classical symbol. In particular, (5) is not defined by the rootwise ratio FN(qNM)/FN(M)F_N(q_NM)/F_N(M).

3. Fixed small colors determine the exact operator period

The following reconstruction is the all-level finite-pointing theorem, in the marked MM-coordinate of (2). For a fixed integer j≥1j\ge1, (3) and (6) imply that the Laurent polynomial Hj(q)=∑i=0dai(q,−qj)Cj+i(q) H_j(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_i(q,-q^j)C_{j+i}(q) \tag{7} vanishes at every sufficiently large qNq_N with N≥j+dN\ge j+d. It therefore vanishes identically. This derives the generic recurrence from the actual root family and the quotient colors, without an assumed equality of ADO and colored-Jones recursion ideals.

Write the collected leading coefficient as ad(q,M)=∑(a,b)∈𝒮ca,bqaMb,Aq=max𝒮a−min𝒮a,D=max𝒮b−min𝒮b,s=|𝒮|. a_d(q,M)=\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathcal S}c_{a,b}q^aM^b,\qquad A_q=\max_{\mathcal S}a-\min_{\mathcal S}a,\qquad D=\max_{\mathcal S}b-\min_{\mathcal S}b,\qquad s=|\mathcal S|. \tag{8} Choose j0=Aq+1j_0=A_q+1. For j≥j0j\ge j_0, the exponents a+bja+bj of ad(q,−qj)a_d(q,-q^j) are distinct for distinct support pairs: a collision with different bb’s would give j≤Aqj\le A_q. Hence the leading coefficient is never identically zero on these generic trajectories.

Let Bj(q)B_j(q) be the forward dd-dimensional companion matrix of (7), evaluated at M=−qjM=-q^j. Its first d−1d-1 rows shift the coordinates left, and its last row is −1ad(q,−qj)(a0(q,−qj),…,ad−1(q,−qj)). -\frac{1}{a_d(q,-q^j)} \bigl(a_0(q,-q^j),\ldots,a_{d-1}(q,-q^j)\bigr). \tag{9} Fix, once and for all, c0(q)=(Cj0(q),…,Cj0+d−1(q))𝗍,rN(q)=ed𝗍BN−d(q)⋯Bj0(q). \begin{aligned} c_0(q)&=(C_{j_0}(q),\ldots,C_{j_0+d-1}(q))^{\mathsf t},\\ r_N(q)&=e_d^{\mathsf t}B_{N-d}(q)\cdots B_{j_0}(q). \end{aligned} \tag{10} The generic recurrence gives rN(q)c0(q)=CN(q)r_N(q)c_0(q)=C_N(q) identically. Consequently the operator period is 𝒫N(P,c0)=CT⁡t=0[rN(qN(1+t))c0(qN(1+t))]=KN(K). \boxed{\quad \mathscr P_N(P,c_0) =\operatorname{CT}_{t=0} \bigl[r_N(q_N(1+t))c_0(q_N(1+t))\bigr] =K_N(K). \quad} \tag{11} Here CT\operatorname{CT} takes the constant coefficient of the whole Laurent germ. Its product is regular, even if individual companion factors have poles. No singular step is deleted or evaluated by separately substituting a zero denominator.

Only a bounded number of quantum-parameter jets is needed. For all sufficiently large NN, ad(qN,M)a_d(q_N,M) is nonzero and has at most DD zeros among the distinct orbit points −qNj-q_N^j in (10). At any such step, ad(q,−qj)a_d(q,-q^j) has vanishing order at most s−1s-1: if a nonzero Laurent polynomial with ss monomials and distinct exponents vanished with multiplicity ss, its first ss logarithmic derivatives would contradict the Vandermonde determinant. Thus BN:=∑j=j0N−dord⁡q=qNad(q,−qj)≤B:=D(s−1). B_N:=\sum_{j=j_0}^{N-d} \operatorname{ord}_{q=q_N}a_d(q,-q^j) \le B:=D(s-1). \tag{12} This is a bound on moving-qq tangencies, not merely fixed-qq meridian zeros.

Write rN(qN(1+t))=∑ℓ≥−BNRN,ℓtℓ,Jk(N)=[tk]c0(qN(1+t)). \begin{aligned} r_N(q_N(1+t))&=\sum_{\ell\ge-B_N}R_{N,\ell}t^\ell,\\ J_k(N)&=[t^k]c_0(q_N(1+t)). \end{aligned} Then (11) is the finite formula 𝒫N(P,c0)=∑k=0BNRN,−kJk(N),BN≤D(s−1). \boxed{\quad \mathscr P_N(P,c_0)= \sum_{k=0}^{B_N}R_{N,-k}J_k(N),\qquad B_N\le D(s-1). \quad} \tag{13} Every Jk(N)J_k(N), 0≤k≤B0\le k\le B, is bounded as N→∞N\to\infty. Its entries are qNkCj(k)(qN)/k!q_N^kC_j^{(k)}(q_N)/k! for a fixed finite set of colors, analytic near q=1q=1. The operator, its order, its support, j0j_0, and these initial polynomials are fixed after the knot and its twist weights are fixed. They do not vary to fit a level or a desired asymptotic.

4. The operator-period volume theorem and its peripheral factor

Pointed quantum AA-period theorem. For the knot (1), for any specified actual all-level annihilator (5) and the fixed pointing (10), limN→∞2πNlog⁡|∑k=0BNRN,−kJk(N)|=V. \boxed{\quad \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N} \log\left| \sum_{k=0}^{B_N}R_{N,-k}J_k(N) \right| =V. \quad} \tag{14} The same theorem holds for the localized minimum-order generator. The expression inside the logarithm is nonzero for all sufficiently large NN.

Proof: the exact whole-germ identity (11) identifies the left side with the original invariant at every sufficiently large level. The strict Conway theorem proves its nonzero asymptotic and the limit (14) for the fixed long-filled knot. No additional choice of a root-level solution, rank-one kernel, semisimplicity hypothesis, or regular orbit is involved. This proof uses the already established original source volume theorem; it does not claim a new asymptotic estimate obtained directly from the companion matrix coefficients.

For the full literature ADO state, all peripheral data remain explicit: 𝒩N(M)=dN(M)FN(M),dN(M)=ηNM−M−1MN−M−N=ηNMN−1∑r=0N−1M2r,ηN=iN+3,dN(1)=ηNN,𝒩N(1)=ηNN𝒫N(P,c0). \begin{aligned} \mathcal N_N(M)&=d_N(M)F_N(M),\\ d_N(M)&=\eta_N\frac{M-M^{-1}}{M^N-M^{-N}} =\eta_N\frac{M^{N-1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}M^{2r}}, &\eta_N&=i^{N+3},\\ d_N(1)&=\frac{\eta_N}{N},& \mathcal N_N(1)&=\frac{\eta_N}{N}\mathscr P_N(P,c_0). \end{aligned} \tag{15} The last row evaluates the whole regular germ at M=1M=1. At the reference point M=−qNM=-q_N, the full state instead has the nonzero residue fixed in the pointing theorem. Replacing the full state by an unspecified scalar normalization would lose this datum.

The full-to-reduced operator transport is the fixed Ore gauge dN−1TdN=g(qN,M)T,g(q,M)=−q−1q2M2−1M2−1. d_N^{-1}Td_N=g(q_N,M)T,\qquad g(q,M)=-q^{-1}\frac{q^2M^2-1}{M^2-1}. \tag{16} Thus a full-state literature operator may first be transported to (5). At M=1M=1, (15), rather than a formal substitution into the meromorphic coefficient gg, specifies its actual period. Since |ηN|=1|\eta_N|=1 and log⁡N/N→0\log N/N\to0, the full-state period has the same volume limit (14). This keeps the generic peripheral factor and its completion lattice, not only its exponential rate.

5. A positive determinant version of the same operator limit

There is also a positive algebraic left side, from the pointed Gram-period theorem. For completeness, its data are specified here. Use the fixed bound B=D(s−1)B=D(s-1). At each level introduce the BB-jets of the color values for j0≤j≤Nj_0\le j\le N. Let 𝖬Ny=bN\mathsf M_Ny=b_N be the square banded system which fixes the first dd jet blocks to the actual jets of (10), and imposes (7) modulo tB+1t^{B+1} at every subsequent step. Let 𝖱N\mathsf R_N select the constant coefficient of every color block, and put 𝖧N=𝖬N†𝖬N,vN=𝖬N†bN,βN=bN†bN,νN=dim⁡ker⁡𝖬N,QN(λ)=det⁡(𝖧N+λI),DN(λ,z)=det⁡(𝖧N+λI+z𝖱N†𝖱NvNvN†βN). \begin{aligned} \mathsf H_N&=\mathsf M_N^\dagger\mathsf M_N,& v_N&=\mathsf M_N^\dagger b_N,& \beta_N&=b_N^\dagger b_N,& \nu_N&=\dim\ker\mathsf M_N,\\ Q_N(\lambda)&=\det(\mathsf H_N+\lambda I),\\ D_N(\lambda,z)&= \det\begin{pmatrix} \mathsf H_N+\lambda I+z\mathsf R_N^\dagger\mathsf R_N&v_N\\ v_N^\dagger&\beta_N \end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned} \tag{17} The variables λ,z\lambda,z are ordinary polynomial variables, not peripheral eigenvalues. All matrices come from (5) and the fixed small-color jets. Their size is O(N)O(N) and their bandwidth and jet block size are fixed.

The finite jet valuation argument gives ker⁡𝖬N⊂ker⁡𝖱N\ker\mathsf M_N\subset\ker\mathsf R_N and νN≤B\nu_N\le B. Consequently the exact positive coefficient ratio is 𝒢N(P,j0)=[λνNz]DN(λ,z)[λνN]QN(λ)=∑j=j0N|Cj(qN)|2. \mathscr G_N(P,j_0)= \frac{[\lambda^{\nu_N}z]D_N(\lambda,z)} {[\lambda^{\nu_N}]Q_N(\lambda)} =\sum_{j=j_0}^{N}|C_j(q_N)|^2. \tag{18} The denominator is positive. To see the identity, restrict to the orthogonal complement of ker⁡𝖬N\ker\mathsf M_N, where 𝖧N\mathsf H_N is positive definite. The derivative at z=0z=0 of the bordered Schur complement is the squared norm of the unique constant-color output. Both determinants acquire precisely the common factor λνN\lambda^{\nu_N}. Cauchy–Binet expresses their coefficients as nonnegative sums of squared minors, with the actual source border retained.

The positive ordinary-color bound implies, for any fixed braid presentation with bb strands, 𝒢N(P,j0)=|KN(K)|2+OK(N2b−1). \mathscr G_N(P,j_0)=|K_N(K)|^2+O_K(N^{2b-1}). \tag{19} The ordinary colors j<Nj<N have polynomial size; no such bound is asserted for the complete color. Equations (14) and (19) therefore give the positive operator-period volume identity limN→∞πNlog⁡𝒢N(P,j0)=V. \boxed{\qquad \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\pi}{N}\log\mathscr G_N(P,j_0)=V. \qquad} \tag{20} Two actual operators, with a common sufficiently large fixed starting color, give exactly the same value in (18). Different fixed starting colors change it only by finitely many bounded terms.

In the normalization of the marked regulator norm, log⁡∥𝒫hyp∥=2V/π\log\|\mathcal P_{\mathrm{hyp}}\|=2V/\pi. Thus (14) and (20) also say 4Nlog⁡|𝒫N(P,c0)|−log⁡∥𝒫hyp∥→0,2Nlog⁡𝒢N(P,j0)−log⁡∥𝒫hyp∥→0. \frac4N\log|\mathscr P_N(P,c_0)| -\log\|\mathcal P_{\mathrm{hyp}}\|\longrightarrow0, \qquad \frac2N\log\mathscr G_N(P,j_0) -\log\|\mathcal P_{\mathrm{hyp}}\|\longrightarrow0. \tag{21} These are norm equalities for the actual selected periods. They do not assert an isomorphism of unpointed torsors or of quantum modules.

6. What has been settled and what remains

For the covered knots, a genuine literature quantum AA-annihilator, its finitely pointed complete period, and that period’s volume limit are all available. The construction of the operator precedes the period evaluation; the bounded initial jets contain no level-dependent exponential normalization. The scalar equality (11) is exact, so no new knot invariant is being substituted.

The stronger classical main-meridian question remains separate: identify the selected geometric factor in the q→1q\to1 characteristic polynomial, and compare the corresponding pointed quantum module with the original marked regulator germ. The auxiliary crossing-circle deformation used in the volume theorem holds the main meridian complete; it is not by itself a main-meridian ADO WKB theorem. Nor does a rational root-state solution identify a root-central spectral curve with the classical AA-polynomial: it always supplies a central monodromy eigenvalue 11, as the pointing note explains.

No assertion is made here about equality of full annihilator ideals with another knot recurrence ideal, equality of classical symbols, minimal-order computation, growing syntax, arbitrary short twists, or all arborescent presentations. None of these is required for (14), (20), or (21).
