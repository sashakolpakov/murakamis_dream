Saturated angular faces: reduced energy and matched signs

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The endpoint-face theorem already computes the energy of an original angular endpoint and characterizes its feasible deletion. This note assembles that result with the reduced-angle fiber problem and finite real-period compactness. The resulting identity is for the entire reduced energy on a compatible face, with arbitrary positive charges. At an actual finite-period limit, the sharp corner data give additional sign and incidence constraints. These specify the classical rate a genuine source reduction would have to produce. They do not identify an angular side current with a deleted quantum source.

1. The original charged face and its exact fiber

Fix the original finite connected circle tree, a nonempty selected set SS, and all edges incident to SS, counted once. Outside angles are complete, and every selected vertex has positive original degree. Keep the original real powers nvn_v and positive real charges cvc_v. In the integral filling convention nv=ηv−2pvn_v=\eta_v-2p_v, neither the carrier nor the slope is changed. Write tv=sv+ihv,rv,e=kv,e+igv,e,Pv=∑e∋vrv,e−invtv/2.(1) t_v=s_v+i h_v,\qquad r_{v,e}=k_{v,e}+i g_{v,e},\qquad P_v=\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}-i n_vt_v/2. \qquad\text{(1)} The closed original angle body has |sv|≤π|s_v|\leq\pi, outside sb=0s_b=0, and |sv|+2|gw,e|≤π,|sw|+2|gv,e|≤π,∑e∋vgv,e−nvsv/2=−πcv(v∈S).(2) |s_v|+2|g_{w,e}|\leq\pi,\qquad |s_w|+2|g_{v,e}|\leq\pi,\qquad \sum_{e\ni v}g_{v,e}-n_vs_v/2=-\pi c_v\quad(v\in S). \qquad\text{(2)} Assume strict feasibility. Its energy and reduced energy are ℰG=4∑e(EP,e+EQ,e),eG,c(s)=ming satisfying (2)ℰG(s,g).(3) \mathcal E_G=4\sum_e(E_{P,e}+E_{Q,e}),\qquad e_{G,c}(s)=\min_{g\text{ satisfying }(2)}\mathcal E_G(s,g). \qquad\text{(3)} Only nonempty fibers are assigned a value. No uniqueness of a closed boundary fiber minimizer is asserted.

Choose a nonempty set C⊆SC\subseteq S, signs εv=±1\varepsilon_v=\pm1, and set sv=εvπs_v=\varepsilon_v\pi on CC. Delete CC and every incident edge; let rCr_C be the number of deleted edges, counted once. Write G′G' for the retained forest and c′=c|S\Cc'=c|_{S\setminus C}. Retain all surviving selected vertices, including isolated ones, with their original powers and rows. In particular, an isolated vertex must satisfy nwsw=2πcw,|sw|≤π.(4) n_ws_w=2\pi c_w,\qquad |s_w|\leq\pi. \qquad\text{(4)} It is not silently removed. The retained outside data remain complete. Define eG′,c′(s′)e_{G',c'}(s') by the same closed fiber minimization, with the sum over retained edges; this also covers a fiber with no edge variables if its remaining rows hold.

Proposition 1 (exact reduced face factorization). The full fiber over (επ,s′)(\varepsilon\pi,s') is nonempty if and only if the retained fiber is nonempty and |εvnv−2cv|≤Lv(s′):=∑e=vww∉C(1−|sw|/π)(v∈C).(5) \boxed{\quad |\varepsilon_v n_v-2c_v| \leq L_v(s') :=\sum_{\substack{e=vw\\w\notin C}} (1-|s_w|/\pi)\quad(v\in C). \quad} \qquad\text{(5)} For every such s′s', eG,c(επ,s′)=rCπM∞+eG′,c′(s′),M∞=4GCat/π.(6) \boxed{\quad e_{G,c}(\varepsilon\pi,s') =r_C\pi M_\infty+e_{G',c'}(s'),\qquad M_\infty=4G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/\pi. \quad} \qquad\text{(6)} The surviving charge lifts in this identity are exactly c′c'. Here GCat=∑ℓ≥0(−1)ℓ/(2ℓ+1)2G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}=\sum_{\ell\geq0}(-1)^\ell/(2\ell+1)^2.

Proof. At an edge incident to v∈Cv\in C, (2) forces gw,e=0g_{w,e}=0. Both incidences on an internal CC-edge therefore vanish. Every restored incidence at a surviving vertex is zero, so its row is precisely its retained row, including (4).

The remaining choices at a removed vertex are independent intervals |gv,e|≤(π−|sw|)/2(w∉C),∑e=vw,w∉Cgv,e=π2(εvnv−2cv).(7) |g_{v,e}|\leq(\pi-|s_w|)/2\quad(w\notin C),\qquad \sum_{e=vw,\ w\notin C}g_{v,e} =\frac{\pi}{2}(\varepsilon_v n_v-2c_v). \qquad\text{(7)} Their sum interval gives (5). Choices for different removed vertices are independent, even if they have a common surviving neighbor. Consequently the full fiber is exactly the product of the retained fiber and the interval-section polytopes (7).

For clarity, the original square coordinates at an ordered edge are AP=sv/4−gw,e/2,AQ=sv/4+gw,e/2,yP=gv,e/2+sw/4,yQ=gv,e/2−sw/4.(8) A_P=s_v/4-g_{w,e}/2,\quad A_Q=s_v/4+g_{w,e}/2,\qquad y_P=g_{v,e}/2+s_w/4,\quad y_Q=g_{v,e}/2-s_w/4. \qquad\text{(8)} The archived endpoint-face computation gives the same value at every side and every corner: E▫|∂[−π/4,π/4]2=GCat/2=πM∞/8.(9) E_\square|_{\partial[-\pi/4,\pi/4]^2} =G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/2=\pi M_\infty/8. \qquad\text{(9)} For example its normalization is E▫(π/4,0)=14∫0π/2log⁡(sec⁡u+tan⁡u)du=12∫π/4π/2log⁡tan⁡vdv=GCat/2.(10) E_\square(\pi/4,0) =\frac14\int_0^{\pi/2}\log(\sec u+\tan u)\,du =\frac12\int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2}\log\tan v\,dv =G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/2. \qquad\text{(10)} The side derivative is zero in the continuous boundary limit, and continuity joins the side values at all corners. Each deleted edge has both square factors on their boundaries by (8), hence contributes exactly 4(EP,e+EQ,e)=4GCat=πM∞.(11) 4(E_{P,e}+E_{Q,e}) =4G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}=\pi M_\infty. \qquad\text{(11)} Every retained square is unchanged. The energy is therefore the retained energy plus this constant on the entire product fiber. Taking minima proves (6). ▫\square

Proposition 1 is the charged reduced-fiber form of the existing endpoint-face theorem. The product proof does not require a matched complex limit or sharp corners. The same algebra remains valid for other specified real charges, including zero, whenever the displayed fibers are defined; it does not authorize changing a charge in the original finite source.

2. The uniform classical gap on this face

Suppose additionally that the original positive solution at P=−πicP=-\pi i c is supplied. Let z0z_0 be its angle assignment, E0=ℰG(z0)E_0=\mathcal E_G(z_0), and let mm be the original number of edges. The reduced-angle theorem gives Δc=min∂𝒫cℰG−E0>0,MG=mM∞−E0/π.(12) \Delta_c=\min_{\partial\mathcal P_c}\mathcal E_G-E_0>0,\qquad M_G=mM_\infty-E_0/\pi. \qquad\text{(12)} The boundary is relative to the original affine charge rows. Because the original polytope has an interior point and every removed vertex has an incidence, every feasible face in Proposition 1 is on that boundary. Equations (6) and (12) give the uniform bound (m−rC)M∞−eG′,c′(s′)π≤MG−Δc/π<MG.(13) \boxed{\quad (m-r_C)M_\infty-\frac{e_{G',c'}(s')}{\pi} \leq M_G-\Delta_c/\pi<M_G. \quad} \qquad\text{(13)} The same bound holds for any retained assignment in that fiber, since its energy is at least its fiber minimum. This is a definite positive gap depending only on the supplied original charged problem. It requires no condition |nv|≥2|n_v|\geq2. In place of that condition it requires the exact compatibility (5).

If a retained assignment is a matched positive inverse-period point, its expression in (13) is its calibrated real charged action. Otherwise (13) is only its angle-energy value. Deleting the saturated vertices from a matched boundary point P=u−πicP=u-\pi i c, as in Section 3 below, does not in general preserve the real periods: at a surviving vertex it removes the real returns kw,ek_{w,e}. If the retained edges remain positive, the new period is Pw′=(uw−∑e=wvv∈Ckw,e)−πicw.(14) P'_w=\left(u_w-\sum_{\substack{e=wv\\v\in C}}k_{w,e}\right) -\pi i c_w. \qquad\text{(14)} It need not have real part zero. For degenerate retained assignments no positive metric or stationary interpretation is assigned by (13).

3. Sharp finite-period saturation

Now assume SS is proper in the connected original tree, and take a bounded real-period sequence P(t(j))=u(j)−πicP(t^{(j)})=u^{(j)}-\pi i c of positive points tending to a finite image boundary. Pass to the joint finite limits supplied by the compactness theorem. Let C={v∈S:|sv|=π},sv=εvπ,(15) C=\{v\in S:|s_v|=\pi\},\qquad s_v=\varepsilon_v\pi, \qquad\text{(15)} and suppose C≠⌀C\ne\varnothing. This CC includes all saturated vertices. Write d0(v)d_0(v) for its number of edges from vv to vertices outside CC, and din(v)d_{\mathrm{ in}}(v) for its number of neighbors in CC.

Proposition 2 (matched corner signs). Every cut neighbor w∉Cw\notin C of CC has sw=0s_w=0. At a cut edge e=vwe=vw, gw,e=0,gv,e=τvπ/2,sgn⁡kv,e=εv,τv=sgn⁡hv∈{−1,1}.(16) g_{w,e}=0,\qquad g_{v,e}=\tau_v\pi/2,\qquad \operatorname{sgn}k_{v,e}=\varepsilon_v,\qquad \tau_v=\operatorname{sgn}h_v\in\{-1,1\}. \qquad\text{(16)} Both incidences on internal CC-edges are zero. Consequently εvnv−2cv=τvd0(v).(17) \boxed{\quad \varepsilon_v n_v-2c_v=\tau_v d_0(v).\quad} \qquad\text{(17)} In particular the capacity inequality (5) is saturated.

Proof. The opposite incidence is zero by (2), so both AA-coordinates in (8) equal εvπ/4\varepsilon_v\pi/4. There are no open-side squares at a finite-period limit. Thus both yy-coordinates are corners. Solving their two possible signs gives exactly (sw,gv,e)∈{(0,π/2),(0,−π/2),(π,0),(−π,0)}.(18) (s_w,g_{v,e})\in \{(0,\pi/2),(0,-\pi/2),(\pi,0),(-\pi,0)\}. \qquad\text{(18)} The last two alternatives put ww in CC.

At a sharp square corner the finite covectors satisfy sgn⁡x=sgn⁡A,sgn⁡B=sgn⁡y,sinh⁡(2|x|)sinh⁡(2|B|)=1.(19) \operatorname{sgn}x=\operatorname{sgn}A,\qquad \operatorname{sgn}B=\operatorname{sgn}y,\qquad \sinh(2|x|)\sinh(2|B|)=1. \qquad\text{(19)} For a cut edge yP=yQ=gv,e/2y_P=y_Q=g_{v,e}/2. The original matching identities kv,e=xP+xQ,hv=2(BP+BQ)(20) k_{v,e}=x_P+x_Q,\qquad h_v=2(B_P+B_Q) \qquad\text{(20)} therefore give both signs in (16), strictly. The proper-selection observation bound also excludes hv=0h_v=0 at every saturated vertex, including one with no cut edge: otherwise tv=±πt_v=\pm\pi would make cos⁡(tv/2)=0\cos(t_v/2)=0. Thus τv\tau_v is defined throughout CC. All cut incidences at a given vertex have the same sign. Summing the unchanged primitive row gives (17). ▫\square

At a cut neighbor sw=0s_w=0 means tw=ihwt_w=i h_w, not necessarily tw=0t_w=0. Such a surviving selected vertex must not be relabeled as a complete outside vertex. If it becomes isolated after deletion, its unchanged row (4) and sw=0s_w=0 force cw=0c_w=0. Hence this cannot occur under the positive charges of the present theorem. For a separate zero-charge sector it is an actual remaining row obligation, not a license to discard the vertex or its finite trace.

The limiting assignment minimizes its fixed-ss fiber: it minimizes ℰ−u*⋅s\mathcal E-u_*\cdot s, whose second term is constant on that fiber. Proposition 1 therefore identifies its exact reduced energy with the deleted reduced energy. If (12) is available, (13) applies to this boundary value with the same charge lifts and gap.

4. The component sign and an arithmetic resonance restriction

Retain the original edge root qeq_e, rather than resetting its sign. The finite observation identities are av=tan⁡(tv/2),qe2=1+av2+aw2,cosh⁡rv,e=cos⁡(tw/2)qe.(21) a_v=\tan(t_v/2),\quad q_e^2=1+a_v^2+a_w^2,\qquad \cosh r_{v,e}=\cos(t_w/2)q_e. \qquad\text{(21)} At a cut edge, write av=icoth⁡(hv/2)a_v=i\coth(h_v/2) and aw=itanh⁡(hw/2)a_w=i\tanh(h_w/2). Thus qe=iQeq_e=iQ_e, Qe≠0Q_e\ne0. Equations (16), (21), and cos⁡(tw/2)=cosh⁡(hw/2)>0\cos(t_w/2)=\cosh(h_w/2)>0 give sgn⁡Qe=εvτv.(22) \operatorname{sgn}Q_e=\varepsilon_v\tau_v. \qquad\text{(22)} At an internal CC-edge both tangent imaginary moduli exceed one, so |Qe|>1|Q_e|>1. Since gv,e=gw,e=0g_{v,e}=g_{w,e}=0, its two instances of (21), with cos⁡(tv/2)=−iεvsinh⁡(hv/2)\cos(t_v/2)=-i\varepsilon_v\sinh(h_v/2), give sgn⁡Qe=εvτv=εwτw.(23) \operatorname{sgn}Q_e =\varepsilon_v\tau_v=\varepsilon_w\tau_w. \qquad\text{(23)} Hence σC:=εvτv\sigma_C:=\varepsilon_v\tau_v is constant on each connected component of CC. Internal edges never have qe=±iq_e=\pm i.

Here is a consequence usable by a separate exact deck calculation. Fix v∈Cv\in C and a nonempty set JvJ_v of incident edges with qe=σeiq_e=\sigma_e i, σe=±1\sigma_e=\pm1; put rv=|Jv|r_v=|J_v|. They are cut edges, rv≤d0(v)r_v\leq d_0(v), and every σe=εvτv\sigma_e=\varepsilon_v\tau_v. Therefore nv+∑e∈Jvσe=εv{2cv+τv(d0(v)+rv)}.(24) n_v+\sum_{e\in J_v}\sigma_e =\varepsilon_v\{2c_v+\tau_v(d_0(v)+r_v)\}. \qquad\text{(24)} If a source calculation requires the left side to vanish, positivity of cvc_v forces precisely the necessary arithmetic conditions τv=−1,2cv=d0(v)+rv,nv=εvrv.(25) \tau_v=-1,\qquad 2c_v=d_0(v)+r_v,\qquad n_v=\varepsilon_v r_v. \qquad\text{(25)} Moreover original strict feasibility implies 2πcv≤|nv||svint|+2∑e∋v|gv,eint|<π(|nv|+dv)(26) 2\pi c_v \leq |n_v||s_v^{\mathrm{ int}}|+2\sum_{e\ni v}|g_{v,e}^{\mathrm{ int}}| <\pi(|n_v|+d_v) \qquad\text{(26)} at any supplied strictly feasible assignment. Substituting (25) and dv=d0(v)+din(v)d_v=d_0(v)+d_{\mathrm{ in}}(v) gives din(v)>0.(27) d_{\mathrm{ in}}(v)>0. \qquad\text{(27)} Thus an isolated saturated vertex cannot satisfy this resonance, even when only some of its incident wires have q=±iq=\pm i. In particular all incidences cannot be escaping wires. At unit charge a remaining candidate has d0(v)=rv=1d_0(v)=r_v=1, nv=εvn_v=\varepsilon_v, and at least one saturated neighbor. These are necessary conditions; no realization or global exclusion of the remaining candidates is claimed. Equation (24) is an arithmetic consequence of the original period rows, not by itself a finite-source deck identity.

5. What still requires the actual source

The constant in (11), the exact reduced factorization (6), and the gap (13) are classical statements about the original angle problem. The archived one-step Ward current has its own exact physical-node support and retained coupons. A general angular side at tv=±π+ihvt_v=\pm\pi+i h_v, a wire-end residue, or a deck defect is not identified with that current by a shared limiting real angle. Their full Hermite normals, angular aliases, wire seams, and exterior contractions must still be retained.

In particular, an energy comparison on a compatible deleted fiber does not bound the entire deleted quantum source or show that a given side current lands in that fiber. An unconstrained deleted forest may have other assignments failing (5). Nor does the sign restriction (25) imply that a nonresonant bulk term has a smaller whole-current rate. The result supplies an exact boundary rate and matching constraints for such a future reduction, without claiming an arbitrary-short-filling asymptotic or a nonzero cycle coefficient.
