Relative homology and compact periods in a positive gluing chart

Research proof, 23 November 2026.

A supplied positive critical point determines the entire ordinary relative homology of its positive logarithmic chart above a sufficiently high sublevel. There is one compact Legendre disk, and its coefficient in any finite relative cycle is an explicit angle-projection degree. For a holomorphic growing period this gives its full leading term, including the retained density and this integer coefficient.

This is a statement about finite chains in one fixed marked chart. It does not provide such a representative for an original quantum source, or identify its cut, lip or infinite boundary currents.

In the gluing application [G] supplies exactly this potential after positive quad selection. No existence assertion is implicit in this hypothesis.

2. A cutoff retracts the whole pair

Define the fixed-radius height f(θ)=h(ρ*,θ)f(\theta)=h(\rho_*,\theta). It is strictly concave on all of Θ\Theta, stationary at θ*\theta_*, and extends continuously to [0,π]t[0,\pi]^t. Here the dilogarithms have their upper-side boundary values; their values remain continuous even when a boundary shape equals 11. Every other point of this closed cube has strictly smaller height. To see the boundary assertion, restrict to a segment from θ*\theta_*: its derivative starts at zero and is strictly decreasing at every interior point. Its endpoint limit is therefore strictly smaller than its value at the center.

Choose levels c0<c<h*,{θ∈Θ:f(θ)≥c0}⋐𝒫.(8) c_0<c<h_*,\qquad \{\theta\in\Theta:f(\theta)\geq c_0\} \Subset\mathcal P. \qquad\text{(8)} Such levels exist by compactness of the closed cube and θ*∈𝒫\theta_*\in\mathcal P. Choose a smooth cutoff 0≤χ≤10\leq\chi\leq1 depending only on ff, equal to zero for f≤c0f\leq c_0 and to one for f≥cf\geq c. Set ρ0(θ)=ρ*+χ(θ)(ρL(θ)−ρ*),Ac={x∈X:h(x)≤c},Rs(ρ,θ)=((1−s)ρ+sρ0(θ),θ).(9) \rho_0(\theta)=\rho_*+\chi(\theta)(\rho_L(\theta)-\rho_*),\quad A_c=\{x\in X:h(x)\leq c\},\quad R_s(\rho,\theta)=((1-s)\rho+s\rho_0(\theta),\theta). \qquad\text{(9)} Outside 𝒫\mathcal P define ρ0=ρ*\rho_0=\rho_*. This is a smooth global definition because the support of χ\chi is compact in 𝒫\mathcal P. The maps RsR_s strongly retract XX onto its graph; we now verify that they preserve AcA_c.

If f(θ)≥cf(\theta)\geq c, the target ρ0=ρL\rho_0=\rho_L is the global radial minimum. If f(θ)<cf(\theta)<c, radial convexity and H(θ)≤f(θ)H(\theta)\leq f(\theta) show h(ρ0,θ)≤f(θ)<ch(\rho_0,\theta)\leq f(\theta)<c; outside 𝒫\mathcal P this is the definition of ρ0\rho_0. Thus a starting point of AcA_c always has its target in AcA_c. Radial convexity proves the same for the whole joining segment. This proves a strong deformation retraction of pairs, not just of two reference chains.

Moreover, h(ρ0(θ),θ)>c⇔θ∈Ωc:={θ∈𝒫:H(θ)>c}.(10) h(\rho_0(\theta),\theta)>c \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta\in\Omega_c:=\{\theta\in\mathcal P:H(\theta)>c\}. \qquad\text{(10)} Indeed a high graph point must have f≥cf\geq c, hence lies on the Legendre graph; conversely H>cH>c implies f>cf>c and χ=1\chi=1. The graph pair is therefore (Θ,Θ\Ωc)(\Theta,\Theta\setminus\Omega_c). Notice also that (9) preserves every sublevel h≤dh\leq d for d≥cd\geq c. It need not decrease height monotonically below cc.

Strict concavity makes Ωc\Omega_c convex and nonempty. Since H≤fH\leq f, its closure lies compactly inside 𝒫\mathcal P. It is exactly Kc={H≥c}K_c=\{H\geq c\}: every level point can be approached along its segment to θ*\theta_* from higher values. Clamp each ray from θ*\theta_* to its intersection with ∂Kc\partial K_c, leaving points of KcK_c fixed. Linear interpolation of the radial distance gives a strong deformation of (Θ,Θ\Ωc)(\Theta,\Theta\setminus\Omega_c) onto (Kc,∂Kc)(K_c,\partial K_c). It stays in Θ\Theta by convexity; a point outside Ωc\Omega_c remains outside until it reaches ∂Kc\partial K_c. The radial intersection depends continuously and smoothly on direction, since KcK_c is smooth and contains θ*\theta_* in its interior.

Combining this clamp with (9) proves (X,Ac)≃(Kc,∂Kc),Hj(X,Ac;ℤ)={ℤ,j=t,0,j≠t.(11) (X,A_c)\simeq (K_c,\partial K_c),\qquad H_j(X,A_c;\mathbb Z)= \begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&j=t,\\0,&j\ne t.\end{cases} \qquad\text{(11)} The compact disk Γc=xL(Kc)\Gamma_c=x_L(K_c), with its standard θ\theta-orientation, is a generator, and its boundary has height exactly cc. All homology here uses ordinary finite singular chains. In particular no noncompact end is filled by this calculation.

3. Its coefficient is an angle-projection degree

For every ρ\rho, radial minimality at θ*\theta_* gives h(ρ,θ*)≥h*>ch(\rho,\theta_*)\geq h_*>c. Hence angle projection is a map of pairs π:(X,Ac)→(Θ,Θ\{θ*}),π(ρ+iθ)=θ.(12) \pi:(X,A_c)\longrightarrow (\Theta,\Theta\setminus\{\theta_*\}),\qquad \pi(\rho+i\theta)=\theta. \qquad\text{(12)} It sends [Γc][\Gamma_c] to the positive local fundamental class at θ*\theta_*. Both groups in degree tt are ℤ\mathbb Z, so π*\pi_* is an isomorphism. Consequently every finite relative cycle Γ\Gamma has the exact coefficient formula [Γ]=k[Γc],k=deg⁡θ*(πΓ)∈ℤ.(13) [\Gamma]=k[\Gamma_c],\qquad k=\deg_{\theta_*}(\pi\Gamma)\in\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(13)} This degree means the induced local relative homology class. For smooth transverse chains it is the signed count of intersections with {θ=θ*}\{\theta=\theta_*\}, counting the sign of the angle projection on each oriented chain. This convention fixes the intersection orientation without an additional ambient sign.

Since XX is contractible and t≥1t\geq1, every compact closed tt-cycle in XX has zero class in (11). A nonzero generator therefore needs a relative boundary in AcA_c, or separately controlled ends followed by a justified truncation. An entire compact closed quantum cycle cannot simply be placed in this unpunctured chart and assigned degree one.

4. The resulting compact-period formula

Let Γ\Gamma be a finite piecewise-smooth relative tt-cycle in (X,Ac)(X,A_c). The retractions above and finite-chain homology in the ball give compact piecewise-smooth chains B,RB,R with Γ−kΓc=∂B+R,supp⁡R⊂Ac.(14) \Gamma-k\Gamma_c=\partial B+R,\qquad \operatorname{supp}R\subset A_c. \qquad\text{(14)} One can triangulate the compact homotopies and the ball filling to obtain these chains. The clamp is smooth on either side of its boundary, so this needs only piecewise-smooth chains. Equivalently use smooth approximation relative to the regular level h=ch=c.

Suppose, on a neighborhood of these compact chains, the actual forms are holomorphic top forms ωN=NμeNS(x)/(2πi)aN(x)dx1⋯dxt.(15) \omega_N=N^\mu e^{NS(x)/(2\pi i)}a_N(x)\,dx_1\cdots dx_t. \qquad\text{(15)} Assume aNa_N is polynomially bounded on that neighborhood, and aN=a0+O(N−1)a_N=a_0+O(N^{-1}) uniformly holomorphically near x*x_*, where a0(x*)≠0a_0(x_*)\ne0. Holomorphic top forms are closed. Stokes and (14) give the exact identity and the bound ∫ΓωN=k∫ΓcωN+∫RωN,|∫RωN|≤NCeNc/(2π).(16) \int_\Gamma\omega_N =k\int_{\Gamma_c}\omega_N+\int_R\omega_N, \qquad \left|\int_R\omega_N\right| \leq N^C e^{Nc/(2\pi)}. \qquad\text{(16)} The constant includes the finite volume of RR. No independent bound on a noncompact homotopy or on its ends is used.

Write φ(θ)=S(xL(θ))/(2πi)\varphi(\theta)=S(x_L(\theta))/(2\pi i). At the center φ′=0\varphi'=0 and ℋ=−φ″(θ*),ℜℋ=−H″(θ*)/(2π)>0,C*=(2π)t/2a0(x*)det⁡JL(θ*)det⁡ℋ≠0.(17) \mathcal H=-\varphi''(\theta_*),\qquad \Re\mathcal H=-H''(\theta_*)/(2\pi)>0,\qquad C_*=(2\pi)^{t/2} \frac{a_0(x_*)\det J_L(\theta_*)}{\sqrt{\det\mathcal H}}\ne0. \qquad\text{(17)} The square root is the Gaussian branch on matrices with positive real part; the orientation is the stated θ\theta-orientation. Strict concavity gives a fixed height gap outside every small neighborhood of θ*\theta_* in KcK_c. Taylor expansion there, Gaussian rescaling and the positive real Hessian in (17) give ∫ΓωN=kC*Nμ−t/2eNS(x*)/(2πi)(1+O(N−1))+O(NCeNc/(2π)).(18) \int_\Gamma\omega_N =k\,C_* N^{\mu-t/2}e^{NS(x_*)/(2\pi i)}(1+O(N^{-1})) +O(N^C e^{Nc/(2\pi)}). \qquad\text{(18)} The local estimate uses the uniform holomorphic expansion of the amplitude; its derivative bounds follow on a smaller neighborhood by Cauchy estimates. Odd Gaussian terms integrate to zero, and the remaining Taylor error is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). The complement is exponentially smaller by the fixed height gap. Thus if k≠0k\ne0 the period has its retained nonzero leading constant. If k=0k=0, (16) instead gives the lower sublevel bound directly.

5. What this does and does not assemble

[L] constructs the Legendre chain and controlled homotopies of specified reference chains. Here the cutoff controls the whole positive-chart pair and computes the coefficient of every finite relative chain by (13); (18) then assembles its compact period. The argument works for the tilted height in (6), without assuming purely imaginary peripheral targets or using a descent flow.

It does not show that an original Kashaev cycle, with its actual amplitude and closing row, has a representative satisfying these hypotheses. Quantum divisors, cut banks, finite flat faces and noncompact ends may supply additional chains outside this pair. Their actual marks and boundary currents must be retained. Different logarithmic windings give different potentials, not additional classes silently identified by (11). No source degree, scalar lower bound for a knot, or finite-exception theorem follows without the corresponding original-source construction.
