A Fourier Hilbert representation of the local entropy defect

31 October 2026. Workspace research lemma; root derivation and a separate agent’s full analytical review PASS. This is internal review, not external peer review; the note is unpublished. This is an exact identity for the entropy function, not a claim that the original quantum series has a Plancherel bound. The original shared face constraints still need to be imposed separately.

Put ℓ=1/2\ell=1/2 and

f(t)=Λ(πt)+Λ(π/2−πt)π,0≤t≤ℓ. f(t)=\frac{\Lambda(\pi t)+\Lambda(\pi/2-\pi t)}{\pi}, \qquad 0\le t\le\ell.

Here Λ(a)=−∫0alog⁡|2sin⁡u|du\Lambda(a)=-\int_0^a\log|2\sin u|\,du. We have f(0)=f(ℓ)=0f(0)=f(\ell)=0, f(ℓ−t)=f(t)f(\ell-t)=f(t), f′(t)=log⁡cot⁡(πt)f'(t)=\log\cot(\pi t), and f″(t)=−2π/sin⁡(2πt)<0f''(t)=-2\pi/\sin(2\pi t)<0 in the interior. Extend ff periodically to ℝ/ℓℤ\mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z.

1. Exact positive Fourier coefficients

For n≥1n\ge1 write Hnodd=∑j=1n(2j−1)−1H_n^{\mathrm{odd}}=\sum_{j=1}^n(2j-1)^{-1}. Then the uniformly convergent identity is

f(t)=∑n=1∞Hnoddπ2n2(1−cos⁡(4πnt)). \boxed{\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{H_n^{\mathrm{odd}}}{\pi^2n^2} \bigl(1-\cos(4\pi nt)\bigr).} \tag{1}

To check all constants, the cosine coefficient for period 1/21/2 is an=4∫01/2f(t)cos⁡(4πnt)dt=−1π2n∫0π/2log⁡cot⁡xsin⁡(4nx)dx. a_n=4\int_0^{1/2}f(t)\cos(4\pi nt)\,dt =-\frac1{\pi^2n}\int_0^{\pi/2} \log\cot x\,\sin(4nx)\,dx. Use the primitive (1−cos⁡(4nx))/(4n)(1-\cos(4nx))/(4n) of sin⁡(4nx)\sin(4nx). The logarithmic endpoint singularities have zero boundary product, so In:=∫0π/2log⁡cot⁡xsin⁡(4nx)dx=14n∫0π1−cos⁡(2ny)sin⁡ydy. I_n:=\int_0^{\pi/2}\log\cot x\,\sin(4nx)\,dx =\frac1{4n}\int_0^\pi\frac{1-\cos(2ny)}{\sin y}\,dy. If the last integral is JnJ_n, then J0=0J_0=0 and Jn−Jn−1=2∫0πsin⁡((2n−1)y)dy=42n−1. J_n-J_{n-1}=2\int_0^\pi\sin((2n-1)y)\,dy =\frac4{2n-1}. Thus In=Hnodd/nI_n=H_n^{\mathrm{odd}}/n and an=−Hnodd/(π2n2)a_n=-H_n^{\mathrm{odd}}/(\pi^2n^2). Reflection makes the sine coefficients zero. These coefficients are absolutely summable; Fourier uniqueness and the value at zero fix the constant term and prove (1). No asymptotic of a quantum coefficient is used here.

Let cn=Hnodd/(π2n2)>0c_n=H_n^{\mathrm{odd}}/(\pi^2n^2)>0. In the real Hilbert space ℓ2(ℕ;ℝ2)\ell^2(\mathbb N;\mathbb R^2), define u(t)=(cn/2[cos(4πnt)−1],cn/2sin(4πnt))n≥1. u(t)=\left( \sqrt{c_n/2}\,[\cos(4\pi nt)-1],\quad \sqrt{c_n/2}\,\sin(4\pi nt) \right)_{n\ge1}. Absolute summability proves that this is well defined, and directly ∥u(t)∥2=f(t),∥u(t)−u(s)∥2=f(t−s). \|u(t)\|^2=f(t),\qquad \|u(t)-u(s)\|^2=f(t-s). \tag{2} In particular the periodic entropy kernel is conditionally negative definite: for finitely many real rir_i with ∑iri=0\sum_i r_i=0, ∑i,jrirjf(ti−tj)=−2∥∑iriu(ti)∥2≤0. \sum_{i,j}r_ir_jf(t_i-t_j) =-2\left\|\sum_i r_i u(t_i)\right\|^2\le0.

2. The actual one-sided score and the completion slack

Use the original half-block triangle allocations from the joint entropy identity: α=(a,μ,ℓ−μ−a),β=(b,ν,ℓ−ν−b),ξ=a−b,w=ν−μ,γ=(ξ,ℓ−w,w−ξ). \alpha=(a,\mu,\ell-\mu-a),\quad \beta=(b,\nu,\ell-\nu-b),\quad \xi=a-b,\quad w=\nu-\mu, \quad\gamma=(\xi,\ell-w,w-\xi). All entries are nonnegative; 0≤ξ≤w0\le\xi\le w and every triangle has total ℓ\ell. Set U(α)=⨁j=13u(αj)U(\alpha)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^3u(\alpha_j) and C(α)=∑jf(αj)C(\alpha)=\sum_jf(\alpha_j). Equations (1)–(2), together with reflection of ff, give exactly C(α)=∥U(α)∥2,C(β)=∥U(β)∥2,C(γ)=∥U(α)−U(β)∥2. C(\alpha)=\|U(\alpha)\|^2,\quad C(\beta)=\|U(\beta)\|^2,\quad C(\gamma)=\|U(\alpha)-U(\beta)\|^2. \tag{3} The pure triangle vertices all map to zero; this is not an injective parameterization of the closed triangle simplex.

For the reference entropy h=C(α)−C(β)h=C(\alpha)-C(\beta), local energy bound g=C(γ)g=C(\gamma), and joint upper score h̃=(h+g)/2\widetilde h=(h+g)/2, we obtain h̃=⟨U(α),U(α)−U(β)⟩,τ:=C(α)−h̃=⟨U(α),U(β)⟩. \boxed{\displaystyle \widetilde h =\langle U(\alpha),U(\alpha)-U(\beta)\rangle, \qquad \tau:=C(\alpha)-\widetilde h =\langle U(\alpha),U(\beta)\rangle.} \tag{4} This identifies the local completion slack as an exact inner product, not a guessed norm of a quantum state.

For completeness the nonnegative sign follows independently from the elementary circular triangle inequality. Concavity and f(0)=0f(0)=0 give f(r+s)≤f(r)+f(s)f(r+s)\le f(r)+f(s) for r,s≥0r,s\ge0, r+s≤ℓr+s\le\ell. For 0≤b≤a≤ℓ0\le b\le a\le\ell, reflection then gives f(a−b)=f(ℓ−a+b)≤f(ℓ−a)+f(b)=f(a)+f(b). f(a-b)=f(\ell-a+b)\le f(\ell-a)+f(b)=f(a)+f(b). This proves the triangle inequality for the periodic kernel by choosing representatives in [0,ℓ][0,\ell]. Sum it over the three coordinates in (3) to obtain τ≥0\tau\ge0. Its other orientation gives h̃≥0\widetilde h\ge0. Thus 0≤h̃≤C(α). 0\le\widetilde h\le C(\alpha).

3. An exact transported closure for the angle constraints

Let RsR_s rotate the nn-th real two-plane by 4πns4\pi ns. These operators form an orthogonal representation of ℝ/ℓℤ\mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z. Directly from the definition, u(s+t)=u(s)+Rsu(t). u(s+t)=u(s)+R_su(t). \tag{5} Consequently for any ordered list of real loads t1,…,tmt_1,\ldots,t_m, u(∑j=1mtj)=∑j=1mRt1+⋯+tj−1u(tj). u\left(\sum_{j=1}^m t_j\right) =\sum_{j=1}^m R_{t_1+\cdots+t_{j-1}}u(t_j). \tag{6} An original face target one, or a triangle target 1/21/2, therefore gives an exact zero on the right. The order may be the actual cyclic incidence order. This is a transported linear closure, with the rotations determined by the original partial angle sums. It is stronger information than an untransported sum of vectors, which need not vanish.

This cocycle is explicitly a coboundary: with v=(cn/2,0)n≥1v=(\sqrt{c_n/2},0)_{n\ge1}, we have u(t)=Rtv−vu(t)=R_tv-v. The vector vv belongs to the Hilbert space. Thus the affine action z↦Rtz+u(t)z\mapsto R_tz+u(t) has the fixed point −v-v. It would be incorrect to call this a nontrivial algebraic A-torsor or to infer a quantum period from it. Its precise use is to encode the original additive angle constraints by an explicit unitary transport, frequency by frequency.

4. Scope of the Fourier reformulation

Equation (4) may organize the outer-transfer compensation in a Hilbert space. It does not make the original nonlinear gluing constraints linear. In particular, a face sum of angles does not make the untransported sum of vectors u(t)u(t) vanish. It controls a product of phases and the rotated sum in (6). Comparing those frames at different ports is an additional problem. No cross-port orthogonality or Plancherel contraction follows just from (4)–(6). The inequality comparing the global shared score with the complete hyperbolic volume is still open. This note records an exact representation of its local defect, with no novelty claim about the general theory of conditionally negative definite kernels.

5. A positive interval-cut realization of the same defect

This additional section also passed a separate full analytical review, including the interval measure, integrability, and transported closure.

The kernel also has a realization in which the local slack is a positive intersection measure. Keep the circle length ℓ=1/2\ell=1/2, and put d(t)=min⁡(t,ℓ−t)d(t)=\min(t,\ell-t) for 0≤t≤ℓ0\le t\le\ell. On 0<a<ℓ/20<a<\ell/2, define w(a)=−f″(a)=2πsin⁡(2πa)>0. w(a)=-f''(a)=\frac{2\pi}{\sin(2\pi a)}>0. Since f′(ℓ/2)=0f'(\ell/2)=0, integration of the derivative gives f(t)=f(d(t))=∫0ℓ/2min⁡(d(t),a)w(a)da. f(t)=f(d(t))=\int_0^{\ell/2}\min(d(t),a)w(a)\,da. \tag{7} The integral converges because aw(a)a w(a) is bounded near zero. For each aa, let Ata=t+[0,a)A_t^a=t+[0,a) in ℝ/ℓℤ\mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z. Normalized angular units are not used: circle measure is the ordinary length dsds, with total ℓ\ell. Direct interval geometry gives |Ata△Asa|=2min⁡(d(t−s),a). |A_t^a\mathbin\triangle A_s^a|=2\min(d(t-s),a). On (0,ℓ/2)×(ℝ/ℓℤ)(0,\ell/2)\times(\mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z), use the positive measure dν=12w(a)dadsd\nu=\tfrac12w(a)\,da\,ds, and set $$ v(a,s)=1_{A_0^a}(s),\qquad u_{\rm cut}(t)(a,s)=1_{A_t^a}(s)-1_{A_0^a}(s). $$ Both functions belong to L2(ν)L^2(\nu): ∥v∥2=12∫0ℓ/2aw(a)da<∞\|v\|^2=\tfrac12\int_0^{\ell/2}a w(a)\,da<\infty. Equations (7) and the interval identity show $$ \|u_{\rm cut}(t)\|^2=f(t),\qquad \|u_{\rm cut}(t)-u_{\rm cut}(s)\|^2=f(t-s). \tag{8} $$ Translation of the circle variable gives the same unitary cocycle and transported closure (5)–(6).

There is a useful pointwise feature beyond a generic Hilbert embedding. For fixed (a,s)(a,s), all nonzero $u_{\rm cut}(t)(a,s)$ have the SAME sign: they are −1-1 inside A0aA_0^a, and +1+1 outside it. Write Et={(a,s):s∈Ata△A0a}E_t=\{(a,s):s\in A_t^a\mathbin\triangle A_0^a\}. Then $$ u_{\rm cut}(t)u_{\rm cut}(s)=1_{E_t\cap E_s},\qquad u_{\rm cut}(t)\bigl(u_{\rm cut}(t)-u_{\rm cut}(s)\bigr) =1_{E_t\setminus E_s}. $$ For the original endpoint triples α,β\alpha,\beta, equations (3)–(4) therefore have the exact positive forms τ=∑j=13ν(Eαj∩Eβj),h̃=∑j=13ν(Eαj\Eβj). \boxed{\displaystyle \tau=\sum_{j=1}^3\nu(E_{\alpha_j}\cap E_{\beta_j}), \qquad \widetilde h=\sum_{j=1}^3\nu(E_{\alpha_j}\setminus E_{\beta_j}).} \tag{9} These are finite measures even though the ambient measure ν\nu is infinite. No hyperbolic solid or manifold volume is being assigned to the sets EtE_t. Equation (9) makes the local entropy defect a positive cut quantity. A global comparison must still respect the original face loads and the incidence-dependent transport frames; arbitrary independent cut choices would enlarge the source and are not justified.
