An exact positive moment form for the original flat-cap coefficients

31 October 2026. analytical research lemma. Root derived the proof; two independent readers checked it in full. This constructs a positive inner product from the original cap weights. It does not prove that the adjacent CROSS polynomial is a contraction for that form, or establish the open original-demand convolution bound.

1. The actual finite weights

Fix an integer N>=3, d=N-1 and theta=pi/N. The raw protected cap coefficient is (-1)^j U_j, where

U_j=csc^2(theta/2) product_(r=1)^j
                   sin^2(r theta)/sin^2((r+1/2)theta),
0<=j<=d.

Write m_j=U_j/U_0. The statement is that there is a positive random variable X_N such that

E X_N^j=m_j, 0<=j<=d.                         (1)

Its support is not confined to [0,1]. In particular no Hausdorff-moment or pointwise contraction conclusion follows from (1).

2. Exact Euler product and beta factors

Euler’s sine product gives, with positive factors for this whole range,

m_j=[Gamma(j+1)Gamma(3/2)/Gamma(j+3/2)]^2
      product_(k>=1) B_(kN)(j)^2,             (2)

where

B_a(s)= Gamma(a)Gamma(a-s-1/2)
         /[Gamma(a-1/2)Gamma(a-s)]
      * Gamma(a+s+1)Gamma(a+3/2)
         /[Gamma(a+1)Gamma(a+s+3/2)].         (3)

Indeed each k contributes, before squaring,

product_(r=1)^j
   [(kN-r)/(kN-r-1/2)] [(kN+r)/(kN+r+1/2)],

which is exactly (3). The remaining product r/(r+1/2) is the prefactor in (2). This derives the shifts and constants from the literal cap, not from a free choice of moment functional.

Let T_1,T_2 be independent Beta(1,1/2) variables. For each k>=1 and i=1,2 let independently

A_(k,i) ~ Beta(kN-1/2,1/2),
B_(k,i) ~ Beta(kN+1,1/2),
Z_(k,i)=B_(k,i)/A_(k,i).

All variables mentioned are mutually independent. The elementary beta integral gives

E T_i^s=Gamma(s+1)Gamma(3/2)/Gamma(s+3/2),
E Z_(k,i)^s=B_(kN)(s),                         (4)

for -1<s<N-1/2; these restrictions are sufficient simultaneously for the factors here. In particular all moments in (1) are finite.

Define finite positive products

X_(N,K)=T_1 T_2 product_(k=1)^K Z_(k,1)Z_(k,2).

Equations (2)–(4) give the desired moments in the limit, once convergence and uniform integrability are checked.

3. Convergence with no formal infinite-product inference

For fixed s in a compact subinterval of (-1,N-1/2), the gamma ratio expansion in (3) gives

log B_a(s)=O_s(a^-2), a->infinity.             (5)

The a^-1 terms of the inverse-beta and beta factors are respectively s/(2a) and -s/(2a), and cancel. The expansion and its first two s derivatives are uniform on a small neighborhood of zero. Consequently

E log Z_(k,i)=O((kN)^-2),
Var(log Z_(k,i))=O((kN)^-2).                  (6)

Also log T_i belongs to L2 by its beta density at zero and one. The means are absolutely summable. Independence makes the centered partial sums Cauchy in L2, since their tail variance is the sum of the tail variances. Thus log X_(N,K) converges in L2 to a finite real random variable Y_N. Set X_N=exp(Y_N)>0. The products converge in probability.

Fix an integer 1<=j<=d. Choose epsilon>0 with j+epsilon<N-1/2. The beta moments at that exponent exist; (5) shows

sup_K E X_(N,K)^(j+epsilon)<infinity.         (7)

Hence X_(N,K)^j are uniformly integrable. Convergence in probability and uniform integrability give convergence of their expectations to E X_N^j. Equations (2)–(4) identify the limit as m_j. The zero moment is one. This proves (1) with every original weight retained.

Removing any one factor leaves the same convergent log series, measurable in the other independent variables. Thus the separated factor remains independent of the positive limiting remainder. The measure is non-atomic and has unbounded support. For non-atomicity, separate the independent T_1 factor, which has a continuous positive density on (0,1). For unbounded support, separate the independent 1/A_(1,1) factor, which is unbounded, from the remaining positive random product. The remainder is bounded below by some positive number on an event of positive probability. Independence then gives arbitrarily large X_N with positive probability.

4. Exact finite-dimensional Cauchy inequality

For m with 2m<=d, put on polynomials of degree at most m

<p,q>_N=U_0 E[p(X_N) conjugate(q(X_N))].

Its matrix in the monomial basis is EXACTLY (U_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<=m). It is positive definite: a nonzero polynomial has finitely many roots, while X_N is non-atomic. Thus

|<p,q>_N|^2 <= <p,p>_N <q,q>_N.               (8)

At the even levels N=2m0 of the original cap/CROSS problem, its cutoff M=m0-1 satisfies 2M=d-1. The entire original cutoff polynomial space therefore carries this positive Gram form. The alternating cap character is retained by evaluating p at -X_N, rather than deleting (-1)^j.

To express the precise missing step, use the coefficients C_(j,h) and range J_n of the original-demand convolution. Define the literal projected polynomial

F_(N,n)(t)=sum_(j in J_n) C_(j,n-j) t^j.

Then its actual signed coefficient is exactly

(-1)^d U_0 E F_(N,n)(-X_N).                  (9)

For n>M the cutoff in J_n remains in F; (9) is not a full-sum replacement or an orthogonality claim. Formula (8) supplies a genuine positive inner product, but no bound here compares its quadratic norm of F_(N,n)(-t) with the endpoint convolution involving V_j. That comparison is the remaining issue. In particular unbounded support prevents replacing the expectation by a maximum on [0,1].

No numerical asymptotic test is needed for (1)–(9). They neither transport projected sectors through the full Casimir source nor prove the original invariant’s volume conjecture.

5. The moment threshold and the original even-level cutoff

The convergence argument also gives E X_N^s<infinity for EVERY real 0<=s<N-1/2: choose a slightly larger moment exponent still inside that interval and apply the same uniform-integrability argument. Conversely, separate the independent factor A_(1,1)^(-1) from the limiting product. The beta density gives

E A_(1,1)^(-s)=infinity whenever s>=N-1/2.

The remaining factor is strictly positive and independent. Tonelli’s theorem therefore gives the sharp statement

E X_N^s<infinity  iff  0<=s<N-1/2.             (10)

Here the equivalence is restricted to nonnegative s. No assertion about negative moments is needed. A nonzero polynomial p of degree r satisfies |p(t)|^2>=c t^(2r) for all sufficiently large positive t, while the lower degree terms bound its squared modulus above by C(1+t^(2r)). Consequently its squared norm is finite exactly when 2r<N-1/2.

At even N=2m0 this is r<=m0-1=M, precisely the original RIGHT cutoff. Thus that entire polynomial space has finite positive norm, while every polynomial of the next or a higher degree has infinite norm. At odd levels the maximal degree is floor((N-1)/2); its identification with an original one-sided sector is NOT asserted, since the odd central row belongs to CROSS.

There is also an exact finite functional relation. Define L_N(p)=U_0 E p(X_N), and let D=t partial_t act diagonally on monomials. Sines of D mean the corresponding finite diagonal operators. The literal ratio U_(j+1)/U_j gives

L_N( t sin^2(theta(D+3/2))p
              - sin^2(theta(D+1))p )=0,
deg p<=d-1.                                   (11)

Indeed (11) on t^j is exactly sin2((j+3/2)theta)U_(j+1)-sin2((j+1)theta)U_j=0. The degree restriction is essential. At j=d the formal numerator sin^2(N theta) vanishes, but the other term would require the divergent Nth moment. Equation (11) is not an identity on all degree-d polynomials and does not authorize deleting that terminal contribution. It supplies no self-adjointness or contraction claim for the adjacent continuant.

6. A finite algebraic evaluation at even levels

The beta product proves positivity, but the required finite functional also has an exact algebraic realization. Keep N=2m0 and M=m0-1, so d=2M+1. In this section L_N includes the original factor U_0. Gram–Schmidt on 1,t,…,t^M gives monic orthogonal polynomials p_r and positive squared norms h_r=L_N(p_r^2), 0<=r<=M. All these coefficients are real algebraic: the moments U_j are products of squared sines at rational multiples of pi, hence belong to a real cyclotomic field, and Gram–Schmidt uses only field operations.

Define the (M+1)-by-(M+1) symmetric tridiagonal matrix J_cap by

(J_cap)_(r,r)=L_N(t p_r^2)/h_r, 0<=r<=M,
(J_cap)_(r-1,r)=(J_cap)_(r,r-1)=sqrt(h_r/h_(r-1)), 1<=r<=M.

Every displayed moment is finite: the largest degree is 2M+1=d. For any vector v, let P_v=sum_r v_r p_r/sqrt(h_r). Orthogonality implies that multiplication by t has exactly the displayed entries, since t p_r has degree r+1. Thus

v^* J_cap v=L_N(t |P_v(t)|^2)>0 for v!=0.

The matrix is positive definite; its off-diagonal entries are positive. Its eigenvalues lambda_1,…,lambda_(M+1) are therefore positive and simple, and every eigenvector has nonzero first coordinate (use the tridiagonal recurrence starting at that coordinate). Define

w_a=U_0 |(unit eigenvector at lambda_a)_0|^2>0.

The characteristic polynomial p_(M+1) is still orthogonal to every polynomial of degree at most M. To check this without asserting its squared norm, define it by the last tridiagonal recurrence: the t p_M term pairs to zero against p_s for s<=M-2 by symmetry, and the chosen last two recurrence coefficients cancel its pairings with p_(M-1) and p_M. Only moments through 2M+1 occur. Its square need not be integrable, and in fact is not.

For any polynomial f of degree at most 2M+1, divide it by p_(M+1):

f=p_(M+1) q+r, deg q<=M, deg r<=M.

The first term has zero L_N value and vanishes at every eigenvalue. The spectral measure above reproduces L_N on degree<=M: by the tridiagonal recurrence, p_j(J_cap)e_0 is a multiple of e_j for j<=M, so both functionals vanish on p_j for 1<=j<=M and give U_0 on p_0. It follows that

L_N(f)=sum_(a=1)^(M+1) w_a f(lambda_a),
deg f<=d.                                     (12)

All nodes and weights are real algebraic in a finite algebraic extension of the moment field. In particular the original cap convolution (9) is an exact finite algebraic evaluation with positive weights. Its original cutoff remains in F_(N,n). Formula (12) does not extend to degree d+1: the original moment there diverges, while the finite quadrature would assign it a finite number.

This constructs the cap’s own positive Jacobi matrix. It does not identify it with the different Jacobi matrix obtained by normalizing the adjacent continuant. No norm comparison between those two metrics, or volume bound for the original source, follows from (12).

Inputs

The weights are those of the exact protected cap. The polynomial in (9) is precisely the original-demand cap/CROSS convolution. No triangle factor, unused spectral product or projected endpoint is changed by defining this inner product.

The positive moment metric retains the stated degree cutoff. The endpoint norm comparison remains unproved, and the next square moment diverges.
