The oversized weak alias fails at the last Pachner norm wall

28 October 2026. This traces the certified oversized weak alias of the nine-tetrahedron counterexample backward through its actual last 2→32\to3 move. The first inverse step already has no boundary-preserving nonnegative angular preimage. The exact five-term map locates the norm divisor and its direction data. This does not prove that the original quantum source assigns zero to the wall.

The exact audit is audit_oversized_alias_last_pachner_wall.py, with results. It derives the local vertex labels from the actual saved face permutations, verifies the shape map in the certified cubic field, and checks the angle and norm identities symbolically.

1. A universal angular preimage criterion

Label a triangular bipyramid by equatorial vertices A,B,CA,B,C and apices D,ED,E. The actual old tetrahedron orderings in this example are

(D,A,B,C),(B,E,C,A). (D,A,B,C),\qquad (B,E,C,A).

Its new orderings are

(E,D,C,B),(E,D,B,A),(E,D,A,C). (E,D,C,B),\qquad(E,D,B,A),\qquad(E,D,A,C). \tag{1}

Write each new angle triple as (αj,βj,γj)(\alpha_j,\beta_j,\gamma_j), j=0,1,2j=0,1,2. The new internal edge DEDE and its opposite equatorial edge both carry αj\alpha_j. Each triple sums to π\pi.

Boundary angle preservation uniquely forces the old triples to be

X=(β1+γ2,β0+γ1,γ0+β2),Y=(γ0+β1,γ1+β2,β0+γ2). \begin{split} X={}&(\beta_1+\gamma_2,\ \beta_0+\gamma_1,\ \gamma_0+\beta_2),\\ Y={}&(\gamma_0+\beta_1,\ \gamma_1+\beta_2,\ \beta_0+\gamma_2). \tag{2} \end{split}

For example, old edge DADA is split between the new β1\beta_1 and γ2\gamma_2 corners; the other entries follow in the same way from the actual vertex labels.

Both forced old sums are

∑X=∑Y=∑j(βj+γj)=3π−∑jαj. \sum X=\sum Y=\sum_j(\beta_j+\gamma_j) =3\pi-\sum_j\alpha_j. \tag{3}

Consequently:

A boundary-preserving replacement by two nonnegative angle tetrahedra exists if and only if the new internal angle is exactly 2π2\pi. When this holds, (2) supplies the replacement and every old triple sums to π\pi. If the new non-internal angles are positive, both old triples are positive. The three equatorial angle equations are then also preserved; for instance

X0+Y1=α0,X2+Y2=α1,X1+Y0=α2. X_0+Y_1=\alpha_0,\quad X_2+Y_2=\alpha_1,\quad X_1+Y_0=\alpha_2. \tag{4}

This is an angular boundary statement, not an assertion that arbitrary off-critical shape moduli or quantum integration cycles are transported by this linear map.

In particular the angular image of an ordinary positive Pachner chart, and its continuous closure with its angle marks retained, has internal winding one. Exponentiating the edge equation and then admitting every weak nonnegative winding locus forgets this local support condition.

2. The certified face fails at the first reverse step

The last forward move in the six-move sequence is (1,0)(1,0): old tetrahedron 1, face opposite its vertex 0, is adjacent to old tetrahedron 0. The actual face maps identify the new tetrahedra as final tetrahedra 0,1,20,1,2 with orderings (1).

Their new edge is final global edge 7, locally 0101 in every new tetrahedron. Its three opposite equatorial edges are final global edges 8,6,38,6,3, respectively.

At the oversized rational point the three new triples are

(0,1/20,19/20),(0,2/5,3/5),(0,1/10,9/10), (0,1/20,19/20),\quad (0,2/5,3/5),\quad (0,1/10,9/10), \tag{5}

in units of π\pi. Their new edge winding is zero. Formula (2) would force the old triples

X=(13/10,13/20,21/20),Y=(27/20,7/10,19/20), X=(13/10,13/20,21/20),\qquad Y=(27/20,7/10,19/20), \tag{6}

each summing to 33, rather than 11. Equivalently, preserving the three equatorial angles would require their sum to be zero, whereas the sum of those six old angles is necessarily 2π2\pi.

Thus the face cannot be pulled back even one step through the previous nonnegative angular contour with its boundary marks fixed. No earlier part of the six-move chain is needed to find the obstruction. Each forced old local sum has an extra 2π2\pi; removing it requires a non-positive angle or a change of branch/boundary charge.

3. The exact actual five-term map

Use the cross-ratio convention

[p,q;r,s]=(p−r)(q−s)(p−s)(q−r). [p,q;r,s]=\frac{(p-r)(q-s)}{(p-s)(q-r)}.

Normalize D=∞,A=0,B=1,C=xD=\infty,A=0,B=1,C=x. If yy is the old native shape of (B,E,C,A)(B,E,C,A), then

E=xyx+y−1. E=\frac{xy}{x+y-1}.

For the new native shapes u,v,wu,v,w of (1), direct substitution gives

u=x1−y,v=(1−x)(1−y)xy,w=y1−x,uvw=1. \boxed{\quad u=\frac{x}{1-y},\qquad v=\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}{xy},\qquad w=\frac{y}{1-x},\qquad uvw=1. \quad} \tag{7}

The audit verifies these identities for the actual old and new geometric shapes in the field a3−2a2+3a−1=0a^3-2a^2+3a-1=0.

The inverse is

x=u(1−w)1−uw,y=w(1−u)1−uw. \boxed{\quad x=\frac{u(1-w)}{1-uw},\qquad y=\frac{w(1-u)}{1-uw}. \quad} \tag{8}

In the positive geometric branch the new edge has the marked relation

Log⁡u+Log⁡v+Log⁡w=2πi. \operatorname{Log}u+\operatorname{Log}v+ \operatorname{Log}w=2\pi i. \tag{9}

For a positive tetrahedron with angles (α,β,γ)(\alpha,\beta,\gamma), the native shape is

z=eiαsin⁡βsin⁡γ. z=e^{i\alpha}\frac{\sin\beta}{\sin\gamma}.

At (5), α=0\alpha=0 and β+γ=π\beta+\gamma=\pi, so all three radial minimum shapes are exactly

u=v=w=1. u=v=w=1.

Their marked log sum is zero, not 2πi2\pi i. The inverse (8) is simultaneously 0/00/0, on the norm divisor 1−uw=01-uw=0. The weak point is not a limit of the positive geometric branch: near three positive real unit shapes their principal arguments are all small positive numbers and cannot sum to 2π2\pi.

Away from the simultaneous corner, the inverse logarithmic connection forms have genuine divisor residues

Res⁡1−uw=0dlog⁡x=Res⁡1−uw=0dlog⁡y=−1. \operatorname{Res}_{1-uw=0}d\log x =\operatorname{Res}_{1-uw=0}d\log y=-1. \tag{10}

At the simultaneous corner the numerator zeros matter. Blow up by

u=1+εA,w=1+εB,AB(A+B)≠0. u=1+\varepsilon A,\qquad w=1+\varepsilon B, \quad AB(A+B)\ne0.

Then

x→BA+B,y→AA+B,x+y→1. x\longrightarrow\frac{B}{A+B},\qquad y\longrightarrow\frac{A}{A+B},\qquad x+y\longrightarrow1. \tag{11}

Thus the exceptional divisor retains the direction A:BA:B. Its finite old shapes cannot both lie in the upper half-plane, since their sum is real. Formula (10) is a generic divisor residue; it must not be copied unchanged to the simultaneous corner where the numerator and denominator valuations cancel.

4. Exact comparison with the retained cyclic pentagon norms

Use the primitive pentagon of cyclic_tetrahedron_pentagon.md, with its actual centers and roots:

aNN=1−U,bNN=1−V,hNN=H=1−U+UV. a_N^N=1-U,\qquad b_N^N=1-V,\qquad h_N^N=H=1-U+UV.

The exact substitution for (7) is

U=w,V=x,H=1−y. U=w,\qquad V=x,\qquad H=1-y. \tag{12}

Its left centers (V,(1−V)U)(V,(1-V)U) are precisely (x,y)(x,y). Its right centers are

(U,−UV1−U,VH)=(w,11−v,u). \left(U,\frac{-UV}{1-U},\frac{V}{H}\right) =\left(w,\frac1{1-v},u\right). \tag{13}

The middle new shape has undergone the indicated quad rotation; its center cannot be frozen at vv.

Under (11), the three right norm-root powers behave as

aNN=1−w∼−εB,(hN/aN)N=1−y1−w∼−1ε(A+B),(aNbN/hN)N=(1−w)(1−x)1−y∼−εA. \begin{split} a_N^N&=1-w\sim-\varepsilon B,\\ (h_N/a_N)^N&=\frac{1-y}{1-w} \sim-\frac1{\varepsilon(A+B)},\\ (a_Nb_N/h_N)^N&=\frac{(1-w)(1-x)}{1-y} \sim-\varepsilon A. \tag{14} \end{split}

Their valuations are therefore (1/N,−1/N,1/N)(1/N,-1/N,1/N) on a compatible root cover. The middle center in (13) goes to infinity. The anchored cyclic pentagon theorem is localized away from these norm divisors and does not itself supply this simultaneous boundary extension or its source/co-source residues.

Equivalently, a small loop of ε\varepsilon around zero, at fixed generic direction A:BA:B, changes the three compatible root frames by (Q,Q−1,Q)(Q,Q^{-1},Q), where Q=e2πi/NQ=e^{2\pi i/N}. The two old norm roots bN,hNb_N,h_N have no local winding at a generic finite direction. This is a concrete root-torsor monodromy charge. It cannot be removed by choosing the three right roots independently.

Norm-root zeros cannot justify deleting the wall. For example, in the normalized finite atom of that note put t=1−centert=1-\text{center} and choose the spectral root μ=(1−t)1/N\mu=(1-t)^{1/N}. Its eigenvalues obey

f0=1,fj=tj/N(μ;Q)j,Dj=γfj,γN=(∏jfj)−1. f_0=1,\qquad f_j=\frac{t^{j/N}}{(\mu;Q)_j}, \qquad D_j=\gamma f_j,\qquad \gamma^N=\left(\prod_jf_j\right)^{-1}.

For 1≤j<N1\le j<N, exactly one denominator factor vanishes: 1−μ∼t/N1-\mu\sim t/N. All other factors are nonzero at a primitive NNth root, including composite NN. Hence

ord⁡tD0=N−12N,ord⁡tDj=2j−N−12N(1≤j<N). \operatorname{ord}_tD_0=\frac{N-1}{2N},\qquad \operatorname{ord}_tD_j=\frac{2j-N-1}{2N}\quad(1\le j<N). \tag{15}

There are both zeros and poles for every N≥2N\ge2, while their sum is zero. A vanishing norm root does not give a vanishing operator or source.

This is consistent with the original cap-wall residue theorem: at its actual Fermat cusp a raw source has a compulsory simple pole with nonzero cap residue. The relevant boundary extension must retain those residues, the dual shift, and the central Jacobian, rather than assign zero from the norm alone.

A removable singularity for the full normalized local product

There is nevertheless a precise local extension, with a narrower claim than separate atom or source extension. Fix a generic holomorphic blow-up curve with x(0)=x0∉{0,1}x(0)=x_0\notin\{0,1\} and y(0)=1−x0y(0)=1-x_0 as in (11). Choose regular initial cyclic clock/shift frames and the compatible root component of the strict primitive pentagon. Then its full normalized right three-atom product, expressed between the common initial and final frames, extends holomorphically and invertibly across the ramified corner.

Indeed the left centers are xx and yy, whose limiting values are neither zero nor one. Their norm roots bN,hNb_N,h_N are therefore locally nonzero holomorphic functions; their normalization determinants are nonzero as well. The two left matrices are holomorphic and invertible. The common final splitting roots are bNb_N times the initial first root and the initial second root divided by hNh_N; they remain regular and nonzero. On the punctured compatible root cover the exact pentagon identifies the right product with this left product. The equality supplies its removable extension.

This conclusion retains all determinant roots and common-frame identifications. A raw product must retain its raw scalar from the pentagon theorem. No claim is made that its unnormalized individual factors have removable singularities. The argument gives no estimate uniform in NN, no right to drop intermediate residues, and no comparison with an original knot source unless the required exterior source/co-source maps have also been transported.

5. The precise constructive target

The local classical support test is now explicit: an inverse positive angular Pachner replacement must retain new-edge winding one, not merely the exponentiated equation uvw=1uvw=1. For the certified excessive face, the failing move, its marked integer defect, the norm divisors, and the direction A:BA:B are known.

The remaining quantum task is a matched source/carry extension of this last move across (14), proving how its marked 2π2\pi charge is represented and paired. Original integer degree conservation can retain information lost modulo NN, but no identification of that integer carry with this geometric edge winding has been proved here. The existing Ward augmentation shows that some wall charge survives; it does not allow all walls to be discarded.

In particular we have not proved a zero quantum coefficient for (5), nor inferred an oversized original period. What is available for a general proof is a universal local angular admissibility criterion and an exact divisor calculation on which the required quantum source theorem can be tested.
