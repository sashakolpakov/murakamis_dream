An explicit volume theorem for ordered trees of crossing circles

The number of twists at a region is not the only quantity that can make filling controllable. In the construction below, a crossing circle of large degree meets many geometric pieces. Its complete cusp makes even a small integer twist correspond to a long normalized filling slope. This gives a numerical sufficient condition for the volume conjecture in which some twist coefficients may be short or zero.

The local subtree theorem gives another sufficient condition for exactly this construction: max⁡vdv/(dv2+nv2)≤2−120\max_v d_v/(d_v^2+n_v^2)\le2^{-120}. Its constant is smaller, but it has no sum over the vertices. In particular one universal twist threshold suffices for any finite tree. The summed criterion proved below remains a separate, often numerically better test.

The knot construction

A two-string tangle is drawn in a ball with four ordered boundary points: lower left, lower right, upper right, upper left. A product places one ball above another and joins their two adjacent endpoints by uncrossed arcs. The order of the factors is retained. The operation TT rotates an entire ball through the specified quarter-turn in the diagram plane. Cylindrical closure joins lower left to upper left and lower right to upper right outside the final ball.

Fix a finite tree 𝒯\mathcal T with at least one edge, together with the cyclic order of the edges and the tangle frame at each vertex. There is one original crossing circle at every vertex, including the terminal vertices. There is no unmarked product junction. Each edge represents an actual quarter-turn of a child tangle, rather than a crossing of two drawn edges.

Here is a precise recursive way to specify the diagrams. Choose a terminal vertex as root. At a nonroot vertex vv, put the integer braid RnvR^{n_v} between the two ordered lists of its child tangles: Wv=(∏w∈Iv−T(Ww))Rnv(∏w∈Iv+T(Ww)). W_v=\left(\prod_{w\in I_v^-}T(W_w)\right)R^{n_v} \left(\prod_{w\in I_v^+}T(W_w)\right). \tag{1} The lists Iv−I_v^- and Iv+I_v^+ specify the position of the crossing box in the original cyclic order. Empty lists are allowed. If ww is the root’s unique neighbor, close Rn0T(Ww)R^{n_0}T(W_w) cylindrically. This is an actual link diagram, not a tensor network considered only up to scalar identities. Other choices of cutting section are related by the physical closed-collar moves, with their induced rotations and reversals of factor order retained.

The augmented diagram replaces each RnvR^{n_v} by its crossing circle and a retained half twist ϵv∈{0,±1}\epsilon_v\in\{0,\pm1\}, where nv≡ϵv(mod⁡2)n_v\equiv\epsilon_v\pmod2. Filling that circle on its original disk-framed slope μv+pvλv\mu_v+p_v\lambda_v, with nv=ϵv−2pv, n_v=\epsilon_v-2p_v, \tag{2} restores exactly the indicated integer braid. Here μv\mu_v goes once around the auxiliary circle and λv\lambda_v follows its punctured disk. The main strands are not filled.

We require that the closed main strands form one knot KK. This includes excluding a closed main component created inside an intermediate box. The test uses only the strand pairings and the parities of the nvn_v; large magnitudes alone do not guarantee it. Write dvd_v for the degree of vv in the circle tree and E=|E(𝒯)|E=|E(\mathcal T)|. This graph degree is unrelated to the quantum level NN.

The quantitative theorem

Let FN(M)F_N(M) be the zero-framed reduced Akutsu–Deguchi–Ohtsuki (ADO) invariant of the actual knot, normalized to one on the unknot. Its marked meridian coordinate is MM, and at qN=eπi/Nq_N=e^{\pi i/N} its complete value is the reduced Kashaev invariant KN(K)=FN(1)K_N(K)=F_N(1). Moving the meridian on the scale considered here means setting M=qNsM=q_N^s, with ss in a fixed complex disk. Define 𝔖(𝒯,𝒏)=∑vdvdv2+nv2,HN(s)=sin⁡(πs)Nsin⁡(πs/N),HN(0)=1. \mathfrak S(\mathcal T,\mathbf n) =\sum_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2},\qquad H_N(s)=\frac{\sin(\pi s)}{N\sin(\pi s/N)},\quad H_N(0)=1. \tag{3} Let w(D)w(D) be the oriented writhe of the actual filled diagram, using the same positive checked-crossing convention as RR. The exact framing and crossing factor is AN(s)=qNa(D)s2/2,a(D)=−w(D)−∑vnv. A_N(s)=q_N^{a(D)s^2/2},\qquad a(D)=-w(D)-\sum_v n_v. \tag{4} The power uses the fixed logarithm log⁡qN=πi/N\log q_N=\pi i/N. All crossing and strand-turn factors are included in (4); it is not a chosen normalizing phase.

Theorem. Fix the ordered tree, its diagram frames, and its signed integer powers before letting NN tend to infinity. Suppose the main strands form a knot and 𝔖(𝒯,𝒏)≤2−25. \mathfrak S(\mathcal T,\mathbf n)\le2^{-25}. \tag{5} Then the knot complement is hyperbolic, and limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN(K)|=Vol⁡(S3\K). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N(K)| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K). \tag{6} For every fixed R<1R<1, the stronger statements FN(qNs)FN(1)AN(s)HN(s)=1+O𝒯,𝒏,R(N−1),|s|≤R, \frac{F_N(q_N^s)}{F_N(1)A_N(s)H_N(s)} =1+O_{\mathcal T,\mathbf n,R}(N^{-1}), \qquad |s|\le R, \tag{7} and 2πNlog⁡|FN(qNs)|→Vol⁡(S3\K) \frac{2\pi}{N}\log|F_N(q_N^s)| \longrightarrow\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K) \tag{8} hold uniformly on that disk. In particular these values are nonzero there for all sufficiently large levels. Both level parities and composite levels are included. The tree, its degrees, its twist numbers and the radius RR do not grow with NN.

The constant in (5) is conservative. It makes the condition explicit; no optimality is asserted. There is no additional strict-angle hypothesis for this literal class. Indeed an unturned elementary circle has attainable output interval (1/2,1)(1/2,1), and a turned subtree has interval (0,1/2)(0,1/2). The exact product interval shows that one upper-interval factor together with any number of lower-interval factors again has the full upper interval, in any fixed association. The turn changes it to the lower interval. At the terminal root the two complementary intervals give a strict closing pair. This inductive construction supplies the strict augmented geometry used by the finite-source proof. In the two-circle case the excluded two-odd pattern is not a knot; the knot test retains the necessary even, or pure, leaf.

Two consequences make the range of (5) concrete.

  • For a tree with EE edges, every tuple satisfying |nv|≥8192E|n_v|\ge8192\sqrt E satisfies (5), because ∑vdv=2E\sum_vd_v=2E.
  • Start with any ordered tree on mm vertices, with arbitrary fixed powers at those vertices. Attach BB new terminal circles at each vertex and give each added circle a power of magnitude at least BB. The resulting sum is strictly less than 2m/B2m/B. Hence B≥226mB\ge2^{26}m suffices, with the original powers still arbitrary, including zero. The knot/parity test remains necessary.

An unmarked product junction can also be included by inserting the literal identity braid R0R^0 at its prescribed position in the ordered stack. Add a crossing circle there in the augmented diagram and fill it with ϵ=p=n=0\epsilon=p=n=0. The filled diagram is unchanged. The augmented exterior is different, and the added degree-dd circle contributes 1/d1/d to (3). This gives a direct application to constructions with sufficiently large-degree unmarked junctions when the same total bound holds. It does not treat such junctions as free or assert that a low-degree unmarked junction satisfies (5).

All these are finite constructions. Their potentially large size is fixed before the quantum limit. Related results use several invariant and root conventions; the coverage discussion records their precise scopes.

Why the original exterior has the required geometric pieces

Cut the augmented diagram along the projection sphere and the original crossing disks. The two resulting balls carry white projection faces and shaded disk triangles. At a leaf with half-segments (v0,v1,v2,v3)(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3), the shaded triangles are (Ov,v1,v2)(O_v,v_1,v_2) and (Ov,v3,v0)(O_v,v_3,v_0), where OvO_v is the auxiliary-circle end. The literal strand matching fixes every cyclic vertex order and hence every white face boundary.

Adding one terminal child supplies an explicit local cell attachment. Let hvh_v denote the quarter-turned crossing-circle tangle. Write its parent’s unturned box as Pv=T−1hvP_v=T^{-1}h_v, so its exposed tuple is (v3,v0,v1,v2)(v_3,v_0,v_1,v_2). Thus this is an attachment in the original cut, not a recognition based on equal volumes.

The left-insertion version attaches along the other parent triangle. It allows the parent box to remain at its specified position among its children. Start from one tree edge and insert the remaining children in the appropriate left and right order, carrying each rotated child ball with its frame. Each projection half is a tree of EE octahedra attached along E−1E-1 triangles. Its remaining shaded faces are exactly the original two per circle.

The internal interfaces give the additional disk pages. At a circle of degree dvd_v, the dvd_v incident blocks occur in the original cyclic order. One distinguished seam retains the original half twist; the other seams are the untwisted insertion maps. In particular the construction does not put one copy of the retained crossing on every incident edge. It also retains the actual half-turn produced when a turn is moved across the closing sphere. Restoring the labeled faces recovers the original augmented link and its primitive disk paths. The ordered decomposition proof gives the full half-segment and face-word calculation.

The action and the invariant have the same normalization

The exact finite angular sum has a Gaussian bound weighted by the vertex degrees. Its real decay constant can be taken as 1/4001/400 and its imaginary growth constant as 7070.

A geometric form of the numerical condition

At the complete auxiliary cusp the primitive shape is 2icv/(1−iϵvcv)2i c_v/(1-i\epsilon_v c_v), where cv=1/dvc_v=1/d_v. The normalized length of a slope means its Euclidean length divided by the square root of the cusp area. For the actual primitive filling slope this gives ℒv2=dv2+nv22dv,𝔖=12∑vℒv−2. \mathcal L_v^2=\frac{d_v^2+n_v^2}{2d_v},\qquad \mathfrak S=\frac12\sum_v\mathcal L_v^{-2}. \tag{20} Thus (5) is equivalently ∑vℒv−2≤2−24\sum_v\mathcal L_v^{-2}\le2^{-24}. This identity explains why high degree can compensate for a small crossing power. The proof above establishes the completion for these particular ordered exteriors; it does not import an unrelated normalized-length criterion or classify all short fillings.

The preferred cusp and the first correction

The remaining cusp belongs to the knot itself. Normalize its meridian translation to one. Its preferred longitude, which has linking number zero with the knot, then translates by a complex number τK\tau_K, the preferred cusp shape. This geometric normalization is needed to interpret the first correction in the quantum profile.

The same limit from a pointed quantum A-operator

Fix a genuine all-level quantum A-operator PP annihilating the knot’s reduced ADO family. Fix the finite initial vector c0c_0 of ordinary-color polynomials selecting that family. The pointed operator construction, Sections 2–3, forms the ordered recurrence row rN(q)r_N(q) and proves the exact identity 𝒫N(P,c0)=CT⁡t=0[rN(qN(1+t))c0(qN(1+t))]=KN(K). \mathscr P_N(P,c_0) =\operatorname{CT}_{t=0} \bigl[r_N(q_N(1+t))c_0(q_N(1+t))\bigr]=K_N(K). \tag{25} Here CT\operatorname{CT} extracts the constant Laurent coefficient of the whole product. This retains the cancellations at singular recurrence steps and the required derivatives of the fixed initial data. It does not evaluate each singular factor separately.

Thus the new quantitative class also has an operator period on the left and its actual complement volume on the right. The finite initial data remain essential. Identifying the selected geometric factor of the classical symbol of PP is a separate problem; no such identification is assumed in this corollary.
