Exact spectral coefficient energy at a protected outer face

31 October 2026. Independently reviewed theorem. A one-sided original coefficient with one outer degree zero has an explicit real-spectral maximum, uniformly through all closed index boundaries and at both level parities. Its endpoint-conditioned energy is exactly a triangle entropy in the limit. This is a local coefficient theorem, not a global shared-flow bound or a new filling theorem.

1. The actual coefficient and its positive reference

Put d=N−1d=N-1, m=⌊N/2⌋m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor, and

cr=2cos⁡(2r+1)πN,sr=2sin⁡πrN,VN(σ)=χN(σ)(σ−ΩN)−1. c_r=2\cos\frac{(2r+1)\pi}{N},\qquad s_r=2\sin\frac{\pi r}{N},\qquad V_N(\sigma)=\chi_N(\sigma)(\sigma-\Omega_N)^{-1}.

Use the original homogeneous polynomial HVNH_{V_N}. On LEFT support set y=0y=0 and take

v=(j−i,i,d−j,0),0≤i≤j<m. v=(j-i,i,d-j,0),\qquad0\le i\le j<m.

Every dimer vanishes; every lowering step must use xx. The original coefficient has one physical path. Up to its recorded sign,

|aN(v;σ)|=(∏r=i+1jsr2)(∏0≤r<Nr∉{i,…,j}|σ−cr|). \left|a_N(v;\sigma)\right| =\left(\prod_{r=i+1}^j s_r^2\right) \left(\prod_{\substack{0\le r<N\\r\notin\{i,\ldots,j\}}} |\sigma-c_r|\right).\tag{1}

All unused rows are present, including the entire omitted half. This is the exact triangular-source path formula, not an absolute replacement inside a recurrence or a division at a node. The coefficient before absolute values has sign (−1)j−i(-1)^{j-i} times the displayed product with its signed linear factors.

Retain the positive reference

FN={−VN(−2),Neven,VN(−2)−N−2VN′(−2),Nodd,pN(v)=[xvxbvbcvcyvy]HFN>0. F_N= \begin{cases} -V_N(-2),&N\ \text{even},\\ V_N(-2)-N^{-2}V_N'(-2),&N\ \text{odd}, \end{cases} \qquad p_N(v)=[x^{v_x}b^{v_b}c^{v_c}y^{v_y}]H_{F_N}>0.\tag{2}

Write μ=i/N\mu=i/N, ν=j/N\nu=j/N, w=ν−μw=\nu-\mu, and define on [0,1/2][0,1/2]

f(t)=Λ(πt)+Λ(π/2−πt)π,f′(t)=log⁡cot⁡(πt),f(0)=f(1/2)=0. f(t)=\frac{\Lambda(\pi t)+\Lambda(\pi/2-\pi t)}{\pi}, \qquad f'(t)=\log\cot(\pi t),\qquad f(0)=f(1/2)=0.\tag{3}

The closed triangle entropy is C(α)=∑rf(αr)C(\alpha)=\sum_r f(\alpha_r), ∑rαr=1/2\sum_r\alpha_r=1/2.

2. Uniform theorem

Define the exact coefficient supremum

kN*(v)=1Nlog⁡maxσ∈[−2,2]|aN(v;σ)|. k_N^*(v)=\frac1N\log\max_{\sigma\in[-2,2]}|a_N(v;\sigma)|.

Uniformly over all indices in (1), at both parities,

kN*(v)=f(μ)+f(ν−μ)−f(ν)+O(log⁡NN),1Nlog⁡pN(v)=2(f(μ)−f(ν))+O(log⁡NN). \begin{aligned} k_N^*(v) &=f(\mu)+f(\nu-\mu)-f(\nu) +O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right),\\ \frac1N\log p_N(v) &=2\bigl(f(\mu)-f(\nu)\bigr) +O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right). \end{aligned}\tag{4}

Consequently the exact relative-energy profile is

1Nlog⁡maxσ∈[−2,2]|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)=2f(w)+O(log⁡NN)=C(w,12−w,0)+O(log⁡NN). \boxed{\quad \frac1N\log \frac{\max_{\sigma\in[-2,2]}|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)} =2f(w)+O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right) =C(w,\tfrac12-w,0)+O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right). \quad}\tag{5}

The constants are independent of i,j,Ni,j,N. The formula includes diagonal limits, μ=0\mu=0, and the limiting upper edge ν=1/2\nu=1/2. Outer reflection gives x=0x=0; original row reflection gives RIGHT. Replacing the NN-normalized degrees by v/dv/d preserves the same continuous limit.

3. Uniform control of the exact product

Set σ=−2cos⁡(2πδ)\sigma=-2\cos(2\pi\delta), 0≤δ≤1/20\le\delta\le1/2, and

K(μ,ν;δ)=∫μνlog⁡sin⁡2(πt)|cos⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πδ)|dt. K(\mu,\nu;\delta)=\int_\mu^\nu \log\frac{\sin^2(\pi t)} {|\cos^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\delta)|}\,dt.\tag{6}

Every logarithmic singularity is integrable. We first prove

kN*(v)=max0≤δ≤1/2K(μ,ν;δ)+O(log⁡NN) k_N^*(v)= \max_{0\le\delta\le1/2}K(\mu,\nu;\delta) +O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right)\tag{7}

uniformly on the closed index domain.

A contiguous mesh sum of log⁡|2sin⁡(π(t−a))|\log|2\sin(\pi(t-a))| has upper Riemann error O(log⁡N)O(\log N), uniformly in its shift and interval endpoints. Remove at most two cells nearest a zero. Their integral is O(log⁡N/N)O(\log N/N), while a sample exceptionally close to the zero can only improve the upper bound. On the other cells, |f′(t)|≤C/dist⁡(t,a+ℤ)\lvert f'(t)\rvert\le C/\operatorname{dist}(t,a+\mathbb Z) gives an accumulated harmonic-sum error O(log⁡N)O(\log N). If every sampled distance to a zero is at least c/Nc/N, the same proof gives a two-sided error. Cosine factors follow by translation.

For tr=(r+1/2)/Nt_r=(r+1/2)/N, the unused factors in (1) satisfy

|σ−cr|=4|cos⁡(π(tr+δ))cos⁡(π(tr−δ))|. |\sigma-c_r| =4|\cos(\pi(t_r+\delta))\cos(\pi(t_r-\delta))|.

The integral of this logarithm over 0≤t≤10\le t\le1 is zero. Apply the uniform upper mesh estimate separately to the unused prefix and suffix, and the sine estimate to the used monomer factors. Their difference is exactly (6), with only O(log⁡N/N)O(\log N/N) error. No sampled vanishing factor has been divided out.

For the lower bound in (7), choose a point within O(1/N)O(1/N) of a maximizing δ\delta on the lattice

δN∈{N−1ℤ,Neven,N−1(ℤ+12),Nodd,0≤δN≤12. \delta_N\in \begin{cases} N^{-1}\mathbb Z,&N\ \text{even},\\ N^{-1}(\mathbb Z+\tfrac12),&N\ \text{odd}, \end{cases} \qquad0\le\delta_N\le\tfrac12.\tag{8}

At these points χN(σ)=4\chi_N(\sigma)=4, and every sampled cosine stays at least half a mesh step from its zero. Thus the two-sided estimate applies to all unused intervals. The modulus of continuity of each logarithmic integral under an O(1/N)O(1/N) shift is O(log⁡N/N)O(\log N/N), uniformly in the interval. This proves (7), including its endpoint cases. Nonempty monomer products start at r≥1r\ge1, so their lower endpoint uses the same elementary sine estimate.

At even levels (2) is the product (1) at δ=0\delta=0. At odd levels LEFT skips the unique central zero row. The derivative in (2) removes exactly that factor; its N−2N^{-2} scalar and the single removed mesh factor have only O(log⁡N)O(\log N) logarithmic cost. Hence, uniformly,

1Nlog⁡pN(v)=2∫μνlog⁡tan⁡(πt)dt+O(log⁡NN)=2(f(μ)−f(ν))+O(log⁡NN). \frac1N\log p_N(v) =2\int_\mu^\nu\log\tan(\pi t)\,dt +O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right) =2\bigl(f(\mu)-f(\nu)\bigr) +O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right).\tag{9}

In particular this is not a comparison with bare odd VN(−2)V_N(-2), whose one-sided coefficients vanish.

4. The exact maximizing spectral point

For 0<μ<ν<1/20<\mu<\nu<1/2, differentiation of (6), or of its explicit logarithmic primitive, gives

∂δK=log⁡|cos⁡(π(μ+δ))cos⁡(π(ν−δ))cos⁡(π(ν+δ))cos⁡(π(μ−δ))|. \partial_\delta K =\log\left| \frac{\cos(\pi(\mu+\delta))\cos(\pi(\nu-\delta))} {\cos(\pi(\nu+\delta))\cos(\pi(\mu-\delta))} \right|.\tag{10}

It is positive before the pole interval (1/2−ν,1/2−μ)(1/2-\nu,1/2-\mu) and negative afterwards. Inside that interval its sign is the sign of

cos⁡(π(μ+ν))+cos⁡(π(ν−μ))cos⁡(2πδ). \cos(\pi(\mu+\nu)) +\cos(\pi(\nu-\mu))\cos(2\pi\delta).

This expression strictly decreases. There is a unique maximum, given by

cos⁡(2πδ*)=−cos⁡(π(μ+ν))cos⁡(π(ν−μ)). \boxed{\quad \cos(2\pi\delta_*) =-\frac{\cos(\pi(\mu+\nu))}{\cos(\pi(\nu-\mu))}. \quad}\tag{11}

The quotient has modulus less than one. For μ=0<ν<1/2\mu=0<\nu<1/2, the maximum is δ*=1/2\delta_*=1/2 and K*=0K^*=0; for 0<μ<ν=1/20<\mu<\nu=1/2, it is δ*=0\delta_*=0. The diagonal interval has zero action. At μ=0,ν=1/2\mu=0,\nu=1/2 the maximum need not be unique. Continuity treats all these overlaps.

For an explicit primitive, let J(t)=−Λ(πt)/πJ(t)=-\Lambda(\pi t)/\pi, extended periodically. Then

K(μ,ν;δ)=2[J(ν)−J(μ)]−J(12+ν+δ)+J(12+μ+δ)−J(12+ν−δ)+J(12+μ−δ). \begin{aligned} K(\mu,\nu;\delta) ={}&2[J(\nu)-J(\mu)]\\ &-J(\tfrac12+\nu+\delta)+J(\tfrac12+\mu+\delta)\\ &-J(\tfrac12+\nu-\delta)+J(\tfrac12+\mu-\delta). \end{aligned}\tag{12}

5. Simplification to a triangle entropy

Put a=πμa=\pi\mu, b=πνb=\pi\nu, w=ν−μw=\nu-\mu. Equation (11) gives the exact unsquared identities

cos⁡(2a)+cos⁡(2πδ*)=sin⁡(2a)tan⁡(πw),cos⁡(2b)+cos⁡(2πδ*)=−sin⁡(2b)tan⁡(πw). \begin{aligned} \cos(2a)+\cos(2\pi\delta_*)&=\sin(2a)\tan(\pi w),\\ \cos(2b)+\cos(2\pi\delta_*)&=-\sin(2b)\tan(\pi w). \end{aligned}\tag{13}

Let h0=2(f(μ)−f(ν))h_0=2(f(\mu)-f(\nu)) and e=2K(μ,ν;δ*)−h0e=2K(\mu,\nu;\delta_*)-h_0. Directly from (6),

e=2∫μνlog⁡|sin⁡(2πt)cos⁡(2πt)+cos⁡(2πδ*)|dt. e=2\int_\mu^\nu \log\left| \frac{\sin(2\pi t)} {\cos(2\pi t)+\cos(2\pi\delta_*)} \right|dt.\tag{14}

The spectral derivative vanishes at the maximizer, so the envelope rule and (13) yield

∂μe=−2log⁡cot⁡(πw),∂νe=2log⁡cot⁡(πw). \partial_\mu e=-2\log\cot(\pi w),\qquad \partial_\nu e= 2\log\cot(\pi w).

Thus ee depends only on ww, and its zero-width value is zero. Integrating f′(w)=log⁡cot⁡(πw)f'(w)=\log\cot(\pi w) gives

e=2f(w),K*=f(μ)+f(w)−f(ν). e=2f(w),\qquad K^*=f(\mu)+f(w)-f(\nu).\tag{15}

Continuity proves these equalities on the full closed domain. Since f(1/2−w)=f(w)f(1/2-w)=f(w), equations (7), (9), and (15) prove (4)–(5).

6. Global limitation and validation

The limiting LEFT degree vector is (w,μ,1−ν,0)(w,\mu,1-\nu,0). The triangle entropy in (5) is the entropy of the complete boundary allocation

(w,12−w,12,0). (w,\tfrac12-w,\tfrac12,0).

This moves 1/2−ν1/2-\nu units from the overloaded middle to the other middle, while preserving the outer degree. It does not preserve the shared global face targets by itself. On a middle-edge forest, fixing the other triangle allocations generally fixes the middle transport uniquely. A global redistribution argument remains necessary before (5) can bound the shared conditioned energy.

Root and an independent peer completed full analytical reviews of the literal coefficient, both parity grids, uniform logarithmic mesh bounds, odd derivative, unique spectral maximum, and identity (15): PASS.

It checks 144 monomer coefficients, 6,408 original grid entries including 2,082 illegal-total zeros, 29 reference coefficients, 21 exact χN=4\chi_N=4 samples and 125 node avoidances, three symbolic identities and six fixed integral identities. These exact checks supplement the proof; no large-NN rate samples are used as proof of (4) or of a shared energy bound.
