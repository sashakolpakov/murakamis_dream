Mesoscopic diagonal currents on the actual cup-slide packet

29 October 2026. The original cup-slide normalization packet admits a coherent subexponential estimate with operator-valued diagonal clock couplings of growing, sublinear Laurent bandwidth. This uses the source-exact packet before taking absolute values, not a dephasing of its individual factorial coefficients.

The statement is about the specified exposed recorded packet. It is not automatically an operator identity on an arbitrary remaining physical head. The all-input distinction is explicit in Section 4.

1. Literal source packet and its exact controlled-clock contraction

Use the original notation and full Cartan factor of the all-input cup slide:

N>=2, d=N-1, Q=exp(2pi*i/N), x=exp(pi*i*d/N),
F_j=(Q;Q)_j, b_j=x^(-d)Q^(-j) conjugate(F_j),
Gbar_N(z)=sum_(j=0)^d conjugate(F_j) z^j.

This is exactly b=T R12 R23 p, with the original physical recorded middle-color projectors, ordinary cap and specified lowest marked cut. All original external framing/peripheral factors chi_N stay outside. The finite identity Q^(j+1)F_j=F_j-F_(j+1), F_N=0, gives

P_N(z):=sum_(j=0)^d Q^(-j) conjugate(F_j) z^j
      =Q[(1-1/z)Gbar_N(z)+1/z].                       (1)

The apparent singularity at zero is removable. In particular P_N(1)=Q and the original packet closes to x^(-d)Q, with its full unit phase.

Let A_r(t), |r|<=D, be operators on any spectator space. Define the EXPLICIT diagonal recorded-color exterior

f_j(t)=sum_(|r|<=D) A_r(t) (tQ^r)^j.

The exact contraction, preserving original operator order, is

sum_j f_j(t)b_j=x^(-d) sum_(|r|<=D) A_r(t)P_N(tQ^r).  (2)

Only distributivity is used in (2). The A_r need not commute with one another or be scalar. Their norm is retained below. In particular an arbitrary exterior with O(N) Laurent bandwidth is not silently replaced by a fixed-band clock polynomial.

2. Mesoscopic source bound

The independently proved clock-disk theorem gives, for 0<C<=N/2,

sup_(|z-1|<=C/N)|G_N(z)| <= B_N(C),
B_N(C)=2exp(2C)N^2 (1+2N/(3C))^(C/4).                 (3)

Coefficient conjugation gives the SAME bound for Gbar_N. Suppose |t-1|<=c/N and put C=c+2pi*D. Then

|tQ^r-1| <= |t-1|+|Q^r-1| <= C/N, |r|<=D.           (4)

When C<=N/2, every argument in (2) has modulus at least1/2. Equations (1)–(4) consequently prove the actual coherent inequality

||sum_j f_j(t)b_j||
  <= (sum_r ||A_r(t)||) [2+(2C/N)B_N(C)].             (5)

Any supplied norm on the spectator coefficient space may be used. No N-dependent change of physical source metric appears in (5). The phase x^(-d) has modulus one and remains in the exact identity (2).

Let now c=c_N and D=D_N grow with N. If

c_N+D_N=o(N),
log^+ sup_(|t-1|<=c_N/N) sum_r||A_r(t)|| = o(N),      (6)

then the right side of (5) is exp(o(N)). Indeed

log B_N(C)=O(log N+C+C log(1+N/C))=o(N),

since s log(1+1/s) tends to zero as s=C/N tends to zero. The cases C=0 or D=0,t=1 follow directly from P_N(1)=Q. Fixed bandwidth, fixed c, and polynomial spectator mass give a polynomial bound.

This controls a genuinely growing set of diagonal clock observables on the known original packet. Individual |F_j| can still be exponential; the coherent identity (1) has been used before estimation.

3. Normal jets and compatible normalization leaves

If the coefficient operators are holomorphic on a slightly larger disk with the same subexponential mass condition, Cauchy’s formula also bounds fixed clock-normal derivatives. For a radius r_N>0 with log^+(1/r_N)=o(N),

|| (1/s!) partial_t^s sum_j f_j(t)b_j |_(t=1)||
  <= r_N^(-s) sup_(|t-1|=r_N)||sum_j f_j(t)b_j||.    (7)

Thus fixed s costs exp(o(N)); growing orders are allowed whenever s log^+(1/r_N)=o(N), in addition to the stated supremum bound. This is a derivative of the recorded packet, not a meridian identification or a physical two-color raising/lowering insertion.

The normalization-current quotient may be composed with (2) when its literal all-input pointing map exposes this packet and its spectator coefficients in exactly this slot. For example, its fixed-forest radial Mellin Taylor coefficients have polynomial coefficient mass; (5) then retains that mass and gives the same subexponential clock bound. Each leaf contraction must be an actual tensor/source identity before it is estimated. No independent mark or new 1/N average is introduced for such a leaf.

The assertion is deliberately typed. It does not say that any internal recorded-color seam can be moved to the marked cut, or that arbitrary original crossings preserve this filtered clock class. Their factorial dependence and frequencies can have order N.

4. Why the scalar packet does not already solve physical gluing

There is a simple all-input test. The original cup slide is

(R12 R23)(p12 tensor I)=I tensor p23.

Keep an arbitrary physical input color a on the remaining head, record the middle color n, and perform the SAME terminal R23^2 and ordinary cap as before. Write L=kappa^(-1)R12 on degree d. The resulting actual open-head operator is diagonal, with entries

C_a(t)=sum_(n=0)^d t^n x^(n-a)Q^(-a)(L^2)_(d-a,n).   (8)

To derive (8), multiply the literal cup coefficient N x^(n-d), the full crossing square kappa2(L2)_(d-a,n), and the cap x(-a)Q(-a)/N, using kappa2=xd. All original turns and phases are therefore fixed. At t=1, Hopf closure gives C_a(1)=kappa^2 for EVERY a. At a=d, (8) is exactly x^(-d)P_N(t), the scalar packet estimated above.

A scalar bound for this last row does not prove a uniform all-a operator bound for (8). Likewise applying a unilateral shift to the RECORDING register is not automatically inserting physical Ecal or F before the projector: Pi_j Ecal Pi_k retains two colors and changes the remaining terminal coefficients. Such a move needs an actual two-color/all-port identity. Equations (2)–(7) apply to diagonal controlled-clock exteriors on the already exposed original packet; they do not assume those missing identities.

This is a quantitative source-current class, not a sharp full-knot volume theorem. Binding the complete internal currents to the knot’s geometric regulator remains unproved.

Audit checks the original physical packet, its full phase and operator-valued controlled-clock contractions; it also tests the quantitative disk comparison on growing bandwidths without separating factorial modes.
