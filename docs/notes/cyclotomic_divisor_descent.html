Uniform descent of a rank-one comparison by divisor flow

Research note, 27 October 2026. This proves cases of the proposed cyclotomic rigidity mechanism without assuming bounded degree for the levelwise Hilbert-90 gauges. The hypotheses are instead a fixed split divisor presentation, or an unramified nonresonant algebraic divisor presentation, of the generic rank-one comparison coefficient. Cyclic transport detects its divisor charges; a finite flow solves the comparison and gives an explicit bound on its support.

This is a theorem about comparison lines, not about the entire quantum A-module of a knot. No claim is made that the missing actual/geometric comparison line already has the presentation or norm compatibility required here. The volume conjecture remains unproved.

1. The literature category is cyclotomic q-curvature

For coverage, the abstract of Di Vizio–Hardouin, 1205.1692 describes a generic difference-Galois group through specializations of q at roots of unity. Their parameterized version, 1205.1694 also records the differential structure. These are closer to the user’s proposed cyclotomic rigidity question than a scalar norm equation at one root. Neither theorem nor proof is used below. Only this coverage and the original meaning of cyclic transport are invoked; all implications needed for our restricted statement are proved directly.

Use the actual parameters q_N=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2 and the even meridian Y=M^2. Let sigma(Y)=QY, with q fixed. For a rank-one coefficient a(q,Y), its cyclic transport at level N is

C_N(Y)=product_(j=0)^(N-1) a(q_N,Q_N^j Y),
Q_N=q_N^2.                                  (1.1)

For a comparison line, C_N=1 means that the two compared norm characters agree, with the full meridian function retained. This is a whole rational identity, not only its value at Y=1.

2. A fixed split-divisor class

Assume a has the fixed finite presentation

a(q,Y)=c(q) Y^s
         product_(c,u) (1-c q^(2u)Y)^(n_(c,u)), (2.1)

where c(q) is a nonzero rational function of q, s and the n_(c,u) are integers, u ranges through finitely many integers, and the distinct labels c are nonzero complex constants. In particular c is not an unknown function of q or Y. We distinguish the scalar c(q) from the finitely many constant labels in the product.

The factors are whole functions on q,Y before root specialization. This class includes finite products of linear factors in known q-shift orbits, with arbitrary integer multiplicities and composite root levels. It is not every rational function of q,Y. A general zero algebraic over C(q) need not be c q^(2u).

Divisor-descent theorem. Suppose C_N(Y)=1 in C(Y) for every sufficiently large N. Then s=0 and

sum_u n_(c,u)=0 for every distinct label c,
c(q)=q^(2k) for an integer k.                (2.2)

There is a rational function f(q,Y), independent of N, such that

a(q,Y)=sigma(f)/f.                          (2.3)

It can be constructed explicitly. Put

e_(c,u)=-sum_(v<=u) n_(c,v),
f(q,Y)=Y^k product_(c,u)(1-c q^(2u)Y)^(e_(c,u)). (2.4)

The e have finite support because their total charges vanish. No bound or coherence for separately chosen root-level gauges is assumed. The original fixed divisor data give the bound afterward.

3. Proof of the divisor and constant statements

Set U=Y^N. The elementary finite product gives

product_(j=0)^(N-1)(1-c Q_N^(u+j)Y)=1-c^N U,
product_(j=0)^(N-1) Q_N^j Y=(-1)^(N-1)U.

Writing m_c=sum_u n_(c,u), the hypothesis is exactly

c(q_N)^N (-1)^(s(N-1)) U^s
                     product_c(1-c^N U)^(m_c)=1. (3.1)

All factors after U^s are units at U=0. Hence s=0, and evaluating at U=0 gives c(q_N)^N=1. It follows also that

product_c(1-c^N U)^(m_c)=1.                  (3.2)

There are infinitely many N for which the finitely many c^N are pairwise distinct. To see this, a collision requires that a ratio c/c’ be a root of unity of order dividing N. Let L be the least common multiple of all these finitely many torsion orders, or one if there are none. Every N coprime to L avoids all collisions. Choose such a sufficiently large N. The distinct linear factors in (3.2) force every m_c=0.

Here is a direct proof of the scalar assertion, including its half-root convention. The identity |c(q_N)|=1 excludes a zero or pole of c at q=1. Choose an analytic logarithm ell(q) near one and set

L_N=N ell(exp(pi*i/N)) in 2pi*i Z.

Expansion of the fixed analytic function, rather than separate level functions, gives

L_N=N ell(1)+pi*i ell'(1)+O(1/N).

Therefore L_(N+1)-L_N=ell(1)+O(1/N^2). These differences belong to the discrete set 2pii Z, so ell(1) belongs to that set and c(1)=1. Choose the logarithm with ell(1)=0. Now L_N converges in the same discrete set and is eventually 2pii k for a fixed integer k. Thus c(q_N)=q_N^(2k) for all sufficiently large N. The rational function c(q)-q^(2k) has infinitely many zeros, so c(q)=q^(2k) identically. This proves (2.2).

The assumption of all sufficiently large levels matters here; an arbitrary subsequence need not fix the scalar character.

4. The remaining equation is a finite flow

For each c the vertices are u in Z and the edge u->u+1 carries the flow e_(c,u). Equation (2.4) solves the divergence equation

n_(c,u)=e_(c,u-1)-e_(c,u).                  (4.1)

The vanishing of total charge is exactly the condition for a finite-support flow. Since sigma(1-cQuY)=1-cQ(u+1)Y, equation (4.1) proves the product part of (2.3), and sigma(Yk)/Yk=Q^k gives its scalar part.

If the u support for label c is between u_min and u_max, at most u_max-u_min flow factors are needed. Their total absolute exponent is at most

(u_max-u_min) sum_u |n_(c,u)|.               (4.2)

Together with |k| this bounds the Y degrees of the rational gauge by the fixed input data. This is one way to supply the bounded comparison space required by the whole-period reconstruction theorem. It is a divisor-flow condition, rather than a nilpotence assumption on the entire braid image.

For a rank-one module T e=a e, the new frame e’=f^(-1)e satisfies T e’=e’. The remaining ambiguity in f over C(q,Y) is multiplication by C(q)^*: a rational sigma-invariant in Y has no finite nonzero pole, since its q^2 orbit would be infinite; it is Laurent in Y, and invariance then leaves only its constant term. A specified regular generic anchor fixes this scalar. Root-level central functions of Y^N have not been silently identified with generic difference constants.

An extension to moving algebraic zeros and poles

The fixed labels in (2.1) can be replaced by more general algebraic functions under a finite nonresonance condition. This supplies a second, broader sufficient condition for the comparison line.

Write a rational coefficient, over its algebraic splitting field, as

a(q,Y)=r(q)Y^s
       product_(i=1)^d (1-alpha_i(q)Y)
         /product_(j=1)^e (1-beta_j(q)Y).     (4.3)

Assume every alpha_i,beta_j is holomorphic and nonzero near q=1. They may coincide at q=1; the hypothesis is an unramified choice of the individual branches, not distinctness of all limiting roots. Let C be the set of their distinct values at q=1. Assume

c!=c' in C => c^k!=c'^k for 1<=k<=max(d,e). (4.4)

Thus distinct limiting values have no root-of-unity ratio of these bounded orders. This is a finite condition. It is automatic if all branches have the same limiting value, or if all distinct limiting values are positive real numbers.

Unramified divisor-descent theorem. Under (4.3)–(4.4), the identity C_N(Y)=1 for every sufficiently large N implies s=0, d=e, r(q)=q^(2k_0), and a pairing of the branches such that

alpha_i(q)=q^(2k_i) beta_(pi(i))(q),
                            k_i in Z.       (4.5)

Consequently a=sigma(f)/f with f in C(q,Y)^*. Its Y-degree can be bounded by |k_0|+sum_i |k_i|. In particular no prior bound for root-level gauges is required. The integer shifts are read from the fixed algebraic branches:

k_i=(alpha_i'(1)/alpha_i(1)
               -beta_(pi(i))'(1)/beta_(pi(i))(1))/2. (4.6)

Here the derivatives and the nonresonance condition are in the quantum parameter q. Classical etaleness of shapes over the meridian does not automatically imply these quantum hypotheses.

We first prove an elementary uniqueness lemma. If c_1,…,c_h are distinct nonzero complex numbers and F_i(t) are meromorphic germs at zero, then

sum_i c_i^N F_i(1/N)=0 for all large N       (4.7)

forces every F_i=0. Indeed, among nonzero F_i choose those with largest |c_i|=R, and among these the least Laurent order m. Divide (4.7) by R^N N^(-m). It follows that sum a_i omega_i^N tends to zero, where omega_i=c_i/R are distinct points of the unit circle and at least one leading coefficient a_i is nonzero. Multiplying by omega_j^(-N) and taking Cesaro means gives a_j=0: each other geometric sum has mean zero and the error tends to zero. This contradicts the choice of m. It proves (4.7), including roots-of-unity ratios between the c_i.

To prove the theorem, the full norm identity first gives s=0, r(q_N)^N=1 and equality of the multisets alpha_i(q_N)^N and beta_j(q_N)^N. Hence d=e; the scalar proof in Section 3 gives r(q)=q^(2k_0). Fix the logarithms of alpha_i(q)/alpha_i(1) and beta_j(q)/beta_j(1) with value zero at q=1. For a branch with limiting value c put

A_i(t)=exp(Log(alpha_i(exp(pi*i*t))/c)/t),
B_j(t)=exp(Log(beta_j(exp(pi*i*t))/c)/t).     (4.8)

These are holomorphic units at t=0 because their logarithm numerators vanish there. The k-th power-sum equality is

sum_(c in C) (c^k)^N
   [sum_(alpha_i(1)=c) A_i(1/N)^k
               -sum_(beta_j(1)=c) B_j(1/N)^k]=0.

For 1<=k<=d, (4.4) makes the bases c^k distinct. Lemma (4.7) therefore gives equality of the bracketed holomorphic functions, separately for each c and k. Newton identities, padding each multiset to d elements with zeros, give equal multisets of the A_i and B_j as meromorphic germs. They are units, so the numbers of branches at each c agree. A fixed pairing satisfies A_i(t)=B_(pi(i))(t) identically.

The exponent of their quotient in (4.8) is a holomorphic function whose exponential is one. It is a constant 2pi*i k_i. Thus Log(alpha_i(q)/beta_(pi(i))(q))=2k_i Log q, proving (4.5) as an identity of algebraic branches and giving (4.6).

For k_i>0 the corresponding rational gauge over the splitting field is product_(u=0)(k_i-1)(1-q(2u) beta_(pi(i))(q)Y). For k_i<0 use the inverse product over k_i<=u<0. Their product, times Y^(k_0), gives the stated degree bound.

This gauge descends to C(q,Y). Its finite divisor flow is the unique finite-support solution on each infinite q-shift orbit. Uniqueness makes that divisor invariant under the Galois group of the splitting field. The product part has value one at Y=0, so its normalization is Galois invariant as well. Its coefficients therefore lie in C(q); Y^(k_0) is already rational. This completes the proof without a general q-curvature theorem.

5. Tests which prevent incorrect descent

If only even N are used, the scalar a=-1 has C_N=1 but has no nonzero rational generic gauge for sigma(Y)=q^2Y. If f(q^2Y)/f(Y)=-1, a finite divisor is shift-invariant and hence f is a Laurent monomial; its ratio is q^(2k), a contradiction.

Likewise the rational coefficient

a(q,Y)=(1-q^2 Y^2)/(1-Y^2)                  (5.1)

has C_N=1 at every odd N, but at even N its transport is

C_N=((1+U)/(1-U))^2.                        (5.2)

Indeed the exponent 2 orbit has size N/gcd(2,N). Thus odd-level agreement alone does not justify generic descent. Formula (5.2) is also a concrete reason to retain the peripheral factors 1+U before evaluating U=1.

The half-step factor (1-qY)/(1-Y) has transport (1+U)/(1-U) at every actual q_N: q_N^N=-1, whereas Q_N^N=1. It is not in the class (2.1). Its two norm characters must be retained, just as in the actual bridge-source calculation.

There is a stronger warning for the exact chosen half-root family. The rational coefficient

a_*(q,Y)=(1-Y)(1-qY)/[(1+Y)(1+qY)]          (5.3)

has C_N=1 for every N at q_N=exp(pi*i/N), with no parity exception. Its numerator norm is (1-U)(1+U), while its denominator norm is

(1-(-1)^N U)(1-(-1)^(N+1) U),

the same product. Nevertheless a_* is not sigma(f)/f for any nonzero rational f(q,Y). Normalize a hypothetical f by its q-1 valuation. At q=1 it then has a finite nonzero value in C(Y). Since sigma(Y)=q^2Y tends to Y, sigma(f)/f specializes to one. But a_*(1,Y)=((1-Y)/(1+Y))^2 is not one.

This does not contradict either descent theorem here. The roots in (5.3) include q-shifts outside (2.1), and its limiting labels 1,-1 violate (4.4). Nor is it evidence against a correctly parameterized general q-curvature theorem. The selected q_N family contains only even orders of q. At q=-q_N with N odd, which has odd order N, the same cyclic transport is ((1-U)/(1+U))^2 and detects the obstruction.

Thus all selected root values alone still do not determine a generic comparison. One must supply the additional parameter symmetry or cyclotomic places, a proved divisor condition, or the bounded quantum-normal comparison required by the whole- period reconstruction theorem. The even parameter Q=q^2 and the retained half-root sign must be distinguished explicitly. In particular finite-lift evenness is not an assumed symmetry of an unknown minimal generic operator.

There is a constructive way to choose the smaller parameter for the actual reduced state. The new argument in the even-meridian note applies the finite-sum annihilator proof directly to its statewise even closed lift, with base Q and central variable Q^N. It supplies a specified nonzero actual operator over Q(Q,Y), with shift Y->QY. This is an existence result, not the asserted q-sign stability of the previous half-root minimal operator. The full ADO Tate/sign line remains separate and unchanged. An unknown comparison still must be placed over the correctly marked field and lattice.

The gauge (2.4) can have a zero or pole at the ideal meridian. Its generic existence does not authorize deleting that divisor or changing the marked normal functional. It must be transported in the specified integral lattice. Once a comparison is a unit of the correct marked line and is fixed algebraic across levels, the existing central normalization theorem controls its norm. This note does not infer that unit condition from (2.3).

6. What this contributes to the knot program

This supplies uniform Hilbert-90 descent theorems for the stated classes of rank-one coefficients. They start from all-level cyclic transport, derive a bounded global gauge, and give its explicit divisor flow. The proof is independent of knot-by-knot examples and of the published q-curvature proofs.

To use it for the desired comparison, one still has to construct that comparison line from the actual pointed quantum A-object, prove its split divisor presentation or an appropriate extension, and match its cyclic norm characters with all peripheral factors. Rivin’s algebraic geometric place is available but does not alone supply these quantum hypotheses. The regulator connection and selected source must still be retained. No volume theorem follows just from the vanishing of a rational norm-one comparison class.
