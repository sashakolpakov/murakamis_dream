Local propagation of an actual charged source by a signed heat step

Private written analysis, 16 November 2026.

The entire charged-source heat identity gives a local asymptotic transport theorem with an estimated exterior integral. A sufficiently small real power step preserves a known nonzero geometric expansion on a smaller complex charge domain. This uses the whole initial source, including its physical first normals and its global exponential growth, rather than an assumed coefficient of a formal saddle.

The domain loss is essential. The output is the characteristic image of an eroded initial domain. Iteration transports the same initial set of classical angle labels; it does not supply a fresh complex neighborhood at every step. No continuation to arbitrary short fillings or to charge one is claimed.

3. The small-charge initializer has a complex neighborhood

The frozen small-charge source theorem is stated on the physical charge lattice in the compact real box [1/4000,1/2000]I.(27) [1/4000,1/2000]^I . \qquad\text{(27)} For its specified fixed graph, fixed signed powers and complete outside angles, its proof also gives a uniform holomorphic initializer for the entire coupon family (3) on a sufficiently small fixed complex neighborhood of (27).

This assertion does not follow merely from agreement on increasingly fine lattices. The exact even-coupon trace identity continues to complex χ\chi by the full physical jets, as proved in the dominant-saddle source note, Section 9. In the small-charge proof retain its physical-row decomposition and its actual mixed horizontal/vertical contours. At a fixed real charge in (27), all noncentral terms have uniform strict gaps, and every retained angular contour is bounded. Moving the charges by at most ε\varepsilon changes each coupon exponential bound by at most eC1Nεe^{C_1N\varepsilon}, with a fixed constant for this finite graph. The required normal derivatives cost polynomial factors. The same bound applies to removed physical rows; their angles lie in [−π,π][-\pi,\pi]. Explicitly, |cos⁡(N(1−χ)τ)|≤eπN|ℑχ|,|∂τcos⁡(N(1−χ)τ)|≤N|1−χ|eπN|ℑχ|. |\cos(N(1-\chi)\tau)|\le e^{\pi N|\Im\chi|}, \qquad |\partial_\tau\cos(N(1-\chi)\tau)| \le N|1-\chi|e^{\pi N|\Im\chi|}. The first spectral normals add only the already retained polynomial reciprocal of sin⁡τ\sin\tau; the odd simple node remains a value term.

Choose ε>0\varepsilon>0 below the finitely many gap margins divided by the resulting constants. The mixed-current and removed-row gaps then remain strict relative to the continued selected action. The central saddle moves holomorphically and stays in the same pole-free thin rectangle. A small contour displacement through it is legitimate there, with the connecting faces still in the strict-gap region. The nonzero sine factors, edge amplitudes and Gaussian determinant remain nonzero after one further shrinkage. The exact companion and alias estimates also persist because the real box is separated from both charge endpoints and its heights are uniformly positive.

Compactness of (27) supplies one fixed complex neighborhood. The original proof’s Taylor expansion on these contours gives uniform holomorphic errors to every fixed order there, through both level parities. Thus this is an actual initializer for Theorem 1. Its radius may depend on the fixed graph and powers. No lower bound independent of that data or extension to charge one is asserted.

The same local conclusion holds uniformly on a fixed bounded real-power box after using the canonical real powers. The physical values and normals have the uniform bounds used in (6); the small-charge fixed-point denominators obey |nv+iCv|≥nv2+dv2−dv/30000|n_v+iC_v|\ge\sqrt{n_v^2+d_v^2}-d_v/30000 for real nvn_v as well. The signed vertical-side estimates use only nvsgn⁡(nv)=|nv|n_v\operatorname{sgn}(n_v)=|n_v|, not integrality. One may retain either side at zero power. The full real-power trace identity keeps its endpoint currents. These observations give the same compactness argument without assigning noninteger powers an additional topological meaning.

4. Domain accounting under iteration

The local theorem is an effective propagation result, but it has two explicit restrictions.

First, suppose a regular phase has initial derivative t0(ζ)=iS0′(ζ)t_0(\zeta)=iS_0'(\zeta). The classical heat characteristic is χs=Φs(ζ)=ζ+s2πt0(ζ).(28) \chi_s=\Phi_s(\zeta) =\zeta+\frac{s}{2\pi}t_0(\zeta). \qquad\text{(28)} Indeed differentiating (16) gives Sδ,χ(Φδ(ζ))=S′(ζ)S_{\delta,\chi}(\Phi_\delta(\zeta))=S'(\zeta). Thus the angle is constant along the characteristic. The maps for successive steps compose to (28) with the total power change. In several coordinates the corresponding formula is 𝝌𝒏=𝜻+12πdiag⁡(𝒏−𝒃)𝒕0(𝜻).(29) \boldsymbol\chi_{\mathbf n} =\boldsymbol\zeta+ \frac1{2\pi}\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf n-\mathbf b) \mathbf t_0(\boldsymbol\zeta). \qquad\text{(29)} The coordinate flows commute wherever these charts are regular.

Each use of Theorem 1 requires the input saddle to lie inside the previously certified complex domain with a reserved margin. Consequently this procedure transports a subset of the initial angle labels. Recentring overlapping output discs does not add initial labels that were not already certified. This statement concerns the present local procedure, not an impossibility theorem for analytic continuation by other source estimates.

Second, the explicit envelope recipe (14) spends a quadratic time budget. For a one-coordinate nested-disc certificate, suppose the characteristic charts extend injectively to neighborhoods of the closed initial-label discs and their inverses have a common Lipschitz bound K≥1K\ge1. If these discs have radii RjR_j, reserve a charge margin ρj=(Rj−Rj+1)/K\rho_j=(R_j-R_{j+1})/K. It lies inside the preceding charge image: the inverse Lipschitz bound gives that lower bound on the distance to its boundary. With a common chosen E*E_*, the sufficient steps in (14) obey ∑j|δj|≤π4E*∑jρj2≤π4E*K2(R0−Rfinal)2.(30) \sum_j|\delta_j| \le\frac{\pi}{4E_*}\sum_j\rho_j^2 \le\frac{\pi}{4E_*K^2}(R_0-R_{\mathrm{ final}})^2 . \qquad\text{(30)} This is the limitation of that stated conservative certificate, not a necessary inequality for the actual source or for all possible contour estimates. Subdivision into more steps does not increase its guaranteed total time. If one has sharper exterior information than (10), a better domain estimate may be possible. Neither positivity of a local Hessian nor a fixed global envelope alone supplies that sharper information.

For a fixed unit-charge positive point t*=s−iHt^*=s-iH, H>0H>0, the initial charge at any real base power bb is yb=1−(n−b)t*2π.(31) y_b=1-\frac{(n-b)t^*}{2\pi}. \qquad\text{(31)} Successive real-power steps give the same initial label (31). It is real only if b=nb=n, in which case it is one. For a real initializer interval [α,β][\alpha,\beta], β<1\beta<1, orthogonal projection onto this line gives exactly infb∈ℝx∈[α,β]|yb−x|=(1−β)H|t*|>0.(32) \inf_{\substack{b\in\mathbb R\\x\in[\alpha,\beta]}}|y_b-x| =(1-\beta)\frac{H}{|t^*|}>0 . \qquad\text{(32)} The complex neighborhood of Section 3 must actually contain the required label; its existence alone does not establish this. Formula (32) does not rule out containment in a sufficiently large proved neighborhood, nor does it rule out a different continuation.

The new conclusion is therefore local and quantitative: a known actual complex initializer admits a nonzero signed heat step with an explicitly bounded whole-source exterior. Establishing enough initial-label coverage or replenishing the complex domain to reach every arbitrary short partial filling remains a separate problem.
