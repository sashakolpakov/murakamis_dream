The finite-exception problem: current research status

25 November 2026. The finite-exception theorem is open. This note records the current question and the general reductions being pursued. The papers contain the established volume results; the research reductions below do not enlarge their hypotheses.

The established result and the stronger question

Fix one finite strict Conway construction, its crossing circles and a half-twist pattern whose strand connections give a knot. Write p=(p1,…,pb)∈ℤbp=(p_1,\ldots,p_b)\in\mathbb Z^b for its auxiliary integral filling coefficients. The Conway theorem gives a threshold P0P_0 such that the volume identity holds whenever every |pi|>P0|p_i|>P_0. All these coefficients are fixed before the quantum level NN tends to infinity. This covers density one in the assignment lattice; its complement still contains infinitely many assignments.

The stronger target is:

After fixing any short partial filling, all sufficiently long fillings of the remaining coordinates that produce hyperbolic knots satisfy the volume identity, with a threshold allowed to depend on the fixed data.

The finite-branching argument explains how the required statements for successive partial fillings would imply finitely many exceptional assignments for one template. The definitions specify the tangle operations, primitive slopes, component test and quantifiers.

Exact tangle reductions can have infinite fibers: infinitely many assignments can describe one unresolved core knot. The affine-fiber theorem decides this on each recorded reduction branch. The padding argument in the finite-branching note shows why the general assignment statement already entails a volume theorem for every hyperbolic core allowed by the construction. Redundant assignments cannot simply be discarded.

What orthogonality changes

For a finite synthesis map BB, an expanded state BcBc has energy c*B*Bcc^*B^*Bc. The Gram matrix B*BB^*B retains exact linear relations; replacing it by the identity can create spurious energy. The conceptual chapter and its formal statements give this algebra and its limitations. A norm estimate alone gives no lower bound for a specified closing functional.

The normalized finite-source construction encodes the actual packet equations and scalar closing in one residual energy. Its proof retains the normalization budget that connects this energy to the original invariant. The linear-elimination theorem then removes every linearly entering packet exactly.

The remaining theorem

The task is to evaluate the retained finite source at the geometric exponential rate, including its normalization budget, for arbitrary fixed short partial fillings. A successful argument must control the original closing observation and possible cancellation, beyond the existence of a local geometric critical point or a positive ambient norm.
