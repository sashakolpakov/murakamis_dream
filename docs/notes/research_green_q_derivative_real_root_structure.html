A real-root preserving q-derivative on the original protected-Green degree range

31 October 2026. Internally reviewed analytical research theorem. The theorem below gives new positive structure for the FULL spectator polynomial. It does not claim that truncation preserves roots, that the full polynomial is integrable against the Green moment law, or that the original signed coefficient is bounded by ΦD\Phi_D.

1. The exact finite operator

Put N≥3N\ge3, θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N, q=eiθq=e^{i\theta}, sj=2sin⁡(jθ)s_j=2\sin(j\theta), and Sa=∏j=1asjS_a=\prod_{j=1}^a s_j. Let 0≤a<N0\le a<N and D=N+a−1D=N+a-1. On polynomials define

E=zddz,∂qP(z)=P(qz)−P(q−1z)iz,∂qzk=skzk−1. \begin{gathered} E=z\frac{d}{dz},\qquad \partial_qP(z)=\frac{P(qz)-P(q^{-1}z)}{iz},\\ \partial_qz^k=s_kz^{k-1}. \end{gathered}\tag{1}

The removable value at z=0 is understood polynomially. Thus

∂qaP(z)=∑k≥apk(∏j=0a−1sk−j)zk−a. \partial_q^aP(z) =\sum_{k\ge a}p_k\left(\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}s_{k-j}\right)z^{k-a}. \tag{2}

Terms with k<a are zero. Terms with N<=k<=N+a-1 are also EXACTLY zero, since their product contains s_N. No limiting sine quotient is used. For a<=k<=N-1 all factors in (2) are strictly positive.

Theorem. If PP is a real polynomial of degree at most DD with nonnegative coefficients and all its zeros nonpositive real, then ∂qaP\partial_q^aP is either zero or has nonnegative coefficients and all its zeros nonpositive real. Its degree is at most N−a−1N-a-1.

This theorem concerns the compound a-th derivative. The intermediate single q-derivatives need not preserve nonpositive roots once the degree exceeds N. A zero output is possible, for example P=z^D when a>0.

2. Elementary proof by Euler’s sine product

We first record two ordinary operations on the cone of nonnegative polynomials with nonpositive real zeros.

  • Ordinary differentiation, multiplication by z, and fixed-degree reversal Rev⁡DP(z)=zDP(1/z)\operatorname{Rev}_DP(z)=z^DP(1/z) preserve this cone, or give zero. Reversal is allowed when degree P<D, in which case it adds zeros at 0.
  • For r<0, E-r preserves this cone. For r>D, r-E also preserves it on degree-at-most-D polynomials.

Here is a self-contained verification of the second assertion. For c>0 and a polynomial P whose d zeros are distinct and strictly negative, the derivative of t^c P(-t) has at least one positive zero between 0 and its first positive zero, and between each consecutive pair of its positive zeros. Its nontrivial polynomial factor is c P(-t)+t d/dt P(-t), of degree d. Thus cP+EP has all its d roots negative and has nonnegative coefficients. Repeated or zero roots follow by perturbing the roots and taking coefficient limits. Finally

Rev⁡D(r−E)P=(r−D+E)Rev⁡DP. \operatorname{Rev}_D(r-E)P =(r-D+E)\operatorname{Rev}_DP.\tag{3}

which proves the assertion for r>D. This proof retains all polynomial degrees; there is no assumption about a quantum inner product.

The bottom and top multiplier blocks are respectively

Ba(E)=∏j=0a−1(E−j)=za∂za,Ta(E)=∏j=0a−1(N+j−E)=Rev⁡DBa(E)Rev⁡D. \begin{aligned} B_a(E)&=\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(E-j)=z^a\partial_z^a,\\ T_a(E)&=\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(N+j-E) =\operatorname{Rev}_D B_a(E)\operatorname{Rev}_D. \end{aligned}\tag{4}

Each WHOLE block preserves the cone. Individual factors of T_a or B_a whose zero lies inside [0,D] are not being asserted to do so. The two blocks commute, being polynomials in E.

For k in the finite set {0,…,D}, Euler’s sine product gives

∏j=0a−1sk−j=(2πN2)a∏j=0a−1(k−j)(N+j−k)∏j=0a−1(1+k−jN)⋅∏n=2∞∏j=0a−1(1−k−jnN)(1+k−jnN). \begin{aligned} \prod_{j=0}^{a-1}s_{k-j} &=\left(\frac{2\pi}{N^2}\right)^a \prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(k-j)(N+j-k) \prod_{j=0}^{a-1}\left(1+\frac{k-j}{N}\right)\\ &\quad\cdot \prod_{n=2}^{\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{a-1} \left(1-\frac{k-j}{nN}\right) \left(1+\frac{k-j}{nN}\right). \end{aligned}\tag{5}

The identities at the zero coefficients hold by the same entire-function product, without division by those zero factors. All remaining linear factors have their roots OUTSIDE [0,D]: the n=1 root is j-N<0; for n>=2 the roots are j-nN<0 and j+nN>D. The last strict inequality uses a<N. Thus each finite product of the remaining factors, applied as a polynomial in E after the two blocks in (4), preserves the cone. Every coefficient multiplier in its nonzero range is positive.

The paired infinite product converges for each k, hence uniformly on the finite coefficient set. The limit polynomial is therefore in the same closed cone, or zero. For completeness, its closedness follows by polynomial convergence and Hurwitz on each compact subset off the nonpositive real axis; degree loss can only send roots to infinity. Formula (5) says the limiting polynomial is exactly

za∂qaP(z). z^a\partial_q^aP(z).\tag{6}

Removing its forced z^a proves the theorem. The support and degree claims follow directly from (2). For a=0 the assertion is the identity operator and the empty products are one.

No finite numerical sample, scalar orthogonal-polynomial identification, or external multiplier-sequence theorem is needed in this proof.

3. The original odd endpoint spectator really satisfies the hypotheses

Consider an ORIGINAL simple marked source with R ports, R ordinary face variables and two protected variables. Let a selected Green coupon have ordered faces (x,b,c,y), with y protected and x,b,c distinct ordinary faces. Assume every other coupon is its actual CROSS endpoint at sigma=-2. We do not change any source diagram, matching, mark, or face demand.

At odd N=2k+1, d=N-1 and M=k-1. The exact endpoint coupon is

Pe,CROSS(−2)=N2Dk(xe,be,ye)Dk(xe,ce,ye),Dk(x,b,y)=det⁡[bI+x2H−+y2H+]. \begin{aligned} P_{e,\mathrm{CROSS}}(-2) &=N^2D_k(x_e,b_e,y_e)D_k(x_e,c_e,y_e),\\ D_k(x,b,y)&=\det\left[bI+\frac{x}{2}H_-+\frac{y}{2}H_+\right]. \end{aligned}\tag{7}

after removal of its fixed common raw unit sign. The physical matrices H_-,H_+ are positive-definite Hermitian. For variables with positive imaginary parts, the imaginary part of the matrix in (7) is positive definite. Its determinant is nonzero, so each factor is real stable. It has nonnegative coefficients by its positive mixed-minor expansion.

Products and identification of shared variables preserve real stability. Derivatives and specialization of variables to a real boundary value preserve it or give zero; these follow by the one-variable Gauss–Lucas argument, approximation from the upper half-plane and Hurwitz. In particular extraction of any fixed nonnegative power, performed as a derivative followed by specialization at 0, is allowed. All protected variables are specialized to0 before extraction.

Let the exact residual spectator be

P(x,b,c)=[∏fordinaryf∉{x,b,c}Lfd]∏e≠GreenPe,CROSS(−2;L). P(x,b,c)= \left[\prod_{\substack{f\ \mathrm{ordinary}\\f\notin\{x,b,c\}}}L_f^d\right] \prod_{e\ne\mathrm{Green}}P_{e,\mathrm{CROSS}}(-2;L). \tag{8}

It is zero or a nonnegative real-stable polynomial, homogeneous of degree 2d: (R-1)d-(R-3)d=2d. Its full coefficients retain every shared ordinary target already extracted in (8). There is no independent optimization of different occurrences of a face.

At the original lower node a<=M the input determinant has expansion

Da(x,b,0)=∑v=0ada,vxa−vbv,da,v≥0. D_a(x,b,0)=\sum_{v=0}^a d_{a,v}x^{a-v}b^v, \qquad d_{a,v}\ge0.\tag{9}

For each v define the actual binary residual

Pv(x,c)=[bd−v]P(x,b,c). P_v(x,c)=[b^{d-v}]P(x,b,c).\tag{10}

It has degree d+v=N+v-1, not degree d in general. It is zero or nonnegative real stable. Consequently P_v(1,z) has only nonpositive real roots, including possible zero roots; this follows from real coefficients, upper-half-plane nonvanishing, and coefficient positivity. Since v≤av\le a, its degree is at most N+a−1N+a-1, exactly the range of the theorem. Thus each FULL polynomial

Qv(z)=∂qaPv(1,z). Q_v(z)=\partial_q^aP_v(1,z).\tag{11}

is zero or nonnegative and negative-rooted, with degree at most N−a−1N-a-1.

There is a stronger combined statement: include the ACTUAL input determinant before extracting the b target. Define

Fa(x,c)=[bd]Da(x,b,0)P(x,b,c)=∑v=0ada,vxa−vPv(x,c). \begin{aligned} F_a(x,c)&=[b^d]D_a(x,b,0)P(x,b,c)\\ &=\sum_{v=0}^a d_{a,v}x^{a-v}P_v(x,c). \end{aligned}\tag{11a}

Both factors before this extraction are real stable, so F_a is zero or a nonnegative real-stable binary polynomial. Its degree is exactly d+a=N+a-1 when it is nonzero. Therefore the SINGLE full polynomial

Qa(z)=∂qaFa(1,z). Q_a(z)=\partial_q^aF_a(1,z).\tag{11b}

is zero or negative-rooted with nonnegative coefficients. This combines all input b-loads with their original weights; it is not an unsupported assertion that an arbitrary positive sum of stable polynomials stays stable. The multiplication and coefficient extraction in (11a) prove the required stability directly.

This is a genuine original-source statement for arbitrary valence of the selected Green faces: no two-port internal-demand reduction is used. It does require the stated CROSS endpoint exterior. An unextracted fan can have larger c degree; adding arbitrary signed exterior coupons need not preserve real stability. No assertion about that broader maximal-component situation follows from (11).

The same reasoning works whenever an actual protected-source extraction has the stated degree bound and stable exterior. If b is also protected, only v=0 remains and the direct two-variable degree count gives d; this optional specialization adds no new moment or spectral claim.

4. Exact Green pairing and the two surviving cutoff issues

For the source variables and node above, keep the original Green law X=X_(N,a), m=M-a, and the original total cutoff in the parent degree c. Its exact moments are

𝔼Xr=Ar=∏i=1rsa+is2a+i+1,r∈{0,…,m},𝔼Xt<∞⇔−a−1<t<N−2a−1. \begin{gathered} \mathbb E X^r=A_r= \prod_{i=1}^r\frac{s_{a+i}}{s_{2a+i+1}}, \qquad r\in\{0,\ldots,m\},\\ \mathbb E X^t<\infty \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -a-1<t<N-2a-1. \end{gathered}\tag{12}

The real interval in (12) is the OPEN Mellin domain; integer moments used by the original source have 0<=r<=m. In the protected outer case, the original sine-binomial identity gives

Hr(Wa(x,0))=Ba(r)xr,Ba(r)=Sa+rSaSr. H_r(W_a(x,0))=B_a(r)x^r,\qquad B_a(r)=\frac{S_{a+r}}{S_aS_r}.

Here Pv,≤MP_{v,\le M} truncates Pv(1,z)P_v(1,z) in zz; later Fa,≤MF_{a,\le M} likewise truncates Fa(1,z)F_a(1,z). The normalized original pairing obeys exactly

ℒa(Pv)=∑r=0m(−1)rArBa(r)[za+r]Pv(1,z)=1Sa𝔼[∂qa(Pv,≤M)(−X)]. \begin{aligned} \mathcal L_a(P_v) &=\sum_{r=0}^m(-1)^rA_rB_a(r)[z^{a+r}]P_v(1,z)\\ &=\frac1{S_a}\mathbb E\!\left[ \partial_q^a(P_{v,\le M})(-X)\right]. \end{aligned}\tag{13}

The b and x degree targets have not disappeared: after the term x(a-v)bv in (9), the b target is exactly (10); homogeneity of P_v makes every term in (13) have total x degree d. Thus, with all original ordinary demands imposed, the normalized nodal Green/CROSS coefficient is

ℬa,N=(−1)mγN,a∑v=0ada,vℒa(Pv),γN,a=Ns2a+1sm+1,Nodd. \begin{gathered} \mathcal B_{a,N}=(-1)^m\gamma_{N,a} \sum_{v=0}^a d_{a,v}\mathcal L_a(P_v),\\ \gamma_{N,a}=\frac{N}{s_{2a+1}s_{m+1}}, \qquad N\ \mathrm{odd}. \end{gathered}\tag{14}

Equivalently, using the single actual stable polynomial (11a),

ℬa,N=(−1)mγN,aSa𝔼[∂qa(Fa,≤M)(−X)]. \mathcal B_{a,N} =\frac{(-1)^m\gamma_{N,a}}{S_a} \mathbb E\!\left[\partial_q^a(F_{a,\le M})(-X)\right]. \tag{14a}

All input determinant coefficients have been included in F_a. The factor 1/S_a is exact and can have exponential size; it is not absorbed into the polynomially bounded gamma.

This is the already fixed original nodal block normalization RaDa,m=γN,aDaR_aD_{a,m}=\gamma_{N,a}D_a. The common raw source sign and the UNUSED Green scalar ζN(u)\zeta_N(u), present when the nodal block is interpolated back into the original numerator, remain outside this displayed block. Equation (14) does not evaluate or cancel that scalar at a sampled zero.

Although Q_v in (11) is negative-rooted, the polynomial in (13) is its initial degree segment:

∂qa(Pv,≤M)(z)=Qv(z)−∑s=M+1N−1([zs]Pv(1,z))SsSs−azs−a. \partial_q^a(P_{v,\le M})(z) =Q_v(z)-\sum_{s=M+1}^{N-1} \bigl([z^s]P_v(1,z)\bigr) \frac{S_s}{S_{s-a}}z^{s-a}. \tag{15}

Terms absent from the actual P_v are zero. Thus the upper band N<=s<=N+a-1 vanishes for algebraic reasons, but the ACTUAL cutoff current M<s<N remains. Truncation can destroy negative-rootedness; no root claim is made for the left side of (15).

Nor can (15) be integrated termwise after dropping the cutoff. Its full degree can be N-a-1, whereas the moment threshold in (12) is N-2a-1. Some currents therefore need unavailable moments, even though every term in the original truncated (13) is integrable. For a=0 the top degree N-1 is itself at the excluded threshold. The identity is a polynomial identity at finite X, not a license for subtracting divergent expectations.

The same exact cutoff identity and moment warning apply to the combined F_a,Q_a in (11a)–(14a); this is the version retaining the entire input polynomial before any absolute value.

5. A genuine full-polynomial phase identity, with its exact limitation

If the combined QaQ_a is nonzero, write Qa(z)=Kzℓ∏j(z+λj)Q_a(z)=Kz^\ell\prod_j(z+\lambda_j), where λj>0\lambda_j>0 and K>0K>0. For any r>0r>0 put pj=r/(r+λj)p_j=r/(r+\lambda_j). Then its actual phase ratio is exactly

|Qa(reiϕ)|Qa(r)=∏j[1−4pj(1−pj)sin⁡2(ϕ/2)]1/2≤exp⁡[−2sin⁡2(ϕ/2)∑jpj(1−pj)]. \begin{aligned} \frac{|Q_a(re^{i\phi})|}{Q_a(r)} &=\prod_j\left[1-4p_j(1-p_j)\sin^2(\phi/2)\right]^{1/2}\\ &\le \exp\left[-2\sin^2(\phi/2)\sum_jp_j(1-p_j)\right]. \end{aligned}\tag{16}

The zero-root monomial has modulus one in this ratio. The variance is also exactly

(r∂∂r)2log⁡Qa(r)=∑jpj(1−pj). \left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^2\log Q_a(r) =\sum_jp_j(1-p_j).\tag{17}

In particular the negative-axis value admits a genuine factor-by-factor cancellation formula, before any absolute value is taken on its coefficients. This uses the SAME actual fully extracted polynomial, not an independently chosen positive majorant.

Equations (15) and (12) are the precise remaining obstacles to using (16) inside the original Green expectation. In addition, no uniform order-N lower bound on (17) has been proved from the original exterior constraints. Thus the theorem establishes a general actual-source positive structure, but neither an absolute ΦD\Phi_D bound nor a signed Green contraction.
