A local geometric period chart for the original knot state

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

For every hyperbolic knot and every fixed finite braid presenting it, the original reduced state has an exact finite residue-period presentation whose integrand has a nonzero local amplitude at a regular geometric principal critical point. Its corrected principal action has the hyperbolic volume. The representation uses the literal factorial crossing coefficients, rather than their additional coherent occupation transforms.

This is a local statement about an exact presentation. It does not show that its original residue cycle has a nonzero component at that critical point. The centralizer direction, other source boundaries and equal-rate aliases are still present.

The complete proof directs are A, the literal slit-chart amplitudes; F, the common framing and complex cut; and V, the actual critical-action volume calibration. Their pins appear below. The ordinary binomial and residue-kernel steps are proved here.

1. The exact lattice and its coordinates

Fix a braid of a hyperbolic knot, with cc signed crossings. Put N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, h=2πi/Nh=2\pi i/N, and c0=2πic_0=2\pi i. Keep the original crossing scalars, tail pivots and prescribed zero framing.

View its closed diagram as the directed crossing graph. It has 2c2c wire arcs and cc vertices. Integer occupation on its wires satisfies input sum equals output sum at each crossing. The graph is connected and Eulerian. Choose a spanning tree avoiding the cut edge, which is not a bridge. The m=c+1m=c+1 chord-wire occupations k=(k1,…,km)(1) k=(k_1,\ldots,k_m) \qquad\text{(1)} are integral coordinates for its entire circulation lattice. Indeed integer values on the chords determine the tree values uniquely by leaf elimination of the conservation equations. No finite-index sublattice or determinant factor is introduced. One of the chords is the cut edge.

At each positive crossing the transfer is n=cout−binn=c_{\mathrm{ out}}-b_{\mathrm{ in}}; at a negative crossing it is n=bin−coutn=b_{\mathrm{ in}}-c_{\mathrm{ out}}. Thus all wire and transfer indices are integral linear forms in (1). Let SN⊂ℤmS_N\subset\mathbb Z^m be the original finite support: every wire index is between 00 and dd, with the actual signed crossing inequalities on nn. Nothing extends this support to a cube of independent occupations.

Let WN(k)W_N(k) be the original summand for the unnormalized diagonal partial trace CN(M)aaC_N(M)_{aa}, before the cut average. A trace-one diagonal probe therefore inserts its weight directly into WNW_N. If one instead starts with the old 1/N1/N cut-averaged summand, the insertion is NN times that weight. These are the same normalization; an extra 1/N1/N is not retained.

2. A frame in which every local length is admissible

F supplies a regular complete geometric frame, arbitrarily near the midpoint frame, for which every required wire and transfer clock has modulus different from one. It repairs the wire logarithms by an integer spanning-tree flow while preserving the chosen cut logarithm. Choose its cut parameter θ=ζcutc0,θnear 1/2,ℑθ≠0.(2) \theta=\frac{\zeta_{\mathrm{ cut}}}{c_0},\qquad \theta\ \text{near }1/2,\quad \Im\theta\ne0. \qquad\text{(2)} Use F’s exact complex-binomial cut βθ,a=(da)θa(1−θ)d−a. \beta_{\theta,a}=\binom da\theta^a(1-\theta)^{d-a}. Its weights sum to one. The actual scalar endomorphism identity therefore gives, exactly at every level, KN=∑k∈SNβθ,a(k)WN(k)(3) K_N=\sum_{k\in S_N}\beta_{\theta,a(k)}W_N(k) \qquad\text{(3)} in the original reduced zero-framed convention. All original external factors in that convention remain in WNW_N. The marked-cut difference is F’s explicit Ward commutator; (3) does not assert equality of arbitrary marked families.

Set xj=ζj/c0x_j=\zeta_j/c_0 for the chosen chord logarithms. At the framed geometric point x*x_*, every chord xjx_j is nonreal: its wire clock is off the unit circle. Every affine crossing factorial length is also nonreal, including both numerator and denominator lengths in A. There is consequently a fixed small polydisc UU about x*x_* on which

  • every such length lies in a common compact subset of 𝒟=ℂ\((−∞,0]∪[1,∞))\mathcal D=\mathbb C\setminus((-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty));
  • each chord imaginary part has a fixed nonzero sign and absolute value at least some δ>0\delta>0;
  • the cut coordinate stays near θ\theta, with its real part strictly between zero and one.

The frame, θ,U,δ\theta,U,\delta are fixed independently of NN. They may depend on the knot and diagram. The finite offsets involving d=N−1d=N-1 remain in the literal formulas.

Equation (6) concerns the same interpolated lattice summand whose supported values occur in (3). It is not a theorem about the additional coherent crossing sums. A supplies the uniform error and every fixed holomorphic derivative on smaller compacts.

4. An exact period with a locally nonzero residue-kernel amplitude

For each NN, let ΓN\Gamma_N be the oriented sum of small product circles about the points of SNS_N. Choose them small enough to enclose no other integer or factorial poles. The interpolant is holomorphic at these supported points: every physical factorial length is between zero and N−1N-1, and common endpoint ratios mean the cancelled finite product. For an integer vector n∈ℤmn\in\mathbb Z^m, define ΩN,n(z)=1(2πi)me−c0n⋅zIN(z)∏j=1mπcot⁡(πzj)dzj.(7) \Omega_{N,n}(z)=\frac1{(2\pi i)^m} e^{-c_0n\cdot z}I_N(z) \prod_{j=1}^m\pi\cot(\pi z_j)\,dz_j . \qquad\text{(7)} At an integer state the new character is one, and each cotangent factor has residue one. Multivariable residues therefore prove the exact identity ∫ΓNΩN,n=KN.(8) \int_{\Gamma_N}\Omega_{N,n}=K_N. \qquad\text{(8)} All support and source data are the original ones. The character is part of a specified alternate interpolant, not an assertion that the two meromorphic forms agree off the integer lattice.

The cotangent factors also have a nonzero uniform local amplitude in the chart UU. The elementary exponential formula for cotangent gives πcot⁡(πNxj)=−iπsgn⁡(ℑxj)+O(e−2πδN).(9) \pi\cot(\pi N x_j)= -i\pi\,\operatorname{sgn}(\Im x_j) +O(e^{-2\pi\delta N}). \qquad\text{(9)} This is uniform with fixed derivatives after shrinking UU; polynomial differentiation factors are absorbed by a slightly smaller exponential constant. Thus the actual residue kernel, not just the factorial summand, is a holomorphic unit there for all sufficiently large NN. Scaling z=Nxz=Nx in (7) gives a local density ΩN,n(Nx)=Nm−(c+1)/2ã(x)eN(ϕθ(x)−c0n⋅x)(1+O(N−1))dx1⋯dxm,ã(x)≠0.(10) \Omega_{N,n}(Nx)= N^{m-(c+1)/2}\,\widetilde a(x) e^{N(\phi_\theta(x)-c_0n\cdot x)} (1+O(N^{-1}))\,dx_1\cdots dx_m , \qquad \widetilde a(x)\ne0. \qquad\text{(10)} The constant factors in (7), (9) are included in ã\widetilde a. No pole or zero of the cotangent kernel has been suppressed near this point.

5. Critical action and volume

Write the actual principal action as Sθ(ζ)=S0(ζ)+gθ(u),ϕθ(x)=Sθ(c0x)/c0,gθ(c0z)=c0bθ(z).(11) S_\theta(\zeta)=S_0(\zeta)+g_\theta(u),\qquad \phi_\theta(x)=S_\theta(c_0x)/c_0,\qquad g_\theta(c_0z)=c_0 b_\theta(z). \qquad\text{(11)} Here S0S_0 includes all original crossing, pivot and framing terms. A’s reversed factorial products can express some dilogarithms with reciprocal arguments compared with V’s five-shape notation. The inversion identity changes their Gaussian part by a real quadratic polynomial, an imaginary linear polynomial and a real constant; D(z−1)=−D(z)D(z^{-1})=-D(z) gives the same crossing regulator. Thus the bare-state logarithmic identity used below has the same normalization for this literal factorial phase. F proves that the geometric point is a regular exponentiated principal critical point, with u=c0θu=c_0\theta. On compatible logarithms let ∂ζjSθ(ζ*)=c0nj,nj∈ℤ.(12) \partial_{\zeta_j}S_\theta(\zeta_*)=c_0 n_j, \qquad n_j\in\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(12)} Choose precisely this vector in (7). The exponent in (10) then has zero first derivative at x*x_*. This is additive principal stationarity in an exact period presentation, not just an exponentiated equation.

At this point gθ=gθ′=0g_\theta=g_\theta'=0 on the matching sheets. Its real part need not be zero for the complex cut. Thus (10) has the calibrated geometric exponential rate. Compatible changes of entropy or dilogarithm sheets carry their corresponding integer character and retained real constant, as in V; they are not arbitrary shifts of the imaginary action.

The original external modified-dimension factor, if supplied, remains its whole function. At M=1M=1 its specified value is a unit phase times 1/N1/N; it changes the explicit power and phase, not (14). No meridian normal is deleted.

6. What the theorem does not say

The point x*x_* need not lie on a contributing deformation of ΓN\Gamma_N. The principal critical family retains its centralizer direction, so this is not an isolated Gaussian saddle theorem. Even the nonzero local density in (10) does not fix its relative-cycle coefficient.

Changing the integer character changes the off-lattice integrand and its possible boundary behavior. Equation (8) is exact on the stated original small-circle cycle; it does not permit a subsequent unrecorded contour change. All other source boundaries, support walls, escaping decorations and equal-rate aliases still require their own comparison. No scalar lower bound or new volume-conjecture knot case is asserted.

Where the underlying finite coefficients have their specified analytic parameter germs, the residue equality also preserves those germs. For genuine fixed-color quantum normals one uses the original path M=−qNM=-q^N, with d=N−1d=N-1 fixed, as in A. A fixed-MM auxiliary array is not identified with a generic-qq representation by scalarity alone.
