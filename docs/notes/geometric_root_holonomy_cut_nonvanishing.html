The ordinary one-cut section is not generically zero on the figure-eight geometric root component

Independent audit, 27 October 2026. The ordinary quantum dimension of an individual cyclic color is zero, but this does not force the pivotal one-cut functional of the closed-braid Hom line to vanish. For the figure-eight root-holonomy component, generic nonvanishing holds at every level N≥2N\ge2. There are also literal full-quantum nonzero tests at complete irreducible points, for N=2,3,4N=2,3,4, in both Riley embeddings.

This concerns the full root-center object constructed here, not an identification of its root-central geometric point with the semiclassical geometric block of the original nilpotent state. It gives no volume estimate and no general-knot nonvanishing theorem.

1. Zero quantum dimension does not decide the one-cut section

In a regular color Kaa=xQ−aK_{aa}=xQ^{-a}, and Tr⁡(g)=x1−N∑a=0N−1Qa(N−1)=0,g=K1−N. \operatorname{Tr}(g)= x^{1-N}\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}Q^{a(N-1)}=0, \qquad g=K^{1-N}. For a knot Hom-line frame JJ, the partial pivotal trace is ptr⁡(J)=cI\operatorname{ptr}(J)=cI. The full trace equation only says Tr⁡(g)c=0\operatorname{Tr}(g)c=0, which is 0=00=0. It cannot imply c=0c=0. The identity tensor, whose partial trace does vanish, is not a model for every braid Hom-line frame.

Vanishing is independent of a nonzero scalar rescaling of JJ. It is also independent of a change of splitting-module bases: conjugate every local module and pivot, conjugate JJ by the tensor basis change, and the cut conjugates by the first basis change. Thus zero/nonzero evaluation is an intrinsic question on the admissible splitting cover, not a determinant-root normalization.

2. A direct algebraic Riley component, including the attachment

Use the classical action word (σ1σ2−1)2(\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^2. Put A=(z10z−1),B=(z0uz−1),C=A−1BAB−1A. A=\begin{pmatrix}z&1\\0&z^{-1}\end{pmatrix}, \quad B=\begin{pmatrix}z&0\\u&z^{-1}\end{pmatrix}, \quad C=A^{-1}BAB^{-1}A. The first fixed-braid equation is built into this expression for CC. The second is CA=BCCA=BC; preservation of the ordered product then gives the third. Direct multiplication yields CA−BC=(0P/z−uzP−uP),P=u2+(z2+z−2−3)(u−1). CA-BC= \begin{pmatrix}0&P/z\\-uzP&-uP\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \boxed{P=u^2+(z^2+z^{-2}-3)(u-1).} \tag{2.1}

At z=1z=1, the equation is u2−u+1=0u^2-u+1=0, giving the two complete irreducible figure-eight Riley representations. For the root center, retain the selected actual Casimir branch by putting z=MN,Ω=−M−M−1. z=M^N,\qquad \Omega=-M-M^{-1}. This is compatible with the central Chebyshev relation and Ω(1)=−2\Omega(1)=-2. It is not an assumption equating a root center with a principal symbol.

The curve P(u,MN)=0P(u,M^N)=0 is irreducible over ℂ(M)\mathbb C(M) for every NN. Its discriminant is (M4N−3M2N+1)(M4N+M2N+1)M4N. \frac{(M^{4N}-3M^{2N}+1)(M^{4N}+M^{2N}+1)}{M^{4N}}. Each numerator factor has only simple nonzero roots: write y=M2Ny=M^{2N}, and its two roots are distinct and nonzero. The two factors have no common root, since their difference is 4M2N4M^{2N}. The discriminant therefore has 8N8N simple zeros and is not a square in ℂ(M)\mathbb C(M). This proves irreducibility of the quadratic in uu.

Let φ=(1+5)/2>1\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2>1. At u=0,z=φu=0,z=\varphi, z2+z−2=3z^2+z^{-2}=3, so (2.1) holds. The representation is upper triangular and nonabelian: BB is diagonal but does not commute with the nonzero upper entry of AA, since φ≠φ−1\varphi\ne\varphi^{-1}. It is an actual nonabelian reducible Alexander attachment point on the same irreducible Riley curve as the complete geometric points.

The upper-triangular chart has central FiN=0F_i^N=0, all central crossing divisors equal to one, and a common KK-root x=qNN−1M*x=q_N^{N-1}M_*, where M*=φ1/N>1. M_*=\varphi^{1/N}>1. The semicyclic and balanced coherent crossing constructions give invertible frames there, regular through their zero section. Thus the Hom line and its evaluation are defined at this attachment point.

3. A uniform positive anchor proves generic nonvanishing

The exact whole-cut source-independence theorem, proved by the nonnegative wrap-degree argument in balanced coherent braiding, identifies the selected cut on this FN=0F^N=0 family with the actual reduced figure-eight value FN(M*)F_N(M_*), up to the nonzero chosen Hom-frame unit. A wrap contributes +N+N to residue degree; the one-cut range cannot absorb it, so this is an equality of whole cut matrices.

The independently proved whole figure-eight identity is FN(Y)=∑n=0N−1∏r=1n(Y+Y−1−QNr−QN−r),Y=M2. F_N(Y)= \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \prod_{r=1}^n\bigl(Y+Y^{-1}-Q_N^r-Q_N^{-r}\bigr), \qquad Y=M^2. At Y=M*2>1Y=M_*^2>1, every factor is Y+Y−1−2cos⁡(2πr/N)≥Y+Y−1−2>0. Y+Y^{-1}-2\cos(2\pi r/N) \ge Y+Y^{-1}-2>0. Hence FN(M*)>0for every N≥2. \boxed{F_N(M_*)>0\qquad\text{for every }N\ge2.} \tag{3.1}

A section of the dual of an invertible line on an irreducible component cannot be identically zero if it is nonzero at one regular point of that component. Trivialize the line near that point: the section is an ordinary function with a nonzero value, and thus a nonzero rational function. Applying this to (3.1) gives:

All-level conclusion. On the framed Riley component containing the complete geometric representations, with the selected Casimir branch and its admissible source cover, ev⁡β,N\operatorname{ev}_{\beta,N} is not identically zero for any N≥2N\ge2. It is nonzero on a dense open set.

The statement is intrinsic under splitting-basis changes. The root-cover phases change a chosen frame by a unit; they do not turn a nonzero section into the zero section. At charts where one chain presentation degenerates, an isomorphic splitting chart is required, rather than interpreting its singular basis as a vanishing period.

This does not prove that the section is nonzero at every complete point, or exclude a zero divisor elsewhere.

4. Exact full-quantum tests at complete irreducible points

The script audit_geometric_root_holonomy_cut.py constructs the literal global quantum automorphism at the complete Riley tuple. It does not replace its matrix intertwiner by the classical or semiclassical braid map.

It performs the following independent steps:

  1. Start from the two parabolic matrices at a root u2−u+1=0u^2-u+1=0, whose distinct invariant lines prove irreducibility over the algebraic closure.
  2. Apply an explicit conjugation, the prescribed δ=(−1)N−1\delta=(-1)^{N-1} meridian sign, and the prefix Gauss inverse to obtain the root-center parameters.
  3. Choose the actual splitting modules with Ω=−2\Omega=-2. Evaluate the whole checked rational quantum automorphism in operator-word order, including its contravariant center convention.
  4. Construct an invertible global matrix-unit intertwiner. Check every factor E,F,KE,F,K equation and its commutation with the actual diagonal coproduct.
  5. Take the original pivotal partial trace g=K1−Ng=K^{1-N}. Check that the whole cut matrix is scalar.

These are certificates of nonzero characteristic-zero values, not floating-point evidence. All defining denominators, roots, projectors and frame determinants are units at the displayed reductions. The cyclotomic, Riley, and KK-root equations are etale there. Hensel lifting therefore gives a compatible algebraic characteristic-zero point; a nonzero residue cannot come from a zero characteristic-zero section. The two Riley choices give the geometric and conjugate complete branches, with the prescribed complex root embedding chosen after lifting.

The scalar values depend on the chosen Hom-line frame. Only their zero/nonzero status is asserted intrinsically. No determinant-normalization phase was discarded to test that status.

5. What remains for a geometric-source comparison

The generic ordinary one-cut functional on this irreducible root-holonomy component is genuine and does not vanish for a universal quantum-dimension reason. A modified/derived source is therefore not forced merely by such a generic vanishing claim.

At a point where a particular evaluated section does vanish, its first nonzero normal coefficient, divided source, or another marked relative pairing may still be needed; generic nonvanishing gives no uniform lower bound across levels or control of that order.

The original Kashaev root augmentation has all central meridians equal to II. The geometric root points here have noncentral parabolic meridians. They are different interior central data even when the peripheral scalar is M=1M=1. Nothing above identifies their periods, their source lattices, or their growth. The missing geometric-source and regulator comparison remains a separate theorem.
