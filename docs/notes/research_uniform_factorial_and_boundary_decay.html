Uniform factorial bounds and decay in a free boundary angle

Research proof, 2 November 2026. This note gives upper bounds on the exact Newton interpolation, including the places where a factorial argument crosses zero. It also proves a decrease of the real phase in the remaining boundary angle. These are inputs to a contour argument, not a proof that any particular contour represents the dominant contribution.

1. A midpoint estimate that includes zero

Let GNG_N be the canonical factorial in the factorial continuation note, and let JJ denote the primitive of log⁡(2sin⁡πw)\log(2\sin\pi w) continued from 0<w<10<w<1. Fix 0<δ<10<\delta<1 and a finite H>0H>0.

Lemma 1. There is a constant C=C(δ,H)>1C=C(\delta,H)>1 such that, for all N≥3N\ge3 and all ww with 0≤ℜw≤1−δ0\le\Re w\le1-\delta, |ℑw|≤H|\Im w|\le H,

C−1NeNℜJ(w)≤|GN(Nw−1/2)|≤CNeNℜJ(w).(1) C^{-1}\sqrt N\,e^{N\Re J(w)} \le |G_N(Nw-1/2)| \le C\sqrt N\,e^{N\Re J(w)}. \qquad\text{(1)}

At w=0w=0, set J(0)=0J(0)=0. The estimate includes that point.

Proof. Substitute z=Nw−1/2z=Nw-1/2 into the defining Gamma product:

GN(Nw−1/2)=(2π/N)Nw−1/2Γ(Nw+1/2)∏a≥1Γ(N(a+w)+1/2)Γ(N(a−w)+1/2)(Na)2Nw.(2) G_N(Nw-1/2)= (2\pi/N)^{Nw-1/2}\Gamma(Nw+1/2) \prod_{a\ge1} \frac{\Gamma(N(a+w)+1/2)} {\Gamma(N(a-w)+1/2)(Na)^{2Nw}}. \qquad\text{(2)}

Keep the first Gamma factor exact. For zz in the closed right half-plane, define

Q(z)=Γ(z+1/2)ezz−z,Q(0)=π, Q(z)=\Gamma(z+1/2)e^z z^{-z},\qquad Q(0)=\sqrt\pi,

using the principal logarithm. Its absolute value is bounded above and bounded away from zero on that half-plane. On its intersection with a fixed disk this follows by continuity, nonvanishing of Gamma, and zlog⁡z→0z\log z\to0. Outside a sufficiently large disk, the sectorial midpoint Stirling expansion gives Q(z)=2π(1+O(1/|z|))Q(z)=\sqrt{2\pi}(1+O(1/|z|)) uniformly for |arg⁡z|≤π/2|\arg z|\le\pi/2.

In every other factor of (2), the real parts of a±wa\pm w are at least δ\delta. Apply the same Stirling expansion there. Its first corrections occur in the difference

−124N∑a≥1(1a+w−1a−w), -\frac1{24N}\sum_{a\ge1} \left(\frac1{a+w}-\frac1{a-w}\right),

which is uniformly convergent because its terms are O(a−2)O(a^{-2}). The remainders are bounded by a uniformly summable O(N−3a−3)O(N^{-3}a^{-3}) sequence, with the finitely many initial terms absorbed into a constant depending on δ,H\delta,H. The resulting error in the logarithm of the product is Oδ,H(1/N)O_{\delta,H}(1/N).

The leading logarithm is NJ(w)NJ(w): differentiating its convergent series gives Euler’s product for log⁡(2sin⁡πw)\log(2\sin\pi w), and the value at zero fixes the integration constant. The remaining constant is N/(2π)Q(Nw)\sqrt{N/(2\pi)}\,Q(Nw). Its modulus is between two positive multiples of N\sqrt N. This proves (1), including w=0w=0. The argument uses only the defining Gamma product and the usual sectorial Stirling expansion. ▫\square

2. Reciprocal bounds across the negative real axis

For ℜw<0\Re w<0, choose J+(w)J_+(w) above the negative real axis and J−(w)J_-(w) below it. Their real boundary values on the axis agree. The exact identity from the continuation note is

1GN(Nw−1/2)=2cos⁡(πNw)NGN(−Nw−1/2).(3) \frac1{G_N(Nw-1/2)} =\frac{2\cos(\pi Nw)}N G_N(-Nw-1/2). \qquad\text{(3)}

On the upper side, J+(w)=−J(−w)+iπwJ_+(w)=-J(-w)+i\pi w and 2cos⁡(πNw)=e−iπNw(1+e2πiNw)2\cos(\pi Nw)=e^{-i\pi Nw}(1+e^{2\pi iNw}). The factor in parentheses has modulus at most two. On the lower side use the conjugate sign. Lemma 1 therefore implies the following uniform upper estimate:

|1GN(Nw−1/2)|≤CN−1/2e−NℜJside(w),−1+δ≤ℜw≤1−δ,|ℑw|≤H.(4) \left|\frac1{G_N(Nw-1/2)}\right| \le C N^{-1/2}e^{-N\Re J_{\mathrm{ side}}(w)}, \quad -1+\delta\le\Re w\le1-\delta, \quad |\Im w|\le H. \qquad\text{(4)}

For nonnegative real part, JsideJ_{\mathrm{ side}} is the ordinary continued branch and (4) follows directly from (1). At negative real arguments, (3) proves the bound with both cosine terms retained. Reciprocal factorial zeros do not cause an exception: the left side can vanish, and (4) is an upper bound. Thus (4) does not require staying a fixed distance from the negative real axis or from zero.

3. The exact Newton interpolation

Put d=N−1d=N-1 and

BN(x,t)=GN(d/2+Nt/(2π))GN(d/2−Nt/(2π))GN(d/2−Nx+Nt/(2π))GN(d/2−Nx−Nt/(2π))GN(Nx)2. B_N(x,t)= \frac{G_N(d/2+Nt/(2\pi))G_N(d/2-Nt/(2\pi))} {G_N(d/2-Nx+Nt/(2\pi))G_N(d/2-Nx-Nt/(2\pi))G_N(Nx)^2}.

The numerator is exactly NN, by GN(z)GN(d−z)=NG_N(z)G_N(d-z)=N. At every integer kk with 0≤k<N0\le k<N, this is the original Newton polynomial ek(−2cos⁡t)e_k(-2\cos t) when x=k/Nx=k/N.

Let xx range over a compact rectangle 0<α≤ℜx≤β<1/20<\alpha\le\Re x\le\beta<1/2, |ℑx|≤Hx|\Im x|\le H_x. Let tt range over a bounded complex set on which the real parts of

w+=12−x+t2π,w−=12−x−t2π w_+=\frac12-x+\frac{t}{2\pi},\qquad w_-=\frac12-x-\frac{t}{2\pi}

stay in [−1+δ,1−δ][-1+\delta,1-\delta] for some δ>0\delta>0. Apply (4) to w±w_\pm and (1) to x+1/(2N)x+1/(2N); the finitely many small levels can be handled by increasing the constant. Since xx stays away from zero and one, N[J(x+1/(2N))−J(x)]N[J(x+1/(2N))-J(x)] is uniformly bounded. It follows that

|BN(x,t)|≤CNexp⁡{N𝔟(x,t)},𝔟(x,t)=−ℜJside(w+)−ℜJside(w−)−2ℜJ(x).(5) |B_N(x,t)|\le\frac{C}{N}\exp\{N\mathfrak b(x,t)\}, \qquad \mathfrak b(x,t)= -\Re J_{\mathrm{ side}}(w_+)-\Re J_{\mathrm{ side}}(w_-)-2\Re J(x). \qquad\text{(5)}

The estimate includes all moving-zero faces and their intersections. On a contour where the chosen branches are holomorphic, 𝔟(x,t)=ℜb(x,t)\mathfrak b(x,t)=\Re b(x,t). Elsewhere (5) specifies the real upper exponent directly, using the side approached by each argument. One must use those same branches in a certificate for a contour face.

For a real boundary angle u∈[−π,π]u\in[-\pi,\pi], the conditions on w±w_\pm hold uniformly on the entire rectangle in xx. Thus (5) also supplies an exact bound on the second boundary factor throughout the whole real angular period.

4. Decay in the real boundary angle

Lemma 2. Write x=ξ+ihx=\xi+ih, where 0<ξ<1/20<\xi<1/2. For −π≤u≤π-\pi\le u\le\pi one has

𝔟(x,u)−𝔟(x,0)≤−sin⁡(2πξ)4πcosh⁡2(πh)(1−cos⁡u).(6) \mathfrak b(x,u)-\mathfrak b(x,0) \le-\frac{\sin(2\pi\xi)}{4\pi\cosh^2(\pi h)} (1-\cos u). \qquad\text{(6)}

Proof. Evenness reduces to 0≤u≤π0\le u\le\pi. Away from zeros, differentiation of the continued primitive gives

∂u𝔟(x,u)=14πlog⁡AB, \partial_u\mathfrak b(x,u) =\frac1{4\pi}\log\frac{A}{B},

A=cos⁡2(πξ+u/2)+sinh⁡2(πh),B=cos⁡2(πξ−u/2)+sinh⁡2(πh). A=\cos^2(\pi\xi+u/2)+\sinh^2(\pi h),\qquad B=\cos^2(\pi\xi-u/2)+\sinh^2(\pi h).

Here B−A=sin⁡(2πξ)sin⁡u≥0B-A=\sin(2\pi\xi)\sin u\ge0 and B≤cosh⁡2(πh)B\le\cosh^2(\pi h). Hence

∂u𝔟(x,u)≤−sin⁡(2πξ)sin⁡u4πcosh⁡2(πh). \partial_u\mathfrak b(x,u) \le-\frac{\sin(2\pi\xi)\sin u}{4\pi\cosh^2(\pi h)}.

Integrating proves (6). When h=0h=0, an interior zero of AA gives an integrable logarithmic singularity. Apply the argument on the two sides of that point and pass to the continuous primitive; no bound on the pointwise logarithm at the zero is required. ▫\square

In particular, on the fixed wire rectangle there is c>0c>0 such that

|BN(x,u)|≤CNexp⁡[N{𝔟(x,0)−c(1−cosu)}](−π≤u≤π).(7) |B_N(x,u)|\le\frac{C}{N} \exp\!\left[N\{\mathfrak b(x,0)-c(1-\cos u)\}\right] \qquad(-\pi\le u\le\pi). \qquad\text{(7)}

This applies to positive and negative wire heights and to every intermediate height on a connecting surface. If a complete contour argument bounds the remaining factors at the selected rate when u=0u=0, (7) supplies the extra real-boundary loss on the same contour. It does not supply the missing contour argument or its nonzero leading coefficient. Its role is to make that argument usable after the terminal tangle is glued to another tangle.
