Reduced Frobenius hubs, strict support, and a positive complete-character kernel

31 October 2026. source theorem. The calculations use the full repeated-root algebra, with all independent physical leaf colors and first normal coefficients. They give a sharp strictness criterion and an exact recursive source interface. No tangle isotopy is inferred from an algebraic rerooting.

The flag-free case reduces to source estimates already proved for the completed grammar and isolated three-leaf closure. The case with one flagged leaf at every hub is covered by the companion decorated-character spectral theorem. Mixed flagged and unflagged hubs retain a distinct unresolved analytic operation, specified below. This note itself does not assemble a filling or knot-volume theorem.

1. Source conventions and the exact local tensor

Let d=N−1d=N-1, ℋN=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2)\mathcal H_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2), and er=B̂re_r=\widehat B_r, 0≤r≤d0\le r\le d. Use the physical turn TT, marked functional ϕ\phi, and independent leaves hi=T(pi),pi=RϵiLji,1≤ji≤N,ϵi∈{0,±1}. h_i=T(p_i),\qquad p_i=R^{\epsilon_i}L_{j_i},\qquad 1\le j_i\le N,\quad \epsilon_i\in\{0,\pm1\}. No two colors or signs are identified. The proved source rules are Ter=ed−r,ϕ(eiej)=𝟏i+j=d,eiej=∑r=max⁡(i,j)i+jμi,j,rer,μi,j,r=si+jsrsisjsr−isr−jsi+j−r>0(i+j≤d),eiej=0(i+j>d),sr=∏t=1r2sin⁡πtN,s0=1. \begin{gathered} Te_r=e_{d-r},\qquad \phi(e_i e_j)=\mathbf1_{i+j=d},\\ e_i e_j=\sum_{r=\max(i,j)}^{i+j}\mu_{i,j,r}e_r,\qquad \mu_{i,j,r}= \frac{s_{i+j}s_r}{s_i s_j s_{r-i}s_{r-j}s_{i+j-r}}>0 \quad(i+j\le d),\\ e_i e_j=0\quad(i+j>d),\qquad s_r=\prod_{t=1}^r2\sin\frac{\pi t}{N},\quad s_0=1. \end{gathered}\tag{1} These are the exact source compiler, §1, and the underlying positive Newton multiplication theorem. Their proof is polynomial with multiplicities, so first normal coefficients are retained.

The symmetric trivalent tensor C(i,j,k)=ϕ(eiejek)C(i,j,k)=\phi(e_i e_j e_k) is the exact factorial tensor in the isolated three-leaf source, §1. Its support is i+j,i+k,j+k≤d,i+j+k≥d. i+j,\ i+k,\ j+k\le d,\qquad i+j+k\ge d. \tag{2} Every supported entry is positive. In scaled strict coordinates its domain is pairwise sums less than one and total sum greater than one. Thus a product gate with inputs x,yx,y and output zz is this tensor with legs x,y,1−zx,y,1-z: max⁡(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \max(x,y)<z<x+y<1. \tag{3} This identifies strict support without relying on an equality between signed scalar sums.

Write 𝑱ij=𝟏i+j=d\mathbf J_{ij}=\mathbf1_{i+j=d}, so 𝑱−1=𝑱\mathbf J^{-1}=\mathbf J. Internal propagation with no turn is 𝑱\mathbf J; an inserted turn gives 𝑱T=I\mathbf JT=I. A leaf with edge parity σ\sigma supplies TσhiT^\sigma h_i. These conventions follow by raising the output index of CC with 𝑱\mathbf J in (1).

2. An exact product-hub support lemma

For nn fixed basis inputs ei1,…,eine_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_n}, the product is zero unless ip+iq≤d(p≠q). i_p+i_q\le d\qquad(p\ne q). \tag{4} If (4) holds, its positive support is exactly [maxpip,min(d,∑pip)]∩ℤ. \left[\max_p i_p,\ \min\!\left(d,\sum_p i_p\right)\right]\cap\mathbb Z. \tag{5} For n=1n=1 this is its singleton support. Necessity of (4) follows by associating a violating pair first. For sufficiency, use any binary parenthesization. Each child has the interval (5) inductively. The two lower endpoints have sum at most dd, by (4); the exact interval compiler therefore gives (5) for the parent. Every coefficient asserted positive has a positive summand. This proves the result for every grouping, not merely after choosing a favorable grouping.

Consequently the kk-valent tensor Ck(𝒊)=ϕ(∏peip)C_k(\mathbf i)=\phi(\prod_p e_{i_p}) has positive support exactly ∑pip≥d,ip+iq≤d(p≠q). \sum_p i_p\ge d,\qquad i_p+i_q\le d\quad(p\ne q). \tag{6} For k=2k=2 this is i1+i2=di_1+i_2=d; for k≥3k\ge3 it is a nontrivial positive tensor. Its value is defined by (1), including all scalar factors, not by a new normalization.

There is a corresponding strict statement with fixed external labels 0<xp<10<x_p<1. A product of n≥2n\ge2 inputs admits strict intermediate gates with prescribed output zz precisely when xp+xq<1(p≠q),maxpxp<z<min⁡(1,∑pxp). x_p+x_q<1\quad(p\ne q),\qquad \max_p x_p<z<\min\!\left(1,\sum_p x_p\right). \tag{7} This holds for every binary parenthesization. Necessity follows by following any two leaves to their first common gate: its two input labels are at least those leaf labels and have sum less than one. The output is strictly larger than each input and strictly smaller than their sum, which also gives the remaining inequalities.

For constructive sufficiency, for a child set SS put LS=maxp∈Sxp,US=min⁡(1,∑p∈Sxp). L_S=\max_{p\in S}x_p,\qquad U_S=\min\!\left(1,\sum_{p\in S}x_p\right). A nonsingleton child can take every value in (LS,US)(L_S,U_S); a singleton takes its fixed value. At a parent with target zz, restrict both child values below zz. Their available sum has lower endpoint L1+L2<1L_1+L_2<1 and upper endpoint min⁡(U1,z)+min⁡(U2,z)>z\min(U_1,z)+\min(U_2,z)>z. Both child sets are nonempty because z>Liz>L_i. Thus choose a sum strictly between zz and one. If both children are singletons, their single sum already lies there. This gives the strict parent gate and then strict children by induction.

A closed kk-hub, k≥3k\ge3, in any resolution into trivalent product tensors, admits strict internal wires precisely when ∑pxp>1,xp+xq<1(p≠q). \sum_p x_p>1,\qquad x_p+x_q<1\quad(p\ne q). \tag{8} Split at any root pairing. The two child intervals from (7), with singleton exceptions, contain values summing to one: their lower endpoint sum is less than one, and their upper endpoint sum is greater than one. This proves sufficiency. Necessity follows from the same gate inequalities and the final complementary relation. For k=2k=2 the root pairing is the equality x1+x2=1x_1+x_2=1, with no retained gate; that exception remains separate.

In particular, collapsing or resolving an internal edge with no turn preserves strict feasibility at the same external labels. This is the strict-support justification that an appeal to associativity alone would not supply.

3. The reduced unrooted source and its sharp strictness criterion

Start with a literal cylindrical expression C(FG)C(FG) with H≥3H\ge3 original leaves. Contract unary turn chains modulo T2=IT^2=I and suppress the degree-two closing vertex. At each multiplication use the tensor and metric conventions of §1. This gives an unrooted trivalent tree with independently labelled physical leaves and turn parities on its edges.

Collapse every internal edge of parity zero. Adjacent symmetric tensors contract through 𝑱\mathbf J to CkC_k, by Frobenius associativity. The resulting tree has product hubs of valence at least three, all hub-to-hub edges of parity one, and a flag σi\sigma_i on each leaf edge: σi=0:hi=T(pi),σi=1:Thi=pi. \sigma_i=0:\ h_i=T(p_i),\qquad \sigma_i=1:\ Th_i=p_i. \tag{9} The reduced tree is unique up to isomorphism with the original leaf labels retained: it is obtained by contracting the components of the zero-parity internal edges. This uniqueness is only for the named algebraic moves, not a classification modulo every identity of ℋN\mathcal H_N or every tangle isotopy.

Strict-hub theorem. The closed expression has a strict reduced state if and only if each reduced hub is incident to at most one flagged leaf.

Necessity follows from (8). Two flagged original leaves have leg labels 1−ai>1/21-a_i>1/2 and 1−aj>1/21-a_j>1/2, violating their pair inequality. The hub resolution lemma makes this argument independent of the initial parenthesization.

For sufficiency, first suppose there is a hub-to-hub edge and root there. Looking toward a parent edge, each hub has at least two other incident branches. A leaf branch has type L=(0,1/2)L=(0,1/2) if unflagged and H=(1/2,1)H=(1/2,1) if flagged. Inductively, a child hub has product output of type HH if it contains one flagged leaf, and A=(0,1)A=(0,1) otherwise: incoming hub branches have types T(H)=LT(H)=L or T(A)=AT(A)=A, and cannot supply an HH input. At most one HH input is allowed, and the exact strict table gives precisely these H/AH/A outputs. Across the root TT edge the possible closed pairs are A/A,A/L,H/A,H/L, A/A,\qquad A/L,\qquad H/A,\qquad H/L, \tag{10} all strict. These are the original full type intervals, not selected pointwise labels.

For a single hub, no flag means a product of H≥3H\ge3 leaves of type LL; exposing one leaf gives the strict pair L/AL/A. With one flag expose that leaf, giving H/AH/A. This proves sufficiency. The local resolution lemma transports the resulting strict state back to the original syntax.

With two original leaves there is no hub; the only datum is the edge turn parity. The strict case has parity one and closed pair L/HL/H. This two-leaf case is separate from the H≥3H\ge3 packet and from knot-component claims.

4. Exact recursive interface and the extent of grammar reduction

Choose a flagged leaf at a root hub when one is present. For any other hub vv, let QvQ_v be its outgoing coupon after the edge TT toward its parent. Its exact recursion is Qv={T(pi(v)∏wQw∏lhl),if v has a flagged leaf i(v),T(∏wQw∏lhl),if v has no flagged leaf. Q_v= \begin{cases} T\!\left(p_{i(v)}\prod_w Q_w\prod_l h_l\right), &\text{if \(v\) has a flagged leaf \(i(v)\)},\\ T\!\left(\prod_w Q_w\prod_l h_l\right), &\text{if \(v\) has no flagged leaf}. \end{cases} \tag{11} Products contain all other incident branches, with original labels. They have at least one factor in the first case and at least two in the second. The final scalar at the chosen flagged root is ZN=ϕ(p0∏wQw∏lhl). Z_N=\phi\!\left(p_0\prod_w Q_w\prod_l h_l\right). \tag{12} These are exact identities for arbitrary independent colors and half signs, including all normal directions. The child messages have type LL in the first case of (11) and AA in the second. These two operators, rather than arbitrary matrices or a semisimple quotient, are the compositional spectral interface.

The recursive notation may initially give a closing character with only one decorated child. This is not an extra H≥3H\ge3 boundary case. Writing Di[E]=T(piE)D_i[E]=T(p_iE), self-adjointness gives exactly ϕ(p0D1[E])=ϕ((Tp0)p1E)=ϕ(h0p1E). \phi(p_0D_1[E])=\phi((Tp_0)p_1E)=\phi(h_0p_1E). \tag{13} If E=1E=1, there are exactly two physical leaves and no hub. If EE is a nonempty product of recursive child coupons, the right side is rooted at character p1p_1, with ordinary satellite h0h_0 and all factors of EE. There are at least two root satellite factors and hence valence at least three. Every physical color and half sign is retained. The turn moves across the exposed pairing, not through a product.

If there are no flagged leaves, root at any internal edge, or split a single hub into two groups of at least two leaves. Both open sides belong to E::=hh∣T(E)∣EE∣hE∣Eh. E\ ::=\ hh\ \mid\ T(E)\ \mid\ EE\ \mid\ hE\ \mid\ Eh. \tag{14} Thus H≥4H\ge4 reduces as a full source to the completed/completed-root grammar; H=3H=3 is the isolated C(hhh)C(hhh) case. All independent colors remain. The root cut exists because each side of an internal edge contains at least two leaves. This is a source reduction, not a topological move.

A single hub with one flag is the proved root star: ϕ(p0∏ihi)=ϕ(h0T(∏ihi)). \phi\!\left(p_0\prod_i h_i\right) =\phi\!\left(h_0T\!\left(\prod_i h_i\right)\right). \tag{15} If there is precisely one flagged hub but further hubs, it is C(h0T(E))C(h_0T(E)) with a completed child EE. Multiple flagged hubs are governed by (11). In general they do not disappear by the named Frobenius rerooting, associativity, commutativity and T2T^2 moves: those moves preserve the reduced flagged tree. This is an obstruction to that restricted simplification procedure, not to additional source identities or to the desired asymptotic theorem.

For example, the two-hub source ϕ((Th1)h2T((Th3)h4)) \phi\!\left((Th_1)h_2\,T((Th_3)h_4)\right) \tag{16} is strict and has one flagged and one unflagged leaf at each hub. Under the named moves it is neither a flag-free completed-root tree nor a single-hub root star. This is a structural witness, not a numerical knot experiment.

The cap identity D(FG)=D(F)D(G),D=ϕT, D(FG)=D(F)D(G),\qquad D=\phi T, still holds with its separate marked topological interpretation. It applies when a DD-product is exposed. It is not a rule for factoring the CC-contractions (11)–(16); no cap factor has been inserted here.

5. A positive complete-character kernel and the fully flagged action

At pure complete colors pi=LNp_i=L_N, define KN(E)=T(LNE)K_N(E)=T(L_NE). Coefficient reversal and the exact complete trace identity give, for every full Hermite element EE, [er]KN(E)=ϕ(erLNE)=N∑k=0N−1er(ck)E(ck),ck=2cos⁡(2k+1)πN. [e_r]K_N(E)=\phi(e_rL_NE) =N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e_r(c_k)E(c_k),\qquad c_k=2\cos\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{N}. \tag{17} The trace identity retains the repeated-node multiplicities. It is not a substitution of a different closure: in ϕ(LNF)\phi(L_NF) the two original normal contributions cancel exactly. See the root-character contraction, §2, and its prior squared-kernel identity.

For r>M=⌊d/2⌋r>M=\lfloor d/2\rfloor, ere_r vanishes at all nodes. Thus KN(E)K_N(E) has degree at most MM and is determined by the finite kernel KN(E)(cl)=N∑k=0N−1[∑r=0Mer(cl)er(ck)]E(ck). K_N(E)(c_l)=N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left[\sum_{r=0}^{M}e_r(c_l)e_r(c_k)\right]E(c_k). \tag{18} On the distinct nodes c0,…,cMc_0,\ldots,c_M, every er(ck)e_r(c_k) is nonnegative and is zero for k<rk<r. Let nkn_k be its multiplicity in the full grid: two except at the odd simple node, where it is one. Put Bkr=er(ck)B_{kr}=e_r(c_k) and 𝑫=diag⁡(nk)\mathbf D=\operatorname{diag}(n_k). The matrix in (18) is NBB𝗍𝑫NBB^{\mathsf t}\mathbf D. It is entrywise nonnegative, self-adjoint and positive definite for the inner product weighted by 𝑫\mathbf D, because BB is triangular with nonzero diagonal. Its symmetric conjugate is the positive Gram matrix N𝑫1/2BB𝗍𝑫1/2. N\mathbf D^{1/2}BB^{\mathsf t}\mathbf D^{1/2}. \tag{19}

This kernel is not a contraction with polynomial norm. Its exact exponential size is the useful feature. Let c*=cMc_*=c_M and G0=g(1/4),g(u)=J(2u)−4J(u),J(u)=∫0ulog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dt. G_0=g(1/4),\qquad g(u)=J(2u)-4J(u),\qquad J(u)=\int_0^u\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt. The uniform Newton estimate, §3, is log⁡er(−2)=Ng(r/N)+O(log⁡N)\log e_r(-2)=Ng(r/N)+O(\log N), with max⁡g=G0\max g=G_0. Consequently maxl|KN(E)(cl)|≤exp⁡{N(2G0+o(1))}maxk|E(ck)|. \max_l|K_N(E)(c_l)| \le \exp\{N(2G_0+o(1))\}\max_k|E(c_k)|. \tag{20} For a nonnegative nodal EE, keeping k=Mk=M and r=⌊N/4⌋r=\lfloor N/4\rfloor gives KN(E)(c*)≥exp⁡{N(2G0−o(1))}E(c*). K_N(E)(c_*)\ge \exp\{N(2G_0-o(1))\}E(c_*). \tag{21} At odd levels c*=−2c_*=-2. At even levels c*+2=O(N−2)c_*+2=O(N^{-2}); all quarter-index factors stay away from zero, so this changes their logarithm by O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). The shifts and nodal multiplicities are therefore harmless at both parities. The tree and its number of factors are fixed before the limit.

Suppose every reduced hub has exactly one flagged leaf. Root at one of them. Every nonroot flagged hub is KNK_N of a product of its child messages; every unflagged original leaf is hN=KN(1)h_N=K_N(1). All these messages are nonnegative on the nodal grid by (18). Inductively, (20)–(21) show that their maximum nodal rate and their c*c_* rate agree and add 2G02G_0 for each KNK_N operation. The final scalar is N∑kEroot(ck)N\sum_kE_{\mathrm{root}}(c_k), with no further exponential cost. There is one KNK_N operation for every physical leaf except the flagged root. Hence the positive complete scalar has the exact all-level rate limN→∞1Nlog⁡ZN(N,…,N)=2G0(H−1). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log Z_N(N,\ldots,N) =2G_0(H-1). \tag{22} This supplies both a lower and an upper bound for the original complete source. Its equality with the original strict classical action uses the existing positive coefficient and strict lattice theorem; no oversized norm has been renamed a geometric action. The rigid-spine geometry independently derives the same value from the rigid LL profiles.

At noncomplete colors or with half twists, (11)–(12) remain exact, but positivity of (17)–(21) is not asserted. The companion decorated-character theorem proves a full-source spectral class closed under E↦T(RϵLjE), E\longmapsto T(R^\epsilon L_jE), \tag{23} and finite products, at exactly this complete rate. Together with the source adapter above, this covers the case in which every hub is flagged. An unflagged hub requires E↦T(E)E\mapsto T(E), the second case of (11); it is not an instance of (23) with an existing physical character. Mixed flagged/unflagged trees therefore remain outside that closure statement.

6. Scope

The finite support lemma, reduced-hub criterion, recursive source identities and positive complete-character kernel follow from the recorded exact source. No finite audit or numerical scan is used. The classification concerns the specified full-source contraction and its strict support, not all tangle isotopies or knot types. Transferring a source estimate to a literal knot-volume theorem additionally uses its original local packet, whole filling transfer and marked geometric interface.
