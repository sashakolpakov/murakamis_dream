Non-diagonal maximizers have no free zero degrees

, 31 October 2026. This uses the actual shared entropy theorem and the labeled complete geometric all-cross flow. It does not assume a global entropy redistribution or exchange inf and max. It supplies ordinary finite positive Lagrange multipliers on a precise stratum. The remaining diagonal strata are expressly excluded.

1. Source setting and the admissible perturbation

Use the shared entropy theorem, with the actual two protected faces and fixed incidence map. A maximizer includes the two triangular allocations at each cross port. First perform the proved batch terminal reduction: every one-sided port with nu=1/2 is retagged cross at the SAME degree vector and entropy. Eventual feasibility of the new pattern follows by mixing with the geometric all-cross flow, scaling by d and rounding with strict middle slack.

Assume now that no remaining one-sided port is diagonal. Thus at each one-sided port

0<=mu<nu<1/2, xi>=0, eta=nu-mu-xi>=0.              (1)

The positive interval length and upper endpoint margin are fixed at this particular finite-dimensional maximizer. No uniform margin over all possible maximizers is being asserted.

Let qG be the actual geometric all-cross flow, with its literal triangular allocations. Every ordinary triangular corner of qG is positive; protected corners are zero. Every ordinary middle is strictly less than1/2, because its two outer faces form a matching edge and cannot both be the marked MAIN pair. Protected middle loads are zero.

For a fixed sufficiently small epsilon>0 put

q(epsilon)=(1-epsilon)q+epsilon qG.                (2)

At cross ports mix the triangular allocations themselves. At a one-sided port interpret its four mixed incidences by the SAME affine (mu,nu,xi) parameterization, or its actual reflection. It is immaterial that qG alone lies outside this one-sided domain: the strict margins in (1) keep (2) inside it for all small epsilon. Shared ordinary targets remain one and BOTH protected targets remain zero. The original label pattern is unchanged. Its eventual feasibility was already proved, so (2) is a legitimate limiting source flow in that same pattern.

This perturbation activates every zero ordinary local incidence and every zero ordinary cross-triangle corner, while it leaves all protected zeros exactly zero.

2. All corner expansions for the actual E

Write

C(alpha)=sum_j f(alpha_j),
f(t)=[Lambda(pi t)+Lambda(pi/2-pi t)]/pi,
f(t)=-t log t+O(t) as t->0,
f(1/2-t)=f(t).                                    (3)

For a cross triangle along an affine admissible path, the coefficient of epsilon log(1/epsilon) in its entropy change is

sum_(alpha_j=0) dot_alpha_j
               -sum_(alpha_j=1/2) dot_alpha_j.    (4)

All these terms are nonnegative. At a vertex it is twice the sum of the activated zero corners; on an open edge it is the activation of its single zero corner. The remainder is O(epsilon), uniformly when the fixed direction ranges over a compact admissible set. This follows from t log t and boundedness of a log a for bounded nonnegative slopes.

For a one-sided port set eta=nu-mu-xi. Along an affine path satisfying (1) for small epsilon, the coefficient h in

E(epsilon)-E(0)=h epsilon log(1/epsilon)+O(epsilon) (5)

is as follows. Slopes of coordinates that vanish at epsilon=0 are nonnegative.

  • If mu,xi,eta are all positive, h=0.
  • If mu=0 and xi,eta>0, h=dot_mu.
  • If mu>0 and xi=0, h=dot_xi; if eta=0, h=dot_eta.
  • If mu=xi=0, h=2(dot_mu+dot_xi).
  • If mu=eta=0, h=2(dot_mu+dot_eta).

These cases exhaust (1). Here is a proof from the ACTUAL closed coefficient entropy, including the simultaneous corner. Use its exact formula from the closed coefficient theorem,

E=C(alpha)-C(beta),
alpha_b=mu, beta_c=nu,
alpha_x-beta_x=xi, alpha_y-beta_y=eta,             (6)

where the input/output triangles have the common outer tilt. Their explicit arcsine reconstruction is the one in the half-block theorem; no independent optimization of alpha and beta is made.

In the first case all corners are strict, so E is smooth. In the second, the tilt stays finite and the only vanishing corner is alpha_b=mu; the arcsine formulas are smooth for the other corners. Equation (3) therefore gives h=dot_mu.

If mu>0 and xi=0, both alpha_x and beta_x vanish, but all their other corners stay strict. The arcsine formulas are smooth at kappa=-1 because mu,nu are strictly between0 and1/2. Both small corners are O(epsilon), and their difference is EXACTLY xi. Their singular coefficients subtract to dot_xi. The y-reflected case is identical.

For mu=xi=0, the input triangle is the vertex (0,0,1/2), while the output triangle has its x corner zero and its other two corners strict. The common outer tilt gives

tan(pi alpha_x)/tan(pi alpha_y)
                  =tan(pi beta_x)/tan(pi beta_y).

Since alpha_y=1/2-mu-alpha_x and beta_c remains near the fixed nu in (0,1/2), this equality and alpha_x=xi+beta_x imply

beta_x=O(xi(mu+xi)), alpha_x=xi+O(xi(mu+xi)).       (7)

Indeed the left ratio is comparable to alpha_x(mu+alpha_x), while the right is comparable to beta_x. After continuity makes all these quantities small, absorb the beta_x terms. The constants are uniform over compact direction sets at the fixed positive nu. Thus beta_x=O(epsilon^2), alpha_x=epsilon dot_xi+O(epsilon^2), and alpha_y=1/2-epsilon(dot_mu+dot_xi)+O(epsilon^2). Equation (3) gives the coefficient 2(dot_mu+dot_xi) for C(alpha). The change in C(beta) is only O(epsilon), including its O(epsilon^2 log epsilon) zero-corner term. This proves the joint case, even if either or both activation slopes vanish. Reflection proves the remaining case. Every displayed O(epsilon) is for a fixed source point, uniformly over a compact set of admissible directions.

3. General exclusion of free zeros at a non-diagonal maximum

Apply (4)–(5) to (2). Every entropy singular coefficient is nonnegative. If any ordinary local degree or ordinary cross-triangle corner is zero, the geometric witness activates it and at least one coefficient is strictly positive. All other changes are O(epsilon). The sum therefore increases by

h_total epsilon log(1/epsilon)+O(epsilon)>0,
h_total>0,

contradicting maximality. Consequently a maximizer with all terminals removed and with no diagonal one-sided port has ONLY the zeros forced by the two original protected faces. Every ordinary cross-triangle corner and every ordinary incidence in a one-sided port is positive.

For cross ports it is enough to retain the particular mixed triangular split: the optimized cross entropy at the mixed total degrees is at least that value. Thus the proof does not assume the new split is an optimizer, nor differentiate a maximum at a nonsmooth split.

4. Finite positive shared multipliers and the actual local equations

Remove the protected variables, which are fixed to zero. On the stratum just obtained, all remaining local inequalities are strict except those that are identities of a zero-dimensional triangle. The objective is smooth in the resulting affine local coordinates. At a constrained maximum its differential annihilates the kernel of the actual face- balance matrix. Elementary finite-dimensional linear algebra gives FINITE REAL face multipliers lambda_f satisfying

d(sum local entropy)+sum_(ordinary f) lambda_f dq_f=0. (8)

Redundant balances cause no issue: a linear functional annihilating the kernel belongs to the row space. No constraint independence or unproved multiplier compactness across different maximizers is assumed. Put l_f=exp(lambda_f)>0 for ordinary f, and retain l_u=l_v=0. The common-scale freedom is harmless because every port has total degree one and every protected incidence is zero.

At a cross triangle with at least two ordinary corners, f’(alpha)=log cot(pi alpha), so (8) implies

tan(pi alpha_f)=l_f/T,
T^2=sum_(pairs f,g in that triangle) l_f l_g.

This is the actual center-angle formula; its one-ordinary-corner case is the forced angle vector (0,0,1/2).

At a one-sided port with both outer variables ordinary, its outer stationarity gives the actual tilt log(l_x/l_y). If the parent middle is ordinary, row stationarity gives L(mu;l_x,l_y)=l_b; if it is protected, mu=0 and the same equation holds by the endpoint limit. The overloaded middle is ordinary, and its strict terminal equation is L(nu;l_x,l_y)=l_c. When one outer face is protected, the corresponding outer degree is fixed zero and the actual entropy is its extreme value B; the same equations hold with L(t;0,y)=y tan^2(pi t), or its reflection. Both outer faces cannot be protected, since their matching edge is not the protected MAIN edge. These formulas also include the forced joint corner x=b=0: the input is the two-line angle vector and the remaining terminal equation is unchanged.

Thus these maxima supply the FINITE positive ordinary multipliers and strict remaining equations required by the marked signed-circle development. The global signed cut-ball regulator uses the original labeled degree-one fundamental chain, actual face pairings, one reflection of the second ball, and the retained peripheral horospheres. It proves that this stationary entropy is Vol(rho)/(2pi), and therefore at most Phi_D=Vol(Y_D)/(2pi). This invokes that GLOBAL chain proof, not the disproved invariance of a re-framed open crossing word.

Consequently every global maximizer satisfying the no-diagonal hypothesis after batch terminal reduction has value at most Phi_D. If such a maximizer exists, the all-cross lower bound gives W_D=Phi_D. Equivalently, any strict inequality W_D>Phi_D would force a diagonal one-sided port in every maximizer with the minimal number of one-sided labels. This is a general reduction at arbitrary fixed width under the original labeled certificate, not an assertion that diagonals are absent.

5. The diagonal exception is essential

This proof excludes every mu=nu port. In particular a diagonal at mu=nu=0 can LOSE entropy of order epsilon log(1/epsilon) under the same mixing, and can cancel the gains above. If the mixing has parent activation B and outer activations X,Y, then

E(epsilon B,epsilon(B+X+Y),epsilon X)
            =2 max(X,Y) epsilon log epsilon+O(epsilon). (9)

For X>Y, the exact arcsine reconstruction has kappa=1-O(epsilon^2). Both endpoint triangles approach the x vertex; their other-outer and middle loads are O(epsilon). The middle loads differ by -epsilon(X+Y), while their y loads differ by epsilon Y. The vertex singular terms in (3) therefore subtract to 2X epsilon log epsilon. For X<Y use the reflected calculation. For X=Y the tilt is exactly zero; the two outer corners stay near1/4 and only the middle corners are small. Their middle difference is -2epsilon X, giving the same formula. The parent B changes only the O(epsilon) term. The zero-width case X=Y=0 gives E=0 exactly.

No such term was concealed in (5). Diagonal ports require an actual identity-deletion/block argument or another global entropy exchange. The lemma does not settle those strata or the phase-sensitive filling asymptotic.

The underlying exact entropy and finite coefficient checks are in the linked half-block theorem. This finite-dimensional proof does not require a new numerical asymptotic audit. The global signed-chain argument retains its original pairings and marked apex.
