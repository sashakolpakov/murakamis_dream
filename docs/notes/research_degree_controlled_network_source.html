Quantum sums on branching circle trees

The wide-rectangle edge theorem permits a complete source proof on branching graphs. A sufficient local condition is |nv|≥max⁡(8,4dv)|n_v|\ge\max(8,4d_v), where dvd_v is the original degree of the filled vertex. The condition allows unrelated powers and independent signs. Every graph and every power are fixed before the level tends to infinity.

The analytic theorem below concerns an explicit finite graph sum. Its knot-volume consequence is restricted to graphs and markings coming from the original ordered circle-tree construction. An arbitrary graph sum is not declared to be a knot invariant. Frozen manuscripts and public documentation are unchanged.

4. The exact finite trace and its discarded rows

Apply the original derivative-sampling formula (12) and midpoint identity (13) of the path-source assembly to each selected variable. These are one-variable identities in the full Frobenius algebra and place no restriction on its degree in the graph. Interchanging them is an operation on finitely many sums and ordinary derivatives.

For the retained rows, the identity is a sum over every subset of vertical vertices. A negative-side vertical contour retains its extra factor −1-1; the exact sign of a subset is their product. The lower horizontal factor always has principal charge +1+1, with coefficient exactly one, for either sign of the crossing power. The full wide-kernel theorem converts (23) into a bound for these actual finite integrands and all their original normals.

The all-horizontal phase has its unique maximum at t*t^* and ℜℋ≽120πdiag⁡(dv)>0.(24) \Re\mathcal H\succeq \frac1{20\pi}\operatorname{diag}(d_v)>0. \qquad\text{(24)} At each fixed graph all positive depths HvH_v are fixed. The higher charges of the rational trace denominators are therefore exponentially smaller. The original edge amplitudes and sine factors are nonzero at the saddle. The Gaussian calculation is exactly (6): there are N2mN^{2m} from the trace prefactors, N−3e/2N^{-3e/2} from all original edges, and N−m/2N^{-m/2} from the Gaussian. This gives the power in (5), with no new normalization depending on the degree. Odd Gaussian moments vanish, giving the stated O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) error and all further fixed orders.

It remains to retain the rows outside the midpoint interval. Let S≠⌀S\ne\varnothing be the set of vertices at those physical rows. Keep their exact crossing values and first normals, and set their analytic charges to zero. The full centered-incidence bounds in Section 8 of the mixed-current note give an exponent loss 21/20021/200 for each incidence at a removed vertex. An edge with both endpoints removed retains both losses. The comparisons hold for all ordinary mixed spectral derivatives. Their strict reserve permits the O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) midpoint change from π/2\pi/2, at both parities.

For the remaining exact traces choose the stationary heights of the partial-charge solution. Their exponents are the original partial-charge phase, with the removed coordinates zero, minus (21/200)∑v∈Sdv(21/200)\sum_{v\in S}d_v. The wide-kernel theorem and (23) apply to all of their horizontal and vertical terms. The crossing values at removed real rows have modulus one; their derivatives and all finite sampling weights cost powers of NN only. Distributing a first normal over arbitrarily many incident factors is a finite product-rule expansion at a fixed graph. It changes polynomial prefactors, not exponential rates. By (17), the rate of the entire outside-row contribution is <M(𝟏)−(21200−340)∑v∈Sdv=M(𝟏)−3100∑v∈Sdv.(25) <M(\mathbf1) -\left(\frac{21}{200}-\frac3{40}\right) \sum_{v\in S}d_v =M(\mathbf1)-\frac3{100}\sum_{v\in S}d_v. \qquad\text{(25)} If no trace remains, the same comparison retains the complete-edge constants and needs no integral. There are finitely many subsets and product-rule terms, so none can cancel the leading Gaussian. This proves (5) for the complete outside colors.

The removed-incidence lemma already controls its other endpoint throughout the complex wide rectangle. It therefore applies directly to a small complex perturbation of an unselected angle near zero; no inference from bounds only at its physical values is needed. On every retained edge the full kernel bound and phase are holomorphic on a fixed neighborhood of the contour. All the current and removed-row comparisons have strict gaps, and there are finitely many subsets for the fixed graph. A sufficiently small outside neighborhood preserves them, while the implicit function theorem continues each required stationary point inside the local kernel domain. The holomorphic Gaussian expansion follows there. Separate evenness descends the phases, amplitudes and normalized errors to ordinary spectral colors near the ramified complete point; Cauchy’s estimates then give all asserted ordinary outside derivatives. This completes the analytic proof of Theorem 1. ▫\square

5. The original circle-tree geometry

Now suppose Γ\Gamma, its page ordering and carriers arise from an original ordered circle-tree exterior. Retain its disk longitudes, meridians, every main component and every marked annular interface. Choose the original integer fillings pvp_v with nv=ηv−2pv(26) n_v=\eta_v-2p_v \qquad\text{(26)} satisfying (1). The angle solution just constructed supplies the hypotheses of the positive marked-completion theorem. To see positivity directly, put wv=tan⁡(tv*/2)w_v=\tan(t_v^*/2). By (9), the sum of squared imaginary parts at either edge is less than 1/81/8. On the branch qe=1+wv2+ww2q_e=\sqrt{1+w_v^2+w_w^2}, (ℜqe)2≥1+(ℜwv)2+(ℜww)2−(ℑwv)2−(ℑww)2>(ℜwv)2+(ℜww)2.(27) (\Re q_e)^2\ge 1+(\Re w_v)^2+(\Re w_w)^2 -(\Im w_v)^2-(\Im w_w)^2 >(\Re w_v)^2+(\Re w_w)^2. \qquad\text{(27)} This is positivity of all eight original tetrahedra on that edge. The selected heights are strictly negative, and (8) at unit charge is exactly the original full logarithmic period equation ∑rvw=invtv/2−πi\sum r_{vw}=i n_vt_v/2-\pi i. There is no change of peripheral basis or replacement of a slope by its absolute value. The marked-completion theorem therefore proves Vol⁡Y(𝒑)=2πℜΦ(t*).(28) \operatorname{Vol}Y(\mathbf p) =2\pi\Re\Phi(t^*). \qquad\text{(28)} Every main cusp and unselected auxiliary cusp remains complete.

Corollary 2. If all auxiliary vertices are filled, the original strand tracing gives one main component and no internal closed component, and (1), (26) hold, the resulting knot satisfies the original full-level Kashaev volume conjecture: limN→∞2πNlog⁡|⟨K⟩N|=Vol⁡(S3\K).(29) \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N} \log|\langle K\rangle_N| =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K). \qquad\text{(29)} Its complete asymptotic has the nonzero leading coefficient (6), with only the already retained unit carrier and zero-framing factors.

This conclusion uses the original circle-tree source identification and the literal component test. No knot interpretation is attached to an arbitrary graph in the analytic theorem. It also does not imply finite exceptions in every original parameter template: assignments with a short power at a fixed vertex remain outside (1), even when other powers grow without bound.
