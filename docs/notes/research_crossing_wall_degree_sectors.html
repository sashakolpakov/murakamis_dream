Cyclic crossing walls are exact integer-degree sectors

Private written proof, 19 November 2026.

The two cross-wall periods of S have a concrete meaning: they are the low and high integer pair-degree blocks of the original truncated transfer. Consequently the three wall choices inside one crossing must agree. A word with cc crossings has at most 2c2^c surviving sectors, rather than the 23c2^{3c} choices in its Euler-slot expansion.

Each sector also has an exact nilpotent completion. Its ordinary Hankel period equals the complete regularized cyclic-wall period, including P’s endpoint finite parts and cyclic-pole residues. With the original diagonal first cut fixed throughout, H’s complex meridian-collar and genuine jet bounds hold for every completed sector. These are identities of complete periods and absolute tail estimates. They do not identify localized densities or prove a nonzero geometric coefficient.

The complete directs are S (cyclic sewing), P (its regularized cut prescription), and H (actual meridian Hankel transport). The finite residue calculation and the crossing-sector reduction are proved here.

1. The two paths in one actual residue fibre

In the residue kk, put h=k+1∈{1,…,N},bn=en⊗e(k−n)mod⁡N,0≤n<N.(2) h=k+1\in\{1,\ldots,N\},\qquad b_n=e_n\otimes e_{(k-n)\bmod N},\quad 0\le n<N. \qquad\text{(2)} Then Abn=abn+1mod⁡NAb_n=ab_{n+1\bmod N}. 2. Nilpotent completion retains the cyclic-pole currents

Let pp⁡0\operatorname{pp}_0 denote the negative Laurent part at zero. Testing (4) on every monomial f(t)=tνf(t)=t^\nu proves entrywise pp⁡0Ch,b(t)=∑ν=0dt−ν−1TνΠh,b=(tI−T)−1Πh,b.(9) \operatorname{pp}_0 C_{h,b}(t) =\sum_{\nu=0}^{d}t^{-\nu-1}T^\nu\Pi_{h,b} =(tI-T)^{-1}\Pi_{h,b}. \qquad\text{(9)} Thus the difference Dh,b(t)=Ch,b(t)−(tI−T)−1Πh,b(10) D_{h,b}(t)=C_{h,b}(t)-(tI-T)^{-1}\Pi_{h,b} \qquad\text{(10)} is holomorphic at zero. This is an exact change of completion for the same small-circle functional. The second term has no nonzero finite pole; the first generally has the cyclic poles tN=aNt^N=a^N. They are not equal rational matrices.

For the actual root transfers in H, |a|=1|a|=1, and the scalar germ is one of the marked inverse cyclic dilogarithms 𝒜ϵ(t)=∏j=1N−1(1−Qϵjt)−j/N,Q=e2πi/N,𝒜ϵ(0)=1.(11) \mathscr A_\epsilon(t)= \prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-Q^{\epsilon j}t)^{-j/N}, \qquad Q=e^{2\pi i/N},\quad \mathscr A_\epsilon(0)=1. \qquad\text{(11)} P’s regularized Hankel formula applies to Ch,bC_{h,b} and to Dh,bD_{h,b}. Both have at most double nonzero poles and are O(t−1)O(t^{-1}) at infinity. Its prescribed functional is the sum of the marked bank finite parts minus the residues at unramified cyclic poles. At a branch endpoint of exponent −u-u, it subtracts the two Laurent terms b−2x−2,b−1x−1b_{-2}x^{-2},b_{-1}x^{-1} and retains the finite constants −b−2/(u+1)−b−1/u-b_{-2}/(u+1)-b_{-1}/u.

Apply that formula to (10). Its original small-circle period is zero, because Dh,bD_{h,b} is holomorphic there. The result is exactly ℋϵreg[Ch,b]=ℋϵord[(tI−T)−1Πh,b].(12) \boxed{\quad \mathcal H^{\mathrm{ reg}}_{\epsilon}[C_{h,b}] =\mathcal H^{\mathrm{ ord}}_{\epsilon} [(tI-T)^{-1}\Pi_{h,b}]. \quad} \qquad\text{(12)} On the right, the ordinary Hankel integral converges at every branch endpoint and infinity, as in H. Thus (12) incorporates the cyclic-pole currents into the nilpotent completion. It does not omit them from the left side. It holds at odd, even, and composite levels, including the collisions of cyclic poles with branch endpoints. P’s marked sheet and cut-arc convention is retained at infinity.

The independent slot variables permit repeated use of (12) inside any fixed ordered tensor contraction. Equivalently, start from its compact product of small circles, apply (9) one variable at a time, then use the proved cut prescriptions. This argument does not assume absolute convergence of a raw, unregularized cyclic-wall integral. Nor does (12) identify restrictions to a chosen radial subregion or geometric window: the completion change can redistribute its currents.

The hat removes only the common crossing scalar. As defined in H, uN(t)=∑a<Nta/Fau_N(t)=\sum_{a<N}t^a/F_a and vN(t)=∑a<N(−1)aQa(a−1)/2ta/Fav_N(t)=\sum_{a<N}(-1)^aQ^{a(a-1)/2}t^a/F_a. Each signed crossing has three transfer slots and six factorial slots. Every one of its factors preserves the same integer pair degree, so every factor commutes with both projectors (8).

In S’s five-term sewing expansion, any term having even one pure cyclic or diagonal sewing slot has zero complete period: integrate that variable first, whose integrand is holomorphic inside its small circle. All other slots and the chosen coupon are independent of it. Thus a surviving word must choose a cross wall at every transfer slot.

In the all-inverse realization, a uN(T)u_N(T) slot uses 𝒜−(t)(tI−Q−1T)−1\mathscr A_-(t)(tI-Q^{-1}T)^{-1}; the scalar rotation changes neither its two paths nor its projectors. For a three-slot choice (b1,b2,b3)(b_1,b_2,b_3), its block period is therefore R̂0±Πb1Πb2Πb3.(14) \widehat R_0^{\pm}\Pi_{b_1}\Pi_{b_2}\Pi_{b_3}. \qquad\text{(14)} It vanishes unless all three choices agree, in which case it is R̂0±Πb\widehat R_0^{\pm}\Pi_b. Factorial integrations may be performed before or after this calculation, since they are independent and their matrix kernels are diagonal. In the nilpotent completion the projectors commute already with those kernels and the transfer resolvents, so mismatched choices vanish pointwise in that completed network. This last statement is not asserted for the raw cyclic walls.
