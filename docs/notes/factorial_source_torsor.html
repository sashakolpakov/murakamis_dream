The cyclic dilogarithm, its factorial lattice and its cut-source torsor

Research note, 26 October 2026. This identifies the actual finite factorial with a singular frame of the literature cyclic dilogarithm, constructs its flat two-direction source extension, and computes the complete affine transport when its cut is moved. The internal center, meridian norm and full ADO dimension remain functions until whole specialization. The proof is uniform in N, including even and composite levels.

This supplies local relative data for the selected-source comparison. It does not identify a finite factorial model with a topological invariant on arbitrary geometric triangulations, or establish the selected Bloch-regulator norm. The actual invariant remains the prescribed reduced ADO evaluation F_N and its Kashaev value F_N(1).

1. The same local quantum function in two frames

Fix N>=2, Q a primitive N-th root, and h,nu with

nu^N=1-h^N.

Initially assume h nu !=0. For a=0,…,N-1 put

p_a(h)=product_(r=1)^a(1-Q^r h),
phi_a(h)=nu^(-a) p_a(h).                         (1.1)

Then, with indices taken cyclically,

phi_(a+1)=(1-Q^(a+1)h) phi_a/nu, phi_0=1.       (1.2)

For a<N-1 this is the factorial recurrence. At the last step, (1-h)p_(N-1)=1-hN=nuN, which proves the same equation. All its multipliers are units on this punctured Fermat cover. Their product is one; (1.2) uniquely fixes the pointed primitive.

For comparison with a literature definition, let

D_N(x;Q)=product_(k=1)^(N-1)(1-Q^k x)^(k/N).

Taking compatible N-th roots, cancellation of neighboring factors gives

D_N(Qx)/D_N(x)=(1-x)/(1-x^N)^(1/N).

Consequently the cyclic dilogarithm with that compatible root choice has the exact normalized values

phi_a(h)=D_N(Q^(a+1)h;Q)/D_N(Qh;Q).             (1.3)

This is an equality of the pointed cyclic primitives, not a limit formula. Their N-th powers already agree as rational products; (1.2) selects the coherent roots. Equivalent conventions insert the usual argument shift, inverse or common power of 1-h^N. Thus (1.1) reconstructs an existing quantum definition rather than introducing a replacement local quantum function.

The root tetrahedron module is literal here. On functions of a modulo N let

(Xf)_a=Q^(a+1)h f_a,  (Y_op f)_a=nu f_(a+1).

Then XY_op=Q^(-1)Y_op X and

(X+Y_op-1)phi=0,
X^N=h^N, Y_op^N=1-h^N.                          (1.4)

After reversing the index a, these are precisely the clock/shift matrices and the normalized kernel of the pointed tetrahedron construction, with s=Qh, t=nu and R=Q^(-1).

2. The actual boundary is a lattice, not an evaluation of phi

Multiply component a by nu^a. The vector p of (1.1) extends polynomially to h=1, nu=0 and has

p_a(1)=(Q;Q)_a !=0, 0<=a<N,
p_(N-1)(1)=N.                                  (2.1)

The last equality follows by evaluating product_(r=1)(N-1)(1-Qr h)=(1-h^N)/(1-h) at h=1. The diagonal frame diag(1,nu,…,nu^(N-1)) is singular there. The cyclic vector phi generally has poles of orders a on this cover; its rescaled factorial lattice has the stated regular value.

The reciprocal primitive 1/p_a is also regular at h=1. These are the factorial and inverse-factorial frames used in the actual cut state. Products with meridian-dependent factorials must first be compressed to their finite Pochhammers when an individual quotient has a pole. The Laurent-polynomial crossing identity supplies that continuation; separate singular factors are not evaluated.

Thus a nondegenerate geometric tetrahedron and the Kashaev cut boundary lie in different parts of a common relative family. Their fixed central characters have not been identified.

3. Retain the internal center and its chosen cut root

Let C=theta^N, U=Y^N, and use the finite etale algebra

S=K(Y,theta)[t]/(t^N-C).

Its marked components are t=theta Q^a. There are two commuting base shifts:

sigma(t)=Qt, sigma(Y)=Y;
delta(Y)=QY, delta(t)=t,

with theta fixed in both. Put h=Y theta and interpolate p_a(h) as p_theta(t;Y). Let e_partial be supported at t=theta Q^(-1), and e_0 at t=theta. Directly from the finite products,

sigma p_theta=(1-QYt)p_theta+UC e_partial,
delta p_theta=(1-QYt)p_theta/(1-QYtheta).         (3.1)

The first equation includes the last transition; its defect is 1-(1-h)p_(N-1)=h^N=UC. The second is the cancellation of p_a(Qh)/p_a(h). Both are identities of whole finite primitives.

The homogeneous, uncut factorial recurrence in the free variable t has meridian multiplier

a_Y=(1-QYt)/(1-QY).

It is the generic recurrence normalized at index zero before imposing an internal periodicity. This normalization is fixed by p_a(Y)=(QY;Q)_a, not selected to impose a desired central curve.

4. Flatness forces a source line with a nontrivial norm

Seek an extension retaining the bulk multipliers and source in (3.1), with no off-diagonal meridian source in this frame. Its two matrices must be

B_sigma=[ 1-QYt   UC e_partial ],
        [   0          1      ]

B_delta=diag(a_Y,d_Y),
d_Y=(1-QYtheta)/(1-QY).                         (4.1)

Here the source line has sigma=1 and delta=d_Y. The source multiplier d_Y is forced: flatness in the top-right entry requires

sigma(a_Y) UC e_partial=delta(UC e_partial) d_Y.

The source and UC are delta-invariant. On its support, sigma(a_Y)=(1-QYtheta)/(1-QY), giving exactly d_Y. The bulk entry also satisfies sigma(a_Y)(1-QYt)=delta(1-QYt)a_Y. Therefore

sigma(B_delta) B_sigma=delta(B_sigma) B_delta.   (4.2)

This is a genuinely flat two-direction difference extension. The diagonal source line is the norm torsor

Norm_delta(d_Y)=(1-UC)/(1-U)=:V.                (4.3)

The same norm holds for a_Y since t^N=C. At a free internal center this norm need not be one. Setting C=1 first makes it one and removes precisely that varying-center information.

The complete monodromies can be read without a determinant:

B_sigma,cyc=[ 1-UC   UC p_theta ],
            [   0        1     ]
B_delta,cyc=V I_2.                             (4.4)

Indeed the internal bulk product is 1-UC and its distinguished fixed vector is (p_theta,1). The meridian product is diagonal and both diagonal products equal (4.3).

One may express the pointing without making a false rational horizontal-frame assertion. Take a source generator g with sigma g=g and delta g=d_Y g. Then

g (p_theta,1)^T

satisfies both matrix equations (4.1). At theta=1 the source line has d_Y=1, and g=1 is the actual finite-factorial pointing. At a general C its meridian norm is V, so g cannot be replaced by the same rational constant.

5. Exact affine transport when the cut root changes

Keep t and C fixed and change theta to theta’=Qtheta. A product cancellation, including the cut component, gives

(1-QYtheta) p_theta'=p_theta-UC e_0.             (5.1)

Away from e_0 it just drops the first factor of p_a. At e_0 its left side is 1-UC, whereas p_theta=1, proving the correction. The full, unnormalized cut adapter is therefore

J_raw=[ 1  -UC e_0 ],  H=1-QYtheta.             (5.2)
      [ 0     H    ]

It takes (p_theta,1) to H(p_theta’,1). Substitution in (4.1) gives, for both shifts tau=sigma,delta,

tau(J_raw) B_tau,theta=B_tau,theta' J_raw.       (5.3)

Thus the source term and its pointing are transported together. The source-line ratio is d_Y,theta’/d_Y,theta=delta(H)/H, an explicit Hilbert-90 comparison.

After N successive cut moves the root theta returns. The entire adapter, however, has loop

J_raw,loop=[ 1  -UC p_theta ],                  (5.4)
           [ 0     1-UC    ]

not an overall scalar multiplier. To prove it, put G_theta=[[1,p_theta],[0,1]]. Then

J_raw=G_theta' diag(1,H) G_theta^(-1).

The G factors telescope and product H=1-UC. This proves (5.4) uniformly. Treating a source transport as only its scalar determinant would lose this off-diagonal term.

6. Coherent transport on the Fermat root cover

Retain nu^N=1-UC and nu!=0. With the homogeneous comparison fixed to the identity, define

J=G_theta' diag(1,H/nu) G_theta^(-1).            (6.1)

The factor H/nu has delta-norm one. It obeys the same two intertwining equations and takes (p_theta,1) to (H/nu)(p_theta’,1). Every N-step cut loop is now exactly the identity, since product H=nu^N. This is strict local cut-root descent of the extension and its source pointing on the stated cover. Changing the chosen nu records a mu_N character.

Dividing the entire J_raw by nu gives a different answer: its N-loop is G_theta diag((1-UC)^(-1),1) G_theta^(-1), not the identity. The source channel must be normalized as in (6.1); an arbitrary common multiplier is not equivalent to that operation.

At nu=0 the normalized adapters can have poles. They are not evaluated separately at that divisor. A finite collection of cuts admits a lattice stable under this finite cyclic transport: on their direct sum take sum_(r=0)^(N-1) J_total^r Lambda_0. This is a finite fractional lattice because J_total^N=1; over a normalized curve it is torsion free and hence locally free. The construction retains the poles and the pointing. It makes no assertion that individual adapters have a regular invertible matrix in the original factorial frame at the degenerate point.

7. The full ADO factor is a specified endpoint transport

On the actual cut theta=1, the raw source-line transport through N-1 successive roots is

product_(r=1)^(N-1)(1-Q^rY)=S_N(Y)
     =(1-Y^N)/(1-Y).                            (7.1)

At q=q_N, Y=M^2, the full modified dimension is exactly

d_N(M)=eta_N M^(N-1)/S_N(Y), eta_N=i^(N+3).      (7.2)

Thus its nonmonomial factor is the inverse raw endpoint transport of this actual source line. In the coherent frame (6.1) the same endpoint transport is S_N(Y)/nu^(N-1). Recovering (7.2) requires the compensating factor nu^(-(N-1)); discarding it loses the original boundary germ. Their whole ratio specializes to 1/N at Y=1.

The meridian gauge remains exactly

d_N(qM)/d_N(M)=-q^(-1)(QY-1)/(Y-1),
Norm_delta(d_N(qM)/d_N(M))=(-1)^(N-1).          (7.3)

The q convention and phase eta_N are unchanged; a Q-only local formula is not permission to remove this full-state sign.

8. Tensor sources retain every wall intersection

For finitely many factors with sigma f_j=a_j f_j+b_j s_j, where s_j is the source component, the exact product equation is

sigma product_j f_j=(product_j a_j)(product_j f_j)
  +sum_(nonempty S) (product_(j in S) b_j s_j)
                     (product_(j not in S) a_j f_j).       (8.1)

This is the top entry of the tensor of their rank-two extensions. It retains the face and corner sources. For example at two factorial cut walls with s_1=s_2=1 and h_1=h_2=1 the bulk multipliers both vanish and both b_j equal one. The b_1 b_2 corner supplies the entire shifted product one; deleting it gives zero.

The tensor source quotient retains its lower tensor factors and their source lines. In a meridian direction those lines carry the norms (4.3). They are not all replaced by one trivial quotient before the actual relative pushforward is proved.

Commuting shift pullbacks and tensoring preserve flatness by (4.2). In a finite tensor contraction with specified dual pairings, use J on a transported leg and its dual inverse on the paired leg. Interior adapters cancel exactly; the marked boundary vectors and evaluation functional must also move. This is an algebraic transport theorem for an already specified network. It does not assign such a network to every ideal triangulation or prove all its Pachner and turn relations.

9. The actual crossing is retained, not redefined

The actual positive and inverse crossings use the Gaussian factorial at Q and the color primitive

H_a(M^2)=(Q^(-1)M^2;Q^(-1))_a.

For example the positive finite Pochhammer equals

(Qx^2/Q^a;Q)_k=H_a(M^2)/H_(a-k)(M^2), M=-qx.

The Gaussian binomial is p_a(1)/(p_k(1)p_(a-k)(1)). These are exactly the regular factorial lattices of Section 2, in the two orientations. Substituting them in the independently derived crossing formulas gives every original matrix entry, including its Gaussian power, sign and endpoint support. At exceptional parameters cancel to the finite product first. Hence every finite cut contraction, with its original pivot and twist, is exactly the same actual ADO period. This local equivalence neither repairs the earlier positive factorial model’s failed 2-to-3 test nor supplies a new knot invariant.

The new relative data identify how the actual factorial cut can be included in a cyclic tetrahedron family with its source, cut-root cover and normalization. A global transport to the positive geometric triangulation must preserve this whole object and establish its nonzero geometric coupling. The selected regulator norm is still unproved.

The internal shift sigma and meridian shift delta have been distinguished throughout. This level-N factorial family is not being identified with the companion of the actual all-level meridian annihilator. In particular (4.3) is a local source-line norm, not an asserted factor of that operator’s spectral curve.

The subsequent common carry-lattice theorem now controls the complete factorial contraction of arbitrary finite recoupling trees at simultaneous degeneration. Its poles are exactly the integer carries floor(sum i_j/N), and its image lattice is independent of the tree. Local moves preserve the actual sublattice and the full cut functional. A wrapped crossing lift retains the wall sources and has exactly the original ADO cut trace at every meridian color. This resolves integral recoupling for that presentation; assignment to an arbitrary positive geometric triangulation and the selected regulator norm remain additional comparisons.

The subsequent controlled cyclic Euler-source calculation now gives another exact frame of this same factorial source. The coefficients of the existing determinant-normalized cyclic dilogarithm are a reflected phase transform of p_a(nu), multiplied by the specified scalar gamma_N. The entire cyclic source equation, local argument shift and co-source transform with it. A norm product fixes gamma_N, and a direct estimate bounds it and its inverse by powers of N on marked compact charts. Its source value is 1/N; the complete ADO phase remains eta_N. The mixed central pentagon retains its corner source. These are local fixed-root source comparisons; they do not identify the global meridian quantum-A extension or its selected geometric period.

10. Literature coverage and independent diagnostics

Cyclic quantum dilogarithms on Fermat covers are established definitions. The finite-product definition and shift convention are covered by Ip–Yamazaki, 1412.5777, Definition 3.3, including its displayed alternate normalization, and the original Kashaev framework already cited in the Verma transport note. No published pentagon or state-comparison proof is used here. All the source, lattice and adapter formulas follow from the finite products (1.1).

For coverage of the need for lifting data, Ishibashi’s 16 October 2026 correction, 2610.23572v1 states additional mu_N data and a general pentagon phase. Only its abstract was checked. Our N-step cut identity (6.1) is independently proved; it is not a proof that a global pentagon phase or geometric source comparison is settled.

The exact audit checks both flatness directions, both cyclic monodromies, the raw affine adapter, its non-scalar loop, and the strict normalized loop at N=2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12. It compares the cyclic primitive with the independent tetrahedron kernel and with N-th powers of the literature finite product. Eight tensor-wall tests check the necessary corner sources. It reconstructs 6,876 actual positive/inverse entries, 18 closed cut periods, and the entire modified dimension, including generic and Kashaev colors. All passed.
