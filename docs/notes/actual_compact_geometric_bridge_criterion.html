When the compact physical star locus can contain hyperbolic geometry

28 October 2026. The compact Gram intersection is not automatically a real-form locus of zero-volume representations. It CAN contain a discrete-faithful representation: an explicit algebraic figure-eight example is given below. There is also an all-width feasibility criterion, including a necessary common-circle condition on the chosen bridge eigenflags. Thus real compact accessibility depends on the actual bridge marking; it cannot be assumed for a broad knot class.

The original cap has an exact nonzero coefficient on every matched product packet, with no asymptotic normalization ambiguity. That computes the cap half of a weighted geometric overlap. It leaves the original braid transition amplitude, and possible cancellation in the full overlap, unproved.

1. The compact cap locus is an explicit bridge-generator real form

Use the original complete source and the bounded physical meridians of the cup/cap Gram theorem. A compact head character has

|k|=1, e=-conjugate(f), k+k^(-1)+ef=-2,
G=[[k,e],[fk,k^(-1)+fe]].                       (1)

An ordinary adjacent cap relation is H_2 H_1=I. By the triangular factorization, it says L_(-,2)L_(-,1)=L_(+,2)L_(+,1). One is lower triangular with top-left one; the other is upper triangular with bottom-right one. Consequently BOTH are I. All earlier paired L_- prefixes therefore reset after each cap. The chosen top bridge generators are precisely G_1,G_3,…, with their partners inverse.

Conversely for ANY G with data (1), the partner

k_2=k^(-1), e_2=-k^(-1)e, f_2=-k f             (2)

also satisfies the compact star conditions, and its two L products are I. Thus the compact top-cap locus is exactly the product of these explicit bridge-generator sets, with based partners inverse. This also includes G=-I, but a discrete-faithful meridian is noncentral.

Let a representation of a fixed k-bridge plat be given, and let mu_r be its CHOSEN oriented top bridge meridians, with trace two. It lies, up to simultaneous conjugation, in the compact physical intersection IF AND ONLY IF there exists one g in PGL2(C) such that, for EVERY r,

G_r=-g mu_r g^(-1)=[[a_r,b_r],[c_r,-2-a_r]],
|a_r|=1, c_r=-a_r conjugate(b_r).              (3)

Necessity is (1) and the prefix reset. For sufficiency put k=a_r, e=b_r,f=c_r/a_r, construct all partners by (2), and use the given plat representation relations. They are exactly the bottom Nielsen cap constraints. The same common conjugation, not independently chosen frames at the bridges, is essential. Formula (3) is a finite real-algebraic feasibility test at ANY width.

2. Equivalent common Hermitian-matrix criterion

There is a useful coordinate-free version of (3). It is equivalent to the existence of Hermitian matrices B,J such that

B>0, signature(J)=(1,1), J B^(-1) J=B,
mu_r^* J mu_r-J
      =-(1/2)(mu_r-I)^* B(mu_r-I), every r.     (4)

These are actual matrix conditions on the specified meridians. They do not require an arbitrary choice of a geometric action normalization.

Proof in the compact frame. Put J_0=diag(1,-1), B=I. If G is (1), write N=G+I. A noncentral N has null image/kernel line (1,t), |t|=1, and takes the form

N=a [[1,-t^(-1)],[t,-1]],
a=1+k, |a-1|=1.                              (5)

Direct multiplication gives N^J_0 N=0 and N*J_0+J_0N=NN/2. Hence

G^*J_0G-J_0=-N^*N/2,

which is (4), since mu=-G. Conversely for a noncentral trace-minus-two G satisfying that equation, apply it to a nonzero v in ker(G+I). It forces v^*J_0v=0. Thus v=(1,t), |t|=1. In (5), the same equation forces |a|^2=2 Re a, equivalent to |a-1|=1. This recovers (1). The central case is G=-I and is included directly.

Under a common conjugation g set B=g^g and J=g^J_0g. These satisfy (4). Conversely J B^(-1)J=B makes B^(-1/2)J B^(-1/2) a Hermitian involution of signature (1,1). A unitary diagonalization therefore produces g with precisely those B,J, reducing back to the compact frame. This proves equivalence.

3. A genuine all-width obstruction: bridge flags must be concyclic

Every noncentral compact G has its unique eigenflag (1,t) in (5), with |t|=1. Consequently ALL chosen bridge eigenflags of a compact representation must lie on ONE generalized round circle before conjugation. For four distinct flags every cross-ratio must therefore be real. Equivalently they must be null for one indefinite Hermitian form J. This is a necessary condition at arbitrary bridge number; it is not claimed sufficient without the amplitude conditions (4).

Thus a discrete-faithful representation whose chosen bridge flags have a nonreal four-point cross-ratio is absent from that particular compact physical star intersection. It could still contribute to the original scalar through a complex analytic saddle. Ordinary real Gram moment support would miss that exponential mechanism.

The criterion concerns the ACTUAL chosen bridge generators. A change of bridge presentation can change the set of flags. It is not a claim that a given knot has no other compactly accessible presentation.

4. Complete two-bridge criterion: an explicit cardioid

For two noncommuting noncentral trace-two parabolics put

tau=Tr(mu_1 mu_2)-2 !=0, phi=Arg tau in (-pi,pi].

Then (3) holds IF AND ONLY IF

phi!=pi, 0<|tau|<=16 cos^2(phi/2).             (6)

In particular a nonzero negative real tau is excluded. To prove this, write the two compact matrices using (5), with a_j=1+k_j and unit flags t_j. Their trace invariant is exactly

tau=a_1 a_2(2-t_1/t_2-t_2/t_1)
     =4 a_1 a_2 sin^2(delta/2),
a_j=2cos(alpha_j)exp(i alpha_j),
-pi/2<alpha_j<pi/2, alpha_1+alpha_2=phi.

Since cos(alpha_1)cos(alpha_2)<=cos^2(phi/2), this proves necessity. Conversely choose k_1=k_2=exp(i phi), a_1=a_2=1+exp(i phi), and t_1=1,t_2=exp(i delta), where

sin^2(delta/2)=|tau|/[16cos^2(phi/2)].

These give the prescribed invariant. Two irreducible trace-two parabolics are determined up to simultaneous conjugation by tau: use their distinct fixed eigenvectors as a basis, obtaining [[1,s],[0,1]] and [[1,0],[t,1]], then diagonally normalize s=1; the remaining parameter is st=tau. This proves sufficiency for the original pair, not only for some abstract representation.

5. Exact discrete-faithful prototype inside the compact intersection

Use the independently checked figure-eight Riley pair

A=[[1,1],[0,1]], B=[[1,0],[u,1]], u^2-u+1=0,
u=exp(i*pi/3),

from the marked holonomy proof. Set

a=1+u, z=(5+i sqrt(11))/6, z+z^(-1)=5/3,
G_1=[[u,-a],[a,-2-u]],
G_2=[[u,-a/z],[a z,-2-u]].                      (7)

Both matrices satisfy (1), and Tr(G_1G_2)-2=u. There is an explicit conjugation, not just matching dimensions:

F=[[a(z-1),1],[a(z-1),z]],
F A F^(-1)=-G_1, F B F^(-1)=-G_2.             (8)

Its determinant is nonzero. The identity for B uses a^2=3u and 3(z-1)^2+z=0. Thus this is the SAME discrete-faithful representation, at the recorded geometric embedding. All algebraic identities hold at BOTH Riley embeddings. Conjugating u,z and every displayed matrix gives the conjugate discrete-faithful place, including the positive-volume convention used in the earlier regulator calibration. Compact accessibility is therefore not confined to the opposite volume orientation.

With top meridians (G_1,G_1(-1),G_2,G_2(-1)), the actual Nielsen word for the four-plat [2,-1,2,2] satisfies BOTH bottom inverse-pair relations exactly. Symbolically their common Riley factor is u^2-u+1. After the top pair substitutions the remaining free-group relator is a cyclic permutation of the usual figure-eight relation a b^(-1) a^(-1) b a=b a b^(-1) a^(-1) b, so this is the same two-bridge presentation. Consequently the complete compact star intersection can contain a discrete-faithful point. It is not uniformly a trivial, unitary-group or zero-volume representation locus.

This is a prototype diagnostic of the GENERAL criterion, not a new figure-eight asymptotic proof or a broad accessibility claim.

The same prototype has NO wholly real compact source-to-source transport

There is an exact limitation even in this accessible case. The top compact point above does NOT imply that the actual incoming cup point pulled through W is also compact. In fact no simultaneous frame makes both endpoints compact for this discrete-faithful plat.

The other bottom bridge generator of [2,-1,2,2], in the canonical A,B frame, is

C=A^(-1) B A^(-1) B^(-1) A
   =[[2,u],[u-1,0]].

The three distinct fixed flags of A,B,C are 0,infinity,u-1 in the column-ratio coordinate. Their unique generalized circle has, up to a nonzero real scalar, Hermitian form

J_0=[[0,2-u],[1+u,0]], det J_0=-3.

Direct exact multiplication gives

A^*J_0A-J_0=diag(0,3),
B^*J_0B-J_0=diag(3,0),
C^*J_0C-J_0=0.                               (8a)

Any common compact frame for A,B,C would, by Section 3, have this same null circle. But (4) forces a NONZERO negative rank-one defect for every noncentral bridge meridian and every B>0. The last identity in (8a) contradicts it for C, regardless of the scale/sign of J_0.

Thus the bottom Nielsen cap equations in the weak Gram intersection are weaker than demanding that its transported input variables obey the same physical star real form. This is allowed because the original W is nonunitary: Ad_W does not preserve adjoints. The geometric source-to-source transition here must be treated by complex analytic propagation, not by a positive real canonical map between the two compact cup loci in this fixed physical polarization. New frame adapters could change that question, but would have to retain their source and exponential normalization. This explicitly locates a mechanism by which weak Gram support can fail to see the exponential geometry, even when its necessary intersection contains that representation.

6. The original cap packet weight is nonzero EXACTLY

The cap half of an admissible real compact overlap can also be computed without a formal Gaussian approximation. The ORIGINAL normalized cup has coefficients

phat_(i,j)=x^(-j)delta_(i+j,d)/sqrt(N).

For ANY unit vector v in C^N define its actual reflected dual by

(J_x bar v)_j=x^(-j) conjugate(v_(d-j)).

It is a unit vector and direct summation gives

<v tensor J_x bar v, phat>=N^(-1/2).           (9)

For k cups choose arbitrary unit v_r and put

z_0=tensor_r(v_r tensor J_x bar v_r),
z=G_top^* z_0, phihat=G_top^* uhat.

With ALL original pivot phases retained,

<z,phihat>=N^(-k/2).                          (10)

In particular cup-compatible localized product packets have a known nonzero cap coefficient, uniformly in their centers and N. This is the actual positive cap state fixed in the source theorem; it is not a new favorable cap or a division by the knot period.

There is an exact whole-source decomposition. Choose ANY orthonormal basis {v_(r,a)} on the first head of each pair and form the matched packets z_a above. They are orthonormal and

phihat=N^(-k/2) sum_a z_a,
P_N=chi_N N^(k/2-1) sum_a <z_a,W uhat>.        (11)

Thus every cap coefficient in this complete decomposition is the same positive polynomial factor. A single chosen packet may be included by extending its v_r to an orthonormal basis. The individual projector-weighted term is exactly chi_N N^(k/2-1)<z,W uhat>.

For (7) this includes localized packets at the geometric compact point. On each odd head take a Gaussian of width sqrt(N), center N/3-1/2, and phase exp(-i Arg(t_r)j), with t_1=1,t_2=z; normalize on j=0,…,d and use the exact reflected dual above on its partner. The K,e,f mean-square errors relative to (7) and (2) are O(1/N): the clock varies by O(N^(-1/2)) across the packet, the shift error is O(N^(-1/2)), and the endpoint tails are exponentially small. Applying the specified G_top^* changes the shift phase only by Q, so the same leading geometric point is retained. Its cap coefficient is STILL exactly (10), rather than merely a formal Gaussian unit. At the conjugate Riley place the same construction has center 2N/3-1/2 and the conjugated t_r; the source root and pivots remain the original ones.

Equation (11) does NOT say that its braid transition amplitudes have the same phase or that the geometric one is nonzero. Those amplitudes still contain the original whole W and can cancel in their sum. The bounded Nielsen constraints control their classical support, not their action-normalized coefficients. No exponential overlap estimate has been proved here. In particular one may not infer that a geometric point satisfying (3) has positive mass in the actual ket Gram limit.

The exact criterion (3)-(6) and cap amplitude (9)-(11) locate the next missing quantity: the original braid transition to a geometric matched packet, with its actual analytic cycle, and its cancellation against the other transitions. Weak real Gram support alone cannot supply it; when (3) fails it cannot see that geometric point at all.

Audit: exact algebraic compact figure-eight data and literal four-plat Nielsen equations, common Hermitian identity, the two-bridge cardioid construction, and exact original cup/pivot packet coefficients.
