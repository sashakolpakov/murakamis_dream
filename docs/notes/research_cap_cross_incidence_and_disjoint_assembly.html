Exact incidence for a cap/CROSS contraction, and what planarity does not force

31 October 2026. analytical research lemma. Unpublished. This concerns the original marked source and the fixed spectral pair (+2,-2). No general spectral-neighborhood estimate or filled-volume conclusion is asserted.

1. The literal two-port condition is quadrangulation degree two

Let T be the original simple white triangulated sphere, with its recorded pairing of triangles across MATCHING edges. Deleting those edges gives the original simple MAIN quadrangulation Q, with four distinct vertices on every port disk. Fix the original protected MAIN pair u,v.

Name one residual cap as

F=(u,A,v,C),(u,A,C),(u,v,C),matching edge uC. F=(u,A,v,C),\qquad (u,A,C),\ (u,v,C),\qquad \text{matching edge }uC. \tag{1}

The ordinary middle A is overloaded; C is the ordinary outer. LEFT, RIGHT and outer-reflected conventions have the identical incidence statement after relabeling. The protected vertices are still u,v, not new zeros introduced by a contraction.

For any vertex a, let d_T(a) be its triangle valence and let m_T(a) count matching edges incident to it. Then its number of original port incidences is

dQ(a)=dT(a)−mT(a). d_Q(a)=d_T(a)-m_T(a). \tag{2}

Each incident matching spoke joins exactly two consecutive triangles into one port; all other incident triangles contribute their own port. The four-distinct-vertices condition means that a port is counted once at a, never twice. Formula (2) also follows by deleting the matching spokes from the cyclic link of a.

The following are equivalent for the cap child A:

  • A occurs in no port other than F and one further port E;
  • d_Q(A)=2;
  • d_T(A)=3 and its unique matching spoke is AZ, where Z is the third vertex in the link of A besides u,C.

In that case the two remaining triangles at A are

(A,C,Z),(A,u,Z), (A,C,Z),\qquad(A,u,Z),

paired across AZ. Hence the other port is exactly

E=(A,u,C,Z). E=(A,u,C,Z). \tag{3}

up to the original middle/outer reflection conventions. This is the source typing required by the all-demand theorem after u=v=0.

For completeness, if A occurs at F and E only, F contains one triangle at A because A is its middle. E contains at most two. A vertex of a simple triangulated sphere has at least three triangles, so there are exactly three and E contains the other two, paired across AZ. The converse follows immediately from this full cyclic star. Equivalently, each matching spoke consumes two triangles and F consumes one unpaired triangle, so d_Q(A)=2 forces d_T(A)=3 and m_T(A)=1.

Valence three ALONE is not sufficient. The other two triangles can instead pair separately across uZ and CZ. Then all three spokes at A are MAIN and A belongs to three ports. For example, take the triangular bipyramid with apices A,B and equator u,C,Z, match its three equator edges, and mark MAIN edge uB. Its cap candidate is (u,A,B,C), while the other ports are (u,A,B,Z) and (C,A,B,Z), with harmless frame reflections if needed. Here d_T(A)=3 but d_Q(A)=3. This is an exact combinatorial distinction, not an assertion that this candidate is a high-value stationary cap.

2. At the classified maximum, the degree-two neighbor is CROSS

Assume the positive-value maximum classification: all ordinary incidences have positive load, ordinary demands are one, and ports are complete strict CROSS, strict straddling Green, or residual marked caps. At (1), its common positive weights satisfy L_A=L_C and the cap loads are q_A=1-nu, q_C=nu with 0<nu<1/2.

If d_Q(A)=2, the neighboring port has (3). A protected middle cannot be overloaded. The only possible Green child at E is therefore C. But its strict Green relation would imply L_C>L_A+L_Z, contradicting L_C=L_A. Thus E cannot be Green.

If Z is ordinary, E has only one protected vertex and cannot be a residual cap either. Consequently E is strict CROSS at spectrum -2, while F is the flat cap at +2, exactly the fixed pair of the signed contraction theorem. If Z=v is protected, the four displayed triangles are the whole tetrahedral sphere. E could then only be another cap or CROSS. Two caps saturate the two ordinary demands and have total sharp value zero; cap plus strict CROSS cannot supply the C demand. Neither occurs at the stated positive-value maximum. This also follows from the cap-cap exception in the odd MAIN-cycle theorem.

For the algebraic assembly below it is harmless to allow Z=v: setting Z=0 selects the missing C^d coefficient, since M+H=N-2<d. Both projected pair polynomials are identically zero. We do not assume that every typed finite pair has an ordinary Z.

There are at most two residual caps, corresponding to the two distinct triangles adjacent to the marked MAIN edge uv. If BOTH cap children have d_Q=2, their two-port patches are automatically port-disjoint at positive-value classified data. Each neighboring E is CROSS, so it cannot be the other cap. If the two E’s were the same port, that port would have one protected middle and one ordinary middle C. Both caps would consequently have the same ordinary C in their (u,v,C) triangle. But the two triangles incident to uv have distinct third vertices in a simple sphere. This is impossible. Their internal child vertices are also distinct and have no incidence in the other patch.

This conclusion is conditional on d_Q=2 for the cap children. The maximum classification itself has not proved that combinatorial condition.

3. Simultaneous replacement with arbitrary shared exterior variables

Fix any finite collection of port-disjoint typed pairs (E_i,F_i), each with its own ordinary A_i internal exclusively to that pair. The two protected vertices remain the original ones. Allow C_i,Z_i to coincide with remaining variables of other patches or of the unchanged exterior; within an individual port its four actual vertices remain distinct.

At the exact spectra E_i=-2, F_i=+2, retain the original CROSS and one-sided cap projections. Write

Bi(Ci,Zi)=[Aid]HFi,cap(+2)HEi,CROSS(−2). B_i(C_i,Z_i)=[A_i^d]\, H_{F_i,\mathrm{cap}}(+2)H_{E_i,\mathrm{CROSS}}(-2).

and B_i^ref for the positive paired reference after the SAME A_i^d extraction. At even levels this is the positive endpoint projection; at odd levels it is the actual fixed-root regularizer at both ports. The numerator is raw at both parities. The all-demand theorem supplies a factor kappa_N>=1 with log kappa_N=o(N), independent of the remaining degree n, such that

|Bi|coeff≤κNBiref. |B_i|_{\mathrm{coeff}}\le\kappa_N B_i^{\mathrm{ref}}. \tag{4}

The common unit source signs do not affect (4). All remaining degree coefficients are included: n=0,…,N-2 are controlled by the theorem, and the n=d coefficient is zero on both sides. Both polynomials have degree d in their remaining two variables. The original unused factors and normalizations are part of B_i and B_i^ref.

Let G_N be ANY complex polynomial in the remaining original face variables, with no A_i incidence; it can be the unchanged product of the other original spectral coupons at arbitrary fixed parameters. Denote coefficientwise absolute value by |.|_coeff, and let L^t be any remaining original target monomial. Exact extraction and the triangle inequality give

|[L𝒕]GN∏iBi|≤κNs[L𝒕]|GN|coeff∏iBiref,s=#{pairs},log⁡(κNs)=o(N). \left|[L^{\mathbf t}]\,G_N\prod_i B_i\right| \le\kappa_N^s[L^{\mathbf t}]\, |G_N|_{\mathrm{coeff}}\prod_i B_i^{\mathrm{ref}}, \qquad s=\#\{\text{pairs}\},\quad\log(\kappa_N^s)=o(N). \tag{5}

Indeed the A_i extractions commute and see only their own pair. Coefficientwise absolute value is submultiplicative, and positive coefficient inequalities remain valid when remaining variables shared by different patches are identified. Thus disjoint VARIABLES C_i,Z_i are not required. The internal A_i’s, however, really are exclusive: one cannot put an A_i-bearing exterior into this argument.

Every target in (5) is the original target d at an ordinary face and zero at a protected face. Each extracted pair still has its original remaining degree d. This is a conditional original-sector replacement, not a topological smoothing, an identity coupon insertion, or a proof that the reference/exterior contraction is below hyperbolic volume. It also gives no open spectral neighborhood of the pair (+2,-2), so it is not by itself a whole Fourier-period bound.

4. A structural obstruction to a universal two-port elimination

Neither spherical planarity nor the original matching forces a degree-two cap child. The family of bipyramids makes this failure explicit without a knot scan. Let T be the bipyramid over an n-cycle, n>=3, with apices A,B and equatorial vertices z_0,…,z_(n-1). Pair the two triangles on each equatorial edge. The MAIN quadrangulation is the embedded K_(2,n), with ports

(zi,A,B,zi+1) (z_i,A,B,z_{i+1})

up to cyclic frame choice. All quadrilaterals have four distinct vertices, and T is simple. Every MAIN mark joins one apex and one equatorial vertex. For mark B z_0, either possible residual cap has overloaded child A, whose original port-incidence degree is n. For mark A z_0 the roles reverse. Hence for n>=3 NO possible residual cap at ANY allowed MAIN mark has the required internal face. For n=3 this is already the valence-three counterexample in section 1.

There are also arbitrarily large simple spherical quadrangulations with minimum degree at least three: subdivide each face of a cube into an l by l square grid, consistently across cube edges. The eight old corners have degree three and all new vertices have degree four. There are 6l^2 port disks, each with four distinct vertices. The diagonalization theorem gives simple choices and the general Morse construction gives actual sources. In EVERY choice, Q and its vertex degrees are unchanged. Thus no marked cap can meet the two-port internal-face condition anywhere in this family, for any mark.

Euler only forces some quadrangulation vertex to have degree at most three, since its average degree is 4-8/V. It does not force degree two, and it certainly does not place that degree at a cap’s overloaded child. The cap-edge forest and demand-one inequalities concern a different graph; they cannot delete the other original port incidences of A.

These are structural obstructions to proving universal elimination solely from planarity and the known local source theorem. They are NOT examples of dominant non-CROSS maxima, counterexamples to the original volume conjecture, or a proof that a different multi-port block cannot work. A theorem forcing an applicable pair at every actual high maximum would first need an additional exclusion of caps on these incidence patterns. That exclusion is not contained in the existing activation, stationary, or odd-cycle classification.

5. Why an exterior A load is a genuinely different finite problem

At a classified maximum all ordinary incidences are positive. If d_Q(A)>2, any selected F plus one neighboring port leaves positive A-load in the exterior. At a fixed such maximum that load is a positive constant in normalized degree, not a one-unit endpoint error.

Even in the typed pair (3), keeping an exterior A-degree r changes the required extraction to A^(d-r). In the even notation of the exact original-demand convolution, the actual coefficient then becomes

[CnZd+r−n][Ad−r]HF(+2)HE(−2)=(common unit sign)∑j(−1)jUjCj−r,n−j,max⁡(r,n−H,0)≤j≤min⁡(M,n). \begin{aligned} &[C^nZ^{d+r-n}][A^{d-r}]\,H_F(+2)H_E(-2) =\text{(common unit sign)}\sum_j(-1)^jU_jC_{j-r,n-j},\\ &\max(r,n-H,0)\le j\le\min(M,n). \end{aligned} \tag{6}

The theorem controls r=0, not all these shifted diagonals. This is not the already covered variation of the remaining degree n.

In fact an unconditional shifted analogue is false. Set n=r; then the sum in (6) has only j=r and C_(0,0)=N^2. Its ratio to the paired endpoint coefficient is exactly U_r/V_r. For N divisible by eight, N>=16, and r=N/8, the original sine products give

UrVr=∏j=0rcot⁡2((j+1/2)πN)≥3N/8+1. \frac{U_r}{V_r} =\prod_{j=0}^{r}\cot^2\!\left(\frac{(j+1/2)\pi}{N}\right) \ge3^{N/8+1}. \tag{7}

This is precisely the uncontracted-pair obstruction, now expressed as the missing internal-demand shift. It uses a boundary CROSS monomial with zero ordinary incidences and therefore does NOT disprove a possible shifted estimate restricted to the positive-incidence stationary regime. It does show why (4) cannot simply be attached to an A-dependent exterior or promoted to an all-shifts identity.

Dependencies

The source bound is the all-demand, both-parity signed contraction, with the exact typing and coefficients in the original-demand convolution. The maximum hypotheses come from the finite-multiplier stationary classification and odd MAIN-cycle theorem. The actual general-source realization is the matched-sphere Morse construction; its simple-chart existence input is the maximal-diagonalization theorem.

The assembly requires the stated degree-two internal faces and exact unshifted demands. It does not assert that every stationary cap has that incidence pattern.

No numerical audit or purported global realization of a non-CROSS maximum is used. The false preliminary converse “valence three implies two ports” was corrected before preservation: the actual criterion is degree TWO in Q, with the matching condition retained. The conclusions are a conditional fixed-spectrum source replacement and an obstruction to a topology-only universal use of that replacement. They do not rule out an additional theorem restricting high-value stationary maxima, a larger-star contraction, or a spectral-neighborhood estimate.
