The original factorial endpoint has a nonzero Gaussian leading term

29 October 2026. The endpoint in the exact normalization/Green source has the following all-level asymptotic, with its phase fixed:

PN−1:=∑j=0N−1(Q;Q)j=N3/2exp⁡[iπ(14−N12+14N)]+O(N),Q=e2πi/N. P_{N-1}:=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(Q;Q)_j =N^{3/2}\exp\!\left[i\pi\left(\frac14-\frac N{12} +\frac1{4N}\right)\right] +O(\sqrt N),\qquad Q=e^{2\pi i/N}. \tag{1}

The implied constant is independent of every integer N>=2; no parity or residue class is omitted. In particular |P_(N-1)|>=N^(3/2)/2 for all sufficiently large N. This is a polynomial endpoint lower bound, not a positive hyperbolic-volume rate. Its use below is to keep a nonzero coefficient in an actual neighboring-crossing Green current.

1. A finite original-source formula

Write d=N-1, q=e^(pi i/N), F_j=(Q;Q)_j. The original source identity already proved in the terminal Green calculation is P_d=-q Phi_N(r_N^3), with its full Cartan and pivotal factors. For k=0,…,d put

ck=q2k+1+q−2k−1,Ak=q2k+1−q−2k−1,νk=(−1)kqk(k+1). c_k=q^{2k+1}+q^{-2k-1},\quad A_k=q^{2k+1}-q^{-2k-1},\quad \nu_k=(-1)^kq^{k(k+1)}.

The literal spectral-jet source gives the exact finite formula

Pd=q2∑k=0dνk3[ck−32(d−2k)Ak]. P_d=\frac q2\sum_{k=0}^{d}\nu_k^3 \left[c_k-\frac32(d-2k)A_k\right]. \tag{2}

To verify the fold, at every double node the original contribution is -nu_k^3[c_k-3(d-2k)A_k/2] to Phi_N(r_N^3). The index d-k has the SAME c and nu, and the opposite A and d-2k. Thus (2) counts each double contribution exactly once. When N is odd the remaining node has c=-2 and A=0; its half-sum contribution to Phi is precisely nu_k^3, the original simple-node term. N=2 is included. This uses the exact spectral-jet source proof, not a chosen semisimple trace or a fitted endpoint value.

Set s_k=(k+1/2)/N, and define fixed smooth functions on [0,1]

a(s)=(1−2s)sin⁡(2πs),b(s)=cos⁡(2πs),SN(A)=∑k=0N−1A(sk)eiπN(3sk2+sk). a(s)=(1-2s)\sin(2\pi s),\qquad b(s)=\cos(2\pi s),\qquad S_N(A)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}A(s_k)e^{i\pi N(3s_k^2+s_k)}.

Since nu_k^3=-i exp[-3pi i/(4N)] exp[i pi N(3s_k^2+s_k)], (2) becomes EXACTLY

Pd=eiπ/(4N)[−3N2SN(a)−iSN(b)]. P_d=e^{i\pi/(4N)}\left[-\frac{3N}{2}S_N(a)-iS_N(b)\right]. \tag{3}

In particular every phase needed in (1) is specified before taking a limit. The external original period line, if present, remains external; it is not used to change the finite endpoint (2).

2. An elementary half-grid quadratic-sum lemma

For a fixed smooth A on [0,1] with A(0)=A(1), finite Fourier inversion of its periodization gives

SN(A)=N∑m∈ℤ(−1)mIm,N(A),Im,N(A)=∫01A(s)eiπN[3s2+(1−2m)s]ds. S_N(A)=N\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^m I_{m,N}(A),\qquad I_{m,N}(A)=\int_0^1 A(s) e^{i\pi N[3s^2+(1-2m)s]}\,ds. \tag{4}

Indeed the periodized function A(s)exp[i pi N(3s^2+s)] is continuous at its endpoints, since exp(4pi iN)=1. Its piecewise smooth derivative has bounded variation, so its Fourier coefficients are absolutely summable: integrating twice, with the possible derivative jump retained, gives O_N((1+|n|)^-2). Summing its Fourier series at s_k keeps exactly the coefficients n=Nm, with factor exp(pi im)=(-1)^m. This proves (4), including the half-grid sign. The following estimates are uniform in m and N and justify its asymptotic use.

Only m=1,2,3 have stationary points in (0,1), namely

sm=(2m−1)/6,3s2+(1−2m)s=3(s−sm)2−(2m−1)2/12. s_m=(2m-1)/6,\qquad 3s^2+(1-2m)s=3(s-s_m)^2-(2m-1)^2/12. \tag{5}

There is no omitted endpoint resonance: the endpoint derivatives are the odd integers 1-2m and 7-2m, never zero. The endpoint PHASES still matter, and agree exactly at every integer N as used below.

If m is outside this set, the phase derivative has absolute minimum delta_m>=c(1+|m|)>0. Two integrations by parts give

|Im,N(A)|≤{CA/(N2δm2),A(0)=A(1)=0,CA/(Nδm2),A(0)=A(1). |I_{m,N}(A)|\le \begin{cases} C_A/(N^2\delta_m^2),& A(0)=A(1)=0,\\ C_A/(N\delta_m^2),& A(0)=A(1). \end{cases} \tag{6}

For clarity, the first boundary term in the second case is exactly A(0)/(i pi N) times [1/(7-2m)-1/(1-2m)]. Both endpoint phases are one, so this difference is O(delta_m^-2), rather than an unsummable O(|m|^-1). If A vanishes there, that term is zero; the next boundary and integral terms have the stronger N^-2 bound. All derivatives of A in (6) are fixed. Summing the nonstationary terms in (4) therefore costs O(1/N) in the first case and O(1) in the second.

At each of the three stationary points choose a fixed smooth cutoff inside (0,1). Completing the square gives

Im,N(A)=eiπ/43NA(sm)e−iπN(2m−1)2/12+{O(N−3/2),A(0)=A(1)=0,O(N−1),A(0)=A(1). I_{m,N}(A)=\frac{e^{i\pi/4}}{\sqrt{3N}} A(s_m)e^{-i\pi N(2m-1)^2/12} +\begin{cases}O(N^{-3/2}),& A(0)=A(1)=0,\\ O(N^{-1}),& A(0)=A(1). \end{cases} \tag{7}

Here is an elementary error proof. For a compactly supported smooth amplitude B in the centered variable, Fourier inversion and the damped Gaussian integral give

∫B(u)e3πiNu2du=eiπ/43N∫B̂(ξ)e−iπξ2/(3N)dξ. \int B(u)e^{3\pi iNu^2}du =\frac{e^{i\pi/4}}{\sqrt{3N}} \int\widehat B(\xi)e^{-i\pi\xi^2/(3N)}d\xi.

Subtracting B(0)=integral Bhat and using |exp(iv)-1|<=|v| bounds the error by C N^-3/2 integral xi^2|Bhat(xi)|dxi. This integral is finite. The identity follows first with Gaussian damping and then by its limit; compact support and rapid Fourier decay justify that passage. The portion outside the cutoff has no stationary point; the same integrations by parts as (6) give O(N^-2) for zero endpoints and O(N^-1) otherwise. This proves (7) without assuming a selected WKB branch or an asymptotic theorem for a knot invariant.

Combining (4),(6),(7), when A has zero endpoints one obtains

SN(A)=eiπ/4N/3∑m=13(−1)mA(sm)e−iπN(2m−1)2/12+O(N−1/2). S_N(A)=e^{i\pi/4}\sqrt{N/3} \sum_{m=1}^3(-1)^m A(s_m)e^{-i\pi N(2m-1)^2/12} +O(N^{-1/2}). \tag{8}

For a smooth amplitude with matching nonzero endpoints the same formula has error O(1); in particular S_N(A)=O(sqrt N).

3. The two surviving Gaussian terms reinforce at every level

For a(s) above,

a(1/6)=a(5/6)=3/3,a(1/2)=0. a(1/6)=a(5/6)=\sqrt3/3,\qquad a(1/2)=0.

The half-grid signs for m=1 and m=3 are BOTH minus. Their phases coincide at every integer N, since their quotient is exp(-2pi iN)=1. Thus (8) simplifies to

SN(a)=−23Neiπ/4−iπN/12+O(N−1/2). S_N(a)=-\frac23\sqrt N\, e^{i\pi/4-i\pi N/12}+O(N^{-1/2}). \tag{9}

The function b has matching endpoints, so S_N(b)=O(sqrt N). Substitution in the exact source equation (3) proves (1). There is no parity cancellation between the two leading points, and the possible odd simple node was already retained in (2).

4. A quantitative consequence for the actual Green current

The original first-order current obeys

Pt=Ft2[PdN2−∑r=t+1dQrFr2]. P_t=F_t^2\left[\frac{P_d}{N^2} -\sum_{r=t+1}^d\frac{Q^r}{F_r^2}\right]. \tag{10}

If t/N stays in a compact subinterval of (1/2,1), with t<=d, the tail in (10) is O(1/N^2) uniformly. To see this, reflect r=d-k: it is Q-1/N2 times a partial sum of Q^-k conjugate(F_k)^2. Here k runs only up to (1/2-delta)N. Its k=0 term is one, and the rest is uniformly bounded: up to a small fixed fraction of N the factorials decrease geometrically, while on a fixed interval below 1/2 they are exponentially small. Thus the partial absolute sum is O(1). This argument needs no cancellation in that reflected short tail.

Together with (1), this proves on every such compact interval

Pt=Ft2PdN2[1+O(N−3/2)],|Pt|=N−1/2|Ft|2[1+O(1/N)]. P_t=\frac{F_t^2P_d}{N^2}\,[1+O(N^{-3/2})],\qquad |P_t|=N^{-1/2}|F_t|^2[1+O(1/N)]. \tag{11}

The endpoint source in (1) therefore supplies a nonzero polynomial coefficient of the interior factorial current. It does NOT justify dropping that current or bounding it by its tame endpoint. This is the coefficient needed in an all-physical-input neighboring-crossing test; it does not assert that the full closed knot has a positive volume contribution from this sector.

A sharp all-input normal current of one complete original crossing

There is a direct local consequence, with an ordinary ORIGINAL pivotal closure and arbitrary outside spectators retained. Put g_b=theta Q^(-b), theta=x^(1-N), x=q^d, kappa=q(d2/2), and define

ℳ±(t)=Tr⁡2[(I⊗gtJ)R±],J|b⟩=b|b⟩. \mathcal M_\pm(t)=\operatorname{Tr}_2[(I\otimes g\,t^J)R_\pm], \qquad J|b\rangle=b|b\rangle .

This partial trace has NO extra factor1/N. Literal integer-degree conservation makes it diagonal on the remaining physical head. Its complete original coefficients are

ℳ+(t)=κθdiag⁡(Da(t)),Da(t)=Fa2∑b=0aQb2tbFa−bFb2, \mathcal M_+(t)=\kappa\theta\,\operatorname{diag}(D_a(t)),\qquad D_a(t)=F_a^2\sum_{b=0}^a\frac{Q^{b^2}t^b}{F_{a-b}F_b^2}, \tag{12} ℳ−(t)=κ−1θ−1diag⁡(tdDd−aQ¯(t−1)). \mathcal M_-(t)=\kappa^{-1}\theta^{-1} \operatorname{diag}\!\left(t^dD_{d-a}^{\overline Q}(t^{-1})\right). \tag{13}

The superscript in (13) replaces every Q coefficient by Q^(-1). Both known scalar Markov factors have modulus one. For (12), a diagonal positive R coefficient has transfer a-b and value kappa x(-2b)Q(b2)F_a2/(F_(a-b)F_b^2). The original pivot cancels x(-2b)=Qb. The negative formula follows from its literal inverse coefficient by b->d-b and factorial reflection; theta^2Q=1 retains its displayed phase. Thus these are full crossing/source identities, not new favorable traces. They tensor with every spectator identity.

The actual finite packets close under a second-order Ward recurrence:

D0=1,D1=1−Q+Qt,Da=(1+Q−2Qa+Q2a−1t)Da−1−Q(1−Qa−1)2Da−2. D_0=1,\quad D_1=1-Q+Qt,\qquad D_a=(1+Q-2Q^a+Q^{2a-1}t)D_{a-1} -Q(1-Q^{a-1})^2D_{a-2}. \tag{14}

One coefficient check proves this including all endpoints. For b<a, divide by the nonzero t^b coefficient in D_(a-1), and set u=Q^(a-b), v=Q^b. The required identity is

(1−uv)21−u=1−2uv+u+u2(1−v)21−u. \frac{(1-uv)^2}{1-u} =1-2uv+u+\frac{u^2(1-v)^2}{1-u}.

The top coefficient b=a is Q(a2) on both sides. Initial values therefore show D_a(1)=1 at EVERY physical a. At the other source endpoint, root reflection gives an exact whole-polynomial identity

Dd(t)=QtdHN(Q/t),HN(z)=∑jFjzj. D_d(t)=Qt^d H_N(Q/t),\qquad H_N(z)=\sum_jF_jz^j. \tag{15}

Its apparent inverse powers cancel, so it is a polynomial. The Ward equation for H_N at one gives Q^2H_N’(Q)=P_d-1, whence

ua:=Da′(1),u0=0,ud=N−Pd,ua−ua−1=QaPa−1. u_a:=D_a'(1),\qquad u_0=0,\quad u_d=N-P_d,\qquad u_a-u_{a-1}=Q^aP_{a-1}. \tag{16}

To verify the last equality directly, differentiate (14); its successive difference obeys v_a=Q(1-Q(a-1))2v_(a-1)+Q^(2a-1), v_1=Q. The original Green recurrence identifies v_a=Q^aP_(a-1) exactly. The retained current in (16), rather than a dropped exterior forcing, is what a neighboring crossing generates.

The original finite bound |P_j|<=N+4s_j^2 gives

$$ \max_a|u_a|\le N^2+4\sum_js_j^2,\qquad |u_a|\le4N^3(1+s_a^2)\quad\hbox{for every }a. \tag{17} $$

Here is a source-aware proof of the pointwise version. Let p=d-floor(N/6) be a factorial maximum index. If a-1<=p, sum (16) forward. The largest factorial on that prefix is at one of its endpoints, so |u_a|<=N^2+4N max(1,s_(a-1)^2). Use s_(a-1)<=N s_a/4. If a-1>p, sum (16) BACKWARD from the literal endpoint u_d=N-P_d; the remaining factorials decrease, giving |u_a|<=N+|P_d|+N2+4Ns_a2. The proved bound |P_d|<=N^2+floor(N/6) implies (17), with the generous constant displayed. The negative normal current has the corresponding bound with s_(d-a), by (13). No exponentially conditioned eigenframe is used.

Conversely choose p/N->5/6 with p+1<=d. Equation (11) and (16) give

maxa|ua|≥12|up+1−up|=12|Pp|≍N−1/2sp2. \max_a|u_a|\ge\tfrac12|u_{p+1}-u_p| =\tfrac12|P_p|\asymp N^{-1/2}s_p^2.

The uniform factorial estimate in (17) now proves the SHARP all-input first-normal norm for BOTH original signs:

$$ \boxed{\displaystyle \log\|\mathcal M_\pm'(1)\|_{\rm op} =\frac{v_3}{\pi}N+O(\log N).} \tag{18} $$

At phase one the same complete operators are only the known scalar Markov units. Nevertheless Cauchy’s derivative inequality implies that a uniform subexponential bound on a c/N COMPLEX disk, for every physical input, is impossible. More generally its supremum is at least exp[Nv_3/pi-o(N)] on every radius exp[-o(N)]. This does not contradict the specific normalization-clock disk theorem: that theorem keeps one specified recording/source packet, not every physical input of an opened normalization tangle. It also does not contradict a bound with the explicit factorial energy (17), a coherent multi-crossing reduction, or a statement on the actual fixed marked source trajectory. No off-phase change of the chosen cut is made here.
