When one shared positive Cauchy torus loses exponential accuracy

31 October 2026. The theorem concerns the full positive endpoint source on its shared face variables. It does not assert growth of the oscillatory invariant beyond volume.

1. The two different capacities

Fix an actual certified simple-white marked plat with RR ports and RR ordinary faces. Keep its adjacent protected faces exactly zero. Set hf=log⁡Lfh_f=\log L_f at ordinary faces and fix the common-scale gauge ∑fhf=0\sum_fh_f=0. Write

F(h)=∑eXe(eh)−∑fhf,J(h)=∑eHe(eh)−∑fhf,Xe=Q(xe,be,ye)+Q(xe,ce,ye),He=max⁡({Xe}∪{log⁡be:be>0}∪{log⁡ce:ce>0}). \begin{aligned} F(h)&=\sum_e X_e(e^h)-\sum_f h_f,\\ J(h)&=\sum_e H_e(e^h)-\sum_f h_f,\\ X_e&=Q(x_e,b_e,y_e)+Q(x_e,c_e,y_e),\\ H_e&=\max\bigl(\{X_e\}\cup\{\log b_e:b_e>0\} \cup\{\log c_e:c_e>0\}\bigr). \end{aligned}\tag{1}

The complete triangle theorem gives a unique minimizing gauge class h*h^* for FF, with

F(h*)=ΦD=Vol⁡(YD)2π. F(h^*)=\Phi_D=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)}{2\pi}.\tag{2}

In contrast, HeH_e is the weighted rate of the full positive endpoint polynomial, as proved in the closed half-block theorem, Section 2. All variables in each expression are shared across the original ports. Zero protected variables are removed from the supported corner sets.

Theorem. Both infima in (1) are attained on the scale quotient, and

minhJ(h)=ΦD⇔Xe(eh*)≥log⁡be,log⁡ce for every positive middle. \min_hJ(h)=\Phi_D \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X_e(e^{h^*})\geq\log b_e,\ \log c_e \text{ for every positive middle}.\tag{3}

In particular, a strict failure of this nonstrict packing condition at one port implies

minhJ(h)>ΦD. \min_hJ(h)>\Phi_D.\tag{4}

This concerns nonstrict balance in (3). Equality at a local target does not imply the strict condition used by the full filling theorem.

2. Coercivity from the actual triangle incidences

Let atf*a^*_{tf} be the normalized complete triangle angles. Each row has sum 1/21/2, every ordinary face has sum one, and every allowed ordinary corner is positive. A row with just one ordinary corner has its fixed value 1/21/2. For a real ordinary-face vector vv, define

B△(v)=12∑tmaxf∈t ordinaryvf−∑fvf. B_\triangle(v)=\frac12\sum_t\max_{f\in t\text{ ordinary}}v_f -\sum_fv_f.\tag{5}

Using the face balances of a*a^*, (5) is the sum of nonnegative row differences between a maximum and a strictly positive weighted mean. Equality forces vv to be constant on the allowed corners of every row. The ordinary-face adjacency graph is connected: removing the two marked vertices from a simple triangulated sphere leaves a connected graph. Every remaining white edge occurs in an actual triangle row. Hence equality forces all ordinary values to be equal.

On the unit sphere of the zero-sum gauge, B△B_\triangle is continuous and strictly positive. Thus, for some κ>0\kappa>0,

B△(h)≥κ‖h‖2. B_\triangle(h)\geq\kappa\lVert h\rVert_2.\tag{6}

The triangle Legendre identity, evaluated at a vertex with zero entropy, gives Q(ehf:f∈t)≥12max⁡f∈thfQ(e^{h_f}:f\in t)\geq\tfrac12\max_{f\in t}h_f. Consequently F(h)≥B△(h)F(h)\geq B_\triangle(h), and J≥FJ\geq F is also coercive. Both are continuous, so both minima are attained. If the gauge has dimension zero, the same attainment assertion is immediate, and the argument below does not require (6).

Uniqueness of the minimizer of FF can also be seen directly. On each supported row, the strictly concave triangle entropy has an interior maximizer at finite positive weights. Its Legendre potential is strictly convex in directions that are not constant on that row. Connectedness of the ordinary-face graph therefore makes the sum strictly convex modulo common scale. The actual complete angles give its critical point h*h^*.

3. Equality and strict loss

Since J≥F≥ΦDJ\geq F\geq\Phi_D, the nonstrict condition in (3) makes J(h*)=F(h*)=ΦDJ(h^*)=F(h^*)=\Phi_D and proves one direction.

Conversely, suppose min⁡J=ΦD\min J=\Phi_D and let h0h_0 attain that minimum. Then F(h0)≤J(h0)=ΦDF(h_0)\leq J(h_0)=\Phi_D, so uniqueness forces h0=h*h_0=h^*. Every term He−XeH_e-X_e is nonnegative. Their sum can vanish at h*h^* only if every positive middle logarithm is at most XeX_e. This proves (3), and (4) follows immediately. No local optimizers have been chosen independently and no minimax interchange is used.

4. Even level-dependent positive radii cannot remove this loss

For even NN, let d=N−1d=N-1 and let Pe,NP_{e,N} be the original full positive Fejer endpoint polynomial. Define its exact positive Cauchy bound

Cap⁡N=infLf>0∏ePe,N|Lu=Lv=0∏f ordinaryLfd. \operatorname{Cap}_N= \inf_{L_f>0} \frac{\left.\prod_eP_{e,N}\right|_{L_u=L_v=0}} {\prod_{f\text{ ordinary}}L_f^d}.\tag{7}

The infimum permits arbitrary NN-dependent radii. The uniform closed coefficient entropy theorem gives, uniformly on every compact set of ordinary logarithmic weights,

GN(h):=1Nlog⁡∏ePe,N(eh)∏fedhf→J(h). G_N(h):=\frac1N\log \frac{\prod_eP_{e,N}(e^h)}{\prod_f e^{dh_f}} \longrightarrow J(h).\tag{8}

This follows by finite positive Laplace summation: every local coefficient has its uniform closed entropy, the number of monomials is polynomial in NN, and the supported rational grids approximate the compact simplex. The difference between d/Nd/N and one is uniform on compact logarithmic sets. The exact weighted maximum is HeH_e, with its original normalization.

There is no escaping-minimizer loophole in (8). Each allowed pure-face monomial has degree dd and logarithmic coefficient o(N)o(N) uniformly over the finitely many vertices. Its coefficient entropy is zero. Keeping one such monomial at each port gives

GN(h)≥dNBport(h)−εN,εN→0, G_N(h)\geq\frac dN B_{\mathrm{port}}(h)-\varepsilon_N, \qquad\varepsilon_N\longrightarrow0,\tag{9}

where Bport(h)=∑emax⁡f∈e ordinaryhf−∑fhfB_{\mathrm{port}}(h)=\sum_e\max_{f\in e\text{ ordinary}}h_f-\sum_fh_f. The two triangle rows at each port show Bport≥B△B_{\mathrm{port}}\geq B_\triangle. Thus (6) and (9), together with the bounded value GN(h*)G_N(h^*), put all finite-level minimizers in one fixed compact set for sufficiently large NN. At each fixed level coercivity also gives attainment. Compact uniform convergence now proves

limN→∞N even1Nlog⁡Cap⁡N=minhJ(h). \lim_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\text{ even}}} \frac1N\log\operatorname{Cap}_N=\min_hJ(h).\tag{10}

Let ZNZ_N denote the actual original marked coefficient of this same positive product. The complete endpoint theorem gives N−1log⁡ZN→ΦDN^{-1}\log Z_N\to\Phi_D. Combining with (10) yields

limN→∞N even1Nlog⁡Cap⁡NZN=min⁡J−ΦD. \lim_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\text{ even}}} \frac1N\log\frac{\operatorname{Cap}_N}{Z_N} =\min J-\Phi_D.\tag{11}

At a strictly unbalanced complete packing this is a positive exponential loss in the best undivided positive Cauchy bound. It is not a claim of excess growth in the coefficient, which still has the actual volume rate. The statement is already decisive along even levels; no odd regularization is needed for this obstruction.

5. Consequence for the general strategy

One cannot repair a strict packing imbalance merely by optimizing a single positive torus for the full endpoint product, even with radii depending on the level. The proved anchored shared-radius theorem avoids this particular loss by separating genuine source sectors first. It is not contradicted by (4) or (11). The finite actual-sector cover theorem retains those common face constraints while permitting finer decompositions. A certified original source shows that the anchored test itself is not universal, although a finer cover succeeds.

The argument uses finite convex geometry, the original uniform local coefficient theorem and its actual endpoint volume calibration; it does not use a numerical optimizer as a proof.
