A stable exterior controls the whole protected spectral interval

This note proves a strict bound for one left contribution of the original finite source. All the neighboring crossing regions retain their actual central numerators at spectrum −2-2, while the selected spectrum ranges over the entire real interval [−2,2][-2,2]. The application to the original 54-region example holds along odd levels. It retains every original face coefficient and gives the strict inequality (31). It does not estimate all source sectors or prove a new knot-volume limit.

Here a source is the polynomial obtained from the literal finite crossing operators before its original face coefficients and filling functionals are applied. The central contribution contains paths through the middle degree; the left contribution contains paths below it. The original main mark sets two adjacent face variables to zero. These are called the protected variables. At the selected crossing the ordered arguments are (C,0,A,0)(C,0,A,0). Removing this one crossing and extracting every other ordinary face coefficient leaves a homogeneous polynomial in the two remaining face variables A,CA,C. This polynomial is the exterior of the selected crossing; no additional tangle or manifold is inserted.

The proof has two parts. A sine-Cauchy Gram matrix bounds the exact protected spectral functional. Log-concavity then controls the alternating truncated exterior on an unbounded integration interval. The original cutoff leaves enough room for its squared moment to exist. The full polynomial squared would exceed that moment range, and is never substituted for the truncation.

2. A finite alternating-section estimate

A real polynomial is called real stable when it does not vanish if every variable has positive imaginary part. A homogeneous binary real-stable polynomial with nonnegative coefficients factors into positive linear factors. For the finite estimate we use that explicit factorization: suppose the nonzero exterior has P(A,C)=P(1,1)∏r=1d[(1−λr)C+λrA],0≤λr≤1,P(1,1)>0. P(A,C)=P(1,1)\prod_{r=1}^{d}[(1-\lambda_r)C+\lambda_r A], \qquad 0\le\lambda_r\le1,\qquad P(1,1)>0. \tag{11} Write P(t)=P(t,1)P(t)=P(t,1), and define ν=1d∑rλr,k=1d∑rλr(1−λr),a=1−ν−k,b=ν−k. \nu=\frac1d\sum_r\lambda_r,\qquad k=\frac1d\sum_r\lambda_r(1-\lambda_r),\qquad a=1-\nu-k,\qquad b=\nu-k. \tag{12} Here kk is a finite polynomial response, not the protected spectral dual norm. For any fixed T≥1T\ge1, put RT=max⁡{a,b,1−4k,a/T+bT−2k}. R_T=\max\{a,b,1-4k,a/T+bT-2k\}. \tag{13} The following inequality is completely finite: ∥QM∥C≤1+log⁡TP(1,1)RTd/2+max⁡{P(T)/TM,(d+1)pM}+pM. \boxed{\quad \|Q_M\|_C\le \sqrt{1+\log T}\,P(1,1)R_T^{d/2} +\max\{P(T)/T^M,(d+1)p_M\}+p_M. \quad} \tag{14} It requires no lower bound for any coefficient, no bound for a coefficient ratio, and no moment of the full squared polynomial.

First, the coefficients in (11) have contiguous support and are log-concave. This follows directly by multiplying successively by positive linear factors. If (cj)(c_j) is log-concave and qj=ucj+vcj−1q_j=u c_j+v c_{j-1}, then qj2−qj−1qj+1=u2(cj2−cj−1cj+1)+v2(cj−12−cj−2cj)+uv(cjcj−1−cj−2cj+1)≥0. \begin{aligned} q_j^2-q_{j-1}q_{j+1} ={}&u^2(c_j^2-c_{j-1}c_{j+1}) +v^2(c_{j-1}^2-c_{j-2}c_j)\\ &+uv(c_jc_{j-1}-c_{j-2}c_{j+1})\ge0. \end{aligned} \tag{15} The last inequality follows from the ordering of consecutive ratios; endpoint zeros follow by continuity. Monomial factors merely shift the support.

The function log⁡P(ez)−Mz\log P(e^z)-Mz is convex: its second derivative is the variance of the exponent under the positive coefficient weights. If t0>Tt_0>T, its maximum on [log⁡T,log⁡t0][\log T,\log t_0] is at an endpoint. 3. A general original-target rate criterion

Consider a fixed original source and a sequence of levels on which its extracted exterior satisfies (11). Let Φ\Phi denote its complete augmented action, so that 2πΦ2\pi\Phi is the original augmented volume. Suppose, for one fixed T≥1T\ge1, limsupNN−1log⁡PN(1,1)≤F0,limsupNN−1log⁡(PN(T,1)/Td/2)≤FT,limsupNN−1log⁡pN,M≤Φ−γ,γ>0,νN→ν,kN→k. \begin{aligned} \limsup_N N^{-1}\log P_N(1,1)&\le F_0,\\ \limsup_N N^{-1}\log\bigl(P_N(T,1)/T^{d/2}\bigr)&\le F_T,\\ \limsup_N N^{-1}\log p_{N,M}&\le\Phi-\gamma, \qquad\gamma>0,\\ \nu_N\longrightarrow\nu,\qquad k_N&\longrightarrow k. \end{aligned} \tag{23} All coefficients and limits in (23) concern the same original extracted polynomial. Since d/2−Md/2-M is 1/21/2 or 11, replacing Td/2T^{d/2} by TMT^M changes only a bounded factor. Equations (10) and (14) prove limsupN1Nlog⁡supσ∈[−2,2]|SN(σ)|≤max⁡{F0+12log⁡RT,FT,Φ−γ}. \boxed{\quad \limsup_N\frac1N\log\sup_{\sigma\in[-2,2]}|S_N(\sigma)| \le\max\{F_0+\tfrac12\log R_T,\ F_T,\ \Phi-\gamma\}. \quad} \tag{24} In particular, if the first two entries are strictly below Φ\Phi, this is a strict source gap on the entire real cap spectral interval. Polynomial prefactors may depend on the fixed diagram; none changes (24).

For the original odd central exterior, stability is not an additional comparison assumption. At N=2m+1N=2m+1, each actual central endpoint numerator is N2Dm(x,b,y)Dm(x,c,y),Dm(x,b,y)=det⁡(bI+xH−/2+yH+/2), N^2D_m(x,b,y)D_m(x,c,y),\qquad D_m(x,b,y)=\det\bigl(bI+xH_-/2+yH_+/2\bigr), \tag{25} with the actual positive-definite Hermitian matrices H−,H+H_-,H_+. Each determinant in (25) is nonzero when its surviving arguments have positive imaginary parts: the imaginary part of its matrix is positive definite. Products and identification of shared variables preserve this property. For differentiation, fix the other variables in the upper half-plane and apply the one-variable Gauss–Lucas argument; specialization at a real boundary value follows by taking a limit from the upper half-plane. These operations preserve stability or give the zero polynomial. Coefficient extraction is differentiation followed by such a specialization. Thus the nonzero extracted binary polynomial has exactly (11), with all original factors N2N^2 retained. If it is zero, the bound is automatic. Borcea and Brändén’s study of stability-preserving operators provides general background for these closure operations. The quantitative finite inequalities used here are proved in sections 1–2.

The cutoff coefficient in (23) comes from the same omitted-tail capacity as in the original fixed-cap argument. Let R(s)R(s) be the residual complete triangle capacity with the two unextracted loads s,1−ss,1-s. The strict complete optimizer gives max1/2≤s≤1R(s)≤Φ−γD,limsupNN−1log⁡pN,jN≤R(s)if jN/d→s. \max_{1/2\le s\le1}R(s)\le\Phi-\gamma_D, \qquad \limsup_N N^{-1}\log p_{N,j_N}\le R(s) \quad\text{if }j_N/d\longrightarrow s. \tag{26} The second assertion is the closed coefficient upper bound, including boundary loads. Since M/d→1/2M/d\to1/2, it proves the third line of (23) with γ=γD\gamma=\gamma_D, even though MM lies just below the first omitted index. This uses no lower estimate and no asymptotic rounding.

5. Normals, exact scope, and proof inputs

The scalar in (9) is a polynomial of degree at most dd in σ\sigma. The complex-coefficient Markov inequality on [−2,2][-2,2] therefore gives ∥SN′∥[−2,2]≤d22∥SN∥[−2,2]. \|S_N'\|_{[-2,2]}\le\frac{d^2}{2}\|S_N\|_{[-2,2]}. \tag{32} To see that complex coefficients cause no problem, rotate the derivative at a point to a positive real number and apply the real Markov inequality to the real part of the rotated polynomial. Consequently the normalized first normal N−2SN′N^{-2}S_N' has the same strict rate. Alternatively the exact finite full-Hermite filling functional in this one cap variable has norm at most 1+|n|/21+|n|/2 for fixed signed nn, so it too preserves the strict rate. All other spectra remain fixed at −2-2 in these statements. Applying their filling functionals would require estimates not supplied here.

The actual even central exterior is not known to satisfy (11), and its positive stable comparator cannot replace it in a signed estimate. Accordingly (31) is odd-level only. The finite theorem (14), the local spectral metric (6), and the parity cutoff (5) themselves hold at both parities. The proof does not bound the beta-square spectator energy by e2NΦe^{2N\Phi}; it proves the desired spectral functional bound using the separate valid sine-Cauchy norm. It makes no assertion about multiple noncentral regions, varying neighboring spectra, an all-sector remainder, or a new actual knot-volume equality.

The proof uses the following exact source results.

  • The coupled protected spectral functional, sections 1–2, gives the unused-row vector, sine-Cauchy metric, and spectral divided difference. Its subsequent comparison with the beta-square measure is not needed here.
  • The original cap and its two negative-axis intervals, sections 1–4, gives the original exterior targets and the closed boundary capacity (26).
  • Factorization of the central cofactor, section 3, and the boundary model and its curvature give the actual odd central determinant pencils and their scalar normalization.
  • The exterior response criterion, sections 1–3, and the common-axis mean and response bound, sections 3–7, give the positive coefficient packet, its differentiated pressure, and the original complete-flow normalization. Only the response at zero is differentiated in the 54-region application here.
  • Restoring the omitted cap triangles gives a geometric sufficient response region, independent of the 54-region certificate. Its final section combines that region with the finite section estimate proved here; all neighboring spectra stay fixed.
  • The finite filling supremum bound gives the optional one-variable full-Hermite consequence following (32).
