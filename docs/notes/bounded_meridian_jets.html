Bounded meridian jets for a fixed quantum A-operator

Research note, 25 October 2026. A fixed recurrence needs only a bounded amount of meridian jet data to cross all singularities on a root orbit. The bound depends on the operator, not the level. There is no rank-one or regular-orbit hypothesis. The subsequent all-level period theorem uses coherence across levels to replace these potentially unknown meridian jets by controlled jets of finitely many fixed small colors in the quantum parameter.

The state and conventions are those of the ADO pointing calculation:

q_N=exp(pi*i/N), M_j=-q_N^j,
F_N(M_j)=C_j(q_N), 1<=j<=N,
F_N(1)=<K>_N, F_N(-q_N)=1.                       (0.1)

The C_j are the actual reduced fixed-color periods, not new initial conditions chosen to obtain a desired asymptotic. Their identity in (0.1) was independently proved by the filtered quotient-color argument in the root-orbit note.

1. A uniform bound on singularities

Take a specified nonzero reduced-state operator

P=sum_(i=0)^d a_i(q,M)T^i, T h(M)=h(qM), d>=1.    (1.1)

Clear its rational denominators once, retaining the fixed operator and the state normalization, so all a_i are Laurent polynomials. Let D be the M-exponent span of a_d. For all sufficiently large N, a_d(q_N,M) is a nonzero Laurent polynomial and its exponent span is at most D. The excluded levels are finite: any one nonzero q-coefficient of a_d has only finitely many nonzero roots.

Use the same local coordinate at each orbit point:

M=M_j(1+z), M_j=-q_N^j.                           (1.2)

The points M_j, 1<=j<=2N, are distinct. The sum of the multiplicities of all nonzero roots of a_d(q_N,M) is at most D. In particular,

S_N=sum_(j=1)^(N-d) ord_(M=M_j) a_d(q_N,M) <= D.  (1.3)

This is a count with multiplicity. It does not require a torsion-point classification, primeness of N, or estimates on nearly vanishing factors.

2. Exact recovery from finitely many initial jets

Write the forward companion equation on column states as

V_j(z)=(F_N(M_j(1+z)),...,F_N(M_(j+d-1)(1+z)))^t,
V_(j+1)(z)=B_j(z)V_j(z).

The superdiagonal of B_j is one; its last row is (-a_0/a_d,…,-a_(d-1)/a_d), evaluated at (q_N,M_j(1+z)). Each B_j has pole order at most the j-th summand in (1.3). Thus

r_N(z)=e_d^t B_(N-d)(z)...B_1(z)
      =sum_(k=-S_N)^infinity r_(N,k) z^k.        (2.1)

Let

J_k=([z^k]F_N(M_1(1+z)),...,
     [z^k]F_N(M_d(1+z)))^t.

All these are ordinary meridian germs of the actual Laurent polynomial state. The scalar r_N(z)V_1(z) is regular and equals F_N(1+z). Hence

<K>_N=sum_(k=0)^S_N r_(N,-k) J_k.                (2.2)

More generally the output jet of order h needs initial jets only through S_N+h, and therefore through D+h. All negative coefficients of r_N V_1 vanish by the equation and regularity of the actual state; they are compatibility equations, not an excuse to evaluate an undefined quotient.

This gives at most d(D+1) scalar initial data for the value, independent of N. When the orbit is regular, S_N=0 and (2.2) is the ordinary initial value formula. No claim that its higher meridian jets are small is made.

For computation one can avoid multiplying Laurent matrices. Put C_j=a_d(q_N,M_j(1+z)) B_j and multiply the polynomial matrices C_j, together with the complete scalar denominator product. If its order at z=0 is S_N, retain the numerator through order S_N+h and divide by the denominator’s unit part. This computes the same whole germ. For fixed d,D,h this requires O(N) field operations up to a constant depending on the fixed truncation and matrix sizes.

3. An intrinsic bound on the integral kernel defect

The same statement applies to the actual integral solution lattice, not just a selected scalar period. Put n=2N, u=M^n, O=C[u]_(u-1), and represent P on E=C(M) over C(u), as in the cofactor construction. Let

Lambda=ker_(C(u)) P intersection O^n.

This is a saturated free O-module. Complete and use u=(1+z)^n, R=C[[z]]. Evaluation at the n root germs M_j(1+z) is an invertible Vandermonde change of basis over R. It identifies Lambda_R with a saturated submodule of R^n.

Choose an integral basis, with matrix G(z) of size n by r, and let A(z) be its first d rows. Generic recurrence propagation shows r<=d and A has column rank r over C((z)). Its Smith exponents obey

0<=b_1<=...<=b_r<=D.                             (3.1)

Here is a proof of the bound. Propagation through the whole orbit uses each point at most once; the total pole budget is still at most D. Therefore G=L A for a meromorphic n by d matrix L with z^D L integral. Since G is saturated, it has an integral left inverse Q. Consequently

(Q z^D L) A=z^D I_r.

Smith normal form implies every z^b_i divides z^D, proving (3.1). In particular if two integral solutions have the same initial meridian jets through D, their difference vanishes on the entire central fiber. The order-h version follows by the same calculation.

This is the precise bounded extension data in the kernel lattice. It is a genuine linear solution module. No module structure is being inferred from a nonlinear condition on proposed scalar sections.

The bound can be sharp: for f=(M+q)^D and P=f(M)T-f(qM), the solution lattice has rank one, but its first evaluation has order D. Its first nonzero initial jet is the D-th. This example has zero reference value; it does not contradict the earlier rank-one theorem with a nonzero anchor.

4. Ordinary initial values can miss a jet

Consider the fixed second-order operator

P=(M+q)T^2-[(q^2+1)M+q(q+1)]T+q^2(M+1).         (4.1)

It annihilates both 1 and f=(M+q)(M+q^2). At a root of order 2N, N>=3, all rational solutions are C(u)-linear combinations of these two functions. Indeed, putting g=sigma(f)-f, its equation is

g*sigma(Delta h)-sigma(g)*Delta h=0.

Thus Delta h=c(u)Delta f and h=b(u)+c(u)f.

The basis (1,f) is integral and saturated: at the first and third orbit points its evaluation minor is a unit. At the first two points the minor has order one, since

[z] (f(-q^2(1+z))-f(-q(1+z)))=q^2(q^2-1).

The Smith exponents are (0,1). Every h_N=1+c_N f has both ordinary initial values equal to one, yet

h_N(1)=1+c_N(1+q_N)(1+q_N^2).

The exact missing datum is

h_N(1)=1+[(1+q)(1+q^2)/(q^2(q^2-1))]
             *([z]h_N(-q^2(1+z))-[z]h_N(-q(1+z))). (4.2)

Taking c_N=a^N, a>1, gives logarithmic rate log(a), with the same fixed operator and the same two ordinary initial values. These are manufactured states, not knot invariants. The example explains why an independent bound on meridian jets cannot be silently assumed. The all-level theorem below uses additional coherence actually available for the knot state.

5. The full ADO normalization keeps its residue and first jet

Keep the exact factor

N_N=d_N F_N,
d_N=eta_N*(M-M^-1)*M^N/(M^(2N)-1), eta_N=i^(N+3).

The regular numerator is

R_N=(u-1)N_N=eta_N*(M-M^-1)*M^N F_N.

Its reference residue and target jet are

R_N(-q)=eta_N*(-1)^N*(q-q^-1),
[z]R_N(1+z)=2 eta_N <K>_N.                      (5.1)

These follow by direct substitution; the second retains the zero of M-M^-1 until differentiation. The factor M^N has shift character -1, and the exact generic full/reduced gauge is

d_N(qM)/d_N(M)=-(qM-q^-1 M^-1)/(M-M^-1).

On the initial d colors, N>d, the diagonal multiplication by eta_N*(M-M-1)*MN is a meridian unit. At the target it has one zero. Thus the full target transfer row acquires a factor z and the required output first jet compensates for that zero: transport under this actual gauge needs no additional initial jet beyond the reduced bound D. Using a general full-state operator directly also gives the weaker bound D_full+1 by Section 2.

A central unit equal to one at u=1 cannot change (2.2) or (5.1): the whole output is regular, and its value, or first coefficient after the target zero, only sees that unit’s constant term. This is distinct from erasing a generic peripheral factor.

6. Coverage, checks, and the remaining comparison

Desingularization of q-difference equations and systems is existing coverage: Koutschan–Zhang, 1801.04160 and Barkatou–Jaroschek, 1802.01150. Only their stated scope was consulted here. No algorithm or proof from them is a premise; the degree, propagation, and Smith arguments above are complete. No novelty claim is made.

The exact diagnostics check 30 second-order examples, including higher contact orders and the full-state gauge, and 28 examples attaining the first-order bound. Even and composite levels are included. The results are diagnostics of these uniform proofs, not a knot-class or volume test.

For a specified actual annihilator, the finite-jet bound is now unconditional in its root-level rank. The subsequent finite cut-state proof now constructs a nonzero actual all-level annihilator for every knot in principle. Its geometric symbol still requires independent identification. The next theorem removes the unknown meridian jets from the exact period formula using cross-level polynomial coherence. It does not establish the geometric regulator norm.
