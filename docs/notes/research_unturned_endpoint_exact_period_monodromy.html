Exact period monodromy of the unturned endpoint recurrence

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The ordinary unturned recurrence has an explicit two-dimensional monodromy over one full angular period. Its trace follows from the entire characteristic polynomial of a generic cyclic orbit, including the repeated physical eigenvalues. This supplies branch data for a possible homogeneous scalar continuation. It does not choose that continuation’s actual source coefficients or bound them.

1. Ordinary recurrence and generic orbit

Put N≥2,h=N−1,θ=π/N,sr=2sin⁡(rθ),cr=2cos⁡((2r+1)θ), N\ge2,\quad h=N^{-1},\quad \theta=\pi/N,\quad s_r=2\sin(r\theta),\quad c_r=2\cos((2r+1)\theta), cN(x)=2cos⁡(2πx+πh),PN(w)=2TN(w/2)+2=∏j=0N−1(w−cj).(1) c_N(x)=2\cos(2\pi x+\pi h),\qquad P_N(w)=2T_N(w/2)+2=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(w-c_j). \qquad\text{(1)} The original ordinary difference operator is 𝒥NF=(c0+AN+BN)F−ANF(x+h)−BNF(x−h), \mathscr J_NF=(c_0+A_N+B_N)F-A_NF(x+h)-B_NF(x-h), AN(x)=sNx+14s2Nx+1s2Nx+2,BN(x)=sNx4s2Nxs2Nx+1.(2) A_N(x)=\frac{s_{Nx+1}^{\,4}}{s_{2Nx+1}s_{2Nx+2}}, \qquad B_N(x)=\frac{s_{Nx}^{\,4}}{s_{2Nx}s_{2Nx+1}}. \qquad\text{(2)} These are meromorphic coefficient functions of xx, of period one. This is the ordinary operator, not a relation imposed only after canonical reduction.

Initially fix xx with 2Nx∉ℤ.(3) 2Nx\notin\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(3)} Then every coefficient on the orbit x+lhx+lh, l∈ℤl\in\mathbb Z, is finite, and AN,BNA_N,B_N there are nonzero. The NN spectral points cN(x+lh)c_N(x+lh), 0≤l<N0\le l<N, are distinct. Indeed, equality of two cosine values would imply either equality of their indices modulo NN, or 2Nx+l+k+1∈Nℤ2Nx+l+k+1\in N\mathbb Z, excluded by (3).

For a scalar solution of (𝒥N−w)F=0(\mathscr J_N-w)F=0, use the state YF(x)=(F(x)F(x−h)),YF(x+h)=TN(x,w)YF(x), Y_F(x)=\binom{F(x)}{F(x-h)},\qquad Y_F(x+h)=T_N(x,w)Y_F(x), TN(x,w)=((c0+AN(x)+BN(x)−w)/AN(x)−BN(x)/AN(x)10).(4) T_N(x,w)= \begin{pmatrix} (c_0+A_N(x)+B_N(x)-w)/A_N(x)&-B_N(x)/A_N(x)\\ 1&0 \end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(4)} Its period transfer is the ordered product MN(x,w)=TN(x+(N−1)h,w)⋯TN(x,w).(5) M_N(x,w)=T_N(x+(N-1)h,w)\cdots T_N(x,w). \qquad\text{(5)} In particular no inverse-period or opposite-power convention enters this definition.

2. The full twisted cyclic determinant

Write Al=AN(x+lh)A_l=A_N(x+lh), Bl=BN(x+lh)B_l=B_N(x+lh), and aN(x)=(−1)N∏l=0N−1Al.(6) a_N(x)=(-1)^N\prod_{l=0}^{N-1}A_l. \qquad\text{(6)} The sine products give the exact identities ∏l=0N−1BlAl=1,aN(x)=tan⁡2(πNx).(7) \prod_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{B_l}{A_l}=1,\qquad a_N(x)=\tan^2(\pi Nx). \qquad\text{(7)} For the first, the fourth powers in Bl/Al=sNx+l4s2Nx+2l+2/[sNx+l+14s2Nx+2l]B_l/A_l=s_{Nx+l}^4s_{2Nx+2l+2}/ [s_{Nx+l+1}^4s_{2Nx+2l}] telescope over a period. For the second, put ρ=Nx\rho=Nx. The numerator of the product of the AlA_l is 16sin⁡4(πρ)16\sin^4(\pi\rho). Its denominator is ∏m=12Ns2ρ+m=(−1)N(∏m=1Ns2ρ+m)2=(−1)N4sin⁡2(2πρ).(8) \prod_{m=1}^{2N}s_{2\rho+m} =(-1)^N\left(\prod_{m=1}^Ns_{2\rho+m}\right)^2 =(-1)^N\,4\sin^2(2\pi\rho). \qquad\text{(8)} Here ∏m=1N2sin⁡((z+m)π/N)=2sin⁡(π(z+1))\prod_{m=1}^N2\sin((z+m)\pi/N)=2\sin(\pi(z+1)) was used before squaring. Equations (6)–(8) prove (7) at both parities.

For μ≠0\mu\ne0, let Jμ(x)J_\mu(x) be the NN-dimensional cyclic operator (2), with the boundary convention Fl+N=μFl.(9) F_{l+N}=\mu F_l. \qquad\text{(9)} Thus the forward term in row N−1N-1 carries μ\mu, and the backward term in row zero carries μ−1\mu^{-1}.

Lemma. For every N≥2N\ge2, on the generic set (3), det⁡(wI−Jμ(x))=PN(w)+aN(x)(2−μ−μ−1).(10) \boxed{\quad \det(wI-J_\mu(x)) =P_N(w)+a_N(x)(2-\mu-\mu^{-1}). \quad} \qquad\text{(10)}

Proof. First take μ=1\mu=1. The original Newton polynomials ek(C)=∏r<k(cr−C)∏r=1ksr2,0≤k<N, e_k(C)=\frac{\prod_{r<k}(c_r-C)} {\prod_{r=1}^k s_r^2},\qquad 0\le k<N, form a basis of the polynomials of degree less than NN. The ordinary source identity gives 𝒥N[ek(cN(x))]=ckek(cN(x))−sk2ek−1(cN(x)),(11) \mathscr J_N[e_k(c_N(x))] =c_k e_k(c_N(x))-s_k^2e_{k-1}(c_N(x)), \qquad\text{(11)} where the last term is zero at k=0k=0. Evaluation on the NN distinct points in section 1 is invertible. Since these evaluated polynomials are period one in xx, it conjugates J1(x)J_1(x) to the triangular matrix in (11). Consequently det⁡(wI−J1(x))=PN(w).(12) \det(wI-J_1(x))=P_N(w). \qquad\text{(12)} This is a characteristic-polynomial identity. In particular every double eigenvalue and the odd simple eigenvalue has its original multiplicity; vanishing at the distinct physical roots alone would not prove (12).

For N≥3N\ge3, the determinant expansion has only two terms with uncanceled winding across the cyclic boundary. The all-forward permutation contributes (−1)N−1μ∏lAl=−aNμ(-1)^{N-1}\mu\prod_lA_l=-a_N\mu, and the all-backward permutation contributes −aNμ−1-a_N\mu^{-1}, using (7). Every other permutation has winding zero, so its boundary factors cancel. This follows also by decomposing a permutation into cycles: a nearest-neighbor cycle with nonzero winding must traverse all NN vertices. Comparing with μ=1\mu=1 proves (10).

At N=2N=2, the two shifted neighbors occupy the same matrix entry. The off-diagonal entries of wI−JμwI-J_\mu are A0+B0μ−1,B1+A1μ. A_0+B_0\mu^{-1},\qquad B_1+A_1\mu. Their product has the μ\mu-dependent part A0A1μ+B0B1μ−1A_0A_1\mu+B_0B_1\mu^{-1}. Since aN=A0A1=B0B1a_N=A_0A_1=B_0B_1, subtracting its value at μ=1\mu=1 again gives (10). ▫\square

3. Exact monodromy and its branch information

Theorem. For every N≥2N\ge2, the period transfer satisfies det⁡MN(x,w)=1,tr⁡MN(x,w)=2+PN(w)cot⁡2(πNx).(13) \boxed{\qquad \det M_N(x,w)=1,\qquad \operatorname{tr}M_N(x,w) =2+P_N(w)\cot^2(\pi Nx). \qquad} \qquad\text{(13)} The assertion initially holds under (3) and then as a meromorphic identity in x,wx,w.

Proof. The determinant in (4) is BN/ANB_N/A_N, so (7) gives the first assertion. A nonzero state with period multiplier μ\mu exists exactly when either MN−μIM_N-\mu I or wI−JμwI-J_\mu is singular: the recurrence and the two boundary conditions (9) identify their kernels. Multiplying (10) by μ\mu gives μdet⁡(wI−Jμ)=−aN[μ2−(2+PN(w)aN)μ+1].(14) \mu\det(wI-J_\mu) =-a_N\left[ \mu^2-\left(2+\frac{P_N(w)}{a_N}\right)\mu+1\right]. \qquad\text{(14)} For generic ww, this quadratic has distinct roots; they are the two roots of the monic characteristic polynomial of MNM_N. The two monic quadratics therefore agree. Polynomial continuation in ww includes the repeated-root cases, and (7) gives (13). All quantities are meromorphic, which gives the stated continuation in xx. ▫\square

If a homogeneous scalar is continued so that its recurrence and all the required shifted values are defined, Cayley–Hamilton gives F(x+1)+F(x−1)={2+PN(w)cot⁡2(πNx)}F(x).(15) \boxed{\quad F(x+1)+F(x-1) =\{2+P_N(w)\cot^2(\pi Nx)\}F(x). \quad} \qquad\text{(15)} Indeed YF(x+1)=MN(x,w)YF(x)Y_F(x+1)=M_N(x,w)Y_F(x), and period one of the coefficients gives YF(x−1)=MN(x,w)−1YF(x)Y_F(x-1)=M_N(x,w)^{-1}Y_F(x). Equation (15) is not an assertion that a function originally given on a smaller rectangle already has such a continuation.

For the actual microscopic parameter w=2cosh⁡(α/N)w=2\cosh(\alpha/N), PN(w)=4cosh⁡2(α/2).(16) P_N(w)=4\cosh^2(\alpha/2). \qquad\text{(16)} On either horizontal line |Im⁡x|=H>0|\operatorname{Im}x|=H>0, uniformly in Re⁡x\operatorname{Re}x and bounded complex α\alpha, tr⁡MN(x,w)=−2cosh⁡α+OH(e−2πNH).(17) \operatorname{tr}M_N(x,w) =-2\cosh\alpha+O_H(e^{-2\pi NH}). \qquad\text{(17)} Thus its two multipliers tend, as an unordered pair with multiplicity, to −eα,−e−α-e^\alpha,-e^{-\alpha}. On parameter sets avoiding their collisions, individual locally labeled multipliers have the same exponential error as (17). At a collision only the multiset convergence is asserted here.

There is also an exact elementary obstruction to an overly restrictive choice of continuation. If PN(w)≠0P_N(w)\ne0, a solution which is period one in xx on a full generic orbit must vanish: (15) would give PN(w)cot⁡2(πNx)F(x)=0P_N(w)\cot^2(\pi Nx)F(x)=0. Equivalently J1(x)−wJ_1(x)-w is invertible by (12). Consequently a nonzero homogeneous continuation cannot be a globally single-valued function of e2πixe^{2\pi ix} extending regularly around that whole orbit. A local branch on a buffered half-strip is not excluded. The same distinction applies to a proposed root-of-unity hypergeometric or factorial formula.

At PN(w)=0P_N(w)=0, the trace in (13) is exactly two. This does not imply that MN=IM_N=I; a nontrivial Jordan part may remain. The actual matrix and its Jordan data must be retained; the trace identity does not justify a semisimple replacement.

4. Actual source and remaining estimate

The ordinary full kernel is not a homogeneous solution at generic microscopic ww. The pinned original source gives (w−𝒥N)KN(cN(x),w)=ηPN(w)N2eN−1(cN(x))=PN(w)PN(cN(x))N4(cN(x)−c0),η=(−1)N−1.(18) (w-\mathscr J_N)K_N(c_N(x),w) =\frac{\eta P_N(w)}{N^2}e_{N-1}(c_N(x)) =\frac{P_N(w)P_N(c_N(x))} {N^4(c_N(x)-c_0)},\qquad \eta=(-1)^{N-1}. \qquad\text{(18)} The removable interpretation in (18) is the original polynomial one. On a physical bulk window the right side and its first normal vanish, but it cannot be deleted off that grid.

The sharp Green budget in the second pinned source retains both the baseline’s ordinary defect and its actual right boundary packet on every fractional grid. Equation (13) fixes a necessary period-transfer invariant of any exact homogeneous replacement. It does not determine which linear combination or periodic coefficient functions match the kernel’s physical jets. Nor does it bound such coefficients on a tall rectangle, establish the sharp JδJ_\delta comparison, or estimate the complementary original currents. No actual source coefficient, nonvanishing term, or filled-action estimate is inferred from (13)–(17).

5. Coverage and complete direct sources

The ordinary Askey–Wilson recurrence, its polynomial triangular action, and the retained full-kernel forcing are prior results. The additional calculation here is its exact twisted cyclic determinant and full-period transfer trace, with both parity multiplicities and the N=2N=2 case retained.
