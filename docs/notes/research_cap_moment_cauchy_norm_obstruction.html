An exponential obstruction to the bare cap-moment Cauchy estimate

This obstruction keeps the original even-level demand. It disproves the proposed strengthening by a positive Cauchy norm, not the signed convolution, a shared source estimate, or a volume-conjecture limit.

3. The exact endpoint comparator rate

The uniform closed endpoint coefficient theorem in uniform half-block and triangle coefficient theorem and continuous full-kernel coefficient theorem applies to the actual coefficients (1). On a triangle of total load 1/21/2, its entropy is the sum of ff at the three corner loads. The CROSS rate is the maximum of the sum over its two genuine triangles, with one shared outer split. Finite forced degree shifts and polynomial scalar factors are already covered by that uniform closed theorem.

4. A strictly positive excess and its precise limitation

The explicit product (7) yields limN→∞1Nlog⁡|κn|=∫u2ulog⁡sin⁡(πt)dt−∫0ulog⁡sin⁡(πt)dt=k(u). \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|\kappa_n| =\int_u^{2u}\log\sin(\pi t)\,dt -\int_0^u\log\sin(\pi t)\,dt =k(u). \tag{14} For the last equality, rewrite the difference as ∫02ulog⁡sin⁡(πt)dt−2∫0ulog⁡sin⁡(πt)dt\int_0^{2u}\log\sin(\pi t)\,dt -2\int_0^u\log\sin(\pi t)\,dt, then use sine doubling. The endpoint logarithm is integrable; upper and lower sums on the two monotonicity intervals, with its first cell integrated directly, justify these shifted Riemann sums. The error from the finite products to the same integrals with endpoint n/Nn/N is O(log⁡N/N)O(\log N/N); continuity then handles arbitrary n/N→un/N\to u, including integer floors. Combining (8), (9), and (12)–(14) proves the lower bound in (5).

The excess is strictly positive by a direct derivative identity. It is continuous at zero with χ(0)=0\chi(0)=0, and χ′(u)=2f′(u/2)−2f′(u)−2log⁡(2cos⁡(πu))=2log⁡1+cos⁡(πu)2cos⁡2(πu)>0(0<u≤1/4). \begin{split} \chi'(u) &=2f'(u/2)-2f'(u)-2\log(2\cos(\pi u))\\ &=2\log\frac{1+\cos(\pi u)}{2\cos^2(\pi u)}>0 \qquad(0<u\le1/4). \end{split} \tag{15} Indeed if c=cos⁡(πu)c=\cos(\pi u), then 1+c−2c2=(1−c)(2c+1)>01+c-2c^2=(1-c)(2c+1)>0. At u=1/4u=1/4, reflection in (14) also gives k(1/4)=f(1/4)k(1/4)=f(1/4), recovering the special value χ(1/4)=4f(1/8)−3f(1/4)>0\chi(1/4)=4f(1/8)-3f(1/4)>0. No rational or numerical evaluation of special values is required. On each compact subinterval of (0,1/4](0,1/4], (15) gives a positive lower bound for this excess; no uniform positive bound as u↓0u\downarrow0 is asserted.

The constant-tensor component ⟨F(−tu),1⊗1⟩\langle F(-tu),1\otimes1\rangle is the signed coefficient divided by U0U_0. The large component used in (8) lies instead in the orthogonal direction Pn⊗1P_n\otimes1. Its growth cannot be removed from the positive norm, but it contributes zero to that constant-tensor expectation. More explicitly, if FN,n(−tu)=∑a,bda,bPa(t)Pb(u)F_{N,n}(-tu)=\sum_{a,b}d_{a,b}P_a(t)P_b(u), then dn,0=(−1)nCn,0wn/κn,d0,0=SN,n/U0. d_{n,0}=(-1)^n C_{n,0}w_n/\kappa_n,\qquad d_{0,0}=S_{N,n}/U_0. Since gng_n has zero exponential rate, the same proof gives liminf⁡N−1log⁡(U0|dn,0|/DN,n)≥χ(u)>0\liminf N^{-1}\log(U_0|d_{n,0}|/D_{N,n})\ge\chi(u)>0. Thus even a single coefficient defeats a polynomial comparison with the absolute coefficient sum or Euclidean coefficient norm in this fixed explicit basis. This is not a claim about every possible basis, and supplies no lower bound for the distinct coefficient d0,0d_{0,0}. Consequently this proof explains precisely why a bare Cauchy step can lose exponential information. It leaves open a cancellation-aware operator identity or estimate for the original signed expectation. It does not show that any actual shared coefficient exceeds its endpoint rate, nor does it change the original source or its cutoffs.
