Finite-fiber orthogonality and exact integration of linear Conway packets

Written proof, 24 November 2026.

The unchanged positive reciprocal integral gives an all-level lower bound for the original scalar with loss at most the number of leaves. The proof uses the finite degree of the actual residual closing and orthogonal projection on the fibers of an explicit rational map. It is a shorter, weaker comparison than the relative asymptotic of [W]. All linearly entering packets can also be integrated out exactly, leaving one variable packet per binary product. It does not estimate the integral from hyperbolic geometry.

1. The original source and statement

Use the full physical Conway compiler of [P] at any level N≥2N\ge2. Fix a literal tree with s≥1s\ge1 proper vertices and L≥1L\ge1 prescribed leaf occurrences. Each packet has all NN complex coordinates of 𝒜N\mathcal A_N, including the first normals at double Casimir roots. Put D=NsD=Ns, and use physical orthonormal coordinates x∈ℂDx\in\mathbb C^D, with real Lebesgue measure dxdx. Keep the normalized maps, the original closing row cc, and the positive budget product ℬN\mathcal B_N unchanged. Set Gv(x)=Φv((xj)j child of v)−xv,EN(x)=∥G(x)∥2+|cx|2,𝒫N=D!πD∫ℂDEN(x)−D−1dx.(1) G_v(x)=\Phi_v((x_j)_{j\text{ child of }v})-x_v,\qquad E_N(x)=\|G(x)\|^2+|cx|^2,\qquad \mathcal P_N={\frac{D!}{\pi^D}} \int_{\mathbb C^D}E_N(x)^{-D-1}\,dx . \qquad\text{(1)} This is exactly the undamped integral of [U,W]. Its base has degree at most four; its denominator exponent grows with DD. The entire original mark, phases, turns, framing and external factors remain in (1). The integral is extended and nonnegative.

Theorem. With 1/(+∞)=01/(+\infty)=0, at every level and for every prescribed complex leaf choice, ℬN2L𝒫N≤|KN|2.(2) \boxed{\qquad {\frac{\mathcal B_N^2}{ L\mathcal P_N}} \le |K_N|^2 .\qquad} \qquad\text{(2)} The integral is finite and positive when KN≠0K_N\ne0, and infinite when KN=0K_N=0. No prior nonvanishing assumption, physical coefficient smallness estimate, or large-level asymptotic is needed for (2).

Order children before parents. The exact inverse of the residual map is the polynomial recursion H(r)v=Φv((H(r)j)j)−rv,detℂDG=(−1)D,|detℝDH|=1.(3) H(r)_v=\Phi_v((H(r)_j)_j)-r_v,\qquad \det_{\mathbb C}DG=(-1)^D,\qquad |\det_{\mathbb R}DH|=1 . \qquad\text{(3)} Indeed each step solves uniquely for its output packet. A leaf has degree one in rr; a turn preserves degree; at a product, the two child degree bounds add. Each subtree has at least one leaf, so the additional term −rv-r_v respects that bound. Consequently f(r)=cH(r),deg⁡f≤L,k=f(0)=KN/ℬN.(4) f(r)=cH(r),\qquad \deg f\le L,\qquad k=f(0)=K_N/\mathcal B_N . \qquad\text{(4)} The original coefficients define H,fH,f by finite recursion. The scalar kk is used in the proof, not supplied to define (1). Changing variables by (3) makes its denominator ∥r∥2+|f(r)|2\|r\|^2+|f(r)|^2, with exactly the same measure.

2. A finite-fiber change of variables

We prove the needed polynomial integral statement explicitly. Let f∈ℂ[r1,…,rD]f\in\mathbb C[r_1,\ldots,r_D], D≥1D\ge1, satisfy k=f(0)≠0k=f(0)\ne0, and put ν=max⁡(1,deg⁡f)\nu=\max(1,\deg f). Define J(r)=f(r)−∑a=1Dra∂af(r),T(r)=rf(r),dμD(w)=D!πDdw(1+∥w∥2)D+1.(5) J(r)=f(r)-\sum_{a=1}^D r_a\partial_a f(r),\qquad T(r)={\frac{r}{ f(r)}},\qquad d\mu_D(w)={\frac{D!}{\pi^D}} {\frac{dw}{(1+\|w\|^2)^{D+1}}} . \qquad\text{(5)} The map TT is used on {f≠0}\{f\ne0\}. Radial integration gives ∫ℂDdμD=D∫0∞tD−1(1+t)D+1dt=1,(6) \int_{\mathbb C^D}d\mu_D =D\int_0^\infty {\frac{t^{D-1}}{(1+t)^{D+1}}}\,dt=1, \qquad\text{(6)} by the substitution u=t/(1+t)u=t/(1+t). The rank-one determinant identity gives DT=1f(I−r(∇f)𝖳f),detℂDT=JfD+1.(7) DT={\frac{1}{ f}}\left(I-{\frac{r(\nabla f)^{\mathsf T}}{ f}}\right), \qquad \det_{\mathbb C}DT={\frac{J}{ f^{D+1}}} . \qquad\text{(7)} Here ∇f\nabla f denotes the column of holomorphic derivatives; the transpose in (7) does not conjugate.

For a generic ww, all points of its fiber have the form rj=tjw,pw(tj)=0,pw(t)=f(tw)−t.(8) r_j=t_jw,\qquad p_w(t_j)=0,\qquad p_w(t)=f(tw)-t . \qquad\text{(8)} Its constant coefficient is kk, so no tjt_j is zero. Except on an algebraic set of measure zero, pwp_w has degree ν\nu and ν\nu distinct roots. Here is a direct check of the distinct-root assertion. For degree at least two, choose a direction vv with nonzero highest homogeneous coefficient and put g(u)=f(uv)g(u)=f(uv). Along w=avw=av, a repeated root requires g(u)−ug′(u)=0g(u)-ug'(u)=0. This is a nonzero polynomial, since its constant coefficient is kk. Its finitely many roots exclude only finitely many nonzero aa in g(u)=u/ag(u)=u/a; a root with g(u)=0g(u)=0 cannot solve that equation. Thus the discriminant is not identically zero. For constant or affine ff, the assertion is immediate after excluding a possible zero linear coefficient.

At a root in (8), J(tjw)=−tjpw′(tj)≠0.(9) J(t_jw)=-t_jp_w'(t_j)\ne0 . \qquad\text{(9)} For any nonnegative measurable FF, ordinary real change of variables on inverse charts therefore yields D!πD∫ℂDF(r)|J(r)|2(∥r∥2+|f(r)|2)D+1dr=∫ℂD∑T(rj)=wF(rj)dμD(w).(10) {\frac{D!}{\pi^D}}\int_{\mathbb C^D} {\frac{F(r)|J(r)|^2}{(\|r\|^2+|f(r)|^2)^{D+1}}}\,dr =\int_{\mathbb C^D}\sum_{T(r_j)=w}F(r_j)\,d\mu_D(w). \qquad\text{(10)} The exceptional sets do not conceal a boundary term. The polynomial zero sets f=0f=0 and J=0J=0 have measure zero, since both are nonzero at zero. The degree-drop and discriminant sets in the target also have measure zero. On the remaining regular set, partition a countable cover by inverse charts into disjoint measurable pieces, apply the usual change of variables on each, and sum by Tonelli. Preimages of exceptional target sets on these charts are null. This proves (10) without a properness assumption at infinity. Setting F=1F=1 gives the finite-degree area identity D!πD∫|J(r)|2dr(∥r∥2+|f(r)|2)D+1=ν.(11) {\frac{D!}{\pi^D}}\int {\frac{ |J(r)|^2\,dr}{(\|r\|^2+|f(r)|^2)^{D+1}}}=\nu . \qquad\text{(11)} Setting F=|J|−2F=|J|^{-2} away from its null zero set instead gives 𝒫(f):=D!πD∫dr(∥r∥2+|f(r)|2)D+1=∫∑j=1ν1|J(rj)|2dμD.(12) \mathcal P(f):={\frac{D!}{\pi^D}}\int {\frac{dr}{(\|r\|^2+|f(r)|^2)^{D+1}}} =\int\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}{\frac{1}{|J(r_j)|^2}}\,d\mu_D . \qquad\text{(12)}

3. The original constant is an exact fiber trace

The partial-fraction identity for 1/pw(t)1/p_w(t) follows by clearing denominators: the remaining polynomial has degree at most ν−1\nu-1 and vanishes at all ν\nu roots. Evaluating it at zero gives 1k=−∑j=1ν1tjpw′(tj)=∑j=1ν1J(rj).(13) {\frac{1}{ k}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}{\frac{1}{ t_jp_w'(t_j)}} =\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}{\frac{1}{ J(r_j)}} .\qquad\text{(13)} This can also be read as the elementary one-variable residue identity. It retains the complex phases of every sheet. No root ordering or continuous choice of individual roots is needed.

In the standard counting inner product on a regular fiber, the vector (1/J(rj))j(1/J(r_j))_j has constant projection (1/(νk))j(1/(\nu k))_j. Its remaining component has zero sum and is orthogonal to that vector. Equations (6), (12), and (13) give the exact nonnegative decomposition 𝒫(f)=1ν|k|2+∫∑j=1ν|1J(rj)−1νk|2dμD.(14) \mathcal P(f)={\frac{1}{\nu|k|^2}} +\int\sum_{j=1}^{\nu} \left|{\frac{1}{ J(r_j)}}-{\frac{1}{\nu k}}\right|^2d\mu_D .\qquad\text{(14)} In particular |k|2𝒫(f)≥1/ν|k|^2\mathcal P(f)\ge1/\nu. This orthogonality is on the fibers of the explicit source map (5), not an assertion that the original physical cap is a positive trace.

For completeness, 𝒫(f)\mathcal P(f) is finite when k≠0k\ne0. Its denominator has no zero. It is bounded away from zero on each compact set, and at infinity it dominates ∥r∥2\|r\|^2; the normalized tail outside radius RR is at most D/R2D/R^2. If f(0)=0f(0)=0, holomorphy gives |f(r)|≤C∥r∥|f(r)|\le C\|r\| near zero. The integral then dominates a positive multiple of ∫0ρt−3dt\int_0^\rho t^{-3}dt, and is infinite.

4. Consequence for the unchanged Conway quartic

Apply (14) to (4). For nonzero source, ν≤L\nu\le L, so 𝒫N≥1/(L|k|2)\mathcal P_N\ge1/(L|k|^2). Multiplying and rearranging proves (2). At a source zero the radial divergence just proved makes its right-hand comparison zero as prescribed. All levels, arbitrary complex leaf phases, and every full Hermite direction are retained.

The same lower comparison holds for any prescribed positive Hermitian metric on each complete packet space, with any known nonzero scalar normalizations retaining KN=ℬNf(0)K_N=\mathcal B_N f(0). Choose new orthonormal coordinates and rebuild both the residual energy and its product Lebesgue measure in them. Each residual still has coefficient −I-I in its own output variable; thus (3), the degree bound, and the proof are unchanged. Restore the known normalization by |ℬN|2|\mathcal B_N|^2 if it is complex. No comparison with the old metric is required. This permits a metric specified from geometric data, independently of the unknown source. It does not keep the same numerical integral under a metric change, or give a two-sided approximation for arbitrary new metrics.

For fixed LL, the logarithmic lower comparison is 2πNlog⁡|KN|≥−πNlog⁡(𝒫N/ℬN2)−πNlog⁡L.(15) {\frac{2\pi}{ N}}\log|K_N| \ge -{\frac{\pi}{ N}}\log(\mathcal P_N/\mathcal B_N^2) -{\frac{\pi}{ N}}\log L . \qquad\text{(15)} Use extended logarithms at zeros. The last term tends to zero. Thus a lower-bound argument can use this finite-fiber proof in place of the longer asymptotic calibration in [W]. The latter remains stronger: it gives a two-sided relative error tending to zero. Neither comparison supplies a geometric upper estimate for 𝒫N/ℬN2\mathcal P_N/\mathcal B_N^2. Such an estimate is still required to obtain a hyperbolic-volume lower bound. No new knot class or finite-exception theorem is established here.

5. Integrating all packets that enter linearly

There is also an exact reduction of the same integral to its nonlinear packets. Follow [L]: at each of the p=L−1p=L-1 binary products retain one child packet. These selected packets are distinct because every proper packet has a unique parent. Write their coordinates as u∈ℂnu\in\mathbb C^n, n=Npn=Np, and all remaining coordinates as z∈ℂmz\in\mathbb C^m, m=D−nm=D-n. At fixed uu, no product has two variable children. Split the original residuals into the eliminated packets’ own equations and all other equations, including the scalar closing: rz=T(u)z+bz(u),ro=C(u)z+bo(u),EN(u,z)=∥rz∥2+∥ro∥2.(16) r_z=T(u)z+b_z(u),\qquad r_o=C(u)z+b_o(u),\qquad E_N(u,z)=\|r_z\|^2+\|r_o\|^2 . \qquad\text{(16)} Here rz∈ℂmr_z\in\mathbb C^m, ro∈ℂn+1r_o\in\mathbb C^{n+1}, and T=−I+strictly triangularT=-I+\text{strictly triangular} in the tree order. These identities hold on the whole complex spaces. Thus TT is invertible for every uu, with |det⁡T|=1|\det T|=1; its inverse is a finite polynomial. No packet-ball restriction or small norm of uu is used.

Set 𝖣=CT−1,F=bo−CT−1bz,R=In+1+𝖣𝖣*,q=F*R−1F.(17) \mathsf D=CT^{-1},\qquad F=b_o-CT^{-1}b_z,\qquad R=I_{n+1}+\mathsf D\mathsf D^*,\qquad q=F^*R^{-1}F . \qquad\text{(17)} The matrix and scalar depend on uu, and RR is positive definite. Changing to v=Tz+bzv=Tz+b_z preserves Lebesgue measure and gives EN=∥v∥2+∥𝖣v+F∥2E_N=\|v\|^2+\|\mathsf Dv+F\|^2. Complete its Hermitian square with A=Im+𝖣*𝖣A=I_m+\mathsf D^*\mathsf D. The remaining constant is qq, and det⁡A=det⁡R\det A=\det R. Diagonalization and radial integration give ∫ℂm(∥v∥2+∥𝖣v+F∥2)−n−m−1dv=πmn!(n+m)!det⁡Rqn+1.(18) \int_{\mathbb C^m} (\|v\|^2+\|\mathsf Dv+F\|^2)^{-n-m-1}\,dv ={\frac{\pi^m n!}{(n+m)!\,\det R\,q^{n+1}}} . \qquad\text{(18)} For q>0q>0, the radial beta integral proves the displayed constant; for q=0q=0, both sides are infinite. Tonelli now proves the exact whole-source formula 𝒫N=n!πn∫ℂndudet⁡R(u)q(u)n+1.(19) \boxed{\qquad \mathcal P_N={\frac{n!}{\pi^n}} \int_{\mathbb C^n}{\frac{du}{\det R(u)\,q(u)^{n+1}}} . \qquad} \qquad\text{(19)} It is an extended nonnegative equality, including source zeros. If p=0p=0, then n=0n=0, RR is scalar and det⁡Rq=|k|2\det R\,q=|k|^2, recovering the exact affine reciprocal. For branching trees, (19) retains both the boundary Gram and its determinant. It integrates the eliminated packets rather than minimizing them, so its normalization differs from a Schur minimum. The scalar closing is still a row of (16), with all original phases. Equation (2) applies to precisely this reduced integral as well. The lower comparison and this elimination remain valid in the consistently rebuilt packet metrics described in Section 4.
