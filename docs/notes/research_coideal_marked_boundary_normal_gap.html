A polynomial normal gap at the original marked boundary

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

The original marked boundary selects a particular path block of an actual adjacent-crossing difference equation. Its left and right eigenvectors are explicit finite products. They turn the normal operator into a complex weighted path Laplacian, whose compatible inverse has polynomial norm. No positivity of the complex weights is assumed. In the already specified positive height metric the normal gap is at least c0N−12c_0N^{-12}. Here normal means transverse to the specified vector line, not a Casimir derivative or a moving-level normal.

This controls the part transverse to the marked boundary mode. It leaves that mode’s amplitude free and does not bound the original closed scalar below. In particular, an interior crossing with the opposite sign still requires a signed overlap between different normal operators.

6. What this eliminates in the original source

The coefficients in (31) are all known finite source data. The compatible normal problem can therefore be eliminated by cumulative sums and first differences with polynomial loss. Its residual energy remains quadratic in that linear problem. It does not require a dense inverse with an unknown condition number, or an estimate for a discarded boundary coordinate. At greater fixed width the earlier ordinary caps and their constraints remain exactly those of [C,H]; the same local block calculation has the same constants.

This selected normal problem still applies to every attached state at the literal boundary. Let PlP_l be coordinate projection onto IlI_l. It commutes with JJ, has height norm one, and r=rPlr=rP_l. Thus rw=rPlwrw=rP_lw for any full incoming height vector, and the gap applies to PlwP_lw with residual Pl(J−μ*I)wP_l(J-\mu_*I)w. The whole preceding word remains in ww. This does not permit inserting PlP_l before other crossings or give a bound for the unselected component.

The one-dimensional kernel remains. Its coefficient is not controlled by a normal residual and has not been fixed by minimization. The original marked row, its factor βN\beta_N, and the original exterior factor χN\chi_N must still be used when recovering the scalar.

The unresolved opposite-sign seam is explicit. Conjugating (18) gives qJ¯B−=B−Xq\overline J\,B^-=B^-X, whereas the marked row is a left mode of JJ. The general difference equation and cap slide are established mechanisms. The additional assertion is the polynomial normal gap on the block selected by the actual marked boundary, with its explicit compatible flux inverse and existing height metric. The older pivotal-cap reassociation [P] is a different source-basis reduction. The positive Jacobi boundary problem [J] concerns Newton value data in a spectral half-plane and its positive Green resolvent. Here the operator is non-Hermitian, the data are the marked coideal mode, and the estimate holds on every selected path without a half-plane assumption. Neither result supplies a lower bound for a prescribed coherent exterior or the finite-exception volume theorem after arbitrary short partial fillings.
