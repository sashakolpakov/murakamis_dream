Newton products when the main meridian changes

1 November 2026. Joining two tangle boxes requires multiplying their Casimir polynomials. At the complete color, many of the resulting coefficients vanish exactly. Varying the main knot meridian can make them nonzero, even on the microscopic scale used in the meridian profile. This note establishes an exact multiplication rule in the actual equal-color Casimir polynomial algebra. The oriented compiler supplies the physical turns and closing trace separately. The multiplication rule alone gives no bound for a knot invariant.

2. Proof by the Newton recurrence

The elementary node difference is ci(s)−ck(s)=4sin⁡((k−i)θ)sin⁡((k+i+1−2s)θ)=(−ρ2)[k−i][k+i+1−2s]. c_i(s)-c_k(s) =4\sin((k-i)\theta)\sin((k+i+1-2s)\theta) =(-\rho^2)[k-i][k+i+1-2s]. \tag{9} Since (ci−ω)Bk=Bk+1+(ci−ck)Bk(c_i-\omega)B_k=B_{k+1}+(c_i-c_k)B_k, multiplication by the next factor of BiB_i requires Ai+1,j,k=Ai,j,k−1+(−ρ2)[k−i][k+i+1−2s]Ai,j,k. A_{i+1,j,k} =A_{i,j,k-1} +(-\rho^2)[k-i][k+i+1-2s]A_{i,j,k}. \tag{10} The term BNB_N is zero in the quotient. Thus (10) is also the exact recurrence at the top retained index k=N−1k=N-1; there is no wrap-around contribution.

The required scalar identity is [i+1][i+j+1−2s]=[k−j][k−2s]+[k+i+1−2s][i+j+1−k]. [i+1][i+j+1-2s] =[k-j][k-2s] +[k+i+1-2s][i+j+1-k]. \tag{11} For a direct verification, multiply by sin⁡2θ\sin^2\theta. In the difference of the first two sine products, the difference angles are both (2s−j)θ(2s-j)\theta. The product-to-sum formula leaves cos⁡((2k−j−2s)θ)−cos⁡((2i+j+2−2s)θ)2, \frac{\cos((2k-j-2s)\theta) -\cos((2i+j+2-2s)\theta)}2, which is the final sine product in (11).

Substitution of (6) in (10), followed by factorial cancellation and (11), proves the recurrence at the interior indices. One may do the cancellation first for generic ss, then clear the shifted factors. Both resulting sides are entire trigonometric polynomials in ss, so the identity holds at their zeros as well. Equivalently, multiply the recurrence by the common nonzero integer factorial denominator before using (11).

At the lower and upper support endpoints, the vanished [k−i][k-i] factor and the empty product give the recurrence directly. The initial condition B0Bj=BjB_0B_j=B_j is exact. Induction through 0≤i<N0\le i<N proves (5)–(6). Finally, Sr=(2sin⁡θ)r[r]!,−ρ2=(2sin⁡θ)2 S_r=(2\sin\theta)^r[r]!,\qquad -\rho^2=(2\sin\theta)^2 convert AijkSk2/(Si2Sj2)A_{ijk}S_k^2/(S_i^2S_j^2) into (8), with all powers of 2sin⁡θ2\sin\theta canceled. At s=0s=0, if i+j<Ni+j<N, (8) reduces to μijk(0)=Si+jSkSiSjSk−iSk−jSi+j−k, \mu_{ijk}(0)= \frac{S_{i+j}S_k} {S_iS_jS_{k-i}S_{k-j}S_{i+j-k}}, \tag{13} the existing positive complete-source multiplication coefficient. The first derivative has a particularly simple exact form, requiring no bulk assumption: ∂sμijk(0)=4π(−1)hνijk. \boxed{\; \partial_s\mu_{ijk}(0)=4\pi(-1)^h\nu_{ijk}. \;} \tag{18} Both shifted products equal one at zero, and the derivative of 2Nsin⁡(2πs/N)2N\sin(2\pi s/N) there is 4π4\pi. The first derivative depends on the chosen basis

The nonzero derivative in (18) is not an intrinsic first-order change of the quotient multiplication. To see this, identify all the degree-<N<N polynomial vector spaces by keeping the complete basis er(0,ω)e_r(0,\omega) fixed. 4. A uniform shifted-product estimate through its zeros

For fixed R<∞R<\infty and 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/2, uniformly for δN≤n≤(1−δ)N\delta N\le n\le(1-\delta)N and |a|≤R|a|\le R, 𝒜N,n(a)=(Nsin⁡(πn/N)/π)aΓ(1+a)VN,n(a),VN,n=1+OR,δ(N−1), \mathcal A_{N,n}(a) =\frac{(N\sin(\pi n/N)/\pi)^a}{\Gamma(1+a)} V_{N,n}(a), \qquad V_{N,n}=1+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1}), \tag{19} where VN,nV_{N,n} is a nonvanishing holomorphic unit on a neighborhood of that disk. Real positive bases in complex powers use their real logarithms. The estimate and its inverse hold with every fixed aa-derivative on smaller disks.

To establish the zero-preserving version of (19), let g(t)=sin⁡(πt)πt,g(0)=1. g(t)=\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{\pi t},\qquad g(0)=1. Use its analytic logarithm near the compact interval [0,1−δ][0,1-\delta], extended slightly to the left. Factor the product exactly as 𝒜N,n(a)=Γ(n+a+1)Γ(n+1)Γ(1+a)∏r=1ng((r+a)/N)g(r/N). \mathcal A_{N,n}(a)= \frac{\Gamma(n+a+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(1+a)} \prod_{r=1}^{n}\frac{g((r+a)/N)}{g(r/N)}. \tag{20} The factor multiplying 1/Γ(1+a)1/\Gamma(1+a) is nonzero and holomorphic on every fixed aa-disk once NN is large. In particular it defines the unit without dividing by a zero of the sine product.

Taylor expansion of log⁡g\log g, followed by a uniform Riemann-sum estimate, gives ∑r=1n[log⁡g((r+a)/N)−log⁡g(r/N)]=alog⁡g(n/N)+OR,δ(N−1). \sum_{r=1}^n [\log g((r+a)/N)-\log g(r/N)] =a\log g(n/N)+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1}). \tag{21} The uniform Gamma-ratio estimate is Γ(n+a+1)/Γ(n+1)=na(1+OR(n−1))\Gamma(n+a+1)/\Gamma(n+1)=n^a(1+O_R(n^{-1})). Cauchy’s formula on a larger disk supplies the derivative statements.

6. The old bulk sectors acquire a nonconstant bounded multiplier

For the old sector i+j<Ni+j<N, suppose δN≤k≤i+j≤(1−δ)N. \delta N\le k\le i+j\le(1-\delta)N. The exact quotient obtained from (8) and (13) is a product of nonzero factors on every fixed ss-disk for large NN. Taking the logarithm near s=0s=0 gives log⁡μijk(s)μijk(0)=−2πsN∑r=k+1i+jcot⁡(πr/N)+OR,δ(N−1)=−2slog⁡sin⁡(π(i+j)/N)sin⁡(πk/N)+OR,δ(N−1). \begin{aligned} \log\frac{\mu_{ijk}(s)}{\mu_{ijk}(0)} &=-\frac{2\pi s}{N}\sum_{r=k+1}^{i+j} \cot(\pi r/N)+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1})\\ &=-2s\log\frac{\sin(\pi(i+j)/N)}{\sin(\pi k/N)} +O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1}). \end{aligned} \tag{26} Therefore μijk(s)=μijk(0)[sin⁡(π(i+j)/N)sin⁡(πk/N)]−2s(1+OR,δ(N−1)). \boxed{\; \mu_{ijk}(s)=\mu_{ijk}(0) \left[ \frac{\sin(\pi(i+j)/N)}{\sin(\pi k/N)} \right]^{-2s} (1+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1})). \;} \tag{27} The final error is again a holomorphic unit, uniform with every fixed derivative. The leading multiplier is generally not one. Thus even within the old support, a microscopic meridian displacement is not automatically a coefficientwise 1+O(N−1)1+O(N^{-1}) perturbation of this normalized gate. Its exponential rate is unchanged, but its order-one normal dependence must be contracted with the rest of the source.

7. Scope for a tangle compiler

Equations (5)–(8) are exact algebraic multiplication identities in the moving equal-color Casimir algebra. Their proof is valid at both level parities, all composite levels, and all complex meridian displacements. They use no approximation of repeated nodes by simple nodes.

The old complete-color support condition i+j<Ni+j<N is special. Equation (18) shows nonzero first derivatives in the moving basis, while (18b)–(18d) explain why these are not a coordinate-independent first-order obstruction. For nonzero microscopic displacements, the existing strict wire polytope and its old absolute majorant cannot simply be assumed to describe the varied source without additional terms. Conversely the existence of these terms is not a lower bound on a full knot invariant; cancellations and the actual closure remain essential.

Equations (22) and (27) identify their normal multipliers and exponential rates in the stated bulk ranges. They do not supply endpoint-uniform analytic bounds for every new gate state. The oriented compiler gives the exact typed turns between equal-color and opposite-color sectors and the primitive cut normalization. Quantitative control of those operations and the remaining signed contraction is still a separate problem.

8. Reproducing the formulas and diagram

From the repository root, with SymPy, mpmath and Matplotlib installed, run

python3 experiments/audit_main_meridian_newton_algebra.py

The script directly expands the two sides of the generic multiplication formula over the rational numbers, checks the finite connection formula independently, and checks the dual basis in exact cyclotomic fields. Its result file also contains high-precision bulk-factor diagnostics, distinguished from exact polynomial identities. The same command draws the support diagram in PDF, SVG, and PNG. Finite diagnostics illustrate the proved estimates; they do not prove uniform convergence.
