Endpoint asymptotics on a fixed complex neighborhood

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The bulk value formula extends to a fixed, sufficiently thin complex neighborhood after reducing its power saving. Combined with the exact scalar compatibility theorem, this gives an asymptotic formula for one representative of the actual values and first spectral derivatives. At the complete endpoint its leading factor is a holomorphic unit. The same remains true in a specified shrinking neighborhood of that endpoint. This is a local statement; it does not supply the coefficient of a geometric cycle in the whole original trace.

1. A quantitative continuation lemma

Let a<ba<b, H>0H>0, and let rNr_N be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed rectangle a≤ℜz≤b,|ℑz|≤H. a\le\Re z\le b,\qquad |\Im z|\le H. Suppose, uniformly in any additional real parameters, |rN(z)|≤CNP,|rN(x)|≤CN−q(a≤x≤b),P≥0,q>0.(1) |r_N(z)|\le C N^P,\qquad |r_N(x)|\le C N^{-q}\quad(a\le x\le b), \qquad P\ge0,\quad q>0. \qquad\text{(1)} Enlarge the common constant CC if necessary. Fix 0<d<(b−a)/20<d<(b-a)/2 and 0<λ<q0<\lambda<q. Set B=1H+4πd,h=min⁡{H2,λB(P+q)}.(2) B=\frac1H+\frac4{\pi d},\qquad h=\min\left\{\frac H2,\frac{\lambda}{B(P+q)}\right\}. \qquad\text{(2)} Then |rN(x+iy)|≤CN−q+λ(a+d≤x≤b−d,|y|≤h).(3) |r_N(x+iy)|\le C N^{-q+\lambda} \quad(a+d\le x\le b-d,\ |y|\le h). \qquad\text{(3)} On any smaller rectangle, every fixed complex derivative has the same bound, with a derivative-dependent constant.

Here is a direct barrier proof. In the upper rectangle define w(x,y)=yH+2π{arctanyx−a+arctanyb−x}.(4) w(x,y)=\frac yH+\frac2\pi \left\{\arctan\frac y{x-a} +\arctan\frac y{b-x}\right\}. \qquad\text{(4)} The two arctangents are arguments of nonvanishing linear functions in their respective half-planes; both are harmonic. Thus ww is harmonic. It vanishes on the open bottom edge, is at least one on each other edge, and is nonnegative. The subharmonic function log⁡|rN|−log⁡C+qlog⁡N−(P+q)(log⁡N)w \log|r_N|-\log C+q\log N-(P+q)(\log N)w has nonpositive boundary upper limits. At a bottom corner this also holds by continuity of rNr_N, since its value there satisfies the second bound in (1). The maximum principle therefore gives |rN(x+iy)|≤CN−q+(P+q)w(x,y).(5) |r_N(x+iy)| \le C N^{-q+(P+q)w(x,y)}. \qquad\text{(5)} For the indicated buffered real parts, w(x,y)≤y(1H+2π{1x−a+1b−x})≤By. w(x,y)\le y\left(\frac1H+\frac2\pi \left\{\frac1{x-a}+\frac1{b-x}\right\}\right) \le By . This proves (3) above the real axis; reflection gives the lower half. Cauchy’s formula on fixed-radius disks in a smaller rectangle proves the derivative assertion. Zeros of rNr_N are harmless in the subharmonic argument.

In particular, if q=1+ϵq=1+\epsilon, a fixed strip can retain the bound O(N−1−ϵ/2)O(N^{-1-\epsilon/2}), by taking λ=ϵ/2\lambda=\epsilon/2. A strip of width 1/log⁡N1/\log N is useful for keeping the original exponent without loss; it is unnecessary when a smaller positive power saving suffices.

2. The actual endpoint representative

Retain the original notation cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N),cN(x)=2cos⁡(2πx+π/N),wN(α)=2cosh⁡(α/N),KN(w,Z)=∑k=0N−1ek(w)ek(Z),SN(x)=Nπsin⁡(πx).(6) \begin{gathered} c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N),\qquad c_N(x)=2\cos(2\pi x+\pi/N),\\ w_N(\alpha)=2\cosh(\alpha/N),\qquad K_N(w,Z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e_k(w)e_k(Z),\\ S_N(x)=\frac N\pi\sin(\pi x). \end{gathered} \qquad\text{(6)} The eke_k are the original normalized Newton polynomials. Fix a closed interval I⋐(0,1/2)I\Subset(0,1/2) and a compact real parameter interval |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A. The scalar compatibility theorem supplies, on a fixed complex neighborhood of a slightly larger real interval, a polynomially bounded holomorphic function hN(x,α)h_N(x,\alpha) with hN(j/N,α)=KN(wN(α),cj),∂xhN(j/N,α)=cN′(j/N)∂ZKN(wN(α),cj).(7) \begin{split} h_N(j/N,\alpha)&=K_N(w_N(\alpha),c_j),\\ \partial_xh_N(j/N,\alpha) &=c_N'(j/N)\partial_ZK_N(w_N(\alpha),c_j). \end{split} \qquad\text{(7)} It is jointly holomorphic in bounded complex endpoint parameters. For the asymptotic assertion below the parameters are real.

Put ν=α/π\nu=\alpha/\pi, and set k±(ν)=Γ(±iν)Γ(1/2±iν/2)4,C0(ν)=k+(ν)+k−(ν),C1(ν)=iν(k+(ν)−k−(ν)).(8) k_\pm(\nu)=\frac{\Gamma(\pm i\nu)} {\Gamma(1/2\pm i\nu/2)^4},\quad C_0(\nu)=k_+(\nu)+k_-(\nu),\quad C_1(\nu)=i\nu(k_+(\nu)-k_-(\nu)). \qquad\text{(8)} The two CiC_i are regular and even at zero, with C0(0)=2(γE+4log⁡2)π2,C1(0)=2π2. C_0(0)=\frac{2(\gamma_E+4\log2)}{\pi^2}, \qquad C_1(0)=\frac2{\pi^2}. Define, using the removable value of sin⁡(νlog⁡s)/ν\sin(\nu\log s)/\nu, gα(s)=1s{C0(ν)cos(νlogs)+C1(ν)sin⁡(νlog⁡s)ν}.(9) g_\alpha(s)=\frac1s \left\{C_0(\nu)\cos(\nu\log s) +C_1(\nu)\frac{\sin(\nu\log s)}{\nu}\right\}. \qquad\text{(9)} For s=SN(x)s=S_N(x), choose log⁡sin⁡(πx)\log\sin(\pi x) to be real on the real interval. It has a single holomorphic branch on a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood of that interval.

Theorem 1. There exist a fixed complex neighborhood UU of II and ϵ*>0\epsilon_*>0 such that, for real |α|≤A|\alpha|\le A, hN(x,α)=gα(SN(x))+O(N−1−ϵ*)(x∈U).(10) h_N(x,\alpha)=g_\alpha(S_N(x))+O(N^{-1-\epsilon_*}) \quad(x\in U). \qquad\text{(10)} The error is holomorphic in xx. Every fixed xx-derivative has the same bound on smaller fixed neighborhoods. Both level parities are included. The neighborhood can be thin, and its size is not a claim about the location of a geometric critical point.

Proof. The value connection theorem applies to hNh_N, since it is a moderate exact value interpolation. On a buffered real interval containing II it gives hN(x,α)−gα(SN(x))=O(N−1−ϵ)(11) h_N(x,\alpha)-g_\alpha(S_N(x)) =O(N^{-1-\epsilon}) \qquad\text{(11)} for some ϵ>0\epsilon>0, uniformly for the stated real parameters. On a fixed larger rectangle hNh_N has a bound CNPCN^P. On that same rectangle the second term is OA((1+log⁡N)/N)O_A((1+\log N)/N): the imaginary part of log⁡SN(x)\log S_N(x) is bounded, ν\nu is real and bounded, and the removable expression in (9) costs at most CA(1+log⁡N)C_A(1+\log N). Thus their difference satisfies (1), with q=1+ϵq=1+\epsilon and some P≥0P\ge0. Apply (2)–(3) with λ=ϵ/2\lambda=\epsilon/2, then shrink the rectangle to retain fixed derivative buffers. This proves (10) with ϵ*=ϵ/2\epsilon_*=\epsilon/2. No off-real small-error estimate from the value theorem was assumed. ▫\square

At the physical nodes, (7) and (10) give the actual spectral derivative, by division by cN′(x)=−4πsin⁡(2πx+π/N), c_N'(x)=-4\pi\sin(2\pi x+\pi/N), which is uniformly nonzero on a smaller fixed bulk neighborhood. Thus the result does not concern a substitute derivative obtained by moving recurrence coefficients.

3. A nonzero factor near the complete endpoint

Write sinc⁡u=sin⁡u/u\operatorname{sinc}u=\sin u/u, with sinc⁡0=1\operatorname{sinc}0=1. Let β\beta be real and range over a fixed bounded interval, and put αN=βlog⁡N. \alpha_N=\frac{\beta}{\log N}. On the fixed complex neighborhood from Theorem 1, hN(x,αN)=2log⁡NπNsin⁡(πx)sinc⁡(β/π)+O(N−1).(12) h_N(x,\alpha_N)= \frac{2\log N}{\pi N\sin(\pi x)} \operatorname{sinc}(\beta/\pi) +O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(12)} All fixed xx-derivatives have the same additive error on smaller neighborhoods, uniformly for bounded real β\beta.

To prove this, put λ(x)=log⁡(sin⁡(πx)/π)\lambda(x)=\log(\sin(\pi x)/\pi), so log⁡SN(x)=log⁡N+λ(x)\log S_N(x)=\log N+\lambda(x). In (9), ν=β/(πlog⁡N)\nu=\beta/(\pi\log N), and analyticity at zero gives C0(ν)=C0(0)+O((log⁡N)−2),C1(ν)=2/π2+O((log⁡N)−2),sin⁡(νlog⁡SN(x))ν=(log⁡N)sinc⁡(β/π)+λ(x)cos⁡(β/π)+O((log⁡N)−1). \begin{split} C_0(\nu)&=C_0(0)+O((\log N)^{-2}),\\ C_1(\nu)&=2/\pi^2+O((\log N)^{-2}),\\ \frac{\sin(\nu\log S_N(x))}{\nu} &=(\log N)\operatorname{sinc}(\beta/\pi) +\lambda(x)\cos(\beta/\pi) +O((\log N)^{-1}). \end{split} The last expansion is uniform also at β=0\beta=0, by its entire power series. Substitution proves (12), including the derivative bounds.

If |β|≤B<π2|\beta|\le B<\pi^2, the real factor sinc⁡(β/π)\operatorname{sinc}(\beta/\pi) is positive and bounded away from zero. Consequently hN(x,β/log⁡N)=2log⁡NπNsin⁡(πx)sinc⁡(β/π){1+Ohol((log⁡N)−1)}.(13) h_N(x,\beta/\log N)= \frac{2\log N}{\pi N\sin(\pi x)} \operatorname{sinc}(\beta/\pi) \{1+O_{\mathrm{hol}}((\log N)^{-1})\}. \qquad\text{(13)} For all large NN this is a holomorphic unit on a smaller neighborhood. At β=0\beta=0 it applies to the complete endpoint wN(0)=2w_N(0)=2.

For any fixed number rr of factors sharing the same variable, their ordinary product therefore satisfies ∏i=1rhN(x,βi/log⁡N)=(2log⁡NπNsin⁡(πx))r∏i=1rsinc⁡(βi/π){1+Ohol((log⁡N)−1)},(14) \prod_{i=1}^r h_N(x,\beta_i/\log N) =\left(\frac{2\log N}{\pi N\sin(\pi x)}\right)^r \prod_{i=1}^r\operatorname{sinc}(\beta_i/\pi) \{1+O_{\mathrm{hol}}((\log N)^{-1})\}, \qquad\text{(14)} uniformly for |βi|≤B<π2|\beta_i|\le B<\pi^2. It retains the original shared first normal by the product rule and (7). The analogous product over finitely many variables retains every mixed first normal. No spectral variable is reduced before its actual closing functional is applied.

For fixed nonzero real α\alpha, Theorem 1 still applies, but the leading expression (9) oscillates in log⁡N\log N. Equation (13) is not extended to those parameters. In particular no nonvanishing assertion is made at the first zeros β=±π2\beta=\pm\pi^2.

4. What this proves for a local saddle integral

There is a useful precise consequence of (14). Suppose a particular local contour through a nondegenerate critical point z*z_*, in the product of the neighborhoods above, has already been chosen for a bare source. Suppose its local integral has the form IN=eNΦ(z*)N−d/2{A*+o(1)},A*≠0,(15) I_N=e^{N\Phi(z_*)}N^{-d/2}\{A_*+o(1)\},\qquad A_*\ne0, \qquad\text{(15)} and its integrand aN(z)eNΦ(z)dza_N(z)e^{N\Phi(z)}\,dz obeys the usual absolute Gaussian estimate on that contour: in real local coordinates uu, |aN(z(u))dz|≤Cdu,ℜ(Φ(z(u))−Φ(z*))≤−c|u|2.(16) |a_N(z(u))\,dz|\le C\,du,\qquad \Re(\Phi(z(u))-\Phi(z_*))\le-c|u|^2. \qquad\text{(16)} The parametrization is Lipschitz near zero; the fixed local contour has no other point of equal real phase. Polynomial scalar prefactors can be taken outside (15).

Insert the products in (14), with total number of factors rr. After dividing them by (log⁡N/N)r(\log N/N)^r, write the multiplier as bN(z)b_N(z). Equation (14) gives bN(z)=b(z)+Ohol((log⁡N)−1),b(z)=(2π)r∏vsin⁡(πxv)−rv∏isinc⁡(βi/π).(17) b_N(z)=b(z)+O_{\mathrm{hol}}((\log N)^{-1}),\quad b(z)=\left(\frac2\pi\right)^r \prod_v\sin(\pi x_v)^{-r_v} \prod_i\operatorname{sinc}(\beta_i/\pi). \qquad\text{(17)} Here rvr_v counts factors at variable xvx_v, and ∑vrv=r\sum_v r_v=r. On compact sets b(z*)≠0b(z_*)\ne0, uniformly for the indicated real parameters. It follows that the decorated local integral equals (log⁡NN)reNΦ(z*)N−d/2{b(z*)A*+o(1)}.(18) \left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right)^r e^{N\Phi(z_*)}N^{-d/2}\{b(z_*)A_*+o(1)\}. \qquad\text{(18)}

Indeed |bN(z(u))−b(z*)|≤C{(log⁡N)−1+|u|}. |b_N(z(u))-b(z_*)|\le C\{(\log N)^{-1}+|u|\}. Multiplying by (16) and integrating bounds the difference from b(z*)INb(z_*)I_N by CeNℜΦ(z*)N−d/2{(log⁡N)−1+N−1/2}. C e^{N\Re\Phi(z_*)}N^{-d/2} \{(\log N)^{-1}+N^{-1/2}\}. This proves (18) directly from the stated local hypotheses. In particular these endpoint factors cannot make that already specified local Gaussian coefficient zero.

This consequence does not supply (15), choose a contributing contour, or show that z*z_* lies in the thin neighborhood furnished by Theorem 1. Nor does it exclude cancellation with another part of the original closed sum. The full trace retains its outer vertical integrals and complementary physical rows.
