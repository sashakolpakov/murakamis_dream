The finite wire sum before the crossing traces

The polynomial contraction for a Conway construction sums over integer Newton indices on its internal edges. At a junction carrying a crossing circle, a polynomial trace remains to be evaluated. This note first sums the internal indices while keeping one angular variable for each such trace. The resulting finite function is called the wire packet. Near its geometric critical point, it has a uniform complex asymptotic with a nonzero leading coefficient. An exact representation of the finite lattice sum controls the boundary terms when the integration contours move.

The crossing-circle traces are applied only after this common finite sum has been constructed. This order matters when filling coefficients are independently long: a local Fourier error cannot be compared with a product of independently filled exponents. The theorem below constructs the packet and evaluates its meridian-dependent insertion. The joint filling transform then compares the Fourier frequencies of the entire contraction.

The packet is precisely suitable for the joint transfer: exact separate evenness, a nonzero complex wire saddle, diagonal negative angular Hessian, a fixed angular corridor, and real-spectator endpoint bounds.
