Completing a positive primitive solution with its original marking

A positive solution of the original circle-tree filling equations, with negative imaginary boundary angles at the selected circles, already determines the actual marked hyperbolic filling. A path of solutions with fixed slopes is not needed for this conclusion.

The proof uses a different path for a specific purpose: it contracts the positive tangent coordinates to the complete structure while keeping each selected longitude loxodromic. That path identifies the full logarithmic periods of the original peripheral curves. The primitive filling equations are imposed only at its endpoint. The local tube completion then gives the original smooth filling.

This extends the geometric argument in Section 6 of the circle-tree geometry manuscript, with its original cut cells, page order and primitive curves. No manuscript is modified here.

1. Statement and conventions

Let YY be an original ordered circle-tree exterior with at least one edge, as defined by the marked cut construction in that manuscript. Retain every original half-twist carrier, disk frame, page order, main component and auxiliary cusp. At an auxiliary vertex vv, the primitive basis is (μv,λv)(\mu_v,\lambda_v), where λv\lambda_v is the original disk longitude. Choose selected vertices SS, original integer filling coefficients pvp_v, and carriers ηv\eta_v, and put nv=ηv−2pv.(1) n_v=\eta_v-2p_v. \qquad\text{(1)} The outside angles are zero. For a selected vertex use a complex angle tvt_v on the canonical branch |ℜtv|<π|\Re t_v|<\pi. For an edge e={v,w}e=\{v,w\}, put av=tan⁡(tv/2),aw=tan⁡(tw/2),qe2=1+av2+aw2.(2) a_v=\tan(t_v/2),\quad a_w=\tan(t_w/2),\qquad q_e^2=1+a_v^2+a_w^2. \qquad\text{(2)} The positive branch is the one characterized by ℜqe>(ℜav)2+(ℜaw)2.(3) \Re q_e> \sqrt{(\Re a_v)^2+(\Re a_w)^2}. \qquad\text{(3)} This is exactly positivity of the eight original ordered tetrahedra, as proved in the positive edge theorem. Define the original returns by rv,e=arsinh⁡(tan⁡(tv/2)cos⁡(tw/2)),(4) r_{v,e} =\operatorname{arsinh} \bigl(\tan(t_v/2)\cos(t_w/2)\bigr), \qquad\text{(4)} on the same canonical positive branch.

Theorem 1. Suppose (3) holds at every original edge, ℑtv<0\Im t_v<0 for v∈Sv\in S, and the full logarithmic equations are ∑e∋vrv,e=inv2tv−πi(v∈S).(5) \sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e} =\frac{i n_v}{2}t_v-\pi i \qquad(v\in S). \qquad\text{(5)} Then the original cells glue to a hyperbolic metric on YY whose completion is the smooth Dehn filling along the original primitive curves μv+pvλv\mu_v+p_v\lambda_v. Every main cusp and every unselected auxiliary cusp is complete. The filled core at vv has length −2ℑtv>0-2\Im t_v>0.

Let Ψ=∑eψ(tv,tw)\Psi=\sum_e\psi(t_v,t_w) include every original edge, including complete edges outside the selected region. Then Vol⁡Y(𝒑)=2πℜ{Ψ+i4π∑v∈Snvtv2−i∑v∈Stv}.(6) \operatorname{Vol}Y(\mathbf p) =2\pi\Re\left\{ \Psi+\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{v\in S}n_vt_v^2 -i\sum_{v\in S}t_v \right\}. \qquad\text{(6)} This is a geometric statement. It does not assert that an original quantum source has this exponential rate.

2. A positive path used to fix branches

We give the contraction explicitly. On one edge set X=(ℜav)2+(ℜaw)2,Y0=(ℑav)2+(ℑaw)2,Z=ℜavℑav+ℜawℑaw,D02=XY0−Z2.(7) X=(\Re a_v)^2+(\Re a_w)^2,\quad Y_0=(\Im a_v)^2+(\Im a_w)^2,\quad Z=\Re a_v\Im a_v+\Re a_w\Im a_w,\quad D_0^2=XY_0-Z^2. \qquad\text{(7)} Writing q=P+iQq=P+iQ, the identity q2=1+av2+aw2q^2=1+a_v^2+a_w^2 gives X−D02=(P2−X)(X+Q2).(8) X-D_0^2=(P^2-X)(X+Q^2). \qquad\text{(8)} For X>0X>0, positivity (3) is equivalent to D02<XD_0^2<X, with the square root chosen to have positive real part. Conversely this strict inequality excludes a nonpositive real value of q2q^2: such a value would have Z=0Z=0 and Y0≥1+XY_0\ge1+X, contradicting D02<XD_0^2<X. When X=0X=0, positivity is exactly Y0<1Y_0<1.

Therefore av(ρ)=ρav,0≤ρ≤1(9) a_v(\rho)=\rho a_v,\qquad 0\le\rho\le1 \qquad\text{(9)} preserves every positive edge simultaneously. For X>0X>0 its criterion is ρ4D02<ρ2X\rho^4D_0^2<\rho^2X; for X=0X=0 it is ρ2Y0<1\rho^2Y_0<1. The angle branch is tv(ρ)=2arctan⁡(ρav).(10) t_v(\rho)=2\arctan(\rho a_v). \qquad\text{(10)} The positive domain excludes the inverse-tangent cuts av∈i[1,∞)∪−i[1,∞)a_v\in i[1,\infty)\cup-i[1,\infty). For example, if ℜav=0\Re a_v=0, |ℑav|≥1|\Im a_v|\ge1, and ℜaw≠0\Re a_w\ne0, then D02≥XD_0^2\ge X; if both real parts are zero, then Y0≥1Y_0\ge1. Thus (10) is the continuous canonical branch throughout the contraction.

The sign of ℑtan⁡(t/2)\Im\tan(t/2) is the sign of ℑt\Im t in the canonical strip. Also the principal inverse tangent takes the lower half-plane, with its excluded cut removed, to negative imaginary part. It follows that ℑtv(ρ)<0(v∈S,0<ρ≤1).(11) \Im t_v(\rho)<0\qquad (v\in S,\ 0<\rho\le1). \qquad\text{(11)} All outside angles stay zero. The path generally does not satisfy (5), and is not being called a fixed-slope cone path.

3. The original gluing is regular on this whole path

The original face coordinates on an edge are rational functions of a=ava=a_v, b=awb=a_w, and q=qeq=q_e. In particular E=q+iq−i,R=1+b2q2+1,R1=1−R=1+a2q2+1,Z1=q+bq−i.(12) E=\frac{q+i}{q-i},\quad R=\frac{1+b^2}{q^2+1},\quad R_1=1-R=\frac{1+a^2}{q^2+1},\quad Z_1=\frac{q+b}{q-i}. \qquad\text{(12)} On the positive domain, ℜq>0\Re q>0, so q,q±i≠0q,q\pm i\ne0. Moreover ℜ(q±a),ℜ(q±b)>0\Re(q\pm a),\Re(q\pm b)>0, hence these factors are nonzero. The excluded inverse-tangent cuts give a±i≠0a\pm i\ne0 and b±i≠0b\pm i\ne0. Thus E,R,R1E,R,R_1 are nonzero. The further apex denominators factor as Z1−R1=(q+b)(b+i)q2+1,Z1−ER1=(q+b)(b−i)(q−i)2,(13) Z_1-R_1=\frac{(q+b)(b+i)}{q^2+1},\qquad Z_1-E R_1=\frac{(q+b)(b-i)}{(q-i)^2}, \qquad\text{(13)} and are nonzero, as are the expressions obtained by interchanging the endpoints. Every face triple remains distinct because the original tetrahedra are positive.

Consequently all original face maps are nonsingular on (9). The original incoming and outgoing doubled disk triangles have the complex edge ratios in equation ctg:pants\mathrm{ctg:pants} of the manuscript. They depend only on the shared angle through Qv=eitvQ_v=e^{it_v}, including the complex shears. The ordered page gluing therefore remains the same full face identification. The pure and odd edge-return matrix identities in equations ctg:pure-returns\mathrm{ctg:pure\text{-}returns} and ctg:odd-returns\mathrm{ctg:odd\text{-}returns} are rational identities in these regular variables. They continue on the entire path.

In the fixed topological triangulation, every edge product is therefore one. Its sum of positive dihedral angles is continuous, is an integral multiple of 2π2\pi, and equals 2π2\pi at the complete endpoint. It remains 2π2\pi. Thus the abstract glued tetrahedra give a nonsingular oriented hyperbolic metric at every finite point of the original exterior. This does not require that a deformed projection half-ball remain convex or that its development be globally injective.

4. Complete main and outside cusps

The main meridians are the original corner words Wj−1−1WjW_{j-1}^{-1}W_j, or their transported inverses. Their multipliers at the corner flags are identically one. They remain nonidentity on the full positive domain. For example W0−1W1:z↦z+(E−1),E−1=2iq−i≠0.(14) W_0^{-1}W_1:z\longmapsto z+(E-1), \qquad E-1=\frac{2i}{q-i}\ne0. \qquad\text{(14)} The other consecutive white maps cannot coincide projectively: the W1,W2W_1,W_2 pair has respectively zero and nonzero lower-left entries; the W2,W3W_2,W_3 pair has respectively nonzero and zero upper-right entries, since R(E−1)≠0R(E-1)\ne0; and the W3,W0W_3,W_0 pair again has nonzero and zero lower-left entries. Thus all these marked main meridians are nontrivial parabolics.

Every commuting peripheral transformation is a translation in the same fixed-flag affine chart. The horospherical triangles form a nonsingular similarity structure on the compact topological cusp torus. With translation holonomy its Euclidean metric descends and is complete, so the corresponding main end is an ordinary complete cusp.

At an unselected auxiliary vertex, tv=0t_v=0 and rv,e=0r_{v,e}=0 for every incident edge. Its two peripheral multipliers are one. Its similarity holonomy is therefore translational as well, and the same compact-link argument proves completeness. A zero translation for an individual peripheral generator cannot cause a degeneracy: the complete flat link has an injective translation lattice.

5. The full logarithms at a selected end

The actual primitive words of the manuscript give affine multipliers with continued logarithms Uv=2∑e∋vrv,e−iηvtv=−2πΨtv−iηvtv,Vv=2itv.(15) U_v=2\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}-i\eta_vt_v =-2\pi\Psi_{t_v}-i\eta_vt_v,\qquad V_v=2it_v. \qquad\text{(15)} These are initially logarithms of multipliers. We now verify that they are the full periods of the original primitive paths, including their winding integers.

For 0<ρ≤10<\rho\le1, equation (11) gives ℜVv=−2ℑtv>0\Re V_v=-2\Im t_v>0. Send the original selected ideal flag to infinity. The longitude has a nonunit affine multiplier, so the abelian peripheral holonomy has a second common fixed point qvq_v in this affine chart.

Its similarity development misses qvq_v. Indeed the vector field (z−qv)∂z(z-q_v)\partial_z is invariant under the peripheral affine holonomy. Its pullback by the similarity development therefore descends to the compact link torus. Every zero would have index +1+1, because the similarity development is locally orientation-preserving. The sum of indices is the Euler characteristic zero. Thus there are no zeros.

On the simply connected link cover one may consequently set fv=log⁡(dev⁡v−qv).(16) f_v=\log(\operatorname{dev}_v-q_v). \qquad\text{(16)} Its derivative is everywhere nonsingular. The metric |dfv|2|df_v|^2 descends to a smooth Euclidean metric on the compact torus and is complete. Its lifted developing map fvf_v covers the Euclidean plane; since that plane is simply connected, it is a diffeomorphism. The two generator periods therefore form an injective real rank-two lattice.

Let Pμv,PλvP_{\mu_v},P_{\lambda_v} be these periods along the original oriented primitive curves. Their exponentials are the multipliers computed in (15), so Pμv−Uv,Pλv−Vv∈2πiℤ.(17) P_{\mu_v}-U_v,\quad P_{\lambda_v}-V_v \in2\pi i\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(17)} The integers are continuous along 0<ρ≤10<\rho\le1. The link is nonsingular, its selected flag and original paths are retained, and the nonunit multiplier never disappears.

To fix the integers, use a continuous affine frame near ρ=0\rho=0, and write the longitude as z↦a(ρ)z+b(ρ)z\mapsto a(\rho)z+b(\rho). At completeness a(0)=1a(0)=1 and b(0)≠0b(0)\ne0, because the original primitive longitude is a nonzero translation on the complete Euclidean torus. Its second fixed point is qv=b1−a,(18) q_v=\frac{b}{1-a}, \qquad\text{(18)} which tends to infinity as ρ↓0\rho\downarrow0. The original generator paths pass through finitely many link triangles. Their affine images and Euclidean lengths remain bounded, say by BB and LL, near completeness. Hence |Pγ(ρ)|≤L|qv(ρ)|−B→0.(19) |P_\gamma(\rho)| \le\frac{L}{|q_v(\rho)|-B} \longrightarrow0. \qquad\text{(19)} Also Uv,Vv→0U_v,V_v\to0. Thus both integers in (17) vanish near zero, and then along the entire path. The endpoint values in (15) are exactly the original full primitive periods. This argument used the positive unfilled contraction, not the filling equation at intermediate parameters.

6. The endpoint solid torus

At the endpoint, (1) and (5) give Uv+pvVv=−2πi.(20) U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi i. \qquad\text{(20)} In the affine chart sending the two fixed points to zero and infinity, write upper-half-space coordinates as (z,y)(z,y), put r=y/|z|r=y/|z|, and use 𝜚=arsinh⁡(1/r),θ=ℑfv,X=ℜfv+12log⁡(1+r2).(21) \varrho=\operatorname{arsinh}(1/r),\qquad \theta=\Im f_v,\qquad X=\Re f_v+\tfrac12\log(1+r^2). \qquad\text{(21)} Direct substitution into y−2(|dz|2+dy2)y^{-2}(|dz|^2+dy^2) gives d𝜚2+cosh⁡2𝜚dX2+sinh⁡2𝜚dθ2.(22) d\varrho^2+\cosh^2\varrho\,dX^2 +\sinh^2\varrho\,d\theta^2. \qquad\text{(22)} The original ideal end contains all sufficiently high vertical rays over its finitely many similarity triangles. Those triangles miss the second fixed point, and their quotient is compact. Thus the end contains a full punctured tube 0<𝜚<𝜚00<\varrho<\varrho_0 in (21).

The original primitive curve μv+pvλv\mu_v+p_v\lambda_v has zero real period and angular period exactly −2π-2\pi. Its complementary curve is the original λv\lambda_v, since the change of primitive basis has determinant one. Its positive real period is ℓv=ℜVv=−2ℑtv>0.(23) \ell_v=\Re V_v=-2\Im t_v>0. \qquad\text{(23)} Adding the axis 𝜚=0\varrho=0 therefore gives a smooth solid torus with the prescribed primitive meridian, not a multiple cover or an unspecified slope. Its core length is ℓv\ell_v.

After performing this completion at every selected end, the remaining cusp ends are complete and the middle is compact. The resulting smooth metric is complete and has finite volume on the original marked filling Y(𝒑)Y(\mathbf p). Its universal local development covers simply connected hyperbolic space and is an isometry. This proves the geometric assertion of Theorem 1.

Finally the original tetrahedron interiors partition the metric away from their faces and the completed cores, all of which have measure zero. Their volume sum is therefore the actual hyperbolic volume. The pointwise volume identity gives (6), including its absolute additive constant.

7. The explicit negative-corridor class

Corollary 2. In an original circle tree, select a connected path with at least one outside end, with original selected degrees one or two. Fix negative crossing powers nv=−bvn_v=-b_v, with bv≥3(v∈S),(24) b_v\ge3\qquad(v\in S), \qquad\text{(24)} and retain the original carrier parity and integral slopes. Then the selected filling, with all other auxiliary cusps left complete, has its original marked hyperbolic structure. Its volume is given by (6).

Proof. The negative-corridor variational theorem, Theorem 15, gives an interior positive solution of (5) at unit charges. It also proves ℑtv<0\Im t_v<0. Theorem 1 applies. ▫\square

The threshold in (24) is independent of the number of selected vertices. The last two corner cases in the variational proof use an exact rational interval certificate; its full domain and reproducible bounds are given there. The original slopes and carrier conventions are unchanged. This is a geometric conclusion and does not supply a quantum asymptotic.

If the phase includes only edges incident to the selected path, add 8G=2voct8G=2v_{\mathrm{ oct}} for each omitted complete original edge to the right side of (6). Several nonadjacent selected paths may be treated simultaneously: their incident edge sets are disjoint, even when they share an outside vertex. At that vertex the angle is zero, so all original interface maps and complete periods match in their recorded order.

The full logarithmic period Jacobian at an interior solution is nonsingular: the real part of the return Jacobian is positive definite, whereas the crossing contribution is purely imaginary on the diagonal. Hence the implicit function theorem also gives the marked boundary deformation germ near this complete outside point, with all selected primitive periods fixed at (20). Positivity and the core length signs persist locally. No new meridian or longitude is chosen in this continuation.

The theorem does not resolve arbitrary coefficients two and three in a negative corridor. The exact rational normal form still retains those coefficients, boundary shifts and rotations. Nor does the geometric theorem establish saddle survival in the original finite quantum source. Those remain separate problems, and no cofinite assignment statement follows from this corollary.

8. Reproducing the original-coordinate checks

The separate checker solves sixteen original primitive systems in the explicit corridor class. It checks the logarithmic filling residuals, the core signs, the full rational face denominators and positive tetrahedron shapes along the tangent-radial contraction. It also compares the direct tetrahedron volumes with the calibrated stationary action.

.venv/bin/python experiments/check_positive_corridor_completion.py \
  --precision 110 --output /tmp/positive-corridor-completion-replay.json

The saved replay records 900 positive edge checks along those contractions, at 110 decimal digits. Its script SHA256 is 1041957cc7a3ee5fb21133db3d92e6a1d789ec8266d8b4d005aafb11814e2b64. These are finite checks of the formulas and conventions. The marked topological completion and the uniform theorem are supplied by the written proof above.
