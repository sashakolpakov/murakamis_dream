A common period space for the Frobenius mark and Bloch regulator

Research note, 26 October 2026. This constructs an explicit algebraic connection space containing the previously separate Frobenius/Tate normalization and marked Bloch action. Its integral Heisenberg action retains the actual root phases. It also proves that adjoining the rational Frobenius coordinate cannot erase an existing source class.

This is a geometric comparison target, not a new quantum invariant or an identification of the actual Kashaev period. The selected quantum source and cut still have to be transported to this target. The volume conjecture remains unproved.

1. The logarithmic connection space

Use the normalized marked geometric A-curve C of the primary formulation. On its chosen augmented chart write m=M^2 and l=L_intr^2. All complete-point shifts and SL(2) lift characters remain those recorded in the action calculation. The variables U,V below are the centered logarithms of m,l; in particular U=V=0 at the chosen complete lift.

Put tau=pi*i, and use h=Log q, not Log(q^2). On C times G_m,q write omega_m=dm/m, omega_l=dl/l, omega_q=dq/q. Over this base take an additive coordinate h and a Heisenberg frame

Pi=[1 U H;0 1 V;0 0 1].

The horizontal equations are algebraic:

dh=omega_q, dU=omega_m, dV=omega_l,
dH=U omega_l.                                  (1.1)

They are integrable. The first three forms are closed, and the only remaining obstruction is omega_m wedge omega_l=0 on C. Equivalently dPi=Pi Omega with Omega_12=omega_m, Omega_23=omega_l and the other entries zero. An initial marked frame determines its local horizontal continuation. The fundamental Bloch lift fixes its central period, as already recorded by

R=UV/4-H/2, dR=-(U dV-V dU)/4.                 (1.2)

The preferred-longitude action is therefore the following algebraic coordinate on this connection space:

S=R+UV/4=(UV-H)/2, dS=(V/2)dU.                (1.3)

Indeed adding (1.2) to the differential of UV/4 gives the displayed identity. The original longitude lift must still be retained. Write u=Log M and Log L_intr=kappa*tau+V/2, where kappa is the specified integer at completeness. With the same complete action constant, the uncentered preferred action is

S_lift=S+kappa*tau*U, dS_lift=2 Log(L_intr)du. (1.4)

Its exponential is W^kappa Z in Section 2. This is the full peripheral character, not a discarded sign. At completeness W=1. Compatible real constant phases remain marked; they have no volume norm along the prescribed imaginary h direction.

The coordinates h,U,V,H are algebraic coordinates of the connection space. Their specified horizontal values are periods, not algebraic functions of q,m,l. No finite algebraic cover is claimed to supply those horizontal values.

2. Three standard exponential connections

On h!=0 consider the rational functions

f_J=tau^2/(2h),
f_W=tau U/(2h),
f_Z=S/(2h)=(UV-H)/(4h).                       (2.1)

For each f use the rank-one exponential connection d-df, whose normalized horizontal period is exp(f). This is the standard exponential-connection construction, with its sign specified. Let J,W,Z denote these three periods. Restricting their equations to the horizontal distribution (1.1) gives

dlog J=-tau^2 omega_q/(2h^2),
dlog W=tau omega_m/(2h)-tau U omega_q/(2h^2),
dlog Z=V omega_m/(4h)-S omega_q/(2h^2).        (2.2)

These equations are flat: on the full connection space their forms are the exact differentials df_J,df_W,df_Z; restriction to an integrable horizontal distribution preserves flatness. Thus no classification or pushforward theorem is needed here.

At the prescribed literature roots h_N=tau/N, with U=2 Log M,

J_N=i^N, W_N=M^N,
Z_N=exp(N S/(2tau)).                          (2.3)

The Frobenius meridian is precisely W. All the earlier phases are values of the same specified Tate period:

zeta_N=i/J_N=i^(1-N),
epsilon_N=J_N^2=(-1)^N,
eta_N=-i J_N=i^(N+3)=zeta_N^3.               (2.4)

There are no level-dependent arbitrary phase choices. For fixed h, the meridian step U->U+2h sends W to exp(tau)W=-W. This recovers the original half-Frobenius sign. It does not assert that finite meridian transport of Z is already the actual quantum-A operator; Z is the marked action line to which the quantum normal block must be compared.

These formulas specify an explicit Tate exponential lift of the known root phases. Their agreement at roots alone does not prove that the actual full quantum state has the q-direction connection in (2.2).

C here is the commutative geometric A-curve used for the classical action. These root restrictions do not identify its coordinates with the root-central spectral coordinates of an actual quantum operator. The distinction in the bridge, Section 4 remains in force.

3. The integral Heisenberg action lifts by actual monomials

On the marked logarithmic cover consider the standard integral Heisenberg transformations indexed by integers A,B,C_0:

U'=U+2tau A, V'=V+2tau B,
H'=H+2tau A V+4tau^2 C_0.                   (3.1)

They preserve (1.1). Direct substitution into (2.1) gives

J'=J,
W'=J^(2A) W,
Z'=J^(2(AB-C_0)) W^B Z.                    (3.2)

The exponential changes have become algebraic monomials in the retained period coordinates. To check composition, applying g_1 then g_2 gives

A=A_1+A_2, B=B_1+B_2,
C_0=C_1+C_2+A_2 B_1.

The exponent of J in the product of (3.2) is exactly 2(AB-C_0). This proves the full central composition law, not only equality of determinants or absolute values.

For P=exp(2R/tau), (1.2) gives

P'=exp(B U-A V)P.                            (3.3)

The extra exponential is exp(2tau(AB-2C_0))=1, using integrality. Thus the lift recovers the regulator line’s existing deck action. At a root, the central factor J^(2(AB-C_0)) is the retained phase (-1)^(N(AB-C_0)); the other factor is M^(NB).

This is covariance for the displayed integral lattice on the marked continuation cover. It does not assert that every monodromy constant of an arbitrary unipotent connection is a Tate period. Any further flattening cover or central mark must be transported, not silently identified with this lattice. The fixed fundamental Bloch lift is still the datum selecting H’s central value. The displayed lattice acts on the peripheral period charts. Its subgroup preserving specified square-root eigenvalue lifts may be smaller; no action of every integer triple on a single fixed SL(2) augmented component is assumed.

4. Its root norm is exactly the marked regulator norm

Let b(U,V)=Re U Im V-Re V Im U. The existing regulator norm is

log||P||=(2/pi)Im R-b(U,V)/(2pi)
         =2D(beta)/pi.                       (4.1)

Before taking norms, the marked logarithm gives exactly

Z_N=exp((N/4)Log P+N UV/(8tau)),
Log P=2R/tau.

Thus no fractional power of an unmarked scalar P has been chosen.

At h=tau/N define the action-line norm by

log||Z_N||=log|Z_N|-N Re U Im V/(4pi).

Since Im S=Im R+(Re U Im V+Re V Im U)/4, substitution proves the exact identity

log||Z_N||=(N/4)log||P||=N D(beta)/(2pi).      (4.2)

It is invariant under (3.1)–(3.2). J_N has modulus one; the change B log|W_N|=NB Re U/2 in log|Z_N| is canceled by the change in the boundary correction. At U=V=0 no correction is needed. For the marked complete geometric period,

(2pi/N)log|Z_N|=Vol(S^3 minus K).             (4.3)

This is an exact evaluation of the constructed geometric action period. It is not an identity for F_N(1). Replacing Z_N by the actual Kashaev value in (4.3) is precisely the missing comparison, including its full amplitude and selected source coupling.

5. What the meridian blowup resolves, and what it retains

On the identity chart of the uniform source base,

s=sinh(U/2)/sinh h,
U=2 arsinh(s sinh h).

Then W is exactly the previously constructed period

W=exp((tau/h)arsinh(s sinh h)).               (5.1)

It is regular and even in h near h=0 for bounded s, and its exceptional value is exp(tau*s). The cap slope remains (dW/ds)_(s=0)=tau sinh h/h, with its marked limit tau.

The full joint period space also retains J. It is irregular at h=0, although every J_N has modulus one. The finite phase cover J^4=1 is valid on the prescribed root fibers; it is not invariant under the q-direction connection in (2.2):

d(J^4)=-2tau^2 J^4 dh/h^2.

Consequently freezing a residue-class phase, as in the meridian difference base, does not construct its joint q-direction period connection. There is no contradiction with the fixed-sheet parity statements of the preceding note, which concern meridian source coefficients on that sheet.

The root graphs are therefore not Zariski dense in this enlarged Tate space: J^4-1 vanishes on all of them. The earlier density theorem applies on the specified finite phase components of the meridian difference base. It cannot reconstruct the q-direction connection from root evaluations. In particular (2.2) is a specified period lift, rather than a unique interpolation theorem for the full quantum state.

The actual complete cap has a joint lift on this space:

d_cap(h)=-i J(h) h/tau,
dlog d_cap=(1/h-tau^2/(2h^2))dh.              (5.2)

At h_N=tau/N it is exactly eta_N/N, with modulus 1/N. The previous fixed-phase regular cap is the h factor after its specified Tate line is recorded. Formula (5.2) retains that line, including the even-level sign.

Fixed rational Tate constants in an action are also retained by finite root lines of J. For example, Li_2(1)=pi^2/6 gives exp(Li_2(1)/(2h))=J^(-1/6) on its marked cover. Such a factor has modulus one at the prescribed roots. This observation does not control an unknown full quantum amplitude.

The geometric action line retains an additional irregular factor. At the complete hyperbolic point, Im S_0=Vol(M)>0, so the term S_0/(2h) cannot be removed by the meridian blowup. This is the period carrying volume. It remains part of the marked exponential connection, rather than becoming a regular algebraic function after the Frobenius normalization has been resolved.

6. Rational Frobenius base change preserves source classes

Let (F,sigma) be a characteristic-zero difference field and let C in GL_d(F). Adjoin an independent w with sigma(w)=-w and put E=F(w). The Laurent constant-coefficient map at w=0,

r:E->F, r(t)=[w^0]t,

is F-linear, is the identity on F, and commutes with sigma. This is a coefficient extraction, not evaluation of a possibly polar rational function at zero. Existence of the Laurent expansion over F gives all three assertions. Hence

r((sigma-C)t)=(sigma-C)r(t).

It induces a left inverse on the additive source quotients:

F^d/(sigma-C)F^d -> E^d/(sigma-C)E^d.         (6.1)

In particular (6.1) is injective. The same argument works for sigma(w)=chiw, chi in F^, and for finitely many independent diagonally shifted variables, by iterated Laurent extraction. A further finite sigma-stable algebraic cover does not change the conclusion, by normalized trace as in the affine-source proof.

Thus a nonsplit source already defined over C(q,M) remains nonsplit on the rational Frobenius base, including on each specified phase component and after finite stable covers. Principalizing its meridian lattice does not furnish a horizontal splitting. This statement does not say that two specified nonsplit extensions cannot be compared; their class difference is the actual comparison to compute.

The hypothesis that C and the source descend to F matters. This does not by itself classify source matrices that genuinely depend on w, or compare an unknown homogeneous gauge over E. Nor does it apply to unrestricted completed or analytic period extensions such as the logarithmic/exponential construction above.

7. A degree obstruction to compressing a nonsplit root period

Assume F=K(q,M), with K a fixed number field, and a specified nonsplit equation sigma(v)=Cv+c over F. Write C=A_0/D and c=b_0/D with Laurent-polynomial coefficients. Suppose a proposed root-level rational section has a presentation v=a/B in K(q,M,w)^d. Its cleared residual is

R=D sigma(a) B-A_0 a sigma(B)-b_0 B sigma(B).   (7.1)

Some component of R is nonzero: otherwise (6.1) would produce a generic rational splitting. If v is an exact solution on the whole graph q=q_N,w=M^N, that component vanishes on the graph. The root-graph rigidity theorem therefore implies

D_M(R)>=N or [K:Q] D_q(R)>=phi(2N),          (7.2)

where D_M is the M exponent span and D_q is the q exponent span after a Laurent monomial is cleared. All denominators must be defined as rational whole graphs. The coefficient field must remain fixed; allowing arbitrary cyclotomic coefficients changes the stated field-degree bound.

For example, if the fixed operator data have absolute M and q exponents bounded by c_M,c_q, and a,B have exponents bounded by d_M,d_q, (7.1) has

D_M(R)<=2c_M+4d_M,
D_q(R)<=2c_q+4d_q+2d_M.                      (7.3)

The last term records sigma(Mj)=qj M^j. No bound on the w degree is needed for the exponent-separation argument. Thus a fixed bounded rational presentation of a nonsplit period cannot recover every large root level merely by adjoining w.

This applies to an actual knot source only after its specified generic source and nonsplitting hypotheses have been proved. The adequacy note supplies such a nonsplitting consequence under its explicitly stated actual-source hypotheses; it does not construct that source for every knot. A bounded comparison between two transcendental periods is not excluded by (7.2): the periods can be complicated while their intertwiner is simple.

8. A normalization criterion on this common space

There is a direct extension of the fixed central normalization theorem. Let a comparison factor have a fixed finite algebraic presentation over C(q,t,M,W,J), restricted by

q=exp(tau/N), t=1/N, W=M^N, J=i^N.

If its chosen whole M=1 value r_N is finite and nonzero, then log|r_N|=O(log N). Indeed J takes only four values. On each phase sheet the previous theorem applies; taking the maximum of the four bounds proves the assertion. Fixed finite roots of J have only finitely many values as well. Poles or zeros common to an entire phase must first be canceled on that restricted algebraic branch, as in the previous whole-germ theorem; an undefined restriction is not an evaluation.

Consequently an actual exact comparison F_N(1)=r_N Z_N(complete) of this specified kind would prove the volume conjecture, with error O(log N/N). This is a sufficient comparison criterion, not a hypothesis being asserted for the actual period. A formal unit in h need not have such a finite algebraic presentation or control its selected root realization. Nor does the criterion permit discarding other period components or their cancellations.

9. Scope, existing coverage, and verification

The positive ideal triangulation supplies beta, its central H mark, and the geometric A-curve used in Section 1. The construction then places its regulator action, the actual half-Frobenius mark, and the root phases in one explicit exponential-connection space. The source-class lemma prevents confusing this larger coefficient space with a splitting of the actual quantum source.

The subsequent controlled cyclic Euler source now supplies a specific local comparison with uniform control. The literature cyclic coefficient vector is an explicit reflected factorial source vector, with its full inhomogeneous equation and co-source retained. Its scalar has a direct norm-product formula and polynomial bounds despite growing algebraic degree. This is one actual local source comparison, not the full source-to-Z map or a proof of its selected geometric coupling.

What remains is the actual source-compatible map, with its cut, lattice transitions, quantum amplitude and selected relative period. No geometric factor of a minimal actual operator or nonzero geometric coupling of the selected finite sum is inferred here.

Polylogarithmic period connections and Bloch regulators have existing coverage in Hain, Classical Polylogarithms. Exponential connections and rescaled twists have existing coverage in Sabbah–Yu, 1406.1339. Only scope and the standard exponential-connection definition are used; no Hodge, strictness, pushforward or comparison theorem from these works is a proof premise. The proofs here are the displayed differentiation, monomial and coefficient calculations. No novelty is claimed for the Heisenberg connection, exponential twists, coefficient descent or elementary cyclotomic degree bound.

The exact audit checks the action differential, all three connection forms, the noncommutative integral deck composition, phase identifications, the invariant root norm, and source-class retraction with moving and polar denominators. A non-knot root example checks that an exact levelwise splitting can fail the generic source equation. Results.
