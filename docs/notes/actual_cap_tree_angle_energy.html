Complete ordinary-cap trees have one positive angle-simplex energy

29 October 2026. An arbitrary binary tree of the ORIGINAL ordinary-cap reassociation packets contracts exactly. Its internal factorials telescope, its actual phases remain explicit, and its full squared kernel is ONE positive angle-simplex weight. After its known row normalization the complete boundary packet is a coisometry; overlaps between any two such normalized cap trees are contractive. This holds at arbitrary fixed width, including all endpoint indices and arbitrary adjoining tensors.

This is a constructive boundary contraction theorem. It does not say that a knot’s interior operator preserves these source packets, that complete-channel Racah matrices are contractive, or that the remaining marked cap is an ordinary cap. No new whole-knot volume estimate follows from this theorem alone.

1. The literal node and its source type

Use N>=2, d=N-1, q=exp(pi i/N), Q=q^2, x=-q^-1, F_a=(Q;Q)_a, s_a=|F_a|, and the physical gauge

B_a=N^(1/4)/sqrt(s_a).

Let A=(C’_23)^(-1)C’_12, with the original finite Euler maps. The proved original pivotal-cap identity uses ellhat=sqrt(N)ell, where ell is the LITERAL original ordinary cap including its pivot. Its gauged map Khat=(ellhat_12 tensor I)A is

Khat_(n;(a,b,c))
  =delta_(b,d)delta_(n,a+c<=d)
     q^[a(n-N-1)] sqrt(w_d(a,c,d-n)),
w_d(i,j,k)=1/(s_i s_j s_k).                              (1)

The b=d selection is the result of the COMPLETE pivotal q-Pascal sum, not a discarded input channel. In the ungauged physical coordinates and with the literal ell the same node is

Kraw_(n;(a,b,c))
  =delta_(b,d)delta_(n,a+c<=d)
    N^(-1) p(a) F_n/(F_a F_c),
p(a)=(-1)^a Q^[a(a-1)/2].                               (2)

The original Cartan/framing/peripheral lines on any exterior are unchanged. Equation (2) uses x=-q^-1; the full unreduced meridian factors in the local theorem must instead be retained off completeness.

2. A source-exact complete tree, with every phase retained

Choose any planar binary tree T with l>=1 ordered leaves. Its original physical input has 2l-1 heads: l active leaves, and one separator head between each two successive leaves. At an internal node, first contract its left and right subtrees, then apply the three-head node (1) to their two outputs and the original separator between them. Each separator is used once. These are ordinary compositions of the specified actual maps.

Every nonzero input has ALL l-1 separator indices d. If the active indices are a_1,…,a_l, its root index is n=sum_i a_i<=d. Conversely every such input is allowed: all intermediate subtree sums are at most n. There is exactly one intermediate root index at every node. Therefore no unperformed internal summation or cyclic alias remains.

For an internal vertex v write n_v for its subtree sum and n_L(v) for its left-subtree sum. The complete normalized-cap, quarter-gauged kernel is

Khat_T(n;a_1,...,a_l; separators)
  =delta_(separators,d)delta_(n=sum a_i<=d)
    N^(-(l-1)/2) sqrt(s_n/product_i s_(a_i)) q^(Theta_T),
Theta_T=sum_(v internal) n_L(v)(n_v-N-1).                 (3)

Proof. Replace each square root in (1) by N^(-1/2)sqrt(s_(n_v)/(s_(n_L)s_(n_R))), using s_j s_(d-j)=N. Every internal s cancels between its output and its parent’s input. The phases multiply to exactly the displayed sum. They have NOT been replaced by one, nor asserted to cancel on a cycle.

There is an independent exact polynomial form for the LITERAL original cap tree before physical gauging:

Kraw_T=N^(-(l-1)) F_n/(product_i F_(a_i))
                        product_(v internal) p(n_L(v)). (4)

This follows by the same cancellation using (2), and specifies every original root phase. In particular it is not a norm-only construction. The literal gauged cap tree equals N^(-(l-1)/2)Khat_T: each use of ellhat in (3) restores its stated ell=ellhat/sqrt(N) factor. All input and output B gauges are explicit, and their internal inverse factors cancel under composition. No separator-state norm is silently substituted for its actual physical coordinate.

3. Exact positive simplex incidence and all-width energy

By reflection, the squared surviving entry (3) is

|Khat_T|^2=N^(2-l)/[s_(d-n) product_i s_(a_i)].            (5)

Thus its true nonnegative integer charges are

(a_0,a_1,...,a_l), a_0=d-n, sum_(i=0)^l a_i=d.           (6)

This is a bijection, not a relaxation: every composition in (6) is one allowed input/root combination of the complete tree. The internal subtree charges are its specified partial sums. All zero angles and zero-length slots are retained by s_0=1.

Let

E_(l,n)=N^(-(l-1))s_n
               sum_(a_1+...+a_l=n) product_i s_(a_i)^(-1).

Different root rows have disjoint input-degree support, hence EXACTLY

Khat_T Khat_T^*=diag(E_(l,0),...,E_(l,d)),
||Khat_T||_op^2=max_n E_(l,n),
||Khat_T||_HS^2=N^(2-l) Z_(l+1,N),
Z_(m,N)=sum_(a_0+...+a_(m-1)=d) product_i s_(a_i)^(-1). (7)

These quantities are independent of the binary tree. Equations (3)–(7) are an exact controlled contraction for the entire ordinary-cap tree, not independent Cauchy bounds at its nodes.

For fixed l>=2 put Lambda(theta)=-integral_0^theta log(2sin t)dt. The uniform factorial estimate at ALL 0<=a<N gives

log||Khat_T||_op^2=log||Khat_T||_HS^2+O(log N)
  =N (l+1)Lambda(pi/(l+1))/pi+O_l(log N).                 (8)

Here the first equality follows directly from the N rows in (7). To prove the second, the continuum objective on (6) is sum_i Lambda(pi a_i/N). On the simplex of nonnegative angles summing to pi its unique maximum, for m=l+1>=3, is m Lambda(pi/m). For completeness its Hessian on the interior is -diag(cot theta_i). If all angles are below pi/2 it is negative on sum-zero vectors. If one angle exceeds pi/2, put S=sum of the other angles<pi/2. Weighted Cauchy and sum tan(theta_i)<tan S=-tan(theta_big) show the same strict negativity on the sum-zero tangent. Equality at pi/2 follows directly from the positive remaining cotangents. Thus the function is strictly concave; symmetry fixes its maximum at equal angles. Continuity includes the boundary. Rounding an equal composition to the integer sum d gives the lower bound at every sufficiently large level; there are only polynomially many compositions and all factorial errors are O_l(log N), giving the upper bound. No lattice progression is needed. For l=1 the map is identity, E_(1,n)=1, and its energy exponent is zero.

There is a stronger UNIFORM root-conditioned formula. For every 0<=n<=d, including both endpoints, put t=n/N. Then

log E_(l,n)=(N/pi)Phi_l(t)+O_l(log N),
Phi_l(t)=l Lambda(pi*t/l)-Lambda(pi*t).                 (9)

The constant is independent of n. For fixed sum of the l leaf angles, at most pi, the concavity argument just given places their maximum at equal values. The case l=2 at total pi may have additional maximizers, without affecting this value. For the lower estimate, distribute the integer n among l parts differing by at most one. Each angle changes by O_l(1/N), whose Lambda error is O_l(log N/N) uniformly even at zero or pi. There are at most (N+1)^(l-1) compositions. Applying the uniform factorial estimate to every term in E_(l,n), and to s_n, therefore proves BOTH sides of (9), with the stated uniform error.

For l>=2 and 0<t<1 the explicit boundary action satisfies

Phi_l'(t)=pi log[sin(pi*t)/sin(pi*t/l)],
Phi_l''(t)=pi^2[cot(pi*t)-(1/l)cot(pi*t/l)]<0.          (9a)

Indeed x cot x is strictly decreasing on (0,pi), since its derivative has numerator sin(x)cos(x)-x<0. Its unique maximum is t=l/(l+1). Using Lambda(pi-theta)=-Lambda(theta), the maximum is exactly (l+1)Lambda(pi/(l+1)), giving (8). Formula (9) also includes a prescribed root tending to t=1, with rate l Lambda(pi/l)/pi. For l=1, E_(1,n)=1 and Phi_1=0 identically.

4. Exact coherent packet overlaps are contractive

Every E_(l,n) is positive and known without a knot value. Define

R_T=diag(E_(l,n)^(-1/2)) Khat_T.

Then R_T R_T^=I_N. Consequently P_T=R_T^R_T is a specified orthogonal projection on the full physical input space, including its literal separator-d selection. For ANY two planar binary trees on the same ordered heads,

R_T R_U^* is diagonal and ||R_T R_U^*||<=1.              (10)

The diagonal entry is the ACTUAL mixed phase sum of the two root rows, divided by E_(l,n); Cauchy proves its modulus at most one. This retains the off-diagonal leaf copies inside P_T and the full phases Theta_T. An arbitrary finite sequence of these packet overlaps is contractive. No artificial dephasing, ordinary positive pivotal trace or favorable choice of unknown source has been introduced.

For a specified remaining row z on the root, its complete original boundary-functional norm remains exactly

||z Khat_T||^2=sum_n |z_n|^2 E_(l,n).                    (11)

One may not drop these root weights, replace a marked row by an ordinary cap, or normalize z using an unknown period. For literal caps multiply the energies by N^(-(l-1)) as stated after (4).

5. What this gives for global gluing

This proves a genuine positive angle incidence for a complete tree source: (6), including all its internal actual partial sums. It is a single angle simplex. It is NOT yet the edge/peripheral incidence of a knot ideal triangulation. The identity applies to arbitrary fixed width and to any polynomial or matrix exterior because it is an actual boundary-operator identity.

The complete-channel Racah transition on an interior port has additional channels beyond these ordinary-cap packets. It is not the compression R_T R_U^*. Nor is an arbitrary interior R word proved to preserve P_T. The full marked packet in the local theorem must also remain. Thus (10) is a constructive boundary-tree overlap estimate; using it as a unitary or complete projection on an arbitrary knot interior would be an extra unproved step. The original full-source goal still needs the corresponding complete-channel or source-specific interior control.
