Kashaev’s paired-face boundary adapter and its cyclic carry

Let N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, and let QQ be a primitive NN-th root of unity. The function in Kashaev’s equation (4.28) defines an explicit map from one crossing index to a pair of boundary-face indices. This note proves its quotient, inverse, norm and cyclic-carry properties directly from that function. Its finite algebra works at every level. The tetrahedral state in the cited paper is restricted to odd levels; no even-level extension of that state is asserted here.

1. The specified finite function

Choose nonzero complex numbers A,BA,B and roots

aN=A,bN=B,r=a/b. a^N=A,\qquad b^N=B,\qquad r=a/b.

Residues [k][k] always lie in {0,…,d}\{0,\ldots,d\}. Define

f(k)=a[k−1]b[−k],Ψkj=Qjkf(k),0≤j,k<N.(1) f(k)=a^{[k-1]}b^{[-k]},\qquad \Psi_{kj}=Q^{jk}f(k),\qquad 0\le j,k<N. \qquad\text{(1)}

In the published notation, a=s0ma=s_{0m}, b=sm5b=s_{m5}, and

A=s(u0)−s(um),B=s(um)−s(u5). A=s(u_0)-s(u_m),\qquad B=s(u_m)-s(u_5).

Thus (1) is exactly the function ψm,jk\psi^k_{m,j} of (4.28), with its modular powers retained. In particular

f(0)=ad,f(k)=ak−1bN−k=Bark(1≤k<N).(2) f(0)=a^d,\qquad f(k)=a^{k-1}b^{N-k}=\frac{B}{a}r^k\quad(1\le k<N). \qquad\text{(2)}

The value at zero is not obtained by extending the second expression without its cyclic carry.

Let Fkj=N−1/2QjkF_{kj}=N^{-1/2}Q^{jk}. Orthogonality of the finite geometric sum gives F*F=IF^*F=I. With Df=diag⁡(f(k))D_f=\operatorname{diag}(f(k)),

Ψ=NDfF,(Ψ−1)jk=Q−jkNf(k).(3) \Psi=\sqrt N D_fF,\qquad (\Psi^{-1})_{jk}=\frac{Q^{-jk}}{Nf(k)}. \qquad\text{(3)}

These are invertible matrices for every stated N,A,BN,A,B, with no primality assumption. The star on FF concerns the fixed root of unity only; no complex conjugation of A,BA,B enters the inverse.

2. The paired-face quotient and its exact projector

Fix a charge c∈ℤ/Nℤc\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z. On a face pair write α−,α+∈ℤ/Nℤ\alpha_-,\alpha_+\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z, and put

k=α−−α++c. k=\alpha_- -\alpha_+ +c.

Define Jc:ℂN→ℂN2J_c:\mathbb C^N\longrightarrow\mathbb C^{N^2} by

(Jcv)(α−,α+)=v(α−−α++c).(4) (J_cv)(\alpha_-,\alpha_+)=v(\alpha_- -\alpha_+ +c). \qquad\text{(4)}

Its image ℋc\mathcal H_c consists precisely of the arrays invariant under simultaneous translation of both face indices. This invariant subspace is independent of cc; the charge specifies its coordinate. Each value of kk has exactly NN preimages. Consequently

Jc*Jc=NI,Pdiag=1NJcJc*=1N∑t∈ℤ/NℤTt,(5) J_c^*J_c=NI,\qquad P_{\mathrm{ diag}}=\frac1N J_cJ_c^* =\frac1N\sum_{t\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z}T_t, \qquad\text{(5)}

where (Ttw)(α−,α+)=w(α−+t,α++t)(T_tw)(\alpha_-,\alpha_+)=w(\alpha_-+t,\alpha_++t). This proves both that the quotient has dimension NN and that ιc=Jc/N\iota_c=J_c/\sqrt N is an isometry onto its invariant realization. Explicitly, the quotient is ℂN2/span⁡{(Tt−I)w}\mathbb C^{N^2}/\operatorname{span}\{(T_t-I)w\}; the averaging projector in (5) identifies it with ℋc\mathcal H_c.

The literal boundary adapter and a specified left inverse are

𝒜c=JcΨ,ℒc=Ψ−1Jc*N. \mathcal A_c=J_c\Psi,\qquad \mathcal L_c=\Psi^{-1}\frac{J_c^*}{N}.

They satisfy

ℒc𝒜c=I,𝒜cℒc=Pdiag,𝒜c=NιcDfF.(6) \mathcal L_c\mathcal A_c=I,\qquad \mathcal A_c\mathcal L_c=P_{\mathrm{ diag}},\qquad \mathcal A_c=N\iota_cD_fF. \qquad\text{(6)}

Thus the adapter is a rank-NN map into the full face-pair space, and an isomorphism onto its diagonal-translation quotient. It is not an isomorphism onto all N2N^2 boundary coordinates. Charge shifts permute the kk coordinate and do not alter any singular value.

3. Exact singular values and uniform decoration bounds

The singular values of Ψ\Psi are N|f(k)|\sqrt N|f(k)|; the nonzero singular values of 𝒜c\mathcal A_c are N|f(k)|N|f(k)|. Formula (2) gives

|f(0)|=|A|d/N,|f(k)|=|A|(k−1)/N|B|(N−k)/N(k≥1). |f(0)|=|A|^{d/N},\qquad |f(k)|=|A|^{(k-1)/N}|B|^{(N-k)/N}\quad(k\ge1).

All exponents are nonnegative and sum to d/Nd/N. Both extreme values occur, at k=0k=0 and k=1k=1. Hence

mink|f(k)|=min⁡{|A|,|B|}d/N,maxk|f(k)|=max⁡{|A|,|B|}d/N, \min_k|f(k)|=\min\{|A|,|B|\}^{d/N},\qquad \max_k|f(k)|=\max\{|A|,|B|\}^{d/N}, κ2(Ψ)=κ2(𝒜c)=(max⁡{|A|,|B|}min⁡{|A|,|B|})d/N.(7) \kappa_2(\Psi)=\kappa_2(\mathcal A_c) =\left(\frac{\max\{|A|,|B|\}}{\min\{|A|,|B|\}}\right)^{d/N}. \qquad\text{(7)}

In (7), the rectangular condition number uses its nonzero singular values. On a fixed compact set of nonzero A,BA,B, the normalized adapter 𝒜c/N=ιcDfF\mathcal A_c/N=\iota_cD_fF and its inverse on the image have bounds independent of NN. No factorial diagonal gauge is being substituted for (1).

These bounds retain every fixed-order decoration derivative. Work on a simply connected parameter neighborhood with holomorphic choices of log⁡A,log⁡B\log A,\log B; each root differs from their exponential roots by a constant root of unity. In (2), f(k)f(k) is then a constant phase times AukBvkA^{u_k}B^{v_k}, where uk,vk∈[0,1]u_k,v_k\in[0,1]. For the inverse the two exponents lie in [−1,0][-1,0]. Differentiation multiplies these powers by falling factorials of bounded real exponents and by fixed negative powers of A,BA,B. On every compact subset KK of this neighborhood, for each fixed multi-index ν\nu,

supK∥∂νDf∥+supK∥∂νDf−1∥≤CK,ν,(8) \sup_K\|\partial^\nu D_f\|+ \sup_K\|\partial^\nu D_f^{-1}\|\le C_{K,\nu}, \qquad\text{(8)}

uniformly in NN and in the constant root choices. Equations (3),(6) give the corresponding normalized operator bounds. Composition with the linear differences of vertex decorations gives the same result for those parameters. This does not differentiate the root QQ off the root-of-unity locus or identify decoration derivatives with the original meridian or quantum-normal directions.

4. The cyclic seam and its complete monodromy

Let (Sv)(k)=v(k+1)(Sv)(k)=v(k+1), with cyclic indices. Direct substitution in (2), including k=N−1k=N-1, gives

Sf−rf=B−Abδ0,f(k+1)f(k)=r[1+(BA−1)δk0].(9) Sf-rf=\frac{B-A}{b}\,\delta_0, \qquad \frac{f(k+1)}{f(k)} =r\left[1+\left(\frac BA-1\right)\delta_{k0}\right]. \qquad\text{(9)}

Equivalently, with E0E_0 the zero-coordinate projector,

Df−1SDf=r[I+(B/A−1)E0]S.(10) D_f^{-1}SD_f=r[I+(B/A-1)E_0]S. \qquad\text{(10)}

This is a rank-one correction to the geometric progression, not an assertion that its norm is one. Its norm contribution is |r||B/A−1||r|\,|B/A-1|, uniformly bounded on the compacts used in (8). The multiplier accumulated around the complete cyclic index is

rNBA=ABBA=1.(11) r^N\frac BA=\frac AB\frac BA=1. \qquad\text{(11)}

Thus (10) has NN-th power II, as also follows by conjugating SN=IS^N=I. With face charge cc, the seam is the actual locus α−−α+=−c\alpha_- -\alpha_+=-c. Replacing (9) by a seam-free progression would change the monodromy unless A=BA=B.

5. Four legs and the full boundary normalization

Take four nonzero pairs Am=amN,Bm=bmNA_m=a_m^N,B_m=b_m^N, charges cmc_m, and a nonzero root s05s_{05}. For an arbitrary four-index array R=(Rj1j2j3j4)R=(R_{j_1j_2j_3j_4}), define its boundary array by the literal right-hand expression of the published (4.25):

ℬR(𝜶−,𝜶+)=s05d∑j1,…,j4Rj1j2j3j4∏m=14Ψαm,−−αm,++cm,jm(m).(12) \mathcal B_R(\boldsymbol\alpha_-,\boldsymbol\alpha_+) =s_{05}^{d}\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_4}R_{j_1j_2j_3j_4} \prod_{m=1}^4\Psi^{(m)}_{\alpha_{m,-}-\alpha_{m,+}+c_m,j_m}. \qquad\text{(12)}

No property of RR is needed for the following exact identities:

ℬR=s05dN4(⨂m=14ιcmDfmF)R, \mathcal B_R=s_{05}^{d}N^4 \left(\bigotimes_{m=1}^4\iota_{c_m}D_{f_m}F\right)R, R=1s05dN4(⨂m=14F*Dfm−1ιcm*)ℬR.(13) R=\frac1{s_{05}^{d}N^4} \left(\bigotimes_{m=1}^4F^*D_{f_m}^{-1}\iota_{c_m}^*\right) \mathcal B_R. \qquad\text{(13)}

In particular the N4N^4 factor is the four-leg paired-face normalization; it is not removed by using normalized Fourier matrices. The image projector is ⨂mPdiag\bigotimes_mP_{\mathrm{ diag}}, and the map has rank N4N^4. Its norm bounds are precisely

|s05|dN4∏mmink|fm(k)|∥R∥2≤∥ℬR∥2≤|s05|dN4∏mmaxk|fm(k)|∥R∥2.(14) |s_{05}|^dN^4\prod_m\min_k|f_m(k)|\,\|R\|_2 \le\|\mathcal B_R\|_2 \le |s_{05}|^dN^4\prod_m\max_k|f_m(k)|\,\|R\|_2. \qquad\text{(14)}

For published vertex decorations, Am+Bm=s05NA_m+B_m=s_{05}^N for every leg. When these differences range over fixed nonzero compacts, |s05|d=|s05N|d/N|s_{05}|^d=|s_{05}^N|^{d/N} and its fixed-order derivatives are uniformly bounded, as are its inverse and the normalized maps (13). The four-leg exponential scale is therefore unchanged under these specific fixed-decoration bounds.

Original tensor contractions use their specified dual rows. If a column is sent to 𝒜cv\mathcal A_cv, its row ℓ\ell is transported to ℓℒc\ell\mathcal L_c; then ℓℒc𝒜cv=ℓv\ell\mathcal L_c\mathcal A_cv=\ell v exactly. The inverse uses f(k)−1f(k)^{-1}, not f(k)¯\overline{f(k)}. Replacing the transported row by a Hermitian adjoint changes the contraction in general. Positive singular-value estimates do not authorize that replacement. For an arbitrary row on the full face-pair space, only its restriction through PdiagP_{\mathrm{ diag}} contributes on the adapter image.

6. Opposite-face sewing retains the original wire clock

Use the same roots a,ba,b on two paired-face adapters. Reverse the face order in the second adapter and impose the charge condition c+c′=1c+c'=1 modulo NN. Their arguments are then

k=α−−α++c,k′=α+−α−+c′=[1−k]. k=\alpha_- -\alpha_+ +c,\qquad k'=\alpha_+ -\alpha_- +c'=[1-k].

The exact complement identity is

f(k)f([1−k])=(ab)d.(15) f(k)f([1-k])=(ab)^d. \qquad\text{(15)}

At k=0,1k=0,1, this is adbd=(ab)da^db^d=(ab)^d. For 2≤k<N2\le k<N, the second residue is N+1−kN+1-k; the two exponents of aa sum to (k−1)+(N−k)=d(k-1)+(N-k)=d, and those of bb sum to (N−k)+(k−1)=d(N-k)+(k-1)=d. Thus (15) also includes both cyclic endpoints and N=2N=2.

There are NN face pairs with each argument kk. Bilinear contraction therefore gives, with Zjj′=Qjδjj′Z_{jj'}=Q^j\delta_{jj'},

∑α−,α+𝒜c(α−,α+;j)𝒜c′(α+,α−;j′)=N(ab)d∑k=0N−1Qjk+j′(1−k)=N2(ab)dQjδjj′.(16) \begin{split} &\sum_{\alpha_-,\alpha_+} \mathcal A_c(\alpha_-,\alpha_+;j) \mathcal A_{c'}(\alpha_+,\alpha_-;j')\\ &\quad=N(ab)^d \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}Q^{jk+j'(1-k)} =N^2(ab)^dQ^j\delta_{jj'}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(16)}

If the specified non-link edge factors are a−db−da^{-d}b^{-d}, they cancel the decoration factor in (16), leaving N2ZN^2Z. The factor QjQ^j is the wire factor appearing in the original planar source (3.6). It is not an identity pairing or a Hermitian square. The statement here requires exactly the roots, reversed face order and complementary charges just specified; it does not prove that a proposed global triangulation has those data.

Even changing only the root branches retains an explicit wire operator. If the second adapter instead uses a′=Qua,b′=Qvba'=Q^u a,\ b'=Q^v b, then f′(l)=Q(u−v)l−uf(l). f'(l)=Q^{(u-v)l-u}f(l). Repeating the same finite sum yields ∑α−,α+𝒜ca,b(α−,α+;j)𝒜c′a′,b′(α+,α−;j′)=N2(ab)dQj−uδj′,j−u+v.(17) \sum_{\alpha_-,\alpha_+} \mathcal A_c^{a,b}(\alpha_-,\alpha_+;j) \mathcal A_{c'}^{a',b'}(\alpha_+,\alpha_-;j') =N^2(ab)^dQ^{j-u}\delta_{j',\,j-u+v}. \qquad\text{(17)} Thus branch changes give a recorded phase and index translation; they cannot silently be replaced by the common-root formula.

7. The literal fixed cut and its one-face marginal

The planar definition in the primary paper fixes both open-edge indices to zero in (3.2). Its rank-one cut operator in that index basis is E00E_{00}. Transport this particular operator, with the inverse row from (6), to the paired-face space:

ℰc=𝒜cE00ℒc. \mathcal E_c=\mathcal A_cE_{00}\mathcal L_c.

For row index (α−,α+)(\alpha_-,\alpha_+), column index (α−′,α+′)(\alpha'_-,\alpha'_+), and their respective differences k,k′k,k', (3),(6) give

(ℰc)α−α+;α−′α+′=f(k)N2f(k′).(18) (\mathcal E_c)_{\alpha_-\alpha_+;\,\alpha'_-\alpha'_+} =\frac{f(k)}{N^2 f(k')}. \qquad\text{(18)}

Thus ℰc2=ℰc\mathcal E_c^2=\mathcal E_c, its rank and trace are one, and Pdiagℰc=ℰcPdiag=ℰcP_{\mathrm{ diag}}\mathcal E_c=\mathcal E_cP_{\mathrm{ diag}}=\mathcal E_c. These identities follow either from (6) or directly from (18). They retain the fixed-index cut; no change of the original planar index basis is made in identifying E00E_{00}.

Trace out the second face and call the resulting NN-dimensional matrix MM. Put t=A/B=rN,h=[α′−α]. t=A/B=r^N,\qquad h=[\alpha'-\alpha]. Then Mαα′=1N2∑k=0N−1f(k)f([k+h])=r−hN2[(N−h)+ht].(19) M_{\alpha\alpha'} =\frac1{N^2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{f(k)}{f([k+h])} =\frac{r^{-h}}{N^2}\bigl[(N-h)+ht\bigr]. \qquad\text{(19)} For h=0h=0, every summand is one. For 1≤h<N1\le h<N, the ratio is r−hr^{-h} at N−hN-h indices and tr−htr^{-h} at the remaining hh. More explicitly, k=0k=0 and N−h<k<NN-h<k<N give the latter value; 1≤k≤N−h1\le k\le N-h give the former, including the denominator endpoint k+h=Nk+h=N. This proves (19) without dropping either seam endpoint. The charge has disappeared because kk ranges over every residue. Tracing out the first face gives M𝖳M^{\mathsf T}, so the two marginals have the same spectrum.

The Fourier vector with entries N−1/2QjαN^{-1/2}Q^{j\alpha} has eigenvalue λj=1N2∑h=0N−1[(N−h)+ht]zjh,zj=r−1Qj.(20) \lambda_j=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{h=0}^{N-1} [(N-h)+ht]z_j^h,\qquad z_j=r^{-1}Q^j. \qquad\text{(20)} If t≠1t\ne1, then zjN=t−1z_j^N=t^{-1} and zj≠1z_j\ne1. All eigenvalues are nonzero when t≠1t\ne1, even though the two-face cut itself has rank one. Their sum is Tr⁡M=1\operatorname{Tr}M=1. If t=1t=1, (19) instead gives Mαα′=r−[α′−α]/NM_{\alpha\alpha'}=r^{-[\alpha'-\alpha]}/N; this is the rank-one Fourier projector onto the unique character with Qj=rQ^j=r.

There is a positive case of the actual fixed cut. Suppose |A|=|B||A|=|B|, so |r|=1|r|=1. Then all |f(k)||f(k)| are equal, and (18) is an orthogonal rank-one projector. Its marginal is consequently positive semidefinite. If also t=eiθ≠1t=e^{i\theta}\ne1, write zj=eiφjz_j=e^{i\varphi_j}; (21) gives λj=sin⁡2(θ/2)N2sin⁡2(φj/2),sin⁡2(θ/2)N2≤λj≤1.(22) \lambda_j=\frac{\sin^2(\theta/2)} {N^2\sin^2(\varphi_j/2)},\qquad \frac{\sin^2(\theta/2)}{N^2}\le\lambda_j\le1. \qquad\text{(22)} The denominator is nonzero because zjN=t−1≠1z_j^N=t^{-1}\ne1. Equation (23) concerns a positive operator on one face against this explicitly transported fixed cut. It is not a lower bound for arbitrary signed crossings, two-face operators, or the remaining contracted knot network. It also does not identify a different uniform-vector mark in another matrix basis with the planar E00E_{00} used here.

8. Tetrahedral and source scope

Equations (1)–(23) prove a concrete boundary-module adapter and transport its literal fixed-index cut. They do not prove that the nine-tetrahedron state sum equals (12). The published identity (4.25) makes that additional assertion for its specified crossing tensor at odd NN. Its four paired boundary faces, tangle edges, vertex measures, charge sums and root phases must all be retained in any use of that assertion. In particular, (13) alone does not supply an adapter from an independently assigned five-cell or pointed tetrahedral state.

The sewing statement (16) supplies the original local wire phase under its stated charge and edge-factor conditions. No identification with the original marked meridian, a free-vertex deletion, a globally charged triangulation, or a selected geometric period is inferred. The explicit seam in (9), the inverse dual row, and the factor s05dN4s_{05}^dN^4 are part of the boundary data for that comparison, rather than discarded normalizations.
