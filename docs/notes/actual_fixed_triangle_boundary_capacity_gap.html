Quantitative loss on a fixed boundary face of the complete angle flow

31 October 2026. analytical lemma. This concerns the original complete triangle capacity and its actual shared face equations. It does not turn the weighted LEFT envelope into a coefficientwise comparison.

1. Strong concavity with the source normalization

For every actual triangle corner use the normalized load a=θ/(2π)∈[0,1/2]a=\theta/(2\pi)\in[0,1/2], with each triangle row summing to 1/21/2. Protected-face corners are zero, and every ordinary face has total load one. Let 𝒫\mathcal P be this compact feasible polytope, retaining all actual corner incidences. Its complete source entropy is

E(a)=∑t,ff(atf),f(s)=Λ(πs)+Λ(π/2−πs)π. E(a)=\sum_{t,f}f(a_{tf}),\qquad f(s)=\frac{\Lambda(\pi s)+\Lambda(\pi/2-\pi s)}{\pi}.

This is the normalization in the triangle Legendre identity. For 0<s<1/20<s<1/2, direct differentiation gives

f″(s)=−2πsin⁡(2πs)≤−2π. f''(s)=-\frac{2\pi}{\sin(2\pi s)}\leq-2\pi.

Consequently f(s)+πs2f(s)+\pi s^2 is concave, including both endpoints by continuity. Summing this fact proves, for a,b∈𝒫a,b\in\mathcal P and 0≤t≤10\leq t\leq1,

E((1−t)a+tb)≥(1−t)E(a)+tE(b)+πt(1−t)‖a−b‖22. E((1-t)a+tb)\geq(1-t)E(a)+tE(b) +\pi t(1-t)\lVert a-b\rVert_2^2. \tag{1}

Let a*a^* maximize EE on 𝒫\mathcal P, and set Φ=E(a*)\Phi=E(a^*). In the actual geometric case, Φ=Vol⁡(YD)/(2π)\Phi=\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)/(2\pi). Since the left side of (1), with a=a*a=a^*, is at most Φ\Phi, dividing by t>0t>0 and letting t↓0t\downarrow0 gives

E(b)≤Φ−π‖b−a*‖22. \boxed{\quad E(b)\leq\Phi-\pi\lVert b-a^*\rVert_2^2.\quad} \tag{2}

No derivative at a forced zero or at a singleton row is used. In particular the maximizing assignment is unique even on a proper supported face.

2. Fixing selected triangles to their corner vertices

Choose a set JJ of distinct original triangles and an allowed ordinary corner c(t)c(t) in each. Impose the additional constraints

at=vt:=12ec(t),t∈J. a_t=v_t:=\tfrac12 e_{c(t)},\qquad t\in J.

Write 𝒫J\mathcal P_J for the resulting face of 𝒫\mathcal P. If it is nonempty, (2) proves the explicit bound

max𝒫JE≤Φ−π∑t∈J‖vt−at*‖22. \max_{\mathcal P_J}E \leq\Phi-\pi\sum_{t\in J}\lVert v_t-a_t^*\rVert_2^2. \tag{3}

If it is empty, its entropy maximum is −∞-\infty. The loss in (3) is strict as soon as one chosen row was not already that singleton row at the complete solution. Distinct rows make the displayed squared losses additive; their shared face constraints remain in force.

There is an exact shared-radius dual formulation. Write hf=log⁡lfh_f=\log l_f for ordinary faces and retain protected zeros by deleting their allowed corners. With the same half-degree triangle function QQ, put

BJ(h)=∑t∉JQ((ehf)f∈t)+12∑t∈Jhc(t)−∑fhf. B_J(h)=\sum_{t\notin J}Q((e^{h_f})_{f\in t}) +\tfrac12\sum_{t\in J}h_{c(t)}-\sum_f h_f. \tag{4}

Then

infh∈ℝFordBJ(h)=max𝒫JE. \inf_{h\in\mathbb R^{F_{\mathrm{ord}}}} B_J(h) =\max_{\mathcal P_J}E. \tag{5}

Here the right side has value −∞-\infty when infeasible; positive minimizing radii need not exist. To verify (5), take the product of all remaining compact triangle simplexes and the selected singleton rows, let AA be its original face-incidence map, and define H(d)=max⁡Aa=dE(a)H(d)=\max_{Aa=d}E(a). Its hypograph is closed and convex, because the domain is compact and EE is continuous and concave. Weak duality follows directly from the local Legendre formula. Strict separation of (𝟏,H(𝟏)+ε)(\mathbf1,H(\mathbf1)+\varepsilon) from that hypograph gives an affine majorant of height at most H(𝟏)+εH(\mathbf1)+\varepsilon at 𝟏\mathbf1. The separating coefficient of the vertical coordinate is positive: it is nonnegative by downward closure, and if it were zero the feasible point with the same face coordinate would contradict strict separation. Normalizing that coefficient to one produces (4). Let ε↓0\varepsilon\downarrow0. When 𝟏\mathbf1 is outside the compact incidence image, separation in face coordinates and scaling the separator sends (4) to −∞-\infty.

3. What this resolves in the one-sided envelope

For a LEFT port (x,b,c,y)(x,b,c,y), its term

Q(x,b,y)+12log⁡c Q(x,b,y)+\tfrac12\log c

is exactly (4)’s local expression when the original (x,c,y)(x,c,y) triangle is fixed to its cc-corner vertex. At the actual complete packing with c>0c>0 and x+y>0x+y>0, this triangle has at least two positive weights, so its maximizing row is not that vertex. Thus (3) gives a strict, explicit capacity loss for any fixed nonempty collection of these triangle restrictions, with the original common face constraints. The reflected statement applies to RIGHT ports.

This proves a statement about the specified comparison capacities. It does not bound the full LEFT polynomial by that one comparison: the proved weighted envelope also has the branch log⁡c\log c. A pointwise maximum of the two branches does not permit each branch to choose its own Cauchy radii before the original coefficient is extracted. An additional finite comparison, or a bound for their convex mixtures with the same face constraints, is still necessary for a general full-invariant theorem.

Validation

The proof is analytic: scalar differentiation, strong concavity on the closed simplex, and finite convex separation. No finite-width experiment is used to infer (2), (3), or (5). No comparison with the complete one-sided envelope is supplied.
