Bulk Fourier coefficients of one original endpoint kernel

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

For one microscopic endpoint color, the upper half of the original kernel’s Fourier coefficients has an exact checkerboard sign and a positive finite sum. On every fixed degree interval strictly between zero and the fold, a short prefix of that sum is an analytic unit of order N−1N^{-1}. Its relative error at the physical degree nodes is exponentially small. A localized sinc correction gives one exact analytic interpolant on a fixed complex strip, with the same leading coefficient and no extensive phase after the checkerboard is removed.

This is a statement about the ordinary original kernel, including its full spectral normals. It is not a bound for a whole interface integral. The checkerboard, its complex growth, the two degree endpoints, and all other source factors remain.

5. Scope and direct-source readbacks

The original ordinary polynomial was used throughout Proposition 1. Thus both parities, including the odd simple physical node, and every physical first normal are retained automatically. The degree variable xx in (28) is an interpolation variable; its derivative is not being substituted for a spectral normal.

The theorem concerns a single endpoint kernel and the coefficients with s/Ns/N in a fixed compact subinterval of (0,1/2)(0,1/2). It gives no uniform endpoint or fold limit. Fourier coefficients of shared products require their actual convolutions and are not identified with (3). Real bounded α\alpha is the parameter range proved here; no complex-α\alpha unit assertion is needed. Neither positivity of β\beta nor the moderate interpolation estimates the joined central, outer, and vertical source. In particular (35) does not suppress the external degree connectors or imply a filled-action upper bound or scalar noncancellation.

The ordinary endpoint bounds and the general localized interpolation method are earlier results. The new input is the exact signed positive Fourier sum, its uniform bulk contraction, and the explicit nonzero analytic symbol for these actual coefficients.
