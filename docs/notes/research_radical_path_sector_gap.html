Bounding radical edge contributions by the pieces of a path

Research proof, 3 November 2026. The high Newton sector can be estimated after its exact contraction, without evaluating its individual polynomials on a complex geometric contour. On a path, the contracted source separates into pieces with physical boundary values. Existing bounds over the whole boundary period control many of these pieces.

This gives two conclusions proved below. For paths whose selected powers have magnitude at least sixty-four, every nonempty radical matching has a uniform strict rate gap. For the internal pair of powers 3,33,3, its entire radical contribution also has a strict gap, established by an elementary comparison of convex energies. The latter result does not estimate the remaining low-sector trace.

5. Why ordinary-color bounds do not settle the remaining pieces

The colors cac_a above are physical values of an auxiliary pair Casimir. The main strands still carry their original complete root color. The ordinary-color comparison in the representation audit instead specializes the main knot color and uses the one-component quotient argument to replace all its strands by an ordinary color. Those hypotheses do not follow from fixing auxiliary Casimir values.

In particular, a general bound (4) cannot be imported from that ordinary-color theorem. Nor do the existing bounds for the normal determinant at each color establish it: they permit exponential cancellation between different physical colors. For arbitrary short path components, their complete-boundary rate and their whole-boundary upper bound must still be compared within the original source. The results above remove this issue for the explicitly identified components and for the radical sector of the internal 3,33,3 pair, while leaving its low-sector problem visible.

8. Contracting several cutoff forcings before estimating them

Orient any nonempty set FF of selected edges of the original path. On each e=(v,w)∈Fe=(v,w)\in F, replace its kernel by the literal right side of the low-kernel difference equation: Je,N(zv,zw)=−sL+12eL(zv)eL+1(zw).(44) J_{e,N}(z_v,z_w) =-s_{L+1}^2 e_L(z_v)e_{L+1}(z_w). \qquad\text{(44)} Leave every other edge equal to its full original kernel, and take all the original crossing traces. Denote this finite source by 𝒥F,N\mathscr J_{F,N}. The edges of FF need not form a matching. This definition specifies a product of actual cutoff forcings; it does not assert that applying arbitrary difference operators to a network produces only these terms.

9. What this does and does not supply in an induction

Suppose whole-real-boundary upper bounds at the complete geometric rate are known for every original path source with fewer than mm traced vertices. Under the geometric comparison just specified, all nonempty radical matching terms of an mm-vertex path, and all terms (44), have a strict gap below its geometric rate. Their bounds consume only sources with at most m−2m-2 traced vertices. They require no nonzero leading coefficient in those shorter sources.

Let ZΓ,NlowZ_{\Gamma,N}^{\mathrm{ low}} replace precisely the edges joining selected vertices by KNlowK_N^{\mathrm{ low}}; edges to the original outside boundary remain full. The exact radical expansion and the induction hypothesis give sup𝒖∈[−π,π]∂Γ|ZΓ,N(𝒖)−ZΓ,Nlow(𝒖)|≤CNaeN(MΓ(𝟏)−1/1000).(49) \sup_{\mathbf u\in[-\pi,\pi]^{\partial\Gamma}} |Z_{\Gamma,N}(\mathbf u)-Z_{\Gamma,N}^{\mathrm{ low}}(\mathbf u)| \le C N^a e^{N(M_\Gamma(\mathbf1)-1/1000)}. \qquad\text{(49)} Hence an upper bound at rate MΓ(𝟏)M_\Gamma(\mathbf1) for the original mm-vertex source is equivalent to the same upper bound for its joint low-sector source, up to polynomial factors. At complete boundary values a nonzero leading geometric asymptotic for either source transfers to the other unchanged.

The remainder in (49) is an ordinary polynomial of degree at most N−1N-1 in each original boundary variable. Chebyshev expansion therefore extends (49) to complex outside angles at the additional rate ∑j|ℑuj|.(50) \sum_j|\Im u_j|. \qquad\text{(50)} Fixed ordinary derivatives cost further powers of NN. This is sufficient to preserve a strict gap on a sufficiently small fixed boundary neighborhood; it is not a free geometric-rate bound on a large complex internal domain.

The remaining induction step is the joint low-sector contour. The unsummed phase theorem supplies a nondegenerate local geometric contour. It does not identify every complementary current with (44) or with a radical matching. The exact facet rule lists where general factorial currents are supported, but gives no reduction of all of them to shorter physical-boundary sources. Moreover, a geometric upper bound for the individual low kernels is false on some actual positive all-three paths, by the low-kernel obstruction. Summing each vertex first does not solve that issue either: the traced three-crossing vertex has a larger endpoint term near such configurations.

Thus the missing assertion is specific: a contour identity for the joint low source whose main geometric term has its original nonzero coefficient, and whose entire complementary part either has a proved direct gap or reduces to the shorter sources bounded above. None of the preceding upper bounds alone provides that identity or its coefficient.
