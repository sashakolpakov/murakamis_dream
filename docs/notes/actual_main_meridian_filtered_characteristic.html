A classical characteristic selected by the microscopic meridian state

The reduced ADO invariant is a function of a marked meridian eigenvalue MM. Its exact quantum recurrence compares the values at M,qM,q2M,…M,qM,q^2M,\ldots. We prove that, for the knot classes specified below, the complete asymptotic expansion on the scale M=qNsM=q_N^s selects one analytic curve in the classical symbol of every such recurrence. The curve is determined by the actual finite invariant and its original normalization.

The selected curve passes through (M,L)=(1,−1)(M,L)=(1,-1), has the preferred cusp shape as its logarithmic tangent, and is invariant under (M,L)↦(M−1,L−1)(M,L)\mapsto(M^{-1},L^{-1}). We also give a rational formula for its longitude coordinate in the original internal variables. The circle-tree longitude theorem and its extension through unmarked product junctions identify this entire germ with the geometric meridian–longitude germ for all the full-source classes listed below. Together with this note, they give the selected geometric factor of every genuine classical symbol. The marked isotopies in the net-twist and closure reductions transfer the intrinsic conclusion to the corresponding original knots.

Fix a knot admitting one of the following presentations, with its original endpoint orders, orientations and peripheral markings:

  • a strict ordered Conway presentation with independently long fixed filling powers, under the hypotheses of the filled-meridian theorem;
  • an ordered all-circle tree satisfying the summed degree-and-twist criterion, ∑vdv/(dv2+nv2)≤2−25\sum_v d_v/(d_v^2+n_v^2)\le2^{-25};
  • an ordered all-circle tree satisfying the local degree-and-twist criterion, max⁡vdv/(dv2+nv2)≤2−120\max_v d_v/(d_v^2+n_v^2)\le2^{-120}.

In the last two cases dvd_v is the number of edges incident to vertex vv, and nvn_v is its signed integer crossing power. The construction must give one knot, with no additional component closed inside a tangle. The diagram, powers and compact parameter sets are fixed before the level tends to infinity. Both level parities are retained.

Those theorems supply an actual finite-sum representation with a nonzero, nondegenerate joint first Fourier contribution and fixed exponential bounds for all discarded terms. The proof here extends that representation to the finitely many translated microscopic regions needed by an arbitrary fixed-order recurrence, then uses every order of its canonical factorial expansion. A leading volume limit or a finite cusp jet alone would not suffice.

7.3. Scope of the geometric identification

The marked Conway deformation, Section 3, has a leaf function g(x,t)=J(2x)−2J(x)−J(x−t)−J(x+t),K(x,t)=g(x,t)−g(x,0). g(x,t)=J(2x)-2J(x)-J(x-t)-J(x+t), \qquad K(x,t)=g(x,t)-g(x,0). \tag{40} That equality of scalar functions does not identify their peripheral uses. In the existing leaf construction tt is the hidden auxiliary-circle parameter. Section 5 of that note proves that every actual main meridian remains parabolic identically: the two adjacent white maps have the same derivative at their common corner. The primitive collar calculation likewise identifies an auxiliary meridian and disk longitude. The preferred-cusp theorem computes a translation in their parabolic main peripheral commutant. None of these statements gives a main meridian with eigenvalue eπite^{\pi it} simply by substituting this tt into its existing cells.

The original marked braid source supplies a different nonparabolic construction. The critical-correspondence theorem, Sections 4–6, identifies its regular exponential critical scheme with framed knot representations with actual meridian eigenvalue MM. The longitude-insertion theorem, Sections 1–4, identifies its preferred longitude, full meridian gauge, and logarithmic action differential. The actual geometric normal-period theorem, Sections 3–5, constructs the local geometric formal block of that braid scheme. Those results do not yet identify the grouped modified equations (30) with that particular critical block.

The strict-tree longitude theorem now supplies the missing comparison at unmarked product junctions. It inserts a zero-crossing box with an auxiliary circle, proves the exact stationary elimination including its constant, and checks the full based boundary maps after meridian filling. Thus the whole geometric representation is recovered, including a possible half-angle fold. It does not infer the preferred longitude from agreement of the cusp tangent alone.

The argument also does not claim a physical asymptotic formula for fixed M≠1M\ne1, or for ss growing with NN. The variable tt in Section 4 is an auxiliary analytic parameter used to organize all fixed-ss coefficients. The ordinary-color divisors remain in (2), (18)–(20). The translated charts in Section 2 stay away from those divisors; no uniform nonvanishing state across ordinary-color resonances has been asserted.

For every full-source class listed at the start, the selected curve is therefore the geometric eigenvalue germ, and its irreducible Laurent factor divides the first nonzero classical symbol. The directed all-orders theorem now proves the needed expansion on shifted charts for every certificate of the directed degree-and-twist theorem. Its additional cases therefore have the same selected geometric-factor conclusion. No equality with the full A-polynomial or with an entire annihilator ideal is claimed.
