Boundary multiplication preserves concentrated original sources

Multiplication by a fixed crossing power preserves a boundary asymptotic when the original boundary polynomial has a strict angular decay bound. The multiplication is performed in the full Hermite quotient. Its canonical polynomial is exponentially close, on the real physical period and on a smaller complex disk, to the product with the original Gaussian representative of that crossing.

The angular decay assumption is essential. The crossing polynomial by itself has exponentially large band-edge terms on a complex strip, as shown in the polynomial crossing calculation. The proof here suppresses those terms using the decay of the factor being multiplied. An unrestricted analogous assertion for the quarter-turn is false; Section 7 gives an original-source counterexample.

1. Statement for the original quotient

Let D=N−1,η=(−1)D,𝒜N=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2),z(t)=−2cos⁡t.(1) D=N-1,\qquad \eta=(-1)^D,\qquad \mathcal A_N=\mathbb C[z]/(2T_N(z/2)+2),\qquad z(t)=-2\cos t. \qquad\text{(1)} The Chebyshev polynomial has its ordinary normalization. Every double root retains its first derivative; at odd levels the root −2-2 is simple. Write can\operatorname{can} for the representative of degree less than NN, and let RR be the original crossing.

Suppose FN(t)=fN(−2cos⁡t)F_N(t)=f_N(-2\cos t), where deg⁡fN<N\deg f_N<N, is an original boundary source. Assume that there are constants c,b>0c,b>0, B≥0B\ge0, C>0C>0, d≥0d\ge0, and M∈ℝM\in\mathbb R, independent of NN, such that |FN(x+iy)|≤CNdexp⁡N{M−c(1−cos⁡x)+By2},|x|≤π,|y|≤b.(2) |F_N(x+iy)|\le C N^d \exp N\{M-c(1-\cos x)+B y^2\}, \qquad |x|\le\pi,\quad |y|\le b. \qquad\text{(2)} Polynomial prefactors and the starting level may depend on the fixed source. For a fixed signed integer nn, define Pn,N(t)=can⁡(RnfN)(−2cos⁡t),CN,n=exp⁡(πinD24N),gn,N(t)=CN,nexp⁡(inNt24π)FN(t).(3) \begin{split} P_{n,N}(t)&=\operatorname{can}(R^n f_N)(-2\cos t),\\ C_{N,n}&=\exp\left(\frac{\pi i nD^2}{4N}\right),\\ g_{n,N}(t)&=C_{N,n}\exp\left(\frac{inNt^2}{4\pi}\right)F_N(t). \end{split} \qquad\text{(3)} Negative powers in the first line are taken in 𝒜N\mathcal A_N, before choosing the representative. The scalar in the second line has the displayed logarithm convention, not a principal fractional power of another numerical scalar.

Theorem 1. Under (2), there are δ>0\delta>0, r>0r>0, and fixed polynomial prefactors such that sup|t|≤π,t∈ℝ|Pn,N(t)−gn,N(t)|≤CnNdneN(M−δ),(4) \sup_{|t|\le\pi,\ t\in\mathbb R} |P_{n,N}(t)-g_{n,N}(t)| \le C_nN^{d_n}e^{N(M-\delta)}, \qquad\text{(4)} and sup|t|≤r|Pn,N(t)−gn,N(t)|≤Cn′Ndn′eN(M−δ/2).(5) \sup_{|t|\le r}|P_{n,N}(t)-g_{n,N}(t)| \le C_n'N^{d_n'}e^{N(M-\delta/2)}. \qquad\text{(5)} The constants δ,r\delta,r can be chosen using only c,B,b,nc,B,b,n. Both estimates preserve all physical first normals and hold at both parities of NN.

Suppose in addition that on a fixed complex disk FN(u)=ϵNNαA(u)eNW(u)(1+O(N−1)),|ϵN|=1,(6) F_N(u)=\epsilon_N N^\alpha A(u)e^{NW(u)}(1+O(N^{-1})),\qquad |\epsilon_N|=1, \qquad\text{(6)} uniformly, where WW is even and holomorphic, AA is holomorphic and nonzero, ℜW(0)=M\Re W(0)=M, and −ℜW″(0)>0-\Re W''(0)>0. Then, on a possibly smaller fixed disk, Pn,N(u)=ϵNCN,nNαA(u)exp⁡N{W(u)+inu24π}(1+O(N−1)).(7) \boxed{\displaystyle P_{n,N}(u)=\epsilon_N C_{N,n}N^\alpha A(u) \exp N\left\{W(u)+\frac{in u^2}{4\pi}\right\} (1+O(N^{-1})).} \qquad\text{(7)} It also satisfies a bound of the form (2), with positive constants c′,b′c',b' and a finite B′B'. Thus the class of these concentrated original boundary sources is closed under every fixed signed boundary crossing multiplication.

No uniformity as nn grows with NN is asserted. A family with common constants in (2), a common disk for the full expansion (6) on which every amplitude is nonzero, common bounds on the normalized phases W−W(0)W-W(0), and a common positive curvature bound has common output constants for each fixed finite set of powers nn. Amplitude sizes, error constants and starting levels may still depend on the individual source.

2. Exact Hermite folding of a smooth periodic function

The physical angles are τj=π(D−2j)N,j=0,…,D.(8) \tau_j=\frac{\pi(D-2j)}N, \qquad j=0,\ldots,D. \qquad\text{(8)} The original crossing has the value and angular derivative of CN,neinNt2/(4π)C_{N,n}e^{inNt^2/(4\pi)} at these angles. Therefore the canonical product in (3) has exactly the values and angular derivatives of gn,Ng_{n,N} there. At the odd simple angle zero, the angular derivative vanishes by evenness and adds no spurious condition.

Let ff be an even smooth periodic function with Fourier coefficients aℓa_\ell, with enough decay that its value and first derivative series converge absolutely. Let p(t)=∑|m|<Nbmeimtp(t)=\sum_{|m|<N}b_me^{imt}, b−m=bmb_{-m}=b_m, be its full physical Hermite interpolant. Sampling on (8) gives Vm=∑k∈ℤηkam+kN,Wm=∑k∈ℤ(m+kN)ηkam+kN.(9) V_m=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\eta^k a_{m+kN},\qquad W_m=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}(m+kN)\eta^k a_{m+kN}. \qquad\text{(9)} For 1≤m<N1\le m<N, the two band coefficients in this residue class obey Vm=bm+ηbm−NV_m=b_m+\eta b_{m-N} and Wm=mbm+η(m−N)bm−NW_m=mb_m+\eta(m-N)b_{m-N}. This includes every alias and the repeated-root derivatives.

The term k=−1k=-1 in (10) is zero. All other terms apart from ama_m have |m+kN|≥N|m+kN|\ge N. Summing their absolute values, and including the discarded original Fourier coefficients, gives ∥p−f∥L∞(ℝ)≤C0∑|ℓ|≥N(1+|ℓ|/N)|aℓ|.(12) \|p-f\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)} \le C_0\sum_{|\ell|\ge N} (1+|\ell|/N)|a_\ell|. \qquad\text{(12)} Here C0C_0 is absolute. 3. The endpoint cutoff and the complete Fourier tail

We suppress the subscript n,Nn,N temporarily. Since 1−cos⁡x≥2x2/π21-\cos x\ge2x^2/\pi^2 on [−π,π][-\pi,\pi], completing a square gives |nxy|2π≤c2(1−cos⁡x)+n2y216c. \frac{|nxy|}{2\pi} \le\frac c2(1-\cos x)+\frac{n^2y^2}{16c}. Thus the analytic function gg satisfies |g(x+iy)|≤CNdexp⁡N{M−(c/2)(1−cos⁡x)+B*y2},B*=B+n216c.(13) |g(x+iy)|\le C N^d \exp N\{M-(c/2)(1-\cos x)+B_*y^2\},\qquad B_*=B+\frac{n^2}{16c}. \qquad\text{(13)}

Choose an even smooth cutoff χN\chi_N which is one on |t|≤aN=π−3π/(4N)|t|\le a_N=\pi-3\pi/(4N), zero on |t|≥bN=π−π/(4N)|t|\ge b_N=\pi-\pi/(4N), and whose fixed derivatives are bounded by CjNjC_jN^j. Such cutoffs are obtained by rescaling one fixed smooth function. The last physical angle is π−π/N<aN\pi-\pi/N<a_N, so f(t)=χN(t)g(t)(14) f(t)=\chi_N(t)g(t) \qquad\text{(14)} has exactly the same values and first derivatives at every physical angle. Extending it periodically gives an even smooth function. Its Hermite interpolant is consequently exactly Pn,NP_{n,N}, not a new model for that polynomial.

Choose a fixed height h>0h>0 so small that h<b/4,B*h≤1/4,B*h2≤c/8.(15) h<b/4,\qquad B_*h\le1/4,\qquad B_*h^2\le c/8. \qquad\text{(15)} Conditions containing B*B_* are vacuous if it is zero. Cauchy’s estimate on disks of radius 1/(10N)1/(10N), with a slightly larger height bound if needed, shows that each fixed derivative of gg costs only a fixed power of NN in (13). These disks stay inside the original real window at every point used in the cutoff and central contour. Errors of size O(1/N)O(1/N) in the exponent are absorbed in the constant.

Integrate the Fourier coefficient of (14) by parts four times. There are no endpoint terms because the cutoff is zero near the ends of the period. Terms with a derivative of χN\chi_N, and the part of χNg(4)\chi_Ng^{(4)} outside [−aN,aN][-a_N,a_N], lie in the endpoint layers. On those layers 1−cos⁡x≥11-\cos x\ge1, so their total contribution before the factor |ℓ|−4|\ell|^{-4} is bounded by CnNd+4eN(M−c/4)C_nN^{d+4}e^{N(M-c/4)}.

For the remaining integral of g(4)e−iℓtg^{(4)}e^{-i\ell t}, move [−aN,aN][-a_N,a_N] to height −sgn⁡(ℓ)h-\operatorname{sgn}(\ell)h. This is an ordinary holomorphic deformation on this central rectangle. Its horizontal part is bounded by CnNd+4eN(M+B*h2)−|ℓ|h. C_nN^{d+4}e^{N(M+B_*h^2)-|\ell|h}. Both vertical sides have 1−cos⁡aN≥11-\cos a_N\ge1. Equations (13),(15) bound them by CnNd+4eN(M−c/4)C_nN^{d+4}e^{N(M-c/4)}; the Fourier factor decreases along each vertical side. In particular those sides have been estimated, not discarded. Altogether, for |ℓ|≥N|\ell|\ge N, |aℓ|≤CnNd+4|ℓ|4[eN(M+B*h2)−|ℓ|h+eN(M−c/4)].(16) |a_\ell|\le \frac{C_nN^{d+4}}{|\ell|^4} \left[e^{N(M+B_*h^2)-|\ell|h}+e^{N(M-c/4)}\right]. \qquad\text{(16)} Since B*h2≤h/4B_*h^2\le h/4, summation gives ∑|ℓ|≥N(1+|ℓ|/N)|aℓ|≤CnNd+1eN(M−δ),δ=min⁡(h/2,c/8)>0.(17) \sum_{|\ell|\ge N}(1+|\ell|/N)|a_\ell| \le C_nN^{d+1}e^{N(M-\delta)},\qquad \delta=\min(h/2,c/8)>0. \qquad\text{(17)} Increasing a polynomial prefactor does not affect the fixed gap. Equations (12),(17), together with the exponentially small difference between ff and gg in their endpoint layers, prove (4).

The cutoff is used solely to estimate Fourier coefficients. It is not continued to a complex domain, and it does not replace the original finite source in any subsequent trace.

4. Local complex comparison

Set EN=Pn,N−gn,NE_N=P_{n,N}-g_{n,N}. This is analytic near the whole real window. The real bound (4) holds, whereas (2)–(4) also give the crude bound sup⁡ℝ|Pn,N|≤CnNdneNM\sup_{\mathbb R}|P_{n,N}|\le C_nN^{d_n}e^{NM}. Each of its Fourier coefficients is therefore bounded by the latter quantity. Its band |m|<N|m|<N implies |Pn,N(x+iy)|≤CnNdn+1eN(M+|y|).(18) |P_{n,N}(x+iy)|\le C_nN^{d_n+1}e^{N(M+|y|)}. \qquad\text{(18)}

Fix a<min⁡(b/2,π/2)a<\min(b/2,\pi/2). On the semicircle |z|=a|z|=a, (13),(18) give |EN(z)|≤CnNdn′eN(M+Ca),Ca=max⁡(a,B*a2).(19) |E_N(z)|\le C_nN^{d_n'}e^{N(M+C_a)},\qquad C_a=\max(a,B_*a^2). \qquad\text{(19)} On its real diameter the exponent is M−δM-\delta. In the upper half-disk, the harmonic measure of the curved boundary is explicitly ω(z)=2πarg⁡a+za−z. \omega(z)=\frac2\pi\arg\frac{a+z}{a-z}. For |z|≤a/2|z|\le a/2, it is at most 16ℑz/(3πa)16\Im z/(3\pi a). The two-constants inequality for the subharmonic function log⁡|EN|\log|E_N| therefore gives exponent M−δ+(δ+Ca)ω(z). M-\delta+(\delta+C_a)\omega(z). The lower half-disk has the identical estimate with |ℑz||\Im z|. Choose r≤min⁡(a/2,3πaδ32(δ+Ca)).(20) r\le\min\left(a/2,\frac{3\pi a\delta}{32(\delta+C_a)}\right). \qquad\text{(20)} Then (5) follows. This argument uses the analytic function ENE_N, not the smooth Fourier periodization.

Under (6), shrink this disk once more so that ℜ(W(u)+inu2/(4π))≥M−δ/4\Re(W(u)+inu^2/(4\pi))\ge M-\delta/4 there. The amplitude is bounded away from zero on a smaller closed disk. The error (5) is then exponentially small relative to the main term, proving (7). The same argument works uniformly for a family with the common phase bounds stated after the theorem. No common positive lower bound on the amplitudes is required: each fixed source has its own starting level.

5. Recovering a whole-period Gaussian strip bound

On the real period, (4) and (2) immediately imply |Pn,N(x)|≤CnNdneN{M−c1(1−cos⁡x)},c1=min⁡(c,δ/2)>0.(21) |P_{n,N}(x)|\le C_nN^{d_n} e^{N\{M-c_1(1-\cos x)\}},\qquad c_1=\min(c,\delta/2)>0. \qquad\text{(21)} Near zero the new phase in (7) has exactly the old real curvature: −ℜ(W″(0)+in2π)=−ℜW″(0)>0. -\Re\left(W''(0)+\frac{in}{2\pi}\right)=-\Re W''(0)>0. Its evenness and a fixed bound on its higher derivatives give, on a small fixed complex disk, ℜ(W(x+iy)+in(x+iy)24π)≤M−ax2+B1y2(22) \Re\left(W(x+iy)+\frac{in(x+iy)^2}{4\pi}\right) \le M-a x^2+B_1y^2 \qquad\text{(22)} for some a>0a>0, B1<∞B_1<\infty. For example, bound the mixed term from the imaginary quadratic coefficient by completing a square, then absorb higher terms by shrinking the disk. Formula (7) gives the same bound for the source, with a polynomial prefactor.

For clarity, the far-angle strip can be obtained without continuing the exponentially small interpolation error. Fix a small r0>0r_0>0 inside this local disk. The spectral interval J=[−2cos⁡(r0/2),2]J=[-2\cos(r_0/2),2] corresponds to real angles r0/2≤|x|≤πr_0/2\le|x|\le\pi. On JJ, (21) has a fixed gap Δ=c1(1−cos⁡(r0/2))\Delta=c_1(1-\cos(r_0/2)). Expanding the degree-DD polynomial in rescaled Chebyshev polynomials bounds its complex value by 2NsupJ|Pn,N|eNGJ(z), 2N\sup_J|P_{n,N}|\,e^{NG_J(z)}, where GJ(z)=log⁡|ζ+ζ2−1|G_J(z)=\log|\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}| is zero on JJ, ζ\zeta being the affine coordinate taking JJ to [−1,1][-1,1], and the exterior branch is used. This elementary Chebyshev estimate is the interval Bernstein–Walsh bound used in the earlier source proofs. Continuity of GJG_J makes it at most Δ/4\Delta/4 on z=−2cos⁡(x+iy)z=-2\cos(x+iy), r0≤|x|≤πr_0\le|x|\le\pi, for a fixed sufficiently small |y|≤b′|y|\le b'. This remains true at the endpoint z=2z=2.

Thus the far-angle strip retains a fixed gap. Combine it with (22), choosing c′>0c'>0 smaller than both the local quadratic constant and Δ/8\Delta/8, and taking B′≥B1B'\ge B_1. This proves the asserted bound (2) for the canonical product on one fixed whole-period strip. All choices are uniform under common input constants and local phase bounds; there is no iteration over the level.

The same proof also handles a fixed finite product of concentrated boundary sources. More precisely, if Fj,NF_{j,N}, 1≤j≤s1\le j\le s, satisfy (2),(6), then can⁡(Rn∏j=1sfj,N)(−2cos⁡u)=CN,n(∏jϵj,N)N∑jαj(∏jAj(u))exp⁡N{∑jWj(u)+inu24π}(1+O(N−1)).(22a) \operatorname{can}\left(R^n\prod_{j=1}^s f_{j,N}\right)(-2\cos u) =C_{N,n}\left(\prod_j\epsilon_{j,N}\right) N^{\sum_j\alpha_j}\left(\prod_j A_j(u)\right) \exp N\left\{\sum_jW_j(u)+\frac{inu^2}{4\pi}\right\} (1+O(N^{-1})). \qquad\text{(22a)} This canonical product also has a whole-period Gaussian bound. To verify the claim directly, use the analytic function CN,neinNt2/(4π)∏jFj,N(t)C_{N,n}e^{inNt^2/(4\pi)}\prod_jF_{j,N}(t) in Sections 2–4. Its input constants are M=∑MjM=\sum M_j, c=∑cjc=\sum c_j, and B=∑BjB=\sum B_j. The input product need not have degree less than NN: only the output Hermite interpolant needs that band, and its physical jets are exactly those of the original quotient product. The local real curvatures add, so Section 5 applies as well. The number of factors and their powers are fixed before NN tends to infinity. This proves closure under multiplication and fixed crossings; it still makes no assertion about the turn.

6. Actual families to which the theorem applies

The hypotheses are already proved for every terminal source Hp,NH_{p,N}, |p|≥2|p|\ge2, with common phase, angular and strip constants in the uniform terminal-family theorem. They are also proved for the one-boundary pieces in the recursive high-valence theorem. Consequently arbitrary fixed boundary crossing powers can be attached to any of those actual pieces without losing its full boundary asymptotic or its strict angular bound.

A useful application concerns a pendant chain of k≥1k\ge1 vertices, all of power 2σ2\sigma, σ=±1\sigma=\pm1. Its exact quotient identity is proved in the marked chain reduction: Fk,σ,N=κNσkR−σH−σ(k+1),N,κN=exp⁡πiD22N.(23) F_{k,\sigma,N}=\kappa_N^{\sigma k} R^{-\sigma}H_{-\sigma(k+1),N},\qquad \kappa_N=\exp\frac{\pi iD^2}{2N}. \qquad\text{(23)} Apply Theorem 1 to its canonical representative. On a common fixed disk, for each fixed kk, Fk,σ,N(u)=κNσkCN,−σϵ−σ(k+1),NA−σ(k+1)(u)exp⁡N{W−σ(k+1)(u)−σiu24π}(1+Ok(N−1)).(24) \boxed{\displaystyle F_{k,\sigma,N}(u)= \kappa_N^{\sigma k}C_{N,-\sigma}\epsilon_{-\sigma(k+1),N} A_{-\sigma(k+1)}(u) \exp N\left\{W_{-\sigma(k+1)}(u) -\frac{\sigma i u^2}{4\pi}\right\} (1+O_k(N^{-1})).} \qquad\text{(24)} The common real angular gap, complex strip and normalized phase bounds can be chosen independently of kk: the terminal family is uniform and the new boundary multiplier is only RR or R−1R^{-1}. The amplitude and its error constant may depend on kk.

This strengthens the earlier quotient reduction to an asymptotic of the actual canonical free-boundary polynomial. The existing marked tangle identity still supplies the geometric interpretation after attachment. Formula (24) does not identify the turn of an arbitrary packet, solve a general low-valence connected region, or justify discarding any current in a subsequent trace. Those operations still need their own estimates.

7. Why unrestricted closure under turns is false

The turn problem cannot be solved by proving that every concentrated source stays concentrated. For any fixed |p|≥2|p|\ge2, the actual terminal source is Hp,N=T(Rp). H_{p,N}=T(R^p). It satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 1. But the original turn is the Newton reversal, so T2=IT^2=I in the full algebra and T(Hp,N)=Rp.(25) T(H_{p,N})=R^p. \qquad\text{(25)} Let rp,N(t)=can⁡(Rp)(−2cos⁡t)r_{p,N}(t)=\operatorname{can}(R^p)(-2\cos t). Its exact physical values are rp,N(τj)=CN,peipNτj2/(4π),|rp,N(τj)|=1.(26) r_{p,N}(\tau_j)=C_{N,p}e^{ipN\tau_j^2/(4\pi)}, \qquad |r_{p,N}(\tau_j)|=1. \qquad\text{(26)} This excludes concentration of the kind used here, regardless of the rate one attempts to assign to it.

Indeed choose a physical angle tNt_N nearest zero: it is zero at odd levels and has magnitude π/N\pi/N at even levels. If a nonzero local asymptotic (6) existed for this polynomial, its even phase and nonzero amplitude would give 0=log⁡|rp,N(tN)|=NℜW(0)+αlog⁡N+log⁡|A(0)|+O(N−1).(27) 0=\log|r_{p,N}(t_N)| =N\Re W(0)+\alpha\log N+\log|A(0)|+O(N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(27)} Dividing by NN forces M=ℜW(0)=0M=\Re W(0)=0. In particular a positive rate is incompatible with the nonzero local expansion. A negative rate is already incompatible with (2) at these same near-zero nodes.

Now fix any t0∈(0,π)t_0\in(0,\pi), and choose physical nodes sN→t0s_N\to t_0. At rate zero, a real angular bound (2) with c>0c>0 would require 1=|rp,N(sN)|≤CNde−Nc(1−cos⁡sN),(28) 1=|r_{p,N}(s_N)|\le C N^d e^{-Nc(1-\cos s_N)}, \qquad\text{(28)} whose right side tends to zero. This is a contradiction at both parities. Thus a source in the proved class can leave that class after a single turn.

A useful next theorem must therefore control the actual composed source and its relevant hyperbolic, nondegenerate geometry, with hypotheses that exclude this example. It cannot assert closure under all turns. This counterexample does not disprove a volume identity for a hyperbolic closure; it identifies a false unrestricted intermediate claim. Neither hyperbolicity alone nor a local geometric critical point has been shown here to supply the missing global source estimate.

8. Reproducing the finite normalization checks

Run

python3 experiments/check_boundary_crossing_packet_preservation.py \
  --precision 120 \
  --output repro/results/research-boundary-crossing-packet-preservation.json

The script compares multiplication by the original triangular Newton crossing matrices with the Hermite Fourier reconstruction from the Gaussian values and all angular derivatives. It uses the full bare-edge source and the two actual terminal sources of powers 2,−32,-3, at every level 2≤N≤122\le N\le12, for multiplying powers −3,−1,1,2-3,-1,1,2. The 132 level/source/power cases agree at 120-digit precision; the largest relative physical-jet discrepancy is below 3.0⋅10−1173.0\cdot10^{-117}, and the largest off-grid reconstruction discrepancy is below 2.4⋅10−1192.4\cdot10^{-119}.

The saved output also records 24 comparisons of the actual canonical product with the chirped source at levels 16,17,32,33,64,6516,17,32,33,64,65, on sampled real angles and two complex angles. The exponential estimate, its uniformity, and the whole-source conclusions follow from the proof above, not from these samples.
