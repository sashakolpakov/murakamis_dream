The full geometric peripheral rows are m=M2,l=L_intr2. Thus R_intr has exactly the connection and invariant norm in (1.1)–(1.2). A full complex-period comparison retains phase and integral-lift data. The nonzero contribution of this action to the actual quantum period, and control of the remaining contributions, are still unproved.

There is now an exact intrinsic scalar on the quantum side for this norm comparison. In squared-meridian coordinates let U=Y^N and

t_N=(1/N)Tr_(C(Y)/C(U))(Y F_N(Y))|_(U=1).

The regular-period theorem proves t_N=R_N(1) and K_N=N*t_N+O(N^b), where b is any fixed knot braid strand count. The entire polar contribution to the full ADO value at completeness has size O(N^(b-1)). It also gives the positive norm

H_N=(1/N)sum_(j=0)^(N-1)|F_N(Q_N^j)|^2
   =|K_N|^2/N+O(N^(2b-2)).

For positive hyperbolic volume the target (5.1) is equivalently the same regulator limit for (4/N)log|t_N| or (2/N)log H_N. These are exact consequences of the pointed state and its controlled ordinary colors. They do not supply the still-missing norm estimate.

For the operators satisfying the normal-period theorem’s hypotheses, this last norm problem is an exact inverse-matrix pairing. In the specified cyclic trace metric,

K_N=-N e_0^* B_0^+ B_1 v_0,
||B_1||=O(1/N), ||v_0||=O(sqrt(N) D_N),

where v_0 is fixed by the ordinary colors. A checked Weyl reciprocity restricts the inverse to its -1 sector, whose source and complete functional have the required parity. The full phase and the normal factor 1/N are retained. Estimating this inverse together with its actual source coupling remains necessary; a small singular value alone is not a volume theorem.

There is an exact reason to keep this distinction. A nonzero rational root-level solution gives a module map to (C(x),sigma), on which sigma^n=1. It follows that H-1 is not invertible, hence D_n(u,1)=0. The rational ADO states have this central v=1 sector. It is not automatically the geometric classical branch. Retaining the original q-family and its selected period is essential.

The requested volume statement, with all constants fixed, is

(4/N)log|F_N(1)|-log||P_hyp|| -> 0.              (5.1)

By (1.2) and (4.1), this is exactly (2pi/N)log|<K>_N| -> Vol(S^3 minus K). It is not proved here.

For the intended torsor proof, the missing work is to identify the selected all-level quantum-A period with the calibrated geometric action period, preserving the peripheral lattice and its realization across levels. This also requires justifying the relevant geometric factor’s survival in the actual summed operator. The action’s classical peripheral marking and regulator norm are now proved, but no comparison of the full pointed quantum period objects has been constructed here.

What Hilbert 90 proves is the comparison of the algebraic modules once their central data match. It does not identify the two central curves, select the regulator lift, or bound the remaining scalar across unrelated levels. For example a central unit (u/2)^n fixes an unpointed root-level module and changes a period at u=1 by 2^(-n). This illustrates missing comparison data, not freedom to alter the already normalized Kashaev invariant.

The reference anchor above removes this scalar freedom on the Kashaev fiber once a common rational solution line and its anchored comparison are established. It does not prove that a proposed geometric period belongs to that line or has the required residue. Without the rank-one hypothesis, the all-level theorem supplies the actual quantum period by finite fixed-color pointing instead. Its uncontrolled growth is now explicitly in the long operator product and its whole germ; it is not an arbitrary initial normalization. The comparison of that expression with the marked regulator remains unproved.

A useful sufficient normalization control is proved: a fixed nonzero meromorphic or algebraic Puiseux germ c(q) at q=1 satisfies log|c(exp(2pi*i/n))|=O(log n), provided evaluation is defined and nonzero. Factor c(q)=(q-1)^a h(q), h(1)!=0. Such a comparison factor cannot change the volume rate. Producing that controlled comparison for the actual quantum-A period is part of the missing theorem.

The central specialization theorem strengthens this normalization control. A factor on a fixed finite algebraic cover of (q,t,M,z), specialized by t=1/N, q=exp(c/N), z=M^(kN), has at most polynomially growing nonzero whole-germ value at M=1. This includes the exact ADO factor, including its specified root. It controls known algebraic comparisons; it does not construct the unknown comparison to the geometric period.

No alternatingness assumption is used in these structural lemmas. This does not establish the volume conjecture for all hyperbolic or alternating knots. The work above identifies explicit torsors, an algebraic regulator connection, and the exact remaining compatibility.
