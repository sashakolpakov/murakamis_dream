Uniform estimates for a tree whose junctions all carry crossing circles

This note proves two uniform estimates used in the quantitative filling limit. It does not identify a complex critical value with the volume of a filled complement. The latter still requires the original marked geometry and smooth completion.

Fix a finite ordered tree. Every vertex carries its actual crossing-circle operator, including the degree-one vertices. Write dvd_v for a vertex degree and av=dv/(2π)a_v=d_v/(2\pi). All graph data and integer crossing powers are fixed before the level NN tends to infinity. Constants in polynomial prefactors may depend on these fixed data. The exponential constants below do not depend on the tree.

1. A uniform loss outside the retained interval

Use the fixed complete Newton basis, the physical diagonal edge factors, the full value-and-first-derivative norm, and the fixed-power crossing bounds from the full polynomial trace bounds. A low basis element has norm at most a polynomial times exp⁡(Ng(k/N))\exp(Ng(k/N)); a high basis element has polynomial norm. Here g(x)=J(2x)−4J(x),J(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πu)du, g(x)=J(2x)-4J(x),\qquad J(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi u)\,du, g′(x)=2log⁡cot⁡(πx),g″(x)=−4πsin⁡(2πx)≤−4π. g'(x)=2\log\cot(\pi x),\qquad g''(x)=-\frac{4\pi}{\sin(2\pi x)}\le-4\pi. Thus, with G0=g(1/4)G_0=g(1/4), g(x)≤G0−2π(x−1/4)2. g(x)\le G_0-2\pi(x-1/4)^2.

Each physical edge index occurs at its two ends. Quotient multiplication, a fixed crossing power, and the trace cost only a polynomial in the full norm. Consequently the absolute scalar sum is bounded by a polynomial times the sum of the products of the squared basis bounds. There are only polynomially many edge-index assignments for a fixed tree. Its complete maximum is F0=2|E|G0F_0=2|E|G_0.

Retain the interval I=[1/5,3/10]I=[1/5,3/10] on every edge. A low edge outside II loses at least π/100\pi/100 in this rate. A high edge loses 2G02G_0, which is larger. The sum of every discarded assignment therefore has upper rate at most F0−π/100+o(1). F_0-\pi/100+o(1). Equivalently, any fixed loss strictly smaller than π/100\pi/100, such as π/200\pi/200, holds with a polynomial prefactor for all sufficiently large levels. This statement retains both level parities and every first normal. It uses no comparison with a complete tensor at the same high index.

3. The explicit action of one edge

The two-ended Newton action is the critical value in xx of R(x,t)+R(x,u),R(x,t)=∫0xlog⁡(2cos⁡(2πv)+2cos⁡t)dv−2J(x). R(x,t)+R(x,u),\qquad R(x,t)=\int_0^x\log(2\cos(2\pi v)+2\cos t)\,dv-2J(x). The logarithm is continued from (x,t,u)=(1/4,0,0)(x,t,u)=(1/4,0,0). Put p=tan⁡(t/2)p=\tan(t/2), q=tan⁡(u/2)q=\tan(u/2). The wire critical equation gives tan⁡2(πx)=11+p2+q2. \tan^2(\pi x)=\frac1{1+p^2+q^2}. If ψ(t,u)\psi(t,u) denotes the selected critical value, direct differentiation of the integral yields ψt=−1πarsinh⁡p1+q2, \psi_t=-\frac1\pi\operatorname{arsinh} \frac{p}{\sqrt{1+q^2}}, ψ″(t,u)=12π1+p2+q2(−(1+p2)pqpq−(1+q2)),det⁡ψ″=14π2. \psi''(t,u)=\frac1{2\pi\sqrt{1+p^2+q^2}} \begin{pmatrix}-(1+p^2)&pq\\pq&-(1+q^2)\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det\psi''=\frac1{4\pi^2}. All square roots and inverse functions use their branches at the origin. The full packet action is the sum over its physical edges. Its Hessian at zero is therefore −diag⁡(dv/(2π))-\operatorname{diag}(d_v/(2\pi)). On the real angular chamber the edge Hessian is strictly negative definite. For the complex contours, this real statement must be supplemented by an explicit holomorphic bound; it does not prove global complex nondegeneracy or the geometric filling assertion.
