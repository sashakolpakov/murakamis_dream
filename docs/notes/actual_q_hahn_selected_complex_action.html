The actual q-Hahn column selects a complex leading action

30 October 2026. The negative-root theorem gives an asymptotic for the ACTUAL local Fourier sum, including its cancellation. The normalized logarithm converges on the entire slit plane to the analytic continuation of its positive real variational action. In the right half-plane this selects a nondegenerate internal saddle with its original logarithms.

This is a local leading-order theorem at both level parities. It does not give a complete amplitude expansion, a uniform generic-q normal, or a noncancellation theorem for the assembled plat source.

1. The exact finite polynomial and its logarithm

Use the original coefficients of the phase compiler:

P_N(w)=sum_a E(t_N,j_N,u_N,a) w^a,
E(t,j,u,a)=beta(j,a) beta(u,a) beta(t-u,j-a)/beta(t,j),
a0_N=max(0,u_N-t_N+j_N), a1_N=min(j_N,u_N).

Here 0<=j_N<=t_N<N and 0<=u_N<=t_N. Every beta is the positive sine binomial at theta=pi/N. No fitted scalar or knot value enters this definition. The negative-root theorem proves the exact factorization

P_N(w)/P_N(1)=w^a0_N product_(r=1)^(a1_N-a0_N)
                                  (w+rho_(N,r))/(1+rho_(N,r)),
rho_(N,r)>0.                                         (1)

Consequently on Omega=C minus (-infinity,0] the actual polynomial has a unique holomorphic logarithm that is real at w=1. Define

L_N(w)=Log(P_N(w))/N.                                 (2)

This is a specified logarithm, not the principal argument of a possibly many-times-wound numerical value. Its real part is always log|P_N(w)|/N, independent of that qualification.

2. The real variational problem is strict

Suppose

(t_N,j_N,u_N)/N -> (tau,xi,upsilon),
0<xi<tau<1, 0<upsilon<tau.                            (3)

Set a0=max(0,upsilon-tau+xi), a1=min(xi,upsilon), and use

J(s)=integral_0^s log(2 sin(pi y))dy,
B(v,a)=J(v)-J(a)-J(v-a),
Ecal(alpha)=B(xi,alpha)+B(upsilon,alpha)
             +B(tau-upsilon,xi-alpha)-B(tau,xi).

The interval (a0,a1) is nonempty. For every real w>0 the function Ecal(alpha)+alpha log w has a unique interior maximum, denoted alpha(w), and write its value as psi(w). Its derivative is

Ecal'(alpha)=log[sin^2(pi(xi-alpha)) sin(pi(upsilon-alpha))
            / (sin^2(pi alpha) sin(pi(tau-upsilon-xi+alpha)))].

The derivative tends to +infinity at a0 and -infinity at a1. Its second derivative is strictly negative:

Ecal''=-pi{2[cot(pi(xi-alpha))+cot(pi alpha)]
         +cot(pi(upsilon-alpha))
         +cot(pi(tau-upsilon-xi+alpha))}<0.           (4)

For the sign, the first paired arguments sum to pi xi in (0,pi); the second pair sums to pi(tau-xi) in (0,pi). Apply cot A+cot B=sin(A+B)/(sin A sin B). All displayed individual arguments lie in (0,pi) on the support interior.

Uniform sine-factorial estimates give

log E(t_N,j_N,u_N,a)=N Ecal_N(a/N)+O(log N),

over the ENTIRE finite support; Ecal_N uses the actual scaled parameters. The support is an interval of the full integer lattice. An upper bound by its largest term and a lower bound by a nearest lattice point to the unique maximizer therefore prove

L_N(w) -> psi(w),           w>0.                      (5)

The argument is uniform when (3) ranges over a compact subset of its strict domain and w ranges over a compact positive interval. Factorial endpoints are included in the upper bound; none is silently removed before taking the maximum.

3. Negative roots prove the complex limit

For each compact K in Omega,

sup_(w in K,rho>=0)
   |Log((w+rho)/(1+rho))| < infinity.                 (6)

Indeed (w+rho)/(1+rho) is on the segment between w and 1. Its modulus stays bounded above and away from zero uniformly on K, and its argument stays on the single branch between Arg w and zero. Equivalently compactify rho by rho/(1+rho) in [0,1]. The same bound applies to Log w. Since a1_N<N, (1) implies that

L_N(w)-L_N(1)

is locally uniformly bounded on Omega. Equation (5) bounds L_N(1). Cauchy estimates and subsequence compactness now give holomorphic subsequential limits, uniformly on compact subsets. All such limits agree on the positive real axis by (5); the identity theorem makes them identical on the connected slit plane. Thus the WHOLE sequence converges:

L_N(w) -> psi_hol(w), locally uniformly on Omega.      (7)

All complex derivatives converge locally uniformly as well. This proves existence and uniqueness of the analytic continuation in (7), instead of assuming that an arbitrary algebraic saddle has that role. The same subsequence argument proves uniformity for compact strict parameter families in (3): any proposed failure has a convergent parameter subsequence, to which the preceding proof applies. Continuity of psi_hol in those parameters follows by the same compactness and positive-axis uniqueness argument.

Only elementary compactness for holomorphic functions is used here: Cauchy estimates give equicontinuity on a compact exhaustion; successive subsequences converge there, and the real-axis values determine their unique holomorphic limit. No contour replacement of the finite source or unproved saddle coefficient is inserted.

4. A positive root measure fixes the branch

Put alpha_hol(w)=w psi_hol’(w). Differentiating (1) yields

w L_N'(w)=a0_N/N+(1/N)sum_r w/(w+rho_(N,r)).          (8)

The measures (1/N)sum_r delta_rho have total mass tending to a1-a0. Compactify their domain by adjoining zero and infinity. Any weak limit mu obeys

alpha_hol(w)=a0+integral w/(w+rho) dmu(rho).          (9)

For real w, the envelope derivative of the strict maximum gives alpha_hol(w)=alpha(w). The barrier equation shows alpha(w)->a0 as w decreases to zero and alpha(w)->a1 as w increases to infinity. Thus mu has no mass at zero or infinity and has total mass a1-a0 on (0,infinity). Formula (9), rather than a chosen ordering of quadratic roots, specifies the actual selected branch.

For Im w>0 its imaginary part is strictly positive. If additionally Re w>=0 and w!=0, then

a0<Re alpha_hol(w)<a1.                               (10)

For (10), each integrand w/(w+rho) has real part strictly between zero and one. At positive real w, alpha_hol is the original real interior maximum. Along the physical first-quadrant arc all four sine arguments in (4) stay nonzero: their imaginary parts are nonzero away from the positive real axis, and their real-axis initial values lie in the support interior. More explicitly, (10) keeps each argument in the vertical strip 0<Re s<1. There Re(sin(pi s))>0, so its logarithm has one analytic branch agreeing with the positive real logarithm.

The saddle equation therefore continues there without an arbitrary logarithm reset:

Ecal'(alpha_hol(w))+Log w=0.                         (11)

It is nondegenerate throughout that quadrant. In fact

alpha_hol'(w)=integral rho/(w+rho)^2 dmu(rho)

never vanishes there. For 0<=Arg w<pi/2 the integrands lie in an open angular sector of width less than pi; at Arg w=pi/2 each has strictly negative imaginary part. Their positive integral is nonzero. Differentiating (11) gives

Ecal''(alpha_hol(w))=-1/[w alpha_hol'(w)] !=0.         (12)

Thus on the physical arc psi_hol is exactly the continued critical value Ecal(alpha_hol)+alpha_hol Log w, with its positive-axis normalization. This conclusion concerns the leading logarithm. It does not by itself compute a Gaussian prefactor or an error O(1/N).

5. Consequence for every actual complete-circle column

On a complete-circle channel, the exact column is

f_(u_N)(c_(j_N))=(-1)^u_N q^[-(j_N+1)u_N] P_N(q^delta_N),
delta_N=t_N+j_N-(N-1).

If (3) holds and xi<=1/2, tau+xi>=1, then

q^delta_N -> w_star=exp[pi i(tau+xi-1)],
0<=Arg w_star<=pi/2.

Equation (7) supplies the actual, cancellation-sensitive limit

lim_N (1/N)log|f_(u_N)(c_(j_N))|
                          =Re psi_hol(w_star).        (13)

Both level parities and all composite levels are included. The explicit unit prefactor gives the unwrapped leading complex phase psi_hol(w_star)+pi i(upsilon-xi upsilon) in the compiler’s convention. At the folded endpoint xi=1/2 this selects the actual local branch of its previously derived quadratic. The high physical degree block uses its already proved exact index translation, so (13) applies there in those translated variables too.

For comparison, negative roots give the finite bound

cos(phi/2)^(a1_N-a0_N) P_N(1)
   <= |P_N(exp(i phi))| <= P_N(1), |phi|<pi.          (14)

The lower factor can be EXPONENTIAL in N. It is not a polynomial normalization loss. Formula (13) determines that cancellation rate through the selected analytic action instead of dropping it.

This does not yet control cancellations among different magnetic or channel states in a whole plat, the mixed-color aliases, the original generic first normals, or the selected global geometric period. Those remain necessary for the general long-filling volume equality.
