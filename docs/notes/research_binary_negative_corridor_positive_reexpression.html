A positive geometric presentation for negative chains of twos and threes

A negative chain made from powers minus two and minus three, with power minus two at both ends, has an exact marked replacement by a positive chain whose magnitudes are at least three. One positive crossing remains at each outside boundary. The established geometric theorem therefore gives a positive decomposition of the same partially filled exterior, without assuming positivity of its old cells.

There is also an exact identity in the full original quantum algebra. It gives another finite presentation of the same source. When the runs of twos satisfy the bounds in Section 6, the established long-power theorem applies to the replacement. This gives a full pendant boundary asymptotic and, after a sufficiently long closing twist, the volume conjecture for the resulting knot. The argument does not require identifying stationary points in the old presentation.

1. The complementary continued fraction

Write the negative magnitudes as 𝒃=(2r0,3,2r1,3,…,3,2rk),r0,rk≥1,r1,…,rk−1≥0.(1) \mathbf b=(2^{r_0},3,2^{r_1},3,\ldots,3,2^{r_k}), \qquad r_0,r_k\ge1,\quad r_1,\ldots,r_{k-1}\ge0. \qquad\text{(1)} Here kk counts the threes, 2r2^r denotes a string of rr twos, and m=k+∑rjm=k+\sum r_j is the path length. Adjacent threes are allowed. If there are no threes, write 𝒃=(2m)\mathbf b=(2^m). Set 𝒄={(m+1),k=0,(r0+2,r1+3,…,rk−1+3,rk+2),k≥1.(2) \mathbf c= \begin{cases} (m+1),&k=0,\\ (r_0+2,r_1+3,\ldots,r_{k-1}+3,r_k+2),&k\ge1. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(2)} There are ℓ=k+1\ell=k+1 complementary entries. All are at least three, except for the single original vertex 𝒃=(2)\mathbf b=(2), whose complement is (2)(2). When k=1k=1, there are no middle entries in the second line of (2).

Lemma 1. For the negative continued fraction α=[b1,…,bm]−=b1−1b2−1⋯−1/bm, \alpha=[b_1,\ldots,b_m]_- =b_1-\frac1{b_2-\dfrac1{\cdots-1/b_m}}, one has α/(α−1)=[𝒄]−.(3) \alpha/(\alpha-1)=[\mathbf c]_-. \qquad\text{(3)}

Proof. If a tail has value β>1\beta>1 and complementary value d=β/(β−1)d=\beta/(\beta-1), then 2−1/β(2−1/β)−1=d+1,3−1/β(3−1/β)−1=2−1d+1.(4) \frac{2-1/\beta}{(2-1/\beta)-1}=d+1,\qquad \frac{3-1/\beta}{(3-1/\beta)-1}=2-\frac1{d+1}. \qquad\text{(4)} Prepending a two increments the first complementary coefficient. Prepending a three inserts a two and increments the previous first coefficient. The last two has complement two. Induction from the right gives (2). ▫\square

Examples are (2,3,2)↦(3,3)(2,3,2)\mapsto(3,3), (2,3,3,2)↦(3,3,3)(2,3,3,2)\mapsto(3,3,3), and (2,2,3,2,2)↦(4,4)(2,2,3,2,2)\mapsto(4,4). The proof holds at every length. The arithmetic is the elementary complementary continued-fraction operation; no novelty is claimed for it.

3. The exact original source

Let MM multiply by the original crossing in the full Newton algebra 𝒜N\mathcal A_N, let JJ reverse its coefficients, and put κN=exp⁡πi(N−1)22N.(15) \kappa_N=\exp\frac{\pi i(N-1)^2}{2N}. \qquad\text{(15)} Repeated spectral roots retain their first normal derivatives. 4. The positive geometric presentation

Theorem 3. Let a selected path in an original circle tree have the powers (1), at least one complete outside end, and all other auxiliary circles complete. Its partially filled exterior has the positive circle-tree decomposition associated with (7), or (12) for a pendant path. Its outside primitive curves are carried by (14), and its original main-component condition is unchanged.

Proof. Every new magnitude is at least three, except the single vertex (2)(2). The negative-corridor geometry theorem, Theorem 15, also gives positive powers: replace a negative solution tvt_v by −tv¯-\overline{t_v}. Its returns become −rv,e¯-\overline{r_{v,e}}, its full unit charges have the positive sign, its core imaginary parts remain negative, and the inequality ℜq>(ℜa)2+(ℜb)2\Re q>\sqrt{(\Re a)^2+(\Re b)^2} is unchanged. The isolated-vertex theorem supplies the exceptional single positive power two.

Retain the outside crossings in their carriers and disk frames. Their periods vanish when the outside angles are zero. The original annular attachments, with the proper rotations transported, are therefore the marked attachments of the new circle-tree presentation. The pointwise marked-completion theorem gives its smooth hyperbolic filling. The tangle isotopy identifies this with the original partially filled exterior. It preserves the main strands and every outside component, so the same original knot or link condition applies. ▫\square

The absolute volume is the positive stationary action of the new whole presentation: 2πℜ{∑new edges eψ(te−,te+)+i4π∑new selected vcvtv2−i∑new selected vtv}.(19) 2\pi\Re\left\{ \sum_{\text{new edges }e}\psi(t_{e^-},t_{e^+}) +\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{\text{new selected }v}c_vt_v^2 -i\sum_{\text{new selected }v}t_v \right\}. \qquad\text{(19)} Every new edge is included, including complete outside edges. This uses the marked-completion theorem’s absolute normalization.

Equations (17)–(18) supply an exact finite source with this positive geometric presentation. They do not identify an old stationary cycle with a new one. No equality between individual old and new stationary actions is inferred from a finite operator identity. A full asymptotic for arbitrary adjacent powers at least three, or a proof of the required cycle transport, is still needed for a volume-conjecture conclusion in that generality. Section 6 gives the consequence when the complementary powers satisfy an already proved asymptotic theorem.

For counting, the path (1) is fixed. Remaining powers undergo fixed integer translations, with permutations and rotations retained. This map is injective: no free powers are merged. Several disjoint fixed paths add their fixed shifts at a shared outside vertex. Thus the replacement has no infinite-fiber loss. It supplies a geometric family containing mixed short powers, not cofinite coverage of all assignments.

6. Pendant boundary asymptotics and a long closing twist

Read the coefficients in (1) and (2) from the outside boundary towards the terminal cap. Thus the positive pendant word is F𝒄,+=T(Rc1T(Rc2⋯T(Rcℓ)⋯)).(20) F_{\mathbf c,+} =T\bigl(R^{c_1}T(R^{c_2}\cdots T(R^{c_\ell})\cdots)\bigr). \qquad\text{(20)} Its terminal power is cℓc_\ell. The original component test is essential: the closure must have one main component and satisfy the original exclusion of internal closed components. It is transported by the literal tangle isotopy. A sufficient condition for (26) is |a|≥Bcl+1|a|\ge B_{\mathrm{ cl}}+1; (26) is the precise translated condition. For each fixed negative binary word satisfying (21), the map of remaining assignments is the injection a↦a+1a\mapsto a+1. Thus the conclusion includes all sufficiently long signed closing assignments on that stratum, with no identification of distinct free parameters.

For a path with two free outside boundaries, (17) is still an exact identity with both variables and all normal data. The one-boundary theorem used above does not by itself give a joint two-boundary asymptotic. Such a replacement can be made inside a whole marked tree and the existing tree theorem applied if the transformed whole tree satisfies its hypotheses, with all outside shifts and rotations retained. No standalone joint packet for an arbitrary complementary chain is asserted here.

7. What repeated complementation can reach

The complementary fraction is defined for every finite list 𝒃=(b1,…,bm)\mathbf b=(b_1,\ldots,b_m) with bj≥2b_j\ge2. Let D(𝒃)D(\mathbf b) be the unique list with entries at least two and [D(𝒃)]−=[𝒃]−[𝒃]−−1.(30) [D(\mathbf b)]_-=\frac{[\mathbf b]_-}{[\mathbf b]_--1}. \qquad\text{(30)} The ceiling algorithm in the definite rational normal form, Section 2, proves uniqueness. Since x↦x/(x−1)x\mapsto x/(x-1) is an involution, so is DD.

Proposition 6. The list D(𝒃)D(\mathbf b) has every entry at least three if and only if 𝒃\mathbf b is a binary list with both end entries two, other than the singleton (2)(2). With the original outside frames, the only uniform-sign chain forms with magnitudes at least two, allowing integral twists at the two ends and the retained proper rotations, are the negative form 𝒃\mathbf b and the positive form D(𝒃)D(\mathbf b). The same assertion holds for a pendant word with its original outside frame and cap.

Proof. For a one-entry list (a)(a), D((a))=(2a−1).(31) D((a))=(2^{a-1}). \qquad\text{(31)} If the complementary list for a tail is (d1,d2,…)(d_1,d_2,\ldots), prepending a≥2a\ge2 to the original list changes it to (2a−2,d1+1,d2,…).(32) (2^{a-2},d_1+1,d_2,\ldots). \qquad\text{(32)} Here an empty string is omitted. Equation (32) follows from (4): prepending two increments the first entry, and each further unit in aa inserts a two at the front.

In this recursion, only the first entry of an existing list can ever increase. If the final entry bmb_m is at least three, (31) initially has a two that can never be increased. If any other bj≥4b_j\ge4, (32) inserts at least two leading twos; the second of them can never be increased. If b1≥3b_1\ge3, the final application of (32) leaves a leading two. Thus absence of twos in the complement requires entries in {2,3}\{2,3\} and both ends two. Formula (2) proves sufficiency, except that the singleton two has itself as complement.

For the statement with two boundaries, write the normalized matrix of the original negative word as (ABCD),0<A,D<C,C/A=[𝒃]−.(33) \begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}, \qquad 0<A,D<C,\qquad C/A=[\mathbf b]_- . \qquad\text{(33)} Integral end twists do not change A,DA,D modulo CC, and the proper rotations have identity linear part on the pillowcase. A uniform-negative chain with entries at least two already has its diagonals in this normalized range, so uniqueness of the ceiling expansion forces its list to be 𝒃\mathbf b. For a uniform-positive chain the normalized diagonal residues are the complementary ones C−A,C−DC-A,C-D. Its list is therefore uniquely D(𝒃)D(\mathbf b), with the two end twists +1+1. The full affine comparison retains the appropriate rotation as in (10)–(11).

For a pendant word, its normalized slope is x=1/[𝒃]−∈(0,1)x=1/[\mathbf b]_-\in(0,1). A negative chain preceded by an integer twist has slope r+1/[𝒅]−r+1/[\mathbf d]_-; equality to xx forces r=0r=0 and 𝒅=𝒃\mathbf d=\mathbf b. A positive chain has slope r−1/[𝒅]−r-1/[\mathbf d]_-; equality forces r=1r=1 and 𝒅=D(𝒃)\mathbf d=D(\mathbf b). The established cap stabilizer supplies the literal isotopy and does not enlarge either list. ▫\square

Consequently the all-magnitudes-at-least-three geometric theorem, combined only with repeated marked complementation and end twists, covers exactly two types of negative lists: those already having all entries at least three, and the binary lists of Proposition 6. The separately proved isolated power two is an additional elementary case. For example, (3,2,3)↔(2,4,2)(34) (3,2,3)\longleftrightarrow(2,4,2) \qquad\text{(34)} has a two in both normalized forms. This is an obstruction to that particular reduction strategy, not to a positive subdivision of the actual filled manifold. Local replacements may produce a mixed-sign chain, but if their eventual result is a uniform-sign chain in the same outside frames, the same uniqueness argument still applies.

No conclusion here permits a quarter-turn of an outside attachment while leaving its primitive curves untouched. Such a move changes the retained boundary frame and would require a separate geometric attachment argument. The fixed integer end shifts and proper rotations already allowed preserve the injective assignment bookkeeping of Section 2.
