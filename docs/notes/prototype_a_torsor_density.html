The same geometric residue in the two prototype A-periods

Research note, 27 October 2026. The user suggested using 4_1 and 5_2 to guide a general proof. Here the two cases give the same exact determinant-line identity, with a simple Riley residue. The calculation uses the original coefficient model and the intrinsic linearized holonomy complex. It does not prove the selected finite period’s volume asymptotic.

1. A precise statement in both complete trace fields

Use the three-strand words, in the fixed Hurwitz action order,

beta_4=[1,-2,1,-2],
beta_5=[1,1,1,2,-1,2].

The first is the figure-eight braid. An independent local SnapPy recognition of the second gives 5_2. The symbolic fixed-point calculation below does not use that recognition as a representation-theoretic input.

Let

A=[1 1;0 1], B=[1 0;t 1].

For 4_1 take C=A^(-1)B A B^(-1)A; for 5_2 put

D=A^(-1)B A, E=D^(-1)A D, C=E A E^(-1).

The respective nonabelian parabolic fixed-point equations are

f_4(t)=t^2-t+1=0,
f_5(t)=t^3+t^2+2t+1=0.                       (1.1)

These follow by substituting the matrices in the Hurwitz word and factoring the entries of F_beta(A,B,C)-(A,B,C). Thus the comparison uses their exact complete trace fields, not a floating-point hyperbolic representation.

Conjugate by the full framing matrix

F(r,s)=[r 1-r;s -s], s!=0.

Let Omega_beta denote the original variable principal normal Gaussian density, retaining both framing derivatives and all crossing, pivot and writhe variable factors. Its fixed base-only quantum normalization line remains separately marked, as in the normal-period construction. Let T_beta be the torsion of the acyclic holonomy complex proved invariant under presentation moves in the uniform Markov theorem.

Prototype proposition. In the respective exact function fields Q[t]/(f_i)(s,r), one has

Omega_beta^2=-(T_beta/2) Omega_cut^2,
Omega_cut=-dr wedge ds/[s(1+s)].             (1.2)

More explicitly,

4_1: -T_beta/2=(2t-1)/3,
5_2: -T_beta/2=(3t^2-6t+1)/23.              (1.3)

These identities hold on the full two-variable framing chart, rather than on finitely many frames. The omitted chart divisors are retained when comparing the selected relative source; (1.2) is a statement on its regular locus.

2. Independent sides of the determinant calculation

The Gaussian side is computed directly from the actual balanced crossing Hessians and amplitudes. The integral closure lattice has d=m+1 variables: d=5 and d=7 in the two words. The Hessian has its two independently differentiated framing null vectors. Its transverse determinants are nonzero, of ranks 3 and 5. The squared normal density is

a_beta^2 det(d chi/d(r,s))^2/det(-H_perp).

The torsion side is computed independently from dF_beta-I, infinitesimal conjugation and d(H_b…H_1)(H_b…H_1)^(-1). It does not use the crossing Hessian or an expected density formula. Exact ranks make the holonomy complex acyclic. Its determinant ratio is evaluated at the ordinary frame (r,s)=(2,3); the general framing invariance of that ratio was proved by the chain argument, not by interpolation.

The audit then proves (1.2) by exact equality of these independent expressions in the full function field. FLINT polynomial arithmetic in s,r and an explicit reduced quadratic or cubic number-field arithmetic are used. No finite-field sampling, interpolation or numerical rounding is part of this equality.

3. The common scalar is an ordinary algebraic residue

Define S_4(t)=-1 and S_5(t)=t^2+2. The two constants in (1.3) satisfy exactly

-T_beta/2=1/[S_i(t) f_i'(t)]
        =Res_(v=t) dv/[S_i(v)f_i(v)].       (3.1)

For 4_1 this is -1/(2t-1)=(2t-1)/3. For 5_2 it is

1/[(t^2+2)(3t^2+2t+2)]
                    =(3t^2-6t+1)/23

modulo f_5. Both S_i f_i’ are units in their trace fields. The Gaussian source coefficient is therefore a nonzero algebraic residue at each simple marked parabolic place.

This is a concrete instance of the requested rigid-period viewpoint: the coefficient is an evaluation in a finite spectral algebra with its residue co-source. It does not assert that the whole finite Kashaev invariant is already the trace of these classical residues.

4. The uniform twist lemma suggested by both cases

This section records the prototype diagnostic, rather than the scope of the main program. The subsequent arbitrary-bridge source reduction uses a 2k-plat with unrestricted k and crossing word, eliminates every intermediate stage with its source and dual, and replaces the single Riley residue by an endpoint determinant complex. No twist-family assumption is imposed on that theorem or the intended volume result.

For the signed twist presentation already used in the A-curve note, let S_j(z) be the second-kind Chebyshev recurrence and put

f_k(t)=(1+t)S_(k-1)(t^2+2)-S_(k-2)(t^2+2).

The prototypes are k=-1 and k=2. A precise next determinant lemma is the following proposed uniform identity at a simple nonabelian parabolic root:

T_k=-2/[S_(k-1)(t^2+2) f_k'(t)].             (4.1)

Together with a uniform Gaussian/holonomy determinant map, it would make the principal cut residue explicit for the entire regular twist family. Equation (4.1) is not claimed proved here for arbitrary k.

There is a direct route to the general algebraic calculation. For A=[M 1;0 M^(-1)], B=[M 0;-u M^(-1)], W=AB(-1)A(-1)B, the complete relation is

A W^k-W^k B=R_k(A-B),
R_k=(s-u-1)S_(k-1)(z)-S_(k-2)(z),
s=M^2+M^(-2), z=u^2+(2-s)u+2.

At R_k=0, W(-k)=S_(k-1)(z)(W(-1)-(s-u-1)I). Differentiating the relator therefore exposes the factor S_(k-1) dR_k. At M=1,u=-t, dR_k=f_k’(t)dt on the parabolic slice. This explains the denominator in (4.1). What remains is the explicit determinant/source map from that one-relator complex to the braid complex, with its normalization. Merely observing the denominator is not a proof of the uniform identity.

5. Relation to the required volume theorem

The geometric regulator action was already calibrated to hyperbolic volume. This proposition fixes its algebraic principal coefficient and its framing/cut dependence in both prototypes, with the same residue rule. It provides a testable common lemma for the general construction.

To prove the original volume limit, one still has to show that the actual selected global quantum period pushes to this marked source with a nonzero, subexponential comparison factor. A nonzero local Gaussian coefficient alone does not prove that statement. Neither this proposition nor the Markov theorem is presented as a volume-conjecture proof.
