The closed-braid period line and the Alexander divisor

Research note, 26 October 2026. This globalizes the local regular holonomy line to an admissible closed-braid fixed-point scheme. It supplies a line and a literal cut functional, whose nilpotent restriction is the original ADO period. It proves an exact equality with that period on upper triangular nonabelian reducible families, by quantum weight descent. It also identifies the normal deformation equations of the abelian representation family. These are classical Alexander equations. They explain where a continuation from that family could reach a nonabelian component, and why the formal neighborhood of the actual root augmentation alone cannot reach complete hyperbolic holonomy.

No global Markov-normalized geometric invariant, quantum meridian source extension, or volume theorem is inferred. The Alexander divisor below is a divisor in the classical representation problem; it is not asserted to divide a specified quantum A-operator.

1. The admissible fixed-point scheme

Let beta be a braid on b strands whose closure is a knot K. Fix q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2, N>=2. Use unrestricted quantum sl_2, the regular splitting modules, and the central parameters of the preceding note. In particular use balanced variables

k=K^N, e=rho^N E^N, f=-rho^N F^N,
k+k^(-1)+ef=-C_N(Omega), rho=q-q^(-1).

Each checked rational algebra automorphism Psi_i=tau_i composed with r_i preserves the iterated coproduct. The local identities imply the braid relations. Its center-point map is the inverse Hurwitz transport, after the recorded Borel prefix change. Account for the reversal of composition under pullback: the center map of the actual operator word is the inverse of its positive classical braid action. They have the same fixed-point scheme.

Let Z_beta be this center fixed-point scheme, also imposing the individual Casimir identifications. Restrict to the open charts on which every central divisor used by the word and its inverse is a unit. Pass to a cover choosing K-eigenvalue roots and the regular splitting charts. This is admissibility, not a choice of a new state. At the actual augmentation the roots and all word charts extend uniquely as formal germs. At other points the covers, closing identifications and their transitions must be retained.

The explicit prefix/Gauss calculation in the marked braid note identifies the central fixed equations with the braid presentation of the knot group. In the root convention the meridians are

mu_j=delta B_(-,j-1)^(-1)G_j B_(-,j-1),
delta=(-1)^(N-1),
G_j=[[k_j,e_j],[k_j f_j,k_j^(-1)+e_j f_j]].      (1.1)

The constant delta leaves the Hurwitz conjugation rules unchanged. At the actual Kashaev color, k_j=delta, e_j=f_j=0, Omega_j=-2, and every mu_j is I. Near this point the K-root cover and Casimir branch are etale: C_N’(-2)=delta N^2 is a unit. Thus the completed central fixed scheme is precisely the completed SL_2 representation scheme at the trivial representation, in these coordinates.

2. A global Hom line and a cut functional

On the indicated splitting cover let V_i be the rank-N regular module and W=V_1 tensor … tensor V_b. The full tensor central quotient is End(W), by the explicit matrix-unit argument already proved. Because the center and Casimirs are fixed, Psi_beta induces an automorphism of this matrix algebra. Define

L_(beta,N)={J in End(W):
             rho(Psi_beta(a))J=J rho(a) for all a}.          (2.1)

This is an invertible module: two splitting representations of the same matrix algebra are locally intertwined by an invertible matrix, and all such matrices differ by a scalar. To see this without a Skolem–Noether citation, choose matrix units E_rs in the target. Their images under the other action give rank-one projectors and arrows. Choose a frame of the first projector’s image and apply the arrows to obtain the columns of an invertible intertwiner. Every other intertwiner is determined by its first column, proving the rank-one assertion and the scalar transition functions. Invertible elements of (2.1) form its G_m frame torsor.

Since Psi_beta fixes the diagonal coproduct, every J in (2.1) commutes with the diagonal unrestricted quantum-group action. Put g=K^(1-N). At the specified root S^2=Ad_g and g is group-like. Take the actual right pivotal trace on strands 2,…,b:

ptr(J)=Tr_(V_2...V_b)((I tensor g_2 tensor ... tensor g_b)J).
                                                        (2.2)

This is an endomorphism of V_1 as a quantum-group module. Here is a direct check of the closure rule. The ordinary dual has action u.phi=phi composed with S(u). The coevaluation sum w_j tensor w_j^* is invariant. The opposite evaluation is w tensor phi -> phi(gw): its invariance is the identity

sum S(u_2)g u_1=epsilon(u)g,

which follows by substituting g u_1=S^2(u_1)g and applying S to sum S(u_1)u_2=epsilon(u). Inserting these two invariant maps around J gives exactly (2.2). Hence the cut commutes with E,F,K.

The splitting quotient on V_1 is the full matrix algebra, whose commutant consists of scalars over the base ring. Therefore

ptr(J)=ev_(beta,N)(J) I_(V_1),
ev_(beta,N):L_(beta,N) -> O_(Z_beta).            (2.3)

This constructs a global scalar-valued period functional on the line, with the source cover and matrix-unit transitions retained. It does not prove that the functional is nonzero at a particular geometric point. A line frame is invertible; its evaluated period can vanish. These are different assertions.

The local determinant-normalized crossing frames give a specified formal frame of (2.1) at the actual augmentation and obey braid relations. The intrinsic line and functional make sense on the admissible fixed scheme, without extending that one frame to a canonical global section. Global root phases and closing maps are still part of a proposed comparison.

3. Exact recovery of the actual ADO germ

On the nilpotent meridian section all colors are equal:

E_i^N=F_i^N=0, x_i=x_0 M, x_0=q^(N-1).

Every checked center step fixes this tuple. Write a=N-1 and R^0 for the actual uncheck R with common Cartan factor removed. The previously constructed local frame is

R_bar=x^a q^(-a^2/2)R^0,
theta_bar=q^(-a^2/2).

For a word of writhe w let J_beta be the product of these checked frames and their inverses. The scalar change of R^0 is precisely the ratio of theta_bar to its actual residual twist x^(-a). Consequently the crossing changes cancel the zero-framing changes:

theta_bar^(-w) ev_(beta,N)(J_beta)=F_N(M).       (3.1)

This is an exact equality of the original reduced ADO germs, for every knot braid in the actual nilpotent section. It is not a choice of a different holonomy-character invariant. In particular the value at M=1 is the already fixed Kashaev invariant.

The full period remains d_N(M)F_N(M), with

d_N(M)=i^(N+3)M^(N-1)/S_N(M^2),
S_N(Y)=1+Y+...+Y^(N-1).

Equation (3.1) does not erase this function or replace it by its value i^(N+3)/N. Nor does it prove a Markov normalization off the nilpotent section; that would require compatible global turn/cap and framing data. The construction in Section 2 is for a specified braid and its admissible holonomy fixed scheme.

The subsequent geometric-root audit now proves generic nonvanishing of this ordinary cut section on the figure-eight geometric Riley component for every N. A positive actual ADO anchor at a nonabelian reducible attachment and direct irreducibility prove that assertion. Exact complete-point tests also certify nonzero values at N=2,3,4 in both embeddings. Zero quantum dimension alone therefore does not force the one-cut section to vanish. This does not imply complete-point nonvanishing at every level, a general-knot result, or equality of those interior central data with the original augmentation below.

4. Uniform formal rigidity at the actual augmentation

Let A be a local Artin C-algebra with maximal ideal m. Any knot-group representation with all meridians mu_i congruent to I modulo m has all its meridians equal. Thus it factors through the abelianization.

Here is an elementary proof that also controls nonreduced fibers. Use a Wirtinger presentation. At a crossing the outgoing understrand meridian is a conjugate of the incoming one. Suppose all meridians are equal to mu_1 modulo m^r. Write mu_i=mu_1+D_i, D_i in m^r, and mu_1=I+X, X in m. Then

[mu_i,mu_j] as an additive matrix commutator lies in m^(r+1),

because [X,D_i], [X,D_j] have that order and [D_i,D_j] has order 2r>=r+1. Multiplying by the inverse matrices shows that each Wirtinger conjugation changes its understrand meridian only modulo m^(r+1). The understrand arcs form one connected cyclic sequence for a knot. All meridians therefore equal mu_1 modulo m^(r+1). The induction starts at r=1 and terminates when m is nilpotent.

Conversely choosing any mu_1 in SL_2(A) congruent to I and sending every meridian to mu_1 satisfies all relations. The two constructions are inverse on all Artin test rings, hence

completed Rep(K) at trivial = completed SL_2 at I.           (4.1)

This is a scheme-level assertion, including infinitesimal thickenings. For complete separated local bases it follows by passage to the Artin quotients. Since the representation scheme is of finite type, faithful flatness of local completion also gives a Zariski neighborhood of the trivial representation consisting entirely of abelian ones.

Consequently the complete hyperbolic representation cannot be reached inside this formal germ of the closed-braid period line. Its class in a positive ideal triangulation is still meaningful on the geometric component elsewhere in the representation scheme. The existence of the line (2.1) at both types of points does not identify its periods across those components.

5. The normal equations along the abelian family

The precise classical attachment divisor can also be derived directly. Fix an abelian representation mu_i=D(s)=diag(s,s^(-1)). Write a first-order variation as mu_i=(I+X_i)D(s) and fix X_1=0. At a Wirtinger relation mu_k=mu_j mu_i mu_j^(-1), linearization gives

X_k=Ad_D X_i+(I-Ad_D)X_j.                      (5.1)

On an eigenspace of Ad_D of eigenvalue t this is

x_k-t x_i+(t-1)x_j=0.                         (5.2)

These are the ordinary Alexander equations, with the sign and meridian square now explicit. They can equally be obtained from the braid action. For the positive Hurwitz step (mu_1,mu_2)->(mu_1^(-1)mu_2 mu_1,mu_1), its derivative is

[[1-t^(-1), t^(-1)],[1,0]],

and the inverse step has block [[0,1],[t,1-t]]. The independent free-word/Fox calculation gives the same matrices.

For clarity, the Alexander interpretation needs no deformation theorem as input. In a deficiency-one meridian presentation the infinite cyclic cover has d_1=(t-1,…,t-1). The abelianized Fox matrix of the b-1 relators maps into ker(d_1), which has basis e_j-e_1, j>1. Its matrix in that basis is the deleted generator/ relator minor in (5.2), or equivalently of I-B_beta(t). Thus its cokernel is the ordinary Alexander module, and its determinant is Delta_K(t) up to the recorded Laurent unit. At t=1 the braid matrix is the permutation of the strands. For a knot it is one cycle, so the deleted cofactor of I-B_beta(1) is +/-1. This also proves Delta_K(1)=+/-1 in this presentation.

On sl_2, Ad_D has weights 1,s2,s(-2). The determinant of the normalized normal Jacobian is therefore

unit(s)*Delta_K(1)Delta_K(s^2)Delta_K(s^(-2)).   (5.3)

If it is nonzero, the abelian family is the entire local formal representation scheme. Indeed fix the first meridian, now allowed to vary over an Artin base, and compare any solution with the abelian solution having that first meridian. At the first order where they differ the normalized difference solves the invertible Jacobian (5.3), so it vanishes. Induction proves equality at all orders. The argument works at a nondiagonal unipotent abelian meridian too: Ad_mu is triangular with all eigenvalues one, so the same determinant is a unit.

It follows that a nonabelian component can meet the abelian representation family only where

Delta_K(s^2)Delta_K(s^(-2))=0.                  (5.4)

This necessary condition neither proves that a given root integrates to an irreducible branch nor that such a branch is the geometric component. The cokernel of the normal matrix is a concrete torsion module supported on this divisor. Simple roots are candidates for studying the coupling there; they are not a quantum period theorem.

Every root does, however, give an exact nonabelian reducible representation. Let t_*=s^2 be a root. It is not one, since Delta_K(1) is a unit. Choose a nonzero kernel vector h of the deleted Alexander matrix, with h_1=0. The omitted equation follows from preservation of the ordered product of meridians. Put

mu_i(u)=(I+u h_i E_12)D(s).                    (5.5)

Multiplication in this upper triangular group is the semidirect rule n(a)D(s)n(b)D(s)=n(a+s^2 b)D(s)^2. Consequently its braid action on h is exactly the linear Alexander action, with no higher terms. Equation (5.5) is therefore a representation for every u. For u!=0 some h_i is nonzero while h_1=0, and t_*!=1 shows that mu_i and mu_1 do not commute. This is an exact nonabelian reducible family approaching the abelian representation. Its character stays abelian; existence of an irreducible deformation remains additional.

On the actual root-central meridian section s=M^N. Hence the coordinate in these equations is

t=s^2=M^(2N)=U.                               (5.6)

At the Kashaev specialization U=1 the Alexander cofactor is a unit. Its whole function Delta_K(U) must still be retained when studying other attachment points. This is a classical normal-deformation factor, not an established source factor in the actual meridian q-difference equation.

The later semicyclic carry-braiding calculation also supplies an explicit matrix proof of the reducible-period comparison on E^N=0. Its corrected wrap and transported centers give genuine holonomy braid maps globally on that locus. Closing all but one strand forces every contributing path to have no wrap, proving the whole cut operator equals F_N(M) I. The common fixed center line has the exact endpoint marks needed for the integral carry recoupling. These are explicit source operators and evaluations on a reducible locus; mixed E/F coupling to a geometric component remains unproved.

6. Weight descent and an exact reducible period anchor

The quantum line has a compatible algebraic scaling. Define

gamma_z(E)=zE, gamma_z(F)=z^(-1)F, gamma_z(K)=K.

All rational crossing formulas preserve this grading. On the center it scales E^N by z^N and F^N by z^(-N), while fixing K^N and Omega. For the chain module, let D_z v_j=z^(-j)v_j. Directly on its three matrices one has

rho_(z^N epsilon,z^(-N)phi)(a)
       =D_z^(-1)rho_(epsilon,phi)(gamma_z(a))D_z.            (6.1)

Here epsilon,phi are raw central powers and the chosen K-root is unchanged. The nonwrapping entries give this identity immediately; at the E and F wrap entries the extra factors are precisely z^N and z^(-N). The a_j and P depend on epsilon*phi and are unchanged.

The matrix-unit crossing frame respects (6.1). Its spectral projector transforms by conjugation and D_z fixes v_0. Each F’ used in a column contributes z^(-1); the target column basis has exactly the compensating D_z weight. Thus

I(z^N epsilon,z^(-N)phi)
    =(D_z tensor D_z)^(-1) I(epsilon,phi)(D_z tensor D_z).
                                                        (6.2)

The determinant and its specified anchored root are invariant, so the normalized frame satisfies the same equation. Checked flips commute with D_z tensor D_z. Products and inverses preserve (6.2). The pivotal matrices commute with D_z, and the cut is scalar, so its value is invariant under the simultaneous center scaling. This proves descent by this diagonal gauge torus for the specified local frame and cut evaluation. It is not a claim of descent by every change of flat-connection frame or a global Markov theorem.

In formal coordinates the scalar period can depend on the central E and F directions only in weight zero: their mixed products, together with the weight-zero parameters. The invariant monomials are generated by epsilon_i*phi_j. In particular, on the semicyclic family phi_i=0 and fixed common x, the scalar period is independent of all positive-weight epsilon_i on any scaling-stable closed family regular at their zero section.

For the upper triangular representations (5.5), all k_i equal k=delta s, all F_i^N vanish, and E_i^N=u epsilon_i^. The Casimirs and all x_i=x=x_0 M_ are constant, with M_*^N=s. Every central crossing divisor D_c is one. Moreover the operator entering H,G has N-th power zero, so their inverses are finite geometric sums. The matrix-unit frames are polynomial in u. Equation (6.2) shows their determinants are independent of u and nonzero at u=0; hence the frames and their inverses are regular for every u. Their determinant normalization is the same known factor as at the nilpotent section. No new root or arbitrary scalar is inserted.

Let f(u) be the zero-framed cut scalar in this frame. Then f(z^N u)=f(u). It is polynomial and regular at zero, so it is constant. Equation (3.1) identifies that constant exactly:

theta_bar^(-w) ev_(beta,N)(J_beta(rho_u))
                  =F_N(M_*) for every u.        (6.3)

The full period equality retains the same entire factor d_N(M_). At a root of Delta_K(M_^(2N)), this factor is regular because M_*^(2N)!=1. This is an explicit actual-period anchor on an exact nonabelian reducible family, at every level, including even and composite levels. It does not require a simple Alexander root. The right-hand side is not proved nonzero uniformly. When it vanishes, the value alone cannot fix a scalar comparison; the specified Hom frame and whole period germ remain necessary.

This anchor still leaves the opposite-weight coupling undetermined. For example a two-parameter deformation with weights +N,-N can have an invariant scalar H(uv), whose value at uv=0 is fixed by (6.3) while its higher coefficients are not. Those coefficients, and whether an attached irreducible branch is geometric, require additional quantum and global data. The scaling proof supplies neither their regulator norm nor a meridian A-equation for them.

7. Consequences for the proposed regulator comparison

The ideal triangulation bridge supplies the complete geometric fundamental class and its regulator. Sections 1–3 now give a closed-braid quantum line and cut functional whose actual nilpotent section is exactly the prescribed period. Sections 4–5 prevent mistaking its formal augmentation neighborhood for a neighborhood of complete hyperbolic holonomy. Section 6 supplies an exact period anchor on the nonabelian reducible families at Alexander roots. It leaves the mixed positive/negative weight dependence and a geometric irreducible branch undetermined. This provides attachment points for every knot with a nonunit Alexander polynomial. If the Alexander polynomial is a unit, there are no such nonabelian reducible attachment points; a general relative comparison cannot rely solely on this route.

A comparison solely by extending the augmentation Hom frame requires additional global information about the components and the attachment divisor. If the route uses an affine meridian source extension instead, its source and the off-diagonal coupling must actually be constructed and matched to this selected cut functional. Neither the nonabelian branch nor the period coupling can be chosen to enforce the desired regulator value. A common peripheral point U=V=1 does not identify the two interior representations.

There remains no proof of the norm identity

(4/N)log|F_N(1)| -> log||P_hyp||=2Vol(M)/pi.

In particular this note proves neither geometric factor survival in the minimal actual operator nor noncancellation in the selected period.

The subsequent Frobenius Verma trace gives these anchors a global polar interpretation. Its rational root value is exactly F_N(M)/Delta_K(M^(2N)), with the same quantum A-ideal and marked Kashaev fiber. At a simple Alexander root, the selected residue in the central coordinate U is the period in (6.3) divided by Delta_K’(U). This is actual source/pole data, not a proposed freely normalized comparison. The marked Bloch norm and transport to a geometric irreducible period remain unproved.

8. Coverage and independent checks

Alexander loci for reducible/nonabelian deformations have extensive prior coverage. The abstracts of Heusener–Porti, math/0411365 and Heusener–Medjerab, 1402.4294 cover such deformations and local representation varieties. They were consulted for scope only; their existence, smoothness and irreducible-deformation proofs are not used here. No novelty is claimed for the Alexander criterion or formal abelian rigidity. The purpose is to derive their exact role in this fixed root-center and actual cut-period construction.

For quantum coverage, McPhail-Snyder’s dissertation, Section 1.3.2 distinguishes triangular semicyclic holonomy invariants from their decomposable ADO specialization. This is existing coverage of the objects involved. Its comparison and invariance proofs are not used here; (6.1)–(6.3) are derived directly from the regular matrices, the specified Hom frame and the literal cut functional. No general novelty claim for reducible holonomy invariants is made.

The audit compares independent free-word/Fox and braid linearizations for 24 knot braids. It solves 24 normalized fixed fibers through third Artin order, at identity, noncentral unipotent and semisimple abelian base points. Two examples on the Alexander divisor give exact nonabelian reducible fixed tuples, rather than just tangent vectors; a two-component example verifies the scope distinction. Ten generic cyclic quantum cases have both central powers nonzero and test all local intertwinings and scalar closed cut functionals. Ten nilpotent/ideal cases recover thirty original ADO cut matrices, with the twist. Twelve changing-center semicyclic families, through level 8, check four parameter values each: all 48 full cut matrices equal the actual ADO cut at that color. They include 168 explicitly intertwined crossing steps and both signs. Fourteen generic cyclic cases check both central wrap weights and all transformed-generator weights. Even and composite levels are included. The results support the uniform proofs, without asserting a volume asymptotic.
