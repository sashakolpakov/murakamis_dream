A positive two-state transfer for the actual spectral tail

31 October 2026. This exact model of the normalized tail includes the negative coefficients of its usual two-term recurrence. It gives compact-interior Perron and outer-angular estimates, not an exponentially accurate interpolation in both endpoint rows or a global sign-cancellation theorem.

1. Exact normalization and word expansion

Use the original one-sided tail from the relative-energy theorem. For fixed actual rows 0≤i≤j0\le i\le j, let r=j−ir=j-i, θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N, and

sa=2sin⁡(aθ),λ=cos⁡θ+cos⁡(2πδ),ηa=λcos⁡θ−cos⁡((2a−1)θ). s_a=2\sin(a\theta),\qquad \lambda=\cos\theta+\cos(2\pi\delta),\qquad \eta_a=\frac{\lambda}{\cos\theta-\cos((2a-1)\theta)}.

The denominator occurs only at a≥i+2a\ge i+2, so no zero row denominator is used. Put

Qr=Ki,j∏a=i+1jsa2,Q0=1,Q1=x+y,Ql=(x+y)Ql−1+(ηi+l−1)xyQl−2. Q_r=\frac{K_{i,j}}{\prod_{a=i+1}^j s_a^2},\qquad Q_0=1,\quad Q_1=x+y,\quad Q_l=(x+y)Q_{l-1}+(\eta_{i+l}-1)xyQ_{l-2}.\tag{1}

For r≥1r\ge1, initialize (X1,Y1)=(x,y)(X_1,Y_1)=(x,y), and set

(XlYl)=Ml(Xl−1Yl−1),Ml=(xηi+lxyy),2≤l≤r. \begin{pmatrix}X_l\\Y_l\end{pmatrix} =M_l\begin{pmatrix}X_{l-1}\\Y_{l-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_l=\begin{pmatrix}x&\eta_{i+l}x\\y&y\end{pmatrix}, \qquad 2\le l\le r.\tag{2}

Then Ql=Xl+YlQ_l=X_l+Y_l exactly: Yl−1=yQl−2Y_{l-1}=yQ_{l-2}, and summing (2) gives (1). Equivalently,

Qr=∑w∈{x,y}rx#x(w)y#y(w)∏l=2rηi+l𝟏{wl−1=y,wl=x}. Q_r=\sum_{w\in\{x,y\}^r}x^{\#x(w)}y^{\#y(w)} \prod_{l=2}^r\eta_{i+l}^{\mathbf1_{\{w_{l-1}=y,\ w_l=x\}}}. \tag{3}

The initial state contains no transition weight. The empty word gives Q0=1Q_0=1. This proof retains every actual row parameter. For real λ≥0\lambda\ge0, all word weights are nonnegative even if ηa−1<0\eta_a-1<0. No claim of positivity is made for the original unused prefix/suffix factors or for negative λ\lambda in the final spectral cap. The energy Cauchy theorem treats that cap separately without this positivity.

2. Uniform angular contraction in the relative outer phase

Suppose every entry of the real matrices (2) lies in [m,M][m,M], with 0<m≤M<∞0<m\le M<\infty. This holds uniformly when x,yx,y range in a compact positive set, δ\delta stays a fixed distance below 1/21/2, and all rows (i+l)/N(i+l)/N stay in a compact subinterval of (0,1/2)(0,1/2).

For phases ϑx,ϑy\vartheta_x,\vartheta_y, the matrix is obtained by multiplying its two rows by their respective phases. In each entry of a two-step product, the two intermediate states contribute positive weights A,B∈[m2,M2]A,B\in[m^2,M^2] and relative phase ϑ=ϑx−ϑy\vartheta=\vartheta_x-\vartheta_y. Therefore

|Aeiϑ+B|2=(A+B)2−4ABsin⁡2(ϑ/2),4AB(A+B)2≥(m/M)4. \begin{aligned} |Ae^{i\vartheta}+B|^2 &=(A+B)^2-4AB\sin^2(\vartheta/2),\\ \frac{4AB}{(A+B)^2}&\ge(m/M)^4. \end{aligned}\tag{4}

Set κ=(m/M)4\kappa=(m/M)^4. Pair consecutive transfer matrices, apply (4) entrywise, and then multiply nonnegative majorants. The unpaired transfer and initial vector obey ordinary triangle inequalities. This gives the exact bound

|Qr(xeiϑx,yeiϑy)|≤exp⁡[−κ2⌊r−12⌋sin⁡2ϑx−ϑy2]Qr(x,y). |Q_r(xe^{i\vartheta_x},ye^{i\vartheta_y})| \le\exp\!\left[-\frac\kappa2 \left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rfloor \sin^2\!\frac{\vartheta_x-\vartheta_y}{2}\right]Q_r(x,y). \tag{5}

Thus a macroscopic interval has a uniform exponential outer-angular gap away from equal phases. Equal phases are an essential exception: Qr(eiϑx,eiϑy)=eirϑQr(x,y)Q_r(e^{i\vartheta}x,e^{i\vartheta}y)=e^{ir\vartheta}Q_r(x,y). There is no independent contraction in both frequencies. If either outer weight is zero, the surviving polynomial is a monomial and the relative angular statement is vacuous. At λ=0\lambda=0 strict matrix positivity also fails; (5) is not extended there by assumption.

3. A compact-interior relative Perron product

Fix a compact positive set of outer weights, a compact real spectral set with δ<1/2\delta<1/2, and constants ϵ>0\epsilon>0 such that

ϵ≤i/N<j/N≤1/2−ϵ,(j−i)/N≥ϵ. \epsilon\le i/N<j/N\le1/2-\epsilon,\qquad (j-i)/N\ge\epsilon. \tag{6}

For all sufficiently large NN, the matrices (2) are uniformly strictly positive, bounded, and vary by O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) per row. Their Perron roots are

ρl=x+y+(x−y)2+4ηi+lxy2. \rho_l=\frac{x+y+\sqrt{(x-y)^2+4\eta_{i+l}xy}}2.\tag{7}

The square root, ρl\rho_l, and the Perron eigenspaces extend analytically to one fixed small complex neighborhood of the compact parameter set. The second eigenvalue may vanish. Nevertheless the discriminant stays nonzero and the modulus of its ratio to ρl\rho_l is bounded by some a<1a<1, also on a sufficiently small complex neighborhood. No division by the second eigenvalue is used.

Choose right and left eigenvectors vl,ulv_l,u_l, normalized by 𝟏Tvl=1\mathbf1^Tv_l=1 and ulTvl=1u_l^Tv_l=1. With z=(x,y)Tz=(x,y)^T, define

Ai,j,N=(𝟏Tvr)(u2Tz)∏l=3r(ulTvl−1). A_{i,j,N}=(\mathbf1^Tv_r)(u_2^Tz) \prod_{l=3}^r(u_l^Tv_{l-1}).\tag{8}

The empty products retain their usual meanings. Uniformly under (6),

Qr=Ai,j,N∏l=2rρl[1+O(N−1)]. Q_r=A_{i,j,N}\prod_{l=2}^r\rho_l\,[1+O(N^{-1})].\tag{9}

Here Ai,j,NA_{i,j,N} is a nonvanishing analytic factor bounded above and below uniformly, on a smaller fixed complex neighborhood. Indeed each ulTvl−1=1+O(N−1)u_l^Tv_{l-1}=1+O(N^{-1}), while the initial and terminal factors are positive units on the real compact set.

For a proof, use the two instantaneous eigenvectors as a moving basis. After dividing each matrix by ρl\rho_l, successive changes of basis have diagonal entries 1+O(N−1)1+O(N^{-1}) and off-diagonal entries O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). The second coordinate contracts by at most a+O(N−1)a+O(N^{-1}). Its initial contribution feeds the first coordinate by a geometric sum of size O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). Thereafter the generated second coordinate is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) of the first, so its feedback is O(N−2)O(N^{-2}) per step, with total O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). The terminal second-coordinate error has the same size. The diagonal main factors are exactly those in (8). These estimates also hold uniformly for the complex neighborhood, and Cauchy gives the corresponding fixed-parameter derivative estimates on a smaller neighborhood.

The original tail is still Ki,j=(∏sa2)QrK_{i,j}=(\prod s_a^2)Q_r. In the limit t=(i+l)/Nt=(i+l)/N, multiplication of (7) by 4sin⁡2(πt)4\sin^2(\pi t) gives exactly

2sin⁡(πt)[(x+y)sin(πt)+(x−y)2sin⁡2(πt)+4xycos⁡2(πδ)], 2\sin(\pi t)\left[(x+y)\sin(\pi t)+ \sqrt{(x-y)^2\sin^2(\pi t)+4xy\cos^2(\pi\delta)}\right],\tag{10}

the original sharp weighted tail root. Thus no new scalar normalization or absolute recurrence has entered.

4. A regular outer-coefficient saddle

In the regime (6), let k/rk/r stay in a compact subinterval of (0,1)(0,1), and set y=1y=1. There is a unique positive tilt x=eℓNx=e^{\ell_N} with

∂ℓlog⁡Qr(eℓ,1)|ℓN=k. \partial_\ell\log Q_r(e^\ell,1)\big|_{\ell_N}=k.\tag{11}

These tilts lie in a fixed compact interval. To see this directly from (3), condition on all states except one. The conditional ratio of an xx state to a yy state equals eℓe^\ell times at most two transition ratios, which are uniformly bounded above and below. Small or large fixed ℓ\ell therefore puts the expected xx fraction uniformly near zero or one. Positivity of every word makes the mean strictly increasing.

At compact tilts the variance VN=∂ℓ2log⁡QrV_N=\partial_\ell^2\log Q_r satisfies cN≤VN≤CNcN\le V_N\le CN. For the lower bound condition on all odd positions: the even states become independent Bernoulli variables with probabilities bounded away from zero and one. Conditional variance and the law of total variance give a constant times rr. For the upper bound and all higher fixed cumulants, (9) provides an analytic logarithm on a uniform complex tilt neighborhood, with derivatives O(N)O(N).

Cauchy coefficient extraction, (5), and the Taylor expansion of that analytic logarithm now give

[xkyr−k]Qr(x,y)=Qr(eℓN,1)e−kℓN2πVN[1+O(N−1)]. [x^ky^{r-k}]Q_r(x,y) =\frac{Q_r(e^{\ell_N},1)e^{-k\ell_N}} {\sqrt{2\pi V_N}}[1+O(N^{-1})].\tag{12}

For precision, outside a fixed small angular interval (5) is exponentially small. Inside it, the variance lower bound and bounded higher derivatives give Gaussian decay. On the shrinking central interval the cubic term is odd and imaginary and integrates to zero; the quartic and squared cubic terms contribute relative O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). All constants are uniform on the stated strict compact sets. Formula (12) is a coefficient theorem for the actual tail, not for a separately chosen positive comparison polynomial.

5. Original factors and the remaining magnetic problem

A complete original one-sided coefficient includes the omitted-half factor, all unused prefix and suffix rows, and ∏sa2\prod s_a^2. They must multiply (9) or (12) exactly. On a strict sign chamber the unused-row signs form the recorded lattice character. The omitted-half factor retains its two oscillatory modes and can vanish densely; it is not a nonzero analytic amplitude that may be divided out on a fixed spectral disk.

These results control the outer relative frequency and give a relative O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) regular coefficient approximation. They do not give an exponentially accurate analytic interpolation in i/N,j/N,k/Ni/N,j/N,k/N. The literal four-state transfer and analytic switch theorem now provide a separate route for the middle frequencies: the regular-window theorem proves exponential loss outside the exact local alignment, retaining both gauge boundary terms and every unused factor. On an inconsistent shared sign system it improves the original Cauchy bound relative to the window envelope. Compatible phase systems, irregular windows and comparison of that envelope with complete volume remain open. Polynomial relative errors or smooth integration by parts alone would not prove that exponential loss.
