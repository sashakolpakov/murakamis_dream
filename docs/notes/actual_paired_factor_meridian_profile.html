The meridian profile near completeness

31 October 2026.

Fix the quantum parameter qN=eπi/Nq_N=e^{\pi i/N}. The reduced ADO invariant of the figure-eight knot is a Laurent polynomial fN(Y)f_N(Y) in the squared meridian eigenvalue Y=M2Y=M^2. Its value at Y=1Y=1 is the reduced Kashaev invariant KNK_N. Here we vary this meridian while keeping the quantum parameter at its exact root of unity. Write Y=ez/NY=e^{z/N}, with zz in a fixed compact subset of the complex plane. We prove

fN(ez/N)KN→H(z)=2sinh⁡(z/2)z. \frac{f_N(e^{z/N})}{K_N}\longrightarrow H(z)=\frac{2\sinh(z/2)}z.

After subtracting the polynomial determined by the ordinary colors and dividing by their common vanishing factor, the centered regular profile ρN\rho_N tends to one. Section 3 gives these operations explicitly. Both statements hold through every fixed ordinary-color resonance and with every fixed derivative. The regularized Laurent polynomial has the same hyperbolic volume rate there. Its first correction is τz2/(8πiN)\tau z^2/(8\pi iN), where τ=23i\tau=2\sqrt3\,i is the cusp shape for the preferred meridian and longitude. We obtain that coefficient from the finite sum and compute the cusp shape separately from the knot group.

The proof starts from the actual finite cut formula. A general estimate for paired sine factors is then passed through its positive complete sum. Section 2 states exactly what would suffice for a signed source; those contraction hypotheses remain to be established for a general Conway main-meridian deformation. The parameter zz is fixed before NN grows. No assertion about a fixed open neighborhood in MM is obtained by this microscopic scaling.

1. Uniform estimates for paired sine products

For N≥2N\ge2, 0≤n<N0\le n<N, and complex zz, put

PN,n(z)=∏r=1n(1+sinh⁡2(z/(2N))sin⁡2(πr/N)),HN(z)=sinh⁡(z/2)Nsinh⁡(z/(2N)). P_{N,n}(z)=\prod_{r=1}^{n} \left(1+\frac{\sinh^2(z/(2N))}{\sin^2(\pi r/N)}\right), \qquad H_N(z)=\frac{\sinh(z/2)}{N\sinh(z/(2N))}. \tag{1}

The latter expression has its entire continuation. For every fixed RR, all PN,nP_{N,n} are bounded on |z|≤R|z|\le R, uniformly in N,nN,n. Indeed |sinh⁡(z/(2N))|≤CR/N|\sinh(z/(2N))|\le C_R/N and sin⁡(πr/N)≥2min⁡(r,N−r)/N\sin(\pi r/N)\ge2\min(r,N-r)/N. Hence the product of absolute values is at most

exp⁡(CR∑r=1N−1min(r,N−r)−2)≤CR′. \exp\left(C_R\sum_{r=1}^{N-1} \min(r,N-r)^{-2}\right)\le C'_R. \tag{2}

For fixed 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/2 and all sufficiently large NN, there are holomorphic units UN,nU_{N,n} on |z|≤R|z|\le R such that

PN,n=HNUN,n,sup|z|≤R|UN,n−1|≤CR,δ/N,δN≤n≤(1−δ)N. P_{N,n}=H_N U_{N,n},\qquad \sup_{|z|\le R}|U_{N,n}-1|\le C_{R,\delta}/N, \quad \delta N\le n\le(1-\delta)N. \tag{3}

This is a factorization across the zeros of HNH_N, rather than pointwise division by a possibly vanishing value.

To prove it, let TNT_N be the monic Chebyshev polynomial determined by TN(v+v−1)=vN+v−NT_N(v+v^{-1})=v^N+v^{-N}. The root factorization of TN(w)−2T_N(w)-2, evaluated at w=2cosh⁡(z/N)w=2\cosh(z/N) and divided by its value after removing the root w=2w=2, gives

PN,N−1(z)=HN(z)2. P_{N,N-1}(z)=H_N(z)^2. \tag{4}

The normalization uses TN′(2)=N2T_N'(2)=N^2. Factors at rr and N−rN-r coincide. Matching values at z=0z=0 therefore proves

N=2m+1:PN,m=HN,N=2m:PN,m−1=HN/cosh⁡(z/(2N)). \begin{array}{ll} N=2m+1:&P_{N,m}=H_N,\\ N=2m:&P_{N,m-1}=H_N/\cosh(z/(2N)). \end{array} \tag{5}

The quotients in (5) are identities of entire functions; on the fixed disk the displayed cosh is also a unit for large NN. Starting at this midpoint, reach any nn in (3) by multiplying or dividing only the intervening factors. Every intervening index lies a fixed positive fraction of NN away from both endpoints. Each factor is consequently 1+OR,δ(N−2)1+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-2}), is a unit on the disk, and there are at most NN of them. Their product or inverse product is 1+OR,δ(N−1)1+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1}). The cosh correction in the even case is 1+OR(N−2)1+O_R(N^{-2}). This proves (3). Repeating the estimate on a larger disk and applying Cauchy’s formula gives the same O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) estimate for each fixed derivative in zz.

Also HN→HH_N\to H locally uniformly, with all fixed derivatives and error O(N−2)O(N^{-2}), where

H(z)=2sinh⁡(z/2)z,H(0)=1. H(z)=\frac{2\sinh(z/2)}{z},\qquad H(0)=1. \tag{6}

This follows from HN=H[z/(2N)]/sinh⁡(z/(2N))H_N=H\,[z/(2N)]/\sinh(z/(2N)).

2. Passing the estimate through a signed sum

Let ΣN\Sigma_N be a finite set of states and let KN=∑σwN,σ≠0K_N=\sum_{\sigma}w_{N,\sigma}\ne0. Suppose the actual deformed source is exactly

FN(z)=∑σ∈ΣNwN,σ∏j=1mPN,nj(σ)(z), F_N(z)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_N}w_{N,\sigma} \prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{N,n_j(\sigma)}(z), \tag{7}

with fixed mm. Call a state interior if δN≤nj(σ)≤(1−δ)N\delta N\le n_j(\sigma)\le(1-\delta)N for every jj. Assume constants C,c>0C,c>0, independent of NN, satisfy

∑σ|wN,σ|≤C|KN|,∑σ not interior|wN,σ|≤Ce−cN|KN|. \sum_\sigma|w_{N,\sigma}|\le C|K_N|, \qquad \sum_{\sigma\text{ not interior}}|w_{N,\sigma}| \le Ce^{-cN}|K_N|. \tag{8}

Then, on every fixed complex disk and with every fixed derivative,

FN(z)KN=HN(z)m+O(N−1)=H(z)m+O(N−1). \frac{F_N(z)}{K_N}=H_N(z)^m+O(N^{-1}) =H(z)^m+O(N^{-1}). \tag{9}

On the interior states, (3) gives a difference from HNmH_N^m bounded by CR/NC_R/N; (8) bounds its contracted absolute error relative to KNK_N. On the other states, (2) bounds both terms uniformly, so their total error is exponentially small by (8). This proves (9) as a whole holomorphic estimate; Cauchy’s formula on larger disks proves the derivative statement.

Positivity is one sufficient reason for the first inequality in (8), but is not assumed in the theorem. The same argument works for an integral over a specified cycle if the sum of absolute weights is replaced by its total variation. It is essential to prove these inequalities for the actual contraction or its exactly equivalent cycle. An estimate on individual insertions is not (8).

More generally, if the first ratio in (8) is κN\kappa_N, the proof gives error OR(κN/N)O_R(\kappa_N/N) plus the normalized absolute tail. Thus κN=o(N)\kappa_N=o(N) is also sufficient for convergence. An unspecified polynomial bound on κN\kappa_N does not suffice for this first-order estimate.

3. The figure-eight knot

The exact figure-eight source identity, equation (1.1), proves directly from the original cut crossings and cups that

fN(Y)=∑n=0N−1∏r=1n(Y+Y−1−QNr−QN−r),QN=e2πi/N. f_N(Y)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\prod_{r=1}^{n} (Y+Y^{-1}-Q_N^r-Q_N^{-r}), \qquad Q_N=e^{2\pi i/N}. \tag{10}

Put Y=ez/NY=e^{z/N}. Its exact decomposition is

fN(ez/N)=∑n=0N−1tN,nPN,n(z),tN,n=∏r=1n4sin⁡2(πr/N)>0,KN=∑n=0N−1tN,n. f_N(e^{z/N})=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}t_{N,n}P_{N,n}(z), \quad t_{N,n}=\prod_{r=1}^{n}4\sin^2(\pi r/N)>0, \quad K_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}t_{N,n}. \tag{11}

The same source note proves, by monotone Riemann sums at the logarithmic endpoints, log⁡tN,n=NΦ(n/N)+O(log⁡N)\log t_{N,n}=N\Phi(n/N)+O(\log N) uniformly, where Φ(x)=∫0xlog⁡(4sin⁡2πu)du\Phi(x)=\int_0^x\log(4\sin^2\pi u)\,du. Its unique maximum is at x=5/6x=5/6. Choose, for example, δ=1/12\delta=1/12. Compactness gives a fixed positive gap between this maximum and the complement of [δ,1−δ][\delta,1-\delta]. A lattice term within O(1/N)O(1/N) of the maximum supplies the lower bound for KNK_N. The polynomial Riemann-sum errors and the at most NN tail terms are absorbed into a smaller exponential gap. This proves (8), with C=1C=1 in its first inequality.

Consequently the actual reduced meridian profile satisfies

gN(z):=fN(ez/N)KN=HN(z)+OR(N−1)=H(z)+OR(N−1). g_N(z):=\frac{f_N(e^{z/N})}{K_N} =H_N(z)+O_R(N^{-1}) =H(z)+O_R(N^{-1}). \tag{12}

The estimate is locally uniform on the whole zz-plane, including all fixed resonances z=2πikz=2\pi i k, and holds for every fixed derivative. It follows from a whole finite source identity, without using a colored-Jones comparison or a chosen solution of a recurrence.

Let PNordP_N^{\mathrm{ord}} be the canonical degree-at-most-N−2N-2 ordinary-color interpolant and RN=(fN−PNord)/SNR_N=(f_N-P_N^{\mathrm{ord}})/S_N, with SN(Y)=1+⋯+YN−1S_N(Y)=1+\cdots+Y^{N-1}. Retain the full-state centering and put

ρN(z)=e(N−1)z/(2N)NRN(ez/N)KN. \rho_N(z)=e^{(N-1)z/(2N)} \frac{N R_N(e^{z/N})}{K_N}. \tag{13}

The exact identity is gN=HNρN+πNg_N=H_N\rho_N+\pi_N, where πN=PNord(ez/N)/KN\pi_N=P_N^{\mathrm{ord}}(e^{z/N})/K_N. The positive volume growth of (11) and the polynomial ordinary-color bound imply πN\pi_N and all its fixed derivatives are exponentially small on every compact disk. For any prescribed disk, choose a larger boundary circle avoiding the zeros of HH. On that circle |HN||H_N| is bounded away from zero. The exact numerator gN−πN−HNg_N-\pi_N-H_N is divisible by HNH_N, so the maximum principle and (12) prove

ρN(z)=1+OR(N−1) \rho_N(z)=1+O_R(N^{-1}) \tag{14}

locally uniformly with all fixed derivatives. This is the canonical regular quotient. The unsubtracted full modified invariant still has actual poles at ordinary-color resonances; (14) does not remove them without their specified subtraction.

In particular, for every fixed compact set of zz-values, this regularized Laurent polynomial has the uniform volume limit

2πNlog⁡|RN(ez/N)|→Vol⁡(S3\41). \frac{2\pi}{N}\log\left|R_N(e^{z/N})\right| \longrightarrow\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus4_1). \tag{14a}

Indeed (14) is bounded away from zero on that compact set for large NN, and (13) gives RN(ez/N)=KNe−(N−1)z/(2N)[1+OR(N−1)]/NR_N(e^{z/N})=K_N e^{-(N-1)z/(2N)}[1+O_R(N^{-1})]/N. The centering and the factor N−1N^{-1} do not change the exponential rate. The source note identifies the complete rate with the volume of the actual figure-eight complement. Thus (14a) uses that same complement, and includes the ordinary-color resonance values of the regular quotient.

This is a microscopic meridian theorem for the actual figure-eight source, used as a test of (8). The broad strict Conway volume theorem does not yet supply (7)–(8) for its main-meridian deformation. Its auxiliary color estimates leave the main meridian parabolic.

The first correction

The exact midpoint product also determines the first nonconstant regular term. Uniformly on the same bulk interval and compact zz-sets,

N(UN,n(z)−1)=−z24πcot⁡(πn/N)+OR,δ(N−1). N\bigl(U_{N,n}(z)-1\bigr) =-\frac{z^2}{4\pi}\cot(\pi n/N)+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1}). \tag{14b}

Indeed, for every intervening midpoint-to-nn factor,

log⁡(1+sinh⁡2(z/(2N))sin⁡2(πr/N))=z24N2csc⁡2(πr/N)+OR,δ(N−4). \log\left(1+\frac{\sinh^2(z/(2N))}{\sin^2(\pi r/N)}\right) =\frac{z^2}{4N^2}\csc^2(\pi r/N)+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-4}).

The signed sum of these logarithms is a Riemann sum from 1/21/2 to n/Nn/N; its integral is −cot⁡(πn/N)/π-\cot(\pi n/N)/\pi. The midpoint offset and the even cosh correction contribute O(N−2)O(N^{-2}) before multiplying by NN. Exponentiating gives (14b), as a whole holomorphic estimate.

The positive probability weights tN,n/KNt_{N,n}/K_N concentrate at 5/65/6. On the bulk interval the cotangent is bounded, so its weighted mean tends to −3-\sqrt3. The exponentially small tails remain negligible after multiplication by NN. Equations (11) and (14b) therefore give N(gN−HN)−HN3z2/(4π)→0N(g_N-H_N)-H_N\sqrt3 z^2/(4\pi)\to0 locally uniformly. Subtract NπNN\pi_N and divide on the same fixed boundary circles as above. The quotient is entire before applying the maximum principle. Hence

N(ρN(z)−1)→34πz2 N\bigl(\rho_N(z)-1\bigr) \longrightarrow \frac{\sqrt3}{4\pi}z^2 \tag{14c}

locally uniformly with every fixed derivative. Since ρN\rho_N is close to one on each fixed disk, its logarithm there satisfies the same first-order limit. In particular N(log⁡ρN)″(0)→3/(2π)N(\log\rho_N)''(0)\to\sqrt3/(2\pi).

Why the coefficient is the cusp shape

We compute the peripheral curves with their orientations, since the sign of the coefficient cannot be read from an unmarked polynomial. Use the braid (σ1σ2−1)2(\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})^2 for the figure-eight knot and the meridian matrices

A=(M10M−1),B=(M0tM−1),C=A−1BAB−1A. A=\begin{pmatrix}M&1\\0&M^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\t&M^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=A^{-1}BAB^{-1}A.

The braid closure gives t2+(M2+M−2−3)t+3−M2−M−2=0t^2+(M^2+M^{-2}-3)t+3-M^2-M^{-2}=0. Put D=A−1BAD=A^{-1}BA and E=CAC−1E=CAC^{-1}. The successive meridian triples under the four crossings are

(A,B,C),(D,A,C),(D,C,E),(D−1CD,D,E),(D−1CD,E,EDE−1). (A,B,C),\quad(D,A,C),\quad(D,C,E),\quad (D^{-1}CD,D,E),\quad(D^{-1}CD,E,EDE^{-1}).

The closure relation reduces the final triple to (A,B,C)(A,B,C). Track each original strand through the crossings: its accumulated underpass transport is respectively CC, BA−1BA^{-1}, and D−1D^{-1}. Following the closed knot visits these strands in that order, so the transport is W=D−1BA−1CW=D^{-1}BA^{-1}C. The writhe is zero. With the preferred-longitude convention of the peripheral calculation, the longitude is λ=W−1=C−1AB−1A−1BA\lambda=W^{-1}=C^{-1}AB^{-1}A^{-1}BA.

At M=1M=1, direct multiplication using t2−t+1=0t^2-t+1=0 gives

A=(1101),λ=(−14t−20−1). A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \lambda=\begin{pmatrix}-1&4t-2\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

The meridian translates the boundary plane by one. The projective action of the longitude translates it by 2−4t2-4t. The positive Bloch specialization selects t=(1−3i)/2t=(1-\sqrt3\,i)/2, paired with the ideal tetrahedron shape (1+3i)/2(1+\sqrt3\,i)/2 in the upper half-plane. Thus the oriented cusp shape is τ=2−4t=23i\tau=2-4t=2\sqrt3\,i.

The preferred longitude lift still has eigenvalue −1-1 at completeness. To check its variation without dropping that sign, the upper-left entry of the same matrix word is

Lintr(M)=M4−2M2−1+M−2+(M2−M−2)t(M). L_{\mathrm{intr}}(M) =M^4-2M^2-1+M^{-2}+(M^2-M^{-2})t(M).

It satisfies Lintr(1)=−1L_{\mathrm{intr}}(1)=-1 and dlogLintr/dlogM|M=1=2−4t=τ\left.d\log L_{\mathrm{intr}}/d\log M\right|_{M=1}=2-4t=\tau. Centering the longitude as ℓ=−Lintr\ell=-L_{\mathrm{intr}} changes neither this derivative nor the recorded sign of its lift. In the squared coordinates U=2log⁡MU=2\log M, V=2log⁡ℓV=2\log\ell, the ratio dV/dUdV/dU is again τ\tau.

Consequently the coefficient proved from the finite sum is

N(ρN(z)−1)→τ8πiz2,NρN″(0)→τ4πi. N(\rho_N(z)-1)\longrightarrow\frac{\tau}{8\pi i}z^2,\qquad N\rho_N''(0)\longrightarrow\frac{\tau}{4\pi i}.

Here M=ez/(2N)M=e^{z/(2N)}. Both this scale and the primitive peripheral curves are needed for the factor 8πi8\pi i. The calculation proves the match for this knot; it does not infer an analogous asymptotic for other knots merely from their peripheral equations.

4. Individual shifted factorials

There is a useful more general local calculation. For δN≤n≤(1−δ)N\delta N\le n\le(1-\delta)N, let

AN,n(s)=∏r=1nsin⁡(π(r+s)/N)sin⁡(πr/N). A_{N,n}(s)=\prod_{r=1}^{n} \frac{\sin(\pi(r+s)/N)}{\sin(\pi r/N)}. \tag{15}

Uniformly for ss in any fixed compact complex set,

AN,n(s)=[Nsin⁡(πn/N)/π]sΓ(1+s)VN,n(s),VN,n=1+O(N−1). A_{N,n}(s)= \frac{[N\sin(\pi n/N)/\pi]^s}{\Gamma(1+s)} V_{N,n}(s), \qquad V_{N,n}=1+O(N^{-1}). \tag{16}

Here the positive real base fixes its logarithm. The factor VV is a holomorphic unit, including at negative integers where the reciprocal Gamma factor vanishes, for all sufficiently large NN. Each fixed derivative of V−1V-1 has the same bound.

For a direct proof, separate each sine into its linear factor and sinc\operatorname{sinc}. Euler’s product for Gamma, with its tail estimated by ∑r>nOR(r−2)\sum_{r>n}O_R(r^{-2}), gives

∏r=1nr+sr=nsΓ(1+s)[1+OR(n−1)]. \prod_{r=1}^{n}\frac{r+s}{r} =\frac{n^s}{\Gamma(1+s)}[1+O_R(n^{-1})]. \tag{17}

One first removes the finitely many factors whose zeros lie in the chosen compact set. The remaining logarithms have no zero there, so (17) is a holomorphic-unit estimate through those zeros as well. The harmonic-sum correction Hn−log⁡n−γ=O(1/n)H_n-\log n-\gamma=O(1/n) and the convergent logarithmic tail justify the bound without assuming the ratio is nonzero at a Gamma pole.

Now b(t)=log⁡[sin⁡(πt)/(πt)]b(t)=\log[\sin(\pi t)/(\pi t)] is holomorphic on a fixed complex neighborhood of [0,1−δ][0,1-\delta], with b(0)=0b(0)=0. Taylor expansion and a Riemann-sum estimate yield

∑r=1n[b((r+s)/N)−b(r/N)]=sb(n/N)+OR,δ(N−1). \sum_{r=1}^{n}\bigl[b((r+s)/N)-b(r/N)\bigr] =s\,b(n/N)+O_{R,\delta}(N^{-1}). \tag{18}

Exponentiating (18) and combining with (17) proves (16). It explains the paired result: set s=z/(2πi)s=z/(2\pi i) and multiply the shifts s,−ss,-s. Their powers of Nsin⁡(πn/N)/πN\sin(\pi n/N)/\pi cancel, and 1/[Γ(1+s)Γ(1−s)]=sin⁡(πs)/(πs)=H(z)1/[\Gamma(1+s)\Gamma(1-s)]=\sin(\pi s)/(\pi s)=H(z). The exact midpoint argument additionally retains HNH_N and its finite-level resonance zeros.

For a finite product of shifted factors with shifts ajsa_j s and integer powers εj\varepsilon_j, (16) shows explicitly that the power of NN is s∑jεjajs\sum_j\varepsilon_j a_j. It cancels when that signed sum is zero. The remaining leading multiplier is

∏jΓ(1+ajs)−εjexp⁡(s∑jεjajlog[sin(πxj)/π]),xj=nj/N. \prod_j\Gamma(1+a_js)^{-\varepsilon_j} \exp\left(s\sum_j\varepsilon_j a_j \log[\sin(\pi x_j)/\pi]\right), \quad x_j=n_j/N. \tag{19}

When negative powers occur, this statement is made first on compact sets avoiding the denominator zeros; any continuation across them must be justified in the original whole source. Formula (19) does not by itself identify its exponential with a longitude or its Gamma factor with the single-knot factor HH. Those are explicit source and geometric matching obligations. Likewise, the local factorial estimate does not supply the contraction estimate (8).

Relation to earlier asymptotic questions

Colored Alexander asymptotics for the figure-eight knot were already studied by Cho and Jun Murakami in Some limits of the colored Alexander invariant of the figure-eight knot and the volume of hyperbolic orbifolds. The statement proved here fixes qNq_N at its exact root and varies the main color by a bounded complex offset, then retains the canonical ordinary-color subtraction and every fixed derivative. The available bibliographic check did not settle whether this precise normalized profile appears in that earlier work. No priority claim is made. The proof here is the finite-product and concentration argument given above.
