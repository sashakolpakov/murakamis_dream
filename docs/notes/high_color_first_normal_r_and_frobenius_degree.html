The actual first normal high-color crossing has Frobenius degree at most two

Research proof and exact audit, 27 October 2026. The first δ=1−QN\delta=1-Q^N coefficient of the actual integral high-color crossing is a quadratic operator on the classical Frobenius multiplicity variables, with arbitrary finite head coefficients. The first resonant tail has a universal exact factorization. Both statements retain the full Cartan and mixed block signs.

This is an actual first-normal crossing theorem. It does not identify whole low/high color jets or construct the full two-current Gaussian source map.

The normal variable differentiates the original generic integral matrix family with its ordinary pivotal data. A frozen root ADO pivot is a different off-root source and requires its explicit route adapter; no scalarity of that frozen family is assumed.

1. Actual matrices and the filtration being used

Use the high integral color, with dimension N(r+1)N(r+1), m=N(r+1)−1m=N(r+1)-1, and the constant sign basis wa,sw_{a,s}, 0≤a≤r0\le a\le r, 0≤s<N0\le s<N. At the root the ordinary head matrices are independent of a. The divided matrices are $$ \mathsf E=[E^N/[N]_q!]_{q_N}=\tau e_{\rm cl},\qquad \mathsf F=[F^N/[N]_q!]_{q_N}=f_{\rm cl}, \qquad \tau=(-1)^{Nr-1}. $$ The root block number is $a=(r-h_{\rm cl})/2$, so multiplication by a is a degree-one classical enveloping operator.

Give $e_{\rm cl},f_{\rm cl},h_{\rm cl}$ PBW degree one. On two strands use total degree for U(𝔰𝔩2⊕𝔰𝔩2)U(\mathfrak{sl}_2\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2). Head matrix coefficients have degree zero. The full root crossing also has its literal block flip and parity εa+b\varepsilon^{a+b}, ε=(−1)m\varepsilon=(-1)^m; these are retained separately, not asserted to be bounded-degree polynomials in a,b.

Write R(δ)=R0+δR1+O(δ2)R(\delta)=R_0+\delta R_1+O(\delta^2). Then R0−1R1∈U≤2(𝔰𝔩2⊕𝔰𝔩2)⊗End⁡(heads). R_0^{-1}R_1\in U_{\le2}(\mathfrak{sl}_2\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2) \otimes\operatorname{End}(\text{heads}). \tag{1} This assertion is uniform in N and r. Its scalar coefficients may depend on N,r and the marked root. It is not a uniform numerical norm bound on those coefficients.

2. Transfer orders and the universal resonant factor

The full positive un-checked R is qH⊗H/2∑k≥0qk(k−1)/2ρk[k]q!E(k)⊗F(k),E(k)=Ek/[k]q!,ρ=q−q−1. q^{H\otimes H/2} \sum_{k\ge0}q^{k(k-1)/2}\rho^k[k]_q!\, E^{(k)}\otimes F^{(k)}, \qquad E^{(k)}=E^k/[k]_q!,\quad \rho=q-q^{-1}. \tag{2} Each divided matrix has regular integral Gaussian entries. The factor [k]q![k]_q! has δ\delta-order ⌊k/N⌋\lfloor k/N\rfloor, since every positive multiple of N contributes one simple zero. Hence all k≥2Nk\ge2N transfers vanish modulo δ2\delta^2.

For k=N+bk=N+b, 0≤b<N0\le b<N, divided-power multiplication gives DN+b=qN(N−1)/2+NbρN[N]q![N+bb]qDb(E(N)⊗F(N)), D_{N+b}= \frac{q^{N(N-1)/2+Nb}\rho^N[N]_q!} {{N+b\brack b}_q}\, D_b\,(E^{(N)}\otimes F^{(N)}), \tag{3} where DkD_k denotes the k-th term of the nilpotent sum in (2). This is an exact generic identity before taking a normal coefficient.

At the root, [N+bb]qN=(−1)b,qNNb=(−1)b, {N+b\brack b}_{q_N}=(-1)^b,\qquad q_N^{Nb}=(-1)^b, so these factors cancel. Also [qN(N−1)/2ρN[N]q!/δ]qN=N(−1)N−1=:κN. \left[ q^{N(N-1)/2}\rho^N[N]_q!/\delta \right]_{q_N} =N(-1)^{N-1}=:\kappa_N . \tag{4} Indeed ρN[N]q!=(−1)Nq−N(N+1)/2(Q;Q)N\rho^N[N]_q!= (-1)^Nq^{-N(N+1)/2}(Q;Q)_N, and (Q;Q)N−1=N(Q;Q)_{N-1}=N. Therefore the entire first resonant tail is [R≥N/δ]qN=κNR0(𝖤⊗𝖥). [R_{\ge N}/\delta]_{q_N} =\kappa_N R_0(\mathsf E\otimes\mathsf F). \tag{5} Equation (5) includes checked crossings as written: the divided operator is on the INPUT, to the right. The same full root R, including the mixed parity and original common half-Cartan line, must be used. Moving an isolated factor through it without its sign would change this statement.

The Frobenius degree of (5) is two.

3. Nonresonant head terms have degree at most one

Fix k<Nk<N. In EkE^k or FkF^k at most one head boundary can be crossed. If neither side crosses, every factor is [AN+s]q[AN+s]_q, s≢0mod⁡Ns\not\equiv0\bmod N. With δ=1−q2N\delta=1-q^{2N}, its first derivative at the root is, apart from the root sign (−1)A(-1)^A, affine in A: ∂δ[AN+s]q|0=−(−1)A2N[(AN+s)(qNs+qN−s)ρN−[s]qNqN+qN−1ρN]. \left.\partial_\delta[AN+s]_q\right|_0 =-\frac{(-1)^A}{2N} \left[ \frac{(AN+s)(q_N^s+q_N^{-s})}{\rho_N} -[s]_{q_N}\frac{q_N+q_N^{-1}}{\rho_N} \right]. \tag{6} The constant beta gauge cancels every block-dependent root sign in the ordinary head coefficient. Differentiating a product once therefore gives a degree-one polynomial in the two block numbers.

If E crosses one boundary, its one vanishing integer has first normal value proportional to r−a+1r-a+1. The other head factors are nonzero. In the beta gauge its remaining sign is independent of a: for input head s<k the total sign is (−1)kr+1+s(-1)^{kr+1+s}. Thus this contribution is an actual $e_{\rm cl}$ factor with a head coefficient. The F boundary likewise gives b+1b+1, hence $f_{\rm cl}$, with no remaining block-dependent sign. If both sides cross, their two zeros give order δ2\delta^2, so nothing survives in the first normal.

The remaining factors ρk\rho^k, qk(k−1)/2q^{k(k-1)/2}, and [k]q!−1[k]_q!^{-1} are regular head-dependent scalars for k<Nk<N. They do not raise the Frobenius degree. Hence the entire nonresonant nilpotent first-normal coefficient has degree at most one after the retained root parity is factored.

4. The Cartan part raises the bound to two

The unrolled weight is Ha,s=m−2s−2NaH_{a,s}=m-2s-2Na. Since q=qN(1−δ)1/(2N)q=q_N(1-\delta)^{1/(2N)}, ∂δqH1H2/2|0=−H1H24NqNH1H2/2. \left.\partial_\delta q^{H_1H_2/2}\right|_0 =-\frac{H_1H_2}{4N} q_N^{H_1H_2/2}. \tag{7} The multiplier is quadratic in a,b. Its product with a first-normal boundary or resonant term is second order and does not enter R_1. Equations (5)–(7) prove that, after factoring the root signed block swap, every head matrix coefficient of R_1 has total Frobenius degree at most two.

The root crossing is a signed block flip tensored with the original head crossing. Conjugation by it preserves the PBW degree: the flip permutes the two Lie algebras, parity changes the signs of e,f and leaves h fixed, and the head factor only changes head coefficients. This proves the interaction form (1).

For a negative crossing, differentiation of the exact inverse gives (R−1)1=−R0−1R1R0−1. (R^{-1})_1=-R_0^{-1}R_1R_0^{-1}. The same root conjugation argument proves the identical degree bound. The root half-Cartan and zero-framing factors are never replaced by their ideal numerical values.

A coherent realization of the classical multiplicity variables turns (1) into a differential operator of order at most two, with its one-sided source/dual connection terms retained. It does not make the entire divided Hopf action primitive or remove its mixed coproduct terms.

5. The same bound holds for every braid word and its knot closure

This local filtration is stable under arbitrary word composition. Let W(delta) be any fixed braid word on b high-colored strands. The derivative of a product, written in its interaction frame, is a sum of conjugates of the local interactions R_0^(-1)R_1. Every root factor is its head crossing times a signed permutation of the Frobenius factors. These permutations exchange generators and their parity factors change only the signs of e,f. Therefore

W_0^(-1)W_1 lies in
   U_(<=2)(sl2^b) tensor End(all heads).             (8)

There is no crossing-count or bridge-count restriction on (8). Its coefficients and number of terms may depend on the word; the enveloping DEGREE stays at most two. A root pivot has no block dependence, and its first normal coefficient is affine in the unrolled weight, hence of degree at most one. The original zero-framing twist is a scalar on each generic integral color and introduces no extra Frobenius degree.

Now close all but one strand of a braid whose closure is one knot. For the scalar Ward corollary below, use the ACTUAL generic ordinary integral ribbon family, with pivot K(q) and its matching zero-framing twist. Its reduced scalar is C_n(q). The root comparison with the ADO pivot was proved with its explicit signs; it is not an equality of those pivotal families off the root. The root Frobenius permutation has one cycle. In its partial trace, the Frobenius factors of any inserted monomial are multiplied in their order along that component. Degrees add to at most two. Transporting them past parity operators changes only their recorded signs. The total parity around the uninserted component is even, as in the high-color closure proof. Consequently the first normal long-knot matrix has

C_1 in End(head) tensor U_(<=2)(sl2),                (9)

with the actual pivots, twist and ordered insertions retained. This is a statement about the full matrix before choosing a new source or identifying an internal geometric frame.

There are exact resulting Ward identities. The generic actual integral-color long-knot operator is C_n(q) times identity, so its first normal coefficient (9) is also scalar. For r>=2 the image of U_(<=2)(sl2) under the adjoint classical action is the sum of spins zero, one and two, of dimensions 1,3,5. This follows directly by the highest vectors 1,e,e^2 and repeated commutation with f; their distinct h-weights and the nonzero raising strings make the three summands independent. The quadratic Casimir only changes the scalar summand in the given representation. Thus all eight nonscalar spin-one and spin-two coefficients of (9) vanish as head matrices. Its scalar head coefficient is the actual derivative of C_n(q) times the head identity.

These are finite exact source identities for an arbitrary knot, and their coherent differential realization is given by the actual two-flag Ward dictionary. They do not by themselves identify the relevant geometric current or prove that a selected geometric period survives.

If one instead freezes the auxiliary ADO pivot to K(q)^(1-N) off the root, the matrix-degree bound (9) still holds: its normal pivot contribution is still degree one. Its generic long matrix need not be scalar, however. The eight vanishing coefficients then require the exact pivotal/normal source adapter and do not follow from root-only pivotal equality. This qualification is essential to the final Ward corollary, not to (1), (5), or (8).

6. Independent exact tests

The audit assembles the ACTUAL integral R entries over a finite field and its dual numbers, using polynomial Gaussian recursion before specialization. A primitive fourth-level root retains the full half-Cartan phase. Its dual parameter is exactly q=qN(1−δ/(2N))q=q_N(1-\delta/(2N)).

At (N,r)=(2,3),(3,3),(4,4),(5,3)(N,r)=(2,3),(3,3),(4,4),(5,3), it checks:

  • all transfers k≥2Nk\ge2N vanish to first order;
  • 3112 resonant coefficient identities (5), including boundaries;
  • all 174 nonzero head blocks of the parity-stripped first normal lie in the exact PBW-degree-two span;
  • some blocks do not lie in degree one, so the quadratic bound is genuinely needed in these examples.

The PBW span is computed independently from the classical e,f,h matrices, with modular pivot rows and exact reconstruction; no proposed coefficient decomposition is supplied to the test. Results record the ranks and level data. These checks support the uniform proof; they are not a selected-period or global-current theorem.

The actual-source audit checks the high integral matrix, divided source and Ward dictionary used here. The memory-clock proof then applies this crossing bound with its exact first-normal carry factors and independently checked inverse crossing.
