The geometric contribution and all Fourier charges

For a fixed link diagram, the exact finite formula is a coefficient in face variables followed by a linear functional in each crossing variable. Its local polynomial splits into three specified pieces. The central piece, called CROSS in the source formulas, contains paths passing through the middle of the finite recurrence. The other two pieces contain paths restricted to one side. Selecting CROSS at every crossing region gives a contribution to the original invariant; it does not define another topological invariant.

This note proves a nonzero asymptotic for that entire geometric contribution, including all Fourier charges, both level parities, the derivatives at repeated spectral nodes, and the original scalar factors. The diagram may have cups and caps between crossing regions: it need not be a flat plat. Its white triangulation must be a simple sphere with the specified matching and marked strand edge. The local balance inequalities used to bound the other two pieces are not needed here.

The geometric companion constructs the actual complement and identifies the selected action with its hyperbolic volume after sufficiently long fixed signed fillings. A strict estimate for the full contribution minus this geometric term remains a separate requirement for a full-invariant volume theorem.

2. The graph facts replacing a flat-plat assumption

Deleting u,vu,v from a simple triangulated sphere leaves a connected ordinary graph. Indeed reroute a path around the first deleted vertex along its cyclic link, then around the second along its link with the first vertex removed. That remaining link is connected. Every surviving edge belongs to an original shaded triangle with two positive finite-face variables.

No matching edge can have the protected pair as its endpoints: their unique sphere edge is the marked MAIN edge. Consequently every crossing circle has x+y>0x+y>0. At least one of its two triangles is nonmonomial. If both outers are positive, xy>0xy>0; if one outer is protected, at most one middle can be the other protected face, so the other middle is positive. These facts also cover the smallest simple sphere. They require neither complete-radius balance nor a tree in the ordinary graph.

At the actual complete packing, the circle-center angle sums make the common face action stationary. Its Hessian is positive on the common-scale quotient: each nonmonomial triangle detects the differences of its finite logarithmic face radii, and the connected ordinary graph leaves only common scaling in the kernel. The active rows are literal +1,−1+1,-1 differences. Thus the quotient lattice is primitive, has dimension R−1R-1, and has no additional angular aliases.

This is the content needed from The positive face coefficient has one global saddle, §§2–4, and The original boundary source has exactly two flat face directions, §2. Their proofs use the actual triangle graph and finite determinant matrices. Morse bends introduce no new face coordinate, while the exact source identity has already dealt with their cup/cap and mark factors. The old global theorem’s phrase “flat plat” is therefore removed at this specific interface, rather than by an unproved equivalence of presentations.
