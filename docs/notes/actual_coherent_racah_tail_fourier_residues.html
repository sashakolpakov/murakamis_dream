A finite Fourier identity for coherent Racah normal tails

Later result, 29 October 2026: the all-partner first-normal theorem now bounds every actual coherent tail, including the symmetric component left undetermined by the involution below. It uses a positive presentation of the full generic source in addition to these Fourier identities. Higher general normal orders remain open.

29 October 2026. This is an own finite algebraic identity for the actual retained [2N] current. It gives a uniform logarithmic relative bound when the largest face sum occurs at least twice, including genuinely nonempty high-partner tails. For arbitrary partner labels the identity retains a second alternating sum. No general bound on that residual, higher normal jets, or completed physical transport is asserted.

1. The actual tail and its positive core

Use the symmetric quantum-factorial convention and the exact generic sum of the supported normal theorem. Put D=N-1, theta=pi/N, q=exp(i theta), Q=q^2, and

s_r=product_(u=1)^r 2 sin(u theta),  s_0=1.

For any partner of a supported folded sextuple let F_i be its four face sums and Q_k its three opposite sums. Assume the tail is live: min Q_k>2D. Define

a_i=2D-F_i,  b_k=Q_k-2D,  c_k=D-b_k=3D-Q_k.

The exact face-deficit inequalities give

0<=a_i<=c_k<=D-1 for EVERY i,k,
sum_k c_k-sum_i a_i=D.                       (1)

Indeed c_k-a_i=D-(Q_k-F_i)>=0; the equality follows from sum F_i=sum Q_k. Order the four a_i as

A=a_1<=B=a_2<=a_3<=a_4.

The true positive core, indexed by z=2D-v, is

C=sum_(v=0)^A C_v,
C_v=(1/N) product_k s_(c_k-v)
                 /[s_v product_i s_(a_i-v)] >0.              (2)

This is the same C(q) appearing in the ACTUAL generic identity

(-1)^D S/[N]=C(zeta)+[2N] T(zeta).             (3)

The true root current is

T(q)=-2 sin(theta) N^2 sum_(k=0)^(D-c_max-1)
   (-1)^k s_k /
   [product_i s_(a_i+1+k) product_j s_(D-c_j-1-k)].            (4)

All displayed factorial arguments are between zero and D. No invalid factorial has been assigned a value or silently removed.

2. Exact coherent Fourier reduction

Write

H_r=sum_(u=1)^r cot(u theta), H_0=0,
J_v=H_v+sum_k H_(c_k-v)-sum_i H_(a_i-v).

Then the following identity is exact at every level, of either parity:

T(q)=-sin(theta) sum_(v=0)^A C_v J_v
      +(2 sin(theta)/N) sum_(v=A+1)^B (-1)^(A+v) E_v,          (5)

where

E_v=s_(v-A-1) product_k s_(c_k-v)
                /[s_v product_(i=2)^4 s_(a_i-v)] >0.         (6)

An empty last sum is zero. In particular it is absent when A=B, equivalently when the largest face sum occurs at least twice.

The second sum is essential. For N=5 and partner labels (a,b,c,d,e,f)=(2,2,3,3,2,2), one has the ordered arrays a_i=(0,1,1,2), c_k=(2,3,3). The first sum in (5) is zero, whereas the retained residual gives T(q)=-1. Dropping it would incorrectly assert zero current.

3. Own finite algebraic proof

This proof only uses polynomial division, finite sums of roots of unity, and the elementary sine-factorial reflection s_r s_(D-r)=N.

Let

R(x)=product_k product_(u=1)^(c_k)(1-Q^u x)
                  / product_i product_(u=1)^(a_i)(1-Q^u x),
f(x)=x R(x)/(1-x),
K=sum_k c_k(c_k+1)/2-sum_i a_i(a_i+1)/2,
beta=(2 sin(theta)/N) i^(N-2) q^(-K).

In (4) put j=k+1 and use the sine-factorial reflection on its three descending denominators. Converting every remaining sine by 2 sin(u theta)=i q^(-u)(1-Q^u) gives exactly

T(q)=beta sum_(j=1)^(D-c_max) f(Q^j).          (7)

The phase is important: the alternating sign and the degree difference in (1) leave q^(2j)=Q^j, the numerator x in f.

After cancellation, f has SIMPLE poles precisely at

r_v=Q^(-v),  0<=v<=A.

For 1<=u<=A the numerator has three factors 1-Q^u x and the denominator has four. Above A there are no poles because every c_k is at least every a_i. Its polynomial part has degree at most D=N-1: the numerator degree minus denominator degree is D.

Let

B_v=[(1-x/r_v) f(x)]_(x=r_v),
L_v(x)=(1-x/r_v) f(x).

Ordinary partial fractions give a polynomial P of degree at most N-1 with

f(x)=P(x)+sum_v B_v/(1-x/r_v).

Since f(0)=0, one has P(0)=-sum_v B_v. Sum this identity over all Nth roots except its pole set. For a nonconstant monomial of degree less than N the full root sum is zero, and

sum_(x^N=1,x!=r_v) 1/(1-x/r_v)=(N-1)/2.

The finite regular parts at the other omitted roots cancel the corresponding partial-fraction corrections. Thus

sum_(x^N=1,x not a pole) f(x)
   =sum_(v=0)^A [r_v L_v'(r_v)-(N+1)B_v/2].                 (8)

For example, the finite regular part of f at r_v is -r_v L_v'(r_v). This also checks the constant (N+1)/2 in (8).

Direct conversion of the unit factors gives, for EVERY v,

B_v=i^(-D) q^K N C_v,
r_v L_v'(r_v)/B_v=(N+1)/2-(i/2)J_v.         (9)

Here is an explicit verification rather than an assumed residue sign. For v>=1, set (Q;Q)_r=product_(u=1)^r(1-Q^u). Cancelling the three numerator and four denominator zero factors gives

B_v=(-1)^v Q^[v(v-1)/2]
       product_k (Q;Q)_(c_k-v)
          /[(Q;Q)_v product_i (Q;Q)_(a_i-v)].

Converting these factorials to sines leaves the phase i^(-D) q^(K-vN)(-1)^v=i^(-D)q^K, proving the first part of (9). For v=0 it is immediate from R(1). The logarithmic derivative of a unit factor is

-Q^w/(1-Q^w)=1/2-(i/2)cot(w theta).

After removing the pole, the signed number of unit factors is N-1, and the leading factor x contributes one. The real part is therefore (N+1)/2. The negative indices 1-v,...,-1 and the factor 1-x contribute H_v, while the remaining positive indices contribute the other seven sums in J_v. This proves the second part of (9). Since beta B_v=-2i sin(theta) C_v, (8) contributes exactly the first term of (5).

It remains to compare (8)’s root set with (7)’s ACTUAL finite range. For roots x=Q^(-v) outside the range in (7), cancellation shows:

  • 0<=v<=A: these are precisely the omitted poles;
  • A<v<=B: the regular value can be nonzero;
  • B<v<=c_max: the regular value is zero.

The last assertion follows by counting factors: above B, at most two denominator factors remain while at least three numerator factors remain up to a_4; beyond a_4, no denominator factors remain, and at least one numerator factor remains up to c_max. For the middle range the exact unit-factor conversion gives

beta f(Q^(-v))=(2 sin(theta)/N)(-1)^(A+v+1) E_v.              (10)

One can verify (10) before conversion using

f(Q^(-v))=(-1)^(A+1) Q^[vA-A(A+1)/2]
   (Q;Q)_(v-A-1) product_k (Q;Q)_(c_k-v)
    /[(Q;Q)_v product_(i=2)^4 (Q;Q)_(a_i-v)].

Subtracting these extra regular nodes from (8) proves (5), including its residual sign. This is a finite proof for all arrays satisfying (1), not an inference from finitely many knots or levels.

4. A proved nonempty-tail first-normal bound

Suppose A=B. Since H_r=H_(D-r) and positive cotangents obey cot(u theta)<=1/(u theta) for u<N/2,

0<=H_r<=(N/pi)(1+log N),  0<=r<=D.

Equations (2),(5) therefore give the uniform coherent bound

|T(q)| <= 8(1+log N) C(q).                  (11)

Unlike an estimate of the absolute summands in (4), this uses the actual cancellations. The factor 8 is deliberately nonsharp. Together with the existing positive-core jet estimate and the exact normal derivative

dot G(q)=dot C(q)+4N/(q-q^-1) T(q),
G=(-1)^D S/[N],  dot=D_(log zeta),

it proves, with an absolute constant C_*,

|dot G(q)| <= C_* N^2(1+log N) C(q).         (12)

Indeed the sharper form before sin(theta)<=pi/N is applied in (11) cancels the denominator in 4N/(q-q^-1) directly. The analytic face factors have the same unit-factor logarithmic bound. Hence for this entire repeated-maximal-face class, the ACTUAL normalized squared scalar and its local positive square root satisfy

K(q)=W_N^2,
|dot K(q)| <= C_* N^2(1+log N) W_N^2,
H(q)=W_N,
|dot H(q)| <= C_* N^2(1+log N) W_N.           (13)

Their first inverse jets have the corresponding reciprocal bounds. This conclusion includes live [2N] currents; it does not extend to higher generic normal orders merely by differentiating (5), which is an identity on the fixed cyclotomic fiber.

5. The former all-high diagnostic is now a theorem

Take N=3m+1 and all six high spins equal to 2m. Then a_i=0, c_k=m, A=B=0, and C=s_m^3/N. Equation (5) proves EXACTLY

T(q)/C(q)=-3 sin(theta) sum_(u=1)^m cot(u theta).              (14)

More generally, every live partner array with all four faces equal 2D has a_i=0; (14) becomes

T(q)/C(q)=-sin(theta) sum_k sum_(u=1)^(c_k) cot(u theta).

Thus the finite Fourier proof is not confined to the equilateral sequence. The former numerical observations remain archived as their historical record; (14) now follows from the own uniform identity.

6. The residual is an exact partner involution

For a unique minimum A<B, define new arrays

a*=(A,D-c_1+A,D-c_2+A,D-c_3+A),
c*=(D-a_2+A,D-a_3+A,D-a_4+A).                (15)

They again satisfy (1), have the SAME unique minimum A, and applying (15) twice returns the original arrays up to permutation. Substitute v=A+1+k in the residual in (5), and apply s_r s_(D-r)=N to its three numerator factorials. The three factors of N cancel its prefactor exactly. The result is the ACTUAL tail formula (4) for (a*,c*), with no extra factor or sign. Therefore

T(a,c)-T(a*,c*)=-sin(theta) sum_(v=0)^A C_v J_v.               (16)

Moreover the same factorial reflection proves

C*_v=C_(A-v),  C*=C,  J*_v=-J_(A-v).          (17)

The last equality also follows directly from H_(D-r)=H_r. Thus the difference in (16) is logarithmically bounded relative to C, whereas this involution alone leaves the average of the two currents undetermined. Repeated application is not a contraction.

For the actual tetrahedral labels, this is an actual PARTNER move, not just a transformation of abstract factorial arrays. If the unique largest face is F_1=a+b+e, retain those three edges and replace its complementary edges by

(c,d,f) -> (D-c,D-d,D-f).                    (18)

The unchanged face sum is F_1; the other new face sums are the three numbers 2D-Q_k+F_1, and the new opposite sums are 2D+F_1-F_i for i!=1. These are exactly (15). They again have the same unique largest face, because the original tail is live. The other choices follow by tetrahedral relabeling.

In particular (16) controls a difference of the full ACTUAL first normals, not merely a difference of bare root tails. Both core germs have polynomial logarithmic derivatives, their root cores agree by (17), and the four face factors also have polynomial logarithmic derivatives. Differentiating (3) for each branch therefore gives

|dot K_B(q)-dot K_(B*)(q)|
                   <= C_* N^2(1+log N) W_N^2.               (19)

No equality of the two generic normal germs is asserted. Combining these controlled three-edge differences with other exact source relations is a possible next step; (16) by itself does not fix the common first-normal component.

7. What the general residual still requires

For a unique largest face, the last sum in (5) is still alternating. No polynomial relative bound on its absolute envelope has been proved here. Positivity of the residues does not authorize replacing this residual by its absolute sum. The exact formula exposes the partner involution and proves the repeated-maximal-face class, but it does not close the general first-normal transport problem. The transformed summation interval need not be shorter than the original one.

The identity holds for abstract arrays (1) as well as actual partner branches. Applying it to a source retains all original generic normalization, peripheral factors and marked exterior data through the already proved coefficient theorem. It does not alone supply a positive completed recoupling metric or identify a global complement.
