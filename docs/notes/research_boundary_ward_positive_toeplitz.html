A positive Toeplitz Gram controls the full boundary Ward factor

Written research proof, 23 November 2026.

The entire actual boundary Ward sum is one finite Euler coefficient. Its logarithmic factor has exactly the original lowering vacuum as kernel. After that literal shift and an explicit factor of order NN are extracted, the remaining matrix has logarithmic norm and a bounded inverse on the full unit activity disk, at every level N≥2N\ge2. The inverse bound follows from a positive Toeplitz Gram, obtained by ordinary Fourier orthogonality on an auxiliary finite cycle. No activity collar, diagonal conditioning gauge or parity restriction is needed.

The physical occupation grading extracts the Ward coefficient by a unitary Fourier projection. This gives an O(Nlog⁡N)O(N\log N) normal-factor cost before the retained Euler matrix and original factorial. The marked co-source, all normal blocks and the final coefficient remain; no scalar lower bound or geometric-cycle selection is asserted.

1. The literal Ward sum and its ordered resummation

Fix N≥2,d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,m=r+1≥2,x0=−q−1,Fj=(Q;Q)j.(1) N\ge2,\quad d=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2, \quad m=r+1\ge2,\quad x_0=-q^{-1},\quad F_j=(Q;Q)_j. \qquad\text{(1)} Use the original physical Euclidean occupation basis on the remaining rr heads. The operator EE is their ordered lowering coproduct, with one-head coefficient Q−a−1Q^{-a}-1 and the original bottom zero. Let LL be the first-remaining-head unit lowering shift, tensored with identities on other heads: L|a⟩=|a−1⟩(a>0),L|0⟩=0,LN=0,∥L∥=1.(2) L|a\rangle=|a-1\rangle\ (a>0),\quad L|0\rangle=0, \qquad L^N=0,\quad \|L\|=1. \qquad\text{(2)} Write b=bσ1b=b_{\sigma_1} for the actual reduced positive boundary crossing, including its scalar. For a physical Ward order 1≤t<N1\le t<N, input weight λ02=Q−t\lambda_0^2=Q^{-t}, and a=λ0/x0a=\lambda_0/x_0, one has |a|=1|a|=1. The complete first-normal arrow defect [W, (19)–(20)] is 𝔇t=2m∑n=1t(−1)n−1anFtFt−n(1−Qn)Et−nbLn.(3) \mathfrak D_t=2m\sum_{n=1}^{t}(-1)^{n-1}a^n \frac{F_t}{F_{t-n}(1-Q^n)}E^{t-n}bL^n. \qquad\text{(3)} The actual Ward arrow restricts this matrix to its indicated input weight. The displayed finite sum can also be formed on the full occupation space. The factor order is part of the formula. Equation (3) follows from W by the exact cancellation [tn]QFn−1=Ft/[Ft−n(1−Qn)]{t\brack n}_QF_{n-1}=F_t/[F_{t-n}(1-Q^n)]. No denominator has index NN.

Define ℰN(z)=∑j=0dzjFj,ℓN(z)=∑n=1d(−1)n−1zn1−Qn,UN(T)=∑j=0N−2(−1)j1−Q1−Qj+1Tj.(4) \mathcal E_N(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\frac{z^j}{F_j},\qquad \ell_N(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{d}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}z^n}{1-Q^n},\qquad U_N(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}(-1)^j \frac{1-Q}{1-Q^{j+1}}T^j. \qquad\text{(4)} Finite coefficient multiplication proves the exact ordered identity 𝔇t=2mFt[ξt]ℰN(ξE)bℓN(aξL),ℓN(T)=T1−QUN(T)(TN=0).(5) \boxed{\quad \mathfrak D_t=2mF_t[\xi^t]\, \mathcal E_N(\xi E)b\ell_N(a\xi L),\qquad \ell_N(T)=\frac{T}{1-Q}U_N(T)\quad(T^N=0). \quad} \qquad\text{(5)} Left multiplication by EE and right multiplication by aLaL commute as operations on matrices; E,b,LE,b,L need not commute. Thus (5) never divides by either nilpotent operator.

The logarithmic sign and normalization are also finite algebra. In ℂ[z]/(zN)\mathbb C[z]/(z^N), the Euler coefficient recurrence gives ℰN(Qz)=(1−z)ℰN(z),log⁡ℰN(z)=∑n=1dznn(1−Qn),ℓN(z)=−z∂zlog⁡ℰN(−z)=zℰN′(−z)ℰN(−z)−1.(6) \mathcal E_N(Qz)=(1-z)\mathcal E_N(z),\qquad \log\mathcal E_N(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{d}\frac{z^n}{n(1-Q^n)},\qquad \ell_N(z)=-z\partial_z\log\mathcal E_N(-z) =z\mathcal E_N'(-z)\mathcal E_N(-z)^{-1}. \qquad\text{(6)} Indeed the logarithm has constant term zero, and its degree-nn coefficient satisfies (Qn−1)cn=−1/n(Q^n-1)c_n=-1/n. The Euler derivative preserves the ideal (zN)(z^N). Evaluation at a nilpotent matrix makes these identities exact, with finite polynomial inverses. This finite logarithm is prior coverage in [C].

For any original linear co-source Λ\Lambda on the normal insertion, fixed in ξ\xi, the full contraction is exactly Λ(𝔇t)=2mFt2πi∮|ξ|=1Λ(ℰN(ξE)bℓN(aξL))dξξt+1.(7) \Lambda(\mathfrak D_t)=\frac{2mF_t}{2\pi i} \oint_{|\xi|=1}\Lambda\!\left( \mathcal E_N(\xi E)b\ell_N(a\xi L)\right) \frac{d\xi}{\xi^{t+1}}. \qquad\text{(7)} The original weight restriction, exterior, marked closing and scalar lines stay inside this contraction. On a reciprocal pair, its two physical Ward arrows recover all four normal blocks of [W, (24)]. In particular the off-diagonal couplings of W’s original source formula (27) are not dropped by (5) or (7).

2. The exact positive Toeplitz Gram

Put θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N. For 0≤j≤N−20\le j\le N-2, set aj=sin⁡θsin⁡((j+1)θ)>0,1−Q1−Qj+1=q−jaj.(8) a_j=\frac{\sin\theta}{\sin((j+1)\theta)}>0, \qquad \frac{1-Q}{1-Q^{j+1}}=q^{-j}a_j. \qquad\text{(8)} For the moment let N≥3N\ge3 and put p=N−2≥1p=N-2\ge1. The exact integral representation is aj=Nsin⁡θπ∫01xj+xp−j1+xNdx,0≤j≤p.(9) a_j=\frac{N\sin\theta}{\pi} \int_0^1\frac{x^j+x^{p-j}}{1+x^N}\,dx, \qquad 0\le j\le p. \qquad\text{(9)} Here is a direct normalization proof. Integrate zj/(1+zN)z^j/(1+z^N) around a sector of angle 2π/N2\pi/N, with positive orientation. The small and large circular arcs vanish, respectively as O(ϵj+1)O(\epsilon^{j+1}) and O(Rj+1−N)O(R^{j+1-N}). The sole pole is eπi/Ne^{\pi i/N}, so (1−e2πi(j+1)/N)∫0∞xj1+xNdx=−2πiNeπi(j+1)/N.(10) (1-e^{2\pi i(j+1)/N}) \int_0^\infty\frac{x^j}{1+x^N}\,dx =-\frac{2\pi i}{N}e^{\pi i(j+1)/N}. \qquad\text{(10)} This gives the integral π/[Nsin⁡((j+1)θ)]\pi/[N\sin((j+1)\theta)]. Substitute x↦1/xx\mapsto1/x in its part above one. The exponent becomes N−j−2=p−jN-j-2=p-j, proving (9), including its endpoints.

For each 0<x<10<x<1, the matrix C(x)ij=x|i−j|+xp−|i−j|,0≤i,j≤p,(11) C(x)_{ij}=x^{|i-j|}+x^{p-|i-j|},\qquad 0\le i,j\le p, \qquad\text{(11)} is positive semidefinite. To verify this using finite Fourier orthogonality, start with the pp-cycle circulant whose row is cℓ=xℓ+xp−ℓc_\ell=x^\ell+x^{p-\ell}, 0≤ℓ<p0\le\ell<p. Its Fourier eigenvalues are the positive numbers ∑ℓ=0p−1cℓe−2πikℓ/p=(1−xp)(1−x2)1−2xcos⁡(2πk/p)+x2,0≤k<p.(12) \sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}c_\ell e^{-2\pi i k\ell/p} =\frac{(1-x^p)(1-x^2)} {1-2x\cos(2\pi k/p)+x^2},\qquad 0\le k<p. \qquad\text{(12)} Summing the two finite geometric series proves the equality. It also holds for p=1p=1, when its single value is 1+x1+x. The map taking basis vector eje_j, 0≤j≤p0\le j\le p, to e[j]pe_{[j]_p} pulls this positive circulant back to (11): its indices zero and pp are duplicates, exactly as required by cp=c0c_p=c_0. Hence (11) is a positive Gram, without an alias assumption on a physical color.

Integrating (11) with the positive weight in (9) proves Ta=(a|i−j|)i,j=0p≥0.(13) T_a=(a_{|i-j|})_{i,j=0}^{p}\ge0. \qquad\text{(13)} This is an auxiliary cycle Gram. It neither changes the original physical quantum root nor replaces any Hermite boundary variable.

3. A uniform inverse on the full physical unit disk

Work first on the one physical head where the shift acts. Its range is R=span⁡{|0⟩,…,|N−2⟩}R=\operatorname{span}\{|0\rangle,\ldots,|N-2\rangle\}. For |z|=1|z|=1, let η=−q−1z\eta=-q^{-1}z and Dη|h⟩=η−h|h⟩D_\eta|h\rangle=\eta^{-h}|h\rangle. This is a physical diagonal unitary and DηLDη−1=ηLD_\eta L D_\eta^{-1}=\eta L. Equations (4),(8) give UN(zL)=Dη(∑j=0N−2ajLj)Dη−1.(14) U_N(zL)=D_\eta\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}a_jL^j\right)D_\eta^{-1}. \qquad\text{(14)} Restrict to the invariant space RR, of dimension p+1p+1. The Hermitian part of the middle matrix in (14) is exactly (I+Ta)/2(I+T_a)/2: its diagonal is one and its upper diagonals are aja_j. Therefore (13) proves Re⁡(UN(zL)|R)≥12IR,∥(UN(zL)|R)−1∥≤2,|z|=1.(15) \operatorname{Re}(U_N(zL)|_R)\ge\tfrac12 I_R, \qquad \|(U_N(zL)|_R)^{-1}\|\le2, \qquad |z|=1. \qquad\text{(15)} For |z|<1|z|<1, apply the harmonic minimum principle to each quadratic form Re⁡⟨v,UN(zL)v⟩\operatorname{Re}\langle v,U_N(zL)v\rangle, v∈Rv\in R. In the orthogonal decomposition R⊕ℂ|d⟩R\oplus\mathbb C|d\rangle, the whole one-head matrix has the form UN(zL)=(UR(z)v(z)01),∥v(z)∥2≤∑j=1N−2aj2≤π2,|z|≤1.(16) U_N(zL)=\begin{pmatrix}U_R(z)&v(z)\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \|v(z)\|^2\le\sum_{j=1}^{N-2}a_j^2\le\pi^2, \quad |z|\le1. \qquad\text{(16)} For the last bound use aj≤π/[2min⁡(j+1,N−j−1)]a_j\le\pi/[2\min(j+1,N-j-1)]. Each positive denominator occurs at most twice, and ∑k≥1k−2≤2\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-2}\le2. Block inversion and (15) now give ∥UN(zL)−1∥≤2+2π\|U_N(zL)^{-1}\|\le2+2\pi. Tensoring with identities preserves every bound. All estimates are in the stated physical Euclidean basis, not a binomial-gauged norm.

4. The exact endpoint and arbitrary left contexts

Since UN(zL)U_N(zL) commutes with LL and is invertible, (5) gives ker⁡ℓN(zL)=ker⁡L,im⁡ℓN(zL)=im⁡L(z≠0).(18) \ker\ell_N(zL)=\ker L,\qquad \operatorname{im}\ell_N(zL)=\operatorname{im}L\quad(z\ne0). \qquad\text{(18)} The shift is an isometry from (ker⁡L)⟂(\ker L)^\perp onto its range. Using both orders LUN=UNLLU_N=U_NL, the nonzero singular values of ℓN(zL)\ell_N(zL) lie between c|z|/|1−Q|c|z|/|1-Q| and C|z|log⁡N/|1−Q|C|z|\log N/|1-Q|. For any compatible rectangular left matrix BB, either the physical operator norm or the Hilbert–Schmidt norm satisfies c|z||1−Q|∥BL∥≤∥BℓN(zL)∥≤C|z|log⁡N|1−Q|∥BL∥,0<|z|≤1,N≥2.(19) \frac{c|z|}{|1-Q|}\|BL\| \le\|B\ell_N(zL)\| \le\frac{C|z|\log N}{|1-Q|}\|BL\|, \qquad 0<|z|\le1,\quad N\ge2. \qquad\text{(19)} The upper bound is multiplication by (17). For the lower bound, write BL=(BLUN)UN−1BL=(BLU_N)U_N^{-1}; the same argument uses the least singular value of the right factor in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. In particular 4/N≤|1−Q|≤2π/N4/N\le|1-Q|\le2\pi/N proves, at |z|=1|z|=1, cN∥BL∥≤∥BℓN(zL)∥≤CNlog⁡N∥BL∥.(20) \boxed{\quad cN\|BL\|\le\|B\ell_N(zL)\| \le CN\log N\,\|BL\|. \quad} \qquad\text{(20)} The nonzero singular values lie in the same interval [cN,CNlog⁡N][cN,CN\log N]. The vacuum kernel has not been removed from the original source. These bounds allow any matrix already on the left, without a condition number for it. They are not a lower bound for an arbitrary scalar co-source applied to the resulting matrix.

5. Unitary extraction of every physical Ward order

Let 𝗇\mathsf n be total remaining integer occupation and put Dϕ=e−iϕ𝗇D_\phi=e^{-i\phi\mathsf n}. Both EE and LL lower degree by one. The actual bb preserves degree, because every original crossing, each prefix term, and first-head zero extraction have that property. Thus DϕEDϕ−1=eiϕE,DϕLDϕ−1=eiϕL,DϕbDϕ−1=b.(21) D_\phi ED_\phi^{-1}=e^{i\phi}E,\quad D_\phi LD_\phi^{-1}=e^{i\phi}L,\quad D_\phi bD_\phi^{-1}=b. \qquad\text{(21)} The matrices DϕD_\phi are unitary in the physical basis. For F(ξ)=ℰN(ξE)bℓN(aξL)F(\xi)=\mathcal E_N(\xi E)b\ell_N(a\xi L), one consequently has F(eiϕ)=DϕF(1)Dϕ−1,[ξt]F=𝖯tF(1),𝖯tB=12π∫02πe−itϕDϕBDϕ−1dϕ.(22) F(e^{i\phi})=D_\phi F(1)D_\phi^{-1},\qquad [\xi^t]F=\mathsf P_tF(1),\quad \mathsf P_tB=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{-it\phi}D_\phi BD_\phi^{-1}\,d\phi. \qquad\text{(22)} This is the exact projection onto entries of degree difference −t-t. It is contractive in operator norm, by unitary averaging, and an orthogonal projection in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, where different degree differences have disjoint matrix-entry supports. In particular the full family satisfies Parseval; it is not a geometric-window projection.

Combining (5),(20),(22) proves the all-transfer estimate ∥𝔇t∥≤Cm|Ft|Nlog⁡N∥ℰN(E)bL∥,1≤t<N,N≥2,(23) \boxed{\quad \|\mathfrak D_t\|\le C m|F_t|N\log N\, \|\mathcal E_N(E)bL\|, \qquad 1\le t<N,\quad N\ge2, \quad} \qquad\text{(23)} in both of the stated physical norms. All constants in the factor estimates are independent of the number of heads; the displayed mm, factorial and remaining Euler-crossing product are retained. Terms of FF of degree at least NN can occur; exact projection selects the specified order without replacing F(1)F(1) by a truncated matrix. An arbitrary exterior is not commuted through 𝖯t\mathsf P_t. It can always remain in (7), while (19)–(20) apply when it is already on the left of the logarithmic factor.

There is no lower bound after 𝖯t\mathsf P_t or the original marked closing: both can annihilate nonzero matrices. The full-activity Euler matrix and its geometric period remain to be controlled. The small-activity results of [C] are not extended to unit activity by (23). The exact negative-generator and word cocycle laws in [W] retain their actual inverse matrices and normal insertions when applied to (5). No bound for those remaining factors is inferred. All quantum parameters remain at the original root. No physical Cartan weight is replaced by an off-circle geometric eigenvalue, and no fixed-root meridian normal or complementary current is erased.
