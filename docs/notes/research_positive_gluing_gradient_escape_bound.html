A quantitative gradient gap for positive logarithmic gluing

Research proof, 23 November 2026.

A realized positive marked gluing system has a full-gradient bound on its entire positive logarithmic chart. The bound uses curvature only near its solution. It excludes escape of the selected logarithmic coordinates to infinity in finite normalized descent time. Finite flat-angle or cut-boundary exits remain possible and are retained.

This is a classical no-infinite-coordinate-escape statement. It does not identify an original quantum period with this chart or establish the no-ends hypothesis for its relative cycle.

1. Positive coordinates and the marked constraint

Let t≥1t\geq1, and put 𝒮={x∈ℂ:0<ℑx<π},z=ex,v(x)=−Log⁡(1−ex).(1) \mathcal S=\{x\in\mathbb C:0<\Im x<\pi\},\qquad z=e^x,\qquad v(x)=-\operatorname{Log}(1-e^x). \qquad\text{(1)} The logarithm is principal. Apply vv coordinatewise on 𝒮t\mathcal S^t. On this convex product, ℑv′(x)=ℑ(ex)|1−ex|2>0.(2) \Im v'(x)=\frac{\Im(e^x)}{|1-e^x|^2}>0. \qquad\text{(2)} Let A,BA,B be real tt-by-tt matrices with rank⁡(AB)=t,AB𝖳=BA𝖳,FR(x)=Ax+Bv(x).(3) \operatorname{rank}(A\ B)=t,\qquad AB^{\mathsf T}=BA^{\mathsf T}, \qquad F_R(x)=Ax+Bv(x). \qquad\text{(3)} Keep one specified affine logarithmic target cc. In an actual ideal triangulation these are the independent edge rows and one marked primitive peripheral row per cusp of [G]. Every edge winding, quad constant and peripheral logarithmic lift belongs to cc. We assume a positive solution is supplied, not merely that the exponentiated equations have a solution.

3. Curvature near the solution controls the full chart

Choose r>0r>0 so that the closed Euclidean ball B(x*,r)¯\overline{B(x_*,r)} is contained in 𝒮t\mathcal S^t, and put m=miny∈B(x*,r)¯1≤j≤tℑv′(yj)>0.(8) m=\min_{\substack{y\in\overline{B(x_*,r)}\\1\leq j\leq t}} \Im v'(y_j)>0. \qquad\text{(8)} For example take rr smaller than half the distance of every ℑx*,j\Im x_{*,j} from 0,π0,\pi. The numerator in (2) then has a positive lower bound and its denominator a finite upper bound on this ball. All norms below are Euclidean.

Theorem. For every x∈𝒮tx\in\mathcal S^t, ∥∇S(x)∥≥mmin⁡(∥x−x*∥,r).(9) \boxed{\quad \|\nabla S(x)\|\geq m\min(\|x-x_*\|,r). \quad} \qquad\text{(9)} In particular the full gradient is bounded away from zero outside the ball, including at every escaping sequence in this chart.

Proof. Set d=x−x*d=x-x_*. The joining segment remains positive. Every integrand is positive. On the first min⁡(1,r/∥d∥)\min(1,r/\|d\|) portion of the segment each is at least mm. Consequently, for d≠0d\ne0, ℑ(d*∇S(x))≥m∥d∥min⁡(∥d∥,r).(11) \Im\bigl(d^*\nabla S(x)\bigr) \geq m\|d\|\min(\|d\|,r). \qquad\text{(11)} Cauchy–Schwarz and division by ∥d∥\|d\| prove (9). The constants can be uniform in families. Suppose real symmetric PaP_a and targets uau_a have supplied critical points x*,ax_{*,a} in a compact subset of 𝒮t\mathcal S^t. A common positive radius rr makes the union of the closed balls have compact closure in 𝒮t\mathcal S^t, and (2) supplies a common m>0m>0 there. The proof gives (9) for every member, without a bound on its filling coefficients in PaP_a. For varying constraint rows, use a specified common invertible quad chart wherever that family has one. No unrecorded transition between different selected charts is included in the assertion.

4. Growing phase and finite descent time

For the growing classical exponential and its height, use eNS(x)/(2πi),h(x)=ℜS(x)2πi=ℑS(x)2π,∥∇ℝh∥=∥∇S∥2π.(12) e^{NS(x)/(2\pi i)},\qquad h(x)=\Re\frac{S(x)}{2\pi i}=\frac{\Im S(x)}{2\pi}, \qquad \|\nabla_{\mathbb R}h\|=\frac{\|\nabla S\|}{2\pi}. \qquad\text{(12)} At fixed parameters, consider the normalized lowering flow away from the critical point, dxds=−∇ℝh∥∇ℝh∥2,dhds=−1.(13) \frac{dx}{ds}=-\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb R}h}{ \|\nabla_{\mathbb R}h\|^2}, \qquad \frac{dh}{ds}=-1. \qquad\text{(13)} Outside B(x*,r)B(x_*,r) its speed is at most 2π/(mr)2\pi/(mr). For any trajectory remaining in the positive chart, its distance from the center therefore satisfies ∥x(s)−x*∥≤max⁡(r,∥x(0)−x*∥)+2πsmr.(14) \|x(s)-x_*\| \leq\max(r,\|x(0)-x_*\|)+\frac{2\pi s}{mr}. \qquad\text{(14)} Indeed every excursion outside the ball starts either at the initial point or on its radius-rr sphere; its total outside time is at most ss. Inside the ball the distance is already at most rr. Thus no selected logarithmic coordinate can diverge in finite normalized descent time, equivalently during a finite height drop. These bounds are uniform for the families just described. They do not construct a continuation of an arbitrary target path.

5. Boundary and source scope

The flow may reach a finite flat-angle or logarithmic cut boundary, or approach the critical point, where the normalized field is singular. No such exit is removed. A positive quad rotation can send an old z→0z\to0 end to z′→1z'\to1; hence boundedness of the selected logs is not a quad-invariant exclusion of ideal degenerations. All finite 0,1,∞0,1,\infty interpretations and the corresponding boundary currents remain to be treated in the actual coordinates and with their marks.

The Legendre contour of [L] already has a calibrated volume maximum, controlled radial homotopies and explicit alias bulk translations. The estimate here concerns instead the full principal gradient over the entire positive product, using only a local curvature constant. It settles the infinite-coordinate part of a normalized-flow check while that flow stays in this specified positive chart. It proves neither a boundary-action gap nor absence of relative-cycle handles from cuts, finite boundaries or other logarithmic charts.

In particular no original Kashaev source, cap, normal direction or relative current has been replaced. A source-preserving passage to this positive polarization and control of its actual boundary terms remain necessary. There is no selected-cycle, scalar lower-bound or finite-exception conclusion.
