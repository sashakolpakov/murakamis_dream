2. Execute a whole-source boundary audit

Work with the simultaneous head, clock/cap and Gaussian phase. Do not take absolute values after fixing clocks: anchor criticality alone is not the full gluing equation.

For each surviving boundary chart, establish ONE of the following by an exact source identity or a quantitative estimate:

  1. Its complete current cancels or contracts with its prescribed partner.
  2. Its full stationary support lies in the saturated critical closure.
  3. Its original contribution has an independent upper bound at the required volume scale.

The list to cover is concrete: coordinate zero/infinity flags; collisions of root hyperplanes; positive/negative real-axis interfaces of the full functions; cap endpoints; Gaussian ends; and their simultaneous corners. Intersections use a common regularization. A bound on isolated pieces does not replace the bound on their actual aggregate.

Known normalization cells are contracted before estimates, by actual_markov_ray_normalization_and_pruning.md. Some individual ray groups have the full invariant’s rate, so a strict gap for every ray cannot be a premise. Regular cap endpoints already have zero critical volume. Gaussian and sufficiently deep zero ends have existing coarse tail control; the unresolved intermediate and joint boundary layers must be handled explicitly.

Current constructive lead: the original charged polynomial is a product of a fixed number of distinct-root Q-interval blocks. At a nonzero collision class, each block contributes at most one vanishing factor. This limits normal multiplicities independently of N. Determine the full source equations on the resulting bounded-order collision strata, and test saturation there. Do not infer saturation merely from this multiplicity bound: boundary layers of thickness 1/N can retain a missing normal equation.

3. Turn boundary control into a period estimate

Construct a deformation of the AGGREGATE original cycle into the sublevel Re(S) <= Vol/(2*pi)+epsilon, with all retained amplitudes and transport costs exp(o(N)). Here S includes every original exponential normalizer and the prescribed logarithmic marks.

The proof must give quantitative control of the chain and its ends, not just existence of a formal thimble decomposition. Once this is proved, the triangle inequality on that deformed aggregate cycle gives (U); then let epsilon decrease to zero.

A one-variable coefficient contour can be moved to a critical-value lemniscate with polynomial logarithmic length. This is now proved and independently audited as a test of the transport mechanism. Its limitation is explicit: applying it to a fixed clock sector does not supply (U). The higher-dimensional and full-clock source step is required.

4. Geometric survival, using the same source transport

After the upper transport is established, follow the ORIGINAL selected class into the positive geometric chart. Compute its top-volume coefficient, including the nilpotent source extension, rather than assigning a favorable cycle. The existing Legendre–Rivin contour then provides the unique volume maximum and a nonzero local Gaussian density.

Required conclusion: the aggregate coefficient of that geometric period is nonzero and exp(o(N)) in modulus, and the remaining contributions cannot cancel its leading exponential rate. This yields the lower bound and hence the original volume limit.

5. Stop rules for unsuccessful approaches

  • Do not replace the selected source by a positive pivotal trace. The actual closing has Phi(I)=0 but Phi(Pi)=I, with Pi a nonzero nilpotent. Positivity of that closing is impossible.
  • Do not assume a bounded number of short-root normal jets after charges grow beyond N-1. That unrestricted interpolation statement is false; original mixed/Fermat currents or an explicit source window must stay.
  • Do not bound the spanning-tree residue expansion term by term and call its entropy Rivin volume. An actual figure-eight residue channel after forced source fusion has a larger rate; the aggregate cancellation remains essential.
  • Do not count a new torsor presentation, a fixed-clock estimate, or a prototype calculation as completion of (U) or geometric survival.

These checks remove proposed shortcuts without changing the target. No new broad-class sharp bound or volume theorem is established as of this plan. The next claim of a major milestone requires the full source estimate (U), or the broad-class geometric survival theorem in Section 4.
