A regular midpoint frame for complete knot holonomy

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

The actual braid coordinates admit a regular geometric frame with cut position exactly Acut=−1A_{\mathrm{ cut}}=-1. This holds for every complete hyperbolic knot holonomy and every fixed finite braid word, using the five-shape crossing chart of M below. It strengthens the generic-frame statement by fixing the midpoint position in advance.

The obstruction to this choice would be a group element acting as half of the primitive meridian translation. The complete cusp lattice excludes it. The remaining frame parameter can then be chosen from a finite predetermined rational grid.

This is a statement about the actual classical braid chart. It neither identifies the integration cycle of the binomial cut nor proves that a geometric contribution survives in the invariant.

1. Cusp normalization and the midpoint meridian

Let ρ0\rho_0 be the complete faithful projective holonomy of a hyperbolic knot complement. Choose its oriented peripheral basis (μ,λ)(\mu,\lambda). Normalize the cusp fixed point to ∞\infty, and the meridian translation to one. Then ρ0(μ)=J1,ρ0(λ)=Jτ,Jz=(1z01),ℑτ>0.(1) \rho_0(\mu)=J_1,\qquad \rho_0(\lambda)=J_\tau,\qquad J_z=\begin{pmatrix}1&z\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Im\tau>0. \qquad\text{(1)} Here the full stabilizer of this cusp point is Stab⁡ρ0(π1)(∞)={Jm+nτ:m,n∈ℤ}in PSL2(ℂ).(2) \operatorname{Stab}_{\rho_0(\pi_1)}(\infty) =\{J_{m+n\tau}:m,n\in\mathbb Z\} \quad\text{in }\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C). \qquad\text{(2)} Equation (2) uses the full peripheral group of an embedded complete torus cusp, not a proper sublattice. Indeed a lift of that cusp is a horoball whose deck stabilizer is the fundamental group of its torus quotient. Taking the horoball far enough out identifies this stabilizer with the full parabolic fixed-point stabilizer. The meridian is primitive in that torus lattice. Consequently J1/2J_{1/2} is not the image of any group word: m+nτ=1/2m+n\tau=1/2 forces n=0n=0 and then an impossible integer m=1/2m=1/2.

For s≠0s\ne0, use the change of projective frame F(r,s)=(r1−rs−s),F(r,s)−1=(1(1−r)/s1−r/s).(3) F(r,s)=\begin{pmatrix}r&1-r\\s&-s\end{pmatrix},\qquad F(r,s)^{-1}= \begin{pmatrix}1&(1-r)/s\\1&-r/s\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(3)} It sends the selected flag (1,1)(1,1) to ∞\infty, and F(r,s)−1J1F(r,s)=(1+s−ss1−s).(4) F(r,s)^{-1}J_1F(r,s) =\begin{pmatrix}1+s&-s\\s&1-s\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(4)} At s=−2s=-2, the actual convention of M is G=−μG=-\mu, x=−1x=-1, so the cut coordinates are μcut=(−12−23),K=1,e=−2,f=2,Acut=x/K=−1,pnew=1.(5) \mu_{\mathrm{ cut}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\-2&3\end{pmatrix}, \quad K=1,\quad e=-2,\quad f=2,\quad A_{\mathrm{ cut}}=x/K=-1,\quad p_{\mathrm{ new}}=1. \qquad\text{(5)} The last equality follows directly from M’s physical relation f=(1−A)/pnewf=(1-A)/p_{\mathrm{ new}}. Thus (5) is the actual midpoint position and selected flag, with a noncentral complete meridian.

2. A prefix minor cannot vanish throughout this slice

Let PP be any determinant-one matrix representing the projective image of a group word, and write P=(abcd). P=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}. The letter dd in this section is a matrix entry, not a quantum dimension. Multiplication of (3) gives (F−1PF)11=csr(1−r)+(a−d)r+d+bs.(6) (F^{-1}PF)_{11} =\frac cs r(1-r)+(a-d)r+d+bs . \qquad\text{(6)} For fixed nonzero ss, this polynomial is identically zero in rr precisely when c=0,a=d,d+bs=0. c=0,\qquad a=d,\qquad d+bs=0. The determinant then gives d2=1d^2=1, so this case is exactly P=±J−1/s.(7) P=\pm J_{-1/s}. \qquad\text{(7)} At s=−2s=-2, (7) contradicts (2). Therefore every such prefix minor has at most two exceptional values of rr. Signs of determinant-one lifts do not affect this argument: both the zero condition and (2) are projective statements.

This is the reason for the midpoint choice. A general fixed slice ss can fail this test if −1/s-1/s is an actual peripheral translation.

3. The other finite braid-chart conditions

Keep any fixed finite braid presentation and the actual holonomy-braiding convention of M. At each stage its strand meridians are conjugates of the knot meridian. Every ordered prefix Pj=Hj⋯H1P_j=H_j\cdots H_1, with Hj=−μjH_j=-\mu_j, is up to its central sign the image of a group word. The same is true at intermediate braid stages by the explicit Wirtinger rule. Thus Section 2 applies simultaneously to all prefix minors.

For clarity the remaining nonvanishing conditions can also be checked on this fixed slice. Put vr=(rs),αr=(1,(1−r)/s),αr(vr)=1.(8) v_r=\begin{pmatrix}r\\s\end{pmatrix},\qquad \alpha_r=(1,(1-r)/s),\qquad \alpha_r(v_r)=1. \qquad\text{(8)} They are the first column of FF and first row of F−1F^{-1}. Each varies injectively in its affine projective-line chart. Once the prefix minors are nonzero, the Gauss inverse of M has ej=0⇔αr is a left eigenline of Hj,(9) e_j=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \alpha_r\text{ is a left eigenline of }H_j, \qquad\text{(9)} fj=0⇔vr is an eigenline of Pj−1−1Pj.(10) f_j=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ v_r\text{ is an eigenline of } P_{j-1}^{-1}P_j . \qquad\text{(10)} Here the matrices on the right are in the unframed normalization of (1); their framed representatives are used on the left.

One can verify these tests directly. If Pj−1=(puv)P_{j-1}=(p_{uv}), the lower Gauss factor is B−(Pj−1)=(10−p21p11). B_-(P_{j-1})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-p_{21}&p_{11}\end{pmatrix}. This proves (10).

Each matrix in (9)–(10) is a noncentral parabolic: the second is a conjugate of the corresponding meridian. It has a unique projective eigenline. Consequently each condition excludes at most one value of rr.

There are only finitely many stages and strands. Choose one rr outside all their exceptional values. Then all Kj,ej,fjK_j,e_j,f_j are nonzero, and the actual positive and negative crossing denominators are nonzero. In the complete case x2=1x^2=1, the physical identity ejfj=(1−Aj)(1−x2/Aj)x e_jf_j=\frac{(1-A_j)(1-x^2/A_j)}x also excludes Aj=1A_j=1, both before and after every crossing. The transfer shape C≠1C\ne1 follows from the nonzero product e1f2e_1f_2, or its negative-crossing counterpart. M’s five ordered crossing shapes consequently avoid both zero and one. This proves regularity in that five-shape chart; it is not a claim that a four-term subdivision has no pinched crossing.

4. A finite rational choice and bounded frame

Let pp count the prefix minors tested above, and let q+q_+, q−q_- count the eigenline tests (9), (10). There are at most B=2p+q++q− B=2p+q_++q_- bad complex numbers rr. One of the B+1B+1 predetermined rational values rk=kB+2,1≤k≤B+1(11) r_k=\frac{k}{B+2},\qquad 1\le k\le B+1 \qquad\text{(11)} therefore works. In particular it can be chosen in (0,1)(0,1). At s=−2s=-2, the ordinary Euclidean matrix norms satisfy ∥F(r,−2)∥≤3,∥F(r,−2)−1∥≤3/2.(12) \|F(r,-2)\|\le3,\qquad \|F(r,-2)^{-1}\|\le3/2 . \qquad\text{(12)} These follow by bounding their Frobenius norms in (3). The normalization of the complete cusp in (1) has already been made; (12) does not bound the original cusp-normalizing conjugation or the entries of every other holonomy.

If the unframed matrices lie in a coefficient field kk, this choice of frame uses only rational coefficients and preserves that field. No degree bound for kk is asserted. The finite count does not give a uniform lower bound on all chart denominators over a degenerating family of knot holonomies. For each fixed complete representation they are finitely many nonzero numbers, which is sufficient for an open neighborhood.

5. A selected-flag deformation through the same midpoint

There is an explicit local extension beyond the complete meridian. Work on any augmented holomorphic representation family near ρ0\rho_0, with chosen meridian eigenvalue M−1M^{-1} and eigenflag. A local frame sends that flag to ∞\infty, putting the meridian in upper triangular form. Its off-diagonal entry is a unit at completion, so a diagonal change normalizes it to one. Thus it has the form JM=(M−110M). J_M=\begin{pmatrix}M^{-1}&1\\0&M\end{pmatrix}. Keep a good rational rr from (11), and define s(M,r)=−2M−(M−1−M)r,FM=F(r,s(M,r)).(13) s(M,r)=-2M-(M^{-1}-M)r,\qquad F_M=F(r,s(M,r)). \qquad\text{(13)} The function ss is nonzero near M=1M=1. Direct multiplication gives FM−1JMFM=(−MM+M−1−2M2M+M−1).(14) F_M^{-1}J_MF_M =\begin{pmatrix}-M&M+M^{-1}\\-2M&2M+M^{-1}\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(14)} It is independent of rr, its selected eigenvector is (1,1)(1,1), and the actual cut symbols with x=−Mx=-M are K=M,e=−(M+M−1),f=2,Acut=−1,pnew=1.(15) K=M,\qquad e=-(M+M^{-1}),\qquad f=2,\qquad A_{\mathrm{ cut}}=-1,\qquad p_{\mathrm{ new}}=1. \qquad\text{(15)} These agree with the literal symbol formulas of M. Every other prefix minor and crossing denominator that was nonzero at ρ0\rho_0 remains nonzero on a sufficiently small neighborhood of the augmented family. The matrices FMF_M and their inverses remain bounded there.

The existence and parametrization of that augmented family are not proved by this change of frame. The assertion is that whenever the marked family is supplied, (13) puts it in this same actual midpoint chart. No square-root choice or change of the selected peripheral eigenflag is hidden.

6. Consequences and the remaining quantum question

The complete geometric representation has a regular point in the actual source’s classical chart with Acut=−1A_{\mathrm{ cut}}=-1, and this choice persists on a marked nearby deformation. Thus midpoint framing is not excluded by a generic chart divisor, for any fixed finite braid of a hyperbolic knot. No alternating or long-twist hypothesis is used.

The parameter rr has not been integrated out. At fixed cut meridian, varying it is the residual peripheral centralizer action. A binomial cut also changes the boundary phase and its stationary equations. Its concentration near the middle occupation does not, by itself, identify its critical scheme or its original relative integration cycle with this classical slice. The normal residue and the off-divisor source remain different data.

In particular this lemma gives no nonzero geometric cycle coefficient or scalar lower bound, and proves no new volume-conjecture filling case. It supplies the regular classical target for the source comparison.

Coverage and complete proof-source pins

Existence of diagrammatic solutions associated with representations has substantial prior coverage, including Cho–Yoon–Zickert, On the Hikami–Inoue conjecture. Their stated cluster-coordinate criterion retains a boundary-parabolic lift obstruction. The present calculation uses projective group words in the actual five-shape crossing chart and does not identify that chart with their cluster variables or discard their lift condition. The recent arc-faithful braid statement concerns nondegenerate four-term octahedral shapes after rewording. Neither paper’s proof is used here. No novelty claim is made for general representation-associated critical-point existence.
