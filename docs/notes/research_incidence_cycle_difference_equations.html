Exact difference equations for the complete incidence sum

Private written deduction, 14 November 2026. This note concerns the original finite sum, with all phases retained. The additional variables introduced below record its coefficients; they are not identified with meridian or longitude eigenvalues.

There are two useful statements. First, alternating cycles in the event–anchor incidence graph give exact difference equations without boundary remainders, even at the prescribed root of unity. Second, these equations determine every coefficient of the finite generating polynomial from one coefficient. This is rigidity of the coefficient array. Evaluation of that array can still vanish by cancellation.

1. The finite polynomial and its quadratic phase

Let GG be the bipartite multigraph whose vertices are the events and anchors of the original source. Its edges are their distinct incidences. Write EE for this edge set and CC for its unsigned incidence matrix. Parallel edges are retained. Put q=eπi/N,d=N−1,ℱd={n∈ℤ≥0E:Cn=d𝟏},Sj=∏r=1j2sin⁡(πr/N),S0=1.(1) q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad d=N-1,\qquad \mathcal F_d=\{n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}:Cn=d\mathbf1\}, \qquad S_j=\prod_{r=1}^j2\sin(\pi r/N),\quad S_0=1. \qquad\text{(1)} Every coordinate of a feasible nn is at most dd. We assume ℱd≠⌀\mathcal F_d\ne\varnothing. The case of empty support gives the zero polynomial throughout.

Use one event per crossing, together with the original closing events. The complete source formula and its joint-phase calculation give, after taking out a specified scalar independent of nn, wN(n)=qΦN(n)∏e∈ESne,ΦN(n)=12n𝖳Hn+Na𝖳n+b𝖳n.(2) w_N(n)=\frac{q^{\Phi_N(n)}}{\prod_{e\in E}S_{n_e}}, \qquad \Phi_N(n)=\frac12 n^{\mathsf T}Hn+Na^{\mathsf T}n+b^{\mathsf T}n. \qquad\text{(2)} Here HH is a fixed symmetric integral matrix with even diagonal; a,ba,b are fixed integral vectors. Their entries come from the crossing signs, the order of undercrossings, and the closing pivots. They do not depend on the level. An additive constant in ΦN\Phi_N belongs to the extracted scalar.

For precision, the joint-phase formula uses linear functions hj,uj,vj,Jjh_j,u_j,v_j,J_j of the incidences at each crossing. They are, respectively, its auxiliary clock, understrand transfer, overstrand output, and the accumulated understrand input. If αi\alpha_i is the closing index on head ii, the complete exponent is ΦN=∑jσjhj(uj+2Jj)+N∑j(hj−σjJj)+∑jσj(hj+Jj)−2∑i>1αi.(3) \Phi_N= \sum_j\sigma_j h_j(u_j+2J_j) +N\sum_j(h_j-\sigma_jJ_j) +\sum_j\sigma_j(h_j+J_j)-2\sum_{i>1}\alpha_i. \qquad\text{(3)} Thus (2) follows with H=∑jσj{hj⊗(uj+2Jj)+(uj+2Jj)⊗hj},a=∑j(hj−σjJj),b=∑jσj(hj+Jj)−2∑i>1αi.(4) \begin{split} H&=\sum_j\sigma_j\{ h_j\otimes(u_j+2J_j)+(u_j+2J_j)\otimes h_j\},\\ a&=\sum_j(h_j-\sigma_jJ_j),\\ b&=\sum_j\sigma_j(h_j+J_j)-2\sum_{i>1}\alpha_i. \end{split} \qquad\text{(4)} In (4), a linear function denotes its integral coefficient vector. The proof of (3), including its boundary factors, belongs to the joint-phase calculation. The arguments below also apply to any specified phase of the form (2).

Introduce independent nonzero complex variables zez_e, and define FN(z)=∑n∈ℱdwN(n)zn,zn=∏ezene.(5) F_N(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal F_d}w_N(n)z^n, \qquad z^n=\prod_e z_e^{n_e}. \qquad\text{(5)} The original scalar is its prescribed external factor times FN(𝟏)F_N(\mathbf1). No normalization by that value is used. Each coefficient in (5) is nonzero, since Sj>0S_j>0 for 0≤j<N0\le j<N.

Let TeT_e act by replacing zez_e with qzeqz_e, fixing the other variables. These operators commute with one another and satisfy Teze=qzeTeT_ez_e=qz_eT_e. On a monomial znz^n, TeT_e has eigenvalue qneq^{n_e}. 2. Alternating cycles give equations with no boundary remainder

A simple cycle in a bipartite multigraph has even length. Give its successive edges alternating signs +1,−1+1,-1, and give all other edges value zero. Denote the resulting vector by cc. It satisfies Cc=0Cc=0. A two-edge cycle between parallel incidences is allowed. Write c+c_+ and c−c_- for its positive and negative edge sets, and put ℓc=12c𝖳Hc+b𝖳c∈ℤ.(8) \ell_c=\frac12c^{\mathsf T}Hc+b^{\mathsf T}c\in\mathbb Z. \qquad\text{(8)}

Theorem 1. The original generating polynomial satisfies [∏e∈c+s(Te)−zc(−1)a𝖳cqℓc(∏eTe(Hc)e)∏e∈c−s(Te)]FN=0.(9) \boxed{\left[ \prod_{e\in c_+}s(T_e) - z^c(-1)^{a^{\mathsf T}c}q^{\ell_c} \left(\prod_eT_e^{(Hc)_e}\right) \prod_{e\in c_-}s(T_e) \right]F_N=0.} \qquad\text{(9)} The multiplication by zcz^c is on the left of the shift operators, as displayed. Negative powers are interpreted in the Laurent polynomial ring.

Proof. For m,m+c∈ℱdm,m+c\in\mathcal F_d, factorial cancellation gives (∏e∈c+s(me+1))wN(m+c)=qΦN(m+c)−ΦN(m)(∏e∈c−s(me))wN(m).(10) \left(\prod_{e\in c_+}s(m_e+1)\right)w_N(m+c) = q^{\Phi_N(m+c)-\Phi_N(m)} \left(\prod_{e\in c_-}s(m_e)\right)w_N(m). \qquad\text{(10)} The phase difference is exactly ΦN(m+c)−ΦN(m)=(Hc)𝖳m+Na𝖳c+ℓc.(11) \Phi_N(m+c)-\Phi_N(m) =(Hc)^{\mathsf T}m+Na^{\mathsf T}c+\ell_c. \qquad\text{(11)} Since qN=−1q^N=-1, this is the coefficient relation in (9).

It remains to check the boundary of the finite support. On the left of (9), a term with ne=0n_e=0 for any e∈c+e\in c_+ vanishes because s(0)=0s(0)=0. Every remaining term has m=n−c≥0m=n-c\ge0 and Cm=d𝟏Cm=d\mathbf1, and so has an allowed partner in (10). On the right, a term with me=0m_e=0 for some e∈c−e\in c_- vanishes for the same reason. Every remaining term has n=m+c≥0n=m+c\ge0 and Cn=d𝟏Cn=d\mathbf1. The row sums ensure all its coordinates are at most dd; there is no additional upper boundary. This pairs every nonzero term. No value of a sine factorial at NN, no division by a vanishing factor, and no cyclic extension of the index set is needed. ▫\square

There are only finitely many simple cycles for a fixed graph, so (7) and (9) form a finite system whose number of generators does not grow with NN. This is a system in the auxiliary variables zez_e; it is not yet a recurrence in the color or a peripheral quantum AA-polynomial.

3. Rigidity of the finite coefficient array

Theorem 2. Among polynomials supported in ℱd\mathcal F_d, the solutions of all the cycle equations (9) form a one-dimensional complex vector space, spanned by FNF_N.

Proof. First, any two feasible integral flows are joined by simple alternating cycle moves through feasible integral flows. To see this, orient every edge of the bipartite graph from its event to its anchor. For two flows n,n′n,n', the difference n′−nn'-n has zero signed divergence. Direct its positive entries with that orientation and its negative entries against it. At each vertex, total incoming multiplicity equals total outgoing multiplicity. A nonempty such directed multigraph contains a directed simple cycle. Its corresponding alternating vector cc is sign-compatible with n′−nn'-n. Thus n+c≥0n+c\ge0, its margins are unchanged, and its integral distance from n′n' is strictly smaller. Repeating finishes after finitely many moves.

Now let G(z)=∑n∈ℱdg(n)znG(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal F_d}g(n)z^n satisfy (9). For adjacent feasible flows m,m+cm,m+c, comparison of the coefficient of zm+cz^{m+c} gives (10) with gg in place of wNw_N. All sine factors in that relation lie at indices 1,…,d1,\ldots,d and are nonzero. Therefore g(m+c)wN(m+c)=g(m)wN(m).(12) \frac{g(m+c)}{w_N(m+c)} =\frac{g(m)}{w_N(m)}. \qquad\text{(12)} Connectivity makes the ratio constant on the entire support. The polynomial FNF_N itself satisfies the equations by Theorem 1 and is nonzero. This proves the assertion. ▫\square

The support condition is essential. The shift equations alone, on an unrestricted space of functions at a root of unity, do not give this uniqueness conclusion. Also, (12) does not show FN(𝟏)≠0F_N(\mathbf1)\ne0. A linear functional on a one-dimensional space of coefficient arrays can annihilate its generator. The original closing evaluation still requires its own estimate.

4. The associated algebraic equations

There is a direct classical algebraic system behind (9). Replace the commuting shift symbols TeT_e by nonzero variables yey_e, and take the level limit of their scalar coefficients. Thus qℓc→1q^{\ell_c}\to1 and qd→−1q^d\to-1. The result is ∏e∋vye=−1,∏e∈c+(ye−ye−1)=(−1)a𝖳czcyHc∏e∈c−(ye−ye−1).(13) \prod_{e\ni v}y_e=-1,\qquad \prod_{e\in c_+}(y_e-y_e^{-1}) = (-1)^{a^{\mathsf T}c}z^c y^{Hc} \prod_{e\in c_-}(y_e-y_e^{-1}). \qquad\text{(13)} The factors ii cancel because a cycle has equally many positive and negative edges. These are Laurent polynomial equations over ℚ\mathbb Q. Their equations and degrees depend on the fixed diagram, not on NN. On the regular open set ye−ye−1≠0y_e-y_e^{-1}\ne0, cycles multiply according to addition in ker⁡ℤC\ker_{\mathbb Z}C. A lattice basis of cycles consequently suffices for (13) on that open set. At the excluded divisors the uncancelled equations must be retained.

To verify the stationary meaning rather than infer it formally, choose local logarithms ye=eπixey_e=e^{\pi i x_e} with Cx=𝟏Cx=\mathbf1. On a regular simply connected chart choose an analytic primitive 𝒥′(x)=log⁡(2sin⁡πx)\mathcal J'(x)=\log(2\sin\pi x), normalized to 𝒥=J\mathcal J=J on an interval in (0,1)(0,1), where J(t)=∫0tlog⁡(2sin⁡πu)duJ(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi u)\,du. The local action is 𝒮(x;z)=−∑e𝒥(xe)+πi(12x𝖳Hx+a𝖳x)+∑exelog⁡ze.(14) \mathcal S(x;z)= -\sum_e\mathcal J(x_e) +\pi i\left(\frac12x^{\mathsf T}Hx+a^{\mathsf T}x\right) +\sum_e x_e\log z_e. \qquad\text{(14)} Its real restriction at ze=1z_e=1 has the same real part as the concave angle expression in the complete-source note. Direct differentiation gives exp⁡(Dc𝒮)=(−1)a𝖳czcyHc∏e(2sin⁡πxe)−ce.(15) \exp(D_c\mathcal S) = (-1)^{a^{\mathsf T}c}z^cy^{Hc} \prod_e(2\sin\pi x_e)^{-c_e}. \qquad\text{(15)} Thus (13) is precisely the exponentiated stationary equation exp⁡(Dc𝒮)=1\exp(D_c\mathcal S)=1. At a chosen solution, this means Dc𝒮∈2πiℤD_c\mathcal S\in2\pi i\mathbb Z, not automatically zero. Those integers are additive on the cycle lattice.

For completeness they can be represented by an integer vector r∈ℤEr\in\mathbb Z^E. Indeed ker⁡ℤC\ker_{\mathbb Z}C is primitive: its quotient in ℤE\mathbb Z^E is the subgroup im⁡ℤC\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb Z}C of a free abelian group, and is therefore free. Hence every integer functional on the kernel extends to ℤE\mathbb Z^E. Replacing (14) by 𝒮−2πir𝖳x\mathcal S-2\pi i\,r^{\mathsf T}x makes all its tangent derivatives vanish at that solution. This is the usual integer Fourier ambiguity, retained explicitly here.

The equations ∏e∋vye=−1\prod_{e\ni v}y_e=-1 fix CxCx only modulo even integers. The actual source uses the lift Cx=𝟏Cx=\mathbf1 and the specified finite support. Neither that lift nor the contribution of a stationary point is selected by (13) alone.

5. What this contributes to the geometric comparison

Equations (9) act on the exact original sum before a semiclassical approximation. Equations (13) are a fixed algebraic object obtained from them, and (14)–(15) check its relation to the angle action and full quadratic phase. This makes the geometric comparison a comparison of explicit equations rather than an assertion that concavity must imply knot-group relations.

The joint-phase note supplies a further comparison: its independent clock equations eliminate rationally, and the remaining regular equations have the original physical crossing differential. The earlier marked-braid notes already identify that physical correspondence with meridian representations. This avoids treating the classical representation comparison as a newly discovered general theorem. It does not turn coefficient rigidity into a nonzero contribution under the original evaluation. Theorems 1–2 prove no volume limit or finite-exception theorem.

The general use of hypergeometric state sums and difference equations is established territory. Coverage references include Garoufalidis–Lê, The colored Jones function is q-holonomic and Garoufalidis, On the characteristic and deformation varieties of a knot. No proof from those papers is used above. The derivation here checks the precise finite root-of-unity support, its boundary cancellations, and the actual prescribed phase; it makes no novelty claim for the general difference-equation method.
