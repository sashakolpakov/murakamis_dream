A general A-operator growth bound with the cyclic source retained

Research note, 27 October 2026. This gives a one-sided exponential estimate for the original pointed period, under explicit conditions on a specified actual quantum A-operator. The proof allows eigenvalue collisions, nonnormal matrices and exactly vanishing leading columns. It uses the finite jet source, rather than assuming a regular growing solution of a classical recurrence. There is no bridge-number bound.

The estimate is an upper bound, not the volume conjecture. Its classical largest-root integral can be strictly larger than the selected period’s rate. Sections 6–7 make that limitation explicit.

1. The Bloch boundary relation forces roots of unity at a leading edge

This general algebraic property already follows from our marked Bloch relation; no published K-theory proof is needed here. On the function field of a geometric gluing curve, or a finite augmented cover of its peripheral curve, the existing calculation gives

sum_j epsilon_j z_j wedge (1-z_j)=-2 M wedge L
                             in wedge^2(F^* tensor Q). (1.1)

For a discrete valuation v and units after uniformizer extraction, define the multiplicative residue

tau_v(f,g)=(-1)^(v(f)v(g))
            [f^v(g)/g^v(f)]_residue.                (1.2)

It is multiplicative in each input, and alternating after tensoring the target with Q. Directly tau_v(z,1-z)=1: when v(z)>0 the residue of 1-z is 1; when v(1-z)>0 that of z is 1; when v(z)<0 the quotient z/(1-z) has residue -1, and the two sign exponents sum to v(z)^2+v(z), which is even. The remaining unit case is immediate. Thus (1.2) applied to (1.1) makes tau_v(M,L) torsion.

At a place where M has finite nonzero residue M_0 and L has a pole, this says that M_0^v(L) is a root of unity. Hence M_0 is a root of unity. If the valuation is first defined on the peripheral curve, extend it to the finite gluing field; the valuation order is multiplied by its positive ramification index and the same conclusion follows. This keeps the marked lifts.

Every nonzero root of the leading L coefficient of a geometric A-factor gives a point (M_0,infinity) of its projective closure. A place on the normalization above that point has v(M)=0 and v(L)<0, so the preceding argument applies. The same conclusion holds in the squared meridian Y=M^2. Thus that leading coefficient has only roots of unity in C^*, apart from its Laurent monomial. This is the leading-edge part of the usual temperedness property.

This proof concerns the geometric peripheral factor supported by (1.1). It does not identify a given actual quantum operator’s classical symbol with that factor, remove additional operator factors, or prove a quantum deformation is unramified.

2. Conditions for a source-preserving growth estimate

Use a specified even-meridian actual all-level annihilator

A=sum_(i=0)^d a_i(q,Y) T^i, T Y=q^2 Y T,
Y=M^2, a_i in C[q^(+/-1),Y^(+/-1)].                 (2.1)

Even descent for the original state was proved in the signed norm note. Clear denominators and remove a common power of q-1. Assume:

  1. a_0(1,Y) and p(1,Y), p=a_d, are nonzero Laurent polynomials.
  2. Every zero of p(1,Y) on |Y|=1 is a root of unity.
  3. Near each such zero zeta the zero divisor of p(q,Y) splits into analytic branches Y=lambda_r(q), lambda_r(1)=zeta, with multiplicity allowed. Its local remaining factor is a unit.

The third condition holds for simple classical roots, and for products of the usual binomials 1-zeta q^a Y^b. It is a genuine condition: a ramified Puiseux branch is not silently treated as analytic. Roots away from the unit circle require no such condition. The theorem does not claim these conditions for every knot’s unknown minimal operator. They are directly checkable operator data.

Let A_cl(Y,L)=sum a_i(1,Y)L^i and, outside the finite leading-zero set, let

R(theta)=max{|L|:A_cl(exp(i theta),L)=0},
E(A)=integral_0^(2pi) log R(theta) dtheta.           (2.2)

The logarithmic singularities at the excluded finite set are integrable, as shown in Section 5. No selected source is replaced by a choice of the largest root in the definition of the invariant.

Pointed growth theorem. Under conditions 1–3, the original Kashaev value recovered from this actual operator satisfies

limsup_(N->infinity) (2pi/N)log|<K>_N| <= E(A).      (2.3)

Zero values on the left are interpreted as log 0=-infinity. For the positive pointed Gram period of the previous note,

limsup_(N->infinity) (pi/N)log G_N <= max(0,E(A)).    (2.4)

We prove these statements below. Neither is an equality assertion.

3. A fixed-size positive transport keeps every singular step

Use the exact generic color recurrence and bounded q-jet budget B from the pointed Gram construction. Write V_j=(C_j,…,C_(j+d-1))^t. Before specializing any root,

p(q,q^(2j)) V_(j+1)=N_j(q) V_j,                    (3.1)

where N_j has p on its superdiagonal and last row (-a_0,…,-a_(d-1)), evaluated at Y=q^(2j). Put q=q_N(1+t) and use the scaled variable s=N t. Let K_(N,j) be the lower Toeplitz lift of N_j(q_N(1+s/N)) through s-degree B. Its size is d(B+1), independent of N, and its entries are uniformly bounded.

Let m_j be the actual order of p(q,q^(2j)) at q_N and define

eta_j=[s^m_j]p(q_N(1+s/N),q_N^(2j)(1+s/N)^(2j)),
beta_N=sum_(j=j_*)^(N-d)m_j <= B.                   (3.2)

The fixed small-color jet column u_N is bounded. Multiplying (3.1) in the generic field and only then taking the indicated coefficient gives the exact formula

<K>_N=e_(beta_N,d)^t
   K_(N,N-d)...K_(N,j_*)u_N / product_j eta_j.       (3.3)

Indeed the unlifted numerator is (product_j p_j)V_(N-d+1). The product has first nonzero s-coefficient product_j eta_j in degree beta_N, and the color column is regular. Thus (3.3) does not divide by a vanishing specialized leading coefficient.

There is an exact positive transport of its squared norm. With rho_0=u_N u_N^dagger propagate

rho_(j+1)=K_(N,j) rho_j K_(N,j)^dagger,
|<K>_N|^2=Tr(E_(beta_N,d)rho_final)
                                  /product_j |eta_j|^2. (3.4)

The last effect is its specified rank-one coordinate projector. All phases and jet couplings remain in the matrices and source. This is a positive matrix transport, not an entrywise-positive recurrence or a license to drop the source. The denominator is the same whole leading germ used in the original A-period.

4. The leading germ has a controlled logarithmic product

Under conditions 2–3 one has

sum_j log|eta_j|=N I_p+O(log N),
I_p=(1/(2pi))integral_0^(2pi)log|p(1,exp(i theta))|dtheta.
                                                    (4.1)

Here is a proof retaining exact root zeros. Away from a fixed neighborhood of the classical unit-circle zeros, p(q_N,Y)/p(1,Y) is uniformly 1+O(1/N). Near a zero zeta, each analytic root branch moves by O(1/N). At grid points with distance at least K/N from zeta, choose K large enough so the root displacement is less than half that distance. Factoring locally gives a log-modulus error O(1/(N distance)). Summation over these points costs O(log N).

Only a bounded number of points lie within K/N of zeta. Because zeta is a root of unity, write zeta=exp(2pi i a/r). On each residue class of N modulo r, every such point is

j=aN/r+k, Y_j=zeta q_N^(2k),                      (4.2)

where k is in a fixed finite set of rational numbers; use both endpoint representations a/r=0,1 when zeta=1. Its ordinary value is a fixed analytic germ p(q,zeta q^(2k)) at q=1. Unless identically zero, that germ has a finite order, so its nonzero value at q_N is bounded above and below by powers of N.

For an exactly zero value use the q-jet along the actual color trajectory. A coefficient of fixed jet degree is a finite sum of terms

c zeta^b q_N^(u+2bk) N^(-m)
                   binom(u+2b(aN/r+k),m).           (4.3)

Here u is the q-exponent of the monomial and b its Y-exponent. Formula (4.3) is meromorphic in 1/N with a finite-order pole or zero. The finite-monomial Vandermonde bound from the jet theorem bounds the first nonzero degree m. For each of the finitely many residue classes and offsets, that leading coefficient is therefore bounded above and below by powers of N for all sufficiently large N. If all bounded-degree germs vanished identically, their evaluations would violate that Vandermonde bound on every large actual trajectory. Thus exact zeros, including coalescing analytic branches, cost only O(log N) at this bounded number of points. The fixed discarded initial and final colors have the same control.

Finally the classical grid product itself has the claimed mean. Factor p(1,Y) over C. Off-circle roots contribute N times their circle mean plus O(1), by the identity product_(Y^N=1)(Y-lambda) =(-1)N(lambdaN-1). A unit-circle root of unity either has zeta^N!=1, giving a nonzero value in a finite set, or is a grid root. In the latter case omitting its zero factor gives modulus N by differentiating Y^N-1 at zeta. Multiplicities and the fixed removed points add O(log N). Combining these facts proves (4.1).

The analytic-branch condition is used exactly in the O(1/N) displacement above. Classical temperedness alone does not prove it.

In particular, if p(1,Y) is a Laurent monomial times cyclotomic factors with leading scalar of modulus one, then I_p=0. The entire product of leading germs is then bounded above and below by powers of N. Individual factors may still vanish or become numerical multipliers at a root. Their complete germs, rather than those separate numerical values, give this conclusion.

5. A direct upper bound for slowly varying matrix products

We need only the following elementary norm lemma. Suppose bounded matrices K_(N,j) tend uniformly to a continuous matrix K(x), x=j/N, of fixed dimension. If log rho(K(x)) is integrable, then

limsup (1/N)log||product_j K_(N,j)||
                   <=integral_0^1 log rho(K(x)) dx. (5.1)

For completeness first fix epsilon>0 and set a(x)=rho(K(x))+epsilon. The matrix

H(x)=sum_(n>=0)(K(x)^dagger)^n K(x)^n/a(x)^(2n)

converges uniformly, is continuous, satisfies H>=I and is bounded above on the compact interval. Uniform convergence follows, for example, by unitary triangularization: bounded upper triangular matrices of fixed size have power growth bounded by a fixed polynomial times (rho+epsilon/2)^n, uniformly with constants depending on epsilon and the dimension. Its induced norm obeys ||K(x)v||(H(x))<=a(x)||v||(H(x)).

Uniform convergence of K_(N,j) and uniform continuity of H make each step cost at most a(j/N)+o(1), and the change between the two neighboring H norms costs 1+o(1), uniformly. Multiplying, taking logarithms, and dividing by N yields integral log(rho+epsilon). Let epsilon decrease to zero. In the present application integrability and the passage to that limit follow from the polynomial lower bound on rho stated below. No diagonalization through an eigenvalue collision and no spectral-gap hypothesis enters this upper bound.

For our lifted matrices the limit K(x) is lower triangular in jet degree, with the same diagonal block at every degree:

N_0(x)=[0 p 0 ...; ...; -a_0 -a_1 ... -a_(d-1)]
                                 at q=1,Y=exp(2pi i x).

Therefore rho(K(x))=rho(N_0(x)). Where p!=0 its eigenvalues are p(1,Y)L_i(Y), so

log rho(K(x))=log|p(1,Y)|+log R(2pi x).             (5.2)

Both sides are integrable. Indeed the characteristic coefficients of N_0 are a_i p^(d-1-i), and a_0 p^(d-1) is nonzero by condition 1. Their finite-order zeros give a polynomial lower bound on rho near its finitely many possible zeros. This justifies the limiting norm argument as well as the integral in (2.2).

Apply (5.1) to (3.3). The input is bounded, the selected coordinate has norm one, and (4.1) subtracts I_p from the growth rate. Equation (5.2) gives (2.3). The polynomial ordinary-color remainder in the Gram theorem then gives (2.4). This proves the pointed upper bound.

6. The source prevents turning this bound into a volume formula

An exact countercheck is the unknot with the actual annihilator

(T-c)(T-1), c>1,
C_j(q)=1 for every color.

All conditions above hold. The entropy is E=2pi log c, but the selected period is identically one and has zero rate. Its source is entirely in the L=1 line; the positive evolution (3.4) retains this fact. The same left multiplication can be applied to an actual hyperbolic knot operator and can enlarge E arbitrarily without changing its pointed period. Thus source reachability and a sharp estimate cannot be inferred from root moduli alone.

Even full classical temperedness does not fix this source issue. Let C(Y,L) be the figure-eight geometric polynomial displayed below, and form the dummy left factor C(Y,T). The operator C(Y,T)(T-1) is an actual annihilator of the unknot’s constant period. Its classical symbol is (L-1)C(Y,L), whose edge polynomials are cyclotomic, yet its selected period is still one. This is not claimed to be a standard quantum figure-eight operator; it is an exact countercheck on any argument using only the tempered characteristic polynomial and omitting the actual source.

There is also a useful geometric calibration. For the figure-eight geometric polynomial in Y,L,

A_geo=L^2-(Y^2-Y-2-Y^(-1)+Y^(-2))L+1,

put c(theta)=1+cos theta-cos(2theta). Then log R is arcosh c(theta) on (0,2pi/3) and (4pi/3,2pi), and is zero on the remaining arc. Hence

E(A_geo)=2 integral_0^(2pi/3)arcosh c(theta)dtheta
        =2 Vol(4_1).                               (6.1)

The last equality is the already calibrated classical cone/regulator comparison (historical context omitted), not an identification of that note’s custom transfer state with Kashaev. This factor of two shows why the full-circle largest-root integral is not the desired selected volume. The quantum boundary and resonance data must choose the appropriate action; (2.3) does not prove that selection or its lower bound.

7. Coverage and remaining scope

The leading-edge root-of-unity statement has existing coverage in Cooper–Culler–Gillet–Long–Shalen, Proposition 5.10. The direct residue proof in Section 1 uses only our recorded Bloch boundary relation. Existing regular q-difference asymptotics include Garoufalidis–Geronimo, whose abstract specifies noncollision and nonvanishing assumptions. Only their stated scope was used for coverage; none of their proofs is a premise for Sections 3–5.

The new step relative to the previous repo notes is a one-sided estimate for the actual pointed A-period that retains the complete cyclic leading germ, a bounded number of singular jets, and its fixed source, while allowing eigenvalue collisions. It requires the explicit leading-divisor hypotheses in Section 2. It neither proves those hypotheses for every knot operator nor proves a sharp geometric rate. Identifying and estimating the source-selected geometric action is still the central unresolved part of the volume conjecture.
