Circle traces and leaf coefficients near the complete meridian

A crossing circle contributes its ordinary quantum character, and an adjacent half-twist contributes the physical crossing operator. This note studies their trace, and the leaf coefficients obtained by a quarter-turn, while the main meridian varies near completeness. The formulas keep the crossing scalar, the ordinary-circle pivot, and the orientation-dependent quarter-turn. It proves an exact formula for pure leaves, and a relative asymptotic for half-twisted leaves on any compact interval of regular low Newton indices. In particular the moving leaf ratio is derived from its finite source, rather than inferred from the bounded turn multiplier. The Newton compiler fixes the orientation spaces and cut pairing; the turn comparison fixes the coordinate formula used below. Section 10 constructs the canonical Gamma interpolation needed in the grouped wire packet. The direct crossing trace then treats arbitrary fixed crossing powers. A relative profile for a whole knot requires the additional global comparisons in the filled-volume theorem.

8. Scope of the coefficient statements

The exact pure identity holds for every physical auxiliary color. The half-twisted complete-color theorem (20)–(22) holds for every compact regular low-index interval and every main disk R<1R<1. The joint small-deficit continuation (24)–(35) is proved on R<1/2R<1/2; it agrees exactly with every physical color on its grid and has an exponential relative grid-shift error. The statements are all-level, with the original parity, crossing and typed-turn factors intact.

The proof does not control the entire coefficient range or supply a deformed global source lower bound. It also does not compare a filled invariant to its own smaller volume. The estimates through Section 9 are uniform over the physical integer low-index window. Section 10 supplies a specified analytic factorial-and-integral continuation in the complex wire coordinate, with exponential agreement on that physical grid.
