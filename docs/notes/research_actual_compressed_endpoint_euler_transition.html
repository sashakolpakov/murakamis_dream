An analytic Euler transition on actual endpoint head blocks

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

After explicit factorial endpoint gauges, a compressed literal crossing has a uniformly bounded transfer generator. Its entire finite Euler polynomial converges in operator norm to the exponential of that generator, with error O(N−1)O(N^{-1}). The inverse has the corresponding estimate. This is an actual finite-matrix transition, not substitution of commuting symbols into a formal Euler series.

A single quadratic zero permits a head collar of width O(N1/2)O(N^{1/2}). Two quadratic zeros permit two collars of width O(N3/4)O(N^{3/4}). Every collar is a single interval. Monotonicity proves the exact compression identity even when a transfer can exit a collar. All complementary blocks remain source currents, with no smallness claim. The finite Fourier projector and the original quantum/meridian normal rows are retained.

The complete directs are B, the literal balanced crossing; E, the actual endpoint factorial chart; and V, the common two-wall module. Their pins are below. The new analytic estimates and compression proof are given in full here. No neighboring-network packet hypothesis is inferred.

Away from the ordinary-color path they are auxiliary finite matrices, not a generic finite-dimensional Hopf representation. V retains their exact Hopf defect.

3. The two exact endpoint gauges

Define diagonal matrices, all regular and invertible on (9), by GFwa=Fa(Q)wa,GEwa=Ha(Q,M)−1wa,Ha=∏j=1aαj,H0=1.(14) G_F w_a=F_a(Q)w_a,\qquad G_E w_a=H_a(Q,M)^{-1}w_a,\qquad H_a=\prod_{j=1}^a\alpha_j,\quad H_0=1. \qquad\text{(14)} Direct coefficient calculation gives GF−1FGF=S,GF−1ℰGFwa=βawa−1,βa=(1−Qa)αa,(15) G_F^{-1}FG_F=S,\qquad G_F^{-1}\mathcal E G_F\,w_a=\beta_a w_{a-1}, \quad \beta_a=(1-Q^a)\alpha_a, \qquad\text{(15)} where SS is the physical unilateral raising matrix, zero at dd. Similarly GE−1ℰGE=S*,GE−1FGEwa=βa+1wa+1(a<d),(16) G_E^{-1}\mathcal E G_E=S^*,\qquad G_E^{-1}FG_E\,w_a=\beta_{a+1}w_{a+1}\quad(a<d), \qquad\text{(16)} and the last arrow is still zero. No factor at index NN is inverted or used to replace that forced zero.

At M=1,h=hNM=1,h=h_N, βa=(1−Qa)2Qa=−4sin⁡2(πa/N),Ha=Q−a(a+1)/2Fa.(17) \beta_a=\frac{(1-Q^a)^2}{Q^a}=-4\sin^2(\pi a/N), \qquad H_a=Q^{-a(a+1)/2}F_a. \qquad\text{(17)} Thus each gauge has E’s endpoint condition-number bound, up to reversal: cond⁡(G|I)≤N(eNR)2R(18) \operatorname{cond}(G|_I) \le N\left(\frac{eN}{R}\right)^{2R} \qquad\text{(18)} for an interval I=[0,R]I=[0,R] or [d−R,d][d-R,d], R≤d/2R\le d/2; interpret the bracket as one for R=0R=0. In particular every collar R=o(N)R=o(N) has subexponential gauge cost.

The same conclusion holds uniformly on (9), with an additional fixed power of NN. Each factor relative to its root value differs from one by at most Cη/min⁡(j,N−j)C\eta/\min(j,N-j); it stays in a fixed disk away from zero. Summing its log-modulus over j<Nj<N costs at most Clog⁡NC\log N. This proves the extra polynomial bound for both the gauges and their inverses, including upper collars. Their fixed normal derivatives have the usual further polynomial cost by Cauchy’s formula on smaller bidisks.

One may use a globally tame change of head basis throughout the original network. For each chosen interval set G̃=GPI+(I−PI).(18a) \widetilde G=G P_I+(I-P_I). \qquad\text{(18a)} E’s separate bounds for the gauge and its reciprocal, and the factor-ratio argument above, give ∥G̃∥∥G̃−1∥≤NC(eNR)2R.(18b) \|\widetilde G\|\,\|\widetilde G^{-1}\| \le N^{C}\left(\frac{eN}{R}\right)^{2R}. \qquad\text{(18b)} This is subexponential for R=o(N)R=o(N). The internal interval arrows are exactly (15) or (16); interface arrows use their literal two endpoint gauge factors. The compressed operators below are therefore the same whether one uses GG or G̃\widetilde G. No exponentially conditioned full-head change is needed to obtain them.

All changes are exact source changes of basis: neighboring tensors carry the two inverse factors, exposed rows and columns transform, and diagonal pivots commute with the gauges. The transformation is not an equality of two independently reset boundary probes.

4. Monotone compression retains the exact cutoff

Let I,JI,J be single intervals of integer head indices and let P=PI⊗PJP=P_I\otimes P_J. In any of the diagonal gauges above, AA moves (a,b)(a,b) to (a−1,b+1)(a-1,b+1), or is zero. If its initial and final indices lie in I×JI\times J, every intermediate index does. Therefore, exactly, PAnP=(PAP)non im⁡P.(19) P A^n P=(PAP)^n\quad\text{on }\operatorname{im}P. \qquad\text{(19)} This remains true when a transfer can exit the interval: it cannot return. Hence, writing T=PAP/hT=PAP/h on that image, PΘN(A)P=ΘN(hT),PΘN(A)−1P=ΘN(hT)−1.(20) P\Theta_N(A)P=\Theta_N(hT),\qquad P\Theta_N(A)^{-1}P=\Theta_N(hT)^{-1}. \qquad\text{(20)} The two compressed factors are exact inverses, not merely asymptotic ones. The union of an upper and a lower collar is not a single interval and is not covered by (19); its cross-collar blocks remain.

There are two useful bounded-generator regimes. Constants in the following bounds may depend on the fixed collar constants. Every collar width also satisfies 0≤Ri≤⌊d/2⌋0\le R_i\le\lfloor d/2\rfloor:

  • One quadratic zero. Use GFG_F on the donor, leave the recipient balanced, and take the donor interval to be a lower or upper collar of width R1≤C1N1/2R_1\le C_1N^{1/2}. The recipient interval may be any physical interval, including the whole head. On (9), ∥T∥≤C(R1+1)2/N≤C′.(21) \|T\|\le C(R_1+1)^2/N\le C'. \qquad\text{(21)}
  • Two quadratic zeros. Use GFG_F on the donor and GEG_E on the recipient. Let both intervals be lower or upper collars, of widths Ri≤CiN3/4R_i\le C_iN^{3/4}. Then ∥T∥≤C(R1+1)2(R2+1)2/N3≤C′.(22) \|T\|\le C(R_1+1)^2(R_2+1)^2/N^3\le C'. \qquad\text{(22)}

For the proof, on either donor collar |βa|≤C(R1+1)2/N2|\beta_a|\le C(R_1+1)^2/N^2, and on either recipient collar |βb+1|≤C(R2+1)2/N2|\beta_{b+1}|\le C(R_2+1)^2/N^2. These estimates follow directly from (9) and (15)–(17), including the actual zero endpoint arrows. The unchanged balanced FF has norm bounded by an absolute constant on (9). A weighted transfer is an injective partial shift of basis vectors, so its norm is the maximum coefficient. Finally |h|−1≤CN|h|^{-1}\le CN. This proves (21)–(22).

Apply (13) to obtain the claimed uniform exponential transitions and uniform inverses on all these compressed blocks. For a checked positive crossing the output projector is 𝖯P𝖯\mathsf P P\mathsf P. The exact remaining factor is κ𝖯D\kappa\mathsf P D. At the physical complete root it is unitary in the gauged coordinate norm, since its diagonal entries have modulus one. Thus the corresponding compressed actual checked block is uniformly invertible as a map between these two projected spaces. The negative block, with the input/output spaces exchanged, is exactly its inverse. Away from that fiber the entire D,κD,\kappa factors are retained; no normal of them is omitted by the Euler estimate.

5. Finite projectors, outgoing blocks and normal rows

The gauge is diagonal and does not change PIP_I. Its exact root Fourier kernel is (PI)jkFourier=1N∑a=LUQa(k−j).(23) (P_I)_{jk}^{\mathrm{Fourier}} =\frac1N\sum_{a=L}^{U}Q^{a(k-j)}. \qquad\text{(23)} Keeping the same unwrapped ratio tt gives the finite polynomial 1NtLKU−L(t),KR(t)=∑a=0Rta,KR(1)=R+1.(24) \frac1N t^L K_{U-L}(t),\qquad K_R(t)=\sum_{a=0}^R t^a,\qquad K_R(1)=R+1. \qquad\text{(24)} Both endpoints, and the upper-collar power td−Rt^{d-R}, remain. No delta-function matching or independent cancellation of the two apparent 1−t1-t poles is part of (13).

The full crossing is not replaced by its compressed block. Its four P,(I−P)P,(I-P) blocks are kept exactly. In particular the unestimated outgoing/incoming currents can be written without changing a coefficient: [ΘN(A),P]=∑n=1d(−1)nqn2Fn(Q)∑j=0n−1Aj[A,P]An−1−j.(25) [\Theta_N(A),P] =\sum_{n=1}^{d}\frac{(-1)^nq^{n^2}}{F_n(Q)} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^j[A,P]A^{n-1-j}. \qquad\text{(25)} The elementary [A,P][A,P] is supported on the interval-edge arrows; the surrounding powers can transport it far outside the collar. The actual head-normal rows also remain. This gives a regular full normal-germ prescription, without erasing either wall or their mixed normal.

There is also a quantitative fixed-jet statement for the compressed transition itself. On a smaller concentric bidisk, (13) and Cauchy’s formula imply for fixed j,ℓ≥0j,\ell\ge0 ∥∂hj∂Mℓ(ΘN(hTN)−eTN)∥≤Cj,ℓN2j+ℓ−1,(28) \left\|\partial_h^j\partial_M^\ell \bigl(\Theta_N(hT_N)-e^{T_N}\bigr)\right\| \le C_{j,\ell}N^{2j+\ell-1}, \qquad\text{(28)} and the same assertion for the inverse. The matrices TNT_N, including their gauges, are differentiated as whole analytic functions. Thus the error is O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) for the corresponding scaled normals. The genuine ordinary-color path M=−eNh/2M=-e^{Nh/2} lies in (9) on a smaller hh-disk of radius proportional to N−2N^{-2}. Restricting the exact identities first therefore retains its actual fixed-NN quantum normals. A fixed-MM auxiliary array is not declared a generic representation.

6. The scalar Poisson profile requires an explicit packet

The paid unconditional conclusion is the operator eTNe^{T_N} with the original finite projection. A scalar eξe^\xi requires more. For example suppose a normalized incoming packet vNv_N lies in im⁡P\operatorname{im}P, |ξN|≤R|\xi_N|\le R, and satisfies ∥(TN−ξN)vN∥≤εN.(29) \|(T_N-\xi_N)v_N\|\le\varepsilon_N. \qquad\text{(29)} Then the integral identity for the difference of two exponentials and the uniform bound for TNT_N give ∥ΘN(hTN)vN−eξNvN∥≤CR(εN+N−1).(30) \|\Theta_N(hT_N)v_N-e^{\xi_N}v_N\| \le C_R(\varepsilon_N+N^{-1}). \qquad\text{(30)} This is a precise sufficient packet hypothesis. It is not assumed for the original neighboring tangle, and (30) does not control the discarded-output blocks in (25).

The quadratic and quartic geometric contacts motivate the collar scales in (21)–(22); the proof itself uses only the literal matrix coefficients and the displayed width conditions. It does not choose which geometric endpoint, normal contour or relative framing interval occurs in the original global state. Uniform invertibility of this local compressed transition is not a lower bound for the knot scalar.
