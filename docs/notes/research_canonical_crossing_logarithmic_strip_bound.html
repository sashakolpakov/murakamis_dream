A logarithmic strip bound for every canonical crossing power

Private written proof, 23 November 2026.

Every signed integer power of the original crossing has a canonical polynomial whose real-boundary norm is logarithmic in the level, with only linear dependence on the absolute power. The same estimate controls its whole periodic microscopic strip and every fixed parameter derivative. Both level parities and all first physical normals are retained.

This improves a polynomial evaluation bound for the actual finite polynomial. It does not estimate a genuine turn or an original closing from below, and supplies no new volume-identity case.

1. The complete original data

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, and the recorded phases q=eπi/N,Q=q2,κ=eπid2/(2N),PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2. q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad \kappa=e^{\pi i d^2/(2N)},\quad P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2. For every n∈ℤn\in\mathbb Z, let rn,Nr_{n,N} be the canonical degree-below-NN representative of κ−nRn\kappa^{-n}R^n in the original full algebra ℂ[z]/PN\mathbb C[z]/P_N. Negative powers mean the actual inverse. Put tj=(2j+1)π/N,cj=2cos⁡tj,νj=(−1)jqj(j+1),g(t)=rn,N(2cos⁡t). t_j=(2j+1)\pi/N,\quad c_j=2\cos t_j,\quad \nu_j=(-1)^jq^{j(j+1)},\quad g(t)=r_{n,N}(2\cos t). The complete source [F] gives exactly hj:=g(tj)=νjn,γj:=g′(tj)=−in2(d−2j)hj(0≤j<N).(1) h_j:=g(t_j)=\nu_j^n,\qquad \gamma_j:=g'(t_j)=-\frac{in}{2}(d-2j)h_j \quad(0\le j<N). \qquad\text{(1)} Thus |hj|=1|h_j|=1, |γj|≤|n|N/2|\gamma_j|\le |n|N/2, and ∑jγj=0\sum_j\gamma_j=0. For the last assertion pair j,d−jj,d-j: their values agree and their derivatives are opposite. At odd NN the self-paired node is −2-2 and its angular derivative is zero. This imposes no additional spectral normal at that simple node.

Define MN,n=1+|n|{π2+4π(1+logN)}.(2) M_{N,n}=1+|n|\left\{\frac\pi2+ \frac4\pi(1+\log N)\right\}. \qquad\text{(2)} Theorem. Uniformly for all N≥2N\ge2 and all integers nn, max−2≤z≤2|rn,N(z)|≤MN,n.(3) \boxed{\quad\max_{-2\le z\le2}|r_{n,N}(z)|\le M_{N,n}.\quad} \qquad\text{(3)} For wN(α)=2cosh⁡(α/N)w_N(\alpha)=2\cosh(\alpha/N), every r≥0r\ge0 and integer k≥0k\ge0 also satisfy sup|ℜα|≤r|rn,N(wN(α))|≤erMN,n, \sup_{|\Re\alpha|\le r} |r_{n,N}(w_N(\alpha))|\le e^rM_{N,n}, sup|ℜα|≤r|∂αkrn,N(wN(α))|≤k!er+1MN,n.(4) \boxed{\quad \sup_{|\Re\alpha|\le r} |\partial_\alpha^k r_{n,N}(w_N(\alpha))| \le k!e^{r+1}M_{N,n}. \quad} \qquad\text{(4)} There is no restriction on the imaginary height in these suprema. The unnormalized crossing has the retained scalar κn\kappa^n, of modulus one, so its absolute bounds are identical.

2. Exact trigonometric Hermite interpolation

For δ=t−tj\delta=t-t_j, define the removable trigonometric polynomials Kj(t)=1N2{sin⁡(Nδ/2)sin⁡(δ/2)}2,Lj(t)=2N2sin⁡2(Nδ/2)cot⁡(δ/2).(5) K_j(t)=\frac1{N^2} \left\{\frac{\sin(N\delta/2)}{\sin(\delta/2)}\right\}^2, \qquad L_j(t)=\frac2{N^2}\sin^2(N\delta/2)\cot(\delta/2). \qquad\text{(5)} The finite geometric series gives Kj(t)=1N2∑ℓ=−(N−1)N−1(N−|ℓ|)eiℓ(t−tj), K_j(t)=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{\ell=-(N-1)}^{N-1} (N-|\ell|)e^{i\ell(t-t_j)}, Lj(t)=1N2{sin(Nδ)+2∑ℓ=1N−1sin(ℓδ)}.(6) L_j(t)=\frac1{N^2}\left\{\sin(N\delta) +2\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\sin(\ell\delta)\right\}. \qquad\text{(6)} At the grid, Kj(tk)=δjkK_j(t_k)=\delta_{jk}, Kj′(tk)=0K_j'(t_k)=0, Lj(tk)=0L_j(t_k)=0, and Lj′(tk)=δjkL_j'(t_k)=\delta_{jk}. The apparent singularity at δ=0\delta=0 gives these same limits. For real tt, Kj(t)≥0K_j(t)\ge0, and finite character summation in (6) gives ∑jKj(t)=1\sum_jK_j(t)=1.

The exact original polynomial is g(t)=∑jhjKj(t)+∑jγjLj(t).(7) g(t)=\sum_j h_jK_j(t)+\sum_j\gamma_jL_j(t). \qquad\text{(7)} Indeed its right side matches all values and angular derivatives. Its possible frequency-NN term vanishes: since sin⁡(N(t−tj))=−sin⁡Nt\sin(N(t-t_j))=-\sin Nt, its coefficient is a multiple of ∑jγj=0\sum_j\gamma_j=0. It therefore has trigonometric degree at most N−1N-1, like gg. Their difference, multiplied by ei(N−1)te^{i(N-1)t}, is a polynomial in eite^{it} of degree at most 2N−22N-2, with double zeros at the NN distinct grid points. It vanishes identically. This argument includes even middle aliases and the odd simple spectral node through precisely the data (1).

3. The logarithmic norm and the whole strip

Fix real tt, choose a closest grid point, and let its circular distance be a≤π/Na\le\pi/N. All numerators sin⁡2(N(t−tj)/2)\sin^2(N(t-t_j)/2) in (5) agree. The closest contribution obeys |Lj(t)|≤2N2N2a242a=a≤πN. |L_j(t)|\le\frac2{N^2}\frac{N^2a^2}{4}\frac2a=a \le\frac\pi N. At a=0a=0 use the removable limit. The other points at circular distance kk grid steps have distance at least (2k−1)π/N(2k-1)\pi/N, with at most two points at each step. Using |cot⁡(δ/2)|≤2/|δ||\cot(\delta/2)|\le2/|\delta| for 0<|δ|≤π0<|\delta|\le\pi, and bounding the odd harmonic sum by HN≤1+log⁡NH_N\le1+\log N, gives ∑j|Lj(t)|≤1N{π+8π(1+logN)}.(8) \sum_j|L_j(t)|\le\frac1N \left\{\pi+\frac8\pi(1+\log N)\right\}. \qquad\text{(8)} The value part of (7) has modulus at most one by positivity and partition of unity. Combine (1) and (8) to prove (3).

For completeness, if a polynomial pp has degree at most dd and real-interval norm MM, then H(ζ)=ζdp(ζ+ζ−1)H(\zeta)=\zeta^dp(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}) is a polynomial bounded by MM on the unit circle. The maximum principle in its disk, and replacement of ζ\zeta by ζ−1\zeta^{-1}, give |p(ζ+ζ−1)|≤Mmax⁡(|ζ|,|ζ|−1)d. |p(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})|\le M\max(|\zeta|,|\zeta|^{-1})^d. Take ζ=eα/N\zeta=e^{\alpha/N} and d=N−1d=N-1 to obtain the first bound in (4). Cauchy’s formula on a unit disk about each α\alpha, using the strip of width r+1r+1, proves the derivative bound. These are fixed-root parameter derivatives, not moving-qq normals.

4. The logarithmic factor is needed uniformly over all powers

Take the actual level-dependent integer power n=2Nn=2N. Its data are hj=1h_j=1 and bj=γj/i=−N(d−2j)b_j=\gamma_j/i=-N(d-2j). For 1≤ℓ<N1\le\ell<N, the exact Fourier–Hermite formula [F] says r2N,N(z)=1+∑ℓ=1N−12aℓTℓ(z/2),aℓ=1N2∑j=0N−1bje−iℓtj=isin⁡(ℓπ/N).(9) r_{2N,N}(z)=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}2a_\ell T_\ell(z/2),\qquad a_\ell=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}b_j e^{-i\ell t_j} =\frac{i}{\sin(\ell\pi/N)}. \qquad\text{(9)} To check the last equality, put s=Q−ℓ≠1s=Q^{-\ell}\ne1. The finite geometric series and its derivative give ∑jsj=0\sum_js^j=0, ∑jjsj=−N/(1−s)\sum_jjs^j=-N/(1-s); substitution in (9) gives −2q−ℓ/(1−Q−ℓ)=i/sin⁡(ℓπ/N)-2q^{-\ell}/(1-Q^{-\ell})=i/\sin(\ell\pi/N). Consequently, at the endpoint itself, r2N,N(2)=1+2i∑ℓ=1N−1csc⁡(ℓπ/N),|r2N,N(2)|=4πNlog⁡N+O(N).(10) r_{2N,N}(2)=1+2i\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\csc(\ell\pi/N),\qquad |r_{2N,N}(2)|=\frac4\pi N\log N+O(N). \qquad\text{(10)} For the asymptotic, subtract 1/(πt)+1/(π(1−t))1/(\pi t)+1/(\pi(1-t)) from csc⁡πt\csc\pi t. The difference is bounded on [0,1][0,1]. Its finite sum is O(N)O(N), whereas the two subtracted sums total 2NHN−1/π2NH_{N-1}/\pi. This proves (10). Thus a bound (1+|n|)o(log⁡N)(1+|n|)o(\log N) cannot hold uniformly over all integer powers. This sharpness uses n=2Nn=2N; it is not a growth claim for a fixed short filling.

5. Full-algebra multiplication in the same norm

For a canonical polynomial ff, write M(f)=max⁡[−2,2]|f|M(f)=\max_{[-2,2]}|f|, and put AN=π+(8/π)(1+log⁡N)A_N=\pi+(8/\pi)(1+\log N). Let can\operatorname{can} mean the remainder modulo the full PNP_N, including every first normal. Then M(can⁡(fg))≤(1+2eAN)M(f)M(g),(11) M(\operatorname{can}(fg))\le(1+2eA_N)M(f)M(g), \qquad\text{(11)} and for every signed integer nn, M(can⁡(rn,Nf))≤{1+(e+|n|/2)AN}M(f).(12) M(\operatorname{can}(r_{n,N}f)) \le\{1+(e+|n|/2)A_N\}M(f). \qquad\text{(12)} Indeed the strip proof applies to every canonical ff. Cauchy’s formula on a unit disk gives |∂αf(wN(α))|≤eM(f)|\partial_\alpha f(w_N(\alpha))|\le eM(f) on the imaginary axis. With α=iNt\alpha=iNt, this implies |∂tf(2cos⁡t)|≤eNM(f)|\partial_t f(2\cos t)|\le eNM(f). The full product has nodal values bounded by M(f)M(g)M(f)M(g) and angular derivatives bounded by 2eNM(f)M(g)2eNM(f)M(g). Insert those data in (7) and use (8) to obtain (11). For (12), use the unit nodal values and the exact normal (1) instead. The angular derivatives sum to zero by reflection in both cases, including the self-paired odd simple node, so (7) is valid for the canonical full product. Quotient carries have already been retained; no semisimple projection is used.

Thus the canonical multiplication constants in this norm grow only logarithmically with NN, with linear dependence on |n||n| for an actual crossing power. This is a Banach norm estimate; it supplies no comparison with a physical Hilbert metric.

6. Gain, limits and complete source versions

The earlier circle estimate [K] gives coefficient mass 2N(1+|n|/2)2N(1+|n|/2); [F] already gives a polynomial real-boundary bound from its first-kind coefficients. The addition here is the logarithmic evaluation estimate for the canonical polynomial, its full periodic strip and derivatives, the full-product estimates (11)–(12), and the actual sharpness sequence (10). The more general free-boundary interpolation estimate [H] deliberately uses unoptimized polynomial cardinal bounds and concerns different data.

Canonical reduction and the full physical normals remain essential. The estimates do not bound the Newton norm of a multiplication operator by MN,nM_{N,n}, make a genuine turn harmless, compare its contour with an analytic representative without currents, or give a lower bound for the original cap. Arbitrary fixed short components still require their whole source and geometric comparison. No volume theorem is proved.
