Actual ideal prism cells and geometric Newton associativity

29 October 2026. Every strictly positive Newton multiplication action is the volume of an explicit convex IDEAL TRIANGULAR PRISM. Its three ordered quadrilateral faces have cross ratios given by derivatives of the actual source action. Thus internal source stationarity is exactly the isometry condition for the prescribed quadrilateral face gluing. Two stationary multiplication cells form an ideal cube; its alternative diagonal cut gives the other parenthesization. The equality holds for the full conjugate-antisymmetric pre-Bloch class, not just its regulator.

This supplies local geometric incidence and composition. It does not by itself identify every closed Conway expression with a complete hyperbolic link complement. Boundary degenerations, the global diagram identification and complete ends still require their own arguments.

1. The actual source action

Use the positive full-Newton multiplication, whose uniform action is

m(x,y,z)=L(x+y)+L(z)-L(x)-L(y)
                -L(z-x)-L(z-y)-L(x+y-z),
L(t)=integral_0^t log(2sin(pi u))du.

Work first in its STRICT domain

p=z-x>0, q=z-y>0, r=x+y-z>0, w=1-x-y>0,
p+q+2r+w=1,
x=q+r, y=p+r, 1-z=r+w.                         (1)

For positive a+b+c=1 let V(a,b,c) be the actual volume of an ideal tetrahedron with angles pi(a,b,c). The proved source identity is

pi m=V(y,x,w)+V(p,q,1-p-q)+V(p+q,r,r+w).         (2)

The source remains its original multiplication table, including its normal directions; no new state sum is being substituted here.

2. Explicit six vertices of a convex ideal prism

In the upper half-space model, use the ideal point infinity and four points on the unit circle in the boundary plane:

v0=1,
v1=exp(2pi i p),
v2=exp(2pi i(p+r)),
v3=exp(2pi i(p+r+q)).                           (3)

The consecutive half-arc lengths are p,r,q,r+w. They are all positive and sum to one. Let P be the intersection of the Euclidean lines (v0,v1) and (v2,v3). The inequalities in (1) imply that P lies beyond v1 on the ray from v0, and beyond v2 on the ray from v3. Indeed the exterior triangle (P,v1,v2) has angles

angle(v1)=pi y, angle(v2)=pi x, angle(P)=pi w.

Their sum is pi and all are positive. The elementary sine rule gives the explicit formula

P=v1+kappa_x(v1-v0)=v2+kappa_y(v2-v3),
kappa_x=sin(pi x)sin(pi r)/(sin(pi w)sin(pi p)),
kappa_y=sin(pi y)sin(pi r)/(sin(pi w)sin(pi q)). (4)

In particular |P|>1. Form the ideal quadrangular pyramid with apex infinity and base (v0,v1,v2,v3), and attach the ideal tetrahedron (infinity,P,v1,v2) along their common triangular face (infinity,v1,v2), on opposite sides of that face.

The pyramid has dihedral angles p,r,q,r+w along its four base edges. Its dihedral angle at an apex edge is one minus the sum of the two adjacent half-arcs. Along the shared face the two apex-edge angles therefore add to pi after the attachment, while the base-edge angle adds to pi(r+w)<pi. The two coplanar face pairs merge. The resulting six-vertex polyhedron is the ideal triangular prism with faces

triangles: (P,v1,v2), (infinity,v0,v3),
quadrilaterals:
   Fx=(infinity,v0,v1,P),
   Fy=(infinity,v3,v2,P),
   Fz=(v0,v1,v2,v3).                           (5)

Convexity is literal. The two merged vertical side planes are the planes over (v0,v1) and (v2,v3). The third vertical side plane is over (v3,v0). The remaining supporting hemispheres have boundary circles (v0,v1,v2,v3) and (P,v1,v2). P is outside the first circle, and v0,v3 are outside the second circle on the side opposite its arc through P. These five half-spaces contain both attached cells, and their intersection has exactly the vertices and faces (5). Thus the union is their convex intersection, not an immersed volume chain.

Equivalently one may use the elementary convex-union argument: the two convex cells lie on opposite sides of the attaching support plane, and all its finite seam angles are at most pi. The union is locally convex. Between any two finite points, a length-minimizing path exists by compactness of a bounded hyperbolic ball containing a minimizing sequence; local convexity makes that path an ambient geodesic. Thus the union is convex, without an assertion at a missing ideal vertex.

The complete edge-angle table is

Edges Dihedral angle divided by pi
P-v1 and infinity-v0 x
P-v2 and infinity-v3 y
v1-v2 and v0-v3 1-z
P-infinity w=1-x-y
v0-v1 p=z-x
v2-v3 q=z-y

Each ideal vertex has three incident angles summing to pi. The three longitudinal angles have sum pi(1-2r)<pi. This last strict inequality is visible directly in the construction; no polyhedron existence theorem is being invoked.

Conversely any convex ideal triangular prism with these ordered ports has this explicit form after an isometry. Send its selected quadrilateral port circle to the unit circle, and one of its two remaining ideal vertices to infinity. The other two quadrilateral faces then lie in vertical planes through the remaining vertex P; their boundary lines are (v0,v1) and (v2,v3). Thus P is exactly their intersection, and its position on the exterior side of the base support hemisphere gives |P|>1. Orient the cyclic base order so that the finite triangular wall is (P,v1,v2). Its positive triangle angles make the four half-arcs (p,r,q,r+w) with w>0. The same sine rule recovers (4), and the edge-angle table recovers x=q+r, y=p+r, z=p+q+r. This verifies that the alternative prism pieces used in section 6 are actual cells of this family.

Triangulating the quadrangular pyramid across (v0,v2) gives tetrahedral angle triples (p,r,1-p-r) and (p+r,q,r+w). Their volume sum is

Lambda(pi p)+Lambda(pi q)+Lambda(pi r)+Lambda(pi(r+w)),

because the complementary intermediate angles cancel. This is also the sum of the last two volumes in (2). The attached tetrahedron has volume V(y,x,w). Therefore this ACTUAL convex prism has volume pi m.

3. The ordered face metrics are the source derivatives

For cyclically ordered ideal points (a,b,c,d) on a circle or line set

kappa(a,b,c,d)=(a-b)(c-d)/((a-d)(b-c))>0,       (6)

using the limit when one point is infinity. This positive real number determines the ordered ideal quadrilateral up to hyperbolic isometry: send three vertices to (infinity,0,1) and the fourth is determined by (6). A one-step cyclic change of vertices, or reversal fixing the first vertex, replaces kappa by its reciprocal.

For the orders in (5), direct substitution and the sine rule give

kappa(Fx)=sin(pi x)sin(pi r)/(sin(pi w)sin(pi p)),
kappa(Fy)=sin(pi y)sin(pi r)/(sin(pi w)sin(pi q)),
kappa(Fz)=sin(pi p)sin(pi q)/(sin(pi z)sin(pi r)). (7)

Differentiating the actual source action, with all other boundary variables fixed, proves the exact equations

log kappa(Fx)=-partial_x m,
log kappa(Fy)=-partial_y m,
log kappa(Fz)=-partial_z m.                    (8)

There is no normalization constant: all factors of 2 in L’ cancel.

The actual h-leaf action g(x)=-2L(x)-2L(1/2-x), 0<x<1/2, likewise has a literal ideal pyramid. Its apex is infinity and its base is the rectangle with vertices

(1, exp(2pi i x), -1, -exp(2pi i x)).

Its four base angles alternate x and 1/2-x, its four apex-edge angles are right angles, and its volume is pi g(x). Its ordered base metric is

kappa_leaf=tan(pi x)^2=exp(-g'(x)).             (9)

4. Source stationarity gives the actual corner-preserving gluing

Mark any child output quadrilateral by C0,C1,C2,C3 in its source order. In a multiplication prism use the lower and upper input maps

lower child: (C0,C1,C2,C3) -> (v0,v1,P,infinity),
upper child: (C0,C1,C2,C3) -> (infinity,P,v2,v3). (10)

These are the literal identity-stack corner identifications: the lower child’s top left and right corners map to infinity and P, which are respectively the upper child’s bottom left and right corners. Thus no unrecorded four-punctured-sphere mutation is inserted.

Relative to (5), the first map is a cyclic shift and the second a reversal. Each replaces kappa by its reciprocal. At an internal wire u, source stationarity says that the child output derivative plus the parent input derivative is zero. Equations (8),(9) therefore give

kappa_child kappa_parent=1.                   (11)

By the complete metric invariant (6), (11) is EXACTLY the condition that (10) is an ideal-quadrilateral isometry. This proves metric matching of the whole face, rather than merely equality of a formal edge product.

For the relevant angle bookkeeping, call output edges C0-C1 and C2-C3 FREE, and the other pair CAP. A leaf output has FREE angles x and CAP angles 1/2-x. A completed product output has FREE angles z and CAP angles 1-z. The input maps (10) send CAP edges to the prism’s two edges with angle x or y, and FREE edges to its longitudinal edges. Thus a leaf-to-prism seam produces right angles on the CAP pair; a product-to-prism seam has angle pi on that pair and becomes flat. The edge common to both inputs has total angle

pi(w+x+y)=pi,

while the output’s two longitudinal edges acquire angles

pi(p+x)=pi z, pi(q+y)=pi z.                   (12)

These identities explain the local right/flat boundary pattern. They do not alone prove the global closure or cusp completeness of an arbitrary expression. A genuine turn rotates a product output quadrilateral once, exchanges FREE and CAP, and replaces its label x by 1-x. It also inverts kappa, exactly as the derivative of the turned profile requires.

5. Equality of the full antisymmetric pre-Bloch class

Write A(z)=[z]-[bar z] in the rational pre-Bloch group. For an ideal tetrahedron with positive angle triple (a,b,c), let its positive shape be z=exp(pi i a)sin(pi b)/sin(pi c), and put u_a=exp(2pi i a), and cyclically. Applying the defining five-term relation to (z,bar z) gives

A(z)=-[u_a^-1]+[u_c]-[u_b^-1].

Subtract its complex conjugate. One obtains the uniform identity

A(z)=1/2 (A(u_a)+A(u_b)+A(u_c)).               (13)

No integral inversion identity or unproved regulator injectivity is used. In particular A(exp(2pi i(1-a)))=-A(exp(2pi i a)) directly from its definition.

Consequently the sum of A over a pyramid’s two tetrahedra depends only on the sum of A(exp(2pi i theta)) over its four half-arcs; complementary diagonal terms cancel. The source ordering (p,q,r,r+w) and the actual prism ordering (p,r,q,r+w) give exactly the SAME class. Equation (13) therefore upgrades (2) to

sum_(three source atoms) A(z_j)
       = sum_(an actual prism triangulation) A(z_j)          (14)

in the rational pre-Bloch group of any common field containing the displayed phases and shapes. For algebraic source stationary points this is a number field, as in the proved source-regulator theorem. No assertion of zero boundary for this open cell is made.

More generally the antisymmetric class of a convex ideal polyhedron is independent of its ideal triangulation, directly from five-term relations. For equal boundary triangulations the difference of two tetrahedral chains is a 3-cycle in the full abstract simplex on their ideal vertices. Coning it to one vertex gives a 4-chain bounding it; the boundary of each 4-simplex is precisely a five-term relation. Different triangulations of a boundary polygon can first be corrected by tetrahedral chains whose four vertices lie on that face’s circle. Their shapes are real, hence their A-symbols are zero. This proves the assertion without assuming a general Pachner-connectivity theorem.

6. Actual ideal-cube geometry proves stationary associativity

Fix positive a,b,c,d with

a+b<1, a+c<1, b+c<1,
max(a,b,c)<d<min(1,a+b+c).                     (15)

For the left parenthesization put

F_L(t)=m(a,b,t)+m(t,c,d),
max(a,b,d-c)<t<min(a+b,d,1-c).                (16)

This interval is nonempty precisely under (15). Its derivative is

F_L'(t)=log[sin(pi(t+c)) sin(pi(a+b-t)) sin(pi(d-t))
                /(sin(pi(t-a)) sin(pi(t-b)) sin(pi(t+c-d)))].

It tends to positive infinity at the lower endpoint and negative infinity at the upper endpoint. Strict concavity of the two actual source actions gives a UNIQUE interior maximizer t*. Therefore the two multiplication prisms glue by (10),(11).

Their two wall-edge seams with angles (1-t)+t become flat. The four triangular walls merge into two quadrilateral walls. The union is an ACTUAL convex ideal cube with four input/output port faces. Its wall-edge angles, in order around either wall, are

pi(a,b,c,1-d),

and its four edges between consecutive port faces have angles

pi(1-a-b), pi(1-b-c), pi(d-c), pi(d-a).        (17)

They are all positive and less than pi by (15). The seam’s remaining edge angles are respectively (t-a)+(d-t)=d-a and (t-b)+(1-t-c)=1-b-c. Thus the two convex cells have no reentrant seam, and their union is the convex ideal hexahedron with these faces.

There is an explicit common reflection, which supplies the other diagonal cut without invoking a rigidity theorem. For the single prism in section 2 define the reflection in the hemisphere centered at P with squared radius |P|^2-1:

R(z)=P+(|P|^2-1)/(bar z-bar P).               (18)

It exchanges infinity with P. The elementary power-of-a-point identity for the unit circle shows that it exchanges v0 with v1 and v3 with v2. Its reflecting plane is perpendicular to all three quadrilateral port planes: it is perpendicular to the two vertical planes through P and orthogonal to the unit hemisphere. It exchanges the prism’s two triangular walls.

The corner maps (10) intertwine the corresponding vertex exchanges. Hence the two reflecting planes agree after gluing, and the ideal cube has a common reflection plane. Its section by that plane is a convex hyperbolic quadrilateral; its four vertices lie on the four edges between port faces. Each such edge is perpendicular to the reflection plane, and its two ideal endpoints are exchanged.

The OTHER diagonal of this quadrilateral determines a hyperbolic plane perpendicular to the reflection plane. It contains both full perpendicular port edges at its endpoints. Consequently it cuts the cube in an IDEAL QUADRILATERAL with those four ideal endpoints. The two pieces are convex ideal triangular prisms. Their external ports are (b,c) and (a,d), exactly the right parenthesization. If u denotes the new internal source label, the angle table in section 2 gives their actions m(b,c,u) and m(a,u,d). Their common face is already isometric, so (8) gives right-side stationarity. The same endpoint and strict-concavity argument makes u its unique maximizer.

Both parenthesizations thus decompose ONE actual ideal cube. They have equal volume and equal full antisymmetric pre-Bloch class by section 5. This is a source-local geometric associativity theorem; it is stronger than deducing equality of exponential rates from the finite algebra’s associativity.

There is also an exact algebraic check of the critical point. Put X=exp(2pi i a), Y=exp(2pi i b), Z=exp(2pi i c), W=exp(2pi i d), T=exp(2pi i t*). The sine equation in (16) is the polynomial identity

(ZT-1)(XY-T)(W-T)=(T-X)(T-Y)(ZT-W).

Its cubic and constant terms cancel, and its nonzero root is

T=(XYZW-XYZ+XY-XW-YW+W)
                      /(XYZ-XZ-YZ+ZW-W+1).                 (19)

The denominator is nonzero under (15): otherwise the polynomial would have no nonzero root or would vanish identically, contradicting existence and strictness of the real stationary point. The root for u* follows by cyclically permuting (X,Y,Z). The selected angle interval, not the rational formula alone, fixes the geometric branch.

The proof above concerns positive local cells and their stationary composition. Flat source faces, boundary maxima and full closed-link incidence must still be handled before turning these local results into a general hyperbolic-volume theorem.

Audit: explicit cells, symbolic classes and actual stationary cubes and recorded results. It checks 163 prisms with exact rational circle coordinates and exact reflection maps, the symbolic five-term phase conversion and critical polynomial, and 20 actual stationary ideal cubes at high precision. The cube checks construct the face-gluing Mobius map, verify all six supporting face planes and convexity, check the common reflection, and test coplanarity of the four endpoints of the alternative cut. The uniform proof is the geometric and algebraic argument above; the finite checks do not infer arbitrary link geometry.

The explicit converse normalization in section 2 identifies the alternative pieces with this source prism family; no general polyhedron rigidity theorem is assumed there.
