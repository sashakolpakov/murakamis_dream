Polynomial upper bounds for an initialized polarized source

Private written proof, 22 November 2026.

An action-relative complex upper bound may lose any fixed power of the level. An actual all-order initializer still propagates through that loss, provided its formal coefficients extend holomorphically. A finite jet of those coefficients suffices for a nonzero leading term at a fixed target. This is a source-specific use of elementary analytic continuation, not a new estimate for the original period.

1. Polynomial losses and a holomorphic initializer

Let D⊂ℂD\subset\mathbb C be connected and open, and let U⊂DU\subset D be a nonempty open set. Suppose fNf_N is holomorphic on DD, with NN tending to infinity through an unbounded subset of the positive integers. Assume:

  1. For every compact K⋐DK\Subset D, there are constants CK,BKC_K,B_K such that supK|fN|≤CKNBK.(1) \sup_K|f_N|\le C_KN^{B_K}. \qquad\text{(1)}
  2. There are holomorphic functions aja_j on DD, j≥0j\ge0, and for every integer L≥1L\ge1, fN−∑j=0L−1ajN−j=Oloc,U(N−L).(2) f_N-\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}a_jN^{-j}=O_{\mathrm{loc},U}(N^{-L}). \qquad\text{(2)}

Then (2) holds locally uniformly on all of DD, at every order. It also holds after any fixed number of complex derivatives.

Here and below all constants may depend on the chosen order. The polynomial exponent used to propagate a remainder will not depend on that order.

Proof. Fix a compact target set K⋐DK\Subset D. Choose a closed disk in UU, finitely many paths in DD joining that disk to a finite disk cover of KK, and a compact neighborhood of these paths. There is a common r>0r>0 and a finite collection of disk chains with successive centers at distance at most rr, whose disks of radius 4r4r remain in that compact neighborhood. Their final disks of radius 2r2r cover KK; the initial disk of radius rr lies in UU. Let J≥1J\ge1 bound the number of interpolation steps in every chain.

For RL,N=fN−∑j=0L−1ajN−j, R_{L,N}=f_N-\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}a_jN^{-j}, (1) and boundedness of the finitely many aja_j on this fixed compact neighborhood give |RL,N|≤CLNB,B≥0,(3) |R_{L,N}|\le C_LN^B,\qquad B\ge0, \qquad\text{(3)} where BB is independent of LL. The initial disk has bound OL(N−L)O_L(N^{-L}).

For a holomorphic function with inner bound M1M_1 on radius rr and outer bound M4M_4 on radius 4r4r, the maximum principle gives the radius-2r2r bound M2≤M11/2M41/2.(4) M_2\le M_1^{1/2}M_4^{1/2}. \qquad\text{(4)} Indeed compare log⁡|R|\log|R| on the intervening annulus with the harmonic function affine in log⁡|z−z0|\log|z-z_0| with those two boundary values. Zeros are handled by the usual subharmonic inequality, or by a positive regularization of the boundary bounds.

The next radius-rr disk lies in the preceding radius-2r2r disk. Iterating (4), and using (3) at every outer disk, therefore gives supK|RL,N|≤CK,LN−Lθ+B(1−θ),θ=2−J>0.(5) \sup_K|R_{L,N}| \le C_{K,L}N^{-L\theta+B(1-\theta)},\qquad \theta=2^{-J}>0. \qquad\text{(5)} Shorter chains obey the same weaker bound. The fixed finite cover makes J,BJ,B common to all of KK, independently of LL.

For any desired order M≥1M\ge1, choose L≥ML\ge M so that Lθ−B(1−θ)≥ML\theta-B(1-\theta)\ge M. Equation (5), followed by adding back the finite tail ∑j=ML−1ajN−j=OK(N−M)\sum_{j=M}^{L-1}a_jN^{-j}=O_K(N^{-M}), proves the order-MM assertion. Apply it on a slightly larger compact set and use the Cauchy integral formula to obtain derivatives. ▫\square

There is a useful finite version. If only a0,…,aL−1a_0,\ldots,a_{L-1} extend to DD, and only their order-LL seed estimate is supplied, the same proof gives order MM wherever L≥M,Lθ−B(1−θ)≥M.(6) L\ge M,\qquad L\theta-B(1-\theta)\ge M. \qquad\text{(6)} In particular a fixed integer L≥max⁡{1,2J(B+1)−B}(7) L\ge \max\{1,\,2^J(B+1)-B\} \qquad\text{(7)} suffices for fN=a0+OK(N−1)f_N=a_0+O_K(N^{-1}). This jet length depends on the domain, upper-bound exponent and disk chains, but not on NN. Nonvanishing at a target still requires a0a_0 to be nonzero there.

The upper hypothesis may instead be the local complex-area estimate ∫K|fN(z)|2dA(z)≤CKN2BK(K⋐D).(8) \int_K|f_N(z)|^2\,dA(z)\le C_KN^{2B_K} \quad(K\Subset D). \qquad\text{(8)} To recover (1), first enlarge KK inside DD, then apply the holomorphic submean inequality on disks of a fixed buffered radius. This uses area on complex disks, not a bound only on their real axes.

2. The actual initialized contour representative

Fix the finite same-index graph and selected vertices of the pattern-adapted source theorem. Write m=|I|m=|I|, let ee be its number of incident kernel edges counted once, and keep outside angles equal to zero, hence outside spectral values equal to −2-2. One may apply the analytic theorem separately to even and odd levels if their formal coefficients differ. The full-sequence leading rate then follows when both target leading coefficients are nonzero and the same marked action is used. A fixed, NN-independent holomorphic charge curve through the seed and the target may replace the diagonal. Its image must retain the asserted initializer and action charts.

This application holds the outside data at their complete values. The exact identities allow ordinary free outside variables, but the quoted initializer does not establish an open seed in those variables. No such boundary initializer is inferred here.

The heat-readout quotient theorem permits the initialized representative in (9) in place of the canonical spectral polarization: their difference has exactly zero readout after every independent signed heat evolution. It does not supply (1), (8), formal-coefficient continuation or a target unit. In particular the cosine family and the canonical finite coupon cannot be substituted for 𝒢N\mathscr G_N away from unit charge.

4. What has been reduced

The continuation criterion asks only for an action-relative complex upper estimate with an arbitrary fixed polynomial loss. It does not ask for a residual lower witness or exact asymptotic power in that upper estimate. The cost of the loss is a finite number of continued formal coefficients when only the leading term is wanted.

The remaining inputs are substantial: a connected regular action domain to the actual target, the required formal jet on that domain, a nonzero target density, and the complex upper estimate itself. A real-charge upper estimate is insufficient for the proof. A zero of the continued leading coefficient does not yield (11) with a nonzero leading term. Exact source-null freedom alone proves none of these analytic inputs.

For a knot-volume conclusion one must additionally retain the exact source-to-knot normalization and peripheral marking, and establish 2πℜS(1)=Vol2\pi\Re S(1)=\operatorname{Vol}. Known nonvanishing factors whose logarithmic modulus is o(N)o(N) do not change that rate; omitted source factors are not licensed. No new filling class or volume lower bound is established here.
