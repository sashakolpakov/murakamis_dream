Exact cap-sum reduction of the source and its volume problem

29 October 2026. There is a general topological reduction of the actual positive Conway class, before any hyperbolic gluing problem is attempted. A cap closure of a stack is a componentwise connected sum of its cap closures. Its original full-normal quantum source factors exactly, and its entire angle polytope and volume functional factor with it.

This does not turn a Conway four-punctured sphere into a rigid pair of pants. The separating surface used here is an explicitly different sphere: it crosses only the TWO wires between stacked boxes, after the outer cap closure has been made. The result applies to arbitrary boxes in the actual Conway algebra, not just to a pure run of circles.

1. Two closures and the exact source identity

Use the physical boxes, genuine turn R, Newton basis Bhat_r, and original pointed functional phi of the actual Conway compiler. Put d=N-1. For a box F write

C_N(F)=phi(F),           D_N(F)=phi(R F).

The first is its ordinary two-strand cylindrical closure. Rotating the second diagram back to the original position of F identifies it with the cap closure: the two upper ports are joined to each other and the two lower ports are joined to each other. The same actual cups, caps, and single mark are retained. This is a description of a literal closed diagram, not of an extra functional invented for the proof.

For F=sum f_r Bhat_r the established actual turn gives

C_N(F)=f_d,              D_N(F)=f_0.

The full repeated-root multiplication rule has only ONE contribution at coordinate zero: i=j=k=0, with coefficient one. Therefore for ANY two actual boxes in this algebra,

D_N(FG)=D_N(F)D_N(G).                              (1)

There is no positivity hypothesis in (1); no root normal, auxiliary color, or framing scalar is removed. All factors are the raw sources of their recorded diagrams. Framing conversions, if desired, retain their separately recorded unit characters. This equality is proved in the actual full Newton algebra, not by citing a multiplicativity theorem for a different link invariant.

For the robust positive class, D_N(F),D_N(G)>0 for N>=3. Iterating (1) gives an exact product for every finite cap-closed stack. The two turns also satisfy C_N(RF)=D_N(F) and D_N(RF)=C_N(F), because R reverses all Newton coordinates. At the level of unmarked closed diagrams, these last relations are realized by rotating the whole closed diagram; when a point is recorded, it must be transported with that rotation.

The supplied source identities already specify their marks. They do not assert that arbitrary choices of a marked component of an arbitrary link have been proved equivalent.

2. The actual separating sphere and its component labels

Put the lower box F and upper box G in disjoint balls, and connect their two interface ports by two short vertical arcs in an otherwise empty collar. Perform the cap closure outside the two boxes: the lower pair of F is capped, as is the upper pair of G. Every auxiliary component stays in its own box. Choose a horizontal disk through the middle of the interface collar. Closing that disk around the lower ball and its lower cap in the empty exterior gives a sphere S which meets the link in exactly the TWO interface arcs, once each, and no other component.

Cut along S and cap its two punctures on each side by a trivial arc. The lower resulting link is exactly the cap closure of F: the newly inserted arc is its upper cap. The upper resulting link is exactly the cap closure of G: the inserted arc is its lower cap. This is a reversible construction, with the original ribbon framing inherited by each arc. Thus, as an actual unmarked link,

cap(FG)=cap(F) #_(upper cap of F, lower cap of G) cap(G). (2)

The subscript is essential. A general cap-closed box can have its upper and lower caps on DIFFERENT main components. Equation (2) does not silently choose an arbitrary distinguished component in either factor. Nor does the proof use a linking matrix to recognize the link: the empty collar and its two-punctured sphere exhibit the connected sum directly.

If c(F) counts all components in cap(F), then

c(FG)=c(F)+c(G)-1.                                 (3)

All components other than the two named cap components remain intact. For more than two factors, use parallel interface spheres, obtaining a tree of componentwise connected sums with the cap labels retained. The original marked point can be followed through this construction; (1) is independently the exact identity for the compiler’s specified marked source, so no change-of-mark theorem is needed to infer it.

This reduction is special to the cap closure. An ordinary cylindrical closure of FG has an outside return running across the same cut and does NOT give this two-punctured sphere. Its exact source is instead the full pairing sum_i f_i g_(d-i), which in general does not factor.

3. The entire variational problem splits, including its boundary

For any fixed robust expression F, let P_F^0 be its wire polytope from the whole-source variational theorem, but with final output 0 instead of 1. Let V_F^0 be the SAME sum of leaf and multiplication tetrahedral volumes, restricted to this face. Equivalently these are the polytope and volume function for R F with its final turn wire deleted. Thus the proved full-sequence limit is

lim_N (2pi/N)log D_N(F)=2 max_(P_F^0) V_F^0.         (4)

For the cap-closed product FG, the final product has output z=0. The exact constraints max(x,y)<=z<=x+y force x=y=0. Its product weight is exactly one at every level, and its continuous product action is m(0,0,0)=0. More explicitly, the three product angle triples are all (0,0,1), so the entire final gate is flat.

Deleting the forced wires therefore gives the literal affine identity

P_(FG)^0 = P_F^0 x P_G^0,
V_(FG)^0 = V_F^0 + V_G^0.                          (5)

These equalities hold on the whole CLOSED polytopes, not just near a saddle. They include boundary maxima and every shared wire inside each factor. Consequently

max V_(FG)^0 = max V_F^0 + max V_G^0.               (6)

At the exact integer level the state sets have the same Cartesian product and the weights multiply. Thus (5) is stronger than deriving additivity after taking a limit: both its quantum and angle versions come from the same forced zero-coordinate conservation law.

The stationarity equations for nonflat atoms split into the two independent systems as well. Any claim of a single nondegenerate geometric critical point for the unreduced whole expression must first remove this forced-flat gate and respect the topological decomposition (2). Uniqueness or algebraicity of the two reduced optima does not erase the connected-sum sphere.

4. A general boundary reduction procedure

Retain a closure flag C or D alongside the actual expression tree. At a turn, switch the flag and retain the child’s diagram and mark. At a product with flag D, split into the two children, each with flag D, and record the sphere and its upper/lower cap labels from section 2. At a product with flag C, stop at that factor. Leaves are retained.

This terminates since each step removes at least one expression vertex. It yields simultaneously:

  • an exact product of original source scalars;
  • an exhibited componentwise connected-sum decomposition of the unmarked closed diagram, with transported marked data recorded;
  • a Cartesian product decomposition of its reduced angle polytopes, and an additive decomposition of their volume functions.

No assertion of primeness is attached to the factors at which this procedure stops. A four-punctured Conway seam inside such a factor still needs its actual incidence and completeness proof. This is an exact boundary reduction, not a complete JSJ algorithm.

5. Positive exponential growth can come from several hyperbolic pieces

Let A=h^3 be the robust seed and set, for k>=1,

E_k=R((R A)^k).

The whole original scalar satisfies, at EVERY N>=3,

C_N(E_k)=C_N(A)^k.                                  (7)

This follows from (1) and R^2=1 on the actual algebra. In this particular family there is no ambiguity about the connected-sum component: A has U main pairing and two internal main circles; R A has through pairing and the same two internal circles. In its cap closure the upper and lower caps therefore lie on ONE main component. Every interface sphere in section 2 joins those components. Rotating the closed factor back identifies it with the actual theta closure C(A), including the transported point on that component.

The theta-link geometry already proves that this factor is the literal untwisted six-component augmented theta link, has four regular ideal octahedra, and satisfies the original-source limit 4v8. Consequently E_k is an actual componentwise connected sum of k such theta links and has

number of components = 5k+1,
lim_N (2pi/N)log C_N(E_k)=4k v8.                    (8)

For k>=2 its complement is not a complete finite-volume hyperbolic manifold. Here is a group proof which avoids inferring topology from the scalar. The explicit sum sphere gives, by cutting collars and van Kampen, an amalgam of the two summand groups over the infinite cyclic subgroup generated by the glued component’s meridian mu. Each theta summand is already proved complete hyperbolic, so its cusp group injects as Z^2. Choose a longitude lambda_1 and lambda_2 in the two adjacent summands. Each commutes with mu, and neither lies in <mu>. The amalgam normal-form argument says that their commutator lambda_1 lambda_2 lambda_1^-1 lambda_2^-1 is nontrivial: its four letters alternate between the factors and none lies in the amalgamated cyclic subgroup. Their images remain nontrivial after further sums by the same normal form.

In a torsion-free orientation-preserving hyperbolic manifold group, the centralizer of a nontrivial parabolic is abelian. To see this directly, conjugate that parabolic to z->z+1. A commuting Mobius map must fix infinity, hence has form z->az+b; commutation forces a=1. All such translations commute. Here mu would be the meridian of an actual cusp and hence a nontrivial parabolic, contradicting the two noncommuting longitudes in its centralizer. This proves the stated nonhyperbolicity.

Equation (8) is therefore equality with the SUM of the volumes of the exhibited hyperbolic summands, not the volume of a nonexistent complete hyperbolic structure on the whole complement. No general simplicial-volume or JSJ-identification theorem is needed or claimed for this statement. It supplies a necessary scope check for any proposed universal Conway volume theorem, while preserving the exact whole-source and variational information.

6. What remains after the reduction

This result supplies a general topology-compatible reduction rather than a new primitive hyperbolic equality. It strengthens the earlier pure cap-run example: the obstruction persists with arbitrary positive rates and with genuine complete hyperbolic factors, so a nonzero variational maximum is not a hyperbolicity certificate.

For each retained cylindrical factor, the outstanding task remains the actual global face/edge/peripheral dictionary for its nonflat angle functional. The two-punctured sum spheres above can be removed before that task. An internal four-punctured seam cannot be removed by the same argument.

Audit: exact full-source, support, and component checks and results. The finite calculations supplement the uniform algebraic identity, explicit sphere construction, and variational Cartesian-product proof; they are not numerical manifold recognition or a proof for unreduced primitive factors.

The cap-sum decomposition identifies connected sums. It does not identify an unreduced diagram with a primitive hyperbolic complement.
