Affine flag frames and transport of the selected vacuum

Research note, 25 October 2026. This gives full-matrix covariance under affine changes of a flag frame. It also identifies a limitation of the unit pointing in the preceding recoupling note: an intertwiner at fixed central character generally transports that unit to a nonconstant vector. The resulting period transport must be retained in the proposed torsor. These are uniform algebraic statements, with no knot examples as inputs.

Throughout, n is odd, omega is a primitive n-th root, and all displayed endpoint roots, affine differences and central characters are nonzero. Work on their common splitting cover. Permutations are literal ones.

1. An explicit Hilbert-90 transport, including its scalar

Fix a central character g=(lambda,mu). Two endpoint pointings have

b^n-lambda*a^n=mu=b'^n-lambda*a'^n.

Use the two modules on K[x]/(x^n-lambda), with common multiplication X and with Y=(b-aX)sigma^(-1), Y’=(b’-a’X)sigma^(-1). Set

r(x)=(b'-a'x)/(b-ax).

Every denominator and numerator is invertible since its cyclic norm is mu. Their ratio has norm one. Choose alpha^n=lambda and specify

h_0=1,
h_j=product_(k=1)^j r(omega^k alpha),  1<=j<n.

Norm one gives h_n=h_0, including the last step at x=alpha. Let H(X) be the unique polynomial of degree less than n with

H(omega^j alpha)=h_j.

It can be written explicitly using the spectral idempotents

e_j(X)=(1/n) sum_(k=0)^(n-1) [X/(omega^j alpha)]^k,
H(X)=sum_j h_j e_j(X).                              (1.1)

All h_j are units, so H is invertible. The recurrence proves

H X=X H,     H Y=Y' H,     H(alpha)=1.              (1.2)

These statements prove existence, not just an appeal to Hilbert 90. They also prove uniqueness: an intertwiner commutes with X and is a polynomial in it; its cyclic recurrence fixes it up to scalar, and the last normalization fixes that scalar. In particular, for three pointings at the same central character and the same marked root alpha,

H_(2,1) H_(1,0)=H_(2,0)                            (1.3)

exactly. This follows either from the product formula or from (1.2). Changing the marked root to omega alpha replaces H by H/h_1. Thus this normalization is a section on a marked spectral cover, not an invariant scalar after forgetting that cover. Its determinant is exactly product h_j.

There is an elementary test for the selected vector. If H*1 is a scalar multiple of 1, then H itself is scalar because powers of X applied to 1 span the module. Equation (1.2) then implies a=a’, b=b’. Therefore distinct endpoint pointings at a fixed central character are not identified by an intertwiner taking unit to unit. This does not obstruct an intrinsic period construction: it specifies the vector that construction must transport.

2. Ordered flags, additive transports and triangle modules

In an affine chart, let p_i be distinct points and choose roots A_i^n=p_i. Assign the oriented edge i<j the affine central character

g_ij=(1,p_i-p_j),     with endpoints A_j -> A_i.

These compose: g_ij*g_jk=g_ik. The construction in pointed_period_descent.md, Section 1, gives the pointed factorization

C_ijk: E_ik tensor V_ijk -> E_ij tensor E_jk,         (2.1)

where the relative triangle module has central character and endpoints

lambda_ijk=(p_i-p_k)/(p_j-p_k),
mu_ijk=-(p_i-p_j)/(p_j-p_k),
incoming=A_j/A_k, outgoing=A_i/A_k.                 (2.2)

Its normalized Steinberg parameter is

t_ijk=p_j(p_i-p_k)/[p_i(p_j-p_k)].

This explains precisely which auxiliary affine-origin data occur in the earlier Steinberg normalization. The unscaled central character in (2.2) is translation invariant; the normalized pointing is not.

The explicit Racah closure of the preceding note extends to these matched endpoint modules. The ordered relative operators (2.2) in that note did not use X_in+Y_in=1. On four flags the right relative pair V_013,V_123 has matching endpoints A_1/A_3. Its total and relative pointed modules are V_023 and V_012, respectively. Thus the same general-endpoint factorization gives

R_0123: V_023 tensor V_012 -> V_013 tensor V_123.     (2.3)

All central powers follow from the affine product law. The four ordered operator identities and the unit ideal certificates prove (2.3) as the actual Racah map, with scalar one. No new choice of quantum function or unaudited matrix identity is used.

3. Full affine covariance, including the triangle matrices

Translate all p_i by B and choose compatible new roots A_i’^n=p_i+B. The edge central characters g_ij stay fixed. Since their lambda is one, mark alpha=1 on every edge and use (1.1) to define

T_ij: E_ij -> E_ij'.

These intertwiners compose exactly for successive endpoint choices. There is a unique triangle map S_ijk satisfying

(T_ij tensor T_jk) C_ijk
    =C_ijk' (T_ik tensor S_ijk).                   (3.1)

Here existence is substantive: conjugate the left side by C_ijk’ and remove T_ik on the total factor. The result commutes with the total full matrix algebra. Its commutant is precisely identity tensor End(V_ijk). Consequently it has the form identity tensor S_ijk. The relative-operator identities also show that S_ijk intertwines the two unscaled triangle algebras (2.2). Everything is invertible, which proves uniqueness and invertibility of S_ijk.

This is a finite, explicit construction of the full triangle matrix; it is stronger than covariance of its determinant. Naturality of the two three-edge evaluation maps now gives the exact tetrahedral identity

R_0123' (S_023 tensor S_012)
      =(S_013 tensor S_123) R_0123.                (3.2)

To check its scalar, insert (3.1) in the two tree evaluations, use the pointed Racah identity, and cancel their common invertible evaluation and T_03. Thus the identity is literal, with all scalar factors intact. Equations (1.3) and (3.1) prove the exact composition law for the S maps.

A common scaling p_i -> u^n p_i, A_i -> u A_i identifies X with X and Y with uY on each edge. The relative operators D=X_1+Y_1/Y_2 and -C=-(Y_1/Y_2)X_2, the endpoint ratios (2.2), and the C and R matrices are unchanged. The edge transport ratios in (1.1) are also unchanged by a common scaling. Together with translations this proves full covariance for the affine group, on the compatible endpoint-root cover. The proof applies at every odd n, including composite n.

For a finite exterior contraction, use the dual S map on the oppositely oriented copy of each shared triangle. Interior maps cancel. The only surviving maps act on boundary tensors and on the chosen period vectors. No convergence or angle assumption is needed. This is a relative statement about finite tensors with the specified duality pairings, not yet about arbitrary literature integration cycles.

4. The vector and normalization lines do not disappear

For B nonzero, the incoming triangle endpoints in (2.2) change: equality of their n-th powers would imply

(p_j+B)/(p_k+B)=p_j/p_k,
B(p_k-p_j)=0,

contrary to distinct flags. The intertwiner S_ijk therefore cannot map the old unit line to the new unit line, by the test in Section 1. The transported vector is S_ijk*1 and must be retained. Thus the unit vacua coherent under recoupling in a fixed frame are not already a frame-independent selected period. An argument using only scalar commutants or determinant roots would miss this matrix-valued change.

Normalization remains equally explicit. Taking determinants in (3.1) gives

(det S_ijk)^n
   =[(det T_ij det T_jk)/(det T_ik)]^n
                     *det C_ijk/det C_ijk'.         (4.1)

The previously proved general-endpoint determinant yields, for translations,

det C_ijk/det C_ijk'
   =(A_k^n/A_k'^n)^(n(n-1)/2).                    (4.2)

Both are identities of generic units. Determinant-normalized frames require the corresponding roots and their phases. The unnormalized construction (3.1) already chooses an exact scalar; one must not replace (4.1) by an unspecified equality after taking absolute values or by specializing a peripheral parameter first.

The endpoint and spectral covers, the full triangle transport and its selected vector, and the determinant line are therefore different parts of the proposed period object. The finite Heisenberg cover alone records only part of it.

5. Literature comparison and the remaining projective problem

This is a direct reconstruction within the existing cyclic Weyl/Borel recoupling definition, not a claim of a new quantum dilogarithm. Baseilhac–Benedetti’s matrix-dilogarithm construction, Sections 3 and 5 and Appendix 8, is a closer existing framework than the surface theory alone. Their analytic families, Introduction and Section 4, already organize rational quantum-hyperbolic state sums over covers of augmented character varieties. Neither global theorem is assumed in the proofs above. The explicit pointed transports here record what would need to match in a comparison with a chosen published state.

The subsequent projective_frame_period_torsor.md handles all projective frames by compatible triangle Weyl rescalings and determinant rigidity on a root cover anchored at the identity. The actual selected literature period and its cycle still need to be identified under this local descent. The regulator comparison across levels remains a further theorem. In particular, finite central characters and semiclassical shape coordinates must be matched before using the rational Faddeev formula for growth. Those last steps are not supplied by the frame theorem or by Hilbert 90.

The later source-flag proof writes this origin dependence intrinsically by adding a marked point s in P^1. Changing s gives the same normalized Hilbert-90 map in the shared face frames. Using the actual reverse edge caps fixes its face scalar and proves dual and cyclic naturality at every level for finite sources. Its first normal coefficient is a concrete affine translation of normal lifts; the selected cut row moves inversely. The global meridian/source and regulator identification remain open.
