The exact marked-cut restriction of the incidence polynomial

Private written proof, 15 November 2026. The incidence cycle equations determine the full coefficient array from one coefficient. This note computes a particular observation of that array using the original closing operator. For every complete braid intertwiner, its restriction to the marked closing fugacity is the original scalar times a universal geometric sum. This gives an exact transport with uniformly bounded comparison factors, but also forced zeros at distance of order 1/N1/N from the prescribed evaluation. All derivatives along this line still contain the same unknown original scalar.

No numerical calculation, mathematical code, or saved numerical result is used. The conclusion concerns an auxiliary closing fugacity, not the meridian variable of the reduced ADO period.

1. The actual closing line

Fix N≥2N\ge2, d=N−1d=N-1, q=eπi/Nq=e^{\pi i/N}, and Q=q2Q=q^2. Use the original incidence polynomial of the cycle-equation theorem: FN(z)=∑n∈ℱdwN(n)zn,wN(n)=qΦN(n)∏eSne,Sa=∏r=1a2sin⁡(πr/N),S0=1.(1) F_N(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal F_d}w_N(n)z^n, \qquad w_N(n)=\frac{q^{\Phi_N(n)}}{\prod_eS_{n_e}},\qquad S_a=\prod_{r=1}^a2\sin(\pi r/N),\quad S_0=1. \qquad\text{(1)} Every incidence remains distinct. The complete quadratic phase, including all closing pivots, is the phase in that theorem. The original invariant is γNFN(𝟏)\gamma_NF_N(\mathbf1), where γN≠0\gamma_N\ne0 is its specified external factor, independent of the incidence state. It is not defined by division by the invariant.

At the unpivoted marked closing event the two incidence charges are (d−a1,a1)(d-a_1,a_1). Let e*e_* be the second incidence, carrying the transmitted physical degree a1a_1. Define the actual one-variable restriction fN(t)=FN(ze=1(e≠e*),ze*=t),DN(t)=1N∑a=0N−1ta.(2) f_N(t)=F_N(z_e=1\ (e\ne e_*),\ z_{e_*}=t), \qquad D_N(t)=\frac1N\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}t^a. \qquad\text{(2)} The marked incidence can be parallel to another incidence; it is not merged with it. All other fugacities are set to their prescribed values.

Theorem. For every complete signed braid with its original closing, fN(t)=FN(𝟏)DN(t).(3) \boxed{f_N(t)=F_N(\mathbf1)D_N(t).} \qquad\text{(3)} This is a polynomial identity, including when FN(𝟏)=0F_N(\mathbf1)=0. It remains true after any fixed signed tangle insertion assembled into such an original intertwiner. The statement is about the complete assembled operator, not an individual clock sector or an arbitrary degree-changing coupon.

Proof. Let WNW_N be that operator on bb original heads, and let gg be the actual pivotal matrix. The whole-source formula is KN=χNNTr⁡[(I⊗g⊗(b−1))WN].(4) K_N=\frac{\chi_N}{N} \operatorname{Tr}[(I\otimes g^{\otimes(b-1)})W_N]. \qquad\text{(4)} The specified scalar χN≠0\chi_N\ne0 includes the exterior normalization. The original partial trace CN=Tr⁡2,…,b[(I⊗g⊗(b−1))WN](5) C_N=\operatorname{Tr}_{2,\ldots,b} [(I\otimes g^{\otimes(b-1)})W_N] \qquad\text{(5)} is a Hopf endomorphism of the remaining head. The finite proof in the prescribed-cut theorem, Section 2, applies: cyclicity of the tail trace and the actual coproduct make CNC_N commute with the first-head generators. The Cartan eigenvalues are distinct and the adjacent raising/lowering arrows are nonzero. Thus CN=cNIC_N=c_NI. This also holds for one head.

Let At=diag⁡(1,t,…,td)A_t=\operatorname{diag}(1,t,\ldots,t^d). The alias-free incidence expansion of (4), with this diagonal insertion on the first head, multiplies every term by precisely ta1t^{a_1}. It does not change its support or its coefficient normalization. Therefore γNfN(t)=χNNTr⁡(AtCN)=χNcNDN(t).(6) \gamma_N f_N(t) =\frac{\chi_N}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(A_tC_N) =\chi_Nc_ND_N(t). \qquad\text{(6)} At t=1t=1, (4) gives γNFN(𝟏)=χNcN\gamma_NF_N(\mathbf1)=\chi_Nc_N. Canceling only the known nonzero γN\gamma_N proves (3). The argument never divides by cNc_N or the knot invariant. ▫\square

The use of scalarity is legitimate before this one-parameter insertion: the complete original WNW_N is an intertwiner. If other incidence fugacities are left arbitrary, the resulting operator need not be an intertwiner. In particular, (3) does not assert that DN(t)D_N(t) divides the full multivariable polynomial.

2. Exact transport and its source

The polynomial DND_N has the whole expression DN(t)=1−tNN(1−t),DN(1)=1.(7) D_N(t)=\frac{1-t^N}{N(1-t)},\qquad D_N(1)=1. \qquad\text{(7)} Its two exact shift laws give (1−Qt)fN(Qt)=(1−t)fN(t),(8) \boxed{(1-Qt)f_N(Qt)=(1-t)f_N(t),} \qquad\text{(8)} and (1−qt)fN(qt)−(1−t)fN(t)=2tNNFN(𝟏).(9) \boxed{(1-qt)f_N(qt)-(1-t)f_N(t) =\frac{2t^N}{N}F_N(\mathbf1).} \qquad\text{(9)} These follow by using QN=1Q^N=1 and qN=−1q^N=-1 in (7). There are no omitted endpoint terms. The right side of (9) contains the unknown prescribed scalar itself. Thus (9) is not an affine source equation with independently known forcing, of the type used in the affine quantum-A period theorem.

There is nevertheless a useful uniformly bounded transport to the midpoint between adjacent QQ-orbit points: fN(q)=2FN(𝟏)N(1−q),2π|FN(𝟏)|≤|fN(q)|≤|FN(𝟏)|.(10) f_N(q)=\frac{2F_N(\mathbf1)}{N(1-q)},\qquad \frac2\pi|F_N(\mathbf1)|\le |f_N(q)|\le |F_N(\mathbf1)|. \qquad\text{(10)} Indeed the magnitude of the coefficient is 1/[Nsin⁡(π/(2N))]1/[N\sin(\pi/(2N))]; the inequalities 2x/π≤sin⁡x≤x2x/\pi\le\sin x\le x on [0,π/2][0,\pi/2] prove (10). The exact identity and both inequalities remain valid if the scalar vanishes. They move the same unknown observation without an exponential normalization cost; they supply no absolute lower bound.

Among polynomials of degree at most dd, equation (8) itself has the one-dimensional solution space spanned by DND_N. In fact, if f(t)=∑a=0dfataf(t)=\sum_{a=0}^df_at^a, comparing its coefficient at tat^a, 1≤a<N1\le a<N, gives (Qa−1)(fa−fa−1)=0(Q^a-1)(f_a-f_{a-1})=0, so all coefficients coincide. The coefficients at zero and NN give identities. This shows explicitly how a rigid restricted equation can still leave its original closing multiplier undetermined.

3. Forced zero locus and the exact scalar connection

If FN(𝟏)≠0F_N(\mathbf1)\ne0, the zeros of fNf_N are exactly the simple points t=Qj,1≤j<N.(11) t=Q^j,\qquad 1\le j<N. \qquad\text{(11)} If FN(𝟏)=0F_N(\mathbf1)=0, the entire restriction is zero. In either case all the nontrivial QQ-orbit evaluations vanish. The closest forced zeros have distance 2sin⁡(π/N)2\sin(\pi/N) from 11. Hence even a nonzero original scalar does not produce an NN-independent zero-free complex neighborhood of the prescribed point in this actual source direction.

For fixed derivative order r≥0r\ge0, the logarithmic moments are exactly (t∂t)rfN(t)|t=1=1N∑a=0N−1arFN(𝟏),(12) \left.(t\partial_t)^r f_N(t)\right|_{t=1} =\frac1N\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}a^r\,F_N(\mathbf1), \qquad\text{(12)} where the r=0r=0 sum is NN. Thus even the entire germ on this line vanishes when the original scalar vanishes. It cannot provide an independent nonzero pointing for that scalar.

On its nonzero locus the exact logarithmic derivative is ∂tfN(t)=aN(t)fN(t),aN(t)=11−t−NtN−11−tN.(13) \partial_t f_N(t)=a_N(t)f_N(t),\qquad a_N(t)=\frac1{1-t}-\frac{Nt^{N-1}}{1-t^N}. \qquad\text{(13)} At t=1t=1 the apparent singularity is removable, with value (N−1)/2(N-1)/2. At every point in (11), aNa_N has a simple pole of residue one. No holomorphic first-order scalar connection on a fixed neighborhood of 11 can transport a nonzero fNf_N for all sufficiently large NN: a nonzero solution of such a scalar equation has no zeros in its connected domain. Equation (13) identifies the required moving singularities explicitly.

The microscopic behavior is equally explicit. Uniformly for ss in each fixed compact complex set, DN(es/N)=es−1s+O(N−1),(14) D_N(e^{s/N})=\frac{e^s-1}{s}+O(N^{-1}), \qquad\text{(14)} with the removable value one at s=0s=0. Thus the nearby zeros belong to the actual source profile; they are not an artifact of a norm estimate.

4. Relation to period rigidity and the remaining estimate

The earlier general flow fusion, Section 2, already gives the full-block geometric sum and its even/odd root values. What is added here is its universal occurrence after the entire intertwiner has been closed, as the exact restriction of the new incidence polynomial. The statement is independent of braid width, crossing signs and the chosen fixed short insertion.

The cycle theorem’s normalized coefficient array is still unique. Restriction (2) takes its one-dimensional solution line to ℂDN\mathbb C D_N with multiplier FN(𝟏)F_N(\mathbf1); that restriction can be zero only if the actual prescribed observation is zero. This makes no alternative choice of a source or an array. It identifies the additional scalar whose estimate coefficient rigidity does not supply.

This agrees with the distinction in the closed-braid period line, Section 2: an invertible intertwiner frame and its evaluated cut are different objects. It also agrees with the function-level source distinction in the global period audit. Those general distinctions alone did not give (3) or (10).

The fixed-presentation hypothesis in whole-period cyclotomic rigidity is relevant to (7). Its degree in tt grows with NN, but retaining the power coordinate w=tNw=t^N gives the fixed whole expression (1−w)/(N(1−t))(1-w)/(N(1-t)). Its value at t=w=1t=w=1 must be taken on that power graph, with the removable germ retained. Dividing out this known whole factor leaves exactly the constant FN(𝟏)F_N(\mathbf1), not a new computation of it. Nor is this auxiliary tt automatically the meridian parameter of an actual quantum-A equation.

Consequently (10) is an exact controlled transport of the prescribed observation, and (11)–(13) obstruct a particular zero-free scalar transport argument. Neither proves or disproves nonvanishing of the original invariant at any level. A phase-sensitive lower bound in a transverse source direction, or a nonzero geometric projection of the actual closing, is still required for the finite-exception target.
