The two-ended limit of the original microscopic endpoint normal

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The first normal at the original physical endpoint has a limit after division by N2N^2. Both ends of its Newton sum contribute. A uniform inverse-square bound controls the intervening indices and justifies the limit on every bounded complex parameter disk, including the apparent zeros of the complementary formula. The resulting function is entire and strictly positive on the real parameter axis.

This is the original endpoint normal constant. It is not either of the two bulk scalar-compatibility coefficients, and does not determine their cancellation with the coefficient-shift channel.

5. Meaning for the original normal data

In the unscaled original index coordinate, c′(0)=−4(π/N)sin⁡(π/N)c'(0)=-4(\pi/N)\sin(\pi/N). Thus the actual initial common-index normal satisfies c′(0)QN(α)=−4π2𝒬(α)+OA(N−1/2),(27) c'(0)Q_N(\alpha) =-4\pi^2\mathcal Q(\alpha)+O_A(N^{-1/2}), \qquad\text{(27)} uniformly on the same bounded complex disks. For real α\alpha, its limiting value is strictly negative. This fixes an actual endpoint datum in the original differentiated recurrence.

The second series in (18) is indispensable: every fixed i=N−mi=N-m contributes at the same N2N^2 scale as a fixed low index. In particular a terminating fixed-degree value limit does not by itself determine (27).

Nothing here compares (27) to the derivative of a chosen moderate bulk value interpolant. The true bulk defect also includes the coefficient-shift and propagated endpoint-source contributions. Its two anchor coefficients, the original trace current, the external connectors, and any filled-action comparison remain separate questions. No source coefficient is inferred from the positive real endpoint constant.
