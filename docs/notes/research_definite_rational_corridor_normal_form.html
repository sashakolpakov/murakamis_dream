A uniform-sign normal form for every fixed rational corridor

Every fixed rational corridor can be changed to a chain whose internal crossing powers have one sign. Except for the integral and unit cases, all their magnitudes can be taken at least two. The change preserves both arbitrary outside attachments. It introduces fixed integral twists at the ends and a specified proper half-turn, rather than a condition on the surviving filling parameters.

The associated tridiagonal chain matrix is definite. Neither definiteness nor the tangle isotopy proves a quantum asymptotic. The exact quantum transformation below retains its scalar, boundary twists and all repeated-root normal data.

1. The original word and its boundary map

Use the original physical crossing RR, clockwise turn TT, and the supported pillowcase representatives R̃n(x,y)=(x+ny+n/2,y),T̃(x,y)=(−y,x+1/2).(1) \widetilde R^{\,n}(x,y)=(x+ny+n/2,y),\qquad \widetilde T(x,y)=(-y,x+1/2). \qquad\text{(1)} Their linear parts are An=(1n01),S=(0−110).(2) A_n=\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(2)} The four marked points are the half-lattice points of the torus, modulo the deck involution z↦−zz\mapsto-z. The corridor compression note explains how these representatives are obtained from the actual supported crossings and how an equality of their affine maps gives an isotopy carrying arbitrary attached tangles.

Let WW be a fixed finite word, and write its full affine lift as W̃(z)=Gz+v,G=(abcd),det⁡G=1,v∈12ℤ2.(3) \widetilde W(z)=Gz+v,\qquad G=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\quad \det G=1,\quad v\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z^2. \qquad\text{(3)} All powers inside this word are fixed before any remaining outside filling parameter is varied.

2. The ceiling Euclidean algorithm and the full matrix identity

Suppose |c|=C≥2|c|=C\ge2. Multiply GG by its deck sign ε=sgn⁡(c)\varepsilon=\operatorname{sgn}(c), so its lower-left entry is C>0C>0. There are unique integers r,sr,s such that G0=A−r(εG)A−s=(ABCD),1≤A,D<C.(4) G_0=A_{-r}(\varepsilon G)A_{-s} =\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}, \qquad 1\le A,D<C. \qquad\text{(4)} Indeed AA and DD are the nonzero residues of εa,εd\varepsilon a,\varepsilon d modulo CC, and r=εa−AC,s=εd−DC.(5) r=\frac{\varepsilon a-A}{C},\qquad s=\frac{\varepsilon d-D}{C}. \qquad\text{(5)} The residues are nonzero because the determinant is one. It also gives AD≡1(mod⁡C)AD\equiv1\pmod C and B=(AD−1)/C≥0B=(AD-1)/C\ge0.

Starting with q1=C,p1=Aq_1=C,p_1=A, repeat bj=⌈qjpj⌉,rj=bjpj−qj.(6) b_j=\left\lceil\frac{q_j}{p_j}\right\rceil,\qquad r_j=b_jp_j-q_j. \qquad\text{(6)} If rj=0r_j=0, stop. The algorithm terminates, preserves coprimality, and gives CA=[b1,…,bk]−:=b1−1b2−1⋯−1bk.(7) \frac CA=[b_1,\ldots,b_k]_- :=b_1-\frac1{b_2-\dfrac1{\cdots-\dfrac1{b_k}}}. \qquad\text{(7)} In particular k≤A≤C−1k\le A\le C-1. The output is unique: at any nonintegral step the first coefficient of an expansion with all later complete quotients greater than one must be the ceiling.

Proposition 1. With (4)–(7), G0=(−1)kSA−b1SA−b2⋯SA−bkS.(8) G_0=(-1)^k S A_{-b_1}S A_{-b_2}\cdots S A_{-b_k}S. \qquad\text{(8)}

Proof. Let HH denote the matrix on the right. For k=1k=1, it is (10b11)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\b_1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right). Adding a new first coefficient sends a tail matrix (a′b′c′d′)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a'&b'\\c'&d'\end{smallmatrix}\right) to (c′d′b1c′−a′b1d′−b′).(9) \begin{pmatrix}c'&d'\\b_1c'-a'&b_1d'-b'\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(9)} Thus the ratio of its lower-left to upper-left entries is the negative continued fraction (7).

The continuant recurrence Kj=bjKj−1−Kj−2,K0=1,K−1=0(10) K_j=b_jK_{j-1}-K_{j-2},\qquad K_0=1,\quad K_{-1}=0 \qquad\text{(10)} shows Kj>Kj−1>0K_j>K_{j-1}>0 for j≥1j\ge1. All matrices have determinant one, so that entry and the upper-left entry are coprime. Their ratio in (7) therefore identifies them as C,AC,A, with no common factor. The lower-right entry is the unique integer in {1,…,C−1}\{1,\ldots,C-1\} whose product with AA is one modulo CC; hence it is DD. The determinant then identifies the remaining entry as BB. 3. The actual two-boundary tangle identity

Form the physical word B=RrTR−b1T⋯TR−bkTRs.(12) B=R^r T R^{-b_1}T\cdots T R^{-b_k}T R^s. \qquad\text{(12)} Its affine lift is obtained from (1). Its linear part agrees with GG up to the one deck sign. Consequently B̃−1W̃(z)=λz+w,λ∈{1,−1},w∈12ℤ2.(13) \widetilde B^{-1}\widetilde W(z) =\lambda z+w,\qquad \lambda\in\{1,-1\},\quad w\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z^2. \qquad\text{(13)} After the deck involution this is translation by δ=λwmod⁡ℤ2\delta=\lambda w\bmod\mathbb Z^2. Use the actual proper rotation δ(0,0)(1/2,0)(0,1/2)(1/2,1/2)Hδ1ρKτ.(14) \begin{array}{c|cccc} \delta&(0,0)&(1/2,0)&(0,1/2)&(1/2,1/2)\\ \hline H_\delta&1&\rho&K&\tau . \end{array} \qquad\text{(14)} The supported collar isotopy gives, for every attached tangle FF, W(F)≃RrT(R−b1T(⋯T(R−bkT(RsHδ(F)))⋯)).(15) \boxed{\; W(F)\simeq R^rT\!\left(R^{-b_1}T\!\left(\cdots T\!\left(R^{-b_k}T(R^sH_\delta(F))\right) \cdots\right)\right). \;} \qquad\text{(15)} It carries all marked arcs, closed components, and any supplied ribbon. In particular the rotation in (14) cannot be erased on an arbitrary outside branch.

This proves that every corridor with |c|≥2|c|\ge2 has a uniform-negative form with all internal magnitudes at least two. Applying the same construction to the mirrored word and then mirroring the whole equality gives a uniform-positive form as well. Both sides of that resulting equality are in the original oriented ambient manifold; the argument does not replace just one tangle by its mirror.

If |c|=1|c|=1, the one-vertex criterion gives a single unit power with integral twists at both ends. If c=0c=0, the word reduces to an integral twist and a proper half-turn with no intervening turn. The last case can merge two variable outside parameters and must retain its fiber information.

For example, the mixed two-boundary corridor has SA−2SA2S=(−21−52). S A_{-2}S A_2S =\begin{pmatrix}-2&1\\-5&2\end{pmatrix}. After the deck sign, C=5,A=2,D=3C=5,A=2,D=3, and 5/2=[3,2]−5/2=[3,2]_-. Here r=0,s=−1r=0,s=-1. Thus its common-sign replacement has internal powers −3,−2-3,-2, with a twist −1-1 on its inner retained end and the rotation computed from (13). It is not a reduction to a single internal vertex.

4. The exact quantum scalar and the boundary chirps

Let MNM_N be original multiplication by RR and JNJ_N the original Newton reversal. Put D=N−1,κN=exp⁡πiD22N.(16) D=N-1,\qquad \kappa_N=\exp\frac{\pi iD^2}{2N}. \qquad\text{(16)} The full algebra includes the first normals at the double roots. Its established identities are JN2=I,JNMNJN=κNMN−1JNMN−1.(17) J_N^2=I,\qquad J_NM_NJ_N=\kappa_N M_N^{-1}J_NM_N^{-1}. \qquad\text{(17)} Use the displayed logarithm of κN\kappa_N and set M̂N=κN−1/3MN\widehat M_N=\kappa_N^{-1/3}M_N. Direct multiplication in (17) gives JN2=I,(JNM̂N)3=I.(18) J_N^2=I,\qquad (J_N\widehat M_N)^3=I. \qquad\text{(18)} These are the defining relations of PSL2(ℤ)\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z) for S,A1S,A_1. One elementary verification of that presentation uses the Farey ideal triangulation: its dual, subdivided at edge midpoints, is a tree, the modular group acts transitively on its edges, an edge has trivial stabilizer, and the endpoint stabilizers have orders two and three. A reduced alternating product of nonidentity elements in these stabilizers moves the base edge along a nonbacktracking path, so cannot be the identity. The two generators also generate every integral determinant-one matrix by the Euclidean algorithm. This proves the presentation used in (18).

For a word WW, let e(W)e(W) be the sum of its signed crossing exponents; turns contribute zero. Equations (11), (18) give the exact operator equality 𝒬N(W)=κNℓMNr[JNMN−b1JN⋯JNMN−bkJN]MNs,ℓ=e(W)−r−s+∑jbj3.(19) \boxed{\; \mathcal Q_N(W) =\kappa_N^\ell M_N^r \bigl[J_NM_N^{-b_1}J_N\cdots J_NM_N^{-b_k}J_N\bigr]M_N^s, \qquad \ell=\frac{e(W)-r-s+\sum_jb_j}{3}. \;} \qquad\text{(19)} The exponent ℓ\ell is an integer. Indeed the same modular presentation has a homomorphism to ℤ/3ℤ\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z sending A1A_1 to one and SS to zero, so equal matrices have equal crossing-exponent sums modulo three.

The actual physical rotation in (15) is still carried in the topological statement. Equation (19) is proved separately on the full original quantum algebra; it is not an inference that the rotation fixes every arbitrary tangle.

Here is the corresponding boundary interpretation. An operator QQ has Frobenius kernel coefficient matrix QJNQJ_N. The identity MNsJN=JN(MNs)T(20) M_N^sJ_N=J_N(M_N^s)^T \qquad\text{(20)} therefore makes the end factors in (19) multiplication by RrR^r and RsR^s in its two boundary variables, followed by canonical reduction. Thus, if the uniform-negative kernel has an already proved whole-boundary phase Φ(u,v)\Phi(u,v), the canonical boundary-multiplication theorem and its two-boundary version give the transformed phase Φ(u,v)+i4π(ru2+sv2).(21) \Phi(u,v)+\frac{i}{4\pi}(ru^2+sv^2). \qquad\text{(21)} The exact additional scalar is κNℓCN,rCN,s,CN,j=exp⁡πijD24N.(22) \kappa_N^\ell C_{N,r}C_{N,s},\qquad C_{N,j}=\exp\frac{\pi i jD^2}{4N}. \qquad\text{(22)} The amplitudes and the polynomial power of NN are unchanged by those multiplication operations. Equations (21)–(22) are conditional on the stated whole-boundary estimate for the new kernel. The exact finite identity (19) needs no asymptotic assumption and retains all normal data.

5. Pendant words and their cap scalar

For a pendant word the rational slope is the second-column ratio p/qp/q, q≥0q\ge0. If q≥2q\ge2, write p/q=r+x,r=⌊p/q⌋,0<x<1.(23) p/q=r+x,\qquad r=\lfloor p/q\rfloor,\quad 0<x<1. \qquad\text{(23)} The ceiling algorithm for 1/x1/x gives x=1b1−1b2−⋯−1/bk,bj≥2.(24) x=\frac1{b_1-\dfrac1{b_2-\cdots-1/b_k}}, \qquad b_j\ge2. \qquad\text{(24)} The pendant word B=RrTR−b1T⋯TR−bk(25) B=R^rTR^{-b_1}T\cdots TR^{-b_k} \qquad\text{(25)} has precisely that slope. The actual cap-stabilizer argument in the corridor note identifies W(I)W(I) with B(I)B(I), where II is the through tangle. No constraint is imposed on any remaining outside power.

There is an exact scalar even though the two full matrices need not agree. Normalize the relative linear matrix to B−1W=(10h1)in PSL2(ℤ).(26) B^{-1}W=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\h&1\end{pmatrix} \quad\text{in }\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z). \qquad\text{(26)} It is the matrix of TR−hTTR^{-h}T. The last Newton vector is an eigenvector of the actual crossing with eigenvalue κN\kappa_N: the diagonal entry is κN(−1)DeπiD(D+1)/N=κN\kappa_N(-1)^D e^{\pi iD(D+1)/N}=\kappa_N. Therefore JNMN−hJNe0=κN−he0. J_NM_N^{-h}J_Ne_0=\kappa_N^{-h}e_0. Combining this with (18) yields 𝒬N(W)e0=κNℓcap𝒬N(B)e0,ℓcap=e(W)−e(B)−2h3∈ℤ.(27) \boxed{\; \mathcal Q_N(W)e_0 =\kappa_N^{\ell_{\mathrm{ cap}}}\mathcal Q_N(B)e_0, \qquad \ell_{\mathrm{ cap}}=\frac{e(W)-e(B)-2h}{3}\in\mathbb Z . \;} \qquad\text{(27)} This equality is between the full original canonical boundary elements. Any outer RrR^r in (25) is handled by canonical multiplication, with its chirp and scalar retained as above.

In the mixed example, TR−2TR2(I)≃TR−3TR−2(I),(28) TR^{-2}TR^2(I)\simeq TR^{-3}TR^{-2}(I), \qquad\text{(28)} and h=1h=1, ℓcap=1\ell_{\mathrm{ cap}}=1. Thus its exact boundary polynomial is κN\kappa_N times that of the uniform-negative word in (28). The known mixed-word theorem consequently also estimates this particular uniform-negative word. It does not estimate arbitrary such words.

If q=1q=1, the pendant word is an integral braid. If q=0q=0, it is the cup-cap tangle. The latter case uses the literal cap-sum decomposition and can retain an unresolved closed core. It is not silently put into the positive boundary class.

6. The definite chain matrix

The expansion (7) has the real symmetric matrix Q𝒃=(b1−1−1b2−1⋱⋱⋱−1bk).(29) Q_{\mathbf b}= \begin{pmatrix} b_1&-1\\ -1&b_2&-1\\ &\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\ &&-1&b_k \end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(29)} For any real vector zz, zTQ𝒃z=∑j=1k−1(zj−zj+1)2+z12+zk2+∑j=1k(bj−2)zj2.(30) z^TQ_{\mathbf b}z =\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(z_j-z_{j+1})^2 +z_1^2+z_k^2+\sum_{j=1}^k(b_j-2)z_j^2. \qquad\text{(30)} This is strictly positive for z≠0z\ne0, also when every bj=2b_j=2. Its determinant is CC, and the principal minor deleting the first row and column is AA, by (7), (10). Its Schur effective first weight is the same fraction C/AC/A.

This is the definite tridiagonal form associated with the negative continued fraction. In the usual continued-fraction surgery presentation, the corresponding chain has the opposite form −Q𝒃-Q_{\mathbf b}. No identification of this intersection form with a quantum phase Hessian is made here.

7. Surviving original parameters and branching

Normalize finitely many disjoint fixed degree-two corridors in an ordered circle tree. They may share retained branch vertices. The end factors in (15) are collected with the original branch powers by the supported twist-sliding isotopy; each passed factor receives its prescribed proper rotation. The total change at a retained vertex is a fixed integer Δv\Delta_v: nv′=nv+Δv.(31) n'_v=n_v+\Delta_v. \qquad\text{(31)} After choosing the transported carrier parity, pv′=pv+ηv′−ηv−Δv2,nv=ηv−2pv.(32) p'_v=p_v+ \frac{\eta'_v-\eta_v-\Delta_v}{2}, \qquad n_v=\eta_v-2p_v. \qquad\text{(32)} These are injective translations of the surviving integer assignments. Independent eventual longness is preserved. Internal old powers were already fixed; the added coefficients in (7) are fixed integers, not new freely variable surgery parameters.

Rotations do not change the signed crossing power of an integral braid. Propagating a rotation through an already normalized chain can reverse its order, but does not change its uniform sign or the condition bj≥2b_j\ge2. Thus normalizing disjoint corridors does not undo a neighboring corridor’s normalization. The actual branch weights do receive the constant shifts (31), which must be retained.

This does not make an arbitrary branching tree definite. For a weighted tree the symmetric matrix with diagonal weights bvb_v and off-diagonal edge entries −1-1 is positive definite exactly when successive leaf elimination produces positive effective weights qv=bv−∑wchildofvqw−1.(33) q_v=b_v-\sum_{w\ {\mathrm{ child\ of}}\ v}q_w^{-1}. \qquad\text{(33)} The assertion follows by completing squares at each leaf; the resulting diagonal factors are the qvq_v. It is independent of which vertex is chosen as root. For example a star with central weight two and five leaves of weight two has central effective weight 2−5/2=−1/22-5/2=-1/2. Every edge or chain in that star has positive weights at least two, but the whole tree matrix is indefinite.

Nor does (30) turn the full root-of-unity source into a positive sum. The original crossings still have their complex entries, and the full trace still retains its normal terms. Equation (19) preserves those facts exactly. A general theorem for uniform-sign chains would be a substantive new analytic input; definite integral surgery data alone does not supply it.

8. Scope of the reduction

The normal form gives a concrete common target for every fixed rational corridor. Its quantum and topological transformations are both exact. In particular a future whole-boundary theorem for all uniform-negative chains of magnitudes at least two would immediately transfer to all fixed rational corridors with c≠0c\ne0, with the original two boundary variables and their normal data retained.

The zero case c=0c=0, the pendant cap case, and connected regions with branching remain separate. A zero merger has infinite parameter fibers when its two ends are still variable. The existing fiber criterion must accompany those reductions; it is not replaced by the continued-fraction algorithm. For a general arborescent component, normalizing its maximal degree-two paths leaves its actual branching pieces in place.

The rational-tangle normal-form background is classical; see Kauffman–Lambropoulou. The independent ceiling algorithm, full affine marking, scalar (19), and original parameter map specify what is used here. No claim of a new rational-tangle classification or of cofinite volume coverage follows from them.

There is now a geometric theorem for the subfamily whose normalized magnitudes are all at least four: the negative-corridor variation has an interior minimum, and the marked-completion theorem identifies its actual filling when the retained outside cusps are complete. The boundary shifts and rotations above still have to be transported. This geometric result does not estimate the original quantum source, and coefficients two and three remain in the general normal form.

9. Reproducing the finite checks

Run the separate checker from the repository root:

.venv/bin/python experiments/check_definite_rational_corridor_normal_form.py \
  --output repro/results/research-definite-rational-corridor-normal-form.json

The checker uses exact integers and rational translations for all words of lengths one through four with powers between minus three and three. Of the 2,800 words, 2,220 have the two-boundary normal form with |c|≥2|c|\ge2, and 2,220 have a nonintegral finite pendant slope. It verifies the full affine identity, retained half-turn, integral scalar exponent, determinant and tail minor. The two sets of 2,220 words need not be the same.

Separately, it compares the original quantum matrices and pendant vectors for five displayed words at levels two through seven, using 100 decimal digits. These 30 matrix and 30 pendant comparisons check the scalar and ordering conventions numerically. The written operator argument proves the identities for all levels.

The saved output contains the script and dependency hashes. Its script SHA256 is 6352af92a2124a407b2a2893560e4987c7679f80837cc2d3f39ee72d430bd354. The output path can be changed for an independent replay.

A 140-digit replay checks the same exact word set and extends the quantum comparisons through level nine, giving 40 matrix and 40 pendant comparisons.
