Exact reduction fibers and the remaining core theorem

Research note, 2 November 2026. The exceptions in this note are original integer filling assignments. Neither replacing those assignments by knot types nor discarding redundant coordinates is part of the argument. The frozen manuscripts are unchanged.

The existing reductions give more than a warning about infinite fibers: on each fixed structural branch, linear algebra decides whether a nonempty fiber is a singleton or is infinite. In the latter case it contains a family on which every genuinely varying original coordinate tends to infinity. Thus the issue cannot be avoided by requiring all remaining original coefficients to be long.

1. The data of a reduction branch

Let p=(p1,…,pd)∈ℤdp=(p_1,\ldots,p_d)\in\mathbb Z^d be the original surgery coefficients. A retained half-twist pattern has already been fixed, so the crossing powers are ni=ηi−2pin_i=\eta_i-2p_i, with ηi∈{−1,0,1}\eta_i\in\{-1,0,1\} fixed. There is no additional parity restriction on pp.

Consider one branch of an exact tangle reduction. Suppose its parameter set has the form

Ω={p∈ℤd:Ap=a,ℓj(p)≠cj(1≤j≤q)},(1) \Omega=\{p\in\mathbb Z^d: Ap=a,\quad \ell_j(p)\ne c_j\quad(1\le j\le q)\}, \qquad\text{(1)}

where all matrices, vectors, and linear forms have rational coefficients. The reduced parameters are

f(p)=Bp+b.(2) f(p)=Bp+b. \qquad\text{(2)}

The reduction must identify the actual closed knot, including the zero-framing convention for the invariant. An equality between finite quantum contractions alone is insufficient for this purpose.

These hypotheses apply to the zero-aware all-root reduction in the Conway reductions paper. Its branch is chosen by the original parities and the zero or nonzero values of finitely many subset sums. Its surviving parameters are sums over disjoint original label blocks. Substituting ni=ηi−2pin_i=\eta_i-2p_i gives precisely affine equations and inequalities of the form (1), and the surviving weights give (2). Fixed numerical values, including the values needed to make an elementary factor an unknot, can be added to Ap=aAp=a. Finite alternatives such as a terminal exponent 11 or −1-1 should first be split into separate branches.

The same description applies to a prescribed finite sequence of the proved degree-two 0,±10,\pm1 moves: a ±1\pm1 move gives affine shifts, and a zero move gives a sum. This statement does not assert a classification of all partially filled exteriors.

2. A nonempty fiber is either a singleton or infinite

Fix a reduced assignment yy, and suppose Ωy={p∈Ω:f(p)=y}\Omega_y=\{p\in\Omega:f(p)=y\} is nonempty. Put

H=(AB).(3) H=\begin{pmatrix}A\\B\end{pmatrix}. \qquad\text{(3)}

Proposition 1. If HH has column rank dd, then Ωy\Omega_y consists of one point. If its rank is smaller than dd, then Ωy\Omega_y is infinite. More precisely, for any p0∈Ωyp_0\in\Omega_y there is a nonzero v∈ℤdv\in\mathbb Z^d such that

p0+kv∈Ωyfor every integer k outside a finite set.(4) p_0+kv\in\Omega_y \quad\text{for every integer \(k\) outside a finite set}. \qquad\text{(4)}

Proof. The difference of two points of the fiber lies in ker⁡ℚH\ker_{\mathbb Q}H. Full column rank therefore gives uniqueness. Otherwise this rational kernel contains a nonzero integral vector vv. Every p0+kvp_0+kv satisfies all equations defining the fiber. If ℓj(v)=0\ell_j(v)=0, its jj-th nonvanishing test remains true because it was true at p0p_0. If ℓj(v)≠0\ell_j(v)\ne0, the equality ℓj(p0+kv)=cj\ell_j(p_0+kv)=c_j holds for at most one integer kk. There are finitely many tests, so only finitely many integers are excluded. Since v≠0v\ne0, the other points are distinct. ▫\square

Thus the nonzero subset-sum tests do not cut an affine fiber down to a nonempty finite set. They remove finitely many points from any suitable line. This conclusion does not apply without further work to a different parameter domain that imposes one-sided sign conditions or arbitrary nonlinear inequalities. The present target allows signed coefficients of unrelated magnitudes.

There is a useful stronger choice of direction. Let

I={i:vi=0 for every v∈ker⁡ℚH},J={1,…,d}\I.(5) I=\{i:v_i=0\text{ for every }v\in\ker_{\mathbb Q}H\}, \qquad J=\{1,\ldots,d\}\setminus I. \qquad\text{(5)}

Every coordinate in II is constant on the affine fiber.

Proposition 2. If Ωy\Omega_y is infinite, the vector vv in (4) can be chosen so that vj≠0v_j\ne0 for every j∈Jj\in J. Consequently pi=p0,ip_i=p_{0,i} for i∈Ii\in I, while |pj|→∞\lvert p_j\rvert\longrightarrow\infty for every j∈Jj\in J, along the family (4) as |k|→∞\lvert k\rvert\longrightarrow\infty.

Proof. For each j∈Jj\in J, the equation vj=0v_j=0 defines a proper linear subspace of ker⁡ℚH\ker_{\mathbb Q}H. A vector space over the infinite field ℚ\mathbb Q is not a finite union of proper linear subspaces. One direct verification is to choose a rational basis w1,…,wrw_1,\ldots,w_r and evaluate w1+tw2+⋯+tr−1wrw_1+t w_2+\cdots+t^{r-1}w_r: each nonzero coordinate form is a nonzero polynomial, so it vanishes at only finitely many rational tt. Choose another tt, clear denominators, and use Proposition 1. ▫\square

The original coefficients need not tend to infinity with the same sign. For a zero merger, the familiar direction is (1,−1)(1,-1). Allowing independent signed long fillings is exactly what makes these directions relevant.

3. What can be pulled back from a cofinite theorem

Suppose an exact reduction identifies the knot at p∈Ωp\in\Omega with the knot at f(p)f(p). Let EE be a finite collection of reduced assignments for which the volume identity remains unresolved. Outside f−1(E)f^{-1}(E) the reduced theorem transfers to the original family.

For a fixed branch (1), Proposition 1 gives the complete fiber test:

  • If HH has full column rank, at most |E|\lvert E\rvert original assignments remain unresolved.
  • If HH has deficient rank, every nonempty fiber over an element of EE is infinite. To prove a cofinite conclusion on this branch, each hyperbolic knot represented by such an exceptional fiber must satisfy the volume identity.

The second assertion is a requirement for the proof, not a claim that any of these knots fails the conjecture. If one did fail, every assignment in its fiber would be a counterexample.

For a finite collection of structural branches, apply this test to each branch separately. All equations accumulated earlier in the reduction must remain in AA. Keeping only the final effective coordinates would lose exactly the information being tested.

There is also a direct consequence for a proposed long-remainder theorem. Suppose that, for every fixed partial assignment of the original template, all sufficiently long remaining original coefficients giving hyperbolic knots satisfy the volume identity. If a hyperbolic reduced knot has an infinite fiber in (1), fix the coordinates in II from (5) and apply that theorem along (4). Eventually all coordinates in JJ satisfy its threshold, and the knot is the same reduced knot at every point. The long-remainder theorem therefore proves the volume identity for that particular core. It cannot merely leave the core among finitely many reduced exceptions.

4. The four-vertex example and the matrix test

Use the original crossing RR, physical quarter-turn TT, and cylindrical closure CC. On the four-vertex path write

K4(a,v,b,c)=C(RaT(RvT(RbT(Rc)))).(6) K_4(a,v,b,c)=C\bigl(R^aT(R^vT(R^bT(R^c)))\bigr). \qquad\text{(6)}

The proved marked zero move gives

K4(a,0,b,c)≃K2(a+b,c).(7) K_4(a,0,b,c)\simeq K_2(a+b,c). \qquad\text{(7)}

For clarity, temporarily use the crossing powers as coordinates in one fixed parity class. Fixing the reduced pair (s,c0)(s,c_0) imposes

v=0,a+b=s,c=c0.(8) v=0,\qquad a+b=s,\qquad c=c_0. \qquad\text{(8)}

The matrix of these three equations in four variables has kernel spanned by (1,0,−1,0)(1,0,-1,0). On the fixed parity lattice, take twice this vector. Hence every hyperbolic K2(s,c0)K_2(s,c_0) has infinitely many preimages of one fixed original parity pattern, with a,ba,b both long and of opposite signs.

The exact topology behind (7), including the reversal under T2T^2, is proved in the scope audit. The rank calculation adds no topological assumption.

Thus a finite reduced exceptional list for two-circle knots is not enough to prove the cofinite statement for this four-circle template. Every hyperbolic knot on that list must be settled before its entire zero-merger fiber is covered.

5. A bounded-size form of the padding implication

The preceding obstruction is not restricted to paths. The existing strict-padding argument has the following quantitative form. The grammar is the enlarged literal strict class of the asymptotics manuscript, which permits an elementary closing child; the statement does not assert membership in its older completed-root subfamily.

Proposition 3. Let K=C(Q)K=C(Q) be a hyperbolic knot represented by a literal strict Conway expression with mm labelled leaves, whose integer powers are all fixed. There is a literal strict template with m+2m+2 labelled leaves and one fixed parity pattern having infinitely many assignments representing KK. Consequently, a cofinite assignment theorem for every strict template with at most m+2m+2 leaves proves the volume identity for every such hyperbolic mm-leaf core, without exceptions.

Proof. Put h(b)=T(Rb)h(b)=T(R^b) and form

E(a,b)=C(h(b)[RaT(Q)]).(9) E(a,b)=C\bigl(h(b)[R^aT(Q)]\bigr). \qquad\text{(9)}

This uses the permitted operations because Ra=T3h(a)R^a=T^3h(a). Reopening QQ turns its former closing product into a retained product. Each allowed closing pair AA,AL,AH,LHAA,AL,AH,LH, in either order, also has a nonempty open product, of type A,A,H,HA,A,H,H, respectively, by the literal type table. Its other products remain allowed. Hence QQ, now used as an open tangle, has type AA or HH. A new strict state may be chosen; the old closed optimizer is not being reused at its former root. Thus T(Q)T(Q) has type AA or LL, while RaR^a has type HH. Their product has type HH, and the outer closing pair has types L,HL,H, which is allowed. Strictness is a property of this formal expression and does not require b≠0b\ne0.

Let DD denote cap closure and τ=T2\tau=T^2 the physical half-turn. The proved cap isotopies give, at b=0b=0,

E(a,0)≃D(RaT(Q))≃D(Ra)#D(T(Q))≃U#C(τQ)≃K.(10) \begin{aligned} E(a,0)&\simeq D(R^aT(Q))\\ &\simeq D(R^a)\mathbin{\#}D(T(Q))\\ &\simeq U\mathbin{\#}C(\tau Q) \simeq K. \end{aligned} \qquad\text{(10)}

Here UU is the unknot. These are the actual marked cap isotopies of the reductions paper, not commutations of open tangles. They retain components and the transported framing; the final zero-framing correction gives the same reduced Kashaev invariant. The last step rotates the entire closed picture: the physical τ\tau permutes the prescribed cylindrical closing pairs {0,3},{1,2}\{0,3\},\{1,2\} and their empty outside arcs. It is not an erasure of a half-turn on an open tangle.

Fix either parity for aa, even parity for bb, and the original core parities. Vary aa through that infinite parity class, keeping b=0b=0. Equation (10) identifies every resulting knot with KK. If KK failed the identity, one fixed (m+2)(m+2)-leaf template would have infinitely many failing assignments. ▫\square

For an explicit family in original surgery coordinates, take ηa=ηb=0\eta_a=\eta_b=0 at the two new circles. Then (pa,pb,𝒑Q)=(r,0,𝒑Q)(p_a,p_b,\mathbf p_Q)=(r,0,\mathbf p_Q), r∈ℤr\in\mathbb Z, gives (a,b)=(−2r,0)(a,b)=(-2r,0). These are distinct original assignments with the core parameters fixed. This one retained pattern passes the full knot test: even changes in a braid power preserve both endpoint pairings and any internal component, while (10) shows that the resulting link is exactly the knot KK.

In particular, for the entire strict Conway class the following statements are equivalent:

  1. Every fixed admissible template has finitely many exceptional hyperbolic assignments.
  2. Every hyperbolic strict Conway core satisfies the volume identity at every integer assignment.

The implication from 2 to 1 has no exceptions at all; the reverse implication is (9)–(10). The proposition makes the increase in template size explicit. It does not claim that all hyperbolic knots lie in this Conway class.

6. A reduction scheme that does not assume its conclusion

For one fixed input template, a valid finite proof can have three kinds of terminal record:

  1. A whole-boundary source theorem proves the volume identity for all sufficiently long remaining original assignments. Only the finitely many choices of a bounded remaining coordinate require successor records.
  2. An exact reduction reaches a knot whose volume identity is already proved, or a branch with no hyperbolic final knot.
  3. An unresolved final record has a finite fiber in the original assignment space.

The reduction tree must have finite branching and a strictly decreasing nonnegative integer complexity, or a specified lexicographic combination of such complexities. Then it is finite. The union of the type-3 fibers is finite. This is the elementary finite-branching argument, now with an exact fiber test available for affine reduction branches.

A type-2 reduction is allowed to pass through an intermediate exterior outside the current geometric source class. What it cannot do is declare its hyperbolic closed descendants solved merely because that intermediate exterior has simplified. The padded family (9) already proves this independently of any classification of those intermediate exteriors.

Nor can an essential-tangle or JSJ decomposition alone bypass the issue. If it supplies an exact reduction of a padded family, every original assignment still represents the same core in (10). A decomposition theorem might locate that core and remove unnecessary parameters, but the required numerical volume identity for the core is unchanged. This observation asserts no unproved JSJ classification.

For a single fixed template, a finite reduction tree may expose only finitely many collapsed hyperbolic cores. Proving their individual identities could finish that template. A theorem for every strict template cannot use a fixed list of solved cores as a substitute: Proposition 3 lets any hyperbolic strict core appear in this role.

The present long-filling and short-component theorems therefore give genuine terminal records of type 1 and 2, but not a general reduction theorem of this form. The remaining general input must handle the connected short sources and their exact degenerations, including every collapsed hyperbolic core that has an infinite original fiber. This is the precise point at which a purely cofinite induction would otherwise become circular.

7. Why arbitrarily long presentations in finitely many parents do not bypass this

A different proposed shortcut would be to represent a fixed core by arbitrarily long fillings of an already controlled strict parent, until the existing long-filling theorem applies. Within a fixed finite collection of such parents this cannot work in general. The following obstruction follows directly from the primitive volume differential already proved in the strict geometry paper; no assertion about cancellation in quantum sums is needed.

Proposition 4. Fix one complete strict parent YY, a nonempty set of its crossing-circle cusps to fill, and a hyperbolic knot exterior XX. There is a constant B(Y,X)B(Y,X) such that no filling of those cusps with all |pi|>B(Y,X)\lvert p_i\rvert>B(Y,X) is isometric to XX.

Here the other cusps, if any, stay complete, and an asserted homeomorphism to XX can be substituted for isometry whenever its hyperbolic volume is known to be the same. The conclusion is that a fixed XX cannot occur arbitrarily far out in the region where every slope is long; it is not a finiteness assertion about all possible presentations of XX.

Proof. Write ri=1/pir_i=1/p_i. The near-complete implicit solution also exists with the meridian equation

Ui+piVi=−2πiλ,0≤λ≤1,(11) U_i+p_iV_i=-2\pi i\lambda,\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1, \qquad\text{(11)}

for all sufficiently small real rir_i. To see the uniformity, solve Vi+riUi=−2πiλriV_i+r_iU_i=-2\pi i\lambda r_i near the complete point: the derivative with respect to the deformation variables at r=0r=0 is the same invertible diagonal matrix for every λ∈[0,1]\lambda\in[0,1]. The implicit neighborhoods can be chosen uniformly on that compact interval, and local uniqueness patches the solutions. The original positive chain and its bounded frames remain in their fixed near-complete neighborhood.

In the conventions of that paper, Ui=−2πiχiU_i=-2\pi i\chi_i and Vi=τ̃iUiV_i=\widetilde\tau_i U_i, where the removable holomorphic ratio satisfies Im⁡τ̃i>0\operatorname{Im}\widetilde\tau_i>0. Equation (11) gives

χi=λriτ̃i+ri,Re⁡Vi=2πλri2Im⁡τ̃i|τ̃i+ri|2>0(λ>0).(12) \chi_i=\frac{\lambda r_i}{\widetilde\tau_i+r_i}, \qquad \operatorname{Re}V_i= \frac{2\pi\lambda r_i^2\operatorname{Im}\widetilde\tau_i} {\lvert\widetilde\tau_i+r_i\rvert^2}>0 \quad(\lambda>0). \qquad\text{(12)}

Let 𝒱\mathcal V be the volume of the original ideal chain. Its proved differential is

d𝒱=12∑i(Re⁡VidIm⁡Ui−Re⁡UidIm⁡Vi).(13) d\mathcal V=\frac12\sum_i \bigl(\operatorname{Re}V_i\,d\operatorname{Im}U_i -\operatorname{Re}U_i\,d\operatorname{Im}V_i\bigr). \qquad\text{(13)}

The real and imaginary parts of (11) imply Re⁡Ui=−piRe⁡Vi\operatorname{Re}U_i=-p_i\operatorname{Re}V_i and dIm⁡Ui+pidIm⁡Vi=−2πdλd\operatorname{Im}U_i+p_i\,d\operatorname{Im}V_i =-2\pi\,d\lambda. Hence

d𝒱dλ=−π∑iRe⁡Vi<0(λ>0).(14) \frac{d\mathcal V}{d\lambda} =-\pi\sum_i\operatorname{Re}V_i<0 \quad(\lambda>0). \qquad\text{(14)}

At λ=0\lambda=0, the absolute volume is Vol⁡(Y)\operatorname{Vol}(Y); at λ=1\lambda=1, the calibrated chain is the smooth filled metric. Consequently every such long filling has volume strictly smaller than Vol⁡(Y)\operatorname{Vol}(Y). Its volume tends to Vol⁡(Y)\operatorname{Vol}(Y) as all ri→0r_i\to0, by continuity of the same chain at the complete point.

If no B(Y,X)B(Y,X) existed, a sequence with min⁡i|pi|→∞\min_i\lvert p_i\rvert\to\infty would have every filled volume equal to Vol⁡(X)\operatorname{Vol}(X). Convergence would force Vol⁡(X)=Vol⁡(Y)\operatorname{Vol}(X)=\operatorname{Vol}(Y), contrary to the strict inequality for each member of the sequence. ▫\square

The maximum of these constants works for a fixed finite set of parents. Thus an exact rewriting procedure cannot guarantee a proof for an arbitrary fixed core simply by making every coefficient arbitrarily long while keeping the parent in a finite controlled set. At least one short parameter, a degeneration outside those parents, or unbounded parent complexity must remain. This agrees with, but is independent of, the affine-fiber obstruction above.

8. A terminal splitting inside the original circle-tree class

There is a padding construction that stays within the original ordered circle-tree syntax, including its subclass of paths. It does not use the enlarged strict closing grammar of Section 5. It also makes a stronger statement for one prescribed template: cofiniteness for a single enlargement forces the identity at every hyperbolic assignment of the smaller template.

Fix a nontrivial original ordered circle-tree template QQ. Choose its terminal root as in the circle-tree paper. Closed cyclicity puts the evaluated expression in the form KQ(s,𝒏)=C(RsT(F𝒏)).(15) K_Q(s,\mathbf n)=C(R^sT(F_{\mathbf n})). \qquad\text{(15)} Here ss is the terminal crossing power; 𝒏\mathbf n contains the other original powers. The ordered expression F𝒏F_{\mathbf n} and all its disk frames are retained. Write τ=T2\tau=T^2 for the physical half-turn, and define one enlarged template PQ(a,v,b,𝒏)=C(RaT(RvT(RbT(τF𝒏)))).(16) P_Q(a,v,b,\mathbf n)= C\!\left(R^aT\!\left(R^vT\!\left(R^b T(\tau F_{\mathbf n})\right)\right)\right). \qquad\text{(16)} This replaces the terminal vertex by a three-vertex path. It adds two vertices and does not create a bare product junction. The half-turn τ\tau propagates through the old expression by reversing its indicated stacks, commuting with TT, and preserving every signed integral power. Thus (16) is one fixed original ordered circle-tree template. If QQ is a path, so is PQP_Q.

Proposition 5. At v=0v=0, for every integer assignment, PQ(a,0,b,𝒏)≃KQ(a+b,𝒏).(17) P_Q(a,0,b,\mathbf n) \simeq K_Q(a+b,\mathbf n). \qquad\text{(17)} The isotopy retains all components and transports ribbons. Fix the carriers of QQ, writing s=ηs−2pss=\eta_s-2p_s. The same enlarged template works simultaneously for all assignments of QQ; it is not chosen anew after fixing a core knot.

A residual finite exceptional list of smaller cores is insufficient. This is a necessary implication of the original target, not a change to it.

9. What the rational normal form leaves unresolved

The exact rational corridor normal form reduces a fixed nondegenerate corridor to a chain of one sign with coefficient magnitudes at least two, with integral boundary shifts and a recorded proper rotation. Its shifts are injective translations of surviving original parameters. It therefore gives a valid reduction of the required source theorem to that family of chains. It does not provide their whole-boundary asymptotics.

A simple exact family shows why the available reductions do not finish this step. Consider the normalized negative continued fraction with r≥2r\ge2 entries, all equal to three. Put k0=1,k1=3,kj=3kj−1−kj−2.(22) k_0=1,\qquad k_1=3,\qquad k_j=3k_{j-1}-k_{j-2}. \qquad\text{(22)} Its normalized two-boundary matrix is (kr−1kr−2krkr−1),krkr−1=[3,…,3]−.(23) \begin{pmatrix} k_{r-1}&k_{r-2}\\ k_r&k_{r-1} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \frac{k_r}{k_{r-1}}=[3,\ldots,3]_- . \qquad\text{(23)} The continuant identity kr−12−krkr−2=1k_{r-1}^2-k_rk_{r-2}=1 follows by induction from (22). In particular this is the determinant-one normalized matrix, not only a slope calculation. Also 1<kr−1<kr−11<k_{r-1}<k_r-1. Hence neither diagonal entry is congruent to 11 or −1-1 modulo krk_r. The exact one-vertex compression criterion therefore rules out compression of this whole corridor to one internal vertex, even allowing the prescribed integral boundary shifts and proper half-turn.

The ceiling algorithm is unique, so normalization again gives the same string of threes. There is no zero or unit coefficient to eliminate and no string of twos to compress. Original path vertices have degree at most two, so re-rooting cannot meet the high-valence threshold in the hybrid tree theorem. For r≥3r\ge3, the new internal 3,33,3 kernel also leaves an adjacent short filling: its proved composition rule requires a sufficiently long intervening power. These observations concern the stated reductions and source theorems; they do not exclude a different exact identity or a future uniform chain theorem.

For a fixed original template, finite branching can produce only finitely many fixed short records at each stage. That does not make their original assignment fibers finite, nor does it prove the missing boundary theorem for such a record. Equations (20)–(21) show that even a record representing one fixed hyperbolic core can already carry the full obstruction. Accordingly, a complete reduction argument still has to do both things: establish the original source theorem for every short record it uses, and settle every unresolved hyperbolic core whose original fiber is infinite. The existing normal form, local eliminations and high-valence theorem do not jointly supply those two conclusions.

10. Which short corridors can be made long, and what conjugation preserves

This section concerns exact presentations. It neither gives a counterexample to the volume conjecture nor asserts that an arbitrary fixed-level quantum representation is faithful.

First apply the marked degree-two moves only where their valence and attachment hypotheses hold. A unit deletes one vertex and translates the two neighboring powers; a zero merges powers and retains the fiber test of Sections 1–3. The physical half-turns, branch ordering, primitive curves, and original component test are part of each record. At a branching vertex these degree-two moves are not automatic. For a fixed remaining rational corridor, the exact ceiling normal form then has internal magnitudes at least two. Its integral end twists are bijective translations of the still-free original parameters; its degenerate zero case is a merger, not such a translation.

Thus the local elimination rules remove 0,±10,\pm1 from an irreducible degree-two record, but they do not remove adjacent coefficients of magnitudes two through seven. The new path source theorem proves the original scalar and a local outside packet for the stated positive-seven and signed-eight classes. The whole-boundary upgrade now supplies the additional real-period and quadratic-strip estimates required by the long-turn reset theorem. Path endpoints already have canonical degree at most N−1N-1, so this is a proved admissible source. This does not give the same conclusion for an arbitrary adjacent short block.

10.1. An exact threshold criterion for the two uniform-sign forms

Let 𝒃=(b1,…,bm)\mathbf b=(b_1,\ldots,b_m), bi≥2b_i\ge2, be the normalized negative list of a nondegenerate fixed corridor, and let

[D(𝒃)]−=[𝒃]−[𝒃]−−1.(24) [D(\mathbf b)]_-= \frac{[\mathbf b]_-}{[\mathbf b]_--1}. \qquad\text{(24)}

The full matrix and affine marking results in the definite normal form and the complement classification show that, with the original two outside frames, the only uniform-sign lists with magnitudes at least two, allowing integral end twists and the retained proper rotations, are 𝒃\mathbf b and D(𝒃)D(\mathbf b). The pendant assertion uses the original cap and its established stabilizer, not an arbitrary attached tangle.

Proposition 6. Fix an integer K≥3K\ge3. Such a corridor has a uniform-sign presentation with all internal magnitudes at least KK if and only if at least one of the following holds:

  1. Every bi≥Kb_i\ge K.
  2. The list is all twos, of length m≥K−1m\ge K-1.
  3. For some k≥1k\ge1, 𝒃=(2r0,3,2r1,…,3,2rk)\mathbf b=(2^{r_0},3,2^{r_1},\ldots,3,2^{r_k}), with r0,rk≥K−2r_0,r_k\ge K-2 and ri≥K−3r_i\ge K-3 for 0<i<k0<i<k.

These are exact marked presentation criteria; applying a source theorem still requires that theorem’s boundary hypotheses.

Proof. Uniqueness leaves only the two lists just specified. The recursion for the complement is

D((a))=(2a−1),D((a,𝒃′))=(2a−2,d1+1,d2,…),D(𝒃′)=(d1,d2,…).(25) D((a))=(2^{a-1}),\qquad D((a,\mathbf b'))=(2^{a-2},d_1+1,d_2,\ldots), \quad D(\mathbf b')=(d_1,d_2,\ldots). \qquad\text{(25)}

For its output to have no two, the input must be binary with both ends two, apart from the excluded singleton two. For a binary input its complement is exactly

D(2m)=(m+1),D(2r0,3,…,3,2rk)=(r0+2,r1+3,…,rk−1+3,rk+2).(26) D(2^m)=(m+1),\qquad D(2^{r_0},3,\ldots,3,2^{r_k}) =(r_0+2,r_1+3,\ldots,r_{k-1}+3,r_k+2). \qquad\text{(26)}

Comparing each entry with KK proves necessity and sufficiency. The matrix equality, fixed end shifts, and proper rotation are those of the cited normal form, so no outside parameter is identified with another. ▫\square

For example K=7K=7, the positive path theorem with complete retained ends, and its whole-boundary upgrade give admissible two-boundary kernels for every fixed binary corridor with endpoint runs of twos of length at least five and internal runs of length at least four. The older threshold-64 proof gave the stronger run requirements 62 and 61. This is a family of arbitrarily long fixed short corridors, not a bounded list of knot checks. Their end twists are retained using the proved canonical boundary-multiplication theorem. All words remain fixed as the quantum level tends to infinity.

10.2. Arbitrarily long conjugators do not remove a cyclic short core

Use the actual pillowcase linear generators

S=(0−110),Ab=(1b01),U=SA1.(27) S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_b=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad U=SA_1. \qquad\text{(27)}

The presentation proved in Section 4 of the definite-normal-form note is PSL2(ℤ)=⟨S,U∣S2=U3=1⟩=C2*C3\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)=\langle S,U\mid S^2=U^3=1\rangle=C_2*C_3. A reduced word alternates nonidentity syllables from the two factors. For a cyclically reduced word of length greater than one, record the cyclic sequence of exponents 11 or 22 of its UU-syllables; SS occurs between consecutive recorded syllables. Conjugacy preserves this sequence up to cyclic rotation. Indeed, when a reduced conjugator meets a cyclically reduced word, cancellation either removes an outer conjugator syllable or moves one end syllable of the word to the other end. Iterating gives precisely the cyclic rotations.

Proposition 7. Suppose

C(𝒃)=SAb1⋯SAbm,|bi|≥3.(28) C(\mathbf b)=SA_{b_1}\cdots SA_{b_m},\qquad |b_i|\ge3. \qquad\text{(28)}

In its cyclic UU-exponent sequence, the block bib_i leaves at least |bi|−2|b_i|-2 consecutive equal exponents. In particular, for every r≥1r\ge1, the element (SA3)r(SA_3)^r is not conjugate to any word (28) all of whose powers have magnitude at least four. More strongly, if it is conjugate to a word (28), every one of that word’s powers has magnitude exactly three.

Proof. Since A1=SUA_1=SU, the original blocks are

SAb=U(SU)b−1(b>0),SA−b=S(U2S)b(b>0).(29) SA_b=U(SU)^{b-1}\quad(b>0),\qquad SA_{-b}=S(U^2S)^b\quad(b>0). \qquad\text{(29)}

At a positive-positive seam, the two adjacent UU’s merge to U2U^2. At a negative-negative seam, the adjacent SS’s cancel and the adjacent U2U^2’s merge to UU. At opposite-sign seams there is no cancellation. This description also applies at the cyclic seam. Each block has at least three UU-syllables before these operations, so its first and last syllables are distinct and at least one interior syllable remains. There is consequently no further cancellation. At most the first and last UU-syllables of each block change; its remaining |bi|−2|b_i|-2 interior syllables have the same exponent and remain consecutive in the recorded sequence.

For a positive list, the entire cyclic exponent sequence is 2,1b1−2,2,1b2−2,…,2,1bm−22,1^{b_1-2},2,1^{b_2-2},\ldots,2,1^{b_m-2}, up to rotation. For bi=3b_i=3 it alternates 2,12,1, with no constant run of length two. Conjugacy preserves that fact, so (29) forces every |bi|−2≤1|b_i|-2\le1. ▫\square

This rules out a genuine conjugation of the all-three cyclic core into the signed-eight path class, regardless of the length or size of the conjugating word. Proper marked rotations cannot evade it: their pillowcase linear part is the identity. It does not classify all isotopies of a particular capped tangle, and it does not forbid a different nonconjugate presentation or an analytic theorem for the original core.

At the exact source level a similarity also transports both observations. If QN′=PNQNPN−1Q'_N=P_NQ_NP_N^{-1}, then

(ℓNPN−1)QN′(PNaN)=ℓNQNaN.(30) (\ell_NP_N^{-1})Q'_N(P_Na_N)=\ell_NQ_Na_N. \qquad\text{(30)}

Here PNP_N denotes the invertible conjugating operator, not the Casimir polynomial. Unitary carrier scalars and the actual physical rotation are retained separately by the marked quantum identities. Keeping the old cap or readout instead of the transported one changes the observed tangle. Thus (30) provides no new nonzero coefficient for the original observation. The long-turn reset theorem starts with an already admissible source; it cannot be invoked for an arbitrary short block solely because that block has been conjugated.

There is a positive similarity for JM3JM^3, namely M(JM3)M−1=κM*MM(JM^3)M^{-1}=\kappa M^*M, but the positive-cycle theorem retains two different transformed boundary vectors for the original open kernel. Its positivity controls matched adjoint caps and the cyclic trace. It is not a positivity theorem for all transported bilinear observations in (30).

The resulting next source problem is therefore concrete: prove a whole-boundary theorem for the normalized chains with coefficients at least two, or a closure theorem for their genuinely adjacent short blocks, with the marked boundary chirps and full normal data. For branching components the corresponding multi-boundary source is still needed. The affine-fiber obligation of Sections 6–8 continues to apply even if this rational-source problem is solved.
