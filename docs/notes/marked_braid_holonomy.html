The actual cut period, its flag torsor, and its classical holonomy

Research note, 26 October 2026. This continues the relative octahedral calculation. It identifies the global classical critical correspondence of the actual cut state with a framed knot representation variety, retaining the boundary vector that gives the original invariant. It also identifies a split Borel framing torsor and proves a boundary-wedge/complete-regulator rigidity statement for the crossing Bloch sum. No assertion about which critical component contributes to the finite period is made.

The holonomy braid map and existence of representation-associated diagrammatic critical points have existing coverage. Section 9 records it. The proofs here are direct calculations from our fixed actual R coefficients, not imports of those papers’ proofs.

1. An exact change of boundary vector, at every level and color

Put q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2, rho=q-q^(-1), x=q^alpha, M=-qx as in the ADO note. Let

F_a=(Q;Q)_a, 0<=a<N,
psi_a=1/F_a, phi_a=F_a/N.                       (1.1)

These coefficients are units, F_(N-1)=N, and

sum_a phi_a psi_a=1.

Let C_N(x) be the actual zero-framed cut operator of a knot. The previously proved intertwining and irreducibility calculation gives C_N(x)=F_N(M) I at generic color; Laurent continuation extends the matrix identity to exceptional colors. Therefore

phi^t C_N(x) psi=F_N(M)                         (1.2)

for every color, including the Kashaev color M=1. This is a bilinear algebraic pairing, with no complex conjugation.

Now use the basis w_a=v_a/F_a. A matrix transforms by D^(-1)CD, where D=diag(1/F_a). In this basis psi is the constant vector 1 and phi is the normalized averaging functional (1/N)sum_a. Equation (1.2) becomes

F_N(M)=(1/N) sum_(a,b) (C_N^new(x))_(a,b).       (1.3)

This is an exact presentation of the existing invariant. It is useful because its boundary vector has a simple geometric flag, without fixing a state coordinate to its endpoint a=0.

2. The actual relative meridian module in that basis

Let A be multiplication by Q^a on functions of a modulo N, and let sigma f(A)=f(QA). Let e_partial be the idempotent supported at a=N-1. The actual conjugated quantum-group matrices are

K=x A^(-1),
F=(1-A)sigma^(-1),
rho^2 E=q^(-1)(x^(-1)-x A^(-1))sigma
           -c(x)e_partial sigma,
c(x)=q^(-1)(x^(-1)-Qx)
     =q^(-1)x^(-1)(1-M^2).                     (2.1)

For F the coefficient at a=0 vanishes, so the cyclic formula already has the actual nilpotent endpoint. For E, cancellation of the adjacent factorials gives

rho^2 E_(a,a+1)=q^(-1)(x^(-1)-xQ^(-a)), a<N-1.

The cyclic formula would add the entry from 0 to N-1 with coefficient c(x). Subtracting it gives precisely the actual truncated E. This proves (2.1), including its exceptional row.

Write E_cyc for the operator in (2.1) before subtracting this rank-one term. Multiplication around its orbit gives

(rho^2 E_cyc)^N=(x^N-x^(-N))I, (rho^2 E)^N=0.   (2.2)

Indeed the product for the first operator is q(-N)x(-N)(1-x(2N))=xN-x^(-N). At M=1 the wall coefficient c and the central defect both vanish, and the cyclic and actual E operators agree. Away from that point E_cyc is a comparison module; replacing E by E_cyc in the knot contraction has not been justified and is not done here.

The full modified dimension now has another exact interpretation:

d_N(M)=q^(N(1-N)/2) c(x)/(x^N-x^(-N))
       =i^(N+3) M^(N-1)/(1+M^2+...+M^(2N-2)).   (2.3)

To prove it, use qx-(qx)^(-1)=-c(x) in the defining modified-dimension formula. Thus the full meridian factor is a specified phase times the ratio of a local boundary defect to the cyclic central defect. At M=1 evaluate their whole ratio, giving d_N(1)=i^(N+3)/N. The two zeros must not be evaluated separately. This agrees with the missing-transition calculation in the preceding note; it retains its full peripheral function.

This is the local color representation, with its internal state-index wall. Turning that wall into a specified global inhomogeneous meridian A-equation requires elimination with the source retained. That identification is not assumed here.

3. Crossing Bloch sums change by a boundary coboundary

For a crossing with input indices a,b and output indices c,d, the basis change gives exactly

r_new=F_c F_d r_old/(F_a F_b).                  (3.1)

For the positive crossing c=b+k,d=a-k, put A=Q^a, B=Qb,C=Qk,X=x^2. Its non-Gaussian finite product is now

F_(b+k)/(F_b F_k) * (QX/A;Q)_k.

Its five-dilogarithm symbol is

beta_+=[B]+[C]+[X/A]-[BC]-[XC/A].               (3.2)

The Gaussian and sign in the preceding note are unchanged. For the inverse crossing c=b-k,d=a+k the product and symbol are

F_(a+k)/(F_a F_k) * (QX/B;Q)_k,
beta_-=[A]+[C]+[X/B]-[AC]-[XC/B].               (3.3)

The difference between either new symbol and the old one is

[A]+[B]-[U]-[V], U=Q^c,V=Q^d.                 (3.4)

It cancels on each shared strand of a finite contraction. The cut vector and covector transform at the remaining endpoints as in Section 1. Thus this basis change does not lose a global period or insert an unspecified normalization.

Both (3.2) and (3.3) have the form

[u]+[v]+[w]-[uv]-[vw].                         (3.5)

They have the five ordered tetrahedron shapes

v, u, 1/(uv), w, 1/(vw),

with ideal vertices infinity,0,1,v,uv, v(1-w)/(1-vw), by the previous cross-ratio proof. The positive case uses (u,v,w)=(B,C,X/A); the negative uses (A,C,X/B). The inverse orientation is consistent: substituting (A,B,C)->(B/C,AC,1/C) in beta_+ gives -beta_- by [1/C]=-[C]. No additional negative sign is inserted in the negative coefficient’s already computed symbol.

4. Deriving the rational crossing map from the actual symbols

For clarity, derive it first in the old basis. Its physical coordinates do not change with (3.1). Let p,r denote the classical eigenvalues of the input Q-shifts and put

P(A)=(1-A)(1-x^2/A)/x,
K_1=x/A, e_1=P(A)p, f_1=p^(-1),
K_2=x/B, e_2=P(B)r, f_2=r^(-1).                (4.1)

These are the commuting symbols of K,rho^2 E,F. They obey

K_i+K_i^(-1)+e_i f_i=x+x^(-1).                 (4.2)

In the new basis, the same coordinates are

K=x/A, e=(1-x^2/A)p_new/x, f=(1-A)/p_new,
p_new=(1-A)p.                                  (4.3)

Let S be the positive generating expression G_++Phi already derived from the actual R coefficient. Its output coordinates are U=BC,V=A/C. The canonical relation on the constraint UV=AB is

exp(S_a)=p_out,2/p,
exp(S_b)=p_out,1/r,
exp(S_c)=p_out,1/p_out,2.                       (4.4)

Here derivatives are in log A,log B,log C. Substitution of the exact contiguity symbols gives

p_out,1=Ar/(Cx),
p_out,2=(BC/x)*(1-A)(1-x^2/A)
                  /((1-A/C)(1-x^2C/A))*p,
C=1/(1-e_1 f_2).                               (4.5)

The last equality follows on cancelling the common factors in the third equation of (4.4); equivalently r(C-1)=C P(A)p. It is not a guessed holonomy map.

Write d=1-e_1 f_2. In physical coordinates the resulting map R is

K'_1=K_2 d,       K'_2=K_1/d,
f'_1=K_1 f_2/d,   e'_2=e_1/(K_2 d),
e'_1=(d e_2+e_1(dK_2-K_2^(-1)))/K_1,
f'_2=K_2(d f_1+f_2(d/K_1-K_1)).                 (4.6)

The inverse is also derived from the negative crossing symbol. Writing c=1-(K_1/K_2)e_2 f_1, it is

K'_1=K_2 c,       K'_2=K_1/c,
e'_1=K_1 e_2,     f'_2=f_1/K_2,
f'_1=f_1+f_2/K_1-f_1/(K_2^2 c),
e'_2=K_2 e_1+e_2-K_1^2 e_2/c.                 (4.7)

All equations are rational identities on their specified open charts. The symbolic audit derives both (4.6) and (4.7) from their respective generating relations and checks that they compose to the identity.

5. A matrix proof that this is meridian holonomy

Define two triangular matrices and a determinant-one matrix by

b_+(K,e)=[ K e ],  b_-(K,f)=[ 1  0 ],
         [ 0 1 ]              [-Kf K ]
G=b_-^(-1)b_+=[ K       e       ].
                [ Kf  K^(-1)+ef ]

The crossing map satisfies three exact factorizations:

b'_(+,2)b'_(+,1)=b_(+,2)b_(+,1),
b'_(-,2)b'_(-,1)=b_(-,2)b_(-,1),
b'_(+,2)b'_(-,1)=b_(-,2)b_(+,1).                (5.1)

The last follows immediately from the four simple entries of (4.6); the other two are the preserved coproducts. Direct substitution proves all three. The map also swaps the two Casimirs K+K^(-1)+ef.

For two strands define

H_1=G_1, H_2=b_(-,1)^(-1)G_2 b_(-,1).

Equations (5.1) imply

H'_2=H_1, H'_1=H_1^(-1)H_2 H_1.                (5.2)

For example H’2=(b’(-,2)b’(-,1))^(-1)b’(+,2)b’(-,1) =b(-,1)^(-1)b_(+,1)=H_1. The total product H_2H_1 is preserved, which gives the other equality. This proves the Hurwitz/Wirtinger conjugation rule by matrix multiplication.

For b strands, set B_(-,j)=b_(-,j)…b_(-,1) and

H_j=B_(-,j-1)^(-1)G_j B_(-,j-1).               (5.3)

The same calculation applies to adjacent strands; later prefix products do not change because their two factors have the same product in (5.1). Hence (5.3) intertwines every mixed braid word with the ordinary conjugation action on meridians. Composition uses the order in which the actual operator acts: rightmost factor first when a braid is written as a matrix product. The conjugation action uses that same convention.

This map is birational, with an explicit inverse. Given H_j, let P_j=H_j…H_1. When its upper-left entries are nonzero put

B_-(P)=[  1     0  ], B_+(P)=[ P_11 P_12 ],
        [-P_21 P_11]          [   0    1  ]

so P=B_-(P)^(-1)B_+(P). Recover b_(+,j)=B_+(P_j)B_+(P_(j-1))^(-1), and the analogous b_(-,j). In particular K_j=(P_j)11/(P(j-1))_11. This proves the inverse, rather than only counting dimensions.

The braid relations now follow from (5.2). On any common regular chart these are identities of schemes, including Artin test rings; the rational identities clear to polynomial identities with unit denominators. No reducedness or isolated-critical-point assertion is needed.

The actual meridians in this convention are mu_j=-H_j. Their traces are M+M^(-1), since x=-M at the classical symbol. The central minus sign preserves (5.2). The cut’s unit vector p_new=1 gives f=1-A,e=(1-x^2/A)/x, and directly

G (1,1)^t=x^(-1)(1,1)^t.                       (5.4)

Thus it selects the peripheral eigenflag 1, with meridian eigenvalue M^(-1). The inverse is explicit; this flag must not be silently called the M-eigenflag when marking holonomies.

6. The actual cut’s critical locus and a nonempty geometric chart

Close strands 2,…,b by the actual pivotal trace and use the constant vector and normalized average on the first strand. For internal variables the exponentiated stationary equations are precisely equality of the closed position and momentum coordinates. At the two cut endpoints the constant boundary vectors give p_new,in=p_new,out=1.

The pivotal factor on a closed strand is

x^(1-N)Q^(a(N-1)).

With h=Log Q, N=2pii/h, its leading logarithmic expression is 2pii(log A-log x). Its internal momentum multiplier is one. The residual writhe factor x^(w(N-1)) depends only on x. Thus these factors do not change the exponentiated internal stationary equations. Their logarithmic branches and peripheral derivatives remain part of the period.

Preservation of the product of K’s forces the two cut positions to agree once all the other strands have closed. Equation (4.3) then makes their physical e,f agree as well. Consequently the critical equations are exactly

R_beta(z)=z, with the first meridian eigenflag equal to 1.   (6.1)

Conversely every point of this open fixed locus satisfies the critical equations and the cut boundary conditions.

Under (5.3), (6.1) is the familiar presentation

<mu_1,...,mu_b | beta(mu_j)=mu_j>

of the braid-closure knot group, with the fixed eigenflag. This presentation follows by applying van Kampen to the braid strands and identifying their endpoints: at each crossing (5.2) is exactly the understrand meridian relation. Thus we have an identification of open critical schemes with framed, augmented representation schemes. This is not yet a claim about the classical symbol of a minimal summed meridian operator.

There is a useful uniform nonemptiness result. Fix any such representation whose meridians are noncentral, and its selected meridian eigenvector w. This includes the complete hyperbolic representation. Choose a covector alpha with alpha(w)=1, then a vector v with alpha(v)=1. In the basis (v,w-v), the chosen flag is 1. For every partial product P in every stage of the finite braid word, its upper-left entry is alpha(Pv).

For one strand in such a stage, e_j=0 exactly when alpha is a left eigenline of H_j. Avoid these finitely many covector lines. Also f_j=0 exactly when v is an eigenline of P_(j-1)^(-1)P_j. These matrices are conjugate to a noncentral meridian, so again there are only finitely many forbidden lines. The conditions alpha(Pv)=0 and v=w exclude finitely many additional points of the affine line alpha(v)=1. None of alpha(Pv)=0 vanishes identically there: P is invertible. One can therefore avoid all of them simultaneously.

This proves that all K_j,e_j,f_j can be made nonzero at every stage, in a frame respecting the actual cut flag. The positive and negative crossing denominators then remain nonzero. Moreover all five shapes in (3.5) avoid 0 and 1: the input conditions exclude A,B in {1,x^2}, the output conditions exclude the corresponding U,V, and e_1 f_2!=0 (respectively e_2 f_1!=0) excludes C=1. This asserts nondegenerate tetrahedron shapes; it does not assert positivity or distinctness of every pair of nonadjacent developed octahedron vertices.

Thus every hyperbolic knot’s complete representation lies in the actual cut state’s complex, exponentiated critical locus on a chart with nondegenerate tetrahedra. A chosen such frame works on a Zariski open neighborhood of the point in the augmented representation scheme. No alternatingness is needed.

7. The framing torsor is explicitly split

Over the irreducible augmented character locus, fixing the selected flag to 1 leaves the group

B_1=Stab_(PGL_2)(1),

a two-dimensional split Borel group. It acts freely on framed representations because their projective centralizer is trivial. Its orbits are precisely the framed realizations of one augmented character. The full framed locus is a B_1 torsor; the regular critical chart is an open subset of that torsor. The open chart is not asserted to be preserved by every B_1 element.

It can be split algebraically. For a non-elementary representation choose fixed group words g,h such that

p, rho(g)p, rho(h)p

are distinct. Send this ordered triple to (1,0,infinity). The unique fractional transformation is rational in its three points and gives a section on their nondegenerate locus. If necessary choose the two words so this frame is also regular in Section 6: the infinite projective orbit is Zariski dense in P^1, its square is dense in the space of pairs, and the good frames form a nonempty open subset. The words can then be held fixed near the complete character.

Equivalently, over the augmented function field H^1(k,B_1)=0. After sending the flag to infinity, B_1 is G_a semidirect G_m. For a Galois cocycle, Hilbert 90 kills its multiplicative part. Its remaining additive cocycle b_gamma is killed by averaging: a=(1/|G|)sum_gamma b_gamma satisfies b_gamma=a-gamma(a). This is a proof of the splitting, not an assumed rigidity claim for the quantum period. It removes an ordinary framing torsor; it does not kill a horizontal difference-extension class or the central Bloch regulator.

8. A global boundary wedge and complete-frame regulator rigidity

Let beta_beta be the sum of (3.2)–(3.3) over the braid, with their actual input/output variables. Work rationally in F^* tensor Q and use delta[z]=z wedge (1-z). Let S be the total classical generating expression, including the retained Gaussian factors. Write x for the common meridian coordinate, and put

L_intr=exp((1/2)[partial_(Log x) S]_critical).    (8.1)

This is the intrinsic conjugate coordinate from the actual meridian q-shift: h=Log Q is twice its step. Initially this is on the logarithmic/root cover specified by the coefficients. The derivative in (8.1) holds internal coordinates fixed before restricting to the exponentiated critical equations; a global flattening for a critical primitive is not being assumed. No topological longitude identification is built into its name.

The subsequent exact longitude proof now supplies that identification. Its finite meridian insertion fixes the square-root sign by an integer-position coboundary. The knot’s eigenvector transport then proves that (M,L_intr), with M=-x classically, is the preferred peripheral pair on the M-eigenflag. The actual cut uses the opposite M^(-1)-flag, whose longitude eigenvalue is L_intr^(-1).

There is an exact rational wedge identity on the critical locus:

delta beta_beta=-2 x wedge L_intr.              (8.2)

Here is the proof. For one crossing, write its five shapes as monomials z_j in A,B,C,x with signs eps_j. If X_i denotes one of these four multiplicative generators, then

delta beta=-sum_i X_i wedge exp(partial_i Phi).

The Gaussian G has symmetric quadratic coefficient matrix in their logarithms, so sum_i X_i wedge exp(partial_i G)=0. Its constant signs are torsion and disappear rationally. Hence Phi may be replaced by S=G+Phi in this identity. The generating relations (4.4) then give exactly

delta beta=A wedge p_in,1+B wedge p_in,2
     -U wedge p_out,1-V wedge p_out,2
     -x wedge exp(partial_(Log x) S).

For the inverse crossing use U=B/C,V=AC and S_c=Log p_out,2-Log p_out,1; the same formula results. Internal leg terms cancel. Closed legs have equal positions and momenta, and the two cut momenta are 1. The last term is -2x wedge L_intr by (8.1). This proves (8.2). It is the previous boundary coefficient -1/2 in m=x2,l=L_intr2. The wedge alone does not fix the marked longitude. The subsequent vector-transport proof linked above fixes its flag, sign and framing.

At the complete color x=-1, (8.2) is zero in the rational wedge. Thus beta_beta is a Bloch class on this critical component. Applying the directly proved Bloch–Wigner differential shows

d D(beta_beta)=0

along its framing torsor. The good complex frame locus is a nonempty Zariski open subset of the connected rational group B_1, hence connected. Its regulator value is independent of the regular frame. This is an explicit classical rigidity argument for the actual crossing sum.

The crossing fundamental-cycle proof now identifies this complete rational Bloch class with the marked idealized fundamental class, and hence the constant with Vol(M). It gives a three-five-term certificate, a four-tetrahedron diagram face table, and the degree-one peripheral check. This supplies the classical datum that the wedge alone did not fix. The quantum source and selected period must additionally be transported, and its exponential norm established. Existence of the critical component does not show that its period coefficient is nonzero or that other components do not cancel it.

9. Coverage, verification, and the remaining target

Coverage checked after deriving these formulas:

  • Kashaev–Reshetikhin, braiding for quantum gl_2 covers holonomy braiding at roots of unity. We reconstruct the rational map from the actual crossing symbols and prove its conjugation rule by (5.1).
  • McPhail-Snyder–Reshetikhin, cyclic modules and flat connections states a four-holonomy-dilogarithm factorization. It is coverage of a comparison framework, not a theorem identifying its geometric-character invariant with the original cut period.
  • Cho, existence of an optimistic-limit solution covers critical equations associated with representations. The existence statement here therefore carries no novelty claim. Its boundary and meridian conventions are instead derived from (1.1)–(2.3) of the actual literature state.

The audit and results check both symbolic generating maps, their inverse, all three matrix factorizations, the exact Hurwitz rule, and the cut flag. They check 300 mixed braid words, each of length twelve, in three finite fields, together with braid relations and Gauss reconstruction. At ten levels, including even and composite ones, they check the boundary vectors, rank-one E correction, cyclic defect and nilpotency, and dimension ratio. Thirty actual cut contractions at levels 2 through 6 give the same period under the boundary/basis change. A three-field algebraic figure-eight calibration checks a nondegenerate geometric critical chart. A high-precision complex calibration checks the regulator’s sign and frame independence against its two-regular- tetrahedron value in three frames. These are validations, not the general mechanism.

The original volume conjecture remains the target. This note supplies an actual-state geometric critical locus, its framing torsor, and a global rational Bloch boundary formula; the next note identifies its complete fundamental-class lift. A global quantum source comparison, the minimal meridian operator’s geometric classical factor, and the selected volume limit remain unproved.

The subsequent regular holonomy-braiding note derives a quantum counterpart of this classical transport. It gives rational generator formulas, the full unrestricted root-center map, and a regular module/intertwiner line through the actual color. Their checked center map is the inverse map (4.7), as required by pullback. Its pointed determinant normalization preserves the original zero-framed knot period after the explicit twist compensation. A global relative gluing source and its selected regulator period remain unproved.
