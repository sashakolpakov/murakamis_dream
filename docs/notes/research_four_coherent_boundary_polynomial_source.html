A polynomial boundary equation for the original four-coherent source

22 November 2026. Written research proof. Earlier final notes and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The original pairing of two coherent products has an exact finite boundary equation. On a fixed product of two boundary parameters it is a polynomial eigenfunction with a known extreme coefficient. Away from specified color resonances, these data determine that polynomial uniquely. At the resonances the actual polynomial family, rather than a normalization by a vanished coefficient, retains the source.

This concerns the full Frobenius contraction. It is not a positive orthogonality assertion, a scalar lower estimate, or a volume theorem.

1. The original scalar and its finite sum

Fix N≥2N\ge2, and put d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,η=(−1)d,κ=eπid2/(2N).(1) d=N-1,\qquad q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad Q=q^2,\qquad \eta=(-1)^d,\qquad \kappa=e^{\pi i d^2/(2N)}. \qquad\text{(1)} Use the full original algebra and the centered basis f0,…,fdf_0,\ldots,f_d of [C]. In particular, in its Newton basis, ϕ(eiej)=δi+j,d,Jei=ed−i,R+=Nf0,R−=Nfd,R+R−=1.(2) \phi(e_i e_j)=\delta_{i+j,d},\qquad Je_i=e_{d-i},\qquad R^+=\sqrt N f_0,\quad R^-=\sqrt N f_d,\quad R^+R^-=1. \qquad\text{(2)} All first derivatives at repeated Casimir roots are included in this algebra and in ϕ\phi. No distinct-root replacement is made.

Define unnormalized coherent polynomials and their actual scalar by Ua=∑j=0dajfj,ΦN(y,z;s,t)=ϕ(UyUzUsUt).(3) U_a=\sum_{j=0}^d a^j f_j,\qquad \Phi_N(y,z;s,t)=\phi(U_yU_zU_sU_t). \qquad\text{(3)} It is a polynomial of degree at most dd in each of its four parameters. Write DN(a)=1−ηaN,FN(a)=∑j=0d(−qa)j=DN(a)1+qa,GN(r)=∑j=0drj,b=yz,r=st.(4) \begin{split} D_N(a)&=1-\eta a^N,\qquad F_N(a)=\sum_{j=0}^d(-qa)^j=\frac{D_N(a)}{1+qa},\\ G_N(r)&=\sum_{j=0}^d r^j,\qquad b=yz,\quad r=st. \end{split} \qquad\text{(4)} The quotients in (4) denote the displayed polynomials at their removable points. Also set Hj(y,z)=(Q;Q)j(Qyz;Q)j(−qy;Q)j+1(−qz;Q)j+1,(a;Q)j=∏h=0j−1(1−aQh).(5) H_j(y,z)= \frac{(Q;Q)_j(Qyz;Q)_j} {(-qy;Q)_{j+1}(-qz;Q)_{j+1}},\qquad (a;Q)_j=\prod_{h=0}^{j-1}(1-aQ^h). \qquad\text{(5)} The full output coefficient formula [E, equation (5)] gives UyUz=∑j=0dBj(y,z)ej,Bj(y,z)=κ2(−q)jDN(y)DN(z)NHj(y,z).(6) U_yU_z=\sum_{j=0}^d B_j(y,z)e_j,\qquad B_j(y,z)=\frac{\kappa^2(-q)^jD_N(y)D_N(z)}{N}H_j(y,z). \qquad\text{(6)} Initially (5)–(6) can be read off the displayed denominator locus. After cancellation they are identities of the original polynomial family, including at its source resonances.

The elementary phase identity κ2(−q)d=1\kappa^2(-q)^d=1, together with (2), proves the exact finite formula ΦN=κ2DN(y)DN(z)DN(s)DN(t)N2∑j=0dHj(y,z)Hd−j(s,t).(7) \Phi_N= \frac{\kappa^2D_N(y)D_N(z)D_N(s)D_N(t)}{N^2} \sum_{j=0}^d H_j(y,z)H_{d-j}(s,t). \qquad\text{(7)} Thus this sum already contains the original closing and all its Hermite channels.

For later use, reflection of the finite products gives Bd−j(s,t)=GN(r)(−q)j(−q/s,−q/t;Q)j(Q,Q/r;Q)j.(8) B_{d-j}(s,t)=G_N(r)(-q)^j \frac{(-q/s,-q/t;Q)_j}{(Q,Q/r;Q)_j}. \qquad\text{(8)} Here and below a tuple in a shifted factorial means a product. For an explicit verification, (Q;Q)d=N(Q;Q)_d=N, and (Q;Q)d−j=N(−1)jQj(j+1)/2(Q;Q)j. (Q;Q)_{d-j}= \frac{N(-1)^jQ^{j(j+1)/2}}{(Q;Q)_j}. Removing the last jj factors of the other three products in (6) produces, for 1≤h≤j1\le h\le j, the ratio (1+qsQ−h)(1+qtQ−h)1−rQ−h=−Q1−h(1+qQh−1/s)(1+qQh−1/t)1−Qh/r. \frac{(1+qsQ^{-h})(1+qtQ^{-h})}{1-rQ^{-h}} =-Q^{1-h} \frac{(1+qQ^{h-1}/s)(1+qQ^{h-1}/t)}{1-Q^h/r}. Its product and κ2(−q)d=1\kappa^2(-q)^d=1 give exactly (8), including the factor (−q)j(-q)^j. No parity sign has been suppressed.

Let 𝔭N(y,z;r)=κ2NFN(y)FN(z)GN(r),U=−q/s,V=−q/t,μ=U+V,UV=Q/r,Wj=(U,V;Q)j,aj=Qj(Qb;Q)j(−q3y,−q3z,Q/r;Q)j.(9) \begin{split} \mathfrak p_N(y,z;r)&=\frac{\kappa^2}{N}F_N(y)F_N(z)G_N(r),\\ U&=-q/s,\quad V=-q/t,\quad \mu=U+V,\quad UV=Q/r,\\ W_j&=(U,V;Q)_j,\qquad a_j=\frac{Q^j(Qb;Q)_j}{(-q^3y,-q^3z,Q/r;Q)_j}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(9)} Equations (6) and (8) now prove ΦN=𝔭N∑j=0dajWj.(10) \boxed{\quad \Phi_N=\mathfrak p_N\sum_{j=0}^d a_jW_j.\quad} \qquad\text{(10)} The entire prefactored expression in (10), not each potentially singular factor separately, is continued at removable parameters. Equivalently this is the finite j=0,…,dj=0,\ldots,d series ΦN=𝔭N4ϕ3(Q−d,Qb,−q/s,−q/t−q3y,−q3z,Q/r;Q,Q).(11) \Phi_N=\mathfrak p_N\, {}_4\phi_3\!\left( \begin{matrix}Q^{-d},\ Qb,\ -q/s,\ -q/t\\ -q^3y,\ -q^3z,\ Q/r\end{matrix};Q,Q\right). \qquad\text{(11)} At the root, (Q−d;Q)j/(Q;Q)j=1(Q^{-d};Q)_j/(Q;Q)_j=1 for j<Nj<N. Formula (11) means this finite series; cancellation does not authorize extending its index beyond dd.

2. A finite triangular operator calculation

The following calculation supplies its own proof of the boundary operator. Temporarily use a generic base pp, generic parameters α,β,γ\alpha,\beta,\gamma, and h=γph=\gamma p. For symmetric Laurent polynomials under u↦h/uu\mapsto h/u, define ℬ(u)=(u−αp)(u−βp)(u−γp)(u−h)(u2−h)(u2−ph),𝒟(u)=p(u−1)2(βu−γ)(αu−γ)(u2−h/p)(u2−h),ℒf(u)=ℬ(u)[f(u/p)−f(u)]+𝒟(u)[f(pu)−f(u)].(12) \begin{split} \mathcal B(u)&= \frac{(u-\alpha p)(u-\beta p)(u-\gamma p)(u-h)} {(u^2-h)(u^2-ph)},\\ \mathcal D(u)&= \frac{p(u-1)^2(\beta u-\gamma)(\alpha u-\gamma)} {(u^2-h/p)(u^2-h)},\\ \mathcal L f(u)&=\mathcal B(u)[f(u/p)-f(u)] +\mathcal D(u)[f(pu)-f(u)]. \end{split} \qquad\text{(12)} Put wk(u)=(u,h/u;p)kw_k(u)=(u,h/u;p)_k, with w0=1w_0=1. Then ℒwk=λkwk+χkwk−1,λk=(p−k−1)(1−αβpk+1),χk=−p−k(1−pk)(1−αpk)(1−βpk)(1−γpk)(k≥1).(13) \begin{split} \mathcal L w_k&=\lambda_k w_k+\chi_k w_{k-1},\\ \lambda_k&=(p^{-k}-1)(1-\alpha\beta p^{k+1}),\\ \chi_k&=-p^{-k}(1-p^k)(1-\alpha p^k) (1-\beta p^k)(1-\gamma p^k) \qquad(k\ge1). \end{split} \qquad\text{(13)} Also ℒw0=0\mathcal Lw_0=0.

Here are the polynomial and endpoint details. Direct substitution gives ℬ(h/u)=𝒟(u)\mathcal B(h/u)=\mathcal D(u), so (12) preserves symmetry. At u2=phu^2=ph, the first shift exchanges u,h/uu,h/u, and its difference vanishes; the other shifted fold is identical. At u2=hu^2=h, the residues of ℬ,𝒟\mathcal B,\mathcal D are opposite, and the two shifted values of a symmetric function agree. Thus these apparent poles cancel. At zero and infinity the Laurent degree does not increase. Every symmetric Laurent polynomial of degree at most kk is a polynomial of degree at most kk in u+h/uu+h/u: subtract its extreme symmetric Laurent pair and induct. Consequently ℒwk\mathcal Lw_k is such a polynomial.

At infinity, ℬ→1\mathcal B\to1, 𝒟→pαβ\mathcal D\to p\alpha\beta, and the leading terms of the two shifts are p−kp^{-k} and pkp^k times the original. Their combination gives λk\lambda_k. At u=p−mu=p^{-m}, for 0≤m≤k−20\le m\le k-2, both neighboring values of wkw_k vanish. For m=0m=0 the reverse coefficient vanishes instead. Thus ℒwk\mathcal Lw_k has all the k−1k-1 generic roots of wk−1w_{k-1}. It follows that the remaining coefficient in (13) is a scalar χk\chi_k.

Evaluate at u=p1−ku=p^{1-k}. Only the forward shifted term survives. The ratio of its first shifted factorial to the corresponding factor of wk−1w_{k-1} is 1−p−k1-p^{-k}. The other ratio is (hpk;p)k(hpk−1;p)k−1=(1−hp2k−2)(1−hp2k−1)1−hpk−1. \frac{(hp^k;p)_k}{(hp^{k-1};p)_{k-1}} =\frac{(1-hp^{2k-2})(1-hp^{2k-1})}{1-hp^{k-1}}. These factors cancel exactly against the two denominators and the last numerator of ℬ(p1−k)\mathcal B(p^{1-k}). What remains is (1−p−k)(1−αpk)(1−βpk)(1−γpk)(1-p^{-k})(1-\alpha p^k)(1-\beta p^k)(1-\gamma p^k), which is the coefficient in (13). This proves the finite identity. Clearing its rational denominators extends it to all specializations where the resulting polynomial expressions are defined; no assertion about distinct roots after specialization is required.

3. The exact original boundary equation and its folds

In (12)–(13), specialize p=Q,α=−qy,β=−qz,γ=r−1,u=U=−q/s.(14) p=Q,\qquad \alpha=-qy,\quad\beta=-qz,\quad\gamma=r^{-1}, \qquad u=U=-q/s. \qquad\text{(14)} Let σ(s,t)=(Qs,Q−1t)\sigma(s,t)=(Qs,Q^{-1}t). Direct simplification gives B=(t+q)2(1−Qys)(1−Qzs)(t−s)(t−Qs),D=(s+q)2(1−Qyt)(1−Qzt)(Qt−s)(t−s),L=B(σ−1)+D(σ−1−1),λk=(Q−k−1)(1−Qk+2b),χk=−Q−k(1−Qk)(1+qyQk)(1+qzQk)(1−Qk/r).(15) \begin{split} B&=\frac{(t+q)^2(1-Qys)(1-Qzs)}{(t-s)(t-Qs)},\\ D&=\frac{(s+q)^2(1-Qyt)(1-Qzt)}{(Qt-s)(t-s)},\\ L&=B(\sigma-1)+D(\sigma^{-1}-1),\\ \lambda_k&=(Q^{-k}-1)(1-Q^{k+2}b),\\ \chi_k&=-Q^{-k}(1-Q^k)(1+qyQ^k)(1+qzQ^k)(1-Q^k/r). \end{split} \qquad\text{(15)} Thus LWk=λkWk+χkWk−1LW_k=\lambda_kW_k+\chi_kW_{k-1}. The elementary coefficient check for (10), directly at the root, is aj+1χj+1=−Q−j(1−Qj+1)(1−bQj+1)aj=(λd−λj)aj,0≤j<d.(16) a_{j+1}\chi_{j+1} =-Q^{-j}(1-Q^{j+1})(1-bQ^{j+1})a_j =(\lambda_d-\lambda_j)a_j,\qquad 0\le j<d. \qquad\text{(16)} Here QN=1Q^N=1 and d=N−1d=N-1 were used in the last equality. The coefficient of WdW_d already has eigenvalue λd\lambda_d. Summing (13) therefore proves the source identity LΦN=λdΦN,λd=(Q−1)(1−Qyz).(17) \boxed{\quad L\Phi_N=\lambda_d\Phi_N,\qquad \lambda_d=(Q-1)(1-Qyz).\quad} \qquad\text{(17)} The prefactor 𝔭N\mathfrak p_N is invariant under σ\sigma, so it has not been removed by a gauge or changed by this calculation. Only 0≤j≤d0\le j\le d occurs in (16). There is no assumed equality of the two endpoints of a continued summand.

For a version without a fold denominator, write Bnum,DnumB_{\mathrm{ num}},D_{\mathrm{ num}} for the two numerators in (15) and set Δ=(t−s)(t−Qs)(Qt−s). \Delta=(t-s)(t-Qs)(Qt-s). Then the exact polynomial identity is λdΔΦN=Bnum(Qt−s)(σΦN−ΦN)+Dnum(t−Qs)(σ−1ΦN−ΦN).(18) \boxed{\quad \lambda_d\Delta\Phi_N =B_{\mathrm{ num}}(Qt-s)(\sigma\Phi_N-\Phi_N) +D_{\mathrm{ num}}(t-Qs)(\sigma^{-1}\Phi_N-\Phi_N). \quad} \qquad\text{(18)} It holds for all four complex parameters, including s=0,t=0s=0,t=0, the shifted folds, and every individual color resonance. It was proved on a dense open set and both sides are polynomials. At a fold, source derivatives are retained by differentiating this whole polynomial identity. In particular, no value-only boundary condition is substituted for the full source there. Coincident shifted folds at N=2N=2 require no exception to (18). The argument uses only Qj≠1Q^j\ne1 for 0<j<N0<j<N, so composite levels are included.

There is a precise generic-base caution. If n=N−1n=N-1 is fixed and the base is moved to pp, the terminating series associated with (12) has second numerator αβpn+1=pN+1yz,λn=(p1−N−1)(1−pN+1yz),q2=p.(19) \alpha\beta p^{n+1}=p^{N+1}yz, \qquad \lambda_n=(p^{1-N}-1)(1-p^{N+1}yz),\qquad q^2=p. \qquad\text{(19)} Keeping the root expression QyzQyz unchanged during that movement would give the wrong auxiliary eigenfunction. This note instead uses the root coefficient check (16). Its generic calculation (12)–(13) is a finite rational proof device, not an identification of a generic-qq quantum source or of its quantum-normal derivatives.

4. The selected polynomial and its resonant family

Fix r≠0r\ne0. Since ΦN(y,z;s,r/s)\Phi_N(y,z;s,r/s) is symmetric under s↔r/ss\leftrightarrow r/s, it is a polynomial of degree at most dd in μ=−q(s+t)/r\mu=-q(s+t)/r. The WkW_k, 0≤k≤d0\le k\le d, form a basis: their leading coefficients in μ\mu are (−1)kQk(k−1)/2(-1)^kQ^{k(k-1)/2}, which do not vanish.

Its leading coefficient is known from the original source: [μd]ΦN=κ2rdNGN(yz).(20) \boxed{\quad [\mu^d]\Phi_N =\frac{\kappa^2r^d}{N}G_N(yz).\quad} \qquad\text{(20)} Indeed the coefficient of sds^d at fixed rr is ϕ(UyUzfdf0)=ϕ(UyUz)/N=GN(yz)/N\phi(U_yU_zf_df_0)=\phi(U_yU_z)/N=G_N(yz)/N, by (2) and ϕ(fafb)=δab\phi(f_af_b)=\delta_{ab}. Since the extreme term of μd\mu^d is (−q/r)dsd(-q/r)^ds^d, (20) follows from (−q)−d=κ2(-q)^{-d}=\kappa^2.

For 0≤k<d0\le k<d, λd−λk=−Q−k(1−Qk+1)(1−yzQk+1).(21) \lambda_d-\lambda_k =-Q^{-k}(1-Q^{k+1})(1-yzQ^{k+1}). \qquad\text{(21)} Consequently, if yz∉{Q−1,…,Q−d}yz\notin\{Q^{-1},\ldots,Q^{-d}\}, the triangular equation (17), restricted to degree at most dd, has a unique eigenpolynomial with the leading coefficient (20). That coefficient is nonzero, since precisely the excluded values are the zeros of GN(yz)G_N(yz). This determines the original four-boundary source without fitting an unknown closed period.

At yz=Q−hyz=Q^{-h}, 1≤h≤d1\le h\le d, the actual source satisfies deg⁡μΦN≤h−1.(22) \deg_\mu\Phi_N\le h-1. \qquad\text{(22)} To see this, work first where the other displayed denominators do not vanish. The factor (Qyz;Q)j(Qyz;Q)_j is zero for every j≥hj\ge h, so (10) has degree at most h−1h-1. The higher polynomial coefficients then vanish identically on this color fiber by continuation, including its simultaneous resonances. A normalization by (20) at that fiber would discard the actual source.

The original polynomial family in y,z,s,ty,z,s,t supplies its full continuation. For example, fixing y≠0,r≠0y\ne0,r\ne0 and writing z=b/yz=b/y, differentiation gives, off the fold denominators, (Lb−λd(b))∂bΦN=(λd′(b)−∂bLb)ΦN.(23) (L_b-\lambda_d(b))\partial_b\Phi_N =(\lambda_d'(b)-\partial_bL_b)\Phi_N. \qquad\text{(23)} At the folds the same statement is read by differentiating (18). All derivatives of the known prefactor are included. These are external parameter derivatives; the internal repeated-root Hermite data were already present in (2)–(7). No extension in the quantum level is inferred from this family statement.

5. Two exact source readouts and actual gluing

The centered compiler gives Uja=κ(−q)j(−qQj)a/NU_{ja}=\kappa(-q)^j(-qQ^j)^a/\sqrt N. The finite geometric sum therefore proves, with their original phases, U−q=κ−1Ned,U−q−1=κN1.(24) U_{-q}=\kappa^{-1}\sqrt N e_d,\qquad U_{-q^{-1}}=\kappa\sqrt N\,1. \qquad\text{(24)} The Newton factors show that mm is evaluation at the first Casimir node: every eje_j, j>0j>0, vanishes there. Thus it is an algebra character. In particular, ϕ(edv)=m(v)\phi(e_dv)=m(v) and m(Ua)=κFN(a)/Nm(U_a)=\kappa F_N(a)/\sqrt N. Hence ΦN(y,z;−q,t)=κ2NFN(y)FN(z)FN(t),ΦN(y,z;−q−1,t)=κN𝒞N(y,z,t).(25) \boxed{\quad \Phi_N(y,z;-q,t)=\frac{\kappa^2}{N}F_N(y)F_N(z)F_N(t), \qquad \Phi_N(y,z;-q^{-1},t)=\kappa\sqrt N\,\mathcal C_N(y,z,t). \quad} \qquad\text{(25)} Here 𝒞N(y,z,t)=ϕ(UyUzUt)\mathcal C_N(y,z,t)=\phi(U_yU_zU_t) is the original three-coherent generating scalar, including its entire source. The first identity also follows from (10): at s=−qs=-q, U=1U=1, all WjW_j with j>0j>0 vanish, leaving its original prefactor. Both identities hold at both parities, including N=2N=2.

Finally, ϕ(JvJw)=ϕ(vw)\phi(Jv\,Jw)=\phi(vw) follows directly from (2). Put uN(a)=Ua/ZN(a)u_N(a)=U_a/\sqrt{Z_N(a)}, where ZN(a)=∑j=0d|a|2jZ_N(a)=\sum_{j=0}^d|a|^{2j}. The actual closing of two turned coherent products is ϕ(J(uN(y)uN(z))J(uN(s)uN(t)))=ΦN(y,z;s,t)ZN(y)ZN(z)ZN(s)ZN(t).(26) \phi\!\left(J(u_N(y)u_N(z))J(u_N(s)u_N(t))\right) =\frac{\Phi_N(y,z;s,t)} {\sqrt{Z_N(y)Z_N(z)Z_N(s)Z_N(t)}}. \qquad\text{(26)} The root shifts in (15) preserve these four normalization factors. There is no replacement of this bilinear closing by a Hermitian norm. Equations (17)–(26) describe a universal four-boundary primitive, not a new knot case or closure theorem for arbitrary trees.

6. Coverage, scope, and complete proof sources

The terminating series in (11) has the standard qq-Racah parameter pattern. See Koekoek–Swarttouw, equation (3.2.1), with parameters α=−qy,β=−qz,γ=(st)−1,δ=1\alpha=-qy,\beta=-qz,\gamma=(st)^{-1},\delta=1. Its standard difference equation is recorded there as (3.2.6). These are coverage references, not proof premises: (12)–(18) give the finite calculation here. No novelty is claimed for the named polynomial or its general difference equation, and no positive finite orthogonality theorem is used.

The new source statements identify the literal four-coherent period, its known normalization, its full resonant family, and its exact unit and socle readouts. No nonzero value at an arbitrary boundary point, geometric action estimate, positive-cycle metric, or finite-exception volume theorem follows. Scalar cancellation remains in the original source polynomial.
