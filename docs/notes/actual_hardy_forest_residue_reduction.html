Exact Hardy-forest residue reduction of the original selected cycle

28 October 2026. All Hardy/reset variables in the continuous-Gaussian cyclic period admit a simultaneous ordinary-residue reduction. The surviving cycle is specified explicitly. Every other annulus component is retained first and then proved zero against the original source by a one-way negative-index barrier.

The normal residue coefficient is exactly +1+1. This is the coefficient of the Hardy pole graph, not a computed intersection number with a hyperbolic thimble.

The exact audit and results check eight original whole-word pivotal matrices with no intermediate Hardy/head projection, 360 signed head columns, 812 negative-current subsets, nonzero surviving negative outputs before the original source, the pole-forest Jacobian, and reduced radius chambers.

1. Hardy poles form a forest

Keep the fully telescoped physical braid word, with its boundary pair fusions and original monomial input/output. Every Hardy factor connects consecutive variables of ONE head in word time:

Ke=11−gezout/zin. K_e=\frac1{1-g_e z_{\mathrm{out}}/z_{\mathrm{in}}}.

Here ge=1g_e=1, except at an actual Gaussian dilation where ge=cseαsξg_e=c_se^{\alpha_s\xi}. Transfer multiplication does not identify different heads. An active factorial kernel breaks the path; its input and output variables remain independent. The monomial source adds no closing Hardy edge. Thus the Hardy pole graph is a forest of head-time segments.

Choose one terminal variable per segment and set

ue=zingezout. u_e=\frac{z_{\mathrm{in}}}{g_ez_{\mathrm{out}}}. \tag{1}

The logarithmic incidence matrix is triangular/unimodular. Using path normals followed by the terminal coordinate gives determinant one with the induced orientation. The base-only dlog⁡ged\log g_e terms vanish when wedged with all Gaussian dξd\xi variables. Consequently

Kedue2πiue=due2πi(ue−1). K_e\,\frac{du_e}{2\pi i u_e} =\frac{du_e}{2\pi i(u_e-1)}. \tag{2}

Every normal pole is now independent, with residue +1+1. No scalar geg_e, determinant root, meridian factor, or Gaussian Jacobian is removed. Such factors remain in the substituted source and cyclic arguments.

2. A reduced cycle before taking the residues

The pole graph sets ue=1u_e=1:

zin=gezout. z_{\mathrm{in}}=g_ez_{\mathrm{out}}. \tag{3}

After imposing it, retain one complex variable per head-time segment and every real Gaussian variable. Equivalently, one may retain the initial coordinate of each head and a new coordinate after each active factorial. Moving the retained coordinate within a segment multiplies it by a known nonzero product of geg_e; its logarithmic Jacobian is one.

There are b+6mb+6m complex coordinates for the displayed strict mm-crossing physical word on bb heads. The two boundary pair-fusion frames of a 2k2k-plat add 6k6k, giving 6m+8k6m+8k in the unshortened presentation. Further exact simplifications may reduce this count. All counts are independent of NN.

Explicit radii without resets

Take ρ0=1/64\rho_0=1/64 and 0<λ≤10<\lambda\le1 below all active color moduli. The following construction works for every fixed Gaussian tuple and every signed word.

For one crossing, let its input head radii be R1,R2R_1,R_2, and put G=|cseαsξ|G=|c_se^{\alpha_s\xi}|. The inverse-fusion part is realized by

b=min⁡(ρ0R2/2,ρ0λR1/2),a=ρ0R1/2,c=max⁡(2b/ρ0,2aG*/(ρ0λ)), \begin{split} b&=\min(\rho_0R_2/2,\ \rho_0\lambda R_1/2),\\ a&=\rho_0R_1/2,\\ c&=\max(2b/\rho_0,\ 2aG_*/(\rho_0\lambda)), \end{split} \tag{4}

where G*=1G_*=1 for the positive crossing and G*=max⁡(1,G)G_*=\max(1,G) for the negative crossing.

The first reciprocal factorial updates head two to bb. Its following V−V_- transfer has argument modulus at most ρ0/2\rho_0/2. The reciprocal factorial on head one updates it to aa, and the positive factorial on head two updates it to cc.

Positively, the central transfer uses radii a,ca,c, then the Gaussian graph changes head two to c/Gc/G. Negatively, that Gaussian graph comes first and the central transfer uses a,c/Ga,c/G. Formula (4) makes its argument small in either order.

For the final fusion choose

e=min⁡(ρ0(c/G)/2,ρ0λa/2),f=2a/ρ0,q=2e/ρ0. e=\min(\rho_0(c/G)/2,\ \rho_0\lambda a/2),\quad f=2a/\rho_0,\quad q=2e/\rho_0. \tag{5}

Its reciprocal second-head diagonal updates to ee, its positive first-head factorial to ff, its U−U_- multiplier leaves these coordinates unchanged, and its final factorial updates head two to qq. Every cyclic argument has modulus below ρ0\rho_0. Spectator coordinates are simply identified, rather than being constrained by an extra radius inequality.

For a boundary fusion use its three diagonal updates and the same preparation of its one transfer; no Gaussian compensation is needed. The inverse boundary fusion is handled in the same way.

The full complex Gaussian relation in (3), including its phase, is imposed; (4)–(5) give its radii. All logarithms of the chosen radii satisfy

|log⁡rj|≤Cword(1+∑ℓ|ξℓ|). |\log r_j|\le C_{\mathrm{word}} \left(1+\sum_\ell|\xi_\ell|\right). \tag{6}

Indeed each update is a minimum/maximum of one preceding logarithm plus a fixed constant and, at most, a term ±log⁡G\pm\log G. There is no multiplicative recursion of logarithms. This gives a specified reduced fibered torus cycle, not separate incompatible local circles.

3. An exact simultaneous annulus decomposition

Let HH be the number of Hardy edges. Starting from the reduced radii above, choose

η=log⁡24(H+1). \eta=\frac{\log2}{4(H+1)}.

In the normal coordinates (1), allow e−η≤|ue|≤eηe^{-\eta}\le|u_e|\le e^\eta independently. Each unreduced head coordinate is a retained coordinate times a product of path normals and Gaussian factors. Each cyclic argument therefore changes in modulus by at most e2Hη<2e^{2H\eta}<\sqrt2. Since its reduced value is below 1/641/64, it remains below 1/161/16 throughout this complete polyannulus.

In particular NO cyclic norm divisor is crossed. Every cyclic root has its original anchored lift. Only the explicit independent poles ue=1u_e=1 occur in these annuli.

The outer torus |ue|=eη|u_e|=e^\eta has every Hardy argument strictly below one. It can be reached from the original reset-cycle chamber without crossing a pole or norm divisor: all radius constraints are affine strict inequalities in logarithmic radii, so interpolate linearly. Both cycles have cyclic arguments below 1/161/16 and Hardy arguments below one. Estimate (6) and the Gaussian majorant hold uniformly in this homotopy.

Now apply the ordinary annulus identity in each independent ueu_e:

∫|ue|=eηFdue2πi(ue−1)=F|ue=1+∫|ue|=e−ηFdue2πi(ue−1). \int_{|u_e|=e^\eta}\frac{F\,du_e}{2\pi i(u_e-1)} =F|_{u_e=1} +\int_{|u_e|=e^{-\eta}} \frac{F\,du_e}{2\pi i(u_e-1)}. \tag{7}

The other variables are retained. Iteration gives a finite sum of 2H2^H relative components: the full pole graph and all possible nonempty sets of inner circuits.

Nothing is shrunk to zero or infinity. Any information inside an inner circuit, including possible distant branch divisors, is still represented by that circuit. The assertion below is that its original source pairing is zero, not that it is zero in unpointed twisted homology.

On each inner circle,

Ke=−∑a<0(gezout/zin)a. K_e=-\sum_{a<0}(g_ez_{\mathrm{out}}/z_{\mathrm{in}})^a. \tag{8}

It is precisely the negative-Laurent current at that head-time edge. Residue edges implement ordinary coordinate identification/dilation. All these series converge absolutely in their stated chambers.

Every branch cycle has the same type of joint bound

CNBexp⁡{C0N+C1N∑|ξℓ|−N∥𝝃∥2/2}. C N^B\exp\{C_0N+C_1N\sum|\xi_\ell| -N\|\boldsymbol\xi\|^2/2\}.

For the absolute coefficient expansion, bound cyclic coefficients on a slightly larger disk still below one; the resulting absolute series has the same exponential-in-NN bound. Hardy series have a fixed geometric ratio e−ηe^{-\eta}. Thus Fubini/Tonelli and coefficient routing are justified, including the noncompact Gaussian base and the radius homotopy.

4. Every nonempty inner-current component is source-null

Use signed integer head indices; do NOT impose a Hardy projection. The exact scalar kernels have these elementary support properties:

  • a reciprocal factorial has only nonnegative diagonal coefficients, so it kills a negative active index;
  • a positive factorial has only diagonal indices at most d=N−1d=N-1; its negative Laurent coefficients are retained and may be nonzero;
  • a transfer has order h≥0h\ge0 and changes indices by (a,b)↦(a−h,b+h)(a,b)\mapsto(a-h,b+h) or (a+h,b−h)(a+h,b-h);
  • a Gaussian dilation preserves every signed index.

Consider the actual fully ordered crossing CDU+C−1C\,D\,U_+\,C^{-1} or CV+D−1C−1C\,V_+\,D^{-1}C^{-1}.

  1. In C−1C^{-1}, a new negative first index is lowered further before the reciprocal first-head factorial kills it. A negative second input is killed immediately by its first reciprocal second-head diagonal.
  2. A negative second index created by the central transfer survives any dilation, then is killed by CC’s first reciprocal second-head diagonal.
  3. The last U−U_- in CC can emit a negative FIRST head. Its other head is nonnegative and at most dd after the last positive factorial. At the next crossing touching the negative head, that head is killed in C−1C^{-1}: immediately if it is second, or after further lowering if it is first. An untouched negative head cannot heal.

Diagonal negative Laurent coefficients never change an index and therefore cannot repair it. This proof uses no cancellation of coefficients and is independent of colors or Gaussian values.

The original cup input is nonnegative. A boundary fusion applied to (d,0)(d,0) produces no negative survivor, since its last positive factorial requires h≤dh\le d. The terminal inverse-fusion frames kill any negative head. Alternatively, an original finite nonnegative output coefficient or pivotal cut kills any untouched negative output.

Thus there is NO supported route from any nonempty negative-current component in (7) to the original output source. Its exact pairing is zero. Arbitrary Laurent-healing coupons inserted inside the word are outside this statement; original finite coupons may be extended by zero outside their heads.

This is a route-support proof on the actual kernels, not reconstruction and evaluation of an entire prefix polynomial. Infinite cyclic tails are handled by the convergence argument in Section 3.

Combining (7)–(8), the original selected period equals the pure all-pole graph integral with coefficient exactly +1+1. All other circuits have been retained and explicitly proved source-null.

5. Explicit reduced scalar phase and cycle coordinates

After the residues:

  • each transfer is multiplication by its cyclic function in the two current head coordinates;
  • every spectator/reset coordinate is identified;
  • an active factorial retains a new complex coordinate and its cyclic convolution kernel;
  • a Gaussian identifies its input/output by w=gs(ξ)zw=g_s(\xi)z, and its ordinary Gaussian variable remains integrated on ℝ\mathbb R.

There are no rational Hardy poles left. The surviving cycle is exactly the fibered product of circles in (4)–(5), with the anchored cyclic roots, not an arbitrary positive-angle chain.

Here is the variable-dependent leading phase of one positive crossing explicitly. Let its input coordinates be z1,z2z_1,z_2, its six successive active factorial output coordinates be u1,…,u6u_1,\ldots,u_6, and put g=−(xy)−1eπ(1+i)ξg=-(xy)^{-1}e^{\sqrt{\pi}(1+i)\xi}. The output coordinates are u5,u6u_5,u_6. With limiting marked colors x,yx,y and phase convention exp⁡(W/h)\exp(W/h),

W+=−Li⁡2(u1/z2)+Li⁡2(u1/(xz1))−Li⁡2(u2/z1)+Li⁡2(u1/u3)−Li⁡2(u2/(xu3))−Li⁡2(gu4/u3)+Li⁡2(u2/u5)−Li⁡2(u4/(yu5))+Li⁡2(u4/u6)+2πi[log(u3/u1)+log(u5/u2)+log(u6/u4)]−πiξ2. \begin{split} W_+={}&-\operatorname{Li}_2(u_1/z_2) +\operatorname{Li}_2(u_1/(xz_1)) -\operatorname{Li}_2(u_2/z_1) +\operatorname{Li}_2(u_1/u_3)\\ &-\operatorname{Li}_2(u_2/(xu_3)) -\operatorname{Li}_2(gu_4/u_3) +\operatorname{Li}_2(u_2/u_5) -\operatorname{Li}_2(u_4/(yu_5)) +\operatorname{Li}_2(u_4/u_6)\\ &+2\pi i\left[ \log(u_3/u_1)+\log(u_5/u_2)+\log(u_6/u_4) \right]-\pi i\xi^2 . \tag{9} \end{split}

For a negative crossing use g=−(xy)eπ(1−i)ξg=-(xy)e^{\sqrt{\pi}(1-i)\xi} and replace its central term −Li⁡2(u2/(xu3))-\operatorname{Li}_2(u_2/(xu_3)) by +Li⁡2(gu2/(yu3))+\operatorname{Li}_2(gu_2/(yu_3)). All other terms follow the same strict order.

The full word phase is the sum of these local phases and boundary-fusion phases, together with 2πi2\pi i times the original initial-minus-terminal source logarithms. The original external χβ,N\chi_{\beta,N} is retained separately. Exact qq half-steps and the difference between NN and N−1N-1 contribute the specified nonzero amplitude; they are not discarded normalization.

All logarithmic marks in (9) come from the original reflected factorial monomials and source. Norm roots retain the continuation from the small-argument cycle.

6. Actual classical coordinates, rather than unnamed auxiliaries

This identifies concrete parts of the known actual fusion chart. For a full CxC_x put A=B1,J=B2A=B_1,J=B_2 and

This gives an explicit comparison with known classical flag variables, not a conclusion that the reduced cycle is already a positive Rivin-angle cycle.

7. What remains unproved

The selected auxiliary Hardy cycle has now been pushed forward exactly, with its normal degree +1+1, all inner currents accounted for, and an explicit reduced phase and cycle. The unreduced Hardy normal directions are no longer being treated as ordinary geometric stationary variables.

The reduced cycle is still the small-argument anchored cycle above. Its transport through cyclic norm divisors and the actual five-term flag atlas to a regular hyperbolic chart has not been proved. Neither the normal residue degree nor the existence of a geometric critical representation computes its geometric intersection number. In particular no volume conjecture or hyperbolic noncancellation is claimed here.
