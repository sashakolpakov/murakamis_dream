The coideal graph Gram has an octahedral volume action

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

The actual corrected coideal graph has an explicit positive Gram on every tail channel. Its complete finite product gives a uniform large-level profile, including the endpoint channels. That profile is the volume of a specified convex ideal octahedron. The graph and its projector therefore have sharp whole-space exponential costs.

The original marked row also simplifies in this graph. Its observed channel has Gram value exactly NN, rather than the maximal volume rate. These are source-specific metric and boundary calculations; they do not bound the original scalar below or identify a global geometric cycle after an arbitrary short component.

3. A uniform profile on the whole finite spectrum

Put t=A/N,ψ=πmin⁡(t,1−t),η=arcsin⁡(sin⁡ψ/2),Λ(u)=−∫0ulog⁡(2sin⁡v)dv(0≤u≤π), t=A/N,\quad \psi=\pi\min(t,1-t),\quad \eta=\arcsin(\sin\psi/\sqrt2),\quad \Lambda(u)=-\int_0^u\log(2\sin v)\,dv\quad(0\le u\le\pi), 𝒱ψ=2{2Λ(η)+Λ(ψ−η)−Λ(ψ+η)}.(13) \mathcal V_\psi =2\{2\Lambda(\eta)+\Lambda(\psi-\eta)-\Lambda(\psi+\eta)\}. \qquad\text{(13)} At ψ=0\psi=0 use the continuous value 𝒱0=0\mathcal V_0=0. There is a universal constant CC such that |loggN(μj)−Nπ𝒱ψj|≤Clog⁡N(N≥2,0≤j<N).(14) \boxed{\qquad \left|\log g_N(\mu_j)-\frac N\pi\mathcal V_{\psi_j}\right| \le C\log N \qquad(N\ge2, 0\le j<N).\qquad} \qquad\text{(14)} No channel cutoff or nondegenerate-endpoint assumption enters this bound.

Here are uniform estimates paying the logarithmic zeros. Write f(v)=log⁡|1−eiv|f(v)=\log|1-e^{iv}|, a periodic integrable function of mean zero. Remove the at-most-two cells nearest each logarithmic zero. On the other cells the total oscillation is bounded by ∫|f′|=O(log⁡N)\int|f'|=O(\log N). A removed sample is at most −log⁡N+C-\log N+C, whereas its cell average is at least −log⁡N−C-\log N-C, proving the upper estimate even at an exact zero. If all samples and their arcs stay at distance at least κ/N\kappa/N from a zero, the same estimate is two-sided, with constant depending only on κ>0\kappa>0. An endpoint cell changes the normalized arc integral by at most Clog⁡NC\log N. These observations also cover (12)’s half shifts.

4. The actual convex ideal octahedron

For 0<ψ≤π/20<\psi\le\pi/2, consider the one-parameter angle assignment consisting of two tetrahedra with angles (u,ψ−u,π−ψ)(u,\psi-u,\pi-\psi) and two with (u,ψ,π−ψ−u)(u,\psi,\pi-\psi-u), where 0<u<ψ0<u<\psi. Their common internal edge has alternating angles π−ψ,ψ\pi-\psi,\psi, of sum 2π2\pi; all boundary angle sums are ψ\psi or π−ψ\pi-\psi. These are off-critical angle assignments. They need not yet develop with consistent tetrahedral moduli; that gluing equation is imposed below. Its volume functional is 𝒲ψ(u)=2{2Λ(u)+Λ(ψ−u)−Λ(ψ+u)},𝒲ψ′(u)=2log⁡sin⁡(ψ−u)sin⁡(ψ+u)sin⁡2u.(18) \mathcal W_\psi(u)= 2\{2\Lambda(u)+\Lambda(\psi-u)-\Lambda(\psi+u)\},\qquad \mathcal W_\psi'(u)= 2\log\frac{\sin(\psi-u)\sin(\psi+u)}{\sin^2u}. \qquad\text{(18)} The ratio decreases strictly from infinity to zero. Its unique maximum is u=ηu=\eta, since 2sin⁡2η=sin⁡2ψ2\sin^2\eta=\sin^2\psi. Thus this is a concrete strictly concave angle-volume maximization, not an assignment of volumes to arbitrary signed summands.

The maximizing tetrahedra develop into a convex ideal octahedron. In the upper half-space boundary take 0,∞,1,v,−1,−v,v=rei(π−ψ),r=sin⁡ηsin⁡(ψ−η).(19) 0,\ \infty,\ 1,\ v,\ -1,\ -v,\qquad v=r e^{i(\pi-\psi)},\quad r=\frac{\sin\eta}{\sin(\psi-\eta)}. \qquad\text{(19)} The four tetrahedra around 0∞0\infty have alternating shapes v,−1/v,v,−1/vv,-1/v,v,-1/v. The first has angles (η,ψ−η,π−ψ)(\eta,\psi-\eta,\pi-\psi), by the ordinary sine rule. The maximizing equation gives r−1=sin⁡η/sin⁡(ψ+η)r^{-1}=\sin\eta/\sin(\psi+\eta), so the second has angles (η,ψ,π−ψ−η)(\eta,\psi,\pi-\psi-\eta).

For completeness all eight boundary planes support the convex hull. The equator (1,v,−1,−v)(1,v,-1,-v) is a strictly convex centrally symmetric quadrilateral. The four planes through ∞\infty are its vertical side planes. For an adjacent equatorial pair a,ba,b, the other face is the hemisphere through 0,a,b0,a,b. Its boundary circle has equation |z|2−ℓ(z)=0|z|^2-\ell(z)=0, with ℓ\ell real linear, ℓ(a)=|a|2\ell(a)=|a|^2, ℓ(b)=|b|2\ell(b)=|b|^2. The four positive tetrahedra fill this convex ideal octahedron, and their boundary dihedral sums are precisely ψ\psi or π−ψ\pi-\psi. Its volume is (13). This proves the local Rivin interpretation of the actual Gram rate.

The maximizing volume increases with ψ\psi: its derivative is 2log⁡[sin⁡(ψ+η)/sin⁡(ψ−η)]>02\log[\sin(\psi+\eta)/\sin(\psi-\eta)]>0 below π/2\pi/2. At ψ=π/2\psi=\pi/2, η=π/4,r=1\eta=\pi/4,r=1, giving the regular ideal right-angled octahedron, of volume 4GCat4G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}. Here GCat=∑n≥0(−1)n/(2n+1)2G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n/(2n+1)^2, and Λ(π/4)=GCat/2\Lambda(\pi/4)=G_{\mathrm{ Cat}}/2, directly from the sine series. The limiting volume at ψ=0\psi=0 is zero.
