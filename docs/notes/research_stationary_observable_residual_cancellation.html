Estimating a boundary derivative using the stationary equations

Research deduction, 6 November 2026.

The terminal comparison needs a bound for the boundary derivative of an action at its critical point. An enclosure of each critical coordinate separately can lose information: the coordinates satisfy the same system of stationary equations. Those equations may be subtracted from the observable before estimating its error. The resulting function has the same value on the critical set, and can have much smaller first derivatives in the uncertain coordinates.

This note proves that elementary implication. It does not supply new numerical signs, a finite-source estimate, or a volume theorem. Its intended application uses the existing terminal reference families and saved bounds for their original logarithms. It does not assert a new principle of asymptotic rigidity.

1. An exact identity with an approximate reference point

Let z∈ℂdz\in\mathbb C^d, b∈ℂb\in\mathbb C, and write x=(z,b)x=(z,b). Let F(x)∈ℂdF(x)\in\mathbb C^d and P(x)∈ℂP(x)\in\mathbb C be holomorphic on a domain containing all line segments used below. The actual reference family satisfies

F(z(b),b)=0.(1) F(z(b),b)=0. \qquad\text{(1)}

Choose a coordinate a=zka=z_k. Suppose the existing enclosure has the form

b=b0+δ,z=z0+uδ+ζ,|δ|≤r,|ζj|≤ηj.(2) b=b^0+\delta,\qquad z=z^0+u\delta+\zeta,\qquad |\delta|\le r,\quad |\zeta_j|\le\eta_j. \qquad\text{(2)}

The stored point x0=(z0,b0)x^0=(z^0,b^0) need not satisfy (1). Put U=(u,1)U=(u,1), and let EjE_j be the coordinate vector for zjz_j in ℂd+1\mathbb C^{d+1}. In particular h=x−x0=Uδ+∑jEjζjh=x-x^0=U\delta+\sum_j E_j\zeta_j.

For any fixed complex row w∈ℂ1×dw\in\mathbb C^{1\times d} and scalar λ∈ℂ\lambda\in\mathbb C, define

R(x)=P(x)−wF(x)−λ(a−a0).(3) R(x)=P(x)-wF(x)-\lambda(a-a^0). \qquad\text{(3)}

Taylor’s formula along the segment x0+thx^0+th, with real 0≤t≤10\le t\le1, gives the exact identity on (1):

P(x)=P(x0)−wF(x0)+λ(a−a0)+DR(x0)Uδ+∑jDR(x0)Ejζj+∫01(1−t)D2R(x0+th)[h,h]dt.(4) \begin{split} P(x)={}&P(x^0)-wF(x^0)+\lambda(a-a^0) +DR(x^0)U\,\delta +\sum_j DR(x^0)E_j\,\zeta_j\\ &+\int_0^1(1-t)D^2R(x^0+th)[h,h]\,dt. \end{split} \qquad\text{(4)}

All derivatives here are complex derivatives and all matrix products are ordinary products, without conjugation. The term −wF(x0)-wF(x^0) must be retained. Replacing the approximate stored point by a critical point without paying its residual would invalidate (4).

Suppose an interval for ℑ(P(x0)−wF(x0))\Im(P(x^0)-wF(x^0)) has midpoint p0p_0 and radius ρ0\rho_0. If

|DR(x0)U|≤μ,|DR(x0)Ej|≤μj, |DR(x^0)U|\le\mu,\qquad |DR(x^0)E_j|\le\mu_j,

and the last integral in (4) has modulus at most BB, then

|ℑP(x)−p0−ℑ{λ(a−a0)}|≤ρ0+rμ+∑jηjμj+B.(5) \left|\Im P(x)-p_0-\Im\{\lambda(a-a^0)\}\right| \le \rho_0+r\mu+\sum_j\eta_j\mu_j+B. \qquad\text{(5)}

This is a bound for the same actual reference family as (2). It does not restrict the independent target coordinates in a subsequent phase comparison. Once (5) is certified, the exact quadratic maximum in the source-cell comparison, Section 2, applies without any change to its original cells.

2. Eliminating the first-order coordinate errors

There is an exact choice of w,λw,\lambda that makes all the first derivatives in (4) vanish whenever the following nonsingularity conditions hold at x0x^0. Let

H=DzF(x0),f=DbF(x0),v=−H−1f,(6) H=D_zF(x^0),\qquad f=D_bF(x^0),\qquad v=-H^{-1}f, \qquad\text{(6)}

and suppose HH is invertible and vk≠0v_k\ne0. Here vv is defined by (6); it is not silently identified with an approximate stored predictor uu. Set

λ=Pb(x0)+Pz(x0)vvk,w=(Pz(x0)−λek𝖳)H−1.(7) \lambda=\frac{P_b(x^0)+P_z(x^0)v}{v_k},\qquad w=(P_z(x^0)-\lambda e_k^{\mathsf T})H^{-1}. \qquad\text{(7)}

Then Rz(x0)=0R_z(x^0)=0 directly. Also

wf=−(Pz−λek𝖳)v=−Pzv+λvk=Pb, w f=-(P_z-\lambda e_k^{\mathsf T})v =-P_zv+\lambda v_k=P_b,

so Rb(x0)=0R_b(x^0)=0. Thus DR(x0)=0DR(x^0)=0, and both first-order error terms in (4) disappear. This argument is valid whether or not F(x0)=0F(x^0)=0.

Equivalently, the map x↦(F(x),a)x\mapsto(F(x),a) has invertible derivative at x0x^0. The nonzero entry vkv_k is the precise condition that aa can serve as a local coordinate on any zero level of FF meeting that inverse chart. If F(x0)≠0F(x^0)\ne0, the inverse-function theorem alone does not assert that the chart meets F=0F=0. The actual family in (1)–(2) is a separate premise of the error estimate. No global inverse or new finite-source identity is asserted.

For a rigorous saved-data calculation one need not compute the exact complex values in (7). One may choose exact Gaussian rational w,λw,\lambda, then enclose the residual derivatives in (5) using the saved analytic intervals. The estimate remains valid for every such choice. A proposed inverse or a small numerical residual is not itself a certificate; the interval bounds μ,μj\mu,\mu_j must still be proved. No choice is prescribed or numerically tested in this note.

3. Combining logarithmic terms before bounding the remainder

The intended stationary equations and observable are affine functions plus linear combinations of the same original logarithmic primitives. More generally, suppose

F(x)=Faff(x)+∑ℓcℓj(ℓℓ(x)),P(x)=Paff(x)+∑ℓdℓj(ℓℓ(x)),(8) F(x)=F_{\mathrm{ aff}}(x)+\sum_\ell c_\ell j(\ell_\ell(x)),\qquad P(x)=P_{\mathrm{ aff}}(x)+\sum_\ell d_\ell j(\ell_\ell(x)), \qquad\text{(8)}

where cℓ∈ℂdc_\ell\in\mathbb C^d, dℓ∈ℂd_\ell\in\mathbb C, each ℓℓ\ell_\ell is affine, and the indicated branches of jj are holomorphic on the entire segment images. Zero coefficients may be inserted to put both formulas on the same list of arguments.

Write Lℓ=DℓℓL_\ell=D\ell_\ell, and suppose saved bounds give |j″|≤mℓ|j''|\le m_\ell on these images. Then a valid value of BB in (5) is

B=12∑ℓ|dℓ−wcℓ|mℓ(r|LℓU|+∑jηj|LℓEj|)2.(9) B=\frac12\sum_\ell |d_\ell-wc_\ell|\,m_\ell \left(r|L_\ell U| +\sum_j\eta_j|L_\ell E_j|\right)^2. \qquad\text{(9)}

Indeed the affine terms, including −λ(a−a0)-\lambda(a-a^0), have zero second derivative. Each remaining term contributes (dℓ−wcℓ)j″(ℓℓ(x))(Lℓh)2(d_\ell-wc_\ell)j''(\ell_\ell(x))\,(L_\ell h)^2 to the integrand in (4). Its modulus has the bound in (9), and ∫01(1−t)dt=1/2\int_0^1(1-t)\,dt=1/2. The coefficients must be combined before taking absolute values to obtain this particular bound. Evaluating PP and wFwF separately gives a valid but potentially larger bound.

For the original notation g′(z)=j(z)+iπ(1/2−z)g'(z)=j(z)+i\pi(1/2-z), one has g‴=j″g'''=j''. Thus the already saved full primitive curvature bounds are of the order required in (9). Their original argument domains and logarithmic lifts still have to be checked for each use; the identity alone does not enlarge those domains.

4. The two existing terminal representations

At an actual coupled terminal reference the shared stationary equation is

fa=g′(a+s)+g′(a+t)−3g′(a)+2πit=0.(10) f_a=g'(a+s)+g'(a+t)-3g'(a)+2\pi it=0. \qquad\text{(10)}

Consequently the two expressions

PO=32g′(a)−g′(a+s),PT=g′(a+t)−32g′(a)+2πit(11) P_O=\tfrac32g'(a)-g'(a+s),\qquad P_T=g'(a+t)-\tfrac32g'(a)+2\pi it \qquad\text{(11)}

represent the same boundary derivative, since PT−PO=faP_T-P_O=f_a. This identity is already present in the original terminal action and height-derivative formulas; see the height-sign proof and the plane allocation. It is one particular subtraction of a stationary equation.

The two expressions generally differ at the stored approximate point. Any Taylor estimate must use the chosen expression’s own constant, derivative and remainder. The whole-affine terminal representation can be estimated using the smaller posterior error of tt, while (3)–(9) also permit combinations of several stationary equations. Whether either choice pays the five remaining phase comparisons is a separate quantitative question. No computation or successful sign is asserted here, and no frozen comparison record is changed.
