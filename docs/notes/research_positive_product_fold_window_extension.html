Full physical fold windows on a positive product chart

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The common product baseline can be normalized through the simultaneous complete fold using the actual three-fifths kernel theorem. This supplies the missing nearby physical values and independent first normals, with exponentially small normalized errors. The comparison uses the canonical kernel only on a small complete bidisk. Outside that bidisk it uses the actual physical-row bounds, rather than an off-grid bound for the canonical kernel.

An exact two-line repair then includes a whole fixed fold window. A closed contour around both angular lifts, with its factor one-half, retains every double spectral normal and the odd simple value. No evenness of the repaired representative or equality of arbitrary free-color functions is asserted. Exterior sides and real interfaces remain.

1. Original data and the common baseline

Use the original normalization N≥2,h=N−1,η=(−1)N−1,Xj=(j+12)h,C(X)=2cos⁡(2πX),t(X)=π−2πX,PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,(1) N\ge2,\quad h=N^{-1},\quad \eta=(-1)^{N-1},\quad X_j=(j+\tfrac12)h,\quad C(X)=2\cos(2\pi X),\quad t(X)=\pi-2\pi X,\quad P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2, \qquad\text{(1)} and KN(z,w)=∑k=0N−1ek(z)ek(w)K_N(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e_k(z)e_k(w). Put JN=⌊(N−1)/2⌋J_N=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor. Its distinct physical rows are ca=C(Xa),Fa(w)=KN(ca,w),0≤a≤JN.(2) c_a=C(X_a),\qquad F_a(w)=K_N(c_a,w),\qquad 0\le a\le J_N. \qquad\text{(2)} For a≤⌊(N−2)/2⌋a\le\lfloor(N-2)/2\rfloor, the original independent normal is Ga(w)=∂zKN(ca,w)G_a(w)=\partial_zK_N(c_a,w). Retain the pinned pole-free representative Ĝa(w)\widehat G_a(w). It has exactly the value and required first spectral normal of GaG_a at every physical second endpoint. It is holomorphic on ℂ\[2,∞)\mathbb C\setminus[2,\infty); it is not the canonical normal polynomial at an arbitrary untraced endpoint.

Fix a positive pair and its product domains from the pinned product gluing theorem. Shrink to buffered inner angular domains 𝒰t,𝒰u\mathcal U_t,\mathcal U_u containing zero and the target. They are bounded and connected, lie in the original angular strips, and retain the single original phase ψ(t,u)\psi(t,u). All compact estimates below are taken with fixed larger buffers in those domains. The baseline of that theorem is denoted by HN(t,u)H_N(t,u); it is already phase normalized: UN(t,u)=eNψ(t,u)HN(t,u),supK|HN|≤eoK(N).(3) U_N(t,u)=e^{N\psi(t,u)}H_N(t,u),\qquad \sup_K|H_N|\le e^{o_K(N)}. \qquad\text{(3)} The baseline is holomorphic through (0,0)(0,0), but this fact alone does not prescribe any fold data.

Choose the common positive real seed intervals used to construct HNH_N inside a sufficiently small complete disk. More precisely, choose fixed numbers 0<ρ0<ρ1<R<3/5(4) 0<\rho_0<\rho_1<R<3/5 \qquad\text{(4)} such that the closed RR-disk lies inside both buffered inner angular domains. The two positive real seed intervals lie in (0,ρ0)(0,\rho_0), with fixed positive length and all required buffers. These choices are possible because the product domains contain a neighborhood of zero. The common baseline theorem then gives exponentially small errors in its two original bulk row families, as holomorphic functions of the other parameter.

2. Actual complete-disk comparison

The pinned three-fifths theorem concerns the full canonical polynomial: KN(−2cos⁡t,−2cos⁡u)=N−3/2a(t,u)eNψ(t,u)(1+Ohol(N−1)),a(t,u)≠0.(5) K_N(-2\cos t,-2\cos u) =N^{-3/2}a(t,u)e^{N\psi(t,u)} (1+O_{\mathrm{ hol}}(N^{-1})), \qquad a(t,u)\ne0. \qquad\text{(5)} It holds on a neighborhood of the closed three-fifths bidisk, at both parities and with all fixed ordinary spectral derivatives, including the ramification at t=0t=0 or u=0u=0. In particular its normalized value and fixed derivatives have polynomial bounds on our complete bidisk.

Lemma 1. With fixed buffers for the radii in (4), there is c>0c>0 such that ∂tp∂uq[HN(t,u)−e−Nψ(t,u)KN(−2cost,−2cosu)]=Op,q(e−cN)(6) \partial_t^p\partial_u^q \left[H_N(t,u)-e^{-N\psi(t,u)} K_N(-2\cos t,-2\cos u)\right] =O_{p,q}(e^{-cN}) \qquad\text{(6)} uniformly for |t|,|u|≤ρ1|t|,|u|\le\rho_1, for every fixed p,qp,q.

Proof. On the two real seed grids the normalized difference has exponentially small values, because the baseline’s actual line data are FaF_a, and Fa(w)=KN(ca,w)F_a(w)=K_N(c_a,w) exactly. Both functions in (6) are bounded by eo(N)e^{o(N)} on the buffered complete bidisk, by (3) and (5).

Apply the two successive dense-grid estimates of the pinned endpoint-role comparison. At the first stage subtract its exponentially small finite sinc correction and use the disk Blaschke product for the remaining exact zeros. Do the same in the second variable. The fixed angular spacing is 2π/N2\pi/N, rather than 1/N1/N; the affine coordinate change (1) gives exactly the same estimate. This yields an exponentially small bound on an open seed product, with a positive exponent independent of NN. The common complete bidisk is connected and both normalized functions have the required bounds there. A finite chain of buffered complex balls propagates the estimate to its smaller closed bidisk. Buffered Cauchy estimates prove all fixed derivatives. This is a comparison of these two bounded functions, not uniqueness inferred from finitely many samples alone. ▫\square

For later use write τa=π−(2a+1)π/N≥0\tau_a=\pi-(2a+1)\pi/N\ge0. The all-row theorem gives, uniformly over every physical row, the upper bound with its own phase |Fa(−2cos⁡u)|+|Ĝa(−2cos⁡u)|≤eNℜψ(τa,u)+o(N)(7) |F_a(-2\cos u)|+|\widehat G_a(-2\cos u)| \le e^{N\Re\psi(\tau_a,u)+o(N)} \qquad\text{(7)} on every fixed compact second-angle domain in the original strip. The normal term is used only at double rows. Fixed ordinary spectral derivatives satisfy the same estimate. On a compact with ℜ(−2cos⁡u)<2\Re(-2\cos u)<2, the o(N)o(N) loss can be replaced by a fixed power of NN. The actual midpoint relation in that source is Ψa/N(−2cos⁡u)=ψ(τa+π/N,u)\Psi_{a/N}(-2\cos u)=\psi(\tau_a+\pi/N,u); its monotone upper comparison gives (7) with τa\tau_a, including the fold. There is no replacement of the row action by its complete maximum.

Lemma 2. Uniformly over every double physical row with 0≤τa≤ρ00\le\tau_a\le\rho_0, e−Nψ(τa,u)[Ĝa(−2cos⁡u)−Ga(−2cos⁡u)]=O(e−cN)(8) e^{-N\psi(\tau_a,u)} [\widehat G_a(-2\cos u)-G_a(-2\cos u)] =O(e^{-cN}) \qquad\text{(8)} on a fixed smaller complete disk in uu, with fixed buffered derivatives. The positive exponent is uniform in the row and in parity.

Proof. Choose the disk in (8) with a larger buffer still inside |u|<R|u|<R, and keep τa≤ρ0<ρ1\tau_a\le\rho_0<\rho_1. This is a compact subset of ℜ(−2cos⁡u)<2\Re(-2\cos u)<2. Formula (7), including its polynomial normal loss, bounds the first term after normalization. The ordinary first-color derivative assertion of (5) bounds the canonical GaG_a by the same phase with a polynomial loss, uniformly even as τa→0\tau_a\to0.

The normalized difference is holomorphic and polynomially bounded on this disk. At every physical second color it vanishes exactly. There are c0Nc_0N distinct such angular points in a fixed positive real subinterval of the disk. A disk Blaschke estimate gives exponential smallness on an open interior disk; finite buffered propagation reaches any smaller complete disk. All constants are uniform in aa, since the bounds and zero set are uniform. Cauchy estimates give the derivative statement. The difference and phase are even in uu, so ordinary spectral derivatives through u=0u=0 also follow on a smaller disk by even holomorphic descent. No equality off the physical grid was used. ▫\square

3. Uniform fold-line data on the whole parameter domain

Write ℋN(X,Y)=HN(t(X),t(Y))\mathcal H_N(X,Y)=H_N(t(X),t(Y)) and Ψ(X,Y)=ψ(t(X),t(Y))\Psi(X,Y)=\psi(t(X),t(Y)). Use all centered angular nodes satisfying |t(Xj)|<ρ0.(9) |t(X_j)|<\rho_0 . \qquad\text{(9)} For such a node set a(j)=min⁡(j,N−1−j).(10) a(j)=\min(j,N-1-j). \qquad\text{(10)} Thus its color is the original distinct color ca(j)c_{a(j)}. In particular a reflected index is not inserted as a new high Jacobi degree into Ĝ\widehat G. Its angular derivative has the actual sign C′(1−Xa)=−C′(Xa)C'(1-X_a)=-C'(X_a).

Define the normalized target line data Tj,0(Y)=e−NΨ(Xj,Y)Fa(j)(C(Y)),(11) T_{j,0}(Y)=e^{-N\Psi(X_j,Y)}F_{a(j)}(C(Y)), \qquad\text{(11)} and, at every double row, Tj,1(Y)=e−NΨ(Xj,Y){C′(Xj)Ĝa(j)(C(Y))−NΨX(Xj,Y)Fa(j)(C(Y))}.(12) T_{j,1}(Y)=e^{-N\Psi(X_j,Y)} \{C'(X_j)\widehat G_{a(j)}(C(Y)) -N\Psi_X(X_j,Y)F_{a(j)}(C(Y))\}. \qquad\text{(12)} At the odd center Xj=1/2X_j=1/2 define Tj,1=0T_{j,1}=0. This is a convenient auxiliary angular completion: C′=0C'=0 and ΨX=0\Psi_X=0 there. Only Tj,0T_{j,0} is prescribed by the original simple spectral node. In particular (12) does not define an additional spectral normal there. Define the second line data Sk,0,Sk,1S_{k,0},S_{k,1} by interchanging the endpoints.

Theorem 3. On every fixed smaller connected second-parameter domain, uniformly for all nodes (9), Tj,a(Y)−∂XaℋN(Xj,Y)=O(e−cN),a=0,1.(13) T_{j,a}(Y)-\partial_X^a\mathcal H_N(X_j,Y) =O(e^{-cN}),\qquad a=0,1. \qquad\text{(13)} The symmetric assertion holds for Sk,bS_{k,b}. All fixed parameter derivatives retain this bound after fixed buffers.

Proof. First restrict the other parameter to a small complete disk. Lemma 1 gives the value estimate, because the canonical kernel’s physical row is exactly Fa(j)F_{a(j)}. At a double row it also compares the angular derivative of the baseline with the canonical derivative C′(Xj)Ga(j)C'(X_j)G_{a(j)}. Lemma 2 replaces that canonical normal by C′(Xj)Ĝa(j)C'(X_j)\widehat G_{a(j)}, with an exponential error. Derivatives of the normalizing exponential produce only fixed powers of NN. At the odd center the canonical angular derivative and ΨX\Psi_X are zero, so Lemma 1 gives the auxiliary derivative estimate as well. Reflection changes only the sign of C′C'; the phase and colors are even.

For fixed jj, regard each difference in (13) as a holomorphic function of the other angle. On every buffered parameter compact it is bounded by eo(N)e^{o(N)}, uniformly over all the rows (9). For the target terms this follows from (7), the fixed compact phase derivative, and |C′|≤4π|C'|\le4\pi. For the baseline and its first derivative it follows from (3) and a fixed first-variable Cauchy buffer around the entire small real fold interval. Its normalized derivative is the derivative of HNH_N itself; the NΨXN\Psi_X term has already been included in (12).

Starting with the uniform open-disk estimate just proved, use a finite chain of buffered disks in the connected second-parameter domain. At each step the three-circles estimate combines an exponential bound with a uniform eo(N)e^{o(N)} outer bound, leaving a positive exponential exponent. The same finite chains work for every jj; their constants depend on the fixed domains, not on the moving physical row. This proves (13). Buffered Cauchy estimates give fixed derivatives. No canonical-kernel bound is used outside the small complete bidisk. ▫\square

At a double row, conversion of an angular derivative error to a spectral first-normal error costs only a polynomial. Indeed |C′(Xj)|=4π|sin⁡(2πXj)|≥c/N(14) |C'(X_j)|=4\pi|\sin(2\pi X_j)|\ge c/N \qquad\text{(14)} for the fold-near double nodes. The odd center is excluded from this division. No evenness or spectral descent of the off-grid baseline is inferred from (14).

4. Exact two-line fold repair

Choose centered mirror windows with midpoint boundaries aN=kN/N→12−ρ/(2π),bN=1−aN,0<ρ<ρ0,(15) a_N=k_N/N\longrightarrow\tfrac12-\rho/(2\pi),\qquad b_N=1-a_N,\qquad 0<\rho<\rho_0, \qquad\text{(15)} and similarly in YY. Include every grid node strictly between the boundaries. All these nodes satisfy (9) for large NN. The chosen original real seed windows can be retained inside these windows by making their initial choice smaller if necessary.

At a joint selected node the target functions have the exact compatibility ∂YbTj,a(Yk)=∂XaSk,b(Xj),0≤a,b≤1.(16) \partial_Y^b T_{j,a}(Y_k) =\partial_X^a S_{k,b}(X_j), \qquad 0\le a,b\le1. \qquad\text{(16)} For two double rows this is the original four angular jets of e−NΨKN(C(X),C(Y))e^{-N\Psi}K_N(C(X),C(Y)), including both independent spectral normals and their mixed normal. If one row is the odd center, its angular derivative is zero. The transposed target functions are even at that center because they are functions of its spectral color and the even phase. Thus (16) also holds there. This is only angular compatibility at a simple spectral node.

For clarity use the explicit one-sided cardinal pairs Ljσ(X)=eσ2πiN(X−Xj)sinc⁡2(N(X−Xj)),Vj,0σ=[1−σ2πiN(X−Xj)]Ljσ,Vj,1σ=(X−Xj)Ljσ.(17) L_j^\sigma(X)=e^{\sigma2\pi iN(X-X_j)} \operatorname{sinc}^2(N(X-X_j)), \quad V_{j,0}^\sigma=[1-\sigma2\pi iN(X-X_j)]L_j^\sigma, \quad V_{j,1}^\sigma=(X-X_j)L_j^\sigma. \qquad\text{(17)} They have the required unit value/derivative jets at their own node and double zeros at all other centered nodes. Write Wk,bτW_{k,b}^\tau for the second-coordinate pairs. Set dj,a=Tj,a−∂XaℋN(Xj,Y),ek,b=Sk,b−∂YbℋN(X,Yk), d_{j,a}=T_{j,a}-\partial_X^a\mathcal H_N(X_j,Y),\qquad e_{k,b}=S_{k,b}-\partial_Y^b\mathcal H_N(X,Y_k), cjkab=∂Ybdj,a(Yk)=∂Xaek,b(Xj),(18) c_{jk}^{ab}=\partial_Y^bd_{j,a}(Y_k) =\partial_X^ae_{k,b}(X_j), \qquad\text{(18)} and define 𝒦Nσ,τ=eNΨ[ℋN+∑j,adj,aVj,aσ+∑k,bek,bWk,bτ−∑j,k,a,bcjkabVj,aσWk,bτ].(19) \mathcal K_N^{\sigma,\tau}=e^{N\Psi}\left[ \mathcal H_N+\sum_{j,a}d_{j,a}V_{j,a}^\sigma +\sum_{k,b}e_{k,b}W_{k,b}^\tau -\sum_{j,k,a,b}c_{jk}^{ab}V_{j,a}^\sigma W_{k,b}^\tau \right]. \qquad\text{(19)} All sums are over the selected mirror windows and a,b∈{0,1}a,b\in\{0,1\}. The same subtraction as in the product gluing theorem cancels the second correction against the mixed correction on a first row, and conversely. Consequently (19) has exactly the targets (11)–(12) on both line families.

In particular its unnormalized value is Fa(j)(C(Y))F_{a(j)}(C(Y)); its angular derivative is C′(Xj)Ĝa(j)(C(Y))C'(X_j)\widehat G_{a(j)}(C(Y)) at double rows. At an odd center its value is the actual simple row and its imposed angular derivative is zero. The latter is not used as an independent spectral datum.

The errors in (18) are exponentially small by Theorem 3. The cardinal pairs and fixed derivatives are polynomially bounded on bounded parts of their designated half-planes. There are only O(N2)O(N^2) summands. Hence on the corresponding inner product compacts, e−NΨ𝒦Nσ,τ=ℋN+O(e−c1N),|𝒦Nσ,τ|≤exp⁡{NℜΨ+o(N)},σℑX≥0,τℑY≥0.(20) e^{-N\Psi}\mathcal K_N^{\sigma,\tau} =\mathcal H_N+O(e^{-c_1N}),\qquad |\mathcal K_N^{\sigma,\tau}| \le\exp\{N\Re\Psi+o(N)\}, \quad \sigma\Im X\ge0,\quad\tau\Im Y\ge0. \qquad\text{(20)} Fixed sufficiently thin opposite collars and all fixed buffered angular derivatives are allowed. Their only extra cardinal cost is exp⁡(4πN[(−σℑX)++(−τℑY)+])\exp(4\pi N[(-\sigma\Im X)_++(-\tau\Im Y)_+]); choose the collars after the positive error exponent. These claims do not assert evenness of (19).

5. The closed mirror trace and its odd simple node

Here is the exact multiplicity, separate from the analytic estimates. Let 𝒜N\mathcal A_N consist of the distinct double spectral rows whose two angular lifts lie in (15). For an original spectral spectator P(Z;…)P(Z;\ldots), keep its value and derivative, all crossing carriers and all shared variables. Define ΦN,fold♯(w;…)=η∑a∈𝒜N{(4−ca2)[Ĝa(w)P(ca)+Fa(w)PZ(ca)]−caFa(w)P(ca)}+𝟏{Nodd}FJN(w)P(−2).(21) \begin{split} \Phi^\sharp_{N,\mathrm{fold}}(w;\ldots) ={}&\eta\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_N} \{(4-c_a^2)[\widehat G_a(w)P(c_a) +F_a(w)P_Z(c_a)]-c_aF_a(w)P(c_a)\}\\ &+\mathbf1_{\{N\ {\mathrm{ odd}}\}}F_{J_N}(w)P(-2). \end{split} \qquad\text{(21)} The last term is a single value. There is no normal at the odd simple node. The original fold trace has the same expression with GaG_a instead of Ĝa\widehat G_a.

Put Dh(X)=(πN)2sec⁡2(πNX)D_h(X)=(\pi N)^2\sec^2(\pi NX), fix the second representative sign τ\tau, and let gσ(X)=sin⁡(2πX)𝒦Nσ,τ(X,Y)P(C(X);…).(22) g_\sigma(X)=\sin(2\pi X) \mathcal K_N^{\sigma,\tau}(X,Y)P(C(X);\ldots). \qquad\text{(22)} Take closed lower and upper window halves with the original orientations: E−E_- goes down the left side, across the lower side and up the right; E+E_+ goes up the right side, across the upper side and down the left. Then ΦN,fold♯(w(Y);…)=−η4π2i{∫E−Dhg−dX+∫E+Dhg+dX+∫aNbNDh(g+−g−)dX}.(23) \boxed{\quad \Phi^\sharp_{N,\mathrm{fold}}(w(Y);\ldots) =-\frac{\eta}{4\pi^2i} \left\{\int_{E_-}D_hg_-\,dX+\int_{E_+}D_hg_+\,dX +\int_{a_N}^{b_N}D_h(g_+-g_-)\,dX\right\}. \quad} \qquad\text{(23)} The contours must remain in the domains where the representatives are defined. All vertical half-sides and the real interface are part of this identity.

Proof. Each centered pole of DhD_h has principal part (X−Xj)−2(X-X_j)^{-2}, so its residue against gg is g′(Xj)g'(X_j). At a double spectral row the actual angular jets give g′(Xj)=−π{(4−ca2)[Ĝa(w)P(ca)+Fa(w)PZ(ca)]−caFa(w)P(ca)}.(24) g'(X_j)=-\pi\{(4-c_a^2) [\widehat G_a(w)P(c_a)+F_a(w)P_Z(c_a)] -c_aF_a(w)P(c_a)\}. \qquad\text{(24)} Both reflected lifts give the same expression: the two factors sin⁡(2πXj)\sin(2\pi X_j) and C′(Xj)C'(X_j) change signs together. Multiplication of their residue sum by −η/(2π)-\eta/(2\pi), the closed coefficient in (23), gives exactly the corresponding term of (21).

At the odd center Xj=1/2X_j=1/2, sin⁡(2πX)=−2π(X−1/2)+O((X−1/2)3)\sin(2\pi X)=-2\pi(X-1/2)+O((X-1/2)^3). Its residue is therefore −2πFJN(w)P(−2)-2\pi F_{J_N}(w)P(-2), regardless of the finite angular derivative of the representative. Since η=1\eta=1 at odd level, (23) gives exactly the final term of (21). Equivalently, PN(C(X))=4cos⁡2(πNX)P_N(C(X))=4\cos^2(\pi NX), and CC has degree two at this angular lift of the simple spectral node. The factor one-half in (23) is compulsory there.

For two different half-plane representatives the real-interface term is the usual split of the closed residue contour. Their full angular jets agree at all selected nodes, so its singularities are removable. This proves (23) without a first-angle parity assumption. ▫\square

The difference between (21) and the original free-ww fold trace is PN(w)P_N(w) times a holomorphic slit function, by the exact full-jet property of Ĝa\widehat G_a. Thus equality with the original follows after its required subsequent complete second trace, including arbitrary original spectators and their normal products. At two selected fold windows, the exact mixed jets of (19) give the original double-window trace directly, with the same mirror factor in each variable. A local parameter chart is not itself a contour for every remaining second row: the full trace is taken on the global finite expression (21), or on a covering retaining those rows.

6. Scope and direct sources

This extends the matched data from fixed bulk windows to a fixed neighborhood of the simultaneous complete fold, including every nearby even last normal and the odd simple value. It does not extend to the original positive-color endpoint t=±πt=\pm\pi, identify an arbitrary free polynomial with (19), assert spectral descent of the repaired functions, or replace a whole open lens by an uneven representative. All shared products retain their actual mixed product rules. The common baseline cancels from differences of the designated representatives, but the original trace kernels, spectators, interfaces and exterior sides remain; no parent-action or scalar noncancellation conclusion is inferred.
