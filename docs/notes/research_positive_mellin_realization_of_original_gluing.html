A positive Mellin realization of the original Conway gluing

Private written proof, 23 November 2026.

The original full Frobenius pairing is a Mellin residue of a positive real measure. This identity is valid for arbitrary ordinary polynomial products, so it retains the actual Conway multiplication, turns and normal channels. A normalized positive integral also approximates the squared original closing with an explicit error uniform in the level. Its regularization parameter can be chosen from local source budgets, without evaluating or dividing by the closed invariant.

These are finite-source identities and estimates. No geometric growth estimate for the positive integral, or finite-exception theorem, follows.

1. Original source and the pure positive measure

Fix N≥2N\ge2, and put D=N−1D=N-1, η=(−1)D\eta=(-1)^D. Use the actual pair algebra, canonical representative and original cylindrical row: PN(z)=2TN(z/2)+2,HN=ℂ[z]/(PN),ϕN(h)=ηN2[zD]rem⁡PNh.(1) P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\qquad H_N=\mathbb C[z]/(P_N),\qquad \phi_N(h)=\eta N^2[z^D]\operatorname{rem}_{P_N}h . \qquad\text{(1)} Here TNT_N is the ordinary Chebyshev polynomial. The monic PNP_N has roots cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N)c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N), counted with multiplicity. The original double roots and odd-level simple root are retained. On x>2x>2, write w=w(x)=x+x2−42>1,x=w+w−1. w=w(x)=\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2-4}}2>1,\qquad x=w+w^{-1}. Then PN(x)=wN+2+w−N>0P_N(x)=w^N+2+w^{-N}>0. Define the pure positive measure dνN(x)=dxPN(x),x>2.(2) d\nu_N(x)=\frac{dx}{P_N(x)},\qquad x>2. \qquad\text{(2)} This is not the Ward current: its density has no hyperbolic-tangent factor. Its source identification will instead be the exact residue in the next section.

Every actual finite Conway packet has a canonical polynomial of degree at most DD. All prescribed external scalar factors may be included in that packet. Thus write its original scalar closing as KN=ϕN(fN)K_N=\phi_N(f_N), without changing its normalization.

For the other original Conway row λ0(f)=f(c0)\lambda_0(f)=f(c_0), use the actual turn Tei=eD−iT e_i=e_{D-i}. The exact identity ϕN(Tf)=λ0(f)\phi_N(Tf)=\lambda_0(f) puts it in the same form. This retains the full original packet and its prescribed closing.

2. Exact regularized gluing at every polynomial degree

For any ordinary polynomial hh, initially define IN(s;h)=∫2∞x−sh(x)PN(x)dx,ℜs>deg⁡h−N+1.(3) I_N(s;h)=\int_2^\infty x^{-s}\frac{h(x)}{P_N(x)}\,dx, \qquad \Re s>\deg h-N+1. \qquad\text{(3)} The zero polynomial causes no exception. The logarithm of the positive real xx fixes x−sx^{-s}.

Theorem 1. This integral continues meromorphically to ℜs>−1\Re s>-1, and ϕN(h)=ηN2Ress=0IN(s;h).(4) \boxed{\qquad \phi_N(h)=\eta N^2\operatorname*{Res}_{s=0}I_N(s;h). \qquad} \qquad\text{(4)} The residue in (4) depends only on the class of hh in HNH_N, with no bound on its ordinary degree. In particular ϕN(fg)\phi_N(fg) is the original full bilinear gluing.

Proof. Divide h=PNq+rh=P_Nq+r, where deg⁡r<N\deg r<N, and let a=[xD]ra=[x^D]r. Write q=∑kqkxkq=\sum_k q_kx^k. Direct integration gives the continuation IN(s;h)=∑kqk2k+1−ss−k−1+a2−ss+∫2∞x−s{r(x)PN(x)−ax}dx.(5) I_N(s;h)= \sum_k q_k\frac{2^{k+1-s}}{s-k-1} +a\frac{2^{-s}}s +\int_2^\infty x^{-s} \left\{\frac{r(x)}{P_N(x)}-\frac a x\right\}dx. \qquad\text{(5)} The last integrand is ON,h(x−2−ℜs)O_{N,h}(x^{-2-\Re s}) at infinity and has no singularity at 22, since PN(2)=4P_N(2)=4. It is holomorphic for ℜs>−1\Re s>-1, locally uniformly with the needed logarithmic derivatives in ss. None of the polynomial terms has a pole at zero. The residue is therefore aa, proving (4). In particular every ordinary multiple of PNP_N has zero residue. ▫\square

Equation (4) retains the full first normals, rather than only the distinct-root values. For example, its original physical form is ϕN(h)=η∑cdouble{(4−c2)h′(c)−ch(c)}+𝟏Noddh(−2).(6) \phi_N(h)=\eta\sum_{c\ {\mathrm{ double}}} \{(4-c^2)h'(c)-ch(c)\} +\mathbf1_{N\ {\mathrm{ odd}}}h(-2). \qquad\text{(6)} This is the same coefficient functional (1), as established by the actual source calculus. There is no new normal at the odd simple node.

Consequently every finite original tensor contraction may replace each Frobenius contraction by (4), leaving its polynomial kernels and crossing coupons unchanged. With one variable per contracted algebra copy, the resulting iterated Mellin residues reproduce the original contraction. To justify the statement directly, expand its finite multivariate polynomial numerator into monomials. In a sufficiently far right half-plane in every ss-variable all integrals converge absolutely. Formula (5), applied coefficientwise, continues them; their iterated residues commute and extract precisely the original quotient coefficients. Arbitrary free spectators remain coefficients. Thus unreduced trivalent products are allowed in this exact identity.

This is a bilinear regularized gluing, not a positive inner product at the residue. Positivity of (2) does not make (6) positive.

3. A positive approximation to the squared original closing

The estimate (8), rather than just this coefficient identity, controls the positive integral at a specified finite regularization parameter.

4. Source-only budgets and the unchanged volume target

Put MN=max⁡[−2,2]|fN|M_N=\max_{[-2,2]}|f_N|. Any supplied BN≥max⁡(1,MN)B_N\ge\max(1,M_N) gives the explicit choice εN=1N6BN2CN,|𝒜N,εN(fN)−|KN|2|≤N−2.(15) \varepsilon_N=\frac1{N^6B_N^2C_N},\qquad \left|\mathcal A_{N,\varepsilon_N}(f_N)-|K_N|^2\right| \le N^{-2}. \qquad\text{(15)} Such a budget need not use the closed coefficient. In the original Newton basis ei(z)=Si−2∏j<i(cj−z),Si=∏r=1i2sin⁡(πr/N),S0=1, e_i(z)=S_i^{-2}\prod_{j<i}(c_j-z),\qquad S_i=\prod_{r=1}^i2\sin(\pi r/N),\quad S_0=1, we have |ei(x)|≤4i/Si2|e_i(x)|\le4^i/S_i^2 on [−2,2][-2,2]. If a known local tensor budget gives LN≥∥fN∥NewtonL_N\ge\|f_N\|_{\mathrm{ Newton}}, take BN=max⁡(1,LN∑i=0D4iSi2).(16) B_N=\max\left(1,L_N\sum_{i=0}^{D}\frac{4^i}{S_i^2}\right). \qquad\text{(16)} For completeness, products of recorded leaf norms and gate Hilbert–Schmidt norm bounds supply LNL_N by induction: the norm of each contracted output is at most the gate norm times the product of its input norms. The actual turn is unitary in the Newton metric. Known external scalar factors are included in this budget. No closed invariant is required as an input, and no efficient algorithm is claimed.

For every fixed positive number VV, (15) implies the equivalence limN→∞2πNlog⁡|KN|=V⇔limN→∞πNlog⁡𝒜N,εN(fN)=V.(17) \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N|=V \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac\pi N \log\mathcal A_{N,\varepsilon_N}(f_N)=V. \qquad\text{(17)} Use log⁡0=−∞\log0=-\infty. If either limit is V>0V>0, its positive quantity grows exponentially and dominates the absolute error N−2N^{-2}. The ratio of the two quantities in (15) then tends to one, proving the other limit. Positivity of VV is essential here.

The finite-ε\varepsilon expression is a controlled approximation to the original squared source, not a substituted knot invariant. The canonical requirement in Theorem 2 is essential; Theorem 1 has no such degree restriction. Full original channels and arbitrary fixed short components remain allowed. A geometric estimate of the positive integral in (17), with its actual packet and chosen parameter, is still unproved. No Casson–Rivin growth or finite-exception case is asserted.
