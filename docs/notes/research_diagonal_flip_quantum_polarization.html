Exact quantum polarization between diagonalizations of a MAIN quadrangulation

31 October 2026. research theorem; not published. The full spectral source polynomials of all diagonal choices form an exact Newton-reversal orbit. Sections 9–11 prove this identity and the corresponding change of the original filled functional. Sections 1–6 distinguish it from a transport of integer twist powers, and sections 7–8 give its full-Hermite kernel and the remaining cancellation problem. Original terminal labels, Cartan factors, and primitive disk framings are retained.

1. What the local change actually does

Keep one quadrilateral of the original MAIN quadrangulation fixed, with cyclic white vertices (x,b,y,c)(x,b,y,c) and diagonal xyxy. In the actual inverse cut, its four cyclic terminals 0,1,2,30,1,2,3 have shaded groups (1,2),(3,0)(1,2),(3,0) and physical untwisted arcs (0,1),(2,3)(0,1),(2,3). This is the dictionary in general Morse source, sections 1–2, and original crossing-disk cut, sections 1–4.

Choosing the other diagonal bcbc rotates the local roles by one cyclic step. With the SAME four exterior terminals, its physical untwisted arcs are (1,2),(3,0)(1,2),(3,0). Thus the zero-twist tangles already have different boundary pairings. Their geometric quarter-turn is a permutation of the boundary data, not an isotopy relative that boundary.

Write T(n)T(n) for the original two-strand box with full signed integer power RnR^n, including its recorded ribbon framing. Let rr be the genuine orientation-preserving quarter-turn, using the original turns. In the unchanged exterior frame the two filled choices are T(n)T(n) and rT(m)r T(m). Reversing all convention signs together does not change the obstruction below. Our sign is calibrated by the exact original identity rT(−1)=T(1)r T(-1)=T(1).

2. Exact fixed-boundary tangle obstruction

The double cover of a ball branched over a trivial two-string tangle is a solid torus. Its boundary is the double cover of the sphere with four marked branch points. Choose primitive boundary classes α,β\alpha,\beta, with intersection one, so the meridian of T(0)T(0) is α\alpha and the lift of one positive half twist acts by α↦α+β\alpha\mapsto \alpha+\beta. The latter is the elementary annular picture: the lift of an arc joining the two exchanged branch points is a simple closed core, and the lifted half twist cuts and reglues its annulus by one full turn. Induction, including inverse twists, gives

μn=α+nβ. \mu_n=\alpha+n\beta. \tag{1}

Represent the four-point sphere as the pillowcase quotient of a square torus. A quarter-turn lifts, up to the deck involution, to α↦β\alpha\mapsto \beta, β↦−α\beta\mapsto -\alpha. Thus the turned tangle has

μm′=β−mα,det⁡(μn,μm′)=1+nm. \mu'_m=\beta-m\alpha, \qquad \det(\mu_n,\mu'_m)=1+nm. \tag{2}

A homeomorphism of tangles equal to the identity on the original boundary lifts to a homeomorphism of their branched solid tori equal to the identity or deck involution on the boundary. It must preserve the rational line of the meridian. Equations (1)–(2) therefore give

T(n)≃∂rT(m)⇒nm=−1. T(n)\simeq_{\partial}rT(m)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad nm=-1. \tag{3}

For integer n,mn,m, the only possibilities are (1,−1)(1,-1) and (−1,1)(-1,1); they are indeed the two original single-crossing turn identities. No classification theorem is needed for the obstruction: the kernel of boundary homology into the solid torus is enough. In the usual rational-tangle fraction convention this is the negative-reciprocal rule, also proved in Kauffman–Lambropoulou, On the classification of rational tangles, sections 2 and 6.

Consequently there is no universal portwise substitution of long integer crossing powers that reverses a diagonal flip while leaving the exterior tangle fixed. This is a general local theorem, not a test of a particular closed knot. It does not say that two specially chosen exteriors can never have isotopic closures.

3. The two different peripheral tori must not be confused

The torus in (1)–(2) is the branched cover of the four-point boundary sphere. It is NOT the crossing-circle cusp torus. The genuine spatial quarter-turn moves the auxiliary circle and its twice-punctured disk together. It preserves that disk’s preferred zero-self-linking longitude and carries its meridian to the corresponding meridian. This is the actual turn statement of original preferred disk longitude, section 5. One must not install the matrix of (2) as an unproved cusp basis change.

For the original integer surgery coordinate, the primitive killed curve is μ+pλ\mu+p \lambda, and the inserted exponent is n=ϵ−2pn=\epsilon-2p. In the flipped standard box, obtaining the old tangle would instead require the rational exponent m=−1/nm=-1/n. Formally this would ask for

p′=ϵ′+1/n2. p'=\frac{\epsilon'+1/n}{2}. \tag{4}

For |n|>1|n|>1 and ϵ′\epsilon' in {−1,0,1}\{-1,0,1\}, (4) is not an integer. This formal expression is not a valid replacement by the already proved integer Gaussian filling functional.

Even allowing surgery on that same unknot with p′=a/bp'=a/b in lowest terms does not give the original local ball move. Its primitive filling curve is bμ+aλb \mu+a \lambda. If the other components are ignored, the filled ambient manifold has first homology ℤ/|b|ℤ\mathbb Z/|b|\mathbb Z, whereas the original integer full-twist fillings have |b|=1|b|=1 and ambient S3S^3. The crossing circles in this construction bound mutually disjoint disks when the MAIN strands are ignored. For several such rational fillings the same homology calculation is the diagonal presentation with summands ℤ/|be|ℤ\mathbb Z/|b_e|\mathbb Z. It supplies no cancellation of these ambient obstructions. A rational-tangle replacement in a ball is a different operation and would require additional tangle data, not the single original crossing-circle filling at that port.

More generally a fixed integral peripheral matrix sends a slope (1,p)(1,p) to (a+bp,c+dp)(a+bp,c+dp), giving p′=(c+dp)/(a+bp)p'=(c+dp)/(a+bp) when the result can be normalized. If bb is nonzero, p′p' stays bounded as |p||p| tends to infinity, and it cannot be integral for infinitely many such pp: a bounded integral subsequence would be constant, contradicting the nonzero determinant. Thus a fixed cusp map sending a cofinal integer long-filling family to another such family must preserve the longitude line and have affine slope action. This is only a necessary condition; (2) does not itself assert a map of the augmented exteriors.

4. A source-preserving finite cover does not erase this local kernel

Suppose one proposes a common finite cover of the branched boundary torus, with the SAME fixed boundary identification used for the two solid tori. Let its integral inclusion be A:L′→H1(T2;ℤ)A:L'\longrightarrow H_1(T^2;\mathbb Z). Over the rationals, AA is an isomorphism. The lifted meridian lines are A−1(ℚμn)A^{-1}(\mathbb Q\mu_n) and A−1(ℚμm′)A^{-1}(\mathbb Q\mu'_m). They coincide only when the original lines coincide, hence only when nm=−1nm=-1.

This excludes a repair by such a source-preserving pullback cover. It does not exclude unrelated covers with different boundary maps, branched covers of the full link, or changes of chamber/wiring. Those would need new peripheral and original quantum source adapters. There is no general Kashaev descent identity supplied by a cover.

5. An exact original nilpotent quantum obstruction

This is a separate finite check with a proof for all integer exponents, not a numerical sample. At N=2N=2, put d=1d=1, q=iq=i, and λ=exp⁡(πi/4)\lambda=\exp(\pi i/4). The actual full pair algebra is ℂ[Ω]/(Ω2)\mathbb C[\Omega]/(\Omega^2). In the Newton basis put

B=B̂1=−Ω/4,B2=0. B=\widehat B_1=-\Omega/4,\qquad B^2=0.

The original crossing Hermite data give r2(0)=1r_2(0)=1, r2′(0)=i/4r'_2(0)=i/4, and retain the Cartan scalar λ=qd2/2\lambda=q^{d^2/2}. Consequently

R=λ(1−iB),Rn=λn(1−inB). R=\lambda(1-iB),\qquad R^n=\lambda^n(1-inB). \tag{5}

The genuine unscaled original turn satisfies ℛ(1)=B\mathscr R(1)=B, ℛ(B)=1\mathscr R(B)=1; these are the N=2N=2 cases of the full physical Newton turn theorem, not an eigenvalue-only transform. Thus

ℛ(Rm)=λm(B−im). \mathscr R(R^m)=\lambda^m(B-im). \tag{6}

For any nonzero scalar aa, equality ℛ(Rm)=aRn\mathscr R(R^m)=a R^n forces mm nonzero and, by comparing the two coefficients, nm=−1nm=-1. In those two integer cases (5)–(6) give the original exact equalities with a=1a=1. In particular allowing an unknown normalization scalar cannot remove the local obstruction. Discarding the nilpotent coefficient would lose exactly this test.

Equations (5)–(6) rule out a coupon-uniform all-level original tensor identity of the proposed power-substitution kind. The topological argument of section 2 is independent of this small-level witness and continues to rule out the relative-boundary move without any level restriction. We do not infer distinguishability of all closed knots from their level-two invariant.

6. A global long-filling obstruction when capacities differ

Let T,T′T,T' be two simple choices on the same MAIN quadrangulation, with their actual complete hyperbolic augmented exteriors and knot parity patterns. Suppose Φ(T)<Φ(T′)\Phi(T)<\Phi(T'). Their complete volumes are 2πΦ(T)2\pi \Phi(T) and 2πΦ(T′)2\pi \Phi(T'). The proved independent long-filling geometry makes the filled volumes converge to these respective values as the minimum absolute integer filling coordinate tends to infinity.

Choose each geometric filling threshold so that the volume error is less than one-third of the positive difference between the two complete volumes. The two resulting volume intervals are disjoint. No pair of knots in these two cofinal all-long families can have homeomorphic complements, and in particular they cannot be the same knot. This obstructs ANY proposed global transport between these families that keeps all new coordinates independently long. It does not assume a local tangle substitution. It applies in particular to a strictly lower-capacity simple chart and a maximal chart.

Equal-capacity ties, transports involving short slopes, and changed rational tangles are not settled by this argument. It is not an obstruction to representing the same knot by arbitrary turned coupons, as in the next section.

7. What does transport exactly: full turned coupons

There is a useful positive finite statement. For any set of changed diagonals, put the genuine turned OLD coupon in every changed disk of the new chart, using the inverse of its local frame rotation. On the original Hopf polynomial algebra the two quarter-turn directions agree, since the genuine turn squares to the identity. Each physical local tangle is then exactly the old tangle in the original exterior frame. Outside arcs and the actual reduced marked closure stay fixed. Morse bends use the original snakes; no ribbon twist or Cartan scalar is discarded. Thus the whole contraction is the SAME original filled knot source, but the new local coupons at flipped ports are ℛ(Rn)\mathscr R(R^n), not new integer powers RmR^m.

By the full pair-commutant theorem these turned coupons are polynomials in the new ordered pair Casimir, with all root Hermite data. Therefore the new general Morse coefficient compiler applies to them. This is not sector transport, multiplicativity of the turn, or transport of a Gaussian filling theorem. The original final knot-framing character must be retained; it is not reset using a fictitious new braid exponent.

Here is the exact spectral kernel of that change of polarization. For all N≥2N\geq 2, let

cj=2cos⁡((2j+1)π/N),χN(z)=∏j=0d(z−cj)=2TN(z/2)+2,Sr=∏j=1r2sin⁡(πj/N),Br(z)=∏j=0r−1(cj−z),B̂r=Br/Sr2. \begin{gathered} c_j=2\cos((2j+1)\pi/N),\quad \chi_N(z)=\prod_{j=0}^{d}(z-c_j)=2T_N(z/2)+2,\\ S_r=\prod_{j=1}^r2\sin(\pi j/N),\qquad B_r(z)=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(c_j-z),\qquad \widehat B_r=B_r/S_r^2. \end{gathered}

The original exact turn is ℛB̂r=B̂d−r\mathscr R\widehat B_r=\widehat B_{d-r}. Newton division of the resolvent numerator, keeping repeated nodes, gives

VN(σ;z)=∑r=0d(−1)rSr2∏j=r+1d(σ−cj)B̂r(z). V_N(\sigma;z)= \sum_{r=0}^{d}(-1)^rS_r^2 \prod_{j=r+1}^{d}(\sigma-c_j)\widehat B_r(z). \tag{7}

This is a polynomial identity representing χN(σ)/(σ−Ω)\chi_N(\sigma)/(\sigma-\Omega). Since cd−j=cjc_{d-j}=c_j and SrSd−r=NS_r S_{d-r}=N, applying the turn and changing k=d−rk=d-r yields

𝒦N(σ,ζ):=(ℛVN(σ))(ζ)=(−1)dN2∑k=0dB̂k(σ)B̂k(ζ). \boxed{\quad \mathcal K_N(\sigma,\zeta) :=(\mathscr R V_N(\sigma))(\zeta) =(-1)^dN^2\sum_{k=0}^{d} \widehat B_k(\sigma)\widehat B_k(\zeta). \quad} \tag{8}

No simple-node interpolation or root-normal deletion was used. This is a polynomial of degree at most dd in each variable, symmetric in them, and it is the exact Newton reproducing kernel times the displayed original factor.

Let fn(σ)=exp⁡[nNarccosh⁡(−σ/2)2/(4πi)]f_n(\sigma)=\exp[nN\operatorname{arccosh}(-\sigma/2)^2/(4\pi i)] in the original slit branch. Choose a contour enclosing every actual spectral node, within its analytic domain and avoiding the cut starting at +2+2. The original full power is qnd2/4fn(Ω)q^{n d^2/4} f_n(\Omega). Thus the turned power is exactly qnd2/4gn(Ω)q^{n d^2/4} g_n(\Omega), where

gn(ζ)=12πi∮fn(σ)χN(σ)𝒦N(σ,ζ)dσ. g_n(\zeta)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint \frac{f_n(\sigma)}{\chi_N(\sigma)} \mathcal K_N(\sigma,\zeta)\,d\sigma. \tag{9}

This is equivalent to the full confluent Newton formula

gn(ζ)=∑r=0d(−1)rSr2fn[c0,…,cr]B̂d−r(ζ). g_n(\zeta)=\sum_{r=0}^{d}(-1)^r S_r^2 f_n[c_0,\ldots,c_r]\widehat B_{d-r}(\zeta). \tag{10}

Finally any polynomial gng_n can itself be inserted against the SAME new-chart numerator by its Cauchy/Hermite functional

gn(Ω)=12πi∮gn(ζ)χN(ζ)VN(ζ)dζ. g_n(\Omega)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint \frac{g_n(\zeta)}{\chi_N(\zeta)} V_N(\zeta)\,d\zeta. \tag{11}

Multilinearity makes (9)–(11) exact for the whole marked source and all its original face targets. Unchanged ports retain their ordinary Gaussian functional. These equations supply the promised actual transformed functional at changed ports; they do not make it Gaussian.

8. The known maximal-chart gap does not alone control this kernel

Even its pointwise absolute size is not subexponential. At the real corner σ=ζ=−2\sigma=\zeta=-2, every square in (8) is nonnegative. Take kN=⌊N/4⌋k_N=\lfloor N/4\rfloor. For both parities this index precedes any vanishing factor. The original sine products give

B̂kN(−2)=∏j=0kN−1cos⁡2((j+1/2)π/N)sin⁡2((j+1)π/N), \widehat B_{k_N}(-2)= \prod_{j=0}^{k_N-1} \frac{\cos^2((j+1/2)\pi/N)}{\sin^2((j+1)\pi/N)}, limN→∞1Nlog⁡B̂kN(−2)=2∫01/4log⁡cot⁡(πt)dt=2Gπ>0, \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N \log\widehat B_{k_N}(-2) =2\int_0^{1/4}\log\cot(\pi t)\,dt =\frac{2G}{\pi}>0, \tag{12}

where G=∫0π/4log⁡cot⁡tdtG=\int_0^{\pi/4}\log\cot t\,dt is Catalan’s constant. The logarithmic endpoint singularity is integrable; splitting off the first fixed small interval and comparing monotone log-sine sums to their integrals proves the displayed Riemann-sum limit. Consequently

liminfN→∞1Nlog⁡|𝒦N(−2,−2)|≥4Gπ. \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1N \log|\mathcal K_N(-2,-2)|\geq\frac{4G}{\pi}. \tag{13}

The maximal-chart non-CROSS estimate supplies a strict source gap, not a bound that automatically pays the exponential size in (13). Thus no inference of its preservation follows by taking absolute values of the transformed kernel. The large kernel is itself compatible with cancellations: at n=1n=1, (9) produces the exact opposite single crossing. We have not claimed the transformed period actually has excessive growth, or that a cancellation-aware estimate cannot use the maximal chart.

The precise remaining problem is a uniform cancellation-aware estimate for (9)–(11) applied to the complete maximal-chart source, together with identification of its actual filled action. The former Gaussian H1/H2 period argument does not already supply that estimate. The exact topology says which old knot such a successful new argument would concern; no rational-power or sector equality is needed.

9. Exact local homogeneous covariance, with canonical physical lifts

This strengthens section 7 to a polynomial identity before any shared coefficient is extracted. Denote the original complete root turn scalar by x0=qdx_0=q^d to distinguish it from a face variable. For an arbitrary full Casimir polynomial ff, the actual unscaled turn has entries

(ℛf)r,s;u,v=qd(r−v)fd−r,u;s,d−v. (\mathscr R f)_{r,s;u,v} =q^{d(r-v)} f_{d-r,u;s,d-v}. \tag{14}

At the new box let the homogeneous face tuple be (X,B,C,Y)(X,B,C,Y). On canonical physical support the new input/output clocks are

Qu=−q−1X/B,Qv=−q−1B/Y,Qr=−q−1X/C,Qs=−q−1C/Y. Q^u=-q^{-1}X/B,\quad Q^v=-q^{-1}B/Y,\quad Q^r=-q^{-1}X/C,\quad Q^s=-q^{-1}C/Y.

The old matrix entry on the right of (14) has the literal face tuple (C,Y,X,B)(C,Y,X,B): its inputs are s,d−vs,d-v and outputs d−r,ud-r,u. Use centered physical face-log differences Log⁡(Lleft/Lright)=2πi(i−d/2)/N\operatorname{Log}(L_{\mathrm{left}}/L_{\mathrm{right}})=2\pi i(i-d/2)/N. They are the original clocks because −q=exp⁡(−πid/N)-q=\exp(-\pi i d/N). Complementing a head is then literal log negation. In particular

Log⁡(XY/(BC))=2πi(r−v)/N. \operatorname{Log}(XY/(BC))=2\pi i(r-v)/N.

The actual old adapter has denominator (XY)d/2(XY)^{d/2}, while the new one has denominator (BC)d/2(BC)^{d/2}. Equation (14) cancels their ratio exactly. There is no extra scalar or parity sign:

Hℛf(X,B,C,Y)=Hf(C,Y,X,B). \boxed{\quad H_{\mathscr R f}(X,B,C,Y)=H_f(C,Y,X,B). \quad} \tag{15}

Here canonical physical support is imposed FIRST. On the remaining modular grids, a wrong unwrapped total makes both matrix entries zero; no individual head is reset by NN. For supported states the centered lift calculation just made applies. After fixing a common face scale, the original local Fourier adapter samples all three independent root grids (with the actual even-level cosets). Both homogeneous polynomials have degree d<Nd<N, so the difference has degree below NN in each normalized variable. Product-grid uniqueness proves the formal identity (15), not merely an equality on supported individual heads.

For clarity the two reflection symmetries used in comparing cyclic frames are also exact. Outer symmetry follows from the source operator depending only on X+Y,XYX+Y,XY. For middle symmetry, its diagonal is cjc_j and its downward coefficients are −sj2(X+Y)-s_j^2(X+Y) and −sjsj−1XY-s_j s_{j-1}XY, where here sj=2sin⁡(πj/N)s_j=2\sin(\pi j/N). With the bilinear pairing [zd]uv[z^d]uv, reflection gives cj=cd−jc_j=c_{d-j}, sj2=sN−j2s_j^2=s_{N-j}^2, and sjsj−1=sN+1−jsN−js_j s_{j-1}=s_{N+1-j}s_{N-j}. Thus the source Casimir, and every polynomial in it, is selfadjoint for that pairing. Equivalently its entries obey Fi,j=Fd−j,d−iF_{i,j}=F_{d-j,d-i}, proving Hf(X,B,C,Y)=Hf(X,C,B,Y)H_f(X,B,C,Y)=H_f(X,C,B,Y).

In particular the flipped frame (b,y,x,c)(b,y,x,c) satisfies

Hf(b,y,x,c)=Hℛf(x,b,c,y),Hℛf(b,y,x,c)=Hf(x,b,c,y). H_f(b,y,x,c)=H_{\mathscr R f}(x,b,c,y),\qquad H_{\mathscr R f}(b,y,x,c)=H_f(x,b,c,y). \tag{16}

All four variables in (16) are the same original labeled white faces. It is the coupon that changes. There is no assertion that HfH_f alone is invariant under a cyclic permutation.

10. The exact spectral action of all independent diagonal flips

Fix the MAIN quadrangulation, one MAIN mark, and a reference choice DD of all diagonals. For any choice let SD(σ1,…,σR)S_D(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_R) be its original full marked spectral polynomial, with VN(σe)V_N(\sigma_e) at every port and all ordinary face targets retained. Each spectral degree is at most dd. There is no geometric or simplicity hypothesis in the following finite statement; the general Morse realization retains any parallel diagonal edges as incidences.

Let ℛe\mathscr R_e denote the full Newton reversal on the degree-at-most-dd polynomial in its spectral variable σe\sigma_e, fixing all other variables. The numerator itself has the useful symmetric form

VN(σ;z)=(−1)dN2∑r=0dB̂d−r(σ)B̂r(z). V_N(\sigma;z) =(-1)^dN^2\sum_{r=0}^{d} \widehat B_{d-r}(\sigma)\widehat B_r(z). \tag{17}

It follows directly from (7) and the same reflected products as (8). Hence ℛzVN=ℛσVN=𝒦N\mathscr R_zV_N=\mathscr R_\sigma V_N=\mathcal K_N. Applying (16) at one disk and then taking the unchanged full marked coefficient proves

SDe=ℛeSD,SDA=(∏e∈Aℛe)SD(A⊆{1,…,R}). \boxed{\qquad S_{D^e}=\mathscr R_e S_D, \qquad S_{D^A}=\left(\prod_{e\in A}\mathscr R_e\right)S_D \quad(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,R\}). \qquad} \tag{18}

Indeed (16) holds already for the product of local face polynomials, and the spectral reversal commutes with the original shared coefficient extraction. Independent reversals commute and each squares to the identity. A second flip returns a local frame rotated by a half turn; the exact outer and middle reflection symmetries above return the same polynomial, with no newly chosen gauge.

The diagonal choices form the ordinary binary cube with its free (ℤ/2ℤ)R(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^R action. Equation (18) is an equivariant assignment into the tensor product of the FULL Hermite algebras. Its image need not be a free orbit: some polynomials may have stabilizers. Thus no stronger torsor or freeness assertion about quantum states is being made.

If

SD=∑𝒓τD(𝒓)∏eB̂re(σe), S_D=\sum_{\mathbf r}\tau_D(\mathbf r) \prod_e\widehat B_{r_e}(\sigma_e),

then flipping ee replaces rer_e by d−red-r_e in this same coefficient tensor. In particular the finite positive norm

∑𝒓|τD(𝒓)|2 \sum_{\mathbf r}|\tau_D(\mathbf r)|^2 \tag{19}

is invariant under all choices. This is the already specified Newton tensor norm. It is not a bound of the complete geometric rate, and does not transport CROSS/LEFT/RIGHT restrictions or their positive envelopes. Such restrictions are not invariant under (15).

11. Filled functionals transform contragrediently

Define the exact original single-port functional on spectral polynomials by

𝔉n,N(p)=qnd2/42πi∮fn(σ)χN(σ)p(σ)dσ. \mathfrak F_{n,N}(p) =\frac{q^{nd^2/4}}{2\pi i}\oint \frac{f_n(\sigma)}{\chi_N(\sigma)}p(\sigma)\,d\sigma. \tag{20}

It is the original Gaussian/Hermite functional before its equivalent whole-interval Fourier deformation. Thus its usual sine, integer charges, and endpoint terms have not been altered or dropped. The original raw filled contraction is ID(𝒏)=(⨂e𝔉ne,N)(SD)I_D(\mathbf n)=(\bigotimes_e\mathfrak F_{n_e,N})(S_D).

For AA the set of flipped disks, (18) gives the exact equality

ID(𝒏)=(⨂e[𝔉ne,N∘ℛe𝟏e∈A])SDA. I_D(\mathbf n) =\left(\bigotimes_e [\mathfrak F_{n_e,N}\circ\mathscr R_e^{\mathbf1_{e\in A}}] \right)S_{D^A}. \tag{21}

The changed local functional is exactly (9)–(11), with the turned polynomial gng_n and the same external Cartan scalar. To see its adjoint convention directly, the coefficient Cauchy pairing is [zd](uvmod⁡χN)[z^d](uv\bmod\chi_N). On the normalized Newton basis it equals (−1)dN−2δi+j,d(-1)^dN^{-2}\delta_{i+j,d}. Newton reversal is selfadjoint for this pairing. Therefore 𝔉n,N(ℛp)\mathfrak F_{n,N}(\mathscr R p) is the Cauchy functional with ℛfn\mathscr R f_n in its first slot, precisely gng_n, rather than a guessed reciprocal Gaussian.

Equations (18)–(21) resolve the exact source relation between charts. They do not contradict the topology of sections 1–6: keeping the old knot on the new chart changes its filling functional. Applying the ordinary Gaussian functional on both sides would instead give the two different integer-twist diagrams. The complete and filled geometric actions of those two procedures must not be conflated.

The preserved quantity (19) does not bypass the exponential kernel in (13). A proof of the general volume conjecture from a maximal chart still needs a cancellation-aware analysis of (21), with the actual old peripheral action and its full source normalization.
