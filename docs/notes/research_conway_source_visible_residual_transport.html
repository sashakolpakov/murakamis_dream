Source-visible residuals and exact correction on a Conway tree

21 November 2026. Research note; previous finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

A trial boundary packet can have a large error which is nearly invisible to the prescribed closing functional. Bounding the norm of that error before applying the closing row loses this information. Here we transport the actual closing row backwards through the linearization of the complete Conway compiler. The resulting correction is a coherent complex scalar. Its remaining error has an explicit quadratic bound.

This is a finite-level error estimate for the original source. It does not establish that a geometric trial field has a nonzero corrected closing or the required volume rate. Its advantage over a full-norm criterion is precise: the first-order error is retained as a source-visible scalar, and only the nonlinear remainder is bounded by a norm.

1. The normalized physical tree

5. A phase-sensitive lower bound at one trial field

The error disk immediately gives Btotmax⁡(0,|k̂(x)|−ℛ(x))≤|KN|≤Btot(|k̂(x)|+ℛ(x)).(22) \boxed{\quad B_{\mathrm{ tot}}\max(0,|\widehat k(x)|-\mathcal R(x)) \le |K_N| \le B_{\mathrm{ tot}}(|\widehat k(x)|+\mathcal R(x)). \quad} \qquad\text{(22)} This does not require minimizing a new function. The uniform radius (15) can reduce the required exponential accuracy of a residual estimate. If the corrected normalized closing has size exp⁡{−αN+o(N)}\exp\{-\alpha N+o(N)\}, it suffices for its quadratic remainder to have a strictly smaller rate; for example ∥rN∥≤exp⁡{−(α/2+ε)N+o(N)}\|r_N\|\le\exp\{-(\alpha/2+\varepsilon)N+o(N)\} with ε>0\varepsilon>0. A direct linear residual disk instead requires an error on the scale of the closing itself. The correction drNdr_N, its complex phase, and the nonzero leading behavior in (24) must still be established. A formal small residual alone does not prove a volume lower bound.

6. Fixed short components and exact linear corridors

Any fixed proper short subtree may first be contracted to its complete actual open packet and inserted as one prescribed leaf. Recompute the known budgets in (1). This preserves the original knot source and all Hermite directions, including a packet which vanishes on the distinct Casimir roots but has a nonzero normal part.

Put the true normalized value at such a leaf. Its residual and error are exactly zero. At a product with one exact child, the corresponding term Qv(e)=Φv(eL,eR)Q_v(e)=\Phi_v(e_L,e_R) in (11) is zero. Attachments through a sequence of exact short sibling components therefore transport the remaining error linearly, with their actual complex matrices. The backward rows in (6) retain those matrices and their phases exactly. Nonlinear remainder terms occur only where two uncertain branches meet.

This is a structural simplification for arbitrary fixed short components; it is not a positivity or nonvanishing theorem for their closing coefficients. The estimates are for the specified original exterior. They do not declare a direction invisible to every other possible exterior.

The correction in (12) has a direct transport interpretation. There is also an exact termination statement in exact arithmetic. Give a leaf rank one and a turn the rank of its child. At a product set νv=max⁡{νL,νR,min⁡(νL,νR)+1}.(32) \nu_v=\max\{\nu_L,\nu_R,\min(\nu_L,\nu_R)+1\}. \qquad\text{(32)} This is the usual Strahler rank, with leaves numbered one. Starting from any feasible trial field and applying (26) repeatedly, xv(j)=av(j≥νv).(33) x_v^{(j)}=a_v\qquad(j\ge\nu_v). \qquad\text{(33)} To prove this, put e(j)=a−x(j)e^{(j)}=a-x^{(j)}. Subtracting (26) from (10) gives the exact recurrence (I−A(x(j)))e(j+1)=Q(e(j)).(34) (I-A(x^{(j)}))e^{(j+1)}=Q(e^{(j)}). \qquad\text{(34)} Leaves are exact after one repair. At a turn, exactness of the new child implies exactness of the new parent. At a product, both new children are exact once j+1≥max⁡(νL,νR)j+1\ge\max(\nu_L,\nu_R); its remaining quadratic term is zero once j≥min⁡(νL,νR)j\ge\min(\nu_L,\nu_R). These are precisely the conditions in (32). Induction through the tree proves (33).

If the tree has nn leaves, a vertex of rank ν\nu has at least 2ν−12^{\nu-1} descendant leaves: an increase of rank requires two children of the same preceding rank. Therefore νv0≤1+⌊log⁡2n⌋.(35) \nu_{v_0}\le1+\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor. \qquad\text{(35)} After this many repairs the whole actual normalized field, and hence its original closing, is exact. The proof needs the bilinear tree structure; a network with shared descendants or contraction loops is outside this assertion.

This finite repair theorem does not solve the asymptotic problem. It is an alternative exact evaluation and error-reduction mechanism. An asymptotic use of (22) still requires control of its coherent corrected closing, or of the successive corrected closings, at the hyperbolic volume rate.

8. Relation to earlier residual estimates

The existing stationary-observable estimate subtracts stationary equations from a scalar observable to cancel its uncertain first derivatives, retaining a quadratic Taylor remainder. The pointed transport estimate, Section 4, gives an exact adjoint-defect identity for a linear cocycle. Those are relevant precedents within these notes. No novelty is claimed for adjoint-weighted residual estimation as a general technique.

Here the backward row is constructed from the actual complete Conway tree, its nonlinear remainder is exactly the finite bilinear expression (11), and both the local radius and the finite repair/pruning statements are proved explicitly. No new knot volume identity or finite-exception class is concluded without the quantitative conditions (23)–(24).
