The transverse Hessian of the actual soft-cut braid phase

Private written proof, 18 November 2026.

Under an actual positive complete ideal gluing certificate, the geometric critical germ of the literal soft-cut braid phase is smooth of dimension one. In its independent occupation coordinates the Hessian has precisely the residual framing direction as kernel. The common momentum has already been quotiented in passing to those coordinates; it is not a further variable to remove from the phase.

This applies to every fixed finite braid of a one-cusped hyperbolic knot exterior supplied with the stated certificate. Existence of such a positive triangulation for every hyperbolic knot is not assumed. The result is a local complex Hessian theorem. It does not compute the residual framing period or any physical cycle coefficient.

The complete proof directs are M (the actual crossing/representation scheme), S (the soft-cut ideal), R (marked positive gluing rigidity), and F (the complex-cut and common slit frame), pinned below. The incidence-coordinate and gradient-scheme adapter is proved here.

1. The actual phase and the theorem

Fix a finite braid of the knot with cc crossings, counting both signs positively. Its closed crossing graph has cc vertices and 2c2c oriented wire arcs. Put Λ=ker⁡(L:ℤ2c→ℤc),(1) \Lambda=\ker(L:\mathbb Z^{2c}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^c), \qquad\text{(1)} where LL has incoming coefficient +1+1 and outgoing coefficient −1-1. Each crossing has two incoming and two outgoing arcs. Loops are allowed. The graph is connected and has rank c−1c-1; therefore Λ\Lambda is free of rank m=c+1m=c+1. Choosing a spanning tree makes its chord occupations an integral basis: the tree occupations are recovered uniquely by leaf elimination. The cut edge may be a chord, since the oriented knot is an Euler circuit and that edge is not an undirected bridge.

Use unwrapped logarithmic occupations ζe\zeta_e, so the local wire space is the linear space W=ker⁡(L:ℂ2c→ℂc).(2) W=\ker(L:\mathbb C^{2c}\longrightarrow\mathbb C^c). \qquad\text{(2)} Choose chord coordinates z1,…,zmz_1,\ldots,z_m on WW. Every signed transfer is its original difference of wire positions. There is no additional independent transfer variable after this linear matching is imposed.

Keep M’s literal signed principal crossing expressions, all original pivots and framing, and a trace-one complex-binomial cut as in F. Write c0=2πic_0=2\pi i, u=ζcutu=\zeta_{\mathrm{ cut}}, and Φθ=S0+gθ(u)−c0∑j=1mnjzj,(3) \Phi_\theta=S_0+g_\theta(u)-c_0\sum_{j=1}^m n_jz_j, \qquad\text{(3)} gθ(u)=c0[−tlogt−(1−t)log(1−t)+tlogθ+(1−t)log(1−θ)],t=u/c0.(4) g_\theta(u)=c_0\!\left[-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t) +t\log\theta+(1-t)\log(1-\theta)\right], \qquad t=u/c_0. \qquad\text{(4)} The logs are the matching sheets near θ=1/2\theta=1/2, with θ≠0,1\theta\ne0,1. The fixed integers njn_j are the actual local Fourier correction which makes the derivative vanish at the chosen exponentiated critical point. It changes neither the finite lattice values nor the Hessian. At θ=1/2\theta=1/2, this is S’s real-binomial cut. F permits nearby complex θ\theta and supplies regular geometric slit frames there. Its exact cut current and exponential cost remain unchanged.

Suppose the actual exterior has R’s marked positive complete ideal gluing certificate. Fix its complete oriented holonomy, the original central lift, and the selected peripheral eigenflag, with the meridian eigenvalue fixed exactly to its complete value. Take a regular source chart as supplied by S/F and choose the compatible logarithmic lift in (3).

Then, near the corresponding geometric point z*z_*, Crit⁡(Φθ) is smooth of dimension 1,ker⁡Φθ″(z*)=Tz*Crit⁡(Φθ).(5) \operatorname{Crit}(\Phi_\theta) \text{ is smooth of dimension }1,\qquad \ker\Phi_\theta''(z_*)=T_{z_*}\operatorname{Crit}(\Phi_\theta). \qquad\text{(5)} Consequently the mm-variable Hessian has rank m−1=cm-1=c. The surviving tangent is the actual rr-framing direction. The assertions hold on a sufficiently small neighborhood of any such chosen frame and cut parameter, not uniformly over all braids.

2. Crossing momenta and the exact incidence adapter

At one positive crossing, write the input log positions a,ba,b and the transfer nn; the output log positions are b+n,a−nb+n,a-n. At a negative crossing they are b−n,a+nb-n,a+n. These are linear coordinates on the three-dimensional conservation hyperplane. M’s exact generating identities, in the factorial basis, say dSv=∑outπv,edζe−∑inπv,edζe,πv,e=log⁡pv,e,(6) dS_v=\sum_{\mathrm{ out}}\pi_{v,e}\,d\zeta_e -\sum_{\mathrm{ in}}\pi_{v,e}\,d\zeta_e, \qquad \pi_{v,e}=\log p_{v,e}, \qquad\text{(6)} on that hyperplane, with the external meridian fixed. They are precisely the actual canonical crossing graph, for both signs. In particular M’s explicit rational inverse proves the equality on local Artin algebras, where all chart denominators remain units. Multiplying every momentum at this crossing by one common scalar does not change (6).

Conversely (6) determines its four log momenta up to that one common additive constant. The annihilator of the conservation hyperplane is the one-dimensional span of its defining linear form. Thus two coefficient lists in (6) differ exactly by the same addition to every πv,e\pi_{v,e}. This is true over each local Artin algebra as well. No local momentum equation is lost: M’s three derivative identities recover its three independent momentum ratios and its regular signed crossing correspondence.

For clarity, one can implement the gluing entirely linearly. Extend each SvS_v holomorphically off its conservation hyperplane by a fixed linear retraction. Its four partial derivatives give one choice of the signed momenta in (6); any other choice adds a vertex constant λv\lambda_v. On an arc from one crossing to the next, let Re=πout,e−πin,e+gθ′(u)1e=cut.(7) R_e=\pi_{{\mathrm{ out}},e}-\pi_{{\mathrm{ in}},e} +g_\theta'(u)\,1_{e={\mathrm{ cut}}}. \qquad\text{(7)} The retained pivotal linear terms can be included in this coefficient list. Their logarithmic multipliers are fixed integer turns; they do not alter the following linear argument or its Hessian. The compatible Fourier correction fixes the same local additive constants.

More explicitly, extend the integer covector nn from the chord basis to the wire space by using its coefficients on the chord edges and zero on the tree edges. Assign each resulting linear term to one incident crossing. Subtracting it from that local primitive changes its half-edge log momentum by an integer multiple of c0c_0, so it does not change its exponentiated canonical graph. We use those local log branches in (7). This realizes the exact corrected derivative in (3), rather than silently dropping a nonzero lattice covector. The pivotal integer turns are treated in the same way.

The differential of the total phase, restricted to WW, is the restriction of the covector RR. Adding the vertex constants changes RR by −Ltλ-L^t\lambda. The exact sequence 0→ℂ→ℂc→Ltℂ2c→W*→0(8) 0\longrightarrow\mathbb C \longrightarrow\mathbb C^c \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{L^t}\mathbb C^{2c} \longrightarrow W^*\longrightarrow0 \qquad\text{(8)} therefore shows that constrained stationarity is equivalent to being able to set every ReR_e equal to zero by local momentum rescalings. Its kernel consists of the one global common rescaling. There is a fixed linear right inverse on im⁡Lt\operatorname{im}L^t, obtained by deleting one vertex row and using the same spanning tree. Thus all these constructions commute with extension to local Artin algebras; they are not merely tangent-space arguments.

Exponentiated stationarity gives the same germ after its prescribed integer correction: near zero, ew−1=we^w-1=w times a holomorphic unit. Local logs of all momenta and chart functions are available because they are units at the chosen point. Equation (8) proves an isomorphism of the actual gradient scheme in the m=c+1m=c+1 chord variables with the glued canonical-graph scheme modulo the single common momentum.

This resolves the coordinate count. The enlarged graph keeps local momenta and their one global scale; the chord phase has already eliminated that scale. Removing another phase variable as a “common momentum quotient” would be incorrect.

3. The soft cut leaves exactly one framing parameter

On every noncut arc (7) is ordinary momentum matching. On the cut it is poutpin=e−gθ′(u)=1−θθuc0−u.(9) \frac{p_{\mathrm{ out}}}{p_{\mathrm{ in}}} =e^{-g_\theta'(u)} =\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\frac{u}{c_0-u}. \qquad\text{(9)} S’s ordered-coproduct ideal calculation applies to precisely these internal rows. Closure on the other strands and the preserved total K,E,FK,E,F force the cut K,e,fK,e,f to close. On the stated unit chart this also forces pout=pinp_{\mathrm{ out}}=p_{\mathrm{ in}}. Hence the ideal of (9) is exactly (u−c0θ).(10) (u-c_0\theta). \qquad\text{(10)} F proves the same assertion for the complex probe, without discarding its finite-source current. Conversely a full braid fixed point satisfying (10) obeys all these weighted rows.

Quotient the one common momentum by setting the cut momentum to one. M’s explicit formula then puts its selected eigenflag at 1=(1,1)t1=(1,1)^t, with the exact inverse eigenvalue convention. Its rational Gauss inverse and Hurwitz identities give an isomorphism, including on Artin test rings, with the selected-flag framed knot representation germ. These are actual braid-closure relations, not only equations for peripheral eigenvalues.

For the one-cusped positive certificate, R identifies the decorated representation germ modulo simultaneous PGL⁡2\operatorname{PGL}_2 with a smooth one-dimensional germ whose coordinate is the marked meridian similarity log. Fixing that log exactly to zero makes this unframed germ a point, also on local Artin algebras. The fixed eigenvalue condition matters: an unspecified invariant flag of a parabolic can have an infinitesimal motion, which R explicitly excludes by retaining the exact eigencharacter.

Complete holonomy is irreducible, and its knot meridians are noncentral parabolics with nonzero trace in the retained lift. A projective centralizer element, lifted to a matrix, conjugates each lifted meridian to itself up to sign. Its nonzero trace forces the sign to be plus. The meridians generate the group, so this matrix commutes with the entire irreducible representation and is scalar: a nonscalar commuting matrix would give a common invariant eigenline. The projective centralizer is therefore trivial. M consequently identifies the remaining framed germ with a smooth open part of the two-dimensional group B1=Stab⁡PGL⁡2(1).(11) B_1=\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_2}(1). \qquad\text{(11)} This statement uses both the Artin-scheme comparison and R’s rigidity; no dimension count replaces either.

Its explicit local coordinates are F(r,s)=(r1−rs−s),F−1J1F=(1+s−ss1−s),Acut=(1+s)−1.(12) F(r,s)=\begin{pmatrix}r&1-r\\s&-s\end{pmatrix},\qquad F^{-1}J_1F=\begin{pmatrix}1+s&-s\\s&1-s\end{pmatrix}, \quad A_{\mathrm{ cut}}=(1+s)^{-1}. \qquad\text{(12)} On the matching log sheet, u=−Log⁡(1+s),du=−ds1+s.(13) u=-\operatorname{Log}(1+s),\qquad du=-\frac{ds}{1+s}. \qquad\text{(13)} Near the midpoint s=−2s=-2, this differential is a unit in the ss-direction. For a nearby complex θ\theta, (10) is therefore precisely s=e−c0θ−1,(14) s=e^{-c_0\theta}-1, \qquad\text{(14)} leaving the free coordinate rr. The derivative remains a unit and s≠0s\ne0 on this neighborhood. Thus the actual gradient scheme of Section 2 is smooth of dimension one. The common momentum is distinct from rr: the former diagonally changes the chosen eigenflag coordinate, whereas the latter conjugates the other holonomies by the stabilizer of the now fixed noncentral cut meridian.

4. Hessian and a transverse holomorphic normal form

The defining ideal in chord coordinates is generated by the actual marked derivatives of Φθ\Phi_\theta. Its Zariski tangent space is therefore the kernel of the Hessian, before any reducedness assumption. Section 3 proves that this scheme is reduced and smooth of dimension one. This proves (5), including exclusion of further infinitesimal null directions. The coordinate rr gives its entire tangent, not a presumed redundant equation.

There is a holomorphic Morse–Bott normal form, if desired without importing a stationary-phase assertion. Choose local coordinates (r,y1,…,ym−1)(r,y_1,\ldots,y_{m-1}) flattening the smooth critical curve to y=0y=0. Its phase value is constant along that curve, since the full differential vanishes there. The Hessian on the normal yy-space is invertible. Taylor’s integral formula gives Φθ(r,y)−Φθ(r,0)=12ytA(r,y)y(15) \Phi_\theta(r,y)-\Phi_\theta(r,0) =\tfrac12 y^t A(r,y)y \qquad\text{(15)} with AA holomorphic, symmetric and invertible near the point. A constant complex congruence makes AA equal to the identity at that point. On a smaller neighborhood its convergent matrix binomial square root is symmetric and holomorphic; setting Y=A(r,y)1/2yY=A(r,y)^{1/2}y is an invertible holomorphic coordinate change. Consequently Φθ=Φθ(z*)+12∑j=1m−1Yj2.(16) \Phi_\theta=\Phi_\theta(z_*)+ \tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}Y_j^2. \qquad\text{(16)} The free coordinate rr remains. Rescaling to normalized occupation variables or multiplying the phase by the nonzero physical constant 1/(2πi)1/(2\pi i) does not change the corank. This applies to the full chord/transfer phase used in an oblique residue presentation; it is not a Hessian of an additional coherent occupation sum.

5. Scope and complete source pins

The conditional “if the old framing kernel has dimension two” in S is now discharged for the stated positive-certificate braid source. The related two-framing result for a different augmented-plat source already appears in its critical-scheme coverage; it is not used as a substitute for Section 2 here.

The positivity hypothesis concerns the supplied complete triangulation of the actual exterior. The local braid shapes need not themselves be positive. No positive triangulation is asserted for every knot. No real-contour negativity, finite-NN gauge quotient, nonzero rr-period, physical intersection number or scalar asymptotic follows from (16). All original cut, pivot, framing and support currents remain.
