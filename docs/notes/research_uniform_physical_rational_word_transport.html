Uniform physical bounds for rational-word Casimir transport

Written proof, 24 November 2026.

Every fixed original rational word transports the two pair-Casimir observables to operators with uniformly bounded norm in the original physical Hilbert–Schmidt metric. The bound is independent of the quantum level. Commutators of any fixed pair of these transported observables are O(N−1)O(N^{-1}) in that same metric.

The new estimate uses a finite cyclic-Weyl representation on the actual four-index arrays, with the physical endpoint factors retained. The exact Fricke identities and their rational-word transport are already proved in [B]. Their role here is to propagate a new uniform physical norm estimate, rather than to assert a new operator algebra.

As a consequence, any sublinear selected family of full generalized eigenspaces of a transported Casimir has a subexponentially conditioned physical frame. No norm bound for the rational word or its inverse, and no lower bound for its original scalar closing, is inferred. An additional application compares low-degree polynomial observations of an actual transported coherent packet with that same packet in the physical metric. Its finite extraction method is already in [O].

7. Source and geometric scope

The construction always uses the genuine physical quarter-turn, the original positive crossing polynomial and its full inverse, and the complete algebra (1). It makes no replacement of the original marked cap, pivot, framing, or external normalization. Its four-index extension is a norm-estimating realization of these same operators, not a changed state sum.

Uniformly bounded conjugated observables do not imply that WNW_N or its inverse has subexponential norm. They also do not imply a lower bound for a selected coefficient or for the original closing of WN(1)W_N(1). No geometric canonical graph or contributing cycle is identified solely from the finite-Weyl symbols.

An arbitrary Conway product of two independent child packets is not an invertible rational-word conjugacy. Its actual mixed forcing, described in [B], is not removed by (22). Turns or changes to overlapping pairings may move a chosen cluster, and its exterior and original closing still require their own source estimates. No new knot-volume lower bound or finite-exception theorem is claimed here.
