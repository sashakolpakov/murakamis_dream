The global LEFT weighted spectral envelope

theorem, 31 October 2026. This bounds the actual triangular-face LEFT sector at fixed positive face moduli. It is not a coefficientwise inequality or an optimized shared-capacity theorem. All original unused prefix and suffix rows are retained.

1. The original finite sector and its continuum upper envelope

Put A=x+y>0A=x+y>0, P=xy≥0P=xy\geq0, d=N−1d=N-1, m=⌊N/2⌋m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor, and

αr(δ)=4cos⁡2(π(r+1/2)N)−4sin⁡2(πδ),sj=2sin⁡(πj/N). \alpha_r(\delta)=4\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(r+1/2)}N\right)-4\sin^2(\pi\delta), \qquad s_j=2\sin(\pi j/N).

The LEFT sector has 0≤i≤j≤m−10\leq i\leq j\leq m-1. Up to its recorded overall sign, its exact start/end summand is

Arest(δ)bicd−j(∏r<iαr)(∏j<r<mαr)Ki,j, A_{\mathrm{rest}}(\delta)b^ic^{d-j} \left(\prod_{r<i}\alpha_r\right) \left(\prod_{j<r<m}\alpha_r\right)K_{i,j},\tag{1}

where Ki,i=1K_{i,i}=1 and

Ki,j=sj2AKi,j−1+sjsj−1αj−1PKi,j−2. K_{i,j}=s_j^2AK_{i,j-1}+s_js_{j-1}\alpha_{j-1}PK_{i,j-2}.

The omitted-half product ArestA_{\mathrm{rest}} is retained. For even NN it is 2Tm(σ/2)2T_m(\sigma/2), up to a sign, and has real-interval modulus at most 22. For odd N=2m+1N=2m+1 it is (σ+2)Pm(σ)(\sigma+2)P_m(\sigma), up to a sign, with

Pm(2cos⁡θ)=cos⁡((m+1/2)θ)cos⁡(θ/2). P_m(2\cos\theta)=\frac{\cos((m+1/2)\theta)}{\cos(\theta/2)}.

The quotient is a finite alternating cosine sum, bounded by NN on the real interval. Thus |Arest|≤4N|A_{\mathrm{rest}}|\leq4N at odd levels, including its removable endpoint. These exact factors have zero exponential upper rate.

The uniform transfer estimate in the global CROSS proof applies to Ki,jK_{i,j} with any interval endpoints, by the same fixed-block matrix power argument. Together with uniform upper Riemann bounds for both unused prefix and suffix, it gives the upper action

FL(μ,ν,δ)=μlog⁡b+(1−ν)log⁡c+∫μνlog⁡Lδ(t)dt,0≤μ≤ν≤12, F_L(\mu,\nu,\delta) =\mu\log b+(1-\nu)\log c+\int_\mu^\nu\log L_\delta(t)\,dt, \qquad0\leq\mu\leq\nu\leq\frac12,\tag{2}

where

α(t)=4(cos⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πδ)),Λ(t)=2sin⁡(πt)[Asin(πt)+(x−y)2sin⁡2(πt)+4Pcos⁡2(πδ)],Lδ(t)=Λ(t)|α(t)|. \begin{aligned} \alpha(t)&=4\bigl(\cos^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\delta)\bigr),\\ \Lambda(t)&=2\sin(\pi t) \left[A\sin(\pi t)+\sqrt{(x-y)^2\sin^2(\pi t)+4P\cos^2(\pi\delta)}\right],\\ L_\delta(t)&=\frac{\Lambda(t)}{|\alpha(t)|}. \end{aligned}

This rewriting uses ∫01/2log⁡|α(t)|dt=0\int_0^{1/2}\log|\alpha(t)|\,dt=0 only after bounding the actual two outside products. It makes no finite division by α\alpha and includes sampled spectral zeros. Logarithmic singularities are uniformly integrable. The O(1/N)O(1/N) endpoint shifts and at most N2N^2 start/end pairs cost o(N)o(N), uniformly in endpoints and real δ\delta.

If b=0b=0, only μ=0\mu=0 is allowed. If c=0c=0, the finite LEFT polynomial vanishes identically because its cc exponent is at least ⌊N/2⌋>0\lfloor N/2\rfloor>0.

The sharp envelope identity is

supδ∈[0,1/2]0≤μ≤ν≤1/2FL(μ,ν,δ)=max⁡{logc,Qb(0)+12logc}, \sup_{\substack{\delta\in[0,1/2]\\0\leq\mu\leq\nu\leq1/2}}F_L(\mu,\nu,\delta) =\max\left\{\log c,Q_b(0)+\frac12\log c\right\},\tag{3}

for c>0c>0 and b≥0b\geq0. Here QbQ_b is the same absolutely normalized original triangle action. Equality in (3) concerns the continuum upper envelope; it does not claim that every oscillatory finite polynomial realizes that rate.

2. Exact endpoint optimization

First suppose b>0b>0 and 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/2. Set

z=sin⁡2(πδ),k=cos⁡(πδ),D=A2−4Pz>0,tc=12−δ. z=\sin^2(\pi\delta),\qquad k=\cos(\pi\delta),\qquad D=A^2-4Pz>0,\qquad t_c=\frac12-\delta.

LδL_\delta increases from 00 to infinity before tct_c and decreases from infinity to

Lend=A+D2z L_{\mathrm{end}}=\frac{A+\sqrt D}{2z}

after tct_c. The endpoint derivatives in (2) are

∂μFL=log⁡b−log⁡Lδ(μ),∂νFL=log⁡Lδ(ν)−log⁡c. \partial_\mu F_L=\log b-\log L_\delta(\mu),\qquad \partial_\nu F_L=\log L_\delta(\nu)-\log c.\tag{4}

Before tct_c, the μ\mu dependence has its unique maximum at μb\mu_b, defined by

sin⁡2(πμb)=k2b2(b+x)(b+y). \sin^2(\pi\mu_b)=\frac{k^2b^2}{(b+x)(b+y)}.\tag{5}

A stationary ν\nu before tct_c is a minimum; a stationary μ\mu after tct_c is a minimum. Thus the only candidates beyond the diagonal μ=ν\mu=\nu have μ=μb\mu=\mu_b and either ν=1/2\nu=1/2 or a post-sign-change stationary νc\nu_c. The boundary μ=0\mu=0 is included by b→0b\to0; for positive bb, the initial positive derivative rules it out unless μ=ν=0\mu=\nu=0. These observations also cover the boundaries of the ordered endpoint triangle.

The diagonal contributes at most max⁡{log⁡c,(log⁡b+log⁡c)/2}\max\{\log c,(\log b+\log c)/2\}. The terminal endpoint ν=1/2\nu=1/2 gives

Qb(δ)+12log⁡c. Q_b(\delta)+\frac12\log c.\tag{6}

The proved derivative Qb′=−log⁡(Eb/D)Q_b'=-\log(E_b/\sqrt D), with

Eb=A+2bz+2zbA+P+b2z, E_b=A+2bz+2\sqrt z\sqrt{bA+P+b^2z},

is nonpositive, strictly negative unless b=P=0b=P=0. Hence (6) never exceeds Qb(0)+12log⁡cQ_b(0)+\tfrac12\log c. Also Qb(0)≥12log⁡bQ_b(0)\geq\tfrac12\log b, so the two quantities in (3) already cover every diagonal candidate.

A post-sign-change maximum exists only when c≥Lendc\geq L_{\mathrm{end}}. If c>max⁡(x,y)c>\max(x,y), this is precisely δ≥δc\delta\geq\delta_c, where

sin⁡2(πδc)=Ac−Pc2,sin⁡2(πνc)=k2c2(c−x)(c−y). \sin^2(\pi\delta_c)=\frac Ac-\frac P{c^2},\qquad \sin^2(\pi\nu_c)=\frac{k^2c^2}{(c-x)(c-y)}.\tag{7}

At δ=δc\delta=\delta_c, νc=1/2\nu_c=1/2; for δ>δc\delta>\delta_c, tc<νc<1/2t_c<\nu_c<1/2. If c≤max⁡(x,y)c\leq\max(x,y), there is no such interior branch.

3. The straddling branch has only spectral minima

Define the post-sign-change stationary action

Jc(δ)=νclog⁡c+∫0νclog⁡|α(t)|dt+∫νc1/2log⁡Λ(t)dt. J_c(\delta)=\nu_c\log c+\int_0^{\nu_c}\log|\alpha(t)|\,dt +\int_{\nu_c}^{1/2}\log\Lambda(t)\,dt.

Then the straddling value is exactly

SL(δ)=Qb(δ)+log⁡c−Jc(δ). S_L(\delta)=Q_b(\delta)+\log c-J_c(\delta).\tag{8}

There is no new additive action constant: (8) follows by subtracting the integrals and using the same complete prefix integral zero as in (2). Put

Tc−=c2z−cA+P,Hc=2cz−A+2zTc−. T_c^-=\sqrt{c^2z-cA+P},\qquad H_c=2cz-A+2\sqrt z\,T_c^-.

On the strict branch c>Lendc>L_{\mathrm{end}}, the exact derivative is

Jc′(δ)=−log⁡HcD. J_c'(\delta)=-\log\frac{H_c}{\sqrt D}.\tag{9}

A direct normalization proof avoids guessing a complex sheet. The envelope rule gives ∂log⁡cJc=νc\partial_{\log c}J_c=\nu_c. Differentiating (7) gives ∂δνc=−csin⁡(πδ)/Tc−\partial_\delta\nu_c=-c\sin(\pi\delta)/T_c^-. Direct algebra gives ∂log⁡clog⁡Hc=csin⁡(πδ)/Tc−\partial_{\log c}\log H_c=c\sin(\pi\delta)/T_c^-. Thus the difference between the two sides of (9) is independent of cc for each fixed δ\delta.

As cc decreases to LendL_{\mathrm{end}}, νc\nu_c tends to 1/21/2. At that endpoint the complete prefix integral has derivative zero, so Jc′J_c' tends to zero. The vanishing tail is regular because α(1/2)=−4z≠0\alpha(1/2)=-4z\ne0. Simultaneously Tc−T_c^- tends to zero and HcH_c tends to D\sqrt D. This fixes the difference to zero and proves (9). Moving interior logarithmic zeros are handled by uniform-integrability and principal-value differentiation of the explicit log-cosine primitive; no finite sampled quotient is used.

Consequently

SL′(δ)=log⁡HcEb. S_L'(\delta)=\log\frac{H_c}{E_b}.\tag{10}

The two positive numbers are larger roots with the same product DD:

Eb=Cb+Cb2−D,Cb=A+2bz,Hc=Cc+Cc2−D,Cc=2cz−A. \begin{aligned} E_b&=C_b+\sqrt{C_b^2-D},& C_b&=A+2bz,\\ H_c&=C_c+\sqrt{C_c^2-D},& C_c&=2cz-A. \end{aligned}

Both Cb,Cc≥DC_b,C_c\geq\sqrt D on this branch, and the larger-root function is strictly increasing in CC. Hence

sign⁡SL′=sign⁡(Cc−Cb)=sign⁡[(c−b)z−A]. \operatorname{sign}S_L' =\operatorname{sign}(C_c-C_b) =\operatorname{sign}[(c-b)z-A].\tag{11}

Since zz increases with δ\delta, this sign can only pass from negative to positive. An interior spectral stationary point is therefore a minimum. At the branch entry the value is (6). As δ→1/2\delta\to1/2, both μb\mu_b and νc\nu_c tend to zero, and uniform integrability gives SL→log⁡cS_L\to\log c. The straddling branch is thus bounded by the right side of (3). The diagonal and terminal candidates were already bounded in Section 2. At δ=0\delta=0, their known maximization attains (3), proving the envelope identity.

The proof extends to b=0b=0 by its sole-start limit and to xy=0xy=0 by the exact first-order recurrence and displayed formulas; AA stays positive. The possibly singular endpoint D=0D=0 occurs only at δ=1/2\delta=1/2, x=yx=y, and is handled by the same continuous endpoint value. The original protected faces are never replaced by positive radii.

4. All face phases and the final spectral cap

The exact continuous-real positivity lemma supplies a finite strengthening. Normalize

Qr=Ki,i+r∏v=i+1i+rsv2. Q_r=\frac{K_{i,i+r}}{\prod_{v=i+1}^{i+r}s_v^2}.

Then

Qr=(x+y)Qr−1+βjxyQr−2,βj=αj−1sjsj−1,j=i+r. Q_r=(x+y)Q_{r-1}+\beta_jxyQ_{r-2},\qquad \beta_j=\frac{\alpha_{j-1}}{s_js_{j-1}},\quad j=i+r.

This recurrence is used only for r≥2r\geq2, hence j≥i+2j\geq i+2; Q1=x+yQ_1=x+y is handled separately, so no denominator s0s_0 is introduced. The identity

αj−1+sjsj−1=4cos⁡2(πδ)−4sin⁡2(π2N) \alpha_{j-1}+s_js_{j-1} =4\cos^2(\pi\delta)-4\sin^2\!\left(\frac\pi{2N}\right)\tag{12}

is independent of jj. Thus βj≥−1\beta_j\geq-1 for |δ|≤1/2−1/(2N)|\delta|\leq1/2-1/(2N), equivalently σ∈[−2,2cos⁡(π/N)]\sigma\in[-2,2\cos(\pi/N)]. Induction using

Qr−xQr−1=y[Qr−1−xQr−2]+(1+βj)xyQr−2 Q_r-xQ_{r-1} =y[Q_{r-1}-xQ_{r-2}]+(1+\beta_j)xyQ_{r-2}

proves coefficientwise nonnegativity on this entire interval. The initial values are Q0=1Q_0=1, Q1=x+yQ_1=x+y. Arbitrary phases of x,yx,y therefore have modulus bounded by the same KK at their positive moduli. In (1), taking absolute values of b,cb,c and of the retained scalar prefix and suffix factors reduces all face phases to the same envelope (2). No one-sided angular gap or absolute-value replacement inside the signed recurrence is used.

The remaining real spectral cap [2cos⁡(π/N),2][2\cos(\pi/N),2] has width O(N−2)O(N^{-2}). For a polynomial pp of degree at most dd bounded by MM on [−2,bN][-2,b_N], bN=2cos⁡(π/N)b_N=2\cos(\pi/N), the exterior-circle maximum principle gives

|p(2)|≤Mexp⁡[darcosh6−bNbN+2]≤CM. |p(2)|\leq M\exp\!\left[d\,\operatorname{arcosh}\frac{6-b_N}{b_N+2}\right] \leq CM.

The same bound holds throughout the cap; its exponent is O(1)O(1) uniformly in N≥3N\geq3. Apply it to the actual LEFT polynomial at each fixed face-phase tuple. Thus (3) bounds the global real-spectral weighted absolute value at arbitrary face phases. The RIGHT version follows by reversing the original row and middle orientation. Applying the same polynomial argument on the full real interval extends the bound to a fixed complex δ\delta strip, at additional cost e2πd|Im⁡δ|e^{2\pi d|\operatorname{Im}\delta|}, uniformly in those face phases.

This is still not coefficientwise domination: the known σ=3\sigma=\sqrt3 individual-coefficient counterexample is compatible with summing at fixed moduli. Nor may the actual shared coefficient be replaced by an unproved infimum of sums of local maxima. At fixed moduli the weighted full upper rate is bounded by

max⁡{Qb(0)+Qc(0),log⁡b,log⁡c}, \max\{Q_b(0)+Q_c(0),\log b,\log c\},

because Qb(0)≥12log⁡bQ_b(0)\geq\tfrac12\log b, Qc(0)≥12log⁡cQ_c(0)\geq\tfrac12\log c, and a geometric mean never exceeds the larger endpoint. Zero-middle terms are interpreted by the exact vanishing statements above. A strict complete-radius balance condition permits a shared-source application; no general capacity equality follows here.

Validation and scope

The recurrence is used only from its second step. This is a weighted local theorem, not a coefficientwise comparison or a shared capacity equality.
