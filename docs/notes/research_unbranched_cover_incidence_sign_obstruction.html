Why an unbranched exterior cover does not repair the replicated incidence sign obstruction

31 October 2026. This is an obstruction to a source-preserving cover, not a fractional atomization argument or a claim that every cover of a link exterior is trivial.

1. The proposed construction and the exact scope

Let D be an actual certified marked simple-white plat. Keep its original white triangulated sphere, pairing of the two triangles at each port, and protected adjacent MAIN faces u,v. At a port e write the four original face labels as (x,b,c,y); its two actual shaded triangles have white incidence sets (x,b,y) and (x,c,y). Their common outer edge xy is MATCHING. The four labels are distinct. In particular the two outer faces cannot both be the protected MAIN pair, so at least one outer face is ordinary.

Let B be the original supported bipartite incidence graph: one node for each port and each ordinary white face, and one edge for every supported port/face incidence. Write εe,f∈𝑭2\varepsilon_{e,f}\in\mathbf F_2 for the literal incidence sign character in the selected source chamber. All exact scalar modes remain separate. A row gauge and common face signs would solve

εe,f=κe+γfin 𝑭2. \varepsilon_{e,f}=\kappa_e+\gamma_f\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_2. \tag{1}

For the strict straddling LEFT chamber the entries are (0,0,1,0), and RIGHT is reflected; the argument below allows any chamber whose two triangle copies of a common outer incidence have the SAME character. This includes the actual characters in the current regular-window route.

The suggested repair is an actual finite unbranched cover of the original drilled exterior, with unchanged source chamber and q data on every copy of each port, followed by the original cut-ball fan construction. To make that construction source-preserving, it must assign one signed clock to each lifted white face and allow local row/triangle gauges, with each lifted incidence retaining its base character. We allow different gauges on the two triangle halves; this is weaker than requiring a single gauge on an entire lifted port. Magnitudes may vary between sheets. No averaging of q, no change of chamber between sheets, and no new incidence signs are permitted in this particular proposal.

Theorem. Under these hypotheses, such a lifted sign assignment exists on an exterior cover only if (1) already has a solution on the original supported graph B. Conversely, if (1) is soluble, its signs pull back to every cover (and the identity cover suffices for this sign issue). Thus an unbranched cover cannot remove a nontrivial original incidence class within the proposed source-preserving construction, in any degree.

This is only a necessary sign test for the original group/fan sewing. Solving it does not supply the other geometric, source-asymptotic, boundary, or regulator hypotheses of that construction.

2. Resolve ports before discussing topology

Let H be the white–triangle incidence graph of ONE original cut ball. There is one node te,+t_{e,+} for (x,b,y), one te,−t_{e,-} for (x,c,y), and one node per original white face. There is one edge for each triangle corner. This is the radial graph of the actual white triangulated sphere, not an abstract replacement of its ribbon structure. Let H’ remove only the two protected white nodes and their incident edges. No ordinary face is removed.

Pull the source character to H’ by

εH(te,+,f)=εe,f,εH(te,−,f)=εe,f. \varepsilon_H(t_{e,+},f)=\varepsilon_{e,f},\qquad \varepsilon_H(t_{e,-},f)=\varepsilon_{e,f}. \tag{2}

The following elementary equivalence is the key algebraic point:

εH is a coboundary on H′⇔ε is a coboundary on B. \varepsilon_H\text{ is a coboundary on }H' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \varepsilon\text{ is a coboundary on }B. \tag{3}

The forward implication is worth proving explicitly. If

εH(t,f)=κt+γf, \varepsilon_H(t,f)=\kappa_t+\gamma_f,

choose an ordinary outer face o of port e. Both halves are incident to o and have the same prescribed character there. Hence

κe,++γo=εe,o=κe,−+γo, \kappa_{e,+}+\gamma_o=\varepsilon_{e,o} =\kappa_{e,-}+\gamma_o,

so κe,+=κe,−\kappa_{e,+}=\kappa_{e,-}. Their common value defines κe\kappa_e in (1). The converse simply copies a row gauge to both halves.

There is also an explicit cycle version. In any supported B cycle, replace a passage f–e–g by f–t–g if the two faces lie in one half. If it must pass from b to c, choose an ordinary outer o and use

b−−te,+−−o−−te,−−−c. b\;--\;t_{e,+}\;--\;o\;--\;t_{e,-}\;--\;c.

The two added outer characters cancel in 𝑭2\mathbf F_2. Each B cycle therefore gives a closed H’ walk with the SAME sign sum. Thus every original nonzero cycle has a specific resolved witness; protected zero targets cannot hide it.

3. Why the resolved obstruction cannot survive inside a lifted ball

The topological input is the ACTUAL cut described in original cut-ball construction, §§1–4. Cut along the projection sphere and the original crossing half disks. Opening each specified half disk cancels its semicircle handle. Each resulting piece is a genuine ball with the original labeled white/shaded boundary. Compact truncation of its ideal vertices retains a genuine compact ball. The truncated rectangles at main and auxiliary contacts are boundary disks, not deleted holes in that ball.

The graph H can be represented on this boundary by face centers and arcs through their actual common edges. The white–triangle adjacency is the dual radial adjacency of that labeled medial cellulation. In particular every H’ loop is null-homotopic in the cut ball. Removing protected nodes from the graph does not remove the protected faces or the filling disks from the manifold. A small MAIN-contact radial four-cycle bounds a disk through the corresponding truncated cusp rectangle; larger supported cycles are no less contractible in the ball. The boundary of one such rectangle is not itself a main-cusp meridian: a genuine meridional curve on the assembled cusp torus traverses the appropriate identified rectangles. One cannot assign nontrivial meridional monodromy to the perimeter of a single retained rectangle.

For example, the original six-port chart in exact face-sign criterion has triangles (5,7,0) at the output of port3 and (6,5,7) at the input of port5. Their shared edge (5,7) is MAIN. The nonzero sign cycle

port3−−face7−−port5−−face5−−port3 \mathrm{port}_3\;--\;\mathrm{face}_7\;--\;\mathrm{port}_5 \;--\;\mathrm{face}_5\;--\;\mathrm{port}_3

is already the resolved four-cycle around that one contact. Its obstruction is a contractible disk relation, not a choice of a main component’s meridional monodromy. The general proof does not depend on this example or assert that its chosen chamber is a full maximum.

For any unbranched covering p:Y′→Yp:Y'\to Y, pull back the characteristic map of this compact cut ball. Since the ball is simply connected, each lift is an actual copy of the entire ball with the same boundary incidence data. This statement does not require a regular, connected, or double cover. It remains true when the original shaded pairing sends a half to another copy, as in a retained half twist: that pairing changes which lifted balls are glued, not the incidence within a single lifted ball.

Now examine just ONE lifted ball. The proposed common signed clocks give one γ\gamma for each of its ordinary white faces. Each shaded triangle has its allowed local gauge κt\kappa_t, possibly coming from a different lifted port than the other half with the same base label. Because every port copy retains the base chamber, their incidence relations are exactly (2). Consequently εH\varepsilon_H MUST be a coboundary on H’. Equation (3) forces the original ε\varepsilon to be exact on B. This proves the theorem.

Equivalently, if a graph voltage cover is being interpreted as actual sheet transition data, all of its resolved H’ loop voltages must vanish because those loops bound disks in one original ball. A nonzero B cycle has the same-voltage H’ witness from §2, violating this necessary extension relation. Such a graph cover does not extend over the original cut ball; it is not an exterior cover with the required source incidence dictionary.

4. Relation to more general covering criteria

For an independently specified graph map i:G→Yi:G\to Y, a binary voltage character extends to a double cover of Y precisely when its class lies in the image of

i*:H1(Y;𝑭2)→H1(G;𝑭2). i^*:H^1(Y;\mathbf F_2)\longrightarrow H^1(G;\mathbf F_2).

Equivalently it must annihilate the kernel of H1(G;𝑭2)→H1(Y;𝑭2)H_1(G;\mathbf F_2)\to H_1(Y;\mathbf F_2). At the presentation level one must also respect every attaching relation; for permutation-valued finite covers the product of the gluing permutations around each actual relation is the identity. Agreement of meridional monodromies along a component is one necessary check, not a substitute for the ball and edge relations.

No canonical map of the already-collapsed source graph B to Y should be assumed without checking the two shaded halves and their repeated outer incidences. The resolved ball graph is what gives the decisive relation here. The theorem does not claim that every failure to extend a 𝒁/2\mathbf Z/2 character prevents every unrelated finite cover. It proves directly that ANY proposed exterior cover carrying the replicated original signs and common-face sewing would already solve (1) on one lifted ball.

5. What this rules out, and what it leaves open

This obstruction is different from fractional-cover obstruction. There is no atomization, no change of angle allocations between sheets, and no Jensen loss in this argument. Replicating q exactly avoids that earlier problem but does not avoid the contractible resolved sign loop.

It rules out repairing an inconsistent real source sign chamber solely by passing to an unbranched cover while retaining the original two-ball white-face and port dictionary. Therefore dividing a hypothetical cover representation volume by its degree cannot bypass this sign obstruction under those hypotheses.

It does not rule out a construction that changes the chamber or local generating function on different copies, changes the projected incidence data, inserts new defect surfaces, or uses a branched cover. Those would need their own exact source/action comparison and fundamental-chain proof. It also does not prove a source upper bound from the unresolved magnitude and phase-cancellation estimates.

The proof uses exact face-sign criterion, conditional signed group sewing, original cut-ball construction §§1–4, original paired fundamental chain §3, and the fractional-cover obstruction above.
