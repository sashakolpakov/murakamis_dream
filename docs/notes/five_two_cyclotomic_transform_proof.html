A finite whole-source proof of the five-crossing cyclotomic transform

28 October 2026. The candidate in Section 6 of the actual five-crossing source is an exact identity for EVERY primitive Nth root Q, N>=2, and arbitrary nonzero Y. No Jones identity, ordinary-color interpolation, or published Bailey proof is used.

Write q=Q in the proof, d=N-1, F_a=(q;q)_a, and

H_n(x)=sum_(j=0)^n {n choose j}_q x^j,
T_n(Y)=product_(r=1)^n(Y+Y^-1-q^r-q^-r).

Then the ORIGINAL reduced whole source satisfies

F_N(Y)=sum_(n=0)^d C_n(q)T_n(Y),
C_n(q)=(-1)^n q^(-n(n+3)/2)H_n(q^(-n-1)).   (1)

Every original turn, cap, Cartan and framing factor is already contained in the starting triple source below. No factor is changed in proving (1). The original full ADO modified-dimension germ, if attached, multiplies both sides unchanged.

The proof supplies three reusable finite identities: a terminating three-factor q-Chu transform, a generic Newton connection, and a root Rogers-polynomial convolution with an arbitrary extra variable.

1. Elementary finite identities

For k<N and arbitrary a,b,

sum_(ell=0)^k (q^-k;q)_ell(a;q)_ell(b;q)_ell q^ell/F_ell
   =sum_(r=0)^k {k choose r}_q a^(k-r)b^r(a;q)_r. (2)

Expand (b;q)_ell, set ell=r+s, and use

sum_(s=0)^n(q^-n;q)_s(c;q)_s q^s/F_s=c^n.    (3)

For completeness, expand (c;q)_s in (3). For each expansion index r the remaining sum is (q^(1-(n-r));q)_(n-r) by the finite q-binomial theorem. It vanishes unless r=n; that last coefficient is c^n. Substituting (3) into the expansion of (2), together with (q^-k)_r/F_r=(-1)^r q^(-kr+r(r-1)/2){k choose r}, cancels every extra sign and power. This proves (2). It is symmetric in a,b, so either factor may be expanded.

We also need the finite q-Vandermonde consequence

sum_(u=0)^a sum_(j=0)^b {a choose u}_q
                      {b choose j}_q q^(ju)=H_(a+b)(1). (4)

To prove (4), compare coefficients in the finite q-binomial expansion of a product of lengths a and b. It gives {a+b choose l}=sum_(u+v=l){a choose u}{b choose v} q^((b-v)u). Replace v by b-j and sum over l. All denominators used in this application have indices at most d; no root-zero factorial is divided out.

2. Reduce the ACTUAL triple to a symmetric polynomial basis

The previously proved original source is

F_N(Y)=sum_(m+ell<=d)sum_(j=0)^m
   Y^(2(d-m))q^(j(m+1)+m+ell+2)
   {m+ell choose ell}_q {m choose j}_q
   (q/Y;q)_(m+ell)(q^(j+1)/Y;q)_ell.        (5)

Replace j by m-j and put k=d-m. The ell sum becomes the left side of (2) with a=q^-k/Y, b=q^(-k-j)/Y, because q^(m+1)=q^-k. Use the SYMMETRIC form of its right side. After the exact prefactor cancellation q^(m(m+2)+2)=q^(k^2+1), equation (5) is

q sum_(k=0)^d Y^k(q/Y;q)_(d-k)
   *sum_(r=0)^k {k choose r}_q
      sum_(j=0)^(d-k){d-k choose j}_q
                      q^(jr)(q^(-k-j)/Y;q)_r. (6)

Expand the last Pochhammer with index s. Use {k choose r}{r choose s}={k choose s}{k-s choose r-s}. The remaining sums over r-s and j are exactly (4), with lengths k-s and d-k. Thus

F_N(Y)=q sum_(k=0)^d Y^k(q/Y;q)_(d-k)
   *sum_(s=0)^k {k choose s}_q(-1/Y)^s
               q^(-ks+s(s-1)/2)H_(d-s)(1). (7)

Interchange k,s, set n=d-s, k=s+r, and reflect the root Gaussian by

{d-n+r choose r}_q
   =(-1)^r q^(-nr+r(r-1)/2){n choose r}_q.  (8)

All powers simplify to

F_N(Y)=q sum_(n=0)^d (-1)^n q^(-n(n+1)/2)
                                  H_n(1) P_n(Y), (9)
P_n(Y)=sum_(r=0)^n {n choose r}_q(-Y)^r
                     q^(r(r+1)/2)(q/Y;q)_(n-r).

For the sign at either parity, the root calculation used here is (-1)^(d-n)q^(-(d-n)(d-n+1)/2) =(-1)^n q^(-n(n+1)/2). It follows from the full identity (-1)^d q^(d(d+1)/2)=1; no even-level sign is discarded.

3. Generic Newton connection

For generic q, and hence at the present root whenever n<N,

P_n(Y)=(-1)^n q^(n(n+1)/2)
              sum_(k=0)^n q^(n-k){n choose k}_q T_k(Y). (10)

Here is a direct verification independent of (5). The defining finite convolution for P_n gives

P_0=1, P_1=1-qX,
P_n=(1-q^n X)P_(n-1)+q^n(1-q^(n-1))P_(n-2),
X=Y+Y^-1.                                  (11)

One way to verify (11) purely formally is to set A(z)=sum_n P_n z^n/F_n. Its defining convolution gives A(qz)/A(z)=(1-z)/[(1-qYz)(1-qz/Y)]; the two first-order series equations follow immediately by comparing successive coefficients in the finite q-binomial series. Comparing coefficients in this displayed equation gives (11). This is an identity of formal rational coefficients, not a rootwise infinite-product evaluation.

Set R_n=(-1)^n q^(-n(n+1)/2)P_n. Then (11) becomes

R_n=(X-q^-n)R_(n-1)+(q^(1-n)-1)R_(n-2).     (12)

The sum on the right of (10), after removing its prefactor, satisfies the same recurrence and initial values. To check a T_k coefficient, use X T_k=T_(k+1)+(q^(k+1)+q^(-k-1))T_k and {n choose k}={n-1 choose k-1}+q^k{n-1 choose k}. The only remaining ratio is {n-2 choose k}/{n-1 choose k} =(1-q^(n-1-k))/(1-q^(n-1)), which makes the two residual terms q^-n-q^(-k-1) cancel. This proves (10).

Substitution of (10) into (9) cancels BOTH quadratic phases and signs. Consequently the actual Newton coefficient is

C_k(q)=q sum_(n=k)^d q^(n-k){n choose k}_q H_n(1). (13)

4. A reusable ROOT convolution evaluates every coefficient

In fact for arbitrary x, as a polynomial identity in x,

q sum_(n=k)^d q^(n-k){n choose k}_q H_n(x)
  =(-1)^k q^(-k(k+3)/2)
                          x^d H_k(q^(-k-1)/x).  (14)

The right side is interpreted after multiplying by x^d, so is polynomial also at x=0. Thus every x-Euler moment can be differentiated in (14), with its source character x^(N-1) retained.

Here is a finite coefficient proof. Put h_x(z)=sum_(n=0)^d H_n(x)z^n. Gaussian generating functions and the exact root product prod_(r=0)^d(1-q^r z)=1-z^N give

h_x(z)=sum_(j=0)^d (xz)^j(q^(j+1)z;q)_(d-j). (15)

Indeed both sides have degree at most d, and their first N coefficients agree by sum_(n>=j){n choose j}z^n=z^j/(z;q)_(j+1). This last generating identity follows directly from q-Pascal; it is used only through degree d where all factorials are units.

Set a=q^(-k-1)=q^(d-k). The convolution

sum_(r=0)^(d-k) a^r {r+k choose k}_q H_(d-r)(x)

is the coefficient of z^d in h_x(z)/(az;q)_(k+1). In (15), every term with j<=d-k-1 cancels the whole denominator, leaving a polynomial of degree d-k-1 and hence ZERO z^d coefficient. Write the remaining j as d-k+s, 0<=s<=k. Its quotient is

x^(d-k+s)z^(d-k+s)/(az;q)_(s+1).

Its z^d coefficient is x^(d-k+s)a^(k-s){k choose s}. Summing over s gives

x^d H_k(a/x).                               (16)

Finally use the exact root reflection {d-r choose k}=(-1)^k q^(-rk-k(k+1)/2){r+k choose k} in the left side of (14), setting n=d-r. Its prefactor is (-1)^k q^(-k(k+3)/2) and the remaining sum is (16). This proves (14) at every parity and composite level.

5. The candidate is proved as a WHOLE meridian identity

Take x=1 in (14) and substitute into (13). This gives exactly (1), proving the candidate (6.1) of the source note. At Y=1, T_n(1)=(-1)^n q^(-n(n+1)/2)F_n^2, so in the ORIGINAL Q orientation the exact reduced state is

<5_2>_N=sum_(0<=j<=n<N)
        Q^[-n(n+2)-j(n+1)](Q;Q)_n^3
                          /[(Q;Q)_j(Q;Q)_(n-j)]. (17a)

This is the source-preserving double sum used by the inner quadrature theorem; its endpoints are not changed. Equivalently, with t=Q^-1,

F_N(Y)=sum_(n=0)^(N-1) t^n(tY;t)_n(t/Y;t)_n
             sum_(j=0)^n t^(j(j+1)){n choose j}_t. (17)

All steps were identities of Laurent polynomials in Y. Therefore every fixed-root meridian derivative, including the normal complete-color source, is retained. No conclusion about generic-q derivatives of the actual whole crossing is obtained by differentiating the root identity in Q.

This proves a finite source transform; it does not establish the saddle selection or the exponential volume lower bound. Those analyses may now use (17) as an identity of the ORIGINAL state, with its original normalization.

Exact audit checks the reusable generic identities, every intermediate root transform, the x-polynomial convolution, and whole-Y Laurent coefficients at small levels. The uniform proof is the finite calculation above, not interpolation of test colors.
