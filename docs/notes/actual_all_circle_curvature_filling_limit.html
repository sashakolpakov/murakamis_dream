A quantitative quantum limit allowing short twists at large junctions

The number of branches incident to a crossing circle can supply part of the estimate previously obtained by taking its twist number large. This note proves the finite-sum limit under an explicit inequality in those two integers. The volume theorem combines it with the ordered octahedral decomposition and positive filling path. The limiting action is therefore the volume of the original knot complement throughout the stated class, including the short coefficients permitted by the inequality.

1. Assertion and scope

Consider an actual reduced Conway operation tree in which every product junction carries an original crossing-circle operator. Include ordinary terminal crossing circles as degree-one vertices. The resulting circle graph 𝒯\mathcal T is a finite tree, with physical quarter-turn edges and no bare junctions. Keep the ordered branches, orientations, knot pattern, primitive cut and zero framing. For the literal construction defined in the volume theorem, the strict angle condition follows inductively from the product intervals; it is not a further restriction on these trees.

Let dv≥1d_v\ge1 be the graph degree of circle vv, and let nv∈ℤn_v\in \mathbb Z be its actual fixed crossing power. The graph degree dvd_v is unrelated to the quantum degree N−1N-1. Put 𝔖(𝒯,𝒏)=∑vdvdv2+nv2. \mathfrak S(\mathcal T,\mathbf n) =\sum_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2}. \tag{1} The following conservative sufficient condition will be proved: 𝔖≤2−25. \mathfrak S\le2^{-25}. \tag{2} It implies a nonzero first joint Fourier charge and the actual reduced microscopic profile through both level parities, on every |s|≤R<1|s|\le R<1. The exponential rate is the real part of the explicitly continued first-charge action. Conditions (1)–(2) are quantitative finite-action conditions, not an assumption that this saddle dominates.

Short, even zero, crossing powers are permitted by this certificate. Section 11 explains how the companion geometric proofs identify the action with the actual knot volume under the same bound. The graph and all powers are fixed before NN tends to infinity.

The invariant in the conclusion is the reduced, zero-framed ADO polynomial FN(M)F_N(M), normalized to one on the unknot. Its complete value is FN(1)=KN(K)F_N(1)=K_N(K). The scalar calculation specifies the same framing convention, including all orientation types. The critical action in the limit is defined in Sections 4 and 7.

2. Dependencies and where the old proof used longness

The dependencies are the current authoritative notes:

  • the exact crossing trace, Sections 2–4 and 7: exact translated-grid trace, full crossing scalar, Fourier coefficients, normal limits and fixed-power polynomial bounds;
  • the finite sum over internal edge indices, Sections 2 and 4–8: primitive lattice, canonical GNG_N, finite cotangent currents and the saddle insertion rule;
  • the joint Fourier calculation, Sections 3–5: real-spectator telescoping and the exact coefficient of charge one;
  • bounds for the full polynomial traces, Sections 1–4, with its cited full spectral-jet and Chebyshev folding bounds;
  • the crossing and framing scalar: the unchanged exact scalar identity for fixed integer powers.

These are mathematical inputs, not numerical diagnostics. The earlier source transfer theorem and the pretzel filling calculation use reciprocal-slope implicit functions; the geometric source-regulator theorem uses a neighborhood of the complete framed representation.

Longness enters the present published joint proof in four analytic places: existence and charge ordering of a nearby critical branch; a nonempty finite charge cutoff with a fixed tail loss; the angular connectors; and comparison of the total critical drop with fixed discarded-source losses. The geometric completion is a separate fifth use. This proof replaces the four analytic uses in the all-circle class.

For two adjacent centers each of degree B+1B+1, with BB leaves at each and every leaf power of magnitude at least BB, both center powers may be arbitrary fixed short integers: 𝔖<4/B\mathfrak S<4/B, so B≥227B\ge2^{27} suffices. The large constants reflect deliberately coarse global estimates. The general quantity (1) allows any number and arrangement of short high-degree circles within the sum budget.

These examples retain the original knot/parity qualification. They are not claims of a new topological family or priority. 11. Identification with the knot volume

Small angular variables do not imply that the primitive meridian logarithms are small: a high-degree circle collects contributions from many edges. The geometric proof therefore follows the entire path of filling equations, with its logarithm branches retained.

The ordered decomposition cuts the original augmented exterior into two octahedra per tree edge. Its literal strand matchings retain every face pairing, half twist and primitive peripheral path. The angular construction subdivides these octahedra into eight positive tetrahedra per edge on the chart |τv|<10−2|\tau_v|<10^{-2}. Section 7 places the whole path

Ψτv+invτv2π=ic,0≤c≤1, \Psi_{\tau_v}+\frac{i n_v\tau_v}{2\pi}=ic,\qquad 0\le c\le1,

inside this chart. The completion proof computes the actual periods

Uv=−2πΨτv−iϵvτv,Vv=2iτv,Uv+pvVv=−2πic. U_v=-2\pi\Psi_{\tau_v}-i\epsilon_v\tau_v,\qquad V_v=2i\tau_v,\qquad U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi ic.

It verifies that the primitive slope bounds a tube of cone angle 2πc2\pi c. At c=1c=1 the tube is smooth, the auxiliary fillings are precisely the original surgeries, and the main knot cusp remains complete. The same proof fixes the additive volume constant at the complete augmented link and integrates the boundary-corrected action. It gives
