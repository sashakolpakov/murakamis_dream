Global logarithmic phases on the proper-return domains

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

The proper-return strip of the original positive primitive equations supports a single global Newton label and value phase. The domain for two disjoint proper subproducts is contractible, and all seven multiplication factorial arguments have fixed global logarithms and primitives calibrated from the original strict real gate. The same statement holds for both signed crossing gates, with their existing integer Fourier characters retained.

These functions pull back to an arbitrary-degree vertex and any binary resolution with allocated integer crossing powers. The resulting phases telescope to the original angular action, independently of the resolution. This is a global statement about logarithmic marks and actions. It does not determine a Fourier coefficient of the complete lattice kernel or an intersection coefficient of the original finite cycle.

Theorem. The domains ℰ,𝒟\mathcal E,\mathcal D and 𝒱d,n\mathcal V_{d,n} defined below are contractible. The globally normalized phases satisfy the multiplication, crossing and complementary identities (23), (32) and (38). Every allocated proper-subproduct resolution on 𝒱d,n\mathcal V_{d,n} therefore has the same marked action (39). The original crossing characters and finite pairing are retained.

4. Arbitrary degree, allocated powers, and the final pairing

The finite crossing carriers and their subleading scalars are those of the pinned spectral adapter. The global action identity does not replace them by their limits.

The positive primitive theorem places every actual selected vertex with positive incident edges in (34). The functions above therefore pull back to any such finite original network. This does not require the network’s geometric realization locus itself to be contractible: the marked functions are already defined on the ambient product of the domains (34). Degree-one traces have no proper-prefix chart here. The degree-two zero-power case retains its exact complementary delta pairing without adding a fictitious strict gate.

Thus this theorem gives coherent phases on the primitive locus and does not carry that existing descent contour into Newton variables.

5. What global phase transport implies about coefficients

The theorem fixes logarithmic marks, additive action constants, and the original crossing Fourier characters throughout the proper-return domains. It removes path ambiguity in those data. It does not place every factorial argument in its original strip: the positive-star construction has a real negative argument, and the exact chamber atlas retains its level-dependent wall factors.

At a chosen point, compare these globally marked primitives with a chamber’s canonical J+J_+ or J−J_-. The difference is an explicitly retained integer-affine Fourier character, as in the atlas. Its integer coefficients are constant on any connected chart where the corresponding branch comparison is defined. Contractibility does not say that this character is zero.

In particular, the support test cannot use the wall polynomial alone. Already on the original strict signed-crossing triangle all wall factors are one, whereas (32) has the nonzero character −2πiσx-2\pi i\sigma x. That character belongs to the original lattice lift, not to a missing wall term. A coefficient test must retain both the lattice kernel and the wall factors with the fixed action above.

Even a nonzero coefficient of a specified local expansion of those complete factors does not compute the original relative-cycle intersection. The finite supported residue cycle, its endpoint currents, and its deformed boundary terms still require control. No full scalar noncancellation, new knot class, or extension of the volume theorem is asserted.
