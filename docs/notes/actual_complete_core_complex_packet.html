A fixed complex external-color packet for the original complete core

30 October 2026. This note assembles the exponentially accurate local interpolation into the actual shared-face period. The theorem applies to each fixed certified augmented plat at arbitrary fixed bridge width. Its object is the exact complete-image term in color reflection. The other reflected terms remain part of the long-filling problem.

Subsequent geometric identification: the marked deformation theorem resolves the boundary reconstruction dependency stated below, retaining the primitive collar and the complex regulator boundary correction. It does not remove the ordinary-color subsets from the full source.

1. The finite object and theorem

Fix a connected labeled convex ideal right-angled augmented source D covered by the signed complete-source theorem. Retain its actual two marked faces, exterior normalization, and a vector of half twists epsilon_i in {0,1,-1}. Let R be its number of crossing circles and n=R-1 the dimension of the finite-face logarithmic torus after fixing scale. All constants below may depend on this fixed labeled diagram. Its bridge number and matching graph are otherwise unrestricted within that geometric certificate.

Set d=N-1, M=floor(d/2), s0=d-2M, and delta_min=s0/(2N). The exact color reflection specifies the complete-image core F_N(h), before any asymptotics, by multiplying each complete channel a_i by cos(pi h_i(d-2a_i)/N). In particular F_N(0) is the original signed complete augmented source. Cosine makes F_N(h) an entire function of its external variables h. No interpolation of an unspecified sequence is being chosen here.

The following holds on a fixed complex polydisc |chi_i|<rho, where chi=h/N. There are holomorphic critical actions S_N(chi), and nonsingular symmetric Hessians K_N(chi) of dimension m=2R-1, such that

F_N(N chi)/F_N(0)
  = exp[N(S_N(chi)-S_N(0))]
    sqrt[det K_N(0)/det K_N(chi)] [1+O(N^-1)].       (1)

The error is uniform on a smaller closed polydisc. The square root is the holomorphic branch equal to1 at chi=0. Thus the actual core is nonzero throughout that closed polydisc for all sufficiently large N. Both parities and composite quantum levels are included. The functions S_N converge holomorphically to S, and

S(0)=Vol(Y_D^epsilon)/(2pi)=Vol(Q_D)/pi,
lim (2pi/N) log|F_N(N chi)|=2pi Re S(chi).          (2)

The second equality is a locally uniform limit. The factor (1) retains S_N in its exponential: holomorphic convergence S_N -> S alone does not permit replacing exp(N S_N) by exp(N S).

Only the COMPLETE VALUE of S in (2) is identified here with the actual hyperbolic volume. Identifying its whole germ with the marked geometric regulator requires the boundary reconstruction described in section7. The theorem also does not estimate the nonempty ordinary-color subsets in the original reflection identity.

2. Exact reflected period and analytic local factors

Write

B_(N,a)(x,b,c,y)
   = beta_a^2 D_a(x,b,y)D_a(x,c,y)P_(d-2a)(x,y).

The exact polynomial reflection extends this original polynomial from 0<=a<=M to 0<=a<=d by B_(N,d-a)=B_(N,a); the half-twist eigenvalue obeys the same reflection. Consequently the original multiplicities k_a and cosine weights unfold to one full finite sum, with no omitted endpoint:

F_N(N chi)=chi_(D,N) N^R product_i omega_M^epsilon_i
  *(2pi)^(-n) integral_(face torus)
    sum_(delta in Lambda_N^R)
    product_i B_(N,a_i)(r exp(i theta))
    /product_(finite faces) (r_f exp(i theta_f))^d
    *exp[pi i N sum_i epsilon_i(delta_i^2-delta_min^2)
                 +2pi i N sum_i chi_i delta_i] dtheta.   (3)

Here delta_i=(d-2a_i)/(2N), a_i=0,…,d. Lambda_N is the physical integer or half-integer lattice of mesh 1/N in the finite interval [-d/(2N),d/(2N)]. At fixed scale one theta coordinate is zero. The two marked face variables remain zero. Equation(3) is the literal marked coefficient measure, including its N^R factor and framing unit.

Use r=r_N, the positive critical radius of the all-M coefficient. The coefficient theorem gives r_N -> r_geom, transverse Hessian cN<=H_face,N<=CN, and fixed logarithmic derivatives O(N) on a common face neighborhood. Positive-matrix preconditioning makes the determinant factors nonzero on a fixed complex neighborhood, including the actual zero patterns.

The exponential interpolation theorem supplies even holomorphic functions Phi_i,N(delta;w)=-H_i,N(delta;r_N exp w)/N on fixed complex delta and face neighborhoods, with Phi_i,N(0;w)=0,

B_(N,a)(r_N exp w)/B_(N,M)(r_N exp w)
  = exp[-N(Phi_i,N(delta;w)-Phi_i,N(delta_min;w))]
      [1+O(exp(-cN))].                              (4)

This comparison holds on the actual REAL channel grid and for COMPLEX face variables w. Uniform holomorphic derivatives and comparison with V_i(delta;r_N exp w) hold on smaller neighborhoods; these functions converge to V_i(delta;r_geom exp w). In particular, at the finite radii,

partial_delta^2 V_i(0;r_N exp w)=2C_i(r_N exp w),
C_i=pi(sqrt(b_i(x_i+y_i)+x_i y_i)
              +sqrt(c_i(x_i+y_i)+x_i y_i))/(x_i+y_i)>0

at positive real radii. The comparison error in (4) is exponential, which is essential when the Fourier transform itself has an exponential decay. A relative O(N^-1) grid estimate would not suffice.

3. Excluding the original complement

Choose small fixed cutoffs delta0,theta0 inside the neighborhoods of (4). If some |delta_i|>delta0, the original all-channel shared-radius bound gives an exp(-cN) gap relative to the complete scale. This is the bound proved from the literal determinant and forced polynomial in the channel-rate theorem, together with the strict maximum at the fold. The external weight in (3) costs at most exp(pi N sum_i |Im chi_i|). A sufficiently small fixed chi polydisc preserves the gap. There are only polynomially many channels.

For all |delta_i|<=delta0 but |theta|>theta0, apply the exact positive matrix contraction to every original determinant. Its pair-trace lower bounds cN are uniform for a/N in [1/2-delta0,1/2]. Indeed the bulk Cholesky-square bounds in the coefficient theorem use a fixed compact index interval strictly below N/2; choose delta0 so that every matrix under consideration contains that same macroscopic interval. The one-variable boundary factors need no pair trace. The remaining actual pair graph is connected and primitive, exactly as in that theorem. Thus, uniformly over these channels,

|product_i B_(N,a_i)(r_N exp(i theta))|
   <= product_i B_(N,a_i)(r_N)
                  *exp[-cN dist(theta,0)^2].         (5)

Each forced polynomial has nonnegative coefficients and cannot increase the torus modulus. This is a global angular estimate on the actual physical summands, not on their local interpolants. Shrinking chi again preserves its fixed gap outside theta0. Both discarded parts of (3) are therefore bounded by a polynomial times exp[N(f_N-c)], where

f_N=N^-1 log[product_i B_(N,M)(r_N)/product_f r_N,f^d].

Any polynomial factor is absorbed by reducing c for large N.

4. The selected joint critical point

On the retained face box put w=i theta and define the marked logarithms

psi_N(w)=N^-1 log[product_i B_(N,M)(r_N exp w)]
                   -(d/N) sum_f log(r_N,f exp w_f),

Psi_N(theta,delta;chi)=psi_N(i theta)
   -sum_i[Phi_i,N(delta_i;i theta)-Phi_i,N(delta_min;i theta)]
   +pi i sum_i epsilon_i(delta_i^2-delta_min^2)
   +2pi i sum_i chi_i delta_i.                       (6)

All logs continue the original positive factors. The even-level anchor delta_min is retained. The framing and omega_M factors stay outside this phase.

At chi=0, evenness makes every delta derivative vanish exactly at delta=0. Also d_theta psi_N(0)=0, and the anchor contributes only O(N^-2), with the same bound for fixed derivatives. The joint critical point therefore lies O(N^-2) from (theta,delta)=(0,0). The negative Hessian there has the blocks

N^-1 H_face,N + O(N^-2),
diag_i[2(C_i(r_N)-pi i epsilon_i)]+o(1).             (7)

Mixed theta/delta blocks vanish exactly at delta=0. The real quadratic part on real integration directions is uniformly positive. All fixed holomorphic derivatives of Psi_N are bounded independently of N.

The analytic inverse-function argument now gives a unique nearby joint critical point z_N(chi) on a fixed small complex polydisc. One direct uniform construction is Newton iteration after multiplying by the inverse Hessian at chi=0: its norm is bounded, and the bounded third derivatives give a common contraction ball. Define

S_N(chi)=Psi_N(z_N(chi);chi),
K_N(chi)=-d_z^2 Psi_N(z_N(chi);chi).                (8)

The Hessian stays nonsingular and its real quadratic part stays positive along the translated real contour. S_N(chi)-f_N is uniformly small when chi is small. The neighborhood can therefore be chosen so that the preceding fixed complement gaps remain gaps relative to the selected critical value, not just relative to f_N.

5. Finite lattice to contour integral, with exponential errors

Use (4) only on the retained physical grid. Its accumulated error in (3) is at most exp[N(f_N-c)] on a smaller fixed chi polydisc: the finite count is polynomial, the model is bounded by the original real scale up to the small complex Fourier cost, and (4) has a fixed exponential rate.

For one channel write its lattice as z=alpha_N+j/N. Enclose its retained nodes in a rectangle whose vertical sides are halfway between the last retained node and the first deleted node, and whose horizontal sides have heights +/-eta. For a holomorphic f the exact residue identity is

(1/N) sum_(retained nodes) f(z)
    =(1/(2i)) integral_(rectangle)
                     cot(pi N(z-alpha_N)) f(z) dz.  (9)

On the upper and lower sides cot approaches -i and +i respectively, with errors O(exp(-2pi eta N)). On the vertical sides it is bounded because their real coordinates are lattice midpoints. The constant upper/lower terms deform to the real interval integral. The vertical closing segments are exponentially small by the original quadratic channel gap and its analytic continuation.

Here are the estimates needed for that last assertion and for the horizontal errors. On the small box, the real Hessian bound from (7) and the bounded derivatives imply, for real theta and real channel coordinates x with imaginary shifts y,

Re Psi_N(theta,x+i y;chi)
   <= f_N+C/N^2-c(|theta|^2+|x|^2)
                  +C|y||x|+C|y|^2+C|chi|(|x|+|y|).

After completing squares this is at most f_N+C/N2+C’(|y|2+|chi|^2). At a vertical edge |x_i| near delta0, retain half its negative quadratic term before completing squares. Choose eta sufficiently small compared with delta0. Then choose chi small compared with eta and delta0, so both the vertical edge loss and the horizontal cotangent loss 2pi eta dominate the possible phase growth. This estimate includes the cross term from the half-twist quadratic; a pointwise claim of only O(eta^2) growth would omit it.

Apply (9) successively in all R channel variables. For the resulting mixed contours, |y|^2<=R eta^2. R is fixed, so reserve constants such that C’R eta^2<pi eta, then shrink chi. Every term containing at least one horizontal cotangent error or vertical segment retains a fixed exponential loss. Thus, uniformly on the local real theta box,

sum_(local grid) exp(N Psi_N)
    =N^R integral_(local real delta box) exp(N Psi_N) ddelta
                          +O(exp[N(f_N-c)]).        (10)

Changing N parity changes alpha_N only. There is no periodic extension past the original physical endpoints and no removal of a source factor.

6. Uniform saddle formula and limiting action

The retained integral now has m=n+R=2R-1 real variables. Move the local box through z_N(chi) inside its holomorphic neighborhood. Its side faces keep the same fixed gap after decreasing rho. On the translated real box the quadratic part has positive real part. Expand at z_N(chi), scale by N^-1/2, and extend the Gaussian integral with an exponentially small error. The odd cubic term integrates to zero; bounded fourth and higher derivatives give relative O(N^-1). This proves

F_N(N chi)=chi_(D,N) N^(2R) product_i omega_M^epsilon_i
   *(2pi)^(-n)(2pi/N)^(m/2)
   *exp[N S_N(chi)]/sqrt(det K_N(chi)) [1+O(N^-1)]. (11)

The Gaussian square root is selected by the real positive quadratic part and continued from chi=0. It cannot vanish on the chosen polydisc. All omitted terms remain exponentially small relative to (11), because |Re(S_N(chi)-f_N)| was reserved below their gaps. Taking the ratio at zero gives (1) with every original normalization factor canceled literally. Uniformity and nonvanishing also permit differentiation of its holomorphic relative error on smaller polydiscs.

For completeness, psi_N converges holomorphically on a smaller face neighborhood. Its real-radius limit is the original determinant and sine-product rate. Uniform bounds for its marked logarithm follow from positive-summand preconditioning and that rate. Cauchy estimates make the family locally compact; any subsequential holomorphic limit has the prescribed values on the real open cube, hence is unique by successive one-variable identity theorems. This proves convergence without exchanging an uncontrolled large determinant with a derivative. The same conclusion for Phi_i,N follows directly from its construction. The uniform critical-point contraction then gives S_N -> S and K_N -> K holomorphically.

At chi=0, the actual facet-coning calculation in the coefficient theorem fixes the additive constant f_N -> Vol(Y_D^epsilon)/(2pi). The anchor and critical displacement tend to zero. Equation(11) has only polynomial outside factors and framing of unit modulus. These facts prove (2), including the actual complete hyperbolic volume.

7. What this proves on the general long-twist path

The theorem supplies a fixed complex external-color neighborhood for the original complete core; the earlier h=O(sqrt N) packet did not supply this. It proves a selected nonzero analytic period, with its complete action fixed by actual geometry, for every fixed diagram in the stated certificate class.

The coupled polynomial system also identifies the ordered local momenta on regular character charts, including the face momenta and peripheral equation. At the two marked zero faces those charts can degenerate. For example x>0,b=y=0 gives E=x and D=x^2 for all channel deformation: the quadratic root is double and no longer records the chosen flag. A separate developing map or finite common-conjugated boundary limit is still needed to identify the whole S germ with the actual marked geometric regulator. The complete volume and cusp-Hessian matches alone do not prove that.

The full color reflection has additional terms with one or more ordinary colors. The core also has an opposite-end alias that cancels only after those terms are restored. Consequently (1) cannot be substituted for a whole-source filling estimate. General proper-subset and combined far-alias bounds remain required for all independently long fixed twists. The twist threshold in that target depends on the fixed diagram; the twist numbers are fixed before N tends to infinity.

Verification scope

The argument uses the coefficient, signed-source, exact-reflection and exponential-interpolation theorems, with the original normalization and both parity anchors.

The focused audit and saved results check 10 literal original W/pi sources with off-grid external clocks, 24 shared K4 complex cores and exact face coefficients, 768 retained reflection entries, 20 cotangent basis identities and two full two-register contour identities, and 36 original fixed-window matrix gap samples. Finite complex values and nonzero samples are diagnostics. The written proof supplies the uniform estimates; these checks do not settle the remaining marked-geometric or mixed-source steps.
