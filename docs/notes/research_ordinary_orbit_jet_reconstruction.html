Ordinary-orbit quantum jets reconstruct the complete source

Private written proof, 17 November 2026. Fix the original reduced, zero-framed knot source. Its complete value at level N is an explicit linear combination of quantum-normal jets of genuine finite colors in the noncomplete residue classes modulo N. The maximum normal order depends only on a fixed braid presentation, and the sum of the absolute coefficient weights is polynomial in N.

The construction uses an omitted-node Hermite residue row and the actual integral-color restriction of the same all-root Verma source. No complete-color value or normal is supplied on the right side. The resulting row is complex and signed. It gives neither a lower bound for that row nor a nonzero geometric coefficient.

1. Source, normal paths, and a uniform Laurent width

Fix a b-strand braid beta of length ell and writhe w, with one closed component and zero-framing normalization. Put c=b−1−w∈2ℤ,D=N−1,q0=eπi/N,Q0=q02,N≥2.(1) c=b-1-w\in2\mathbb Z,\quad D=N-1,\quad q_0=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q_0=q_0^2,\qquad N\ge2. \qquad\text{(1)} Let F_N(M) be its actual reduced ADO source in [V]. By [C, (21)] and the root comparison it is even in M. Write FN(M)=fN(M2),Y=M2,KN=fN(1).(2) F_N(M)=f_N(M^2),\quad Y=M^2,\qquad K_N=f_N(1). \qquad\text{(2)} The symmetric Alexander polynomial is denoted by Delta, with Delta(1)=1. All integer colors C_L(q) below are the original reduced, zero-framed colors, with unknot value one.

Here is a support bound from the literal finite source. The two canceled checked entries of [V, (1.1)–(1.2)] are Rv+n,u−n;u,v+=x−u−v+nQ(u−n)v[un]Q(x−2Qu−n;Q)n,Rv−n,u+n;u,v−=xu+v−nQ−uv[vn]Q(x2Q1−v;Q)n.(3) \begin{split} R^+_{v+n,u-n;\,u,v} &=x^{-u-v+n}Q^{(u-n)v}{u\brack n}_Q (x^{-2}Q^{u-n};Q)_n,\\ R^-_{v-n,u+n;\,u,v} &=x^{u+v-n}Q^{-uv}{v\brack n}_Q (x^2Q^{1-v};Q)_n . \end{split} \qquad\text{(3)} At the finite root head all input and output indices are between 0 and D. In the positive entry n<=u, and its x powers are −u−v+n−2h-u-v+n-2h, 0<=h<=n, lying in [-3D,0]. In the negative entry n<=v, and its powers u+v−n+2hu+v-n+2h lie in [0,3D]. The Gaussian coefficients and root chirps have no x dependence.

The finite head numerator P in [V, Section 3] retains the scalar x^c. Its exact ordinary-pivot to ADO-pivot comparison is PN,β(x;1)=xNcFN(−q0x).(4) P_{N,\beta}(x;1)=x^{Nc}F_N(-q_0x). \qquad\text{(4)} Consequently F_N(-q0 x), rather than P itself, retains the overall power x−(N−1)cx^{-(N-1)c}. Every exponent of this actual reduced source therefore has absolute value at most (3ℓ+|c|)(N−1)(3\ell+|c|)(N-1). Evenness and the marked substitution x=−q0−1Mx=-q_0^{-1}M give supp⁡fN⊂[−AN,AN],A=max⁡(1,⌈3ℓ+|c|2⌉).(5) \operatorname{supp} f_N\subset[-AN,AN],\qquad A=\max\!\left(1,\left\lceil\frac{3\ell+|c|}{2}\right\rceil\right). \qquad\text{(5)} The support interval is deliberately generous and valid at every N>=2. No degree estimate for a different knot invariant is used.

The full ADO normalization, if required, is still exactly 𝒩N(M)=iN+3MN−1SN(M2)fN(M2),SN(Y)=1+Y+⋯+YN−1.(6) \mathcal N_N(M) =i^{N+3}\frac{M^{N-1}}{S_N(M^2)}f_N(M^2),\qquad S_N(Y)=1+Y+\cdots+Y^{N-1}. \qquad\text{(6)} Our target is the reduced complete value (2). Neither the phase, the monomial in (6), nor the distinction between the full and reduced sources is absorbed into an unspecified scalar.

2. The omitted-complete-node residue row

Fix once and for all H=4A+1.(7) H=4A+1. \qquad\text{(7)} Then H(N−1)>2ANH(N-1)>2AN for every N>=2. Define ωN(Y)=YANfN(Y)(Y−1)SN(Y)HdY=YAN(Y−1)H−1fN(Y)(YN−1)HdY.(8) \omega_N(Y)= \frac{Y^{AN}f_N(Y)}{(Y-1)S_N(Y)^H}\,dY =\frac{Y^{AN}(Y-1)^{H-1}f_N(Y)} {(Y^N-1)^H}\,dY. \qquad\text{(8)} The factor Y^{AN} clears all Laurent powers. Its numerator in the first expression has degree at most 2AN, whereas the denominator has degree 1+H(N−1)≥2AN+21+H(N-1)\ge2AN+2. Thus (8) has no pole at zero and has zero residue at infinity. Its residue at Y=1 is K_N/N^H. Every other pole lies at ζL=Q0L\zeta_L=Q_0^L, 1≤L<N1\le L<N, and has order at most H. The residue theorem proves the exact source identity KN=−NH∑L=1N−1Res⁡Y=ζLωN.(9) K_N=-N^H\sum_{L=1}^{N-1} \operatorname{Res}_{Y=\zeta_L}\omega_N. \qquad\text{(9)} Only the ordinary nodes occur on the right side.

We make every coefficient in this Hermite row explicit. Set ℰN=1NY∂Y,ΨA,H,N(t;ζ)=eAtζet/N(ζet/N−1)H−1(tet−1)H,bh,N(ζ)=[th]ΨA,H,N(t;ζ).(10) \mathscr E_N=\frac1N Y\partial_Y,\qquad \Psi_{A,H,N}(t;\zeta)= e^{At}\zeta e^{t/N}(\zeta e^{t/N}-1)^{H-1} \left(\frac{t}{e^t-1}\right)^H,\qquad b_{h,N}(\zeta)=[t^h]\Psi_{A,H,N}(t;\zeta). \qquad\text{(10)} The removable value at t=0 is retained. In the local coordinate Y=ζet/NY=\zeta e^{t/N} one has ζAN=1\zeta^{AN}=1, YN=etY^N=e^t, and ωN=1Nt−HΨA,H,N(t;ζ)fN(ζet/N)dt. \omega_N=\frac1N t^{-H}\Psi_{A,H,N}(t;\zeta) f_N(\zeta e^{t/N})\,dt . Expanding the last factor by its actual Euler derivatives gives KN=−NH−1∑L=1N−1∑a=0H−1bH−1−a,N(ζL)a!(ℰNafN)(ζL).(11) K_N=-N^{H-1}\sum_{L=1}^{N-1}\sum_{a=0}^{H-1} \frac{b_{H-1-a,N}(\zeta_L)}{a!} (\mathscr E_N^a f_N)(\zeta_L). \qquad\text{(11)} This formula includes all normals through H-1.

There is a uniform coefficient bound with no root-spacing loss. For |t|=1, |et−1t−1|≤∑j=2∞1j!=e−2<1. \left|\frac{e^t-1}{t}-1\right| \le\sum_{j=2}^\infty\frac1{j!}=e-2<1. The last strict inequality follows, for example, by bounding the factorial tail geometrically. Cauchy’s coefficient estimate gives |bh,N(ζ)|≤BA,H:=eA+1/2(1+e1/2)H−1(3−e)−H,|ζ|=1,N≥2,h≥0.(12) |b_{h,N}(\zeta)|\le B_{A,H}:= e^{A+1/2}(1+e^{1/2})^{H-1}(3-e)^{-H}, \quad |\zeta|=1,\quad N\ge2,\quad h\ge0. \qquad\text{(12)} Thus the total absolute row weight in (11) is at most eBA,HNHeB_{A,H}N^H. In ordinary derivative coordinates, the coefficient of fN(r)(ζL)f_N^{(r)}(\zeta_L) is OA,H(NH−1−r)O_{A,H}(N^{H-1-r}). Indeed induction gives (Y∂Y)a=∑r=0asa,rYr∂Yr(Y\partial_Y)^a=\sum_{r=0}^a s_{a,r}Y^r\partial_Y^r with fixed integer coefficients; only a>=r contributes and each term in (11) contains N^{-a}. This also shows directly why nearby ordinary nodes cause no inverse-distance blowup in this row.

3. The exact high-color quantum-normal adapter

Use the fixed root-normal coordinate δ=1−q2N,q(δ)=q0(1−δ)1/(2N).(13) \delta=1-q^{2N},\qquad q(\delta)=q_0(1-\delta)^{1/(2N)}. \qquad\text{(13)} The formal fixed-Y completion proved in [C, (25a)–(26)] satisfies 𝒲N(δ,Y)=∑j≥0δjWj,N(Y),W0,N(Y)=gN(Y):=fN(Y)Δ(YN).(14) \mathcal W_N(\delta,Y)=\sum_{j\ge0}\delta^j W_{j,N}(Y), \qquad W_{0,N}(Y)=g_N(Y):=\frac{f_N(Y)}{\Delta(Y^N)}. \qquad\text{(14)} Its coefficients are rational in Y, with poles only at zero, infinity, and the Alexander divisor. They are regular at every ζL\zeta_L. This statement concerns the specified formal completion, not an assumed convergent quantum series.

For 1≤L<N1\le L<N, h>=0 and r>=0 put Jh,L,N(r)=[δh]CL+rN(q(δ)).(15) J_{h,L,N}(r)=[\delta^h]C_{L+rN}(q(\delta)). \qquad\text{(15)} The ANY-positive-color quotient in [C, Section 6] retains every intermediate index and the ordinary pivot before root restriction. Together with the natural meridian Y=q2x2Y=q^2x^2, it gives exactly CL+rN(q(δ))=𝒲N(δ,ζL(1−δ)r+L/N).(16) C_{L+rN}(q(\delta)) =\mathcal W_N\!\left( \delta,\zeta_L(1-\delta)^{r+L/N}\right). \qquad\text{(16)} The equality is one of formal delta germs. Its evaluation of the auxiliary grading at one is legitimate: at the corresponding root x=q0^{L-1} up to sign, the deleted classical compression is nilpotent, so its determinant is identically one in the grading, as proved in [C, (23)]. There is no independently frozen root pivot or identification of fixed-Y normals with integral-color normals.

Let E=Y∂YE=Y\partial_Y and η=log⁡(1−δ)\eta=\log(1-\delta). Expanding the right side of (16) yields ∑a,d≥0δa(r+L/N)dηdd!(EdWa,N)(ζL).(17) \sum_{a,d\ge0}\delta^a \frac{(r+L/N)^d\eta^d}{d!} (E^dW_{a,N})(\zeta_L). \qquad\text{(17)} At order h only a+d<=h occurs. Hence J_{h,L,N}(r) is a polynomial in r of degree at most h, for every r>=0. Its leading coefficient is (−1)h(EhgN)(ζL)/h!(-1)^h(E^h g_N)(\zeta_L)/h!. The elementary h-th finite difference therefore gives Bh,L,N:=∑r=0h(−1)r(hr)Jh,L,N(r)=(EhgN)(ζL).(18) B_{h,L,N}:=\sum_{r=0}^h(-1)^r\binom hr J_{h,L,N}(r) =(E^h g_N)(\zeta_L). \qquad\text{(18)} For h=0 this is the original ordinary-color value. The sign in (18) includes the leading coefficient of log(1-delta); no parity character remains in the squared-meridian coordinate.

Retain the entire Alexander factor by defining αs=(Z∂Z)sΔ(Z)|Z=1,α0=1. \alpha_s=\left.(Z\partial_Z)^s\Delta(Z)\right|_{Z=1}, \qquad \alpha_0=1. Since ζLN=1\zeta_L^N=1, the ordinary product rule gives exactly (ℰNafN)(ζL)=∑h=0a(ah)N−hαa−hBh,L,N.(19) (\mathscr E_N^a f_N)(\zeta_L) =\sum_{h=0}^a\binom ah N^{-h}\alpha_{a-h}B_{h,L,N}. \qquad\text{(19)} In particular neither the denominator in (14) nor its higher normal derivatives have been replaced just by Delta(1).

4. Explicit reconstruction from genuine finite-color jets

Combining (11), (18), and (19) proves KN=∑L=1N−1∑h=0H−1∑r=0hwL,h,r,NJh,L,N(r),(20) K_N=\sum_{L=1}^{N-1}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\sum_{r=0}^h w_{L,h,r,N}J_{h,L,N}(r), \qquad\text{(20)} where every weight is specified by wL,h,r,N=−(−1)r(hr)NH−1−h∑a=hH−1bH−1−a,N(ζL)a!(ah)αa−h.(21) w_{L,h,r,N} =-(-1)^r\binom hr N^{H-1-h} \sum_{a=h}^{H-1} \frac{b_{H-1-a,N}(\zeta_L)}{a!} \binom ah\alpha_{a-h}. \qquad\text{(21)} The source quantities on the right use only the colors 1≤L<N,0≤r≤h≤H−1,L+rN≤HN−1.(22) 1\le L<N,\qquad 0\le r\le h\le H-1,\qquad L+rN\le HN-1. \qquad\text{(22)} No color is divisible by N. In particular no complete-color value or quantum normal is input to this reconstruction. For r>=1, these are genuine high colors in ordinary residue classes; they are not the bounded ordinary colors strictly below N.

All row weights are polynomially controlled. Put AΔ,H=∑s=0H−1|αs|s!. A_{\Delta,H}=\sum_{s=0}^{H-1}\frac{|\alpha_s|}{s!}. Using (ah)/a!=1/(h!(a−h)!)\binom ah/a!=1/(h!(a-h)!), (12) gives |wL,h,r,N|≤BA,HAΔ,H(hr)h!NH−1−h,(23) |w_{L,h,r,N}| \le B_{A,H}A_{\Delta,H}\frac{\binom hr}{h!} N^{H-1-h}, \qquad\text{(23)} and consequently ∑L=1N−1∑r=0h|wL,h,r,N|≤BA,HAΔ,H2hh!NH−h,∑L,h,r|wL,h,r,N|≤e2BA,HAΔ,HNH.(24) \begin{split} \sum_{L=1}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^h |w_{L,h,r,N}| &\le B_{A,H}A_{\Delta,H}\frac{2^h}{h!}N^{H-h},\\ \sum_{L,h,r}|w_{L,h,r,N}| &\le e^2B_{A,H}A_{\Delta,H}N^H. \qquad\text{(24)} \end{split} The maximum derivative order H-1 is fixed with the presentation. The coefficient bounds hold at every N>=2 and for all its ordinary residue classes, without primality assumptions.

Equations (20)–(24) are an exact original-source reconstruction with an explicit polynomially bounded row. They do not claim a polynomially conditioned inverse for all the supplied data, which contain redundancy, or a positive representation of the scalar.

5. What the existing positive norm does and does not provide

For comparison, [P, Sections 1–3] already proves fN(ζL)=CL(q0),|CL(q0)|≤DN:=sin⁡(π/N)1−b≤(N/2)b−1,1≤L<N.(25) f_N(\zeta_L)=C_L(q_0),\qquad |C_L(q_0)|\le D_N:=\sin(\pi/N)^{1-b} \le(N/2)^{b-1},\quad 1\le L<N. \qquad\text{(25)} Its exact positive cyclic norm is ℋN=1N∑YN=1|fN(Y)|2=|KN|2N+1N∑L=1N−1|CL(q0)|2.(26) \mathcal H_N=\frac1N\sum_{Y^N=1}|f_N(Y)|^2 =\frac{|K_N|^2}{N} +\frac1N\sum_{L=1}^{N-1}|C_L(q_0)|^2. \qquad\text{(26)} Thus a positive exponential estimate for that specified norm would control K_N, but such an estimate is not supplied by positivity.

The h=0 portion of (20) has a polynomial bound by (24)–(25). Higher h involve the actual normal data (15). The ordinary-color positive trace bounds do not bound these transverse quantum jets or the high colors with r>=1. In particular (20) alone cannot turn those positive bounds into a lower bound for K_N: its weights are signed and complex, and the identities retain cancellation. The all-root coefficient upper estimates are also not a lower bound for this selected row. No analytic summation theorem is used in (16)–(20), because fixed-order restrictions are exact formal identities of the genuine finite sources.

6. Coverage, scope, and complete proof sources

[P] already contains ordinary-node interpolation with S_N to the first power, the regular quotient, actual ordinary-color bounds, and the positive Fourier norm (26). Full-orbit Fourier/Hermite sampling and bounded meridian jets have also been studied in the existing notes. The present calculation specifically omits the complete node, chooses a fixed higher S_N power from the literal Laurent width, and replaces every required meridian normal by the finite differences of actual quantum-normal data in (18).

Derivative recovery of nonsemisimple knot invariants and recovery of ADO data from the colored-Jones family have literature coverage: Murakami, arXiv:1406.1287 states a derivative relation for logarithmic invariants, and Willetts, arXiv:2003.09854 states a unification determining root ADO data from the colored family. These are statement-level coverage only, not proof premises. No broad novelty is asserted for derivative recovery or Hermite interpolation.
