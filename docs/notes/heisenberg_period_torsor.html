A Heisenberg torsor and a uniform finite period

Research note, 25 October 2026. The purpose is structural: replace matrix-chart restrictions and individual knot calculations by an intrinsic algebraic object. Definitions of the local cyclic quantum dilogarithm already exist. The results below reconstruct its normalization as a Heisenberg torsor, give an exact finite face reduction, and identify the boundary module that any global construction must preserve.

This is not a proof of the full volume conjecture. The missing assertion is identified in Section 7. In particular a splitting module, a selected period vector, and a numerical growth law are different data.

The comparison with existing literature is in structural_literature_delta.md. No novelty is claimed for Hilbert 90, finite Heisenberg representations, cyclic dilogarithms, or Heisenberg covers of Fermat curves.

1. The norm equation identifies the first cover

Fix an odd integer n>=3, a field k of characteristic zero containing a primitive n-th root omega, and t on U=P^1 minus {0,1,infinity}. Let x^n=t. The elementary identity

Norm_(k(x)/k(t))(1-x)=product_j(1-omega^j x)=1-t       (1.1)

is the Steinberg norm relation. It gives a direct splitting of the cyclic algebra

A_t=k(t)<X,Y>/(X^n=t, Y^n=1-t, XY=omega YX).

Indeed on E=k(t)[x]/(x^n-t), let X be multiplication by x and let

Y f(x)=(1-x)f(omega^(-1)x).

These operators satisfy all three relations. After adjoining x they are a diagonal operator with distinct eigenvalues and a cyclic shift with nonzero coefficients, and generate the full matrix algebra. Consequently A_t is already End_(k(t))(E). This proves the splitting; it does not assume a norm-residue or Brauer-group theorem.

Now choose c^n=1-t. The ratio

r(x)=(1-x)/c

has cyclic norm one. The equation

d(omega x)=r(x)d(x)                                 (1.2)

is exactly a Hilbert 90 equation over k(t,c). Its explicit solution on the cyclic spectrum, with its initial value left free, is

w_0=1,
w_j=c^(-j) product_(a=0)^(j-1)(1-omega^a x),
d_j=s w_j, 0<=j<n.

Equation (1.1) closes the recurrence at j=n. Conversely the recurrence determines all d_j from s. Thus the solution set is a G_m-torsor before normalization. This is an elementary proof of the Hilbert 90 instance needed here, rather than an invocation without a specified cocycle.

One can also solve it in the cyclic function field itself. Write sigma(x)=omega*x, sigma(c)=c, and for a in k(t,c)(x) set

H(a)=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) w_j^(-1) sigma^j(a).

The recurrence and w_n=1 give sigma H(a)=r H(a). At least one H(x^k), 0<=k<n, is nonzero: after division by x^k these expressions are the finite Fourier transform of the nonzero vector (w_j^(-1)). The Fourier matrix is invertible. This proves the required field descent constructively as well. Determinant normalization still requires its separately recorded n-th root.

For the user’s generic peripheral factor, put t=-M^n. Since n is odd,

product_j(1+omega^j M)=1+M^n.                        (1.3)

The factor is a cyclic norm, not a disposable numerical multiplier. An unnormalized recurrence changes its n-step central character by this factor. At M=1 it equals 2. This identifies an exact mechanism for such factors; it does not identify the multiplier of the earlier positive factorial model from its one factor-eight test.

2. Determinant normalization gives a Heisenberg cover

Set Delta=product_(j=0)^(n-1) w_j. The determinant-one normalization of the diagonal intertwiner D=diag(d_0,…,d_(n-1)) is

s^n=Delta^(-1).

Because n is odd, the expression simplifies to

s^n=product_(a=0)^(n-1)
                   (1-omega^a x)^(a-(n-1)/2).       (2.1)

Thus all coefficients live on the finite etale cover of U

T_n: x^n=t, c^n=1-t, s^n=Delta^(-1).                (2.2)

Its degree is n^3 as a finite locally free cover. Every factor is a unit over U, so these are actual torsors there, rather than equations silently evaluated at a branch point.

There are three deck transformations, written as maps of points:

A:(x,c,s) -> (omega x,c,s(1-x)/c),
B:(x,c,s) -> (x,omega c,s),
C:(x,c,s) -> (x,c,omega s).

The norm identity and the determinant product show that they preserve (2.2). Direct substitution proves

A^n=B^n=C^n=1, C central, AB=C^(-1)BA.              (2.3)

They give a free transitive action on every geometric fibre: A and B choose the first two roots, and C chooses the last. Therefore T_n is a torsor for the finite Heisenberg group H_n, not just an unspecified scalar normalization line. The extra central root records the failure of the first two root changes to commute on the normalized intertwiner.

For comparison with the standard cyclic dilogarithm convention, let q=omega^((n+1)/2), so q^2=omega and q^n=1, and set

p^-=1/c, p^+=-x/(q c).

Then (p-)n+(p+)n=1 and

p^-+q^(2j+1)p^+=(1-omega^j x)/c.

Thus d_j is exactly the spectral cyclic-dilogarithm definition, with its determinant root retained. This is a reparameterization, not a new local quantum function. The normalization cover, viewed over t, has the explicit Heisenberg structure (2.3).

If U0 e_j=omega^j e_j and P0 e_j=e_(j+1), then

D U0 D^(-1)=U0,
D P0 D^(-1)=P0(1-x U0)/c.                           (2.4)

The commutant of U0,P0 consists of scalars: commuting with U0 forces a matrix to be diagonal, and commuting with P0 makes its diagonal constant. Consequently this intertwiner is unique up to scalar. Determinant one reduces the ambiguity to mu_n. A mu_n-valued algebraic function on an irreducible parameter component is constant, since its n-th power is one. This is the precise rigidity statement available for comparing two intertwiners of the same automorphism.

It does not prove that two arbitrary products implement that same automorphism, nor that every projective scalar cocycle is trivial. Those are separate coherence statements.

3. The face contraction is an exact finite Fourier evaluation

Let X be the ideal compactification of a globally branched knot exterior. Its single ideal vertex and H_1(X;Z)=0 are the topological inputs. Let r be the number of tetrahedra, and use the existing face selectors

D_f=X_0-X_1+X_2, R_f=X_2-X_3, Epsilon=diag(epsilon).

Work over R=Z/nZ. The following argument also works for even n; oddness is needed for the Weyl normalization in Section 5. Define the finite groups and maps

V=ker D_f,
T(v)=Epsilon R_f v, U(v)=X_0 v,
K=ker(T:V->R^r), H=T(V),
W={w in H : (U k).w=0 in R for every k in K}.        (3.1)

For w in W choose any v with Tv=w and put

q_W(w)=-(Uv).w in R.                               (3.2)

This is independent of the lift: changing v by k changes it by -(Uk).w, which vanishes by definition. Its polarization is bilinear, so q_W is a quadratic function on the finite abelian group W.

Use self-dual Haar measure n^(-1/2) times counting measure on R. Its delta function at zero has value sqrt(n). For an arbitrary function F:R^r->C define the charged-kernel-style contraction

Z_n(F)=n^(-r/2) sum_(v in V)
                     omega^(-(Uv).Tv) F(Tv).        (3.3)

All signs and local spectral factors can be included in F. This is the full face measure and all r independent delta factors, not a bare unnormalized sum.

Theorem. For every such knot triangulation, including singular face matrices and every composite n,

|V|=n^r, |W|=n^r/|K|^2,
Z_n(F)=|W|^(-1/2) sum_(w in W) omega^(q_W(w))F(w).   (3.4)

There is no limiting operation or choice of inverse face matrix.

Proof of the topological input over R. The local cocycle lemma of automatic_face_regularity.md uses only addition and sign changes, so it holds over R. The universal coefficient theorem gives H^1(X;R)=Hom(H_1(X;Z),R)=0; with one ideal vertex its edge cocycles themselves vanish. Thus D_f^T has trivial kernel over R. Perfect finite character duality implies D_f is surjective, and |V|=n^r.

For k in K, define a character of V/K by

kappa(k)([v])=omega^(-(Uk).Tv).

This is well-defined. If it is trivial, R_f^T Epsilon Uk annihilates ker D_f and hence belongs to im D_f^T. Choose a with D_f^T a+R_f^T Epsilon Uk=0. Locally write b=X_0k,c=X_2k. The equations D_fk=R_fk=0 give face values (b,b+c,c,c), and the last equation gives the paired position values (a,-a,a+epsilonb,-epsilonb). They are exactly the same local ideal edge cocycle. It vanishes, so k=0. Therefore kappa is injective.

Dualizing this injection gives a surjection V/K -> K^vee. Its kernel is identified by T with W. Hence |W|=|V|/|K|^2.

Finally sum (3.3) over each coset v+K. Its coefficient is

omega^(-(Uv).Tv) sum_(k in K) omega^(-(Uk).Tv).

Character orthogonality makes this zero outside W and |K| times the displayed phase on W. Since n(-r/2)|K|=|W|(-1/2), (3.4) follows, including its exact measure. QED.

This formulation survives primes dividing the integer face torsion. W need not be a free R-module; its cardinality and quadratic function are the invariant data. A rational nullspace dimension cannot replace these data at such a prime.

4. The period is algebraic, with its normalization specified

When F is a product of local cyclic-dilogarithm entries, its coefficients are algebraic functions on products of the torsors T_n. Formula (3.4) is then a finite algebraic matrix coefficient on that parameter space. Specializing an algebraic decorated holonomy gives an algebraic number, with every determinant root and Fourier factor retained.

This is an exact finite period in the representation-theoretic sense. It does not yet identify it with the continuous Faddeev state. The local bridge and the additional dilogarithm period are given in rational_quantum_dilogarithm_torsor.md.

5. Edge reduction gives one irreducible boundary module

Here is the invariant version of the face/edge linear algebra. Let Lambda=R^(2r) with its standard alternating form. The r-1 independent edge rows span a primitive isotropic submodule E_ed. Their primitivity for odd n has a uniform proof. A dependence among all r edge rows says that in each tetrahedron the three opposite-pair sums of edge values are equal. Since 2 is invertible, such values are uniquely lambda_ij=a_i+a_j. A face’s three edge values recover its three a_i, so the a_i agree across glued faces. There is one ideal vertex; the dependence is therefore the constant edge assignment. Deleting one row removes it. The same argument modulo every prime divisor of n gives a unit minor locally, hence a split injection over R.

Isotropy is the local cusp-intersection cancellation already proved in preferred_longitude_cochains.md. The full meridian/longitude pairing is -2, a unit in R. Therefore

E_ed^perp/E_ed is a free symplectic R-module of rank two, (5.1)

with its generators supplied by the marked peripheral curves. It does not depend on the projection rank of a face matrix.

In the r-fold clock/shift module of dimension n^r use Weyl operators

W(a,b)=omega^(-a.b/2) X^a Y^b,
W(v)W(w)=omega^(Omega(v,w)/2)W(v+w).

For an edge character chi define the exact projector

Pi_chi=|E_ed|^(-1) sum_(e in E_ed) chi(e)^(-1)W(e).

All nonidentity Weyl monomials have trace zero, so

rank Pi_chi=n^r/|E_ed|=n.                           (5.2)

The boundary Weyl operators act on H_chi=im Pi_chi. For a class v in E_ed^perp/E_ed, their trace on H_chi is zero unless v=0. Their n^2 matrices are therefore trace-orthogonal and span End(H_chi). This proves irreducibility and scalar commutant directly.

More generally, start with the split local cyclic algebras A_(t_j) from Section 1 over the gluing function field. The classical gluing equations make the commuting edge operators have n-th powers one. Their projector gives the same rank-n boundary module, now with the actual peripheral central characters. After a central-root extension the trace argument above applies, and descends the dimension and irreducibility assertions. Thus the interior gives a splitting of the boundary cyclic algebra on its gluing field.

This is a uniform quantum Hamiltonian reduction. Singular face projections are alternate presentations of the same reduction, rather than additional species of quantum states.

5.1 Reduction of the torsor itself

On a nondegenerate gluing chart pull back one T_n for each classical tetrahedral coordinate t_j. Push out their r central mu_n groups by multiplication, with the recorded orientation convention. The product cover becomes a torsor P for the Heisenberg extension of Lambda. In Weyl coordinates this group has law

(v,z)(w,z')=(v+w,zz'*omega^(Omega(v,w)/2)).

Choosing the inverse central generator matches (2.3); orthogonals and the reduction are unchanged. All fixed signs in edge central characters are incorporated before the next step.

For an edge monomial product_j t_ja_j(1-t_j)b_j=1, its root eta=product_j x_j^a_j c_j^b_j is mu_n-valued. These roots give a moment map P -> E_ed^vee. In root coordinates the edge vector is e=(b,-a); under a deck vector v,

eta_e(p.v)=eta_e(p)*omega^(Omega(e,v)).

For a prescribed character chi its fibre P_chi is a torsor for the preimage H(E_ed^perp). The Weyl lift of E_ed is central in that subgroup by isotropy. Its quotient is a torsor for

H(E_ed^perp)/E_ed = H(E_ed^perp/E_ed).               (5.3)

This is the Heisenberg group of one boundary pair. Indeed on each geometric fibre, primitivity makes Lambda -> E_ed^vee surjective with kernel E_ed^perp. Each moment fibre is nonempty and transitive with that stabilizer; quotienting its free E_ed action proves (5.3). These finite etale constructions descend.

Thus a boundary H_n-torsor is constructed on every gluing chart, without a proper-face hypothesis or an angle limit. Identifying its period section on the intrinsic decorated character space remains the global task; a quotient group alone does not identify the section.

The later pointed tetrahedron gluing theorem extends the edge-lattice step to all levels for S^3 link exteriors. Its integral argument includes the missing prime 2 and proves primitivity without a global branching. With the full Weyl phases retained, the saturated boundary reduction has dimension N^c at every N for c cusps. The marked unhalved peripheral rows are a separate issue: their pairing -2 can give an index-two lattice, whose algebra has two sectors at even levels. The earlier odd-n identification with the full marked boundary algebra is therefore not extended by silently inverting 2. The subsequent integral half-longitude proof now identifies the saturation for a knot as the marked meridian and half of its preferred longitude. A mod-two dual-face cocycle and nullhomology of the longitude give this integral result before N is chosen. The Weyl lift then has TY=q^2 YT; its square retains the full edge character. Matching the actual central longitude, source and selected period still requires comparison.

6. The torsor that still has to be globalized

For two presentations over an identified decorated-character function field, with matched peripheral lifts, let H_X,H_Y be these irreducible boundary modules. Their module-intertwiner space

I_XY=Hom_(A_boundary)(H_X,H_Y)

is one-dimensional. Its invertible part is a G_m-torsor; a specified identification of determinant lines reduces it to a mu_n-torsor. Hilbert 90 handles descent of a specified multiplicative cocycle at the function-field level. It neither chooses a canonical section nor trivializes an arbitrary mu_n or degree-two obstruction.

The local module has a canonical vacuum, the unit killed by X+Y-1, as proved in pointed_steinberg_recoupling.md. Tensoring these units and applying the edge projector gives an algebraic candidate

J_X=Pi_chi(1 tensor ... tensor 1) in H_X.

It can vanish on a locus; irreducibility of its ambient module has not removed that possibility. A face contraction such as (3.4) is a scalar period. Matching its boundary cut and frames to this projected vector is an additional comparison, not an already proved consequence of the scalar formula.

The relevant object is therefore the pointed splitting module (H_X,[J_X]), together with its normalization line. An unpointed intertwiner exists uniformly; proving that it takes [J_X] to [J_Y] is the actual state-invariance assertion. Calling the unpointed intertwiner line a torsor does not discharge that assertion.

The intended intrinsic object is the pointed boundary splitting module with the reduced Heisenberg/dilogarithm period torsor. Its global definition must identify the gluing charts of decorated flat local systems and verify one universal quantum five-term coherence. A list of individually certified knot triangulations cannot substitute for that statement.

7. What rigidity can prove, and the remaining volume assertion

Once two normalized constructions are sections of the same pointed intertwiner line and implement the same quantum automorphism, their ratio is mu_n-valued and constant on each irreducible lifted parameter component. One normalization fixes their exact equality there; descent must still respect the deck transformations (2.3). This is the useful algebraic rigidity mechanism.

The classical dilogarithm regulator is a value of the corresponding period torsor, rather than an asymptotic definition of hyperbolic volume. The requested conjecture still needs a uniform comparison of the normalized quantum period with that classical regulator. Fixed-level Zariski rigidity alone supplies no comparison between different n: the covers T_n and their period vectors vary with n. The theory should replace repeated saddle computations by this single compatibility theorem; at present that theorem has not been proved.

The next proof obligations are consequently structural:

  1. Construct the pointed period object on the intrinsic decorated character space and prove its universal five-term descent.
  2. Match its normalization and cycle with the existing Faddeev state, using the exact local rational-parameter bridge.
  3. Prove compatibility of its normalized size with the dilogarithm regulator on the complete geometric class, uniformly in the class of knots requested by the user.

These preserve the full objective. The finite reductions and torsor identification above are tools toward it, not a redefinition of success.

The subsequent pointed recoupling, period-descent and frame-transport notes now prove local selected-vector coherence and full projective matrix covariance on the stated root covers. In particular projective_frame_period_torsor.md supplies the operator comparison, determinant normalization and identity anchor needed to apply the rigidity mechanism of this section. Actual vector transport is essential: the unit in an endpoint frame is generally not taken to the new unit. The intrinsic equivariant knot period, its literature cycle and the regulator comparison across levels are still outstanding.
