Quarter-turns with a moving meridian: exact coefficients and spectral bounds

A quarter-turn of a two-strand tangle bends one incoming strand into an outgoing strand and one outgoing strand into an incoming strand. In the quantum calculation this changes the ordered tensor product on which the tangle acts. When the meridian parameter moves away from its complete value, the two possible strand colors are dual but unequal. The turn must therefore retain the evaluation maps and the braiding that identifies the two orders of an opposite-color pair.

This note computes that operation in a fixed polynomial basis. Its answer is a reversal of the complete Newton coefficients, multiplied by an explicit paired sine product. The paired product and its inverse are uniformly bounded before the first ordinary-color resonance. A further argument controls both the values and the first derivatives at the repeated complete Casimir nodes. On a small fixed complex parameter disk, the deformed turn has the same exponential spectral norm as the complete turn of the same polynomial.

The proof uses the actual three-strand intertwiner space, including its repeated channels. The exact compiler and its normalization are described in the moving-meridian Newton compiler; the complete crossing matrix is the one in the canonical Newton source calculation. The calculations needed here are included below. The final section distinguishes these operator estimates from a bound for a whole knot contraction.

7. What the comparison does and does not propagate

The estimates retain the actual strand orientations, the full repeated-channel polynomial, and the normalized braiding carrier. They compare turns of the same canonical input polynomial. They do not replace multiplication in a moving same-color algebra by complete multiplication: the relation is 𝖳N(z)+2cos⁡(2πs)=0, \mathsf T_N(z)+2\cos(2\pi s)=0, and the additional high-index multiplication sectors described in the moving Newton multiplication calculation remain present. A bound for a fixed finite product in a signed polynomial norm is also different from a bound for an entire contraction at its geometric exponential rate.

There is a concrete limitation to what node localization alone can imply. The whole-source growth theorem combines this turn formula with the actual crossing-circle operators and a signed whole-junction comparison.
