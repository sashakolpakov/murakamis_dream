A uniform bound for one cap in its moment norm

A cap is the part of the finite source at a crossing region next to the marked strand. Two adjacent face variables there are prescribed to be zero; the other two carry the original coefficient targets. After all other variables have been extracted, the cap acts as a linear functional on a finite list of coefficients. This note proves that its norm grows at most polynomially with the level, uniformly as its real spectral parameter varies between −2-2 and 22.

The inner product uses a full moment matrix, so it retains the mixed terms between different coefficients. The theorem holds at both level parities. To turn it into a bound by hyperbolic volume, one would also have to bound the norm of the remaining coefficients. That is a separate problem; the last section explains why the present estimate alone does not solve it.

3. A quantitative lower density for the original coupled measure

The exact beta-product construction of the protected moments has the following form. Let T1,T2T_1,T_2 be independent Beta⁡(1,1/2)\operatorname{Beta}(1,1/2) variables. Independently, for k≥1k\ge1 and ℓ=1,2\ell=1,2, take Ak,ℓ∼Beta⁡(kN−1/2,1/2),Bk,ℓ∼Beta⁡(kN+1,1/2),G=∏k≥1∏ℓ=12Bk,ℓAk,ℓ,X=T1T2G. The product is defined by the convergent series of logarithms. The beta integral and Euler’s sine product give 𝔼Xj=hj(0≤j≤d). \mathbb E X^j=h_j\qquad(0\le j\le d). \tag{20} These are precisely the original cap moments. To recall why the infinite product is legitimate, a beta ratio pair has logarithmic mean and variance of order (kN)−2(kN)^{-2}. Its real moments on compact subsets of −1<s<N−1/2-1<s<N-1/2 have logarithms of the same summable order. The log series converges in L2L^2, and a slightly larger positive moment gives uniform integrability at each integer j≤dj\le d. Thus (20) follows from finite products, not from an unproved interchange of an infinite product and an integral. This is the original positive-moment theorem; the estimates below quantify its density.

Here are explicit bounds sufficient for that purpose. 4. The finite-degree tail comparison

We first record an elementary weighted inequality. If qq has degree at most MM and α∈{0,1}\alpha\in\{0,1\}, then ∫01|q(t)|2tαdt≤22(M+1)∫01|q(t)|2tα+1/2dt. \int_0^1 |q(t)|^2t^\alpha\,dt \le2\sqrt2(M+1) \int_0^1 |q(t)|^2t^{\alpha+1/2}\,dt. \tag{32} For α=0\alpha=0, put δ=1/[2(M+1)2]\delta=1/[2(M+1)^2]. Inequality (7) shows that the interval [0,δ][0,\delta] contains at most half the unweighted squared norm. The remaining half lies where t1/2≥δt^{1/2}\ge\sqrt\delta, proving (32).

5. The coupled bound and its precise source implication

Inverting the positive matrices in (36) and applying (18) gives κN(σ)≤22(M+1)e200N2π516(M+1)2≤2π564e200N5<e210N5. \begin{aligned} \kappa_N(\sigma) &\le 2\sqrt2(M+1)e^{200}N^2 \frac{\pi^5}{16}(M+1)^2\\ &\le \frac{\sqrt2\,\pi^5}{64}\,e^{200}N^5 <e^{210}N^5 . \end{aligned} \tag{37} This proves (4) for both parities and every real spectral value, including repeated nodes and the odd simple endpoint. No separation into the two one-factor spectral functionals is used.

To state the actual coefficient consequence, retain the original spectator targets and write the resulting homogeneous polynomial as B(x,c)=∑k=0dBkxkcd−k,aj=(−1)jBd−j(0≤j≤M). B(x,c)=\sum_{k=0}^d B_kx^kc^{d-k},\qquad a_j=(-1)^jB_{d-j}\quad(0\le j\le M). \tag{38} The original protected contraction and its exact coupled energy are [xdcd]HL(x,0,c,0;σ)B(x,c)=U0∑j=0Mgj(σ)aj,ℰN(B)=a*Ha. [x^dc^d]H_L(x,0,c,0;\sigma)B(x,c) =U_0\sum_{j=0}^M g_j(\sigma)a_j,\qquad \mathcal E_N(B)=a^*Ha. \tag{39} Finite-dimensional Cauchy–Schwarz with (4) now proves supσ∈[−2,2]|[xdcd]HL(x,0,c,0;σ)B(x,c)|2≤e210N5U02ℰN(B). \sup_{\sigma\in[-2,2]} \left|[x^dc^d]H_L(x,0,c,0;\sigma)B(x,c)\right|^2 \le e^{210}N^5U_0^2\,\mathcal E_N(B). \tag{40} This is a polynomial comparison with the same interference-retaining energy that occurs at σ=2\sigma=2. All exterior coefficients are retained; they are not replaced by independent absolute local bounds.

The energy can itself exceed a positive endpoint comparison exponentially, as the existing cap-moment obstruction proves. Consequently (40) is not a bound by the drilled action, a strict full-minus-central estimate, or a filling-volume theorem. It does remove a specific spectral obstruction: controlling the actual coupled energy would suffice uniformly over the entire real cap spectrum at polynomial cost. Derivative or first-normal estimates are not asserted here; any passage through the finite Hermite functional must retain its separate hypotheses and factors.

6. Dependencies and finite checks

The original coefficient (2) and its normalization are recorded in the protected sharp-scalar derivation and in the frozen spectral-moment projection note. The positive law (19)–(20) is the existing theorem in An exact positive moment form for the original flat-cap coefficients, not a new assertion of positivity. Its single-factor metric and the noninvariance of the monomial-diagonal subspace are also explained in Actual continuant recurrences and an explicit positive cap tensor metric. The present proof instead uses the sine-Cauchy matrix (6), the self-adjoint recurrence (10), and the finite-degree comparison (36).

The accompanying exact finite checker compares the original unused-row products, the direct matrix polynomial (16), and the symmetry identity (10), at representative levels of both parities. Those checks are checks of finite identities. The density and weighted-integral arguments above, rather than a numerical spectrum scan, prove the uniform theorem.
