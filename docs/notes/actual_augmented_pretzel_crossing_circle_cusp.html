The crossing-circle cusp lattice, including its half-twist marking

29 October 2026. The explicit face-labeled antiprism geometry gives the crossing-circle cusp shape without an additional factor two. Put c=cos⁡(π/s)c=\cos(\pi/s). In the positive diagonal half-twist marking,

τstd,+=2ic1+ic=2c2+2ic1+c2. \boxed{\qquad \tau_{\mathrm{std},+} =\frac{2ic}{1+ic} =\frac{2c^2+2ic}{1+c^2}.\qquad} \tag{1}

The opposite half twist has τstd,−=2ic/(1−ic)\tau_{\mathrm{std},-}=2ic/(1-ic), whose real part is negative and whose imaginary part is the same. At s=3s=3, these are ±2/5+4i/5\pm2/5+4i/5. The untwisted marked shape is 2ic2ic. These statements concern the crossing-circle cusps, not the main component cusp.

The words “positive diagonal” specify the peripheral marking below. The metric face pairing alone does not distinguish the two half twists: they differ by a full twist. Consequently (1) is not, by itself, a proof that an independently defined quantum logarithm is the preferred longitude, or a proof of its sign convention. It gives an explicit two-path calibration for making that comparison.

1. Compute one actual horospherical rectangle

Use the explicit 𝒫s\mathcal P_s in the geometric-volume proof. Set α=π/s\alpha=\pi/s, t=tan⁡(π/4−α/2)t=\tan(\pi/4-\alpha/2), and ρ=t−1/2\rho=t^{-1/2}. Before doubling the antiprism its ideal vertices in the complex boundary are

Zk=ρe2ikα,Uk+=ρ−1e(2k+1)iα. Z_k=\rho e^{2ik\alpha},\qquad U_k^+=\rho^{-1}e^{(2k+1)i\alpha}.

Reflection across the base circle |z|=ρ|z|=\rho gives the other half of the doubled polyhedron: Uk−=ρ3e(2k+1)iαU_k^-=\rho^3e^{(2k+1)i\alpha}. The four neighbors of Z0=ρZ_0=\rho are U−1±,U0±U_{-1}^{\pm},U_0^{\pm}. Send Z0Z_0 to infinity by the holomorphic Mobius map m(z)=1/(z−ρ)m(z)=1/(z-\rho). Their images form a Euclidean rectangle.

For an explicit calculation put A=ρ2=(1+sin⁡α)/cos⁡αA=\rho^2=(1+\sin\alpha)/\cos\alpha. The lower left and lower right corners are

m(U0+)=−ρAsin⁡α(1+A2)−iρsin⁡α(1+A2), m(U_0^+)=-\frac{\rho A}{\sin\alpha(1+A^2)} -i\frac{\rho}{\sin\alpha(1+A^2)}, m(U0−)=1ρsin⁡α(1+A2)−iρsin⁡α(1+A2). m(U_0^-)=\frac{1}{\rho\sin\alpha(1+A^2)} -i\frac{\rho}{\sin\alpha(1+A^2)}.

The other two are their complex conjugates. Hence the width and height are

W=1ρsin⁡α,H=2ρsin⁡α(1+A2),H/W=2A1+A2=cos⁡α=c. W=\frac1{\rho\sin\alpha},\qquad H=\frac{2\rho}{\sin\alpha(1+A^2)},\qquad \boxed{H/W=\frac{2A}{1+A^2}=\cos\alpha=c.} \tag{2}

Since all incident faces are vertical planes after this Mobius map, their intersections with any horosphere about infinity give exactly this rectangle, up to its common Euclidean scale. Its vertical sides are the shaded T0+,T0−T_0^+,T_0^- faces. Its horizontal sides are the white Q0,Q−1Q_0,Q_{-1} faces. Normalize by WW and translate to the rectangle [0,1]×[0,c][0,1]\times[0,c].

There are exactly TWO such rectangles at this cusp, one from each projection polyhedron. There are not four cusp rectangles merely because the whole three-manifold contains four antiprisms: doubling across a base merges the two half rectangles into the one just computed.

2. Develop the exact face pairings

Keep rectangle 0 at x+iyx+iy, 0≤x≤10\leq x\leq1, 0≤y≤c0\leq y\leq c. White gluing by the original vertex labels develops rectangle 1 as

x+iy↦x+i(2c−y). x+iy\longmapsto x+i(2c-y). \tag{3}

It lies in [0,1]×[c,2c][0,1]\times[c,2c]. The two white identifications therefore give the vertical period 2ic2ic.

For no half twist, shaded gluing is within each copy and matches the same outer labels. It identifies its left and right sides at the same local yy, giving period 1. Thus the untwisted lattice is

ℒ0=⟨1,2ic⟩ℤ. \mathcal L_0=\langle1,2ic\rangle_{\mathbb Z}. \tag{4}

For a half twist, the original face pairing switches copies AND exchanges the two outer labels. In local rectangle coordinates it sends (0,y)0↦(1,c−y)1(0,y)_0\mapsto(1,c-y)_1. In the development (3) its translation is 1+ic1+ic. The other shaded pair gives 1−ic1-ic. Consequently the half-twisted lattice is

ℒh=⟨1+ic,1−ic⟩ℤ=⟨2,1+ic⟩ℤ. \boxed{\quad \mathcal L_{\mathrm h} =\langle1+ic,1-ic\rangle_{\mathbb Z} =\langle2,1+ic\rangle_{\mathbb Z}.\quad} \tag{5}

Its area is 2c2c, the sum of the two original rectangle areas.

3. Identify the meridian and preferred longitude paths

The shaded faces are the two sides of the original twice-punctured crossing disk. The cusp boundary of that disk runs ALONG their vertical sides and passes through the two white identifications. It has translation

λ=2ic. \lambda=2ic. \tag{6}

This is the disk-framed longitude: pushing the crossing circle off inside its spanning disk has zero linking with the circle itself. Punctures by the other component do not change that self-linking calculation. The loop traverses each of the two shaded sides once; using 4ic4ic would traverse this longitude twice.

A positively transverse local meridian crosses the disk seam once. In the untwisted marking it has translation 1. In the positive diagonal half-twist marking it follows the corner path (0,0)0↦(1,c)1(0,0)_0\mapsto(1,c)_1 in local labels and has

μ+=1+ic. \mu_+=1+ic. \tag{7}

The opposite half-twist marking uses μ−=1−ic=μ+−λ\mu_-=1-ic=\mu_+-\lambda. Both (μ+,λ)(\mu_+,\lambda) and (μ−,λ)(\mu_-,\lambda) are primitive bases of (5): their oriented parallelogram area is 2c2c. Equations (6)–(7) prove (1). In particular choosing a half of λ\lambda would not define a curve in (5), and doubling λ\lambda would not give a peripheral basis.

The explicit integer-shear calibration is

μ++pλ=1+i(2p+1)c,μ−+pλ=1+i(2p−1)c. \mu_++p\lambda=1+i(2p+1)c,\qquad \mu_-+p\lambda=1+i(2p-1)c. \tag{8}

Thus a claimed filling equation must record which of these two base meridians corresponds to its retained physical half twist. The original source R−(2p+1)R^{-(2p+1)} has a specified braid convention and a specified kernel integer pp. To match it to (8), it is enough to identify the p=0p=0 corner path and the direction of the p↦p+1p\mapsto p+1 full twist; one cannot infer that information from the unmarked ideal triangle isometry alone. Replacing R−1R^{-1} by RR changes the half-twist marking by one longitude even though the complete metric volume is unchanged.

4. Comparison with the complete mixed-phase Hessian

If the original mixed-phase coordinates have z=2log⁡Mz=2\log M, ∂G/∂z=log⁡L\partial G/\partial z=\log L, and ∂2G/∂zi2=τs\partial^2G/\partial z_i^2=\tau_s, then the geometric cusp shape they predict is τstd=2τs\tau_{\mathrm{std}}=2\tau_s, since dlog⁡L/dlog⁡M=2dlog⁡L/dzd\log L/d\log M=2\,d\log L/dz. For the stated formal value

τs=eiu*cos⁡u*,tan⁡u*=sec⁡α, \tau_s=e^{iu_*}\cos u_*,\qquad \tan u_*=\sec\alpha,

one gets

2τs=2ic1+ic, 2\tau_s=\frac{2ic}{1+ic},

which is exactly the positive-diagonal shape (1). This independent geometric calculation excludes the proposed extra factor two. It does not supply the missing quantum-to-peripheral coordinate identification or select the half-twist sign by itself.

There is a concrete source sign to check in making that identification. For the literal auxiliary color j=N−hj=N-h, put H=QhH=Q^h. The centered auxiliary meridian Maux=−qjM_{\mathrm{aux}}=-q^j has 2Log⁡Maux=−Log⁡H2\operatorname{Log} M_{\mathrm{aux}}=-\operatorname{Log} H. Thus if a phase calculation uses z=Log⁡Hz=\operatorname{Log} H and Gz=Log⁡LHG_z=\operatorname{Log} L_H, its ratio with this auxiliary meridian is −2Gzz-2G_{zz}, not +2Gzz+2G_{zz}. The positive sign in the preceding comparison requires, for example, the dual longitude Lgeom=LH−1L_{\mathrm{geom}}=L_H^{-1}. This is a coordinate/peripheral sign that must be proved; it cannot be hidden in the rectangle count.

For coverage only, the statement of Theorem 1.1 in Purcell–Reid–Stewart records that crossing-circle cusps have two rectangles, that the longitude uses two shaded sides, and that a half-twist meridian is a diagonal. No proof from that source is used above.
