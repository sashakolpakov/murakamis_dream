A triangle upper bound for every one-sided relative-energy coefficient

31 October 2026. The proof is local and retains the full spectral numerator and positive endpoint reference at both parities. A shared global energy bound is not proved here.

1. Positive dependence on a single spectral parameter

Write θ=π/N\theta=\pi/N, sj=2sin⁡(jθ)s_j=2\sin(j\theta), and normalize the original tail by

qi,j=Ki,j/∏r=i+1jsr2. q_{i,j}=K_{i,j}/\prod_{r=i+1}^j s_r^2.

It satisfies qi,i=1q_{i,i}=1, qi,i+1=x+yq_{i,i+1}=x+y, and

qi,j=(x+y)qi,j−1+(ηj−1)xyqi,j−2,ηj=λdj,dj=cos⁡θ−cos⁡((2j−1)θ)>0,λ=cos⁡θ+cos⁡(2πδ). q_{i,j}=(x+y)q_{i,j-1}+(\eta_j-1)xyq_{i,j-2}, \quad \eta_j=\frac{\lambda}{d_j}, \quad d_j=\cos\theta-\cos((2j-1)\theta)>0, \quad \lambda=\cos\theta+\cos(2\pi\delta). \tag{1}

The actual start has j≥i+2j\geq i+2 in the last term, so no zero sine denominator occurs. On 0≤δ≤1/2−1/(2N)0\leq\delta\leq1/2-1/(2N), λ≥0\lambda\geq0.

For an interval of length r=j−ir=j-i, expand the continuant in the parameters η\eta. A chosen set of disjoint edges contributes (xy)|S|∏e∈Sηe(xy)^{|S|}\prod_{e\in S}\eta_e. The remaining vertex intervals contribute their baseline continuants

hℓ(x,y)=∑k=0ℓxkyℓ−k, h_\ell(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}x^ky^{\ell-k},

which solve (1) with η=0\eta=0. Thus every outer coefficient qi,j,k(λ)=[xkyr−k]qi,jq_{i,j,k}(\lambda)=[x^ky^{r-k}]q_{i,j} is a polynomial in λ\lambda with nonnegative coefficients. This expansion follows by grouping the selected η\eta terms in the usual matching expansion of a tridiagonal determinant; the deleted-edge complements are disjoint intervals.

Let λ0=1+cos⁡θ\lambda_0=1+\cos\theta. Cauchy applied to these nonnegative polynomial coefficients gives the exact finite inequality

qi,j,k(λ)2qi,j,k(λ0)≤qi,j,k(λ2λ0). \frac{q_{i,j,k}(\lambda)^2}{q_{i,j,k}(\lambda_0)} \leq q_{i,j,k}\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{\lambda_0}\right). \tag{2}

It is a coefficient inequality for the actual tail. It does not assume real negative roots or a coefficient central-limit theorem.

2. The original prefix and suffix normalization

For the actual LEFT exponent v=(k,i,d−j,r−k)v=(k,i,d-j,r-k), write aN(v;σ)=BN(δ)qi,j,k(λ)a_N(v;\sigma)=B_N(\delta)q_{i,j,k}(\lambda) with σ=−2cos⁡(2πδ)\sigma=-2\cos(2\pi\delta). Here BNB_N contains the exact omitted-half scalar, all unused lower prefix and suffix diagonals, and ∏r=i+1jsr2\prod_{r=i+1}^j s_r^2. The original endpoint reference is pN(v)=BNrefqi,j,k(λ0)p_N(v)=B_N^{\mathrm{ref}}q_{i,j,k}(\lambda_0), BNref>0B_N^{\mathrm{ref}}>0. At even levels the reference is −VN(−2)-V_N(-2); at odd levels it is VN(−2)−N−2∂σVN(−2)V_N(-2)-N^{-2}\partial_\sigma V_N(-2). For a one-sided odd path the central zero is unused, so the derivative removes precisely that zero; its remaining normalization has only polynomial size. Bare odd VN(−2)V_N(-2) is not the reference.

Consequently (2) implies

∑k=0r|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)xkyr−k≤|BN(δ)|2BNrefqi,j(x,y;λ2/λ0). \sum_{k=0}^{r}\frac{|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)}x^ky^{r-k} \leq\frac{|B_N(\delta)|^2}{B_N^{\mathrm{ref}}} q_{i,j}\left(x,y;\lambda^2/\lambda_0\right). \tag{3}

All exterior and marked factors remain in BNB_N. We will estimate (3) by the uniform tail upper bound and logarithmic prefix/suffix estimates in the global CROSS proof, also used for arbitrary tail intervals in the LEFT theorem.

3. The effective weighted kernel

Put μ=i/N\mu=i/N, ν=j/N\nu=j/N, w=ν−μw=\nu-\mu, and, for 0<t<1/20<t<1/2,

s=sin⁡(πt),c=cos⁡(πt),z=sin⁡2(πδ),kδ2=1−z,A=x+y,ρ=4xy/A2. s=\sin(\pi t),\quad c=\cos(\pi t),\quad z=\sin^2(\pi\delta),\quad k_\delta^2=1-z, \quad A=x+y,\quad \rho=4xy/A^2.

The effective parameter in (2) satisfies cos⁡2(πδeff)→kδ4\cos^2(\pi\delta_{\mathrm{eff}})\to k_\delta^4 uniformly in δ\delta. The exact finite parameter stays in the original positive-tail range. Define

Λeff(t)=2s[As+(x−y)2s2+4xykδ4],αδ(t)=4(c2−z). \Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)=2s\left[As+ \sqrt{(x-y)^2s^2+4xy k_\delta^4}\right], \qquad \alpha_\delta(t)=4(c^2-z).

The uniform transfer upper bound, the upper Riemann bounds for both unused products, and the uniform positive reference estimate yield the weighted upper action

∫μνlog⁡ℒδ(t;x,y)dt,ℒδ=Λeffα0|αδ|2. \int_\mu^\nu\log\mathcal L_\delta(t;x,y)\,dt, \qquad \mathcal L_\delta= \frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\alpha_0}{|\alpha_\delta|^2}. \tag{4}

This rewriting uses ∫01/2log⁡|αδ|=0\int_0^{1/2}\log|\alpha_\delta|=0 only AFTER estimating the actual unused products. No finite division at a spectral zero is made. The tail estimate is uniform in both endpoints and the real spectral parameter, including the repeated-root case. The logarithmic errors are o(N)o(N) uniformly, at fixed positive x,yx,y in a compact set.

Let

Rδ(t)=scc2−z=sin⁡(2πt)cos⁡(2πt)+cos⁡(2πδ),Bρ(v)=1+1−ρ+ρv22. R_\delta(t)=\frac{sc}{c^2-z} =\frac{\sin(2\pi t)}{\cos(2\pi t)+\cos(2\pi\delta)}, \qquad B_\rho(v)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\rho+\rho v^2}}2.

A direct simplification of (4) gives

ℒδ=ARδ2Bρ(kδ2/s). \mathcal L_\delta=A R_\delta^2 B_\rho(k_\delta^2/s). \tag{5}

The complete positive half-block root is

L0(t;x,y)=Atan⁡2(πt)Bρ(1/sin⁡(πt)). L_0(t;x,y)=A\tan^2(\pi t)B_\rho(1/\sin(\pi t)). \tag{6}

It is strictly increasing in tt and its logarithm is integrable.

4. An elementary rearrangement

Define t′=π−1arctan⁡|Rδ(t)|∈[0,1/2]t'=\pi^{-1}\arctan|R_\delta(t)|\in[0,1/2]. The algebraic identity

(c2−z)2+s2c2−kδ4c2=z2s2≥0 (c^2-z)^2+s^2c^2-k_\delta^4c^2=z^2s^2\geq0 \tag{7}

implies kδ2/s≤1/sin⁡(πt′)k_\delta^2/s\leq1/\sin(\pi t'). Since BρB_\rho is nondecreasing,

ℒδ(t;x,y)≤L0(t′;x,y). \mathcal L_\delta(t;x,y)\leq L_0(t';x,y). \tag{8}

The map t↦t′t\mapsto t' preserves the uniform distribution on [0,1/2][0,1/2]. To prove this, set ϑ=2πt\vartheta=2\pi t, a=cos⁡(2πδ)a=\cos(2\pi\delta) and φ=arg⁡(eiϑ+a)\varphi=\arg(e^{i\vartheta}+a). For |a|<1|a|<1 this angle increases from 00 to π\pi, and

ϑ=φ+arcsin⁡(asin⁡φ). \vartheta=\varphi+\arcsin(a\sin\varphi).

The set |Rδ|>r|R_\delta|>r has endpoint angles φ1=arctan⁡r\varphi_1=\arctan r and φ2=π−arctan⁡r\varphi_2=\pi-\arctan r. Their arcsine corrections agree. Its length in tt is therefore 1/2−π−1arctan⁡r1/2-\pi^{-1}\arctan r, exactly the distribution of tan⁡(πt)\tan(\pi t). The endpoint cases are t′=tt'=t and t′=1/2−tt'=1/2-t.

For any measurable set of length ww, the integral of an increasing function of a uniformly distributed t′t' is at most its upper-tail integral of length ww. Apply this elementary rearrangement to the interval [μ,ν][\mu,\nu] and to log⁡L0\log L_0, using truncation for its integrable endpoint singularities. Equations (8) and the distribution identity prove

∫μνlog⁡ℒδ(t;x,y)dt≤∫1/2−w1/2log⁡L0(t;x,y)dt. \boxed{\displaystyle \int_\mu^\nu\log\mathcal L_\delta(t;x,y)\,dt \leq\int_{1/2-w}^{1/2}\log L_0(t;x,y)\,dt.} \tag{9}

5. The coefficient consequence and its current scope

Write ξ=k/N\xi=k/N. Weighted coefficient extraction in (3), (9), and the original complete-triangle Legendre formula give

limsup⁡1Nlog⁡supσ∈[−2,2]|aN(v;σ)|2pN(v)≤C(ξ,w−ξ,1/2−w). \limsup\frac1N\log\frac{\sup_{\sigma\in[-2,2]}|a_N(v;\sigma)|^2}{p_N(v)} \leq C(\xi,w-\xi,1/2-w). \tag{10}

The statement is uniform on the entire closed one-sided simplex. For this, first choose a finite outer tilt within any prescribed error at each limiting degree, use continuity of the triangle entropy to obtain a finite cover, and apply the uniform weighted estimates to these finitely many positive tilts. This includes outer degree zero and collapsing intervals. The omitted final spectral cap has width O(N−2)O(N^{-2}) in σ\sigma; the polynomial Bernstein bound for each actual coefficient adds only O(1)O(1) to its logarithmic supremum. Both parity reference normalizations are kept. RIGHT follows by the original row reflection. If a protected outer is zero, only its exponent-zero coefficients survive, and the already derived sharp boundary formula attains (10).

The middle degrees remain μ\mu and 1−ν1-\nu in the original shared flow. Although the entropy bound depends only on the two outer degrees, it is not permission to reassign middle loads independently. The global energy comparison still requires a proof using the original shared targets.
