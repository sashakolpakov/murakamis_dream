Orthogonal boundary packets under Conway composition

20 November 2026. Research note; the public manuscripts remain frozen.

The full Newton packet already makes a genuine quarter-turn unitary. Multiplication of neighboring tangles requires a further metric. At N≥3N\ge3, different output rows overlap, so row-by-row normalization does not give a coisometry. This note constructs that metric for every finite expression made from Conway multiplication and quarter-turns. It keeps the entire boundary algebra and both original closing rows.

The result is an exact finite-dimensional compatibility theorem. It identifies two quantitative losses that remain to be estimated: the projection of the actual leaf data onto the surviving boundary packet, and its angle with the prescribed closing row. Neither loss is given a positive lower bound here. In particular, this note proves no new hyperbolic-volume identity or finite-exception theorem.

1. The actual boundary algebra

Fix an integer level N≥2N\ge2, and put d=N−1,q=eπi/N,Q=q2,cj=q2j+1+q−2j−1, d=N-1,\qquad q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad Q=q^2,\qquad c_j=q^{2j+1}+q^{-2j-1}, s0=1,sr=∏u=1r2sin⁡(πu/N)(1≤r≤d). s_0=1,\qquad s_r=\prod_{u=1}^r2\sin(\pi u/N) \quad(1\le r\le d). The boundary space is the actual pair-Casimir algebra HN=ℂ[z]/(2TN(z/2)+2). H_N=\mathbb C[z]/\bigl(2T_N(z/2)+2\bigr). Here TNT_N is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. The normalized Newton basis is br(z)=∏j=0r−1(cj−z)sr2,0≤r≤d.(1) b_r(z)=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(c_j-z)}{s_r^2}, \qquad 0\le r\le d. \qquad\text{(1)} The empty product gives b0=1b_0=1. Coefficient vectors in this basis are columns in ℂN\mathbb C^N, equipped initially with the usual Hermitian inner product. A superscript ** means its conjugate transpose. The algebra retains the value and first derivative at every double Casimir root and the simple value at −2-2 when NN is odd. It is not the algebra of functions on the distinct roots alone.

We use the actual source identities proved in the Newton turn note and the multiplication note. The genuine quarter-turn has coefficient matrix Jer=ed−r,J=J*=J−1.(2) J e_r=e_{d-r},\qquad J=J^*=J^{-1}. \qquad\text{(2)} Multiplication is the linear map M:HN⊗HN→HN,M(ei⊗ej)=∑rμijrer.(3) M:H_N\otimes H_N\longrightarrow H_N, \qquad M(e_i\otimes e_j)=\sum_r\mu_{ijr}e_r. \qquad\text{(3)} If i+j≤di+j\le d, its nonzero coefficients are μijr=si+jsrsisjsr−isr−jsi+j−r,max⁡(i,j)≤r≤i+j.(4) \mu_{ijr} =\frac{s_{i+j}s_r} {s_i s_j s_{r-i}s_{r-j}s_{i+j-r}}, \qquad \max(i,j)\le r\le i+j. \qquad\text{(4)} All other coefficients are zero, including every product with i+j≥Ni+j\ge N. Equations (2)–(4) hold in the full repeated-root algebra. In particular M(e0⊗f)=f,μij0=δi0δj0,μij,d=δi+j,d.(5) M(e_0\otimes f)=f,\qquad \mu_{ij0}=\delta_{i0}\delta_{j0},\qquad \mu_{ij,d}=\delta_{i+j,d}. \qquad\text{(5)} The first equality makes MM surjective.

There are two original scalar closures. For f=∑rfrbrf=\sum_r f_rb_r, the original cup followed by the marked cap evaluates f(c0)=f0f(c_0)=f_0. The cylindrical closure, with one ordinary pivotal trace and the prescribed mark, is ϕ(f)=fd\phi(f)=f_d. We retain both rows: λ0=e0𝖳,λd=ed𝖳.(6) \lambda_0=e_0^{\mathsf T},\qquad \lambda_d=e_d^{\mathsf T}. \qquad\text{(6)} In particular the ordinary pivotal trace in the cylindrical closure has not been substituted for the marked cap. The exact Frobenius identity is ϕ(fg)=f𝖳Jg.(7) \phi(fg)=f^{\mathsf T}Jg. \qquad\text{(7)} The positive fixed Newton metric is ⟨f,g⟩=ϕ(f¯Jg)=f*g\langle f,g\rangle=\phi(\overline f\,Jg)=f^*g, where coefficient conjugation is well-defined because (1) has real coefficients. Both rows (6) have dual norm one in that fixed metric.

The literal ribbon operations and normalizations are also recorded in the complete-circle Conway note. Any external framing or normalization factor remains attached to the original diagram; the constructions below introduce no new such factor.

2. Keep every leaf variable before inserting its filling

Let EE be a finite expression obtained from leaves by products EFEF and quarter-turns ℛE\mathscr R E. A leaf stands for an arbitrary boundary packet in HNH_N. It does not stand for a one-dimensional space spanned by the already computed tangle value. Every occurrence of a leaf receives its own copy of HNH_N.

If n(E)n(E) is the number of leaf occurrences, define BE:HN⊗n(E)→HN(8) B_E:H_N^{\otimes n(E)}\longrightarrow H_N \qquad\text{(8)} by the following actual coefficient operations: Bleaf=I,BℛE=JBE,BEF=M(BE⊗BF).(9) B_{\mathrm{leaf}}=I,\qquad B_{\mathscr R E}=JB_E,\qquad B_{EF}=M(B_E\otimes B_F). \qquad\text{(9)} Ordered leaf tensor factors are inherited from the planar expression. Thus no permutation convention is left implicit.

If the actual leaf packets are f1,…,fn(E)f_1,\ldots,f_{n(E)}, then v=f1⊗⋯⊗fn(E),y=BEv(10) v=f_1\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n(E)},\qquad y=B_Ev \qquad\text{(10)} is the full actual boundary packet of the composed tangle. This follows by induction from the full operator identities (2)–(4). It includes the Hermite directions even when a component has zero value at every distinct root.

An actual signed crossing power can be inserted as a leaf packet. Its Newton coefficients include its original common Cartan scalar and all first derivatives. No crossing is replaced by its nodal phases. Fixed short tangles can likewise be inserted as their full packets. With this convention, BEB_E depends on the unfilled expression and NN, but not on the numerical twist assignments or an unknown closed invariant.

Lemma 1. Every BEB_E is surjective.

Proof. This is true at a leaf. A turn preserves surjectivity because JJ is invertible. If BEB_E and BFB_F are surjective, their tensor product is surjective, and (5) says that MM is surjective. Their composite in (9) is therefore surjective. Induction proves the claim. ▫\square

3. The boundary quotient metric and the expanded Gram

Define the real symmetric positive-definite matrices CE=BEBE*,GE=CE−1.(11) C_E=B_EB_E^*,\qquad G_E=C_E^{-1}. \qquad\text{(11)} Positive definiteness follows from Lemma 1. Reality follows because the universal matrices I,J,MI,J,M are real; the actual leaf vectors in (10) can still be complex. The recursion is explicit: Cleaf=I,CℛE=JCEJ,CEF=M(CE⊗CF)M*.(12) C_{\mathrm{leaf}}=I,\qquad C_{\mathscr R E}=JC_EJ,\qquad C_{EF}=M(C_E\otimes C_F)M^*. \qquad\text{(12)} Consequently GℛE=JGEJG_{\mathscr R E}=JG_EJ. In the remainder, ∥y∥GE2=y*GEy\|y\|_{G_E}^2=y^*G_Ey.

Two different Grams must be distinguished. The matrix BE*BEB_E^*B_E acts on the expanded leaf tensor space and is generally singular. It gives the fixed Newton boundary norm ∥BEv∥2=v*BE*BEv\|B_Ev\|^2=v^*B_E^*B_Ev. The matrix GEG_E acts on the NN-dimensional boundary and is nonsingular. It gives the quotient norm inherited from the entire expanded leaf space.

Theorem 2. The least-norm expanded lift of y∈HNy\in H_N is SEy=BE*CE−1y.(13) S_Ey=B_E^*C_E^{-1}y. \qquad\text{(13)} Its squared norm is y*GEyy^*G_Ey. The matrix PE=BE*CE−1BE(14) P_E=B_E^*C_E^{-1}B_E \qquad\text{(14)} is the orthogonal projection onto (ker⁡BE)⟂(\ker B_E)^\perp, and ∥BEv∥GE2=∥PEv∥2,∥v∥2=∥PEv∥2+∥(I−PE)v∥2.(15) \|B_Ev\|_{G_E}^2=\|P_Ev\|^2,\qquad \|v\|^2=\|P_Ev\|^2+\|(I-P_E)v\|^2. \qquad\text{(15)}

Proof. Equation (11) gives BESE=IB_ES_E=I. The range of SES_E lies in ran⁡BE*=(ker⁡BE)⟂\operatorname{ran}B_E^*=(\ker B_E)^\perp. Every other lift of yy is SEy+kS_Ey+k, with k∈ker⁡BEk\in\ker B_E, and the summands are orthogonal. This proves minimality and uniqueness. Direct multiplication gives SE*SE=CE−1BEBE*CE−1=CE−1. S_E^*S_E=C_E^{-1}B_EB_E^*C_E^{-1}=C_E^{-1}. Likewise PE*=PEP_E^*=P_E, PE2=PEP_E^2=P_E, and its kernel is ker⁡BE\ker B_E. These identities prove the projection claim and both equalities in (15). ▫\square

In particular this is the canonical quotient metric once the full leaf Newton inner products have been fixed: y*CE−1y=minBEc=y∥c∥2. y^*C_E^{-1}y=\min_{B_Ec=y}\|c\|^2. Equivalently, CE−1/2BEC_E^{-1/2}B_E is a coisometry: its product with its adjoint is the rank-NN identity on the boundary. This is an exact orthogonal packet for the actual synthesis, with no deleted boundary channel.

4. Compatibility at every multiplication and turn

Consider neighboring subexpressions E,FE,F. Set C=CEFC=C_{EF}, and define the normalized multiplication map AE,F=C−1/2M(CE1/2⊗CF1/2).(16) A_{E,F}=C^{-1/2}M(C_E^{1/2}\otimes C_F^{1/2}). \qquad\text{(16)} Equation (12) gives AE,FAE,F*=I.(17) A_{E,F}A_{E,F}^*=I. \qquad\text{(17)} Thus the original multiplication is a coisometry from the product of the two child quotient metrics to the parent quotient metric. The metric is transported at the gluing; it is not held fixed while an unproved isometry is imposed on MM.

For arbitrary actual child packets f,gf,g, put x=(CE−1/2f)⊗(CF−1/2g),PE,F=AE,F*AE,F. x=(C_E^{-1/2}f)\otimes(C_F^{-1/2}g),\qquad P_{E,F}=A_{E,F}^*A_{E,F}. Then PE,FP_{E,F} is an orthogonal projection, and the exact loss is ∥f∥GE2∥g∥GF2−∥fg∥GEF2=∥(I−PE,F)x∥2.(18) \boxed{\quad \|f\|_{G_E}^2\|g\|_{G_F}^2-\|fg\|_{G_{EF}}^2 =\|(I-P_{E,F})x\|^2.\quad} \qquad\text{(18)} Indeed the two terms on the left are ∥x∥2\|x\|^2 and ∥AE,Fx∥2=∥PE,Fx∥2\|A_{E,F}x\|^2=\|P_{E,F}x\|^2, respectively. Orthogonal decomposition proves (18). For a turn the corresponding equality is ∥Jf∥GℛE=∥f∥GE.(19) \|Jf\|_{G_{\mathscr R E}}=\|f\|_{G_E}. \qquad\text{(19)} This proves exact compatibility through arbitrarily many finite neighboring Conway gluings and turns. At a product, the loss can be nonzero; (18) computes it rather than asserting that it is small.

There is also a parenthesization check. For expressions involving products alone, the two maps associated with (EF)H(EF)H and E(FH)E(FH) coincide, after the usual tensor associator, because they are multiplication in the same actual algebra. Their B,C,G,PB,C,G,P therefore coincide. A genuine turn is not an algebra homomorphism, so no analogous unproved interchange of turns and products is used.

The nullspace removed here is safe in any exterior: if BEv=0B_Ev=0, the full actual boundary operator is zero. Every further linear or multilinear tangle context therefore gives zero. This statement concerns redundant expanded descriptions of a boundary operator. It does not remove a nonzero vector from the boundary space HNH_N. In particular it does not quotient by the kernel of one closing row. That row kernel can be exposed by a different exterior.

This distinction agrees with the contextual source-quotient criterion in the two-sided quotient note, Section 1. The stronger observability results in the adjacent-observable note, Sections 2–3, concern quotients of the true boundary representation; they do not forbid removal of redundant expanded descriptions.

5. Both original closing costs and the remaining angle

For a nonzero boundary row λ\lambda, its squared dual norm is aλ(E)=λGE−1λ*=λCEλ*.(20) a_\lambda(E)=\lambda G_E^{-1}\lambda^* =\lambda C_E\lambda^*. \qquad\text{(20)} It is strictly positive. In particular the two original rows (6) cost a0(E)=(CE)00,ad(E)=(CE)dd.(21) a_0(E)=(C_E)_{00},\qquad a_d(E)=(C_E)_{dd}. \qquad\text{(21)} Equivalently aλ(E)=∥λBE∥2a_\lambda(E)=\|\lambda B_E\|^2: it is an explicit contraction of the expression with its adjoint. This does not identify it with a hyperbolic volume or a regulator. A turn exchanges these two costs. A product has the explicit updates a0(EF)=a0(E)a0(F),(22) a_0(EF)=a_0(E)a_0(F), \qquad\text{(22)} ad(EF)=∑i,k=0d(CE)ik(CF)d−i,d−k=tr⁡(CEJCFJ).(23) a_d(EF)=\sum_{i,k=0}^d(C_E)_{ik}(C_F)_{d-i,d-k} =\operatorname{tr}(C_EJC_FJ). \qquad\text{(23)} For (22), only the input (0,0)(0,0) contributes to row zero of MM. For (23), row dd is the antidiagonal selector in (5). The displayed trace uses the real symmetry established after (11). For a general complex Hermitian covariance the sum is still valid, but its trace form is tr⁡(CEJCF𝖳J)\operatorname{tr}(C_EJC_F^{\mathsf T}J).

Equations (20)–(23) compute the original source cost before the numerical filling packets are inserted. Cauchy gives |λBEv|2≤aλ(E)∥PEv∥2.(24) |\lambda B_Ev|^2\le a_\lambda(E)\,\|P_Ev\|^2. \qquad\text{(24)} It is useful to retain the exact difference between this upper bound and the desired scalar. Suppose first that v≠0v\ne0, and put t=CE−1/2BEv,r=CE1/2λ*aλ(E),∥r∥=1. t=C_E^{-1/2}B_Ev,\qquad r=\frac{C_E^{1/2}\lambda^*}{\sqrt{a_\lambda(E)}}, \qquad \|r\|=1. Define ηE(v)=∥PEv∥2∥v∥2,γE,λ(v)={|r*t|2/∥t∥2,t≠0,0,t=0.(25) \eta_E(v)=\frac{\|P_Ev\|^2}{\|v\|^2},\qquad \gamma_{E,\lambda}(v)= \begin{cases}|r^*t|^2/\|t\|^2,&t\ne0,\\0,&t=0. \end{cases} \qquad\text{(25)} Both numbers lie in [0,1][0,1]. Since λBEv=aλ(E)r*t\lambda B_Ev=\sqrt{a_\lambda(E)}\,r^*t, there is an exact identity |λBEv|2=aλ(E)∥v∥2ηE(v)γE,λ(v).(26) \boxed{\quad |\lambda B_Ev|^2 =a_\lambda(E)\,\|v\|^2\,\eta_E(v)\, \gamma_{E,\lambda}(v).\quad} \qquad\text{(26)} For v=0v=0, define both factors in (25) to be zero; (26) remains valid. For the actual tensor input (10), ∥v∥2=∏j∥fj∥2\|v\|^2=\prod_j\|f_j\|^2.

The first factor in (25) measures how much expanded leaf data survives as a boundary operator. The second is the squared angle with the prescribed closing vector. A nonzero boundary packet can still be orthogonal to that closing vector. An orthogonal change of coordinates does not prevent either factor from being zero or exponentially small. No lower estimate for these factors is asserted. Consistent unitary coordinate changes on the expanded and boundary spaces leave these norms, projections, and angles unchanged; changing an orthonormal basis alone cannot improve ηEγE,λ\eta_E\gamma_{E,\lambda}.

6. A single gluing already has exponential metric cost

The metric update cannot in general be replaced by a harmless scalar normalization. For two variable leaves, C=MM*,C00=1,Cdd=N.(27) C=MM^*,\qquad C_{00}=1,\qquad C_{dd}=N. \qquad\text{(27)} The last two claims follow from the two distinct row descriptions in (5). Hence CC is not a scalar matrix at any N≥2N\ge2. In particular the actual multiplication is not a coisometry, even after one common scalar rescaling, in the fixed Newton metric. For N≥3N\ge3, its covariance is not even diagonal: the input (1,d−1)(1,d-1) contributes positively to both output rows d−1d-1 and dd, by (4)–(5). Hence Cd−1,d>0C_{d-1,d}>0, and diagonal row rescaling alone cannot make these rows orthogonal. At N=2N=2 the rows have disjoint supports; (27) still excludes a common scalar normalization, while separate row normalization does work at that level.

There is a stronger quantitative separation. Take a=⌊N/3⌋a=\lfloor N/3\rfloor. For every N≥3N\ge3, 1≤a1\le a and 2a≤d2a\le d, so (4) applies exactly and gives μa,a,a=s2asa2.(28) \mu_{a,a,a}=\frac{s_{2a}}{s_a^2}. \qquad\text{(28)} Let κ2(C)\kappa_2(C) be the ratio of the largest to the smallest eigenvalue. Its smallest eigenvalue is at most C00=1C_{00}=1, whereas its largest is at least Caa≥μa,a,a2C_{aa}\ge\mu_{a,a,a}^2. Thus κ2(C)≥μa,a,a2.(29) \kappa_2(C)\ge\mu_{a,a,a}^2. \qquad\text{(29)}

We use the Lobachevsky convention Λ(θ)=−∫0θlog⁡|2sin⁡u|du. \Lambda(\theta)=-\int_0^\theta\log|2\sin u|\,du. For fixed 0<x<10<x<1, the integrable Riemann-sum limit is 1Nlog⁡s⌊Nx⌋→−Λ(πx)π.(30) \frac1N\log s_{\lfloor Nx\rfloor} \longrightarrow-\frac{\Lambda(\pi x)}\pi. \qquad\text{(30)} For completeness, subtract log⁡t\log t from log⁡(2sin⁡πt)\log(2\sin\pi t) near zero; the difference extends continuously. The continuous part has ordinary Riemann sums, and the monotone integral bounds for log⁡t\log t give its integrable Riemann sum. This proves (30) and also applies when the index differs by a bounded integer from NxNx, as needed for 2a2a.

Since Λ(2π/3)=−Λ(π/3)\Lambda(2\pi/3)=-\Lambda(\pi/3), equations (28)–(30) give 1Nlog⁡μa,a,a→3Λ(π/3)π>0, \frac1N\log\mu_{a,a,a}\longrightarrow \frac{3\Lambda(\pi/3)}\pi>0, liminfN→∞1Nlog⁡κ2(MM*)≥6Λ(π/3)π.(31) \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\kappa_2(MM^*) \ge\frac{6\Lambda(\pi/3)}\pi. \qquad\text{(31)} The positivity follows from Λ(π/2)=0\Lambda(\pi/2)=0 and Λ(π/3)=∫π/3π/2log⁡(2sin⁡u)du>0\Lambda(\pi/3)=\int_{\pi/3}^{\pi/2}\log(2\sin u)\,du>0. These are lower bounds on conditioning, not an assertion of its optimal exponent. The inverse metric G=C−1G=C^{-1} has the same condition number.

7. What changes relative to the existing packet results

The fixed Newton metric, the unitary actual turn, and the two closing rows were already known. The ordinary-cap tree theorem, Sections 3–4, also gives an exact coisometry after its known diagonal row normalization. That diagonal normalization works because different root rows of that particular cap kernel have disjoint supports. The same note explicitly leaves arbitrary adjoining interior operations outside that conclusion.

Here the neighboring actual Conway multiplication is included. Its overlapping rows are handled by the full covariance (12), rather than by a diagonal weight or an unspecified positive global metric. The recursive metric depends on all unfilled child expressions, remains full dimensional, and has exact multiplication and turn interfaces. The price can be exponential, as (31) already shows for one gate.

This gives a precise version of choosing the orthogonality adapted to the synthesis: redundant expanded directions are orthogonally removed without changing any exterior. It does not make the remaining closing coefficient positive. To use (26) in a volume theorem, one must still estimate the combined exponential rate of its explicit source cost, leaf norms, projection factor, and closing angle. Their separate upper bounds do not supply the missing lower bound. In particular no assertion about generic or finitely many exceptional fillings follows here.

8. Long powers do not change an exact span by themselves

There is a simple limitation on using a smaller exact leaf space for long fillings. Let TT be an invertible operator on a finite-dimensional space and let UU be any prescribed subspace. For every integer p≥0p\ge0, span⁡{Tnu:n≥p,u∈U}=span⁡{Tnu:n≥0,u∈U}.(32) \operatorname{span}\{T^nu:n\ge p,\ u\in U\} =\operatorname{span}\{T^nu:n\ge0,\ u\in U\}. \qquad\text{(32)} Indeed the space on the right, call it WW, is TT-invariant. The restriction T|WT|_W is injective and WW is finite dimensional, so T(W)=WT(W)=W. The left side is TpW=WT^pW=W, proving (32).

This applies to multiplication by the actual crossing or full twist, with its complete Hermite inverse and original Cartan factor. Thus replacing a nonnegative filling half-line by a sufficiently long half-line does not by itself reduce the exact space of allowed leaf packets or the family of linear tests that annihilate it. The advantage of long filling must enter a quantitative estimate. This assertion uses no diagonalization and remains valid for Jordan blocks.
