The full original Casimir resolvent is polynomial at the spectral endpoint

30 October 2026. The original physical Casimir has a uniformly polynomial resolvent at z=2, including all degrees, both level parities and its Jordan blocks. Fixed genuine generic-q and spectral derivatives have polynomial cost on an explicit shrinking neighborhood. This addresses a finite endpoint of the exact operator contour; it does not prove dominance of a geometric saddle or control an entire contour segment merely from its endpoint.

1. Uniform statement

Put q=exp(pi i/N), d=N-1, N>=3, and use the ACTUAL generic physical matrix J_t(zeta) from the original jet theorem. For every 0<=t<=d there is an absolute constant C such that

||(2I-J_t(q))^-1||op<=C N^17.                  (1)

The exponent is a conservative uniform bound, not an optimal rate. The same bound holds for every high-degree block, by the exact generic high-degree adapter, and for their full orthogonal direct sum.

For sufficiently small absolute c>0, on the bidisk

zeta=q exp(h), |h|<=cN^-19, |z-2|<=cN^-18,

the resolvent exists and has norm at most 2C N^17. Consequently its mixed Taylor coefficients satisfy

||[h^r(z-2)^s](zI-J_t(q exp(h)))^-1||op
   <=C_(r,s) N^(17+19r+18s).                  (2)

These are the original matrix normals, with distinct generic upper and lower entries. No semisimple projection or root-only reflection is differentiated in (2).

2. The exact ground-state congruence

Write theta=pi/N, lambda_0=q+q^-1, delta=2-lambda_0=4sin^2(theta/2). The original normalized channel-zero vector is

f_r=(-q^-1)^r.

The root offdiagonal and its positive magnitude are

A_r=-a_r q^(2r+1-t),
a_r=4sin((t-r)theta)sin((r+1)theta)>0, 0<=r<t.

The exact row identity proved in the off-spectrum prefix theorem is D_r=lambda_0+q^-1 A_r+q A_(r-1), with A_(-1)=0. For a leading m-by-m prefix, m<=t, let F=diag(f_0,…,f_(m-1)) and let L_m be the REAL weighted Dirichlet matrix with diagonal a_(r-1)+a_r and offdiagonals -a_r, retaining the right boundary conductance a_(m-1). Then exactly

F(J_[m]-lambda_0 I)F=-q^-t L_m,
F(J_[m]-2I)F=-q^-t(L_m+delta V_m),
V_m=diag(q^(t-2r)).                            (3)

This is a unitary congruence, not a unitary similarity. Its left and right factors nevertheless preserve singular values and determinant magnitudes. Thus it can be used for inverse norms without claiming that J is Hermitian.

The positive matrix L_m has the elementary exact formulas

det L_m=product_(r=0)^(m-1) a_r,
tr L_m^-1=sum_(r=0)^(m-1)(r+1)/a_r.            (4)

Indeed L_m=B^T diag(a_r)B, where (Bv)r=v_r-v(r+1) and v_m=0. The inverse of B has ones above its diagonal; (4) follows directly.

3. Both halves are controlled, even when t approaches N-1

Suppose 1<=m<=floor(t/2)+1 and m<=t. Every argument (t-2r)theta in V_m lies in [0,t theta], and t theta<=pi-theta. Rotation by exp(-it theta/2) therefore gives

Re[exp(-it theta/2)(L_m+delta V_m)]
   >=cos(t theta/2)(L_m+delta I).

Since cos(t theta/2)>=sin(theta/2)>=1/N and delta>=4/N^2,

||(J_[m]-2I)^-1||op<=N^3/4.                  (5)

This remains uniform at the folded physical endpoint t=N-1. For these same half-prefixes one has

a_r>=4(r+1)^2/N^2.                            (6)

For r=0 use sin(t theta)>=sin theta>=2/N. For r>=1, r<=t/2 and t<=N-1 put t-r in [r,N-r-1]. Both sine factors in a_r are at least 2r/N, giving a_r>=16r2/N2 and hence (6). Equations (4),(6) show

delta tr L_m^-1<= (pi^2/4)(1+log N).

Expansion in the diagonal perturbation gives an especially simple determinant bound: all principal minors of L_m are positive, and every diagonal entry of V_m has modulus one. Therefore

|det(L_m+delta V_m)|<=det(L_m+delta I)
   <=det L_m exp(delta tr L_m^-1)
   <=C N^3 product_(r=0)^(m-1) a_r.            (7)

This is an upper bound for the ACTUAL determinant; no estimate on individual nonorthogonal spectral projectors was used. The trailing half at the root is the reflected conjugate of a leading half, so (5),(7) apply to it too. Only these root norm statements use reflection.

4. The exact full determinant prevents a small Schur denominator

The actual characteristic polynomial gives

chi_t(2)=det(2I-J_t(q))
   =product_(r=0)^t [2sin((r+1/2)theta)]^2>0.   (8)

Let s_t=product_(r=1)^t 2sin(r theta). Then

product_(r=0)^(t-1)a_r=s_t^2,
chi_t(2)/s_t^2>=4/N^2.                        (9)

Here is an elementary uniform proof of the second inequality. The positive sequence

H_t=product_(r=0)^t 2sin((r+1/2)theta)/s_t

has successive ratios sin((t+1/2)theta)/sin(t theta). They are first at least one and then at most one, so its minimum occurs at an endpoint. The cyclotomic products give H_(N-1)=2/N, while H_0=2sin(theta/2)>=2/N. Squaring proves (9).

For t>=1 split J_t-2I between its two middle rows. Its diagonal blocks B,C have sizes floor((t+1)/2), ceil((t+1)/2), so (5),(7) apply. Let A be their single connecting root offdiagonal and a=|A|<=4. In (7) the connecting conductance occurs in BOTH Dirichlet halves. Consequently

|det B det C|<=C N^6 a s_t^2.

The two-by-two bridge correction has determinant

Delta=1-A^2(B^-1)_(last,last)(C^-1)_(first,first)
     =det(J_t-2I)/(det B det C).

Using (8),(9) and a<=4 yields

|Delta|>=c N^-8.                              (10)

Every entry of its adjugate is bounded by C N^3 by (5). Its inverse therefore has norm at most C N^11. The rank-two resolvent formula, with two diagonal-block inverse factors of norm at most C N^3, gives C N^(3+3+11)=C N^17 for the whole inverse. This proves (1). The case t=0 is the scalar inverse delta-1<=N2/4.

This argument controls the actual full Jordan resolvent; it does not bound it by a sum of exponentially large individual projector norms.

5. The original generic normals and the contour qualification

Every exponent in the displayed original generic matrix entries is O(N), and the bandwidth is fixed. On |h|<=c/N the matrix perturbation has operator norm at most C N|h|, uniformly in physical t. Combining this with (1), the two perturbations h and z-2 on the stated bidisk have relative inverse norm less than 1/2 for sufficiently small c. The Neumann resolvent formula proves the uniform bidisk bound; two-variable Cauchy gives (2). This argument starts from the genuine generic matrix and so retains all moving-node and moving-projector contributions automatically.

The endpoint matters because the largest root Casimir node tends to 2, while the source-polynomial zeros approach 2 from above. A fixed contour enclosing all nodes cannot remain a fixed compact of the scalar limiting slit plane. Equations (1),(2) provide uniform finite control at 2 and on the displayed shrinking neighborhood. They do not alone estimate the path connecting that neighborhood to a fixed contour, establish a steepest-descent deformation, or show that the geometric contribution is nonzero.
