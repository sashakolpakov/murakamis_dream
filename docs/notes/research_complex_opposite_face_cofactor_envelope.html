Complex third faces and near-opposite MAIN phases in the actual raw cofactor

The raw cofactor theorem applies to the original levels with the specified complex phases. It does not give a shared-volume comparison.

Inputs are the exact nodal adapter, section 1; the uniform complex determinant prefix bound, (5a); and the real integral identity and original interpolation. The full raw cofactor source fixes the finite recurrence, both parity conventions and every unused factor.

1. Statement

Set theta=pi/N, J=floor((N-1)/2), j=J-k for a fixed k>=0. Use the original cleared cofactor

Rj=bj∏r<j(ar+u)+sj2(x+y)Rj−1+sjsj−1(aj−1+u)xyRj−2,ar=4cos⁡2((r+12)θ),sr=2sin⁡(rθ),R0=1. \begin{aligned} R_j&=b^j\prod_{r<j}(a_r+u)+s_j^2(x+y)R_{j-1} +s_js_{j-1}(a_{j-1}+u)xyR_{j-2},\\ a_r&=4\cos^2((r+\tfrac12)\theta),\qquad s_r=2\sin(r\theta),\qquad R_0=1. \end{aligned}\tag{1}

Absent negative indices are zero. Fix 0<c0<=C<=c1 and a compact set of COMPLEX third-face values z. Define

𝒥C(z)=12π∫0πlog⁡max⁡{|z+Ceit|,|z+Ce−it|}dt. \mathcal J_C(z)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^\pi \log\max\{|z+Ce^{it}|,|z+Ce^{-it}|\}\,dt.\tag{2}

Then, uniformly on these compacts and both parities,

limsupN→∞supC,z−4≤u≤0{1Nlog|RJ−k(−C,C,z;u)|−𝒥C(z)}≤0. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\ \sup_{\substack{C,z\\-4\le u\le0}} \left\{\frac1N\log|R_{J-k}(-C,C,z;u)|-\mathcal J_C(z)\right\}\le0.\tag{3}

The logarithm of a zero is -infinity. The same bound holds for a fixed finite collection of k. A degree-j Bernstein extension adds exactly 2pi j |Im delta| when u=-4sin^2(pi delta).

There is also a near-opposite version. For complex x,b with |x+C|+|b-C|<=h, there is a modulus omega(h)->0 such that

limsupN→∞supC,z,x,b|x+C|+|b−C|≤h−4≤u≤0{1Nlog|RJ−k(x,b,z;u)|−𝒥C(z)}≤ω(h). \limsup_{N\to\infty}\ \sup_{\substack{C,z,x,b\\|x+C|+|b-C|\le h\\-4\le u\le0}} \left\{\frac1N\log|R_{J-k}(x,b,z;u)|-\mathcal J_C(z)\right\}\le\omega(h).\tag{4}

Thus after a common homogeneous phase rotation, (4) applies on an actual torus whenever the two MAIN arguments have equal radii and relative phase sufficiently close to pi, with ARBITRARY third-face phase. No individual coefficient estimate is inferred.

2. The exact signed tail and its continuum roots

At every retained lower node u_a=-a_a, 0<=a<=j, the literal identity is

Rj(−C,C,z;ua)=S2aDa(−C,C,z)Ka,j(−C,z;ua),Sr=∏v=1rsv. R_j(-C,C,z;u_a)=S_{2a}D_a(-C,C,z)K_{a,j}(-C,z;u_a), \qquad S_r=\prod_{v=1}^r s_v.\tag{5}

The initial tail vector is (K_(a,a),K_(a,a-1))=(1,0). Its exact transfer has coefficients

pr=sr2(z−C),qr=srsr−1(aa−ar−1)Cz,Ka,r=prKa,r−1+qrKa,r−2. \begin{aligned} p_r&=s_r^2(z-C),&q_r&=s_rs_{r-1}(a_a-a_{r-1})Cz,\\ K_{a,r}&=p_rK_{a,r-1}+q_rK_{a,r-2}. \end{aligned}\tag{6}

For complex z there is NO positive sign gauge. Instead, with alpha=a/N and alpha<=t<=1/2, the transfer converges uniformly on the ENTIRE closed parameter triangle to [[p,q],[1,0]], where

p=4(z−C)sin⁡2(πt),q=16Czsin⁡2(πt)[sin⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πα)]. \begin{aligned} p&=4(z-C)\sin^2(\pi t),\\ q&=16Cz\sin^2(\pi t)\bigl[\sin^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\alpha)\bigr]. \end{aligned}\tag{7}

The convergence is O(1/N), uniformly on the stated C,z compacts. No denominator occurs here. Write rho(t;alpha,C,z) for the maximum modulus of the two roots of lambda^2-p lambda-q=0.

We will prove the uniform upper

limsupN→∞supa,C,z{1Nlog|Ka,J−k|−∫a/N1/2logρ(t;a/N,C,z)dt}≤0. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\ \sup_{a,C,z} \left\{\frac1N\log|K_{a,J-k}|- \int_{a/N}^{1/2}\log\rho(t;a/N,C,z)\,dt\right\}\le0.\tag{8}

This is an upper estimate, including zero tails. It does not choose one scalar phase across a collision or a modulus crossing.

There is also an EXACT finite scalar Jacobi structure. For l=r-a, normalize k_l=K_(a,a+l)/prod_(v=a+1)(a+l)s_v2. Then

k0=1,k1=z−C,kl=(z−C)kl−1+γlCzkl−2,γl=1−sasa+1sa+lsa+l−1∈[0,1],l≥2. \begin{aligned} k_0&=1,\qquad k_1=z-C,\\ k_l&=(z-C)k_{l-1}+\gamma_lCz\,k_{l-2},\\ \gamma_l&=1-\frac{s_as_{a+1}}{s_{a+l}s_{a+l-1}}\in[0,1],\qquad l\ge2. \end{aligned}

This is the actual trigonometric tail identity, not a new quantum level. Formally, or for z!=0 using either square root, set

kl=(iCz)lPl(z−CiCz). k_l=(i\sqrt{Cz})^lP_l\!\left(\frac{z-C}{i\sqrt{Cz}}\right).

The square roots cancel by parity. The P_l obey P_0=1,P_1=w,P_l=wP_(l-1)-gamma_l P_(l-2). Hence P_l is the characteristic polynomial of the REAL symmetric zero-diagonal Jacobi matrix with adjacent entries sqrt(gamma_2),…,sqrt(gamma_l). Its spectrum lies in [-2,2] by the row-sum bound. Pairing its opposite eigenvalues shows that every zero of k_l as a polynomial in z lies on |z|=C: each pair contributes (z-C)2+lambda2 C z, and an unpaired zero contributes z-C. At z=0 the polynomial identity holds by continuation. This proves a scalar tail structure only; it is NOT a semisimple realization of the original complete-colored Casimir or of arbitrary coupon observables. The uniform upper below does not require an empirical zero limit.

3. A uniform slow-transfer upper that permits Jordan points

For a compact family of two-by-two matrices T with norm bounded by M, Cayley-Hamilton gives, for every integer l>=1 and epsilon>0,

∥Tl∥≤Cε(l+1)(ρ(T)+ε)l. \|T^l\|\le C_\varepsilon(l+1)\bigl(\rho(T)+\varepsilon\bigr)^l.\tag{9}

Indeed, if its roots are lambda_1,lambda_2, the coefficient (lambda_1l-lambda_2l)/(lambda_1-lambda_2) is the sum of l monomials, and has modulus at most l rho^(l-1), also when the roots coincide. The other Cayley-Hamilton coefficient is bounded by (l-1)rho^l. Absorbing M/epsilon yields (9), including rho=0.

Divide the actual product into blocks of a FIXED length l. On a block the factors converge uniformly to one frozen matrix as N grows, because (7) is uniformly continuous and the finite coefficients have uniform O(1/N) error. The difference of its block product and T^l is o(1), uniformly in every parameter and the moving initial row a. Since rho+epsilon>=epsilon, for fixed l it may be absorbed into the right side of (9). Multiplication of the block norms gives

limsupN→∞1Nlog⁡∥∏rTN,r∥≤∫α1/2log⁡(ρ(t)+ε)dt+log⁡(2Cε(l+1))l. \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\left\|\prod_r T_{N,r}\right\| \le\int_\alpha^{1/2}\log\bigl(\rho(t)+\varepsilon\bigr)\,dt +\frac{\log(2C_\varepsilon(l+1))}{l}.\tag{10}

The incomplete blocks have bounded length and bounded norm. The Riemann sums are uniform, since log(rho+epsilon) is continuous on the compact parameter triangle. First let N tend to infinity, then l tend to infinity. This proves (10) without its last term.

The epsilon removal is uniform. The root sum and product imply

ρ≥|p|2=2sin⁡2(πt)|z−C|,ρ≥|q|=4C|z|sin⁡(πt)sin⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πα). \begin{aligned} \rho&\ge\frac{|p|}{2}=2\sin^2(\pi t)|z-C|,\\ \rho&\ge\sqrt{|q|}=4\sqrt{C|z|}\sin(\pi t) \sqrt{\sin^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\alpha)}. \end{aligned}\tag{11}

If |z-C|>=C/2 use the first inequality; otherwise |z|>=C/2 and use the second. Hence on every stated compact there is c>0 such that

ρ≥csin⁡(πt)sin⁡2(πt)−sin⁡2(πα). \rho\ge c\sin(\pi t)\sqrt{\sin^2(\pi t)-\sin^2(\pi\alpha)}.\tag{12}

Together with the uniform upper bound, this gives uniform integrability of log rho. Use sin^2(pi t)-sin^2(pi alpha) =sin(pi(t-alpha))sin(pi(t+alpha)); the moving logarithmic endpoints have uniformly vanishing integrals on intervals of vanishing length. Consequently the integral of log rho from alpha to 1/2 is continuous jointly in alpha,C,z, including alpha=0,1/2 and z=0,C. The continuous integrals of log(rho+epsilon) decrease uniformly to it by compact monotone convergence. This proves (8). The missing fixed k terminal rows change the integral by o(1) uniformly, by the same logarithmic integrability; the finite product was always stopped at J-k.

4. Exact complex cancellation with the SYMMETRIC determinant action

Define

L(α)=∫02αlog⁡(2sin⁡(πt))dt,Dsym(α;z)=∫0α[12log|z+Ce2πit|+12log|z+Ce−2πit|−log(4cos⁡2(πt))]dt. \begin{aligned} L(\alpha)&=\int_0^{2\alpha}\log(2\sin(\pi t))\,dt,\\ D_{\mathrm{sym}}(\alpha;z)&=\int_0^\alpha \left[\frac12\log|z+Ce^{2\pi it}|+\frac12\log|z+Ce^{-2\pi it}| -\log(4\cos^2(\pi t))\right]dt. \end{aligned}

Then the exact identity is

L(α)+Dsym(α;z)+∫α1/2log⁡ρ(t;α,C,z)dt=12log⁡max⁡(C,|z|). L(\alpha)+D_{\mathrm{sym}}(\alpha;z) +\int_\alpha^{1/2}\log\rho(t;\alpha,C,z)\,dt =\frac12\log\max(C,|z|).\tag{13}

Here all terms have their integrable endpoint values. We prove (13) by a HOLOMORPHIC identity, not by equality of harmonic functions on a real interval.

For t>alpha put w=z/C and

γ=1−sin⁡2(πα)sin⁡2(πt),0≤γ≤1. \gamma=1-\frac{\sin^2(\pi\alpha)}{\sin^2(\pi t)},\qquad0\le\gamma\le1.

After lambda=4Csin^2(pi t) r, the two roots satisfy

r2−(w−1)r−γw=0. r^2-(w-1)r-\gamma w=0.\tag{14}

The discriminant w^2+(4gamma-2)w+1 has all its zeros on |w|=1. When gamma>0 and the roots have equal modulus, their sum/product give (w-1)^2/(-gamma w) in [0,4]. Thus w+1/w belongs to [2-4gamma,2] subset [-2,2]. Off the unit circle, w+1/w can be real only for real w, where it is outside this interval. Equality of moduli is therefore impossible for |w|!=1. The case gamma=0 has roots w-1 and zero and has the same conclusion away from w=1.

There is a holomorphic dominant root r_out on |w|>1, normalized by r_out/w->1 at infinity, and a holomorphic nonzero root r_in on |w|<1, with r_in(0)=-1. These are precisely the roots of largest modulus there. The ratios r_out/w and -r_in have holomorphic logarithms fixed to zero at infinity and at zero, respectively. For positive real w>1, r_out is positive; for 0<w<1, r_in is negative. These signs fix the imaginary constants that matter below.

Inside the circle, define the analytic determinant action by replacing the pair of log moduli in D_sym with

log⁡C+12Log⁡(1+we2πit)+12Log⁡(1+we−2πit), \log C+\frac12\operatorname{Log}(1+we^{2\pi it}) +\frac12\operatorname{Log}(1+we^{-2\pi it}),

where the logarithms vanish at w=0. Define the tail action using log(4Csin^2(pi t))+Log(-r_in). These expressions are holomorphic in the disk and REAL for 0<w<1. On that interval, they are exactly the real quantities in the real cancellation identity. Their sum with L(alpha) therefore equals .5 log C as a complex identity on a real interval. The identity theorem gives that equality on the entire disk. Taking real parts proves (13) for |z|<C.

Outside the circle, the determinant action uses

Log⁡z+12Log⁡(1+w−1e2πit)+12Log⁡(1+w−1e−2πit). \operatorname{Log}z+\frac12\operatorname{Log}(1+w^{-1}e^{2\pi it}) +\frac12\operatorname{Log}(1+w^{-1}e^{-2\pi it}).

The tail uses Log z+log(4sin^2(pi t))+Log(r_out/w). The TOTAL coefficient of Log z is alpha+(1/2-alpha)=1/2. Subtracting .5 Log z therefore gives a single-valued holomorphic function on the exterior, including infinity. It is real and zero on w>1 by the same real identity. The identity theorem shows it is identically zero, proving (13) outside the circle.

The endpoint cases in alpha and the circle |z|=C follow by continuity. For the tail use (12); for D_sym use uniform integrability of the translated circle logarithm. Thus no lower bound or nonvanishing of the finite determinants or tails has been introduced.

5. From the complex determinant upper to the SAME full envelope

The proved determinant prefix upper uses D_max rather than D_sym:

Dmax(α;z)=∫0α[logmax{|z+Ce2πit|,|z+Ce−2πit|}−log(4cos⁡2(πt))]dt. D_{\max}(\alpha;z)=\int_0^\alpha \left[\log\max\{|z+Ce^{2\pi it}|,|z+Ce^{-2\pi it}|\} -\log(4\cos^2(\pi t))\right]dt.

Their exact difference is

Eα(z)=12∫0α|log|z+Ce2πit|−log|z+Ce−2πit||dt. E_\alpha(z)=\frac12\int_0^\alpha \left|\log|z+Ce^{2\pi it}|-\log|z+Ce^{-2\pi it}|\right|dt.\tag{15}

It is nonnegative and nondecreasing in alpha. Equation (13) therefore gives the nodal upper action

L(α)+Dmax(α;z)+Itail(α;z)=12log⁡max⁡(C,|z|)+Eα(z)≤12log⁡max⁡(C,|z|)+E1/2(z)=𝒥C(z). \begin{aligned} L(\alpha)+D_{\max}(\alpha;z)+I_{\mathrm{tail}}(\alpha;z) &=\frac12\log\max(C,|z|)+E_\alpha(z)\\ &\le\frac12\log\max(C,|z|)+E_{1/2}(z)=\mathcal J_C(z). \end{aligned}\tag{16}

For the last identity, the full symmetric determinant integral is .5 log max(C,|z|), by the circle logarithmic mean, while its denominator integral is zero. All identities include z=0 and the integrable zeros on |z|=C. No real cancellation is continued after replacing an average by a maximum without keeping its explicit extra term.

Combine (5), (8), the uniform determinant prefix upper and

1Nlog⁡S2a=L(a/N)+O(log⁡NN). \frac1N\log S_{2a}=L(a/N)+O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right).

This proves the uniform nodal upper J_C(z) for every 0<=a<=j. The cofactor has spectral degree at most j. To include the k omitted nodes of the full J+1-point Chebyshev/Radau grid, interpolate

Q(u)=Rj(u)∏a=j+1J(u−ua). Q(u)=R_j(u)\prod_{a=j+1}^J(u-u_a).

Its values vanish at the omitted nodes, and its retained values differ by at most 4^k. The full-grid Lebesgue bound is O(log N). Successive Markov derivative division by the k factors costs at most a fixed power of N, also at the roots themselves. This proves (3). All interpolation is in the genuine degree-j cofactor, not the full degree-d Hermite numerator.

6. Uniform stability of the two MAIN arguments

We give the details needed for (4). The exact nodal identity (5) holds for arbitrary x,b,z with the same S_(2a). The determinant remains det[bI+(x/2)H_-+(z/2)H_+], and the homogeneous tail has coefficients s_r^2(x+z), s_r s_(r-1)(a_(r-1)-a_a)xz.

First the determinant. On eta<=t<=1/2-eta, conjugate the perturbed transfer by the BASE opposite-equal eigenvector matrices. Their gap iC tan(pi t), inverse norms and derivatives have the same uniform bulk control, including every compact complex z. The conjugated norm is at most

ρD,base(t;C,z)+Cηh+Oη(N−1). \rho_{D,\mathrm{base}}(t;C,z)+C_\eta h+O_\eta(N^{-1}).

The base rho_D is bounded below on that interval. Hence the logarithmic cost relative to its base action is at most C’_eta h N+o_eta(N). The first and final eta N rows retain the same direct bound O(eta N log(2/eta)+log N), uniformly in the perturbed coefficients. For prefixes that stop in either endpoint portion, use the same truncated estimate; the base omitted integrals are uniformly O(eta log(2/eta)). Thus the perturbed determinant prefix has upper action D_max(alpha;z)+omega_D(h), uniformly in all alpha, where

ωD(h)≤infη>0{e(η)+Cη′h},e(η)→0,ωD(h)→0. \omega_D(h)\le\inf_{\eta>0}\{e(\eta)+C'_\eta h\}, \qquad e(\eta)\longrightarrow0,\qquad\omega_D(h)\longrightarrow0.\tag{17}

Only sufficiently small eta are used; constants depend on the fixed parameter compacts. No perturbed branch is followed through a crossing.

For the tail, its finite transfer again converges uniformly on the whole closed triangle. Its limiting coefficients differ from (7) by O(h). Roots of bounded monic quadratics move by at most O(sqrt h), so their maximum modulus is at most rho_base+C sqrt h. Apply the fixed-block argument (9)-(10) to the perturbed matrices, and keep the base regularization in the resulting upper bound. After epsilon->0, the tail upper action is at most

∫α1/2log⁡(ρbase+Ch)dt≤Itail,base(α)+ωK(h),ωK(h)→0 uniformly. \int_\alpha^{1/2}\log(\rho_{\mathrm{base}}+C\sqrt h)\,dt \le I_{\mathrm{tail,base}}(\alpha)+\omega_K(h), \qquad\omega_K(h)\longrightarrow0\text{ uniformly}.\tag{18}

The last uniform convergence follows from precisely the continuity, lower bound and compact monotone convergence proved after (12). There is no assumption that a perturbed tail is positive or that its own spectral radius has a uniform positive minimum.

The nodal factors S_(2a) are unchanged. Combining (16)-(18) and the same exact spectral interpolation proves (4), with omega=omega_D+omega_K. At each transfer estimate auxiliary parameters are fixed before N tends to infinity and are then sent to their stated limits. No N-dependent vanishing neighborhood is substituted for a fixed face-phase neighborhood. The spectral Bernstein extension has the same additional 2pi j |Im delta| at these perturbed face arguments.

7. The correct full third-face torus envelope

For r>=0 the explicit maximum of (2) on |z|=r is

UC(r)=maxϕ𝒥C(reiϕ)=1π∫0π/2log⁡|r+Ceit|dt. U_C(r)=\max_\phi\mathcal J_C(re^{i\phi}) =\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\log|r+Ce^{it}|\,dt.\tag{19}

For r>0,C>0, write g(t)=log|r+C exp(it)|. This is even and decreasing on [0,pi]. In the upper half-plane J_C(r exp(i phi)) is (1/(2pi)) integral_(-phi)^(pi-phi) g(t)dt for 0<=phi<=pi. Its derivative is [g(phi)-g(pi-phi)]/(2pi), so the maximum is at phi=pi/2. Reflection gives the lower half-plane and the same value. At r=0 the result follows directly. This proof includes r=C by integrable limits at the isolated logarithmic zero.

Unlike the real-face formula, (19) generally exceeds .5 log max(C,r). For example

UC(C)=12log⁡C+Gπ,G=−∫0π/4log⁡tan⁡tdt>0. U_C(C)=\frac12\log C+\frac G\pi,\qquad G=-\int_0^{\pi/4}\log\tan t\,dt>0.\tag{20}

Thus the correct proven complex envelope has a nonzero phase cost. Equation (20) is a property of this UPPER envelope; no matching lower growth for the actual finite cofactor is claimed. It does not prove that a sharper radial raw-cofactor upper is impossible.

Combining (4) and (19) gives an actual near-opposite-MAIN / arbitrary third-phase torus estimate with rate U_C(r)+omega(h). All original targets must still be extracted when this cofactor is used inside a source. This note neither sums the other cofactor terms nor proves that the resulting star envelope is at most Phi_D. It also makes no claim that spectral interpolation preserves a local sector’s matrix covariance: only the exact cofactor polynomial is interpolated.

scope

No numerical audit or asymptotic sampling is needed or claimed. This is an upper bound for the original raw cofactor on the stated face and spectral domains, not a coefficientwise source replacement, a lower asymptotic, an absolute star or Phi_D comparison, or a filling theorem.
