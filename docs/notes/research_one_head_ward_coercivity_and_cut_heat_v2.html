A quantitative Schur lemma for the original head and its marked cut

23 November 2026. Research note. The manuscripts and earlier final notes remain unchanged.

The original complete head admits a polynomial quantitative Schur lemma. Two of its actual generators control the distance of an arbitrary matrix from a scalar matrix. Including the adjoint lowering generator gives a completely positive averaging flow with an explicit spectral gap. Applied to the original highest cut, this flow produces a positive, uniformly conditioned probe in time O(N2log⁡N)O(N^2\log N).

This is a one-head theorem in the uniform physical metric. It does not identify a geometric packet or evaluate a knot invariant. In particular, it supplies no spectral estimate for a many-head coproduct system.

1. The actual generators and metric

Use exactly the balanced complete-root matrices ℰwa=(Q−a−1)wa−1,Fwa=(1−Qa+1)wa+1,Kwa=xQ−awa.(1) \mathcal E w_a=(Q^{-a}-1)w_{a-1},\qquad Fw_a=(1-Q^{a+1})w_{a+1},\qquad Kw_a=xQ^{-a}w_a. \qquad\text{(1)} The missing bottom and top arrows are zero. Here ℰ\mathcal E is the physical lowering operator called EE in the uniform-physical source and ℰ\mathcal E in the Azumaya-head calculation. It is not the latter’s rescaled unrestricted generator ℰ/(q−q−1)2\mathcal E/(q-q^{-1})^2. Directly, K*K=I,KℰK−1=Qℰ,F=−ℰ*,∥ℰ∥op,∥F∥op≤2.(2) K^*K=I,\qquad K\mathcal EK^{-1}=Q\mathcal E,\qquad F=-\mathcal E^*,\qquad \|\mathcal E\|_{\mathrm{ op}},\|F\|_{\mathrm{ op}}\le2. \qquad\text{(2)}

Put c(A)=Tr⁡AN,D(A)=1N∑j=0N−1KjAK−j.(3) c(A)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}A}{N},\qquad D(A)=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}K^jAK^{-j}. \qquad\text{(3)} The trace in (3) is the ordinary matrix trace. 2. Coercivity of the actual commutators

Theorem 1. For every A∈End⁡(VN)A\in\operatorname{End}(V_N), ∥A−c(A)I∥22≤3N28∥[ℰ,A]∥22+N216∥[K,A]∥22.(4) \boxed{\quad \|A-c(A)I\|_2^2 \le\frac{3N^2}{8}\|[\mathcal E,A]\|_2^2 +\frac{N^2}{16}\|[K,A]\|_2^2. \quad} \qquad\text{(4)} There is also the symmetric bound ∥A−c(A)I∥22≤N216(3∥[ℰ,A]∥22+3∥[F,A]∥22+∥[K,A]∥22).(5) \boxed{\quad \|A-c(A)I\|_2^2 \le\frac{N^2}{16} \left(3\|[\mathcal E,A]\|_2^2+ 3\|[F,A]\|_2^2+\|[K,A]\|_2^2\right). \quad} \qquad\text{(5)} Both bounds hold at every level, with the original zero arrows.

Proof. The eigenspaces of the unitary map A↦KAK−1A\mapsto KAK^{-1} are mutually orthogonal in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. They have eigenvalues QmQ^m, m∈ℤ/Nm\in\mathbb Z/N; its eigenvalue-one space consists exactly of diagonal matrices. Every other eigenvalue is at distance at least 2sin⁡(π/N)2\sin(\pi/N) from one. Multiplication by K−1K^{-1} does not change the norm, so ∥A−D(A)∥22≤∥[K,A]∥224sin⁡2(π/N)≤N216∥[K,A]∥22.(6) \|A-D(A)\|_2^2 \le\frac{\|[K,A]\|_2^2}{4\sin^2(\pi/N)} \le\frac{N^2}{16}\|[K,A]\|_2^2. \qquad\text{(6)} The final inequality uses sin⁡(π/N)≥2/N\sin(\pi/N)\ge2/N.

Write D=D(A)=diag⁡(d0,…,dd)D=D(A)=\operatorname{diag}(d_0,\ldots,d_d). The actual lowering arrows give ∥[ℰ,D]∥22=∑j=1d4sin⁡2(πj/N)|dj−dj−1|2.(7) \|[\mathcal E,D]\|_2^2 =\sum_{j=1}^{d}4\sin^2(\pi j/N)|d_j-d_{j-1}|^2. \qquad\text{(7)} This is the weighted path energy of the elementary-cap Laplacian. Its written Hardy estimate, Section 5 of [L], gives ∑j=0d|dj−c(A)|2≤3N28∑j=1d4sin⁡2(πj/N)|dj−dj−1|2.(8) \sum_{j=0}^d|d_j-c(A)|^2 \le\frac{3N^2}{8} \sum_{j=1}^{d}4\sin^2(\pi j/N)|d_j-d_{j-1}|^2. \qquad\text{(8)} That estimate is for this actual path, including both endpoints, and holds also at N=2N=2.

By (2), commutation with ℰ\mathcal E raises the KK-conjugation grade by one. Consequently [ℰ,D(A)]is an orthogonal grade projection of [ℰ,A].(9) [\mathcal E,D(A)] \quad\text{is an orthogonal grade projection of } [\mathcal E,A]. \qquad\text{(9)} Thus its norm is at most the full defect norm. The corresponding statement holds for FF, with grade lowered by one. On a diagonal matrix, the two commutator norms are equal by (2), or directly by (7).

Finally A−D(A)A-D(A) and D(A)−c(A)ID(A)-c(A)I are orthogonal. Adding (6) and (8), and using (9), proves (4). Alternatively average the equal diagonal estimates obtained from ℰ\mathcal E and FF before adding (6). This proves (5). No nonunitary matrix-unit or fusion-frame change occurs.

3. A positive averaging flow

For a matrix XX, let ad⁡X(A)=[X,A]\operatorname{ad}_X(A)=[X,A]. Its Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint is ad⁡X*=ad⁡X*\operatorname{ad}_X^*=\operatorname{ad}_{X^*}. Define ℒ=ad⁡ℰ*ad⁡ℰ+ad⁡F*ad⁡F+ad⁡K*ad⁡K.(10) \mathscr L= \operatorname{ad}_{\mathcal E}^* \operatorname{ad}_{\mathcal E} +\operatorname{ad}_{F}^*\operatorname{ad}_{F} +\operatorname{ad}_{K}^*\operatorname{ad}_{K}. \qquad\text{(10)} This is a self-adjoint nonnegative operator on the matrix space. Its energy is exactly the sum of the three squared Ward defects. Equation (5) implies ker⁡ℒ=ℂI,ℒ|(ℂI)⟂≽163N2I.(11) \ker\mathscr L=\mathbb CI,\qquad \mathscr L|_{(\mathbb CI)^\perp} \succeq\frac{16}{3N^2}I. \qquad\text{(11)} Also ∥ℒ∥op≤36\|\mathscr L\|_{\mathrm{ op}}\le36, since a commutator with XX has Hilbert–Schmidt operator norm at most 2∥X∥op2\|X\|_{\mathrm{ op}}. The order of the lower gap cannot be improved to a larger order: for A=diag⁡(j−d/2)j=0dA=\operatorname{diag}(j-d/2)_{j=0}^d, ∥A∥22=N(N2−1)12,∥[ℰ,A]∥22=∥[F,A]∥22=2N,[K,A]=0.(12) \|A\|_2^2=\frac{N(N^2-1)}{12},\quad \|[\mathcal E,A]\|_2^2=\|[F,A]\|_2^2=2N,\quad [K,A]=0. \qquad\text{(12)} Its Rayleigh quotient is 48/(N2−1)48/(N^2-1).

Theorem 2. For t≥0t\ge0, the map Tt=e−tℒ(13) T_t=e^{-t\mathscr L} \qquad\text{(13)} is completely positive, unital, trace preserving, and Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint. It satisfies ∥Tt(A)−c(A)I∥2≤e−16t/(3N2)∥A−c(A)I∥2.(14) \boxed{\quad \|T_t(A)-c(A)I\|_2 \le e^{-16t/(3N^2)}\|A-c(A)I\|_2. \quad} \qquad\text{(14)}

Proof. Write ℰ=H+iJ\mathcal E=H+iJ and K=U+iVK=U+iV, with all four displayed parts Hermitian. Since KK is normal, [U,V]=0[U,V]=0. Equation (2) gives the exact identity ℒ=2ad⁡H2+2ad⁡J2+ad⁡U2+ad⁡V2.(15) \mathscr L =2\operatorname{ad}_H^2+2\operatorname{ad}_J^2 +\operatorname{ad}_U^2+\operatorname{ad}_V^2. \qquad\text{(15)} For any Hermitian matrix BB and s>0s>0, e−sad⁡B2(A)=14πs∫ℝe−u2/(4s)eiuBAe−iuBdu.(16) e^{-s\operatorname{ad}_B^2}(A) =\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi s}}\int_{\mathbb R} e^{-u^2/(4s)}e^{iuB}Ae^{-iuB}\,du. \qquad\text{(16)} Diagonalizing BB proves (16) by the one-dimensional Gaussian Fourier integral. Its right side is an average of unitary conjugations, hence is completely positive, unital and trace preserving.

Apply (16) separately to the four terms in (15), and take the finite-dimensional Lie product limit. The product limit follows by expanding each factor through first order: one step has error O(m−2)O(m^{-2}), so mm steps converge to the exponential of the sum. Here NN is fixed for this identity; no uniform approximation by a finite number of steps is required. Complete positivity, unitality and trace preservation pass to the limit. Self-adjointness follows directly from (10), not from self-adjointness of a noncommuting finite product. Finally (11) and spectral decomposition prove (14).

4. Quantitative observation at the original cut

Let PP be any positive trace-one matrix in this same physical metric. Define the computable Ward bound Δ(A)2=N216(3∥[ℰ,A]∥22+3∥[F,A]∥22+∥[K,A]∥22).(17) \Delta(A)^2=\frac{N^2}{16} \left(3\|[\mathcal E,A]\|_2^2+ 3\|[F,A]\|_2^2+\|[K,A]\|_2^2\right). \qquad\text{(17)} By Theorem 1, ∥A−c(A)I∥2≤Δ(A)\|A-c(A)I\|_2\le\Delta(A). Since ∥P∥2≤Tr⁡P=1\|P\|_2\le\operatorname{Tr}P=1, |Tr⁡(PA)−c(A)|≤Δ(A),∥A∥22=N|c(A)|2+∥A−c(A)I∥22.(18) |\operatorname{Tr}(PA)-c(A)|\le\Delta(A),\qquad \|A\|_2^2=N|c(A)|^2+\|A-c(A)I\|_2^2. \qquad\text{(18)} Thus, with [u]+=max⁡(u,0)[u]_+=\max(u,0), [[∥A∥22−Δ(A)2]+N−Δ(A)]+≤|Tr⁡(PA)|≤∥A∥2N+Δ(A).(19) \boxed{\quad \left[\frac{\sqrt{[\|A\|_2^2-\Delta(A)^2]_+}}{\sqrt N} -\Delta(A)\right]_+ \le|\operatorname{Tr}(PA)| \le\frac{\|A\|_2}{\sqrt N}+\Delta(A). \quad} \qquad\text{(19)} This uses no lower bound for an unknown overlap or denominator. In particular a relative Ward defect o(N−3/2)o(N^{-3/2}) makes the ratio in (20) tend to one.

The original highest cut is the rank-one orthogonal projector P0=|w0⟩⟨w0|P_0=|w_0\rangle\langle w_0|, with the actual tail pivots and framing factors kept in its one-cut operator. Consequently (18)–(20) apply to this prescribed observation.

5. A canonical softened cut and its source scope

Set ρt=Tt(P0),tN=3N216log⁡(2N).(21) \rho_t=T_t(P_0),\qquad t_N=\frac{3N^2}{16}\log(2N). \qquad\text{(21)} Theorem 2 gives positivity, trace one, and ∥ρt−I/N∥2≤e−16t/(3N2)1−1/N.(22) \|\rho_t-I/N\|_2 \le e^{-16t/(3N^2)}\sqrt{1-1/N}. \qquad\text{(22)} Since operator norm is at most Hilbert–Schmidt norm, for every t≥tNt\ge t_N, I2N≼ρt≼3I2N,cond⁡2(ρt)≤3.(23) \boxed{\qquad \frac{I}{2N}\preceq\rho_t\preceq\frac{3I}{2N},\qquad \operatorname{cond}_2(\rho_t)\le3. \qquad} \qquad\text{(23)} This is an explicit flow from the actual original probe, constructed from its actual head generators.

It is particularly concrete on this probe: diagonal matrices stay diagonal. If Δpath,N\Delta_{\mathrm{path},N} denotes the weighted path Laplacian with edge weights pj=4sin⁡2(π(j+1)/N)p_j=4\sin^2(\pi(j+1)/N), 0≤j<d0\le j<d, direct multiplication in (10) gives ℒ(diag⁡a)=2diag⁡(Δpath,Na),ρt=diag⁡(e−2tΔpath,N𝒆0). \mathscr L(\operatorname{diag}a) =2\operatorname{diag}(\Delta_{\mathrm{path},N}a), \qquad \rho_t=\operatorname{diag} (e^{-2t\Delta_{\mathrm{path},N}}\mathbf e_0). The matrix −2Δpath,N-2\Delta_{\mathrm{path},N} has nonnegative off-diagonal entries and zero row sums. Its exponential therefore preserves nonnegative vectors and their total sum: this follows also by shifting its diagonal to make the entire generator nonnegative and expanding the exponential series. Thus the softened cut is the probability distribution of a specified continuous-time walk on the path, starting at vertex zero. No cyclic endpoint edge has been introduced.

For the fully assembled unmarked one-cut knot operator AN=KNIA_N=K_N I, with its recorded external normalization included, Tr⁡(ρtAN)=KN(t≥0).(24) \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_t A_N)=K_N \qquad(t\ge0). \qquad\text{(24)} The scalarity used here is the exact original-source scalarity recorded in [A, Section 4]. Its qualitative consequence that any trace-one probe gives the same unmarked knot scalar was already known. The new estimates are the actual-generator coercivity, the positive flow and its polynomial mixing time.

For an arbitrary marked matrix, the source is not unchanged. The exact transported identity is instead Tr⁡(P0Tt(A))=Tr⁡(ρtA).(25) \operatorname{Tr}(P_0T_t(A))=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_t A). \qquad\text{(25)} To check it, TtT_t preserves adjoints and is Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, so it is self-adjoint also for the bilinear trace pairing. Equation (18) bounds the discrepancy from the cyclic mean when the Ward defects are controlled. Replacing the marked source by that mean without this control is not licensed.

All averaging in (21)–(25) concerns the complete one-head operator after assembly. It does not replace internal cups, turns or Casimir derivative channels by scalar quotients. There is no assertion that this heat flow intertwines every crossing or that it may be inserted independently on all internal heads. In particular (11) is not a many-head coproduct gap.

The full source still needs a geometric norm estimate and source-faithful control of any proposed geometric approximation. No such approximation, nonzero geometric period, or finite-exception volume theorem is supplied here.
