A scalar response for the quartic on internal Conway edges

21 November 2026. Research note. Earlier finals and public manuscripts remain unchanged.

The quartic source problem on p−1p-1 internal packets still has an exact scalar dual. Its root term is now bilinear rather than linear, but the original tensor normalization makes that term small enough to preserve uniform convexity of the scalar-forced inner problems. The outer response is a contraction in two real coordinates.

This does not eliminate the internal packets. It gives a dimension-independent certificate for the final closing once its internal response is controlled. No geometric rate or finite-exception theorem is proved.

5. Why the actual source structure matters

Fixed degree and coercivity alone do not give this scalar dual. Even the uniformly convex real quartic R(x)=(x2−1)2,g(x)=3x,E(x)=R(x)+|g(x)|2=x4+x2+1 R(x)=(x^2-1)^2,\quad g(x)=\sqrt3\,x,\quad E(x)=R(x)+|g(x)|^2=x^4+x^2+1 has min⁡E=1\min E=1, whereas max⁡λ[min⁡x(R+2λg)−λ2]=0\max_\lambda[\min_x(R+2\lambda g)-\lambda^2]=0: testing x=±1x=\pm1 bounds that expression by −23|λ|−λ2-2\sqrt3|\lambda|-\lambda^2, with equality zero at λ=0\lambda=0. This is a counterexample to a generic optimization inference, not a knot counterexample.

Here the small root map, residual convexity, zero-cost forward field and source comparison come from the retained Conway normalization. They provide the response estimates but no geometric exponential rate by themselves. No dimension-independent bound on algebraic elimination degree, and no complete elimination of the quantum packets, is claimed. The theorem concerns the original p−1p-1-packet quartic; it does not assume that a further partial minimization preserves its root-square decomposition.
