Exact auxiliary colors of the pure Conway leaf

29 October 2026. The literal turned crossing circle has an exact positive core and a disjoint, signed alias at every ordinary auxiliary color. Every alias coefficient is at most N/2 in absolute value, uniformly in the color and at both parities. The complete-circle core also simplifies to one positive factorial ratio. These are identities in the FULL pair Casimir algebra, so they retain its projective normal directions.

The leaf here is h_j=Rturn(L_j), the one used in the positive complete-circle Conway grammar. It has no extra half-twist. The older belt leaf Rturn(R^-1 L_j) has a different alias and is not covered by this bound. This local theorem alone does not prove a global mixed-color gap or a long-filling theorem.

1. Literal source and notation

Set

q=eπi/N,D=N−1,M=⌊D/2⌋,θ=π/N,sr=∏u=1r2sin⁡(uθ),s0=1. q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad D=N-1,\quad M=\lfloor D/2\rfloor, \quad \theta=\pi/N,\quad s_r=\prod_{u=1}^r2\sin(u\theta),\quad s_0=1.

Write 𝖳n(z)=2Tn(z/2)\mathsf T_n(z)=2T_n(z/2), so 𝖳0=2,𝖳1=z\mathsf T_0=2,\mathsf T_1=z, and put

HN=ℂ[z]/(𝖳N(z)+2),ck=q2k+1+q−2k−1,Br(z)=∏k=0r−1(ck−z),B̂r=Br/sr2. H_N=\mathbb C[z]/(\mathsf T_N(z)+2),\qquad c_k=q^{2k+1}+q^{-2k-1},\qquad B_r(z)=\prod_{k=0}^{r-1}(c_k-z),\qquad \widehat B_r=B_r/s_r^2. \tag{1}

The original crossing-circle calculation gives, for the actual auxiliary module of dimension j and its original pivot, with no extra factor 1/j,

Lj=(−1)j−1Uj−1(z/2),1≤j≤N,hj=RturnLj. L_j=(-1)^{j-1}U_{j-1}(z/2),\quad 1\le j\le N, \qquad h_j=R_{\mathrm{turn}}L_j. \tag{2}

The actual turn and marked closure satisfy

RturnB̂r=B̂D−r,ϕ(B̂iB̂j)=δi+j,D,ϕ(LNf)=N∑k=0Df(ck). R_{\mathrm{turn}}\widehat B_r=\widehat B_{D-r},\qquad \phi(\widehat B_i\widehat B_j)=\delta_{i+j,D},\qquad \phi(L_Nf)=N\sum_{k=0}^{D}f(c_k). \tag{3}

The sum counts each of the N listed roots, including repetitions. These equations hold in the repeated-root algebra (1), not just its quotient consisting of values at the distinct nodes. In particular the B̂v\widehat B_v coefficient of RturnfR_{\mathrm{turn}}f is ϕ(fB̂v)\phi(f\widehat B_v).

2. A generic Chebyshev–Newton identity

With q initially generic and [ab]q{a\brack b}_q the symmetric quantum binomial, one has the polynomial identity

Uj−1(z/2)=∑r=0j−1(−1)r[j+r2r+1]qBr(z). U_{j-1}(z/2)=\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}(-1)^r {j+r\brack 2r+1}_q B_r(z). \tag{4}

Here is a direct derivation. For a formal variable t, successive division of 1−zt+t2=(1−crt+t2)+t(cr−z)1-zt+t^2=(1-c_rt+t^2)+t(c_r-z) gives

11−zt+t2=∑r≥0(−t)rBr(z)∏i=0r(1−cit+t2). \frac1{1-zt+t^2} =\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{(-t)^rB_r(z)} {\prod_{i=0}^{r}(1-c_it+t^2)}. \tag{5}

The remainder after r stages is divisible by tr+1t^{r+1}, which justifies the formal identity. The denominator in its rth term is

∏a=02r+1(1−q−(2r+1)+2at). \prod_{a=0}^{2r+1}(1-q^{-(2r+1)+2a}t).

Its reciprocal has coefficient of tnt^n equal to q−(2r+1)n[n+2r+12r+1]q2q^{-(2r+1)n}{n+2r+1\brack 2r+1}_{q^2}, where the binomial on the right is the ordinary Gaussian one. This formula follows by induction on the number of factors using the finite geometric series and the Gaussian Pascal recurrence. Converting to the symmetric binomial cancels the displayed q power. Taking n=j−r−1n=j-r-1 proves (4), since the left side of (5) generates the Chebyshev polynomials.

For 1≤j≤⌊N/2⌋1\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor, all factorial arguments in (4) are less than N. Specialization therefore gives, without any zero-over-zero division,

hj=∑r=0j−1(−1)j−1+rbj,rB̂D−r,bj,r=sr2sj+rs2r+1sj−r−1>0. \boxed{\quad h_j= \sum_{r=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{j-1+r}b_{j,r}\widehat B_{D-r},\qquad b_{j,r}=\frac{s_r^2s_{j+r}}{s_{2r+1}s_{j-r-1}}>0.\quad} \tag{6}

This is an equality of full polynomials of degree below N after the actual turn, so it includes every repeated-node derivative. For larger j one must retain the Laurent-polynomial binomial in (4), rather than substitute a factorial quotient containing zeros. The next identity avoids all such zero quotients.

3. Positive core plus the actual low-color alias

For 0≤h≤M0\le h\le M, define L0=h0=0L_0=h_0=0. Then exactly

hN−h=∑v=hMAN,v(h)B̂v+(−1)Nhh,AN,v(h)=N2s2vsv2sv−hsv+h>0. \boxed{\quad h_{N-h}=\sum_{v=h}^{M}A_{N,v}(h)\widehat B_v+(-1)^Nh_h, \qquad A_{N,v}(h)=\frac{N^2s_{2v}} {s_v^2s_{v-h}s_{v+h}}>0.\quad} \tag{7}

The first sum is the core. If h>0, (6) puts the entire alias in indices v≥N−hv\ge N-h; these are disjoint from the core indices h≤v≤Mh\le v\le M. At h=0 the alias is zero. In particular the complete-circle coefficients are simply

hN=∑v=0MaN,vB̂v,aN,v=N2s2vsv4. h_N=\sum_{v=0}^{M}a_{N,v}\widehat B_v,\qquad a_{N,v}=\frac{N^2s_{2v}}{s_v^4}. \tag{8}

To prove (7), first use the elementary Chebyshev identity

UN−h−1=12(𝖳hUN−1−𝖳NUh−1). U_{N-h-1}=\tfrac12(\mathsf T_hU_{N-1} -\mathsf T_NU_{h-1}).

It follows either by multiplying sines and cosines at z=2cos⁡tz=2\cos t, then extending as a polynomial identity, or directly from the Chebyshev recurrence. In the full algebra (1) it becomes

LN−h=(−1)h𝖳h2LN+(−1)NLh. L_{N-h}=(-1)^h\frac{\mathsf T_h}{2}L_N+(-1)^NL_h. \tag{9}

No passage to root values was made in (9). To compute the turn of its first term, it is now legitimate to use the special trace identity (3). Its coefficient at v is

(−1)hN∑k=0Dcos⁡(h(2k+1)θ)B̂v(ck). (-1)^hN\sum_{k=0}^{D}\cos(h(2k+1)\theta) \widehat B_v(c_k). \tag{10}

For v>M, BvB_v vanishes at all the nodes c_k, so (10) is zero. For v<=M, a finite Laurent expansion gives

Bv(w+w−1)=∑u=−vv(−1)u[2v]q![v+u]q![v−u]q!wu. B_v(w+w^{-1})= \sum_{u=-v}^{v}(-1)^u \frac{[2v]_q!}{[v+u]_q![v-u]_q!}\,w^u. \tag{11}

For example this follows from

Bv(w+w−1)=q−v2(qw;q2)v(q/w;q2)v=(−1)vw−v(q1−2vw;q2)2v, B_v(w+w^{-1}) =q^{-v^2}(qw;q^2)_v(q/w;q^2)_v =(-1)^vw^{-v}(q^{1-2v}w;q^2)_{2v},

and the finite binomial expansion; its q powers cancel on conversion to symmetric binomials. Evaluating at w=q2k+1w=q^{2k+1}, the Fourier average in (10) selects u=+h and u=-h. There are no other Fourier aliases because v+h≤2M<Nv+h\le2M<N. The two equal terms cancel the factor 1/2 in the cosine; at h=0 there is just its constant term. This is exactly (7).

Moreover the core is pointwise dominated by the complete core:

0<AN,v(h)≤aN,v(0≤h≤v≤M). 0<A_{N,v}(h)\le a_{N,v}\quad(0\le h\le v\le M). \tag{12}

Indeed the ratio aN,v/AN,v(h)a_{N,v}/A_{N,v}(h) is sv−hsv+h/sv2s_{v-h}s_{v+h}/s_v^2. Its successive ratio as h increases is

sin⁡((v+h)θ)sin⁡((v−h+1)θ)≥1, \frac{\sin((v+h)\theta)}{\sin((v-h+1)\theta)}\ge1,

since the two arguments sum to (2v+1)θ≤π(2v+1)\theta\le\pi. For h>0 this is strict except at the odd-level endpoint v=(N−1)/2v=(N-1)/2, where equality holds for every allowed h.

4. An exact uniform N/2 bound for every alias coefficient

For all N>=2, 1≤j≤⌊N/2⌋1\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor, and 0≤r<j0\le r<j,

0<bj,r≤N/2,∑r=0j−1bj,r≤jN/2≤N2/4. \boxed{\qquad 0<b_{j,r}\le N/2,\qquad \sum_{r=0}^{j-1}b_{j,r}\le jN/2\le N^2/4.\qquad} \tag{13}

The stronger final N^2/4 follows from j<=N/2. The proof uses the actual sine product, not its limiting action. Rewrite (6) as

bj,r=sr2s2r+1∏u=−rr2sin⁡((j+u)θ). b_{j,r}=\frac{s_r^2}{s_{2r+1}} \prod_{u=-r}^{r}2\sin((j+u)\theta). \tag{14}

For fixed r this positive product, regarded as a function of real j on r<j<N−rr<j<N-r, is symmetric about N/2 and log concave: each summand log⁡sin⁡((j+u)θ)\log\sin((j+u)\theta) has strictly negative second derivative. Hence its maximum occurs at j=N/2. The value of (14) there is

Br=2∏u=1r2sin⁡(2uθ)∏u=0r2sin⁡((2u+1)θ). B_r=2\, \frac{\prod_{u=1}^{r}2\sin(2u\theta)} {\prod_{u=0}^{r}2\sin((2u+1)\theta)}. \tag{15}

Here one cancels (2sin⁡uθ)(2cos⁡uθ)=2sin⁡2uθ(2\sin u\theta)(2\cos u\theta) =2\sin2u\theta. The ratio

Br+1Br=sin⁡((2r+2)θ)sin⁡((2r+3)θ) \frac{B_{r+1}}{B_r} =\frac{\sin((2r+2)\theta)}{\sin((2r+3)\theta)}

is <=1 and then >=1, with at most one change: the comparison changes at (4r+5)θ=π(4r+5)\theta=\pi. Thus the maximum over 0≤r≤⌊N/2⌋−10\le r\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor-1 occurs at an endpoint. At r=0 it is csc⁡θ\csc\theta. If N=2m, the other endpoint is Bm−1=mB_{m-1}=m, because the even sine product in (15) is m and the odd product is 2. These product evaluations follow directly by factoring tm−1t^m-1 and tm+1t^m+1 at t=1. If N=2m+1, reflection sin⁡(kθ)=sin⁡((N−k)θ)\sin(k\theta)=\sin((N-k)\theta) cancels the even factors against the odd factors other than the first, and gives Bm−1=csc⁡θB_{m-1}=\csc\theta. Finally sin⁡(π/N)≥2/N\sin(\pi/N)\ge2/N, proving (13).

Consequently the alias in (7) has a uniform polynomial coefficient norm, even when its physical color h grows proportionally to N. This is not a positive-source assertion: its coefficients genuinely alternate. For odd N and h=1 the alias is −B̂D-\widehat B_D. For even N>=4 and h=2, it has coefficient −c0-c_0 at B̂D\widehat B_D. These signs cannot be dropped in the original mixed-color source.

5. Its real action and a marked local complex interpolation

Let J(x)=∫0xlog⁡(2sin⁡πt)dtJ(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt. Uniformly at all integer arguments between 0 and N-1, the elementary endpoint-aware sum estimate is log⁡sr=NJ(r/N)+O(log⁡N)\log s_r=NJ(r/N)+O(\log N). For a direct proof, subtract log⁡t+log⁡(1−t)\log t+\log(1-t) from the integrand. The remainder extends smoothly to [0,1] and has an O(1) Riemann-sum error. The two subtracted logarithms give ordinary factorial ratios, whose sum-minus-integral errors are O(log N) uniformly, including r=0 and r=N-1. Writing x=v/Nx=v/N and χ=h/N\chi=h/N, (7) therefore has rate

log⁡AN,v(h)=Ngχ(x)+O(log⁡N),gχ(x)=J(2x)−2J(x)−J(x−χ)−J(x+χ), \log A_{N,v}(h)=Ng_\chi(x)+O(\log N),\qquad g_\chi(x)=J(2x)-2J(x)-J(x-\chi)-J(x+\chi), \tag{16}

uniformly over 0≤h≤v≤M0\le h\le v\le M. On the interior 0<χ<x<1/20<\chi<x<1/2,

∂χgχ(x)=log⁡sin⁡π(x−χ)sin⁡π(x+χ)<0,∂χ2gχ(x)|χ=0=−2πcot⁡(πx)<0. \partial_\chi g_\chi(x) =\log\frac{\sin\pi(x-\chi)}{\sin\pi(x+\chi)}<0, \qquad \left.\partial_\chi^2g_\chi(x)\right|_{\chi=0} =-2\pi\cot(\pi x)<0. \tag{17}

The loss is uniform on compact interior sets with chi bounded away from zero. At x=1/2 the continuum loss vanishes, consistently with the exact odd-level endpoint equality in (12); an arbitrary global color gap cannot be inferred without controlling endpoint sources.

There is also a fixed, even, holomorphic continuation of this actual core coefficient, without assigning a formal Newton index the role of a peripheral holonomy. Let G_N be the sine-factorial interpolation proved in the exact interpolation note, with GN(r)=srG_N(r)=s_r, and define

𝒜N(x,χ)=N2GN(2Nx)GN(Nx)2GN(N(x−χ))GN(N(x+χ)). \mathcal A_N(x,\chi)= \frac{N^2G_N(2Nx)}{G_N(Nx)^2G_N(N(x-\chi))G_N(N(x+\chi))}. \tag{18}

It is holomorphic and nonzero wherever the four scaled arguments lie in the interpolation’s strip, and it agrees EXACTLY with (7) at its lattice arguments. On any fixed compact complex corridor where 2x, x, x-chi and x+chi have real parts strictly between 0 and 1, the proved uniform factorial expansion gives

𝒜N(x,χ)=N1/2sin⁡(2πx)23/2sin⁡(πx)sin⁡π(x−χ)sin⁡π(x+χ)eNgχ(x)(1+O(N−1)). \mathcal A_N(x,\chi) =N^{1/2}\, \frac{\sqrt{\sin(2\pi x)}} {2^{3/2}\sin(\pi x) \sqrt{\sin\pi(x-\chi)\sin\pi(x+\chi)}} e^{Ng_\chi(x)}(1+O(N^{-1})). \tag{19}

All logarithms and square roots are continued from the positive real corridor. The error and every fixed derivative are uniform on a smaller corridor. Thus the local coefficient is an even analytic function of the ACTUAL auxiliary-color deficit chi, with the exact finite values and their marks fixed. This does not assert that the whole finite source already has a single analytic saddle packet: its original support, alias pieces, boundary contributions and contour still require their separate estimates.

6. Consequence for arbitrary fixed Conway circuits, and scope

There is a useful intrinsic description of the alias subspace:

𝔫N=span⁡{B̂v:v≥⌈N/2⌉}=Nil⁡(HN),𝔫N2=0. \mathfrak n_N=\operatorname{span}\{\widehat B_v: v\ge\lceil N/2\rceil\} =\operatorname{Nil}(H_N),\qquad \mathfrak n_N^2=0. \tag{20}

Indeed B⌈N/2⌉B_{\lceil N/2\rceil} contains every distinct factor of 𝖳N+2\mathsf T_N+2 once, including its simple factor at odd N. The subspace on the left is therefore the kernel of evaluation at all distinct nodes. Every remaining root has multiplicity two, so this is precisely the square-zero nilradical. Equivalently the exact Newton multiplication vanishes when its two input indices sum to at least N. Formula (6) is triangular with nonzero last coefficient bj,j−1=sj−12b_{j,j-1}=s_{j-1}^2, so

h1,…,h⌊N/2⌋are a basis of 𝔫N. h_1,\ldots,h_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor} \quad\text{are a basis of }\mathfrak n_N. \tag{21}

Thus the signed alias is exactly part of the source’s radical data. In a product of any number of mixed pure leaves, terms with two aliases vanish identically. In particular each robust three-leaf seed contains at most one alias term in its exact expansion. The actual turn reverses the Newton indices and does NOT preserve this nilradical; it cannot be quotiented out of a Conway circuit.

Each ordinary circle color in an arbitrary fixed robust Conway expression can now be replaced exactly by (6) or (7), followed by the existing full Newton multiplication and actual coordinate reversal for every turn. This gives a finite, source-preserving signed circuit with polynomial alias leaf coefficients and positive core coefficients bounded by the corresponding complete ones. All original colors, marked cuts, pivots and normal directions are retained. It is an exact deformation through ordinary AUXILIARY COLORS; it is not yet a deformation in a separately identified geometric meridian or longitude.

Polynomial leaf aliases can acquire exponential growth when multiplied by other circuit factors. Consequently (13) alone is not a proof of an exponentially negligible whole alias sector. That global source comparison, the complex whole-source saddle, and the geometric peripheral interpretation are the additional steps needed before invoking a long-filling transfer theorem.

Audit: generic polynomial identities, all root coefficients and literal physical turns. The principal-root inequalities are proved above; finite-field calculations are used only to verify exact identities.
