Complete actual Casimir packets and their recoupling measure

29 October 2026. Every physical integer-degree block has an explicit complete Casimir frame, including all confluent derivative columns. Its inverse is determined by a closed bilinear Christoffel–Darboux measure. That measure has explicit sine-factorial profiles; at degree N-1 all its double-node weights are polynomial. No tame eigenframe is inferred from tame spectral jets.

The adjacent complete-channel change has an explicit all-entry product formula before restriction to a total-three-head multiplicity module. Its entries admit finite angle-factorial envelopes, with every inverse normalizer accounted for. The original cup and both cap rows are kept. The remaining marked-source norm and coherent overlap action are not asserted to have the sharp volume bound.

1. Literal Jacobi blocks and the high-degree mirror

Put q=exp(pi*i/N), Q=q^2, d=N-1, x=q^d=-q^-1. Use the original balanced F,Ecal,K and tensor Casimir Omega as in the source Hermite theorem. For total degree 0<=T<=d, write physical states |u,T-u>, 0<=u<=T, and set v=T-u. The block J_T of Omega is COMPLEX SYMMETRIC tridiagonal. Its offdiagonal from u to u+1 is

A_u=Q^(-v)(1-Q^v)(1-Q^(u+1)), 0<=u<T.             (1)

Its diagonal is

D_u=(Q^(-u)-1)xQ^(-v)(1-Q^u)
    +(Q^(-v)-1)x^(-1)Q^u(1-Q^v)
    +q x^2Q^(-T)+q^(-1)x^(-2)Q^T.               (2)

Both formulas are direct application of F_tot E_tot and the actual Cartan. Every A_u is nonzero. The product is especially simple:

B_T=product_(u=0)^(T-1) A_u=(-1)^T s_T^2,
s_a=|(Q;Q)_a|.                                   (3)

For T>d, put K=2d-T, a0=T-d. Its block is the SAME J_K after translating the physical first index by a0. One proof uses the actual root Chevalley matrix W|a>=(-1)^a|d-a>: W F W^-1=Ecal, W Ecal W^-1=F, W K W^-1=K^-1. Chevalley followed by tensor flip commutes with Omega, and its coefficient is constant on a fixed integer degree. Thus the high blocks introduce no additional frame cost or missing channels. The same comparison follows from direct entries (1)–(2).

2. Explicit polynomial columns and generic-q confluence

Define continuants

p_0(z)=1, p_1(z)=z-D_0,
p_(u+1)(z)=(z-D_u)p_u(z)-A_(u-1)^2 p_(u-1)(z).

For 0<=u<=T put

f_u(z)=p_u(z)/(A_0...A_(u-1)), f_0=1.            (4)

The characteristic polynomial is

P_T(z)=p_(T+1)(z)=product_(j=0)^T(z-c_j),
c_j=q^(2j+1)+q^(-2j-1).                         (5)

There are only simple or double nodes; a collision is exactly j+l=d. The self-paired node j=d/2, when integral, is simple. At a simple node take the column f(c_j); at a double node take BOTH

f(c_j), f'(c_j).                                 (6)

The last-row recurrence gives (J_T-z)f(z)=-P_T(z)e_T/B_T. Hence (6) is the exact Jordan chain (J_T-c)f'=f at a double node.

These are normalized ACTUAL Clebsch columns and their generic-q divided differences, not an arbitrarily supplied Jordan basis. To see this, vary q to zeta near q with ordinary highest weight d. Its highest vector at degree j has coefficients b_0=1 and

b_a=(-1)^a x^(-a) Q^[a(j-1)-a(a-1)/2]
          (Q^(N-j);Q)_a/(Q^(N-a);Q)_a,           (7)

where in (7) Q=zeta^2, x=zeta^d. The E equation directly gives its successive ratio. Its divided descendant F_tot^(T-j)h_j/(Q;Q)_(T-j) has eigenvalue zeta^(2N-1-2j)+zeta^(-2N+1+2j). Its first physical component is x^(-(T-j))[T choose j]_Q, nonzero near the root because T<N. Thus normalizing that component to one gives the unique first-row recurrence solution f(zeta,lambda_j(zeta)). This proves (5) by generic distinctness and dimension T+1.

For colliding j,l the divided difference of these two normalized columns converges to f’(c); the common zeta derivative cancels. The eigenvalue gap has logarithmic derivative 2N times q^(2j+1)-q^(-2j-1), up to its order sign, and is nonzero. All denominators in (7) and in these low-degree descendants have factorial indices at most d. Exact generic-q duality is retained; no fixed-q meridian splitting is imposed on an exact dual cap.

3. The complete inverse, including every derivative normalizer

Let V_T have the columns (6), grouped by distinct nodes. Different nodes and their chains are orthogonal for the BILINEAR pairing u^T v, because J_T is symmetric. This is not a Hermitian positivity assertion. Define nu_j=(-1)^j q^(j(j+1)).

At a simple node c_j, the bilinear block is the nonzero scalar

sigma_j=x^T nu_j P_T'(c_j)/B_T.                  (8)

At a double node c=c_j=c_l, put Q_j(z)=P_T(z)/(z-c)^2 and let r’_j be the derivative of the original normalized crossing polynomial at c. Its exact block is

K_j=[[0,sigma_j],[sigma_j,sigma_j eta_j]],
sigma_j=x^T nu_j Q_j(c)/B_T,
eta_j=r'_j/nu_j+Q_j'(c)/Q_j(c).                  (9)

In particular sigma_j never vanishes. The full frame and inverse are

V_T^T V_T=K_T=directsum K_j,
V_T^(-1)=K_T^(-1)V_T^T,
K_j^(-1)=sigma_j^(-1)[[-eta_j,1],[1,0]]          (10)

for a double block; a simple block has inverse sigma_j^-1. This proves completeness and gives the ENTIRE inverse, without a condition-number guess or loss of a Jordan channel.

Proof of the normalizers. Christoffel–Darboux for the continuants is

sum_(u=0)^T f_u(z)f_u(w)
  =[P_T(z)p_T(w)-p_T(z)P_T(w)]/[(z-w)B_T^2].

Its simple-node specialization gives P_T'(c)p_T(c)/B_T^2. At a double node it gives Q_j(c)p_T(c)/B_T^2 for f^T f’, and [Q_j(c)p_T'(c)+Q_j'(c)p_T(c)]/B_T^2 for f’^T f’. The first physical row of the original checked R in degree T has only the last column, with coefficient kappa x^-T. Therefore

f_T(c_j)=x^T nu_j,
f_T'(c_j)=x^T r'_j at a double node.

Since p_T=B_T f_T, these formulas prove (8)–(9), including all phases. The actual full Cartan kappa cancels in this argument; it remains on R itself.

4. Exact sine-factorial measures, all parities and all degrees

Write a_j=|1-Q^(2j+1)|. For a simple UNPAIRED j (j<d-T),

|sigma_j|=s_(T-j)s_(T+j+1)/(s_T^2 a_j).          (11)

For a double pair j<l=d-j<=T,

|sigma_j|=N s_(T-j)s_(T+j-d)/(s_T^2 a_j^2).      (12)

For the simple SELF-paired node j=d/2<=T at odd N,

|sigma_j|=N s_(T-j)^2/s_T^2.                    (13)

Every index lies in 0,...,d. To prove these, factor each nonzero node difference into

|c_j-c_k|=|1-Q^(j-k)| |1-Q^(j+k+1)|.

The first product gives s_j s_(T-j), deleting the partner factor when present. The second is the circular interval j+1,…,j+T+1: delete its self factor, and its unique root-zero partner if present. Root complementation s_d=N gives exactly (11)–(13). No separate factorwise N^O(N) bound is used.

At T=d this simplifies to

|sigma_j|=1/|1-Q^(2j+1)|^2 for EVERY double node,
|sigma_(d/2)|=1 at the simple odd-level node.      (14)

Thus the full degree-d channel MEASURE is polynomial; this does not say the Clebsch frame or its Hermitian inverse is tame. For all double blocks, nonzero node gaps are at least 16/N^2 and the independently derived actual jets obey |r'_j/nu_j|<=N^2/8. Consequently |eta_j|<=C N^3. The inverse block in (10) has no additional exponential factor beyond its explicit 1/|sigma_j|.

5. Explicit q-Hahn sums and a quantitative angle profile

For T<=d, set r=T-j and I={max(0,u-r),...,min(j,u)}. At the actual root the value column is

f_u(c_j)=x^r/[T choose j]_Q * sum_(a in I)
   (-1)^a x^[-(r-u+2a)] Q^[a(a-1)/2+a(r-u+a)]
      [j choose a]_Q [u choose a]_Q
                               [T-u choose j-a]_Q.       (15)

This follows by expanding the actual highest vector (7) at the root, then applying the divided coproduct descendant. All three binomial factors retain their true root phases. In particular it is a closed finite Clebsch/q-Hahn sum, with no unknown knot period.

Let beta denote the positive sine-binomial magnitude and put

E_(T,j,u)=sum_(a in I) beta_(j,a)beta_(u,a)
                      beta_(T-u,j-a)/beta_(T,j).          (16)

Then |f_u(c_j)|<=E_(T,j,u). For a double pair j,l,

|f_u'(c_j)|<=C N^2(1+log N)
                          [E_(T,j,u)+E_(T,l,u)].         (17)

For (17), differentiate the ACTUAL generic-q sums before their confluence. Each factorial has index below N and |D_logzeta log F_a|<=N^2(1+log N) at the root. There are a bounded number of factorial ratios and quadratic monomials in every summand and its endpoint normalizer. The eigenvalue-gap derivative has modulus at least eight. Thus differentiation costs only the displayed polynomial times the SAME positive factorial profiles. No exponential frame factor is put into the error term.

Every beta in (16) is N w_d(k,L-k,d-L), with exact sum d. Equivalently E is N^2 times a finite sum of THREE such positive cell weights divided by the known boundary weight w_d(j,T-j,d-T). Their charges and the range I are literal integer Clebsch incidence; the denominator is a compulsory boundary metric. Equations (10)–(13),(16)–(17) give a quantitative profile for the entire frame AND inverse, including every Jordan derivative. They are bounds, not assertions that the q-Hahn phases can be deleted at the sharp exponent or that these cells already form geometric edges. For high physical T extend the notation by E_(T,j,u)=E_(2d-T,j,u-(T-d)), and use the corresponding low-block measure sigma; inadmissible physical indices have value zero.

6. Complete adjacent-channel kernel at arbitrary width

Lift the block frames to the full physical pair, preserving total T. Before restricting the THREE-head total Casimir, its complete pair coordinate change has the following exact entry. For input pair degree T, channel gamma (value or derivative type), and third physical index c, choose an output first physical index u. Put

b=T-u, T'=b+c.

If the physical indices are admissible, the output pair channel delta has coefficient

Rcal_((u,T',delta);(T,gamma,c))
     =V_T[u,gamma] (V_T'^(-1))[delta,b].         (18)

Otherwise it is zero. Here high blocks use the unitary index translation from Section1. Equation (18) is simply the two ACTUAL pair frames sewn at the shared physical index; no complete channel or derivative row has been suppressed. Its entries are products of the finite q-Hahn columns and explicit inverse rows (10).

For a double channel use Ebar=E_j+E_l, and for a simple channel use E_j. Equations (10),(16)–(17) give, uniformly in all admissible ports, an all-channel estimate of the form

|Rcal|<=C N^5(1+log N)^2
         Ebar_(T,gamma,u) Ebar_(T',delta,b)/|sigma_delta|. (19)

All exponential factors on the right are the stated sine-factorial angle profiles. Restricting (18) to a fixed total-three-head Casimir or a source-generated Hom space is the subsequent genuine Racah contraction; it cannot be assumed from (18) alone. This distinction avoids a spurious tensor-multiplicity assertion at the root.

The original signed pair crossing in these frames is exactly kappa times blocks nu_j at simple nodes and [[nu_j,r'_j],[0,nu_j]] at double nodes; its literal inverse retains the inverse derivative. Thus twist phases remain coherent when (18) is used in an arbitrary original braid. This is an all-exterior operator/source coordinate change, not a replacement invariant. The original pivot, framing, modified-dimension germ and peripheral line are transported unchanged. None is divided by the spectral measure sigma or selected afterward from the knot value.

7. The original source and the remaining estimate

At degree d the c_* block may be replaced, by its explicit two-by-two adapter, with the uniformly conditioned p,t source frame of the source Hermite theorem. If g_0=x^-d, its columns in the canonical f,f’ coordinates are

p=N g_0 f(c_*),
t=-(mu_N/gamma_N)p+(g_0/gamma_N)f'(c_*).        (20)

This retains the original cup, ordinary cap and marked row. Ordinary caps vanish on other Casimir blocks and select the corresponding source derivative row; the cup lies in the source value channel. The marked row does NOT vanish on arbitrary other channels. Its complete coefficients are the explicit sums m V_T; in an assembled original plat integer degree forces the final marked pair to degree d, as before. No sectorwise marked-corner substitution is made here.

For example a positive spectral packet metric can be specified by giving each canonical simple/double block the positive scalar |sigma_j| times identity, with the source block (20) handled in its actual p,t frame. This defines a full positive Gram by pullback through the COMPLETE frame. Its original marked dual norm and its adjacent overlap energies are explicit via (10),(15),(18), but no polynomial or sharp-volume bound for them is asserted.

The new result supplies the actual all-channel frame, inverse measure and factorial/angle envelopes needed to ask that question. The remaining sharp step is a coherent estimate of (18) after its actual source-generated restrictions and closure, rather than separate absolute maximization of (16) or of twist eigenvalues.

The subsequent source-generated complete module and positive-source Gram calculation do give polynomial BOTH boundary norms after the actual J recoupling. However its fixed canonical positive-channel energy is nonsharp already for the original figure-eight. This does not invalidate the frames or measures above; a sharp norm estimate must retain a more selective coherent packet, such as the source-exact terminal construction, and control its general propagation.
