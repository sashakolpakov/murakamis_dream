Original crossing layers: a punctured Casimir norm torus and its parabolic limit

30 October 2026. This is derived from the ACTUAL homogeneous root crossing kernel and its coefficient composition. It solves the local classical transport for every integer power. It does not prove a selected finite-cycle contribution, a kernel norm bound, a knot A-polynomial identification or a filled-volume limit.

Throughout N>=3,d=N-1,q=exp(pi i/N), hbar=2pi i/N. Fix the original outer face variables x,y and put A=x+y,P=xy,f(z)=(z+x)(z+y). The letters P and sigma below are not new quantum parameters. The generic algebra is over characteristic zero with xyA nonzero; all source divisors explicitly recorded below remain excluded.

1. The exact original kernels and their composition

The frozen homogeneous theorem gives the original coupon polynomial H_F(x,b,c,y), homogeneous of degree d. For literal polynomials F,G in the SAME pair Casimir, including their full repeated-node data,

H_FG(x,b,c,y)=[z^d] H_F(x,b,z,y) H_G(x,z,c,y).          (1)

Indeed H_G is the matrix of G on input monomials c^j, and H_F is the matrix of F on their output powers z^j. The coefficient condition t_left+i_right=d is exactly matrix multiplication. There is no extra N or mark factor in (1). If F,G are at different original ports, (1) does NOT assert that they commute or share one Casimir.

For epsilon=+/-1 the full original crossing is Repsilon=lambda0epsilon r_N^epsilon(Omega), lambda0=q(d2/2), with the original square-root lift. The normalized homogeneous coefficient at x^k b^i y^l c^t, i+k+l+t=d, is exactly

(-1)^(k+l+1) q^[epsilon(kl-(i+1)(t+1))]
                      N/(S_i S_k S_l S_t).         (2)

No Hermite derivative has been replaced by nodal values. Formula(1) therefore gives R^n with its EXACT lambda0^n for every integer n, including negative n. All original cups, caps, physical endpoint zeros and marked face targets remain those of the homogeneous theorem.

Write normalized simplex variables I,K,L,T with sum1 in the leading limit. The principal phase of (2), INCLUDING lambda0, is

E_simplex + I Log b+K Log x+L Log y+T Log c
   +pi i(K+L)+epsilon pi i(KL-IT)+epsilon pi i/2.  (3)

Here E_simplex=(Lambda(pi I)+Lambda(pi K)+Lambda(pi L) +Lambda(pi T))/pi on its recorded continuation. The finite source is still (2), with i+k+l+t=N-1; its order-one phases and finite offsets are not discarded by using (3) for a principal characteristic.

2. Both signs and the actual input/output convention

At a regular critical point of (3) put E_I=exp(2pi i epsilon I), and similarly E_K,E_L,E_T. Taking the three constrained differences of derivatives gives

E_I^-1=1-b v, E_K=1+x v,
E_L=1+y v, E_T^-1=1-c v.

For either sign, exp(pi i epsilon)=-1 and the actual linear sign in (3) is exp(pi i)=-1. Thus the same equations hold; no epsilon-dependent integer character has been silently changed. Product E_I E_K E_L E_T=1 implies

v[(x+y+b+c)+(xy-bc)v]=0.

The v=0 branch has all sine factors zero and is excluded from the regular critical chart. Consequently, with D=P-bc and L0=A+b+c,

E_I=D/f(b), E_T=D/f(c),
E_K=-(x+b)(x+c)/D, E_L=-(y+b)(y+c)/D.                (4)

The recorded regular open set is

xybc D f(b)f(c)L0 !=0.                              (5)

In particular every E_j-1 remains nonzero. These are exponentiated critical equations, not a claim selecting logarithms or an integration chain. The linear signs and lambda0 are still present in (2),(3).

The homogeneous kernel has LEFT output b, RIGHT input c. The actual coefficient target in (1) is degree d, hence the principal gluing law is T_left+I_right=1, NOT zero. Use the momentum r=exp(2pi i I) at the left face; the matching right momentum is r’=exp(-2pi i T). Then the coefficient/pullback advance from left to right is

F_+(b,r)=(c,r'), c=[P-r f(b)]/b,
                     r'=f(c)/(P-bc),
F_-(b,r)=(c,r'), c=[P-f(b)/r]/b,
                     r'=(P-bc)/f(c).             (6)

Direct substitution proves F_-=F_+^-1. Thus inverse crossings have opposite canonical transport. If the kernel is viewed instead as a map FROM its input phase point TO its output, the displayed advance is reversed. This is an orientation convention, not a new inverse crossing. The exact R R^-1 coefficient contraction is H_1.

For equal signs in two successive layers, (4) and the target give

(P-bz)(P-zc)=f(z)^2.                               (7)

For opposite signs they give b=c on the regular chart, as required by (6). A cancelled pair can have a nonisolated middle critical variable; the finite identity is (1), not a claim about a Gaussian saddle of that identity kernel.

3. The invariant is the principal original Casimir

The original face operator T has principal symbol

T(b,r)=-(r+r^-1)+(2-r-r^-1)(A/b+P/b^2).

Since the actual Casimir is Omega=-T, its symbol is

sigma=2+[(r-1)^2/r] f(b)/b^2.

Insert r=(P-bz)/f(b) (or its reciprocal for the inverse sign):

sigma=2+(b+z+A)^2/(P-bz).                           (8)

This is the actual conserved pair Casimir, not a chosen first integral of an unrelated recurrence. On (7), putting d1=P-bz,d2=P-zc and f=f(z), the relation d1d2=f^2 gives

(b+z+A)^2/d1=(f-d1)^2/(z^2d1)
              =(d1+d2-2f)/z^2
              =(z+c+A)^2/d2.

Hence sigma is preserved, and (7) becomes EXACTLY

c=-sigma z-b-2A.                                  (9)

Conversely (8),(9) imply (7) wherever the recorded units hold. Thus the full local layer sequence obeys

b_(j+1)+sigma b_j+b_(j-1)+2A=0.

Here and in (10), (b,z) denotes the POSITIVE companion chart on the common phase space: z=[P-r f(b)]/b. A negative crossing acts by the inverse of this map, rather than silently reusing its actual negative-layer right neighbor as that positive companion coordinate. All integer powers are powers of the one affine matrix

M_sigma=[[0,1,0],[-1,-sigma,-2A],[0,0,1]],
(b_j,b_(j+1),1)^t=M_sigma^j(b_0,b_1,1)^t.           (10)

Its determinant is1, so this includes negative powers. It is one formula for arbitrary fixed signed twist lengths, not a recurrence established by checking finitely many powers.

4. Explicit norm-one coordinate and the source punctures

The fixed-Casimir conic is

b^2+z^2+sigma bz+2A(b+z)+A^2-(sigma-2)P=0.          (11)

Assume sigma^2!=4 and C0=(sigma-2)[P-A^2/(sigma+2)] !=0. Put

u=b+2A/(sigma+2), v=z+2A/(sigma+2),
lambda^2+sigma lambda+1=0.

Then

(u-lambda v)(u-lambda^-1 v)=C0.                    (12)

The involution is lambda<->lambda^-1. The conic is a torsor for the norm-one group of this quadratic spectral algebra, and the actual map (9) multiplies the first factor by lambda and the second by lambda^-1. In particular it is diagonal after this spectral cover. There is even an explicit norm trivialization:

w=((lambda+1)/(lambda-1))*(x+lambda y),
Norm(w)=C0,
a=(u-lambda v)/w, Norm(a)=1, a ->lambda a.           (13)

To verify the norm, use Norm((lambda+1)/(lambda-1))=-(sigma-2)/(sigma+2) and Norm(x+lambda y)=x^2-sigma xy+y^2. Both factors are units under the stated generic hypotheses. The inverse crossing gives a->lambda^-1 a in the same phase-space convention (6). Thus arbitrary signed powers are explicit multiplication by lambda^n.

The reference point a=1 is b=-x,z=-y. It lies on EVERY conic, but is an EXCLUDED SOURCE POINT: P-bz=0, f(b)=f(z)=0. Thus (13) is an algebraic norm trivialization; it does not make the actual source regular at its reference point. On the spectral cover,

u=[lambda bar(w)/a-lambda^-1 w a]/(lambda-lambda^-1),
v=[bar(w)/a-w a]/(lambda-lambda^-1).

The regular SOURCE STATIONARY chart is the complement of (5) after this substitution. These are divisors of that classical critical chart, not new poles of the finite homogeneous polynomial H_R. In particular the possibly coincident basepoints b,z=(-x,-y),(-y,-x) have norm coordinates 1 and (y+lambda x)/(x+lambda y). Their adjacent lambda and lambda^-1 translates give the f(b) and f(z) zeros. The b=0,z=0 divisors are retained too. A word analyzed in this regular critical chart must retain the preimages of these divisors under ALL its intermediate powers. Transport of the completed algebraic conic is not permission to move the original source contour through them.

5. Exact Darboux factor and existing quantum Gaussian

For this coordinate calculation always use the POSITIVE companion z=[P-r f(b)]/b as a rational coordinate on the common phase space, regardless of the sign of the next actual crossing. Then, for fixed x,y, the actual cotangent two-form is

omega=dLog r wedge dLog b=db wedge dz/(P-bz).

Let F be the left side of (11) and eta=u-lambda v. Then

d sigma=(F_b db+F_z dz)/(P-bz),
lambda F_b+F_z=(lambda-lambda^-1)eta,
d sigma=-(lambda-lambda^-1)dLog lambda.

These imply, with NO factor2 or sign ambiguity,

omega=dLog lambda wedge dLog eta
  =dLog lambda wedge dLog a.                   (14)

The scalar w depends only on lambda at fixed x,y and thus contributes zero to the wedge. With the explicit convention i_X omega=-dH, H=(Log lambda)^2/2 generates the displayed pullback advance a->lambda a. This agrees with (6); the opposite input-to-output interpretation reverses that map.

This recovers an explicit classical coordinate for the EXISTING full-Hermite Gaussian functional calculus, rather than replacing it. The existing Gaussian theorem, section1, proves R(2P)=q(P d^2/2) exp[P K(Omega)/hbar], where K(sigma)=arccosh(-sigma/2)^2 is analytic off[2,infinity) with its removable value at sigma=-2. The same node and derivative check gives for every integer n

R^n=q^(n d^2/4) exp[n K(Omega)/(2hbar)].             (15)

Indeed at c_j=q(2j+1)+q(-2j-1), with x_j=pi i(2j+1)/N, K(c_j)=(x_j-pi i)^2, and the actual normalized crossing value is nu_j=exp[pi i(j^2-dj)/N]. This uses (-1)^j=exp(-pi i j), the unwrapped sign required by the original derivative. The derivative of the exponential at the node is the literal multiplier -n(d-2j)/[2(q(2j+1)-q(-2j-1))]; paired nodes retain this derivative, and the odd simple node needs no quotient0/0. Values and derivatives therefore agree in the full Casimir quotient. Negative n are allowed because all crossing eigenvalues are units.

On the classical spectral cover K=(Log lambda)^2, so (14),(15) give the same Hamiltonian, with the recorded scalar factors unchanged. The finite ratio nu_(j+1)/nu_j=-q^(2j+2) has principal limit lambda=-exp(2pi i j/N), a root of lambda^2+sigma lambda+1=0. Its extra finite q shift is not dropped from (15) or from the kernels. Neither the unit-modulus node values nor the torus action bounds the full quantum operator or its shared-face coefficient.

6. The two endpoint fibers are different

At sigma=-2, with A!=0, the original conic is the reduced parabola

(b-z)^2+2A(b+z)+A^2+4P=0.

Put t=b-z. Then the actual affine map gives t->t+2A, and

b=-[(t-A)^2+4P]/(4A), z=-[(t+A)^2+4P]/(4A).

The affine matrix M_-2 satisfies (M_-2-I)^3=0, and its square is nonzero when A!=0. Thus

M_-2^n=I+n(M_-2-I)+n(n-1)(M_-2-I)^2/2              (16)

for EVERY integer n. This is concrete nilpotent/unipotent classical transport at the parabolic Casimir, with quadratic face dependence. It does not imply polynomial growth of an original quantum period.

At sigma=+2 the SCHEME (11) is the nonreduced double line

(b+z+A)^2=0.

Its reduced line has z=-b-A and the map is the involution (b,z)->(z,b). The full affine matrix retains its Jordan data: (M_2+I)^2(M_2-I)=0. Explicitly, if e=b+z+A with e^2=0, then M_2^2(b,z)=(b-2e,z+2e). Thus the reduction loses a real normal transport even though it is an involution on ordinary points. Do not call this fiber the same additive torsor as sigma=-2, and do not replace the double-line scheme by its reduction when retaining normal data. The regular one-layer critical chart excludes the reduced line because L0=0. This is a degeneration of the closure.

For sigma^2!=4 but C0=0, (12) is a pair of lines rather than a norm-one torsor. Formula(10) still gives exact algebraic transport. The generic torus theorem does not assert that this reducible fiber is nonsingular.
