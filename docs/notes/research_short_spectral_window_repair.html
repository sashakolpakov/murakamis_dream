A single spectral representative on short physical windows

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The common positive-product baseline has exponentially small errors in its actual physical value and normal data. On sufficiently short windows, ordinary spectral Hermite interpolation repairs those errors throughout the fixed complex product domain. Its exponential cost tends to zero with the number of rows divided by the level. This gives one separately even representative with the original action bound on both angular sides. Its closed window trace has no real repair interface.

This does not remove exterior window sides. Adjacent choices have exponentially small normalized differences, whose actual shared spectators must still be included in any estimate of the seam.

1. The baseline and its spectral symmetry

Fix a positive pair and the symmetric buffered product domains of the pinned product-gluing theorem. Work on a fixed smaller product, retaining a larger symmetric product for every estimate. All these choices precede the level NN. Write z=−2cos⁡t,w=−2cos⁡u,ψ(t,u)=ψ(−t,u)=ψ(t,−u).(1) z=-2\cos t,\qquad w=-2\cos u,\qquad \psi(t,u)=\psi(-t,u)=\psi(t,-u). \qquad\text{(1)} The phase is the original one, on its single branch from completeness. Let HN(t,u)H_N(t,u) be the phase-normalized common baseline: UN=eNψHN,supK|HN|≤eoK(N).(2) U_N=e^{N\psi}H_N,\qquad \sup_K|H_N|\le e^{o_K(N)} . \qquad\text{(2)} The bulk seed intervals are chosen inside the small complete disk, as in the pinned fold extension. Its complete-disk comparison gives HN−e−NψKN(−2cos⁡t,−2cos⁡u)=O(e−cN)(3) H_N-e^{-N\psi}K_N(-2\cos t,-2\cos u)=O(e^{-cN}) \qquad\text{(3)} on a fixed smaller complete bidisk, with all fixed buffered derivatives. Only in that bidisk is the canonical off-grid kernel used.

Lemma 1. On every fixed smaller symmetric product compact, the separately even average H¯N(t,u)=14∑ϵ,ϵ′∈{1,−1}HN(ϵt,ϵ′u)(4) \overline H_N(t,u)=\frac14 \sum_{\epsilon,\epsilon'\in\{1,-1\}} H_N(\epsilon t,\epsilon'u) \qquad\text{(4)} satisfies H¯N−HN=O(e−cKN)\overline H_N-H_N=O(e^{-c_KN}), with fixed derivatives. It descends to a holomorphic function hN(z,w)h_N(z,w) on the corresponding spectral product domain. The phase descends there to Ψ(z,w)\Psi(z,w).

Proof. Each reflection difference is exponentially small on the complete bidisk by (3), since the canonical kernel and phase are separately even. It is holomorphic and bounded by eo(N)e^{o(N)} on every buffered compact of the common symmetric product by (2). The finite complex-ball propagation argument in the pinned fold extension therefore makes it exponentially small on any fixed smaller product compact. Averaging proves the first assertion. This is an estimate of the actual baseline’s symmetry defect, not an assumed symmetry of a one-sided repair.

Inside |ℜt|<π|\Re t|<\pi, the fibers of −2cos⁡t-2\cos t are precisely the pairs t,−tt,-t, except for their meeting at zero. The domains are symmetric. An even holomorphic function therefore descends; at zero its even Taylor series is holomorphic in z+2z+2. Apply this separately in both variables, also to the original even phase. Compact spectral subsets have buffered preimages in the angular domains, so spectral Cauchy estimates retain the stated bounds. ▫\square

Choose fixed eligible real row intervals: either the bulk intervals of the product-baseline theorem or the fold neighborhoods of its pinned extension. On each such interval, those theorems and Lemma 1 give exponentially small normalized line errors, uniformly on a buffered compact in the other spectral variable. In spectral coordinates the targets are Tj,0(w)=e−NΨ(cj,w)Fj(w),(7) T_{j,0}(w)=e^{-N\Psi(c_j,w)}F_j(w), \qquad\text{(7)} Tj,1(w)=e−NΨ(cj,w){Ĝj(w)−NΨz(cj,w)Fj(w)},νj=2.(8) T_{j,1}(w)=e^{-N\Psi(c_j,w)} \{\widehat G_j(w)-N\Psi_z(c_j,w)F_j(w)\}, \qquad \nu_j=2. \qquad\text{(8)} The errors Tj,a−∂zahN(cj,w)T_{j,a}-\partial_z^a h_N(c_j,w), a<νja<\nu_j, are O(e−cN)O(e^{-cN}). 3. One exact representative on both sides

Fix a buffered spectral product compact as in Section 1, enlarged within the same domains to contain the eligible real row intervals as well as the target. Let INI_N and JN′J_N' be consecutive subwindows of the eligible row intervals, with at most δzN\delta_zN and δwN\delta_wN distinct rows respectively. The window widths are fixed positive numbers, independent of NN, chosen only after the exponent cc in Section 1. Require ωz(δz)+ωw(δw)<c/4.(20) \omega_z(\delta_z)+\omega_w(\delta_w)<c/4. \qquad\text{(20)} Use the interpolation bases (9)–(10) in each variable; write Wk,b(w)W_{k,b}(w) for the second basis. Set dj,a(w)=Tj,a(w)−∂zahN(cj,w),ek,b(z)=Sk,b(z)−∂wbhN(z,ck).(21) d_{j,a}(w)=T_{j,a}(w)-\partial_z^ah_N(c_j,w),\qquad e_{k,b}(z)=S_{k,b}(z)-\partial_w^bh_N(z,c_k). \qquad\text{(21)} The actual full mixed physical jets give exactly cjkab:=∂wbdj,a(ck)=∂zaek,b(cj),a<νj,b<νk.(22) c_{jk}^{ab}:=\partial_w^bd_{j,a}(c_k) =\partial_z^ae_{k,b}(c_j), \quad a<\nu_j,\ b<\nu_k . \qquad\text{(22)} At a simple endpoint only the value index occurs. In particular (22) does not complete that endpoint by an artificial spectral normal.

Define Ez(z,w)=∑j,adj,a(w)Vj,a(z),Ew(z,w)=∑k,bek,b(z)Wk,b(w),Ezw(z,w)=∑j,k,a,bcjkabVj,a(z)Wk,b(w),𝒦I,J,N(z,w)=eNΨ(z,w){hN(z,w)+Ez(z,w)+Ew(z,w)−Ezw(z,w)}.(23) \begin{split} E_z(z,w)&=\sum_{j,a}d_{j,a}(w)V_{j,a}(z),\\ E_w(z,w)&=\sum_{k,b}e_{k,b}(z)W_{k,b}(w),\\ E_{zw}(z,w)&=\sum_{j,k,a,b}c_{jk}^{ab} V_{j,a}(z)W_{k,b}(w),\\ \mathcal K_{I,J,N}(z,w) &=e^{N\Psi(z,w)} \{h_N(z,w)+E_z(z,w)+E_w(z,w)-E_{zw}(z,w)\}. \end{split} \qquad\text{(23)} Every coefficient function is defined on the buffered spectral parameter domain from Section 1; the polynomial bases introduce no extra poles.

Theorem 3. On the entire fixed smaller spectral product compact, e−NΨ𝒦I,J,N=hN+O(e−c′N),|𝒦I,J,N|≤exp⁡{NℜΨ+o(N)}(24) e^{-N\Psi}\mathcal K_{I,J,N} =h_N+O(e^{-c'N}),\qquad |\mathcal K_{I,J,N}| \le \exp\{N\Re\Psi+o(N)\} \qquad\text{(24)} for some c′>0c'>0, with all fixed buffered spectral derivatives of the normalized error. This holds on both angular sides, without a designated sign or a restriction to a thin opposite collar. Its exact line data are 𝒦I,J,N(cj,w)=Fj(w),∂z𝒦I,J,N(cj,w)=Ĝj(w)(νj=2),𝒦I,J,N(z,ck)=Fk(z),∂w𝒦I,J,N(z,ck)=Ĝk(z)(νk=2).(25) \begin{split} \mathcal K_{I,J,N}(c_j,w)&=F_j(w),& \partial_z\mathcal K_{I,J,N}(c_j,w)&=\widehat G_j(w) \quad(\nu_j=2),\\ \mathcal K_{I,J,N}(z,c_k)&=F_k(z),& \partial_w\mathcal K_{I,J,N}(z,c_k)&=\widehat G_k(z) \quad(\nu_k=2). \end{split} \qquad\text{(25)} The angular pullback is separately even.

Proof. The coefficients in (21)–(22) are O(e−cN)O(e^{-cN}), with the exponent fixed before (20). Lemma 2 controls Ez,EwE_z,E_w; its two operator bounds control EzwE_{zw}. There are only polynomially many terms. Equation (20) leaves a positive exponential margin, proving (24), also on fixed derivative buffers. The baseline satisfies (2).

At a first node, the first correction supplies its target error. By (11) and (22), the jets of EwE_w are exactly those of EzwE_{zw} and cancel. The second-node argument is identical. Removing the normalizing exponential in (7)–(8) proves (25). These are identities as holomorphic functions of the other variable, not merely identities at isolated physical pairs. The spectral construction proves evenness. ▫\square

The small-window choice is essential to the argument: the exponential accuracy is established on a larger fixed row interval before its short subwindow is selected. No assertion makes that accuracy exponent independent of an interval simultaneously shrinking in its construction. For a fixed finite collection of such choices using the same hNh_N, (24) also makes every normalized difference exponentially small on the common compact. The smallest of their finitely many positive exponents may be used.

4. Exact closed traces and the remaining boundary terms

Thus (26) is the same sharp window expression as in the pinned sources. Its difference from the original free-ww window trace is PN(w)P_N(w) times a holomorphic slit function, and equality with the original follows after the required subsequent complete second trace, including every spectator normal. If both endpoints are traced on the selected windows, (22) gives the original double-window functional directly. No arbitrary unreduced free polynomial is replaced.

For a centered mirror angular window, (26) equivalently has the prefactor −η/(4π2i)-\eta/(4\pi^2i) against Dh(X)sin⁡(2πX)P(C(X))𝒦I,J,N(C(X),w)dX,Dh=(πN)2sec⁡2(πNX).(29) D_h(X)\sin(2\pi X) P(C(X))\mathcal K_{I,J,N}(C(X),w)\,dX, \qquad D_h=(\pi N)^2\sec^2(\pi NX). \qquad\text{(29)} It uses the same function on the lower and upper halves. Consequently the real interface term is exactly zero. Both lifts of a double node and the single odd central value have the multiplicity proved in the fold source; no spectral normal is supplied at that center.

This cancellation concerns the repair interface only. The closed contour still has all its exterior sides. If neighboring cells use different short-window representatives, their common side contains their actual difference. Equation (24) bounds that difference by exp⁡{NℜΨ−c′N+o(N)}\exp\{N\Re\Psi-c'N+o(N)\}, but it must be multiplied by the entire original spectator, trace density, and any remaining factors. A larger real boundary action need not be paid by c′c'. No seam is deleted without that further estimate.

The selected windows may cover a supplied compact set of rows inside the eligible intervals by finitely many cells. This gives a finite family of compatible estimates; it does not assert one exact representative for the union with the same bound, a complete grid near t=±πt=\pm\pi, or a controlled global filled cycle. In particular (25) prescribes only the selected short windows, not the full data of an entire periodic angular strip.
