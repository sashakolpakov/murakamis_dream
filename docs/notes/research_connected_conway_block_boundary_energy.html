Boundary energy of a connected Conway block

21 November 2026. A connected collection of short pieces can be removed from the normalized Conway energy while retaining its exact full boundary map. This note proves a pointwise comparison, before the remaining boundary packets are optimized. The comparison factor is uniform in the level, the block size, and the chosen fixed filling coefficients.

The boundary map itself remains coherent and marked. The theorem does not replace it by an operator norm or prove that its geometric closing coefficient is nonzero.

1. A block with clamped input and output packets

Use the complete boundary spaces, original markings and normalized local maps of the convex source theorem. Thus every leaf and linear or bilinear map has norm at most σ=1/16\sigma=1/16. The per-vertex budgets and their original product BtotB_{\mathrm{ tot}} are retained. In particular a normalized product is the actual full Newton multiplication divided by its known local budget. All budgets are held fixed while boundary packets vary. No repeated-root derivative is discarded.

Let Ω\Omega be a nonempty connected set of vertices in the rooted compiler tree. It has one top vertex ω\omega, with one outgoing boundary leg. Every child leg entering Ω\Omega from below is a cut input leg. Write its clamped packet as uju_j, and assume ∥uj∥≤1\|u_j\|\le1. Inputs may also be prescribed normalized leaf packets.

Clamp the top output to a packet yy, with ∥y∥≤1\|y\|\le1, in the top output Hilbert space. All other packets zvz_v, for v∈Ω\{ω}v\in\Omega\setminus\{\omega\}, are internal variables in their unit balls. In each local recursion put xω=yx_\omega=y, xv=zvx_v=z_v at internal vertices, and use uju_j at cut inputs. Define SΩ(z;u,y)=∑v∈Ω∥Φv((xc)c)−xv∥2,CΩ(u,y)=min∥zv∥≤1SΩ(z;u,y).(1) S_\Omega(z;u,y) =\sum_{v\in\Omega}\|\Phi_v((x_c)_c)-x_v\|^2,\qquad C_\Omega(u,y)=\min_{\|z_v\|\le1}S_\Omega(z;u,y). \qquad\text{(1)} When there are no internal variables, this is the displayed local squared residual itself.

The block may contain the physical closing root. In that case its normalized local map is the original row LL, its output space is ℂ\mathbb C, and its clamped output is y=0y=0. The root residual is then |Lxv0|2|Lx_{v_0}|^2, exactly as in the original energy. For the estimates below one can also let this scalar yy vary over its unit disk.

Let FΩ(u)F_\Omega(u) be the exact normalized composite obtained by performing the recursions inside Ω\Omega. It retains every original phase and all cut boundary coordinates. Since the cut inputs have norm at most one, every exact internal packet and FΩ(u)F_\Omega(u) have norm at most σ\sigma.

2. Internal elimination and the output Hessian

Theorem. For each fixed clamped input uu, the internal minimum in (1) exists uniquely and is interior. The boundary value is smooth in the real and imaginary output coordinates, with 6332I≤Dy2CΩ(u,y)≤2I.(2) \frac{63}{32}I\le D_y^2C_\Omega(u,y)\le2I. \qquad\text{(2)} Consequently, with c=63/64c=63/64, c∥y−FΩ(u)∥2≤CΩ(u,y)≤∥y−FΩ(u)∥2.(3) \boxed{\quad c\|y-F_\Omega(u)\|^2\le C_\Omega(u,y) \le\|y-F_\Omega(u)\|^2. \quad} \qquad\text{(3)} All constants are independent of NN, the number of block vertices, and the filling coefficients after the stated budgets are retained.

Proof. First let every vertex output in Ω\Omega, including the top output, vary in its unit ball, while the cut inputs remain fixed. The residual Jacobian is −I+A-I+A. Each child block has norm at most σ\sigma, each row has at most two such blocks, and each child appears in at most one parent row. Hence ∥A∥≤2σ≤1/8\|A\|\le\sqrt2\sigma\le1/8. The half-Hessian residual-curvature term is bounded in absolute value by σ(1+σ)\sigma(1+\sigma) times the squared variation norm: at a product it is 2Re⁡⟨ℛv,Φv(ξL,ξR)⟩, 2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal R_v, \Phi_v(\xi_L,\xi_R)\rangle, and distinct parents have disjoint sibling pairs. At y=FΩ(u)y=F_\Omega(u), the exact recursion gives zero cost. The first output derivative is therefore zero there. The segment from this point to any allowed yy stays in the unit ball. Integrating (2) twice along that segment proves (3). ▫\square

The output derivative has the concrete boundary-flux formula ∇yCΩ(u,y)=2{y−Φω(z*(u,y),u)}.(6) \nabla_y C_\Omega(u,y) =2\{y-\Phi_\omega(z_*(u,y),u)\}. \qquad\text{(6)} Its half differs from y−FΩ(u)y-F_\Omega(u) by at most ∥y−FΩ(u)∥/64\|y-F_\Omega(u)\|/64, by integrating (2) once. 3. Exact linear corridors

When the clamped block is a chain of normalized linear maps AiA_i, the boundary energy has an explicit formula. This includes turns and multiplication by a fixed retained packet, with every full Newton coordinate present. Suppose x0=u,xL=y,S=∑i=1L∥xi−Aixi−1∥2,∥Ai∥≤σ. x_0=u,\quad x_L=y,\qquad S=\sum_{i=1}^L\|x_i-A_ix_{i-1}\|^2,\qquad \|A_i\|\le\sigma. Put Pi=ALAL−1⋯Ai+1,PL=I,W=∑i=1LPiPi*. P_i=A_LA_{L-1}\cdots A_{i+1},\quad P_L=I,\qquad W=\sum_{i=1}^LP_iP_i^*. Then C(u,y)=⟨y−AL⋯A1u,W−1(y−AL⋯A1u)⟩.(7) C(u,y)=\langle y-A_L\cdots A_1u,\, W^{-1}(y-A_L\cdots A_1u)\rangle. \qquad\text{(7)} Indeed the errors ri=xi−Aixi−1r_i=x_i-A_ix_{i-1} satisfy y−AL⋯A1u=∑iPiriy-A_L\cdots A_1u=\sum_iP_ir_i. This proves (7) without ball constraints. The internal minimum with balls is interior by the preceding theorem. It satisfies the same strictly convex quadratic stationary equations, so it is also the unconstrained minimum and (7) is valid with the original balls.

In particular I≤W≤256255I,255256I≤W−1≤I.(8) I\le W\le\frac{256}{255}I,\qquad \frac{255}{256}I\le W^{-1}\le I. \qquad\text{(8)} These follow from ∥Pi∥≤σL−i\|P_i\|\le\sigma^{L-i} and a geometric series. No inverse of an individual AiA_i is used; the maps can be singular. The metric estimate concerns the residual from the exact transported center. It is not a lower bound on the norm of that center.

4. Several short blocks in the original source

Choose any collection of pairwise vertex-disjoint connected blocks. Retain every block output and every cut input as boundary variables, except for the physical root output, which is fixed at zero if present. Eliminate all other variables inside the blocks. Original local terms outside the blocks remain in a nonnegative function AA. For fixed remaining boundary packets, exact elimination gives Ered=A+∑jCΩj(uj,yj). E_{\mathrm{ red}}=A+\sum_jC_{\Omega_j}(u_j,y_j). There is no remaining coupling through an eliminated variable: a vertex outside a connected block can see only that block’s retained top output. Define the compressed residual energy Ecomp=A+∑j∥yj−FΩj(uj)∥2.(9) E_{\mathrm{ comp}} =A+\sum_j\|y_j-F_{\Omega_j}(u_j)\|^2. \qquad\text{(9)} Each composite map uses the same normalized gates as before. In particular BtotB_{\mathrm{ tot}} is unchanged; no new budget is silently assigned to a fused map.

Equation (3), together with A≥0A\ge0, gives the pointwise inequalities cEcomp≤Ered≤Ecomp. cE_{\mathrm{ comp}}\le E_{\mathrm{ red}}\le E_{\mathrm{ comp}}. Taking minima over all remaining unit-ball variables proves cmin⁡Ecomp≤min⁡EN≤min⁡Ecomp.(10) c\min E_{\mathrm{ comp}}\le\min E_N\le\min E_{\mathrm{ comp}}. \qquad\text{(10)} The factor cc occurs once, independently of the number of blocks, because the energies add. No joint input convexity or convexity of EcompE_{\mathrm{ comp}} is used in this argument.

Let γ=197/196\gamma=197/196 be the source constant from the convex theorem. Its original scalar comparison and (10) imply cBtot2min⁡Ecomp≤|KN|2≤γBtot2min⁡Ecomp.(11) \boxed{\quad cB_{\mathrm{ tot}}^2\min E_{\mathrm{ comp}}\le|K_N|^2 \le\gamma B_{\mathrm{ tot}}^2\min E_{\mathrm{ comp}}. \quad} \qquad\text{(11)} Thus absorbing any such collection of fixed short pieces preserves the original exponential rate with a constant loss independent of their number. This is a boundary comparison before the final minimization, not a replacement of the marked invariant by a norm of individual pieces.

5. What this removes, and what remains

The internal energy of an arbitrary connected short block is comparable to its exact boundary residual with uniform constants. This removes an internal metric or assembly loss as a separate exponential obstacle. The fixed filling coefficients can enter the known per-gate budgets and the exact composite maps; all of those factors remain in (11).

What remains is the asymptotic behavior of the compressed source with those actual coherent maps. The estimates do not supply a subexponential bound on an inverse map. Neither (3) nor (11) establishes the map’s value on a geometric boundary field or a nonzero marked closing coefficient. A geometric lower certificate for the remaining boundary problem is still required for the finite-exception volume theorem.
