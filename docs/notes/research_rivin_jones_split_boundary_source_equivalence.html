Recovering a short component from its open matrix contraction

Private research proof, 14 November 2026. The manuscripts remain frozen.

The purpose of this note is to compare two exact descriptions of the same short component: the original Newton boundary polynomial and a contraction in the basis used by Rivin’s braid matrices. A short component may contain any fixed signed crossing powers and any finite number of the original Conway operations. No lower bound on those powers is assumed.

The comparison retains the open boundary, not just a closed invariant. At a free vertex several polynomial factors can meet. Their ordinary product can have degree at least NN, whereas a single pair Casimir remembers a polynomial only modulo PNP_N. We therefore give each free incidence its own pair of matrix indices, recover those separate polynomials, and only then identify their variables. This order is essential.

4. Derivatives and the eventual closing functional

The reconstruction includes the repeated-root derivatives in every independent variable by (7). It also determines derivatives of any fixed order of the unreduced ordinary polynomial in (13). Indeed, ordinary polynomial differentiation gives, for k≥0k\ge0, ∂zukBN=(∑h=(e,u)∂zh)kCN|z(e,v)=zv.(21) \partial_{z_u}^{\,k}B_N= \left. \left(\sum_{h=(e,u)}\partial_{z_h}\right)^k C_N \right|_{z_{(e,v)}=z_v}. \qquad\text{(21)} For an empty incidence set the right-hand side is zero when k>0k>0. The multinomial expansion in (21) includes every way of distributing the derivative among factors at uu. Derivatives at several free vertices follow by taking the product of these commuting differential operators.

There is no ambiguity about subsequent filling. If the remaining signed powers are mum_u, apply the original remaining functional 𝒞𝒎(B)=(⨂u∈Uϕu)[∏u∈UR(zu)muB(𝒛U)].(22) \mathcal C_{\mathbf m}(B)= \left(\bigotimes_{u\in U}\phi_u\right) \left[\prod_{u\in U}R(z_u)^{m_u}\,B(\mathbf z_U)\right]. \qquad\text{(22)} At this final step its arguments are passed to 𝒜N\mathcal A_N, as required by the original trace. Since (13), (17), and (18) recover BNB_N as an ordinary polynomial, (22) is exactly the original completed source scalar. Any prescribed exterior functional applied to that polynomial likewise agrees.

If the completed diagram is instead evaluated as a braid in the external convention, the separate cut lemma gives its exact comparison: the physical word has reversed crossing signs, and the pivot and writhe factors are those of that lemma. They are not absorbed into a positive amplitude. For an exterior that is not an intertwiner, the unrestricted highest-head matrix functional must be retained; its replacement by an averaged trace is not justified.

5. Why a single boundary matrix would have lost information

At one free vertex the map from ordinary polynomials to a single pair operator has kernel (PN)(P_N). The nonzero ordinary polynomial PN(z)P_N(z) is sent to zero. Therefore a boundary product of degree at least NN cannot in general be recovered from one pair operator, even if its values and first derivatives at all Casimir roots are known. Those data recover its class, not the whole ordinary polynomial.

The separate-incidence construction avoids this loss because each of its variables has degree below NN. The tensor product of the faithful maps (5) is injective on that space, and (18) is its explicit inverse. The ordinary diagonal substitution is performed only after this inverse.

For h=|H|h=|H| free incidences there are NhN^h coefficients in (17). Thus the representation has polynomial dimension in NN for each fixed component, with no restriction on its fixed powers. This dimension count is not a bound on the condition number of the reconstruction.

6. What this gives the noncancellation problem

The boundary correspondence removes a concrete ambiguity: the external matrix presentation, its specified closing trace, and the original Newton boundary source now agree exactly, including the derivatives that would be lost by diagonalizing the pair Casimir. The theorem applies to arbitrary fixed short components in the original compositional calculus.

It does not give a lower bound for the geometric contribution after closing. The coefficient observations in (18) form a separating family; the required volume identity uses one prescribed completed contraction. A nonzero vector can have zero value under that contraction. Also, although the single-head change of basis (4) is unitary in the coordinate norms, the Newton reconstruction and the inserted dual observations need not have uniformly bounded condition numbers.

The next analytic statement must therefore control the original geometric observation of (15), with its phases and the original closing functional retained. Exact recoverability of the boundary is available for that statement; a nonzero geometric asymptotic coefficient is not yet a consequence.
