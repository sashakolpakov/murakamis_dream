Cyclotomic descent for rational sources and unipotent torsors

Research proof, 27 October 2026. This addresses the source extension, rather than only its multiplicative norm line. The proof uses the weighted sequence-uniqueness lemma proved in full rank-one descent, and finite flows on rational partial-fraction orbits. No published q-curvature theorem is used as a premise.

The result is uniform in the number of source coordinates. It applies to a specified rational unipotent comparison with whole cyclotomic transport identities. Constructing that comparison for the actual knot period, and verifying its source and peripheral lattice, remain necessary. The volume conjecture is not proved by this conditional descent theorem.

1. An additive descent theorem

Let k be the rational number field, let sigma(Y)=QY on k(Q,Y), and take c in k(Q,Y). Suppose that for every sufficiently large integer N,

sum_(j=0)^(N-1) c(zeta_N,zeta_N^j Y)=0,
zeta_N=exp(2pi*i/N),                          (1)

as an identity of whole rational functions of Y. Then

c(Q,Y)=b(Q,QY)-b(Q,Y)                         (2)

for some b in k(Q,Y). Conversely (2) implies (1) at every level where its rational specializations are defined.

There are no hypotheses on the ramification or limiting values of the moving poles at Q=1. The ambiguity in b is precisely k(Q). The theorem does not assert regularity of b at the ideal meridian.

The same proof works over a subfield k of C if (1) is given at every primitive root for the chosen coefficient embedding. For rational coefficients this follows from Galois conjugation. For a number field, knowing the chosen principal root alone is not silently treated as knowing all its coefficient conjugates.

2. Partial fractions and the charges seen by a root fiber

Over a finite algebraic splitting field of k(Q), write uniquely

c(Q,Y)=sum_m c_m(Q)Y^m
      +sum_(mu,r>=1) a_(mu,r)(Q)/(1-mu(Q)Y)^r. (3)

The first sum is a finite Laurent polynomial; all mu are nonzero algebraic functions, and the second sum is finite. The partial fraction part is proper at infinity and has no pole at zero. All identities below omit the finitely many root levels at which a coefficient specialization fails or two distinct pole functions coincide. A nonzero algebraic function has only finitely many such specialization zeros on its finite algebraic cover.

At a primitive N-th root set U=Y^N and D_U=U partial_U. For r>=1,

sum_j (1-mu zeta_N^j Y)^(-r)
  =N/(r-1)! product_(ell=1)^(r-1)(N D_U+ell)
                                     (1-mu^N U)^(-1). (4)

For r=1 this is the elementary geometric-series identity, first as a power series and then as a rational identity. Applying the operators (D_Y+ell) proves the general formula. Its pole at U=mu^(-N) has exact order r with nonzero highest coefficient.

Terms with the same mu^N in (4) give exactly the same rational function. Compare the highest remaining pole order in (1), then descend in r. At every cluster C of equal values mu(zeta_N)^N,

sum_(mu in C) a_(mu,r)(zeta_N)=0              (5)

for each r. Laurent polynomials have no finite nonzero poles and do not affect this comparison. In particular, for each r,

sum_mu a_(mu,r)(zeta_N) mu(zeta_N)^N=0.       (6)

For xi_N=exp(2piik/N), with fixed positive integer k, its actual order is m=N/gcd(k,N). Apply (5) at order m and raise the common m-th powers to the gcd(k,N)-th power. This proves

sum_mu a_(mu,r)(xi_N) mu(xi_N)^N=0            (7)

for every sufficiently large N. It does not use a primitive N-step formula at a nonprimitive scaled root.

3. Generic orbit charges from the weighted rigidity lemma

Choose the common convergent Puiseux forms near Q=1 provided by the elementary finite-cover argument in the rank-one note. Put t=Log Q/(2pi*i), and write

mu(exp(2pi*i*t))=a_mu t^(nu_mu) exp(lambda_mu(t^(1/e))),
A_mu(t)=exp(lambda_mu(t^(1/e))/t),             (8)

where a_mu!=0, nu_mu is rational, and lambda_mu(0)=0. The coefficients a_(mu,r)(exp(2piit)) are meromorphic Puiseux germs after the same further finite ramification.

At xi_N, (7) becomes a sum of terms

N^(-nu_mu N)(a_mu k^(nu_mu))^N
             a_(mu,r)(exp(2pi*i*k/N)) A_mu(k/N)^k. (9)

The weighted exponential–Puiseux uniqueness lemma separates every pair (nu_mu,a_mu). It applies to the partial-fraction weights as well as to the exponentials; those weights need not be units. For each such pair, and every positive integer k, it gives the identity of punctured analytic germs

sum_(mu in pair) a_(mu,r)(exp(2pi*i*t)) A_mu(t)^k=0. (10)

Here the argument was rescaled from kt to t after applying the lemma. These are identities of functions, not only formal jets.

Group equal functions A_mu. If A_1,…,A_h are the distinct nonzero functions in one pair and w_i is the sum of its weights, then (10) for k=1,…,h has coefficient matrix (A_i^k). Its determinant is a nonzero product of the A_i and their pairwise differences in the field of meromorphic functions on the ramified punctured disk. Hence w_i=0 for every i.

Within a fixed (nu,a), equality A_mu=A_eta is equivalent to

mu(Q)/eta(Q)=Q^v for an integer v.            (11)

Indeed exp((lambda_mu-lambda_eta)/t)=1 forces the exponent to be a constant in 2pi*i Z on the connected punctured cover. Conversely (11) has this property. Thus the generic additive orbit charges vanish:

sum_(mu in each Q-shift orbit) a_(mu,r)(Q)=0  (12)

for every pole order r. The rational-function identities follow from the analytic germ identities because these coefficients are algebraic functions of Q.

4. A finite flow solves the transport equation

On one infinite orbit write mu_v=Q^v mu_0, and set a_v equal to the coefficient in (3), or zero if that pole is absent. The a_v have finite support and sum zero. Define

e_v=-sum_(u<=v) a_u.

Then e_v has finite support, and

b_orbit=sum_v e_v/(1-Q^v mu_0 Y)^r

satisfies sigma(b_orbit)-b_orbit=sum_v a_v/(1-Q^v mu_0 Y)^r. The equality follows coefficientwise from e_(v-1)-e_v=a_v. This flow is unique among finite-support flows on that orbit.

The total proper partial-fraction solution b_pole descends to k(Q,Y). One way to see this is to apply any splitting-field automorphism tau. The difference tau(b_pole)-b_pole is invariant under Y->QY. A rational invariant has no finite nonzero poles, since their Q-orbits would be infinite; its remaining Laurent polynomial is therefore constant in Y. Both proper parts vanish at infinity, so that constant is zero. Thus b_pole is Galois invariant.

Subtract its coboundary from c. The remaining Laurent polynomial still has zero cyclic sum at all sufficiently large roots. Its nonzero powers sum to zero once N exceeds the largest absolute value of their nonzero exponents, so its constant coefficient obeys N c_0(zeta_N)=0 at infinitely many Q-values. Consequently c_0=0. Finally

b_Laurent=sum_(m!=0) c_m(Q)Y^m/(Q^m-1)

solves the Laurent part. This proves (2), its field of definition, and the stated constant ambiguity. The construction does not throw away the factors Q^m-1 or any Y-meridian factor in b.

5. Unipotent and Heisenberg descent

Let C(Q,Y) be a d-by-d upper unitriangular rational matrix. Use the column transport equation

sigma(v)=C v,
C_[N]=sigma^(N-1)(C)...sigma(C) C.            (13)

If C_[N] specializes to I at Q=zeta_N for every sufficiently large N, then there is a rational upper unitriangular G such that

C=sigma(G) G^(-1).                          (14)

To prove this, filter strictly upper triangular matrices by their distance above the diagonal. In the first surviving diagonal of C-I, multiplication adds the entries, so (13) says exactly that their cyclic sums vanish. Solve each by the additive theorem and gauge by a unitriangular matrix carrying those solutions. This kills that diagonal. The cyclic curvature remains identity, because it transforms by conjugation with the specialized G. Repeat through the finite d-1 diagonals. The process terminates and gives (14). Only finitely many bad root levels are introduced by each fixed rational gauge.

For the Heisenberg matrix

C=[1 u w; 0 1 v; 0 0 1],
G=[1 p r; 0 1 q; 0 0 1],

the equations are explicitly

sigma(p)-p=u,
sigma(q)-q=v,
sigma(r)-r=w+u q.                           (15)

The first two are additive. With these solved, set

G0=[1 p 0;0 1 q;0 0 1].

Then sigma(G0)^(-1) C G0 has only central entry w+u q. Its cyclic curvature is identity, so the additive theorem solves the last equation. This retains the central cross term; solving only the two abelian equations would miss the regulator coordinate.

Two solutions of (14) differ on the right by a constant upper unitriangular matrix over k(Q). A regular specified anchor can fix that ambiguity. A generic gauge alone need not preserve the integral cut lattice or the complete meridian specialization.

The combination with multiplicative descent also proves the same existence assertion for every invertible upper triangular rational C, with G upper triangular. Each diagonal entry has cyclic norm one, so the rank-one theorem supplies its rational diagonal gauge. Apply that diagonal gauge first; the remaining matrix is upper unitriangular and the finite induction above applies. This covers a rationally split full flag without assuming unipotence in advance.

In particular an affine source over such a flagged homogeneous module is handled by the augmented triangular matrix

C_aff=[C c; 0 1].

If its full cyclic curvature is identity, the theorem solves its source together with the homogeneous transport. A rational gauge can be chosen with bottom row (0,…,0,1), so its top-right column is precisely an affine particular solution. This is stronger than solving the homogeneous norm while leaving the source arbitrary.

6. What this supplies to the proposed A-torsor argument

This gives the requested group-theoretic termination in a precise setting: finite unipotence makes all transport equations solvable after their whole cyclotomic curvature has vanished. It includes the Heisenberg group underlying the marked regulator torsor, and is independent of crossing number, bridge number, or knot family.

It does not infer that a general quantum A-module is unipotent. If the relevant comparison is a rank-one homogeneous line with a source, first trivialize the line by the full multiplicative theorem, then apply the additive theorem to its transformed source. More generally the triangular result handles every rationally split full flag and its affine source. A flag appearing only on a formal normal completion has not thereby supplied that rational flag.

Rivin’s positive maximizer supplies a marked algebraic geometric branch. The theorem here would descend an already constructed rational quantum/geometric comparison from its full cyclotomic transport identities, without choosing unrelated levelwise gauges. The actual comparison, its whole transport identities, its source and cut anchor, and the selected regulator norm still have to be proved. Algebraicity of the critical shape does not supply those identities by itself.

The regulator applicability audit makes the word comparison essential. A regular rational family with identity absolute curvature has a rationally trivial differential limit, even if its initial rational gauge is singular at Q=1. The marked regulator’s logarithmic residues exclude such absolute triviality. Nor does equality of two noncentral matrix curvatures make their whole Hom curvature identity. The relative comparison object and its specified flat subobject must be constructed first.
