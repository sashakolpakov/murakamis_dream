Phase exclusion for an original regular spectral window

31 October 2026. The straddling-window theorem in §§1–6 estimates a literal start/end window, retaining every unused row, omitted-half mode and raw sign. Its conclusion is an exponential loss relative to that window’s positive envelope, not a bound by hyperbolic volume. Sections 7–8 give the full-row extension and shared-torus consequence.

4. A mismatched middle separates the whole active block

It remains to consider small outer relative phase. Remove the common outer phase ϑo=ϑy\vartheta_o=\vartheta_y. At exactly equal outer phases, a constant unitary diagonal change of basis makes the active companion eiϑoe^{i\vartheta_o} times its real companion. Both active eigenvalues have this common phase relative to their real, signed values.

On the start window α>0\alpha>0, so the scalar phase is ϑb\vartheta_b. A mismatch from ϑo\vartheta_o separates it from the Perron eigenvalue; the window’s modulus condition separates it from the secondary eigenvalue for every phase. On the stop window α<0\alpha<0, the scalar phase is ϑc+π\vartheta_c+\pi, and the same argument applies. If the quadruple stays away from (5), either (9) applies or at least one of these middle mismatches stays uniformly nonzero. Taking the outer-phase threshold sufficiently small preserves the full Sylvester separation.

The exact coefficients are analytic in tt and h=1/Nh=1/N: αh(t)=4cos⁡2(π(t+h/2))−4sin⁡2(πδ),β(t)=4sin⁡2(πt),γh(t)=4sin⁡(πt)sin⁡(π(t−h))αh(t−h). \begin{aligned} \alpha_h(t)&=4\cos^2(\pi(t+h/2))-4\sin^2(\pi\delta),\\ \beta(t)&=4\sin^2(\pi t),\\ \gamma_h(t)&=4\sin(\pi t)\sin(\pi(t-h))\alpha_h(t-h). \end{aligned}\tag{13} The uniform real separation extends to a fixed complex neighborhood of the relevant window. Apply that theorem to (10) or (11), whichever has the mismatch. Its exact off-diagonal product consists of two pure endpoint terms and a sum with an exponentially small residual coupling. The scalar blocks remain bαrb\alpha_r or cαrc\alpha_r. They do not occur in the same Sylvester equation, so no separation between them is required.

6. Limits of the local conclusion

The proof requires positive ordinary face moduli and strict regular endpoint maxima. It does not cover spectral caps, row-endpoint or pole-touching windows, or a maximum on its window boundary. One zero outer weight reduces the active recurrence to first order and admits a scalar version under the same strict-window assumptions, but that case is not used here. A protected middle needs a different start specification.

Incompatible local phase requirements may permit (4) at one port of a shared torus. They do not by themselves compare the resulting envelope with complete volume or show that these windows cover the full source.

7. Full-row regular windows

The polynomial identity extends to the full numerator by taking M=dM=d and Arestα=1A_{\mathrm{rest}}^\alpha=1 in (3) and (12). It is the same original path sum over 0≤i≤j≤d0\le i\le j\le d, with all outside diagonal factors retained. This proves the full-row version with no assumed nonzero sampled factors. It covers the usual regular sign pairs (+,+)(+,+), (+,−)(+,-), and (−,+)(-,+) when the corresponding strict-window hypotheses hold; no classification of all critical or boundary configurations is required.

The finite window audit and its recorded output check 402 exact half-row and full-row matrix/path identities, including sampled spectral zeros and zero outer weights. This checks the finite dictionary, not the analytical exponential estimate or a new physical-matrix interface.

8. An exact shared-torus consequence

Fix the original incidence graph and protected-face specialization. Let JJ be a finite set of ports satisfying the full-row or half-row window theorem above, with all four incident weights positive. Other ports may have any certified absolute torus majorants Me,N(L)M_{e,N}(L). Use one shared positive radius LfL_f for each ordinary face and the original coefficient target dd there; protected variables remain zero.

For each regular port impose (18) on its incident face phases. These are equations of the form ϑf−ϑg=0\vartheta_f-\vartheta_g=0 or π\pi. They have a simultaneous torus solution exactly when their signed incidence graph has even label sum around every cycle. Thus an inconsistent 𝔽2\mathbb F_2 system has no torus solution. Compactness of the phase torus supplies ε>0\varepsilon>0 such that at every phase point some port in JJ is at least ε\varepsilon from its alignment locus.

Apply (4) at that port and the ordinary positive-envelope bound at all other selected ports. The original Cauchy coefficient formula gives |[original targets]∏e∈JWe,N∏e∉JPe,N|≤Ce−cN∏e∈JEe,N(L)∏e∉JMe,N(L)∏f ordinaryLfd. \left|[\text{original targets}] \prod_{e\in J}W_{e,N}\prod_{e\notin J}P_{e,N}\right| \le Ce^{-cN} \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{e\in J}E_{e,N}(L) \prod_{e\notin J}M_{e,N}(L)} {\displaystyle\prod_{f\text{ ordinary}}L_f^d}. \tag{19} There is no independent choice of radii at each incidence and no alteration of the original target. The homogeneous common phase is harmless. Any external source characters must be retained separately in their original normalization.

Formula (19) is an exponential improvement on the specified shared window envelope, uniform for a fixed diagram and compact regular parameters. It does not show that this envelope is at most eNΦDe^{N\Phi_D}, exclude consistent phase systems, or control the omitted irregular windows.
