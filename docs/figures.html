The route to volume equality

Exact source formulas, joint local complex amplitudes and the marked geometric branch are now available at arbitrary fixed width under their stated hypotheses. The diagram highlights the remaining global selection and estimate problem. The frontier states the precise gap; the results chapter states the families already covered by the recorded proofs.

The joint local domain

The blue region shows the limiting full strict physical domain in the real parts of the two wire coordinates. Both the pair degree and magnetic coordinate may vary complexly. The seam and outer boundary are excluded. At finite level the proved zero-free region also excludes a band of width 1/(2N)1/(2N) on each side of the seam. Every fixed compact in the limiting domain is covered eventually.

Two exceptional factors at arbitrary bridge width

Under the geometric strip conditions, the common framing flags determine a line through the convex hull of the normalized white face normals. A generic line meets exactly two facets. The framing obstruction and localization prove that these are exactly the two local factors failing strict physical support. The two facets can be prescribed, including those adjacent to the original marked physical edge. The selected-factor theorem now puts every other ordinary factor on the selected full physical packet. The exceptional block and the global contraction still need a sharp estimate.

The exceptional operator and its selected scalar action

The scalar polynomial and original resolvent have different analytic roles. All polynomial zeros lie above 22, while the physical Casimir nodes lie in [−2,2][-2,2]. The actual geometric target is at most −2-2 under the marked complete certificate. The exact contour retains both factors and their generic normals. Endpoint estimates at +2+2 do not prove the global saddle contribution or settle a parabolic target at −2-2. See the current critical path.

A change of pairing

Four external colors stay fixed while the internal channel changes. This is the local operation used to transport the source through a diagram. The mechanism chapter explains the normalization and why local control must be assembled into a global estimate.

The cube experiment

The four blue rungs are summed in the proved packet, while the eight orange cycle labels remain fixed. The cube discussion records the conclusions and the remaining full-source problem.

The local geometric cell

The blue triangles are polar truncation faces. The hyperbolic metric is specified by the face Gram matrix, not by the Euclidean proportions of this drawing. See the local geometric argument.

The Conway cells

The source leaf is an ideal quadrangular pyramid and multiplication gives an ideal triangular prism. Blue quadrilateral ports carry the matching data; the shaded leaf triangles retain the crossing-disk pairing. The global geometry chapter explains their assembly into the actual complement.

Peripheral marking and the torsor

The left panel draws a primitive filling curve on a schematic torus fundamental domain. Opposite sides are identified; the amber segments join into one loop in class μ+2λ\mu+2\lambda. The drawing does not prescribe a Euclidean cusp shape.

The right panel shows the local sheets of the square-root cover ℓ2=L\ell^2=L, the branch selected by the source, and the integral monomial shear on that cover. It is a local picture, not a claim that the torsor splits globally. The peripheral geometry discussion explains why the primitive marking must be retained through filling.
