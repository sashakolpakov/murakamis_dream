# From the original source to a volume limit

## Preserve the source before taking a limit

The invariant is a specified contraction of quantum operators, cups, caps, and a marked functional. A formula with the same classical A-curve can still represent a different quantum solution or select a different period. The pointed structure keeps the source, lattice, peripheral data, and marked normal information together.

At a root of unity, paired spectral channels can merge. Terms that vanish at the root can contribute to the required derivative. In the current Racah formulas, this includes a retained $[2N]T$ term. Evaluating first and then differentiating a truncated formula loses information.

The [torsor status note](../../notes/a_polynomial_torsor_status.md) explains how this viewpoint relates to earlier work in the repository. A classical A-polynomial alone has not supplied the selected period or its absolute geometric normalization.
