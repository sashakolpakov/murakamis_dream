# The private-left terminal face on the right of the ordinary three-four plateau

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. For the actual ordinary two-vertex
word \((3,4)\), the weak terminal private-left face retains its calibrated
gap as the shared real coordinate moves from \(9/20\) to \(1/2\), at its
original attached height. The complete first cap may be multiplied into
this comparison. A sign for the separate terminal marginal is not
needed: the original stationary equation cancels it exactly.

Sections 6--7 also pay the right real transform face and all twelve
penalized alias anchors on the same added interval. These are specified
source-family estimates. They do not assert a bound for the remaining
real faces, the height taper, the far half-circle, or the whole ordinary
two-boundary kernel. No numerical evaluation is used.

## 2. The private-left face and its force at the reference

Fix \(e_0=27/100\). The exact reduced phase of (2), from
[the retained two-index source](research_mixed_terminal_four_reduced_currents.md),
is
\[
 \Phi(a;t,e)=S(e)+g(a+t)+g(e+t)-3g(t)-g(e)-g(a)
              -\frac{\pi i}{2}(e-e^2)+2\pi iat .
 \tag{6}
\]
The original primitive has \(g'(z)=\log(1-e^{-2\pi iz})\) and
\(g''(z)=\pi\cot(\pi z)-\pi i\) on its regular strip.
All lifts in (6) are the inherited ones.

For \(A\le x\le1/2\), define
\[
 F(x,u)=\Re\Phi(x+ic;u+ih,e_0),\qquad
 f(x)=\max_{3/20\le u\le1/2}F(x,u).
 \tag{7}
\]
The upper-right corner is interpreted by continuous limiting real
phase if \(c+h=0\). Its finite source interpretation is given below.

**Lemma 1.** At \(x=A\), the maximizer in (7) is unique and has
real part strictly greater than \(T\). Moreover
\[
                         f'_+(A)<p.
 \tag{8}
\]

*Proof.* The original terminal stationarity gives
\(\Phi_t(a_*;t_*,e_*)=0\). Consequently
\[
 F_u(A,T)=
 \log\frac{|1-e^{-2\pi i(t_*+e_0)}|}
               {|1-e^{-2\pi i(t_*+e_*)}|}>0 .
 \tag{9}
\]
Here is a direct proof of the strict sign, using only (3). Set
\(r=e^{2\pi h}>0\), \(k=e^{2\pi v}\in(0,1)\),
\(\theta_0=2\pi(T+e_0)\), and \(\theta_1=2\pi(T+E)\).
Both angles belong to \((\pi,3\pi/2)\), and
\(\theta_0<\theta_1\). Thus
\(\cos\theta_0<\cos\theta_1<0\) and
\[
\begin{split}
 |1-re^{-i\theta_0}|^2-|1-rk e^{-i\theta_1}|^2
 =r^2(1-k^2)+2r(k\cos\theta_1-\cos\theta_0)>0 .
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
No phase or root value has been evaluated.

The old reduced-current Hessian is strictly negative in the real
\(t\) direction on the entire interval in (7) at \(x=A\).
It follows that the unique maximum is attained at some
\(u_A>T\), possibly at the upper endpoint.
The derivative in the incoming coordinate is independent of \(e\):
\[
 \Phi_a=g'(a+t)-g'(a)+2\pi it.
 \tag{11}
\]
In particular \(\Re\Phi_a(a_*;t_*,e_0)=p\).
For \(T\le u\le u_A\), (3) gives
\[
        \frac12<A+u<1,\qquad
 \partial_u F_x(A,u)=\pi\Re\cot\pi(A+u+i(c+h))<0 .
 \tag{12}
\]
Integrating proves \(F_x(A,u_A)<p\).
The maximum is unique, and \(F_x\) is continuous near this compact
slice. The one-sided envelope derivative is therefore
\(f'_+(A)=F_x(A,u_A)\), proving (8). \(\square\)

## 3. Joint concavity through the upper endpoint

**Lemma 2.** The function \(F\) is jointly concave on
\([A,1/2]\times[3/20,1/2]\). It is strictly concave on the old
subrectangle \(x\le b\), and strictly concave in \(u\) throughout.
Consequently \(f\) is concave and
\[
                  f(x)\le f(b)+p(x-b)
                         \qquad(b\le x\le1/2).
 \tag{13}
\]

*Proof.* On \(A\le x\le b\), this is the original full
three-variable Hessian bound, restricted to the fixed real private
coordinate \(e_0\) and the heights \(c,h\).
For \(b\le x<1/2\), abbreviate
\[
 C_a=\Re\cot\pi(x+ic),\quad C_t=\Re\cot\pi(u+ih),
 \quad C_{a+t}=\Re\cot\pi(x+u+i(c+h)),
 \quad C_{t+e_0}=\Re\cot\pi(u+e_0+ih).
\]
Direct differentiation of (6) gives the exact quadratic form
\[
 -\pi^{-1}D^2F=
 -C_{a+t}(dx+du)^2+C_a\,dx^2+
                   (3C_t-C_{t+e_0})\,du^2 .
 \tag{14}
\]
The first two coefficients are nonnegative because
\(.6\le x+u<1\) and \(x<1/2\).

The last coefficient is strictly positive. For \(u<1/2\),
\(C_t>0\). If \(u+e_0\ge1/2\), then \(C_{t+e_0}\le0\).
Otherwise the function
\[
 q\longmapsto \Re\cot\pi(q+ih)
 =\frac{\sin(2\pi q)}{\cosh(2\pi h)-\cos(2\pi q)}
\]
is strictly decreasing from \(q=u\) to \(q=u+e_0\).
Indeed \(q\ge.15\), \(|h|<.108\), and
\[
 \cosh(2\pi h)<\frac43,\qquad
             \cos(2\pi q)\le\cos(.3\pi)<\frac34,
 \tag{15}
\]
so the derivative numerator
\(2\pi\{\cosh(2\pi h)\cos(2\pi q)-1\}\) is negative.
For an elementary witness to the first bound, use
\(2\pi|h|<.24\pi<19/25\) and
\[
 \cosh z\le1+\frac{z^2}{2(1-z^2/12)},\qquad
 1+\frac{(19/25)^2}{2(1-(19/25)^2/12)}
       =\frac{9305}{7139}<\frac43.
\]
Thus \(C_{t+e_0}<C_t\) in this case.
At \(u=1/2\), \(C_t=0\) and
\(C_{t+e_0}=\Re\cot\pi(.77+ih)<0\).
This proves the asserted signs in (14).

Concavity extends to \(x=1/2\) by continuity of the phase, including
the logarithmic endpoint \(x+u=1,\ c+h=0\). No derivative at that
endpoint is asserted. Maximization over the same fixed convex compact
\(u\)-interval preserves joint concavity in \(x\): use the convex
combination of any two maximizing \(u\)'s in the joint inequality.
Finally a concave function has nonincreasing one-sided derivatives.
Lemma 1 gives slopes at most \(p\) to the right of \(A\), yielding
(13). \(\square\)

## 5. Finite source, the wall, and the retained scope

The interpolant that defines the terminal face is the original
\(\widehat{\mathcal J}_N\) of the reduced-current theorem:
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal J}_N(a;e,t,w)=
 e^{2\pi iNat}
 \Pi_N(a+t)\Pi_N(e+t)E_N(a)E_N(t)^3E_N(e)
                  \Delta_N(e)^{-1}\mathcal C_N(e,w).
 \tag{18}
\]
Replacing the prescribed notation for the same factorial interpolation
does not change its physical values or its scalar.
Its regular row power is \(N^{-3}\). The old two-index residue
coefficients, signs and outside scalar \(\kappa_N^3N^2\) in (1)
are unchanged.

At the displaced half-grid private cut
\(e_N=e_0+O(N^{-1})\), and the displaced transform endpoints,
the rate in (18) differs from the limiting face rate by
\(O(\log N/N)\), uniformly over the new incoming interval.
Away from \(a+t=1\) this is ordinary regular-symbol uniformity.
At that corner use the
[holomorphic numerator continuation](research_mixed_fourier_right_cap_joint_global_chart.md),
Lemma 1, and the exact shift with \(m_N=\lfloor N/4\rfloor\):
\[
 \Pi_N(z)=\Pi_N(z-m_N/N)
     \prod_{\ell=1}^{m_N}
       \left(1-e^{-2\pi i(z-m_N/N+\ell/N)}\right),
                 \qquad z=a+t.
 \tag{19}
\]
The shifted factorial stays in a fixed regular strip. The finite
product has the uniform one-sided logarithmic integral upper bound,
including its zeros, proved in
[the incoming-wall source lemma](research_mixed_fourier_incoming_taper_all_height.md),
Section 4. Its continuous rate is exactly the limiting original
factorial rate; a displacement of \(O(N^{-1})\) at the logarithmic
zero costs \(O(\log N/N)\). There is no division by \(\Pi_N(1)\)
or removal of a vanishing numerator.

Consequently the exact one-index face current obtained by integrating
(18) along its retained \(t\)-segment at \(e=e_N\), with any of the
original polynomial normalization factors, obeys after multiplication
by the complete first cap
\[
 |U_{0,N}(w_L,e^{-2\pi i(x+ic)})
                     \,\mathcal I^{\,e_L}_{N}(x+ic,w_R)|
 \le C N^A
       \exp N\left(M-\frac1{40000}\right)
                 \qquad(b\le x\le1/2).
 \tag{20}
\]
Here \(\mathcal I^{e_L}_N\) denotes that specified face current, not
the whole terminal polynomial or a deleted part of a coherent sum.
The finite comparison also supplies its anchor rate when this face
occurs in the existing vertical-convexity reduction. Other endpoints
of any such vertical segment must still be bounded separately.
The proof spends one total small rate tolerance in (17), absorbs the
fixed polynomial factors, and uses the complete ordinary-color
endpoint packet in (18). Smaller fixed color disks and Cauchy's
formula give every fixed original ordinary-color derivative with
the same weakened rate.

No conclusion is asserted here for changing the shared height \(c\),
for \(x>1/2\), for a different retained leg chart, or for the other
terminal comparison families. In particular this is not yet a
whole-circle ordinary \((3,4)\) packet.

## 6. The right real transform face, with every private label

Fix \(t_0=1/2\) real. The private coordinate \(e\) may carry any
of its five original labels: its whole main segment, either real cut,
or either penalized horizontal alias. Write \(\mathfrak p_e\ge0\)
for that unchanged alias penalty, and set
\[
 F_e^{\,R}(x)=\Re\Phi(x+ic;t_0,e)-\mathfrak p_e.
 \tag{21}
\]
The incoming derivative is independent of \(e\) and of
\(\mathfrak p_e\).

**Proposition 4.** For every such private profile and \(b\le x\le1/2\),
\[
 F_e^{\,R}(x)\le F_e^{\,R}(b)+p(x-b).
 \tag{22}
\]
Consequently all five right-transform-face anchors, multiplied by the
whole first cap, retain their original raw gap of at least \(1/20000\).
Their finite face-current bounds have the weakened gap \(1/40000\).

*Proof.* Put \(Z=e^{-2\pi ia_*}\), \(R=|Z|\), and
\(\tau=e^{-2\pi it_*}\). The original marginal and the new face
marginal satisfy
\[
\begin{split}
 p&=\log\frac{|\tau^{-1}-Z|}{|1-Z|},\\
 (F_e^{\,R})'(A)&=\log\frac{|1+Z|}{|1-Z|}<p .
\end{split}
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed \(\cos(2\pi A)<0\), so \(|1+Z|^2<1+R^2\).
On the other hand \(h<0\) and
\(.524<A+T<.741\) give
\[
 |\tau^{-1}-Z|^2
 =e^{-4\pi h}+R^2-2e^{-2\pi h}R\cos(2\pi(A+T))
 >1+R^2.
 \tag{24}
\]
For \(A\le x<1/2\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 (F_e^{\,R})''(x)
 =\pi\{\Re\cot\pi(x+1/2+ic)-\Re\cot\pi(x+ic)\}<0.
 \tag{25}
\]
Both inequalities extend as the asserted phase bounds to the continuous
endpoint at \(x=1/2\). Thus (23)--(25) imply (22).

At \(x=b\), the original real right-transform face and its two
real-real corners have raw gap \(1/20000\) relative to the terminal
plane. The two private-alias labels have an even larger gap, as used
in the next section. Add (22) to the complete cap bound (5).
The calculation is exactly the cancellation in (17).
The finite interpretation, ordinary-color neighborhoods and possible
numerator corner are those in Section 5. \(\square\)

## 7. Every penalized terminal alias anchor

The completed saved extraction quoted in
[selected terminal-four height completion, Section 1](research_mixed_terminal_four_selected_height_completion.md)
proves raw deficit less than \(-1/10\) for **all twelve**
penalized alias endpoint families, at every actual reference and
every admitted old real target. Its verified inventory comprises
all 22,932 original alias jobs, including excluded-only jobs under
their original geometric exclusion rules. No new extraction or
source evaluation is used here.

We keep every internal height and every penalty fixed at its old value.
In particular the transform height \(v_t\) is either its original
critical height \(h\), zero, or one of \(\pm.12\), so
\((-v_t)_+\le3/25\).

**Lemma 5.** For \(b\le x<1/2\), \(3/20\le u\le1/2\),
and any fixed incoming height \(c\), the exact incoming part of
every terminal profile satisfies
\[
 \partial_x\Re\Phi(x+ic;u+iv_t,e)
                       \le2\pi(-v_t)_+ .
 \tag{26}
\]
At the actual reference in (3),
\[
                         p>-\tfrac12\log2>-\frac7{20}.
 \tag{27}
\]

*Proof.* Write \(R=e^{2\pi c}>0\), \(q=e^{-2\pi v_t}>0\).
The derivative in (26) is exactly
\[
 \log\frac{|q-Re^{-2\pi i(x+u)}|}
                {|1-Re^{-2\pi ix}|}.
 \tag{28}
\]
Since \(u\ge.15>1-2x\) and \(x+u\le1\),
\[
 \cos(2\pi(x+u))\ge\cos(2\pi x),\qquad \cos(2\pi x)<0.
 \tag{29}
\]
Put \(D=|1-Re^{-2\pi ix}|\); then \(D^2>\max(1,R^2)\).
If \(q\le1\), convexity of the squared numerator as a function
of \(q\) bounds it by the larger of its values at \(0,1\),
both at most \(D^2\). If \(q\ge1\), factor out \(q^2\)
and use convexity in the remaining radius \(R/q\in[0,R]\).
Its endpoint values are \(1\) and
\(|1-Re^{-2\pi i(x+u)}|^2\), both at most \(D^2\).
This proves (26). Numerator zeros are retained by the limiting
logarithmic upper inequality.

By (24) and \(|1-Z|\le1+R\),
\[
 e^p>\frac{\sqrt{1+R^2}}{1+R}\ge\frac1{\sqrt2}.
 \tag{30}
\]
The elementary \(\log2<7/10\) proves (27). \(\square\)

**Theorem 6.** Each of the twelve original penalized alias anchor
profiles, multiplied by the complete first cap, is bounded on
\(b\le x\le1/2\) by
\[
                         M-\frac1{25}
 \tag{31}
\]
at the raw rate level. The corresponding finite current anchor
estimates retain gap \(1/50\), both parities and all fixed ordinary
color derivatives.

*Proof.* At \(b\), the combined cap and alias profile is less than
\(M-1/10-2\kappa_*(b-A)^2\). By (26), moving the alias
profile to \(x\) costs at most \((6\pi/25)(x-b)\).
The complete cap plane changes by
\(-p(x-b)-\kappa_*[(x-A)^2-(b-A)^2]\).
The last bracket is nonnegative. Equations (27) and
\(x-b\le1/20\) therefore bound the total possible increase by
\[
 \frac1{20}\left(\frac{6\pi}{25}+\frac7{20}\right)
 <\frac3{50},
 \tag{32}
\]
using \(\pi<22/7\). Subtracting this from the old \(1/10\)
reserve gives (31).
The exact old aliases and their penalties have not been moved.
Regular-symbol estimates, the zero-safe wall bound (19), and one
total small color/source tolerance give the stated finite weakening.
\(\square\)

These endpoint comparisons may be used in the original separate-height

left real transform face, private-right flattened face, or their
unpaid corners. The positive conclusion is confined to the named

This section records transfer costs, not the outcome of a new source
comparison. The eight real-family indices below are exactly the
zero-based order of \(\mathrm{FACES}\) in the

| 1 | \(t=.5\) real | whole main segment | zero cost, Proposition 4 |

Here is the complete proof of the nonzero budgets. Let \(T_f(x)\)
be any indicated real-face profile, with its remaining private or
transform real coordinate fixed arbitrarily in its entire old interval.
Set
\[
                 D_f(x)=B_U(x)+T_f(x)-M .
 \tag{33}
\]
The exact difference of first-cap planes is
\[
 B_U(x)-B_U(b)
       =-p(x-b)-\kappa_*[(x-A)^2-(b-A)^2].
 \tag{34}
\]
For a real transform label, Lemma 5 gives \(T_f'(x)\le0\).
It follows from (27), \(b>A\), and \(x-b\le1/20\) that
\[
\begin{split}
 D_f(x)-D_f(b)
 &\le-p(x-b)
        -\kappa_*[(x-A)^2-(b-A)^2]\\
 &<\frac7{400}\qquad (b<x\le1/2).
\end{split}
\tag{35}
\]
At \(x=b\) the difference is zero, so the uniform strict upper
budget \(7/400\) holds there as well.
This proves the stated cost for family 0 and all four real corners.
The bound is pointwise in every remaining free coordinate and therefore
also holds after taking its maximum.

For the private-right face, the transform retains its original
height \(h\), with \(-h<27/250\). Thus (26) gives
\(T_f'(x)<27\pi/125\). Equations (27) and (34) yield
\[
\begin{split}
 D_f(x)-D_f(b)
 &\le\left(\frac{27\pi}{125}+\frac7{20}\right)(x-b)
       -\kappa_*[(x-A)^2-(b-A)^2]\\
 &\le\frac{27\pi}{2500}+\frac7{400}
 <\frac{103}{2000}.
\end{split}
\tag{36}
\]
For the last strict inequality, \(\pi<22/7\) leaves the explicit
rational room
\[
 \frac{103}{2000}
 -\left(\frac{27}{2500}\frac{22}{7}+\frac7{400}\right)
                         =\frac1{17500}>0.
 \tag{37}
\]
The same fixed transform interval is maximized on both sides, so
the proof also covers the entire private-right face envelope.

These are **coupled** transfer budgets. Differentiating (33) keeps
the term \(-2\kappa_*(x-A)\le0\); it would have the opposite
quadratic sign if one transported the terminal defect alone relative
to its own supporting plane. No such standalone inference is used.

For example, if an already saved complete source bound proves
\(T_f(b)\le B_V(b)-\delta_f\), then (35) or (36) gives
\[
 D_f(x)\le-\delta_f+\mathrm{cost}_f-2\kappa_*(b-A)^2 .
 \tag{38}
\]
A saved strict reserve larger than its listed cost therefore leaves
a strictly negative coupled rate on the added interval. This note
does not assert such larger reserves for families 0, 3, 4, or 5.
Checking precisely those saved inequalities, with original domain,
tree and source associations retained, is a separate fixed-data task.
It needs no new phase, root or source evaluation, but its result
must not be assumed before the prescribed complete check succeeds.
The zero-safe finite passage is again Section 5, with a fixed
positive part of the surviving reserve allocated to its tolerances.
