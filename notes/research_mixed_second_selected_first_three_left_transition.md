## 5. A high-row endpoint theorem at the positive selected height

For physical transform parameter \(t\), let
\[
 E(t)=\int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(a+s)}|\,ds
      +\int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(z+s)}|\,ds
      -3J_0(t)-2\pi h t,\qquad
 J_0(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi s)\,ds.                          \tag{20}
\]
We claim on (1) that
\[
       \max_{1/2\le t\le1}E(t)=\max\{E(1/2),E(1)\},
                  \qquad E(1)=-2\pi h+2\pi H.                 \tag{21}
\]
This is a new outgoing-positive-height comparison, not an
application of a theorem restricted to negative outgoing heights.

Put \(R_a=e^{2\pi h}>5/8\), \(R_b=e^{-2\pi H}>9/10\).
The first guard is the elementary exponential witness (12) of
[the following-domain upper-row theorem](research_mixed_four_upper_rows_following_domains.md).
For the second, \(\pi/50<1/15\) and
\(e^{1/15}<(1-1/15)^{-1}<10/9\).
Write \(f_R(s)=\log|1-Re^{-2\pi is}|\) and
\(\ell=-\log(R_aR_b)=-2\pi h+2\pi H\).
Exact period reflection, including the radius factored from the
positive-height outgoing numerator, yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 F(v)&=E(1-v)-E(1)\\
 &=-\int_{x-v}^x f_{R_a}(s)\,ds
   -\int_{y-v}^y f_{R_b}(s)\,ds+3J_0(v)-\ell v .
 \end{split}                                                \tag{22}
\]
In particular the incoming chirp and the outgoing radial drift
have both been retained.

For \(0<v\le1/5\), the first arc is bounded below by the
integer-centered arc. The second has \(v\le y\le1/2\) and
is bounded below by the arc \([0,v]\).
The radial primitive inequality
\(\int_0^t f_R\ge J_0(t)\), \(t\le1/2,0<R\le1\), is
proved in [the original asymmetric endpoint theorem](research_mixed_transformed_left_upper_rows.md).
Therefore, exactly as in its short-arc argument,
\[
 F(v)\le2[J_0(v)-J_0(v/2)]-\ell v
       \le v\{\log(4\pi v)-1\}-\ell v<0,                       \tag{23}
\]
using \(4\pi/5<e\).

We next prove \(F'>0\) throughout \([1/5,1/2]\).
Let \(S(v)=2\sin\pi v\).
For \(1/5\le v\le1/3\),
\[
 |x-v|\le v-.1,\qquad |y-v|\le.45-v .
\]
If \(d\ge.1\), \(d\le1/2\), and \(5/8\le R\le1\), then
\[
 |1-Re^{-2\pi is}|\le2\sin\pi d\quad(|s|\le d),               \tag{24}
\]
because \(S(d)^2\ge S(.1)^2=(3-\sqrt5)/2>3/8\ge1-R\).
Indeed at \(|s|=d\) the difference of the squared unit-radius
modulus and this squared modulus is
\((1-R)\{S(d)^2-(1-R)\}\ge0\); angular monotonicity handles
smaller \(|s|\). Both distances above are at least \(1/10\),
so this applies to both radii, including \(R_b>9/10\).

Consequently
\[
 e^{F'(v)}\ge R_aR_b\,T(v),\qquad
 T(v)=\frac{S(v)^3}
       {4\sin\pi(v-.1)\sin\pi(.45-v)}.                         \tag{25}
\]
This last function strictly increases on \([1/5,1/3]\).
To check the sign directly, put \(k=\cot(\pi/10)>3\) and
\(t_0=\cot(\pi v)\in[1/2,7/5]\). Then
\[
 3\cot(\pi v)-\cot\pi(v-.1)
   =\frac{2kt_0-3t_0^2-1}{k-t_0}>0 .
\]
The numerator exceeds \(6t_0-3t_0^2-1\), whose endpoint
values on this interval are \(5/4,38/25\); it is concave.
The remaining term in \((\log T)'/\pi\) is the positive
\(\cot\pi(.45-v)\). The guards on \(k,t_0\) follow from the
pentagon values: \(2\sin(\pi/10)<5/8\),
\(\cos(\pi/10)>15/16\), \(S(1/5)^2>11/8\),
\(S(1/5)>7/6\), and \(\cos(\pi/5)<13/16\).
They require only \(\sqrt5<9/4\) and the elementary cosine
lower bound at \(\pi/10<11/35\).
In particular
\[
 T(1/5)>
 \frac{(11/8)(7/6)}{(5/8)(10/7)}
       =539/300,\qquad
 e^{F'(v)}>\frac9{16}\frac{539}{300}
       =4851/4800>1 .                                      \tag{26}
\]
Here \(\sqrt2<10/7\) bounds the second sine in the denominator.

For \(1/3\le v\le1/2\), the first modulus is at most \(S(v)\).
Indeed \(|x-v|\le v\) and convexity in \(R\in[0,1]\) bounds
it by \(\max(1,S(v))=S(v)\). The other distance is at most
\(.28\), so its modulus is less than \(8/5\):
\(\sin(.28\pi)<\sin(.9)<4/5\), using the sine upper

\[
                 e^{F'(v)}>
                  \frac9{16}\frac{3}{8/5}
                       =135/128>1 .                         \tag{27}
\]
Possible zeros give integrable logarithmic singularities; integrate

The uniform zero-safe finite product bound and the original

This includes the even half row, all revived rows, both parities
