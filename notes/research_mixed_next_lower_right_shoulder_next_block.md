# The complete next block on the lower right shoulder

Research proof, 8 November 2026. No numerical evaluation or modification
of a frozen source is used.

The complete next conditional kernel extends to the right shoulder
with one explicit remaining last-row term. That term is paid after
it is multiplied by the original central first block: its outgoing
logarithmic derivative combines with the first block's derivative
before any estimate. Consequently the whole central first-incoming
integrand, including both complete conditional kernels, is below the
unchanged ordinary-next affine action.

This does not estimate the other first-incoming paths or the whole
lower complement. It does not assert a standalone supporting-plane
bound for the next kernel's last row.

## 1. Exact factors and the actual plane

Use the canonical ordinary-next references and notation of
[the complete lower-plateau theorem](research_mixed_next_complete_lower_plateau.md):
\[
(a_*,s_*,y_*,t_*,z_*)=(r_1,s_2^*,r_2,s_3^*,r_3),\qquad
\alpha=\Re y_*,\quad h_y=\Im y_* .
\tag{1}
\]
Both vertices after the first cap are ordinary. The flags are
\((0,1,\beta)\), \(\beta=0,1\), and all actual typed-reference
correlations are retained. In particular
\[
.24<\alpha<.36,\quad -.102<h_y<-.001,\quad
.23\le\Re t_*\le.45,\quad -.108\le\Im t_*\le.068.
\tag{2}
\]
Put \(P=[.22,.45]\), and use the independent targets
\[
a=x+i\Im a_*,\quad x\in P,\qquad
y=q+ih_y,\quad .45\le q\le.5,\qquad
z=u+i\Im z_*,\quad u\in P .
\tag{3}
\]
Write \(X=e^{-2\pi ia},Y=e^{-2\pi iy},Z=e^{-2\pi iz}\).
The literal finite kernels are
\[
C_{\beta,N}(Y,Z)=\sum_{j<N}
       \frac{Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)}{P_j^{2+\beta}},
\qquad
C_{1,N}(X,Y)=\sum_{j<N}
       \frac{X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)}{P_j^3},
\tag{4}
\]
with \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(r;r)_j\),
\(p_j(V)=(rV;r)_j\). Keep the complete ordinary cap \(U_{0,N}\)
and original degree-\(N-1\) Fourier polynomial \(d_N\). No physical
row, repeated-root normal, or Fourier forcing term is deleted.

The allocated next phase and its unpenalized actual tangent are
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal F_\beta(t;y,z)
 &=g(t+y)+g(t+z)-(2+\beta)g(t)+2\pi iyt
                         -\tfrac32g(y)-(1+\beta/2)g(z),\\
\mathcal B(q,u)
 &=\Re\mathcal F_\beta(t_*;y_*,z_*)
   +P_y(q-\alpha)+P_z(u-\Re z_*).
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
All primitives and lifts are original. The actual prefix action
\(A_*\) and marginal \(p\) are those of
[the central-first shoulder theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_right_shoulder_central_prefix.md),
equation (4). Original stationarity and the split Fourier allocation give
\[
P_y=-p,\qquad
A_*+p(q-\alpha)+\mathcal B(q,u)=\Lambda_{123}(z).
\tag{6}
\]
The outgoing plane \(\Lambda_{123}\) is exactly the one in the
complete lower-plateau theorem. The elementary original marginal
bound in
[selected-plateau comparison, equation (21)](research_mixed_next_filtered_selected_plateau.md)
gives \(P_y>-\log2\).

At the literal last row, the allocated next rate is
\[
\mathcal L(q,u)
 =-2\pi h_y+2\pi(\Im z_*)_+
              -\tfrac12\Re g(y)-\tfrac\beta2\Re g(z).
\tag{7}
\]
This is the maximum of the original four revived carriers at the
actual heights. Its only \(q\)-dependent term is
\(-\Re g(y)/2\).

**Theorem.** Uniformly over (1)--(3), both level parities, and
all real ordinary colors \(w\in[-2,2]\), for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
|U_{0,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(Y)C_{\beta,N}(Y,Z)|
 |d_N(Z)|^{\beta/2}
 \le C N^B e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11}\}} .
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
The same bound, with polynomial cost, applies after the original
central first integration \(+N\int_{P+i\Im a_*}da\).
Fixed real-color derivatives and local fixed mixed derivatives of
the corresponding integer-weight holomorphic product are retained.
Only a separately proved nonzero packet at the actual output may
be used to remove the outgoing halfweight.

## 2. Moving next-index cuts and main support

Set
\[
\ell_\beta^0=\begin{cases}3/20,&\beta=0,\\1/8,&\beta=1,\end{cases}
\qquad
\ell_\beta(q)=\ell_\beta^0+9/20-q .
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(\ell_0\in[.1,.15]\), \(\ell_1\in[.075,.125]\).
Take a common right cut \(b=1/2-\varepsilon\), with
\(\varepsilon>0\) fixed sufficiently small below. The retained
middle has \(t+y,t+z\) strictly in the original regular strip,
even at \(q=.5\). The physical collar between \(b\) and \(1/2\)
will be paid from the strict real half-row comparison. This avoids
using a regular logarithmic expansion through \(t+y=1\).

On the old product, the original ordinary Hessian theorem gives
\(-\Re\mathcal F_\beta''\succeq\mu I\), \(\mu=1/1250000\),
for \(t_R\in[\ell_\beta^0,.5]\), both outer real coordinates in
\(P\), at their fixed actual heights. Its sources are
[horizontal support](research_mixed_horizontal_zero_mode_support.md),
[the half-index extension](research_mixed_half_index_contour.md),
and [the own-four smaller cut](research_mixed_four_left_cut_extension.md).

For \(q\ge.45\) and \(t_R\ge\ell_\beta^0\), the incoming
shifted cotangent is nonpositive, because \(t_R+q>.5\).
The incoming pair negative real Hessian is therefore positive
semidefinite. The old outgoing pair stays positive for
\(t_R\ge.15\). On \(1/8\le t_R\le.15\), the own-four Schur
argument still applies: the incoming Schur cost is nonpositive
and the outgoing cost is less than \(187/120\), whereas the
index contribution is greater than \(3(21/20)\).
Thus the full negative real Hessian is positive semidefinite
through this extension.

Here is the additional small-index estimate. It applies whenever
\(t_R+y_R\ge.5\), so it also controls the straight segment from
the old reference to the new target. Write
\[
U=\Re\cot\pi t,\quad P=\Re\cot\pi y,\quad Q=\Re\cot\pi z,
\quad C_y=\Re\cot\pi(t+y),\quad C_z=\Re\cot\pi(t+z).
\]
Then \(P\ge0,C_y\le0\). The incoming diagonal
\(3P/2-C_y\) is positive on the small-index strip; its index
Schur cost is nonpositive. In both small strips \(U>6/5\).
To see this use \(\cosh(2\pi\Im t)<5/4\). On
\([.1,.15]\) the real-cotangent function has its minimum at an
endpoint: at \(.1\) use
\(\sin(\pi/5)>7/12,\cos(\pi/5)>4/5\), and at \(.15\) use
\(\sin(3\pi/10)>4/5,\cos(3\pi/10)>7/12\).
On \([.075,.125]\), the concavity of
\(\sin\theta+(6/5)\cos\theta\) reduces the same assertion to
the endpoints \(\theta=3\pi/20,\pi/4\).
There \(\sin(3\pi/20)>9/20,\cos(3\pi/20)>89/100\) and
\(\sqrt2/2>7/10\) suffice. These are elementary Taylor or
half-angle inequalities.

When \(C_z\le0\), its Schur cost is nonpositive as well.
Suppose \(C_z>0\), and set \(v=\cot(\pi\Re z)\).
The actual height bound gives
\[
Q>\frac{8v}{9+v^2},\qquad v<5/4.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(\beta=0\), \(v>3/10\), and
\[
C_z<13/20,\qquad Q-C_z>1/4.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed \(\cot(\pi/10)<31/10\), so
\(C_z\le(31v-10)/(31+10v)\).
After positive denominators are cleared, the second inequality
in (11) is
\[
81-214v+329v^2-134v^3>0,\qquad 3/10\le v\le5/4.
\]
Writing \(v=1/2+w\) makes this
\(79/2+(29/2)w+w^2(128-134w)\), positive on
the indicated interval. The first inequality follows from
\(\Re(t+z)\ge.32\), \(.32\pi>1\),
\(\cos1<13/24\), and \(\sin1>5/6\).
Consequently the outgoing Schur cost is less than
\[
13/20+(13/20)^2/(1/4)=117/50<2U.
\tag{12}
\]

For \(\beta=1\), use \(\cot(3\pi/40)<17/4\). Positivity
of \(C_z\) implies \(v>4/17\), and
\[
C_z<4/5,\qquad \tfrac32Q-C_z>1/4.
\tag{13}
\]
The second assertion reduces to
\(-9+168v+191v^2-72v^3>0\), immediate for
\(4/17\le v\le5/4\). For the first, \(t_R+z_R\ge.295\)
and \(.295\pi>9/10\); the alternating sine/cosine bounds
at \(9/10\) give
\(\cos(9/10)<(4/5)\sin(9/10)\).
The index Schur complement is therefore greater than
\[
3(6/5)-\{4/5+(4/5)^2/(1/4)\}=6/25>0.
\tag{14}
\]
For the cotangent guard above, \(\tan x>x+x^3/3\) at
\(x=15/64<3\pi/40\) already gives \(\tan(3\pi/40)>4/17\).
Thus the full Hessian is positive on both added small-index strips.

Every straight real segment from the actual reference to a target
\((t_R,q,u)\), \(t_R\ge\ell_\beta(q)\), stays in these domains.
If its index first passes below \(\ell_\beta^0\), its incoming
coordinate is at least \(2/5\): the worst own-three values are
\(\Re t_*=.23,\alpha=.24,q=.5,t_R=.1\), giving exactly \(2/5\);

on the remainder of the small-index portion.
The initial fraction in the old strictly positive domain is at
least \(8/13\): the index fraction is at least \(8/13\) for
own three and \(21/31\) for own four, and the incoming fraction
before \(q=.45\) is at least \(9/14\).
The exact Taylor integral and original index stationarity give
\[
\Re\mathcal F_\beta(t_R+i\Im t_*;y,z)
\le\mathcal B(q,u)
-\frac\mu2(8/13)^2(q-\alpha)^2
<\mathcal B(q,u)-10^{-9}.
\tag{15}
\]
The last rational comparison uses \(q-\alpha>9/100\).

## 3. Low and half real rows

The original ordinary real-cut certificates give a deficit
\(1/2000\) against \(\mathcal B\) at \(q=.45\), at both
\(\ell_\beta^0\) and \(1/2\); see
[the complete three-row assembly](research_mixed_three_row_source_assembly.md).
They are on sufficient typed products covering these actual
references and independent outgoing targets.

Put \(R=e^{2\pi h_y}\). The original elementary radius and angle
witnesses, also written in the central-first shoulder theorem, give
\[
R>13/25,\qquad |1-Y|>3/2,\qquad |1+Y|<3/5.
\tag{16}
\]
Along the moving real lower cut the exact derivative is
\[
\frac d{dq}\Re\mathcal F_\beta(\ell_\beta(q);y,z)
=(2+\beta)\log(2\sin\pi\ell_\beta)
-\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(\ell_\beta+z)}|
-\tfrac32\log|1-Y|+2\pi h_y .
\tag{17}
\]
Here \(\Re(\ell_\beta+z)\in[.295,.6]\), so the second
modulus exceeds one, regardless of the sign of \(\Im z_*\).
For own three, \(2\sin(3\pi/20)<19/20\), whence the first
term is less than \(-1/10\). For own four use the stronger
\(2\sin(\pi/8)<4/5\). Since \(\log(3/2)>2/5\),
both derivatives are less than \(-7/10<-\log2<P_y\).
At the half row,
\[
\partial_q\Re\mathcal F_\beta(1/2;y,z)
=\log|1+Y|-\tfrac32\log|1-Y|<-11/10<P_y.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus both real cuts keep their original deficit.

Every low physical row increases to its moving lower cut.
The exact row derivative exponent is
\[
\frac{|1-e^{-2\pi i(y+t)}||1-e^{-2\pi i(z+t)}|}
 {R(2\sin\pi t)^{2+\beta}}.
\tag{19}
\]
The first numerator exceeds one; the second exceeds \(49/50\)
when its shifted real part is below \(1/4\), and exceeds one
otherwise on the present range. In fact the minimum over
positive radii is \(\sin(2\pi(\Re z+t))\), and only
\(\Re z+t\in[.22,.25]\) needs that minimum.
The denominator is at most \((19/20)^2<49/50\).
This proves strict increase, retaining the exact zero row.

The full upper-row endpoint theorem extends to the square
\([.22,.5]^2\) at the original height bound .108; see
[the transformed upper-row theorem](research_mixed_transformed_upper_rows.md),
Lemma 1, and its source discussion in
[following upper-row domains, Sections 4--5](research_mixed_four_upper_rows_following_domains.md).
It applies to both own types and retains the chirp.
Equivalently, in the original reflection proof the farthest arc
center remains at .22 since \(2c\ge.72=.22+.5\);
all subsequent corner/radius estimates are unchanged.
Every upper physical row is therefore bounded by the half-row
or (7).

By uniform continuity of the exact zero-safe real-row profiles,
choose the fixed \(\varepsilon\) in Section 2 so the collar
\([1/2-\varepsilon,1/2]\) keeps, for example, a \(1/2500\)
deficit. This is a strict physical-row comparison, not analytic
continuation through a numerator zero.

## 4. Both next aliases and every join

For \(v\in[\ell_\beta(q),b]\) and any finite index height \(\sigma\),
use the original argument
\[
A(t,h)=\arg(1-e^{2\pi h-2\pi it}),\qquad
\pi\min(1-2t,0)\le A(t,h)\le\pi\max(1-2t,0).
\]
Since \(v+q>.5\), the incoming shifted argument is nonpositive.
The exact chirp gives
\[
-7\pi/4<
\Im Q_{\beta,t}(v+i\sigma;y,z)<141\pi/100 .
\tag{20}
\]
For the upper bound use
\(2q+(1-2v-2u)_+\le141/100\).
For the lower bound, if \(v+u<.5\) it is at least
\(-(1+\beta)+2(1+\beta)v\), and otherwise it is at least
\(-\beta+2\beta v-2u\), after division by \(\pi\).
Both exceed \(-7/4\) on (9), with finite-radius strictness.

Use lower height \(-1/5\), upper height \(1\), and the actual
main height \(h_t=\Im t_*\). Integration of (20) gives
\[
\begin{split}
\Re\mathcal F_\beta(v-i/5)-2\pi/5
 &\le\Re\mathcal F_\beta(v+ih_t)-553\pi/25000,\\
\Re\mathcal F_\beta(v+i)-2\pi
 &\le\Re\mathcal F_\beta(v+ih_t)-61\pi/1000.
\end{split}
\tag{21}
\]
Here \(h_t\le17/250\) and \(h_t\ge-27/250\).
The same argument bounds show that each weighted vertical half
decreases away from its real cut. On the two unweighted joins,
the index component of the Hessian in Section 2 gives vertical
convexity between zero and \(h_t\); both endpoints are paid.

All arguments lie in fixed compact regular strips because of
the upper cut \(b\). The original factorial interpolant and
half-grid cotangent identity consequently give exactly two
horizontal remainders, four weighted vertical sides, and two
unweighted joins, with the constant modes contributing
\(+N\) times the main integral. Moving half-grid cuts by
\(O(1/N)\) and uniform factorial symbols cost only a polynomial
and an arbitrarily small rate tolerance.

Combining the entire finite inventory proves the useful intermediate
bound, for every \(\tau>0\),
\[
|C_{\beta,N}(Y,Z)|\,|d_N(Y)|^{1/2}|d_N(Z)|^{\beta/2}
\le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
\left(e^{N(\mathcal B(q,u)-10^{-9}+\tau)}
                 +e^{N(\mathcal L(q,u)+\tau)}\right).
\tag{22}
\]
All real-cut and alias reserves are larger than the main reserve;
a smaller main constant could equivalently be used. Formula (22)
is an upper bound for the complete source, not a deletion of its
last physical row.

## 5. The actual last-row term is paid jointly

For the first kernel keep its exact source atlas from the
[central-first shoulder theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_right_shoulder_central_prefix.md):
moving lower cut \(\ell_1(q)=.575-q\), upper half cut, main
height \(h_s<0\), lower alias \(-.2\), upper alias \(8\),
all four weighted sides, both unweighted joins, and every
omitted physical row. Its full numerator-wall upper bound is
retained at the half cut.

After adding (7), the \(q\)-dependent part of a first-index
profile \(F(s;a,y)\) is exactly
\[
                  g(s+y)-2g(y).
\tag{23}
\]
This uses both incident Fourier halves. The last-row carrier,
the cap and the outgoing halfweight are independent of \(s\).
For every fixed first-index profile with \(\Im s\le0\),
\[
\begin{split}
\partial_q\Re\{F(s;a,y)+\mathcal L(q,u)\}
 &=\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(s+y)}|-2\log|1-Y|\\
 &\le\log\frac{1+R}{1+R^2+(19/10)R}<\log(3/4)<-1/4.
\end{split}
\tag{24}
\]
The rational comparison in the second line follows from \(R>1/2\):
\[
1+R^2+(19/10)R-\tfrac43(1+R)
=R^2+\tfrac{17}{30}R-\tfrac13>0 .
\]
There is no marginal or critical-point substitution in (24).

Thus each first main profile with real index at least \(1/8\)
transports jointly from \(q=.45\). The extra main strip below
\(1/8\) is increasing in the real index at the fixed negative
height \(h_s\), by equation (12) of the central-first theorem;
it is bounded by the \(1/8\) profile at the same \(q\).
On the moving real lower cut, direct differentiation of (23) gives
\[
3\log(2\sin\pi\ell_1)
-\log|e^{-2\pi h_a}-e^{-2\pi i(x+\ell_1)}|
-2\log|1-Y|<-4/5 .
\tag{25}
\]
The shifted modulus exceeds one, the own modulus is below one,
and \(|1-Y|>3/2\). The half row obeys (24). At the first
kernel's last-row branch the two allocations combine to
\(-\Re g(y)\), so the derivative is
\(-\log|1-Y|<-\log(3/2)\).
The physical low and upper rows remain bounded by these cut
and endpoint profiles; this is a profile maximum, not a
positivity assertion for the coherent central sum.

At \(q_0=.45\), every one of these original first profiles and
the next last-row profile is covered separately by the complete
ordinary source theorem. Add the complete cap plane and cancel
the actual first and second marginals. The two incident
ordinary planes retain the loss
\[
2\kappa(q_0-\alpha)^2>162/25000000000>6\cdot10^{-9},
\qquad \kappa=1/2500000.
\tag{26}
\]
All unused first-index, first-incoming and outgoing losses are
nonnegative. Equations (24)--(25) preserve this strict comparison
to the unchanged \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\), which has no \(q\) argument.

The last-row factor (7) is index independent and is kept on

reflection estimate in Section 6 of that theorem remains in
force, including every zero and both parities.
This pays all nine first-kernel central pieces, not only the
negative-height main.
