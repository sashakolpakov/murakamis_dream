# Removing artificial roots from the regular-arc upper exponent

Private written proof, 16 November 2026.

The monic certificate for the repeated-turn equations can introduce
roots which are absent from their principal rank-drop support. They
need not contribute to the integrated upper bound. A local factor
argument removes such roots while preserving membership in the
actual maximal-minor ideal. Patching the resulting estimates gives
the envelope of that full principal support.

This support still includes the source, recovery and clearing
components of the chosen presentation. It is not the geometric
branch alone. The argument does not cross a color at which a
principal shift tends to infinity, supply a smaller initial budget,
or establish a nonzero coefficient.

## 1. The fixed source and its principal support

Use the source column, exact ordered equations, and coefficient
conventions of the projective monic certificate. The outer turn is
\(\tau\in\{1,-1\}\), and the powers and numbers of all input
factors are fixed. For this note fix the microscopic parameters.
Write the exact equations as
\[
 \mathcal R_h(v,E)\mathcal Y_N=0,\qquad
 h=N^{-1},\quad E f(v)=f(e^{2\pi ih}v).
 \tag{1}
\]
Their original residual values and first physical derivatives
vanish at the physical grid. Keep the entire column.

Let \(I\) be the ideal generated by the maximal minors of the
commuting matrix \(\mathcal R_0(v,t)\). On a coefficient
neighborhood its entries are rational, hence holomorphic, in
\(v\), and Laurent polynomial in \(t\). Put
\[
 \Sigma(v)=\{t\in\mathbb C^\times:
                  \operatorname{rank}\mathcal R_0(v,t)<d\},
 \qquad
 r(u)=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{|t|:t\in\Sigma(e^{2\pi iu})\}\bigr),
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(d\) is the column count. An empty support has \(r(u)=1\).
We work on a compact angular interval \(J\subset(0,1/2)\)
whose corresponding colors avoid the set \(\mathcal E_\tau\)
of the projective certificate and the physical endpoints.

That theorem supplies a monic polynomial belonging to \(I\)
near each point of \(J\). It also excludes shift zero and
infinity in the projective closure over a neighborhood of \(J\).
Consequently the fibers in (2) are finite and uniformly bounded.
All statements below retain the full ideal, including its
nilpotent multiplicities.

## 2. A local polynomial with no artificial roots at its center

**Lemma 1.** Fix \(v_0=e^{2\pi iu_0}\) above \(J\). There is a
monic polynomial \(p_0(v,t)\), with holomorphic coefficients on
a neighborhood of \(v_0\), which belongs to \(I\) there and
whose roots at \(v_0\) belong to
\[
                         \Sigma(v_0)\cup\{0\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The constant polynomial one is allowed when this set contributes
no roots. Membership is a finite linear combination of the
original maximal minors with holomorphic Laurent-polynomial
coefficients. In particular it is an exact ideal statement,
not a statement only about the radical.

*Proof.* Start with any monic polynomial \(p\in I\) supplied by
the projective certificate. Multiply by a power of \(t\), if
needed; this adds only roots equal to zero. Choose polynomial
representatives \(f_1,\ldots,f_s\) of the maximal minors by
multiplying each Laurent minor by a sufficiently large power
of \(t\). These are still members of \(I\).
Include \(p\) among these polynomial generators. Let \(J_0\)
be the polynomial ideal they generate. Its nonzero fiber
zeros are exactly \(\Sigma(v)\).

At \(v=v_0\), partition the roots of \(p\), retaining their
full multiplicities. Put in the first group every root which
belongs to \(\Sigma(v_0)\cup\{0\}\), and in the second group
all remaining roots. The two resulting monic factors are
coprime. They therefore extend to a holomorphic factorization
on a smaller neighborhood:
\[
                         p=p_{\rm good}p_{\rm bad}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Here is a direct justification of this factorization. On the
spaces of monic polynomials of the two fixed degrees, the
derivative of the multiplication map is
\[
 (\delta a,\delta b)\longmapsto
              p_{\rm bad}\delta a+p_{\rm good}\delta b.
\]
If it vanishes, coprimality forces \(p_{\rm good}\) to divide
\(\delta a\), and hence \(\delta a=0\) by its degree bound;
then \(\delta b=0\). Domain and range have the same dimension,
so the derivative is invertible. The holomorphic inverse
function theorem gives (4). If either group is empty, that
factor is one and the assertion is immediate.

The polynomial \(p_{\rm bad}(v_0,t)\) has no common zero with
all the generators of \(J_0(v_0)\). This includes the possible
zero root: it was assigned to \(p_{\rm good}\).
Thus the ideal generated by \(p_{\rm bad}\) and the \(f_i\)
is the unit ideal at \(v_0\).

This last statement can be lifted without taking a radical.
The quotient by the monic polynomial \(p\) is free of rank
\(D=\deg p\), with basis \(1,t,\ldots,t^{D-1}\).
In this quotient form the finite matrix whose columns are
the remainders of
\[
 t^k p_{\rm bad},\quad t^k f_i,\qquad 0\le k<D.
\]
At \(v_0\) its columns span the quotient: a one-variable
Bezout identity gives the element one, and multiplication
by \(t\) gives every basis element. Some \(D\)-column minor
is therefore nonzero. It remains a unit nearby. Solving
with those columns gives holomorphic polynomial coefficients
and an exact identity
\[
             u\,p_{\rm bad}+\sum_i b_i f_i+c\,p=1.
 \tag{5}
\]
Polynomial division by monic \(p\) explains the last term
and introduces no coefficient denominator.

Multiply (5) by \(p_{\rm good}\) and use (4). Every term
on the left belongs to \(J_0\), so
\(p_{\rm good}\in J_0\subset I\). Take \(p_0=p_{\rm good}\).
Expanding the finite ideal identities gives the claimed
row-minor expression. No multiplicity of the original ideal
has been discarded. \(\square\)

For every \(\epsilon>0\), continuity of the roots of a monic
polynomial now gives, after shrinking the neighborhood,
\[
 \max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{|t|:p_0(v,t)=0\}\bigr)
                                  \le r(u_0)+\epsilon.
 \tag{6}
\]
This remains true at repeated roots. It does not require a
continuous labeling of individual roots.

## 3. The local quantum equation still retains all normal data

Lift the finite maximal-minor expression for \(p_0\) by the
adjugate construction, always placing coefficients on the left.
Exactly as for the regular-arc certificate this gives
\[
                  \{p_0(v,E)I_d+hK_h(v,E)\}\mathcal Y_N=0.
 \tag{7}
\]
The order is fixed, and the coefficients of \(K_h\) are
holomorphic on a smaller neighborhood, including at \(h=0\).
The proof uses only the finite identity at \(h=0\) and
\(a(e^{2\pi ikh}v)-a(v)=O(h)\), with a holomorphic quotient
by \(h\). Thus it also applies to the holomorphic coefficients
in (4)--(5); rational coefficients are not necessary locally.

Equation (7) is a local equation for the actual grid values
and first normals. It follows by finite left combinations of
the original equations wherever all shifted coefficients
are defined. No global polynomial quotient identity is
asserted for these holomorphic multipliers. At interior
grid points with the fixed finite shift buffer inside the
chart, both original residual jets still vanish exactly.

Remove negative quantum powers by a fixed left shift, and
multiply by \(E-1\). These operations add only zero roots
and a root equal to one to the classical polynomial.
The highest classical coefficient is still a unit. The
remaining quantum shifts, including every order above the
classical order, are retained in \(hK_h\).

The regular-arc theorem applies to this equation with its
actual macroscopic initial packet and a right buffer.
Its first-normal block is triangular with \(p_0\) on both
diagonal blocks, so (6) is its spectral-radius bound as well.
The actual exponential ceiling on the whole source controls
the terminal fast data exactly as in that theorem.

## 4. An upper bound using the full principal support

For an angular coordinate \(a\), define a common budget for
all fixed-size actual initial packets by
\[
 A(a)=\sup_{R\in\mathbb N}
       \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
       \log\!\left(1+
       \max_{|j-\lfloor Na\rfloor|\le R}
       [\,\|y_N(j/N)\|+\|y_N'(j/N)\|\,]\right).
 \tag{8}
\]
It is finite by the actual \(\exp(CN)\) ceiling. Its definition
does not claim a polynomial bound or identify it with fixed-row
initialization near an endpoint.

**Theorem 2.** Fix \(a<t<b\) in the interior of \(J\). For the
actual source and any grid sequence \(t_N\to t\),
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
       \log(\|y_N(t_N)\|+\|y_N'(t_N)\|)
                      \le A(a)+\int_a^t\log r(u)\,du .
 \tag{9}
\]
The microscopic parameters are fixed in this statement.
No crossing of the projective exceptional set is permitted.

*Proof.* First, \(\log r\) is bounded and Riemann integrable
on \(J\). To see the required regularity without an assumption
about simple roots, use the finite rational-polynomial
generators of \(I\) at these fixed parameters. Over the
field \(\mathbb C(v)\), their Laurent gcd describes the
generic common nonzero roots. Outside finitely many zeros
of its coefficients and the denominators of a Bezout
expression, specialization has that same support.
Take its square-free part for this description of the set.
Outside its finite discriminant set the roots are local
analytic branches, and their largest modulus is continuous.
Any additional fiber support lies over the finite excluded
set. Its roots are bounded by the existing local monic
certificates. There are consequently at most finitely many
discontinuities of \(r\).

On a neighborhood of each regular continuity point, Lemma 1
and (6) give a monic exact equation whose root radius is
arbitrarily close from above to the nearby function \(r\).
At each of the finitely many discontinuities, use a fixed
such equation on a small neighborhood. Its radius is
bounded; these finitely many neighborhoods can have
arbitrarily small total length.
Compactness therefore gives a finite subdivision from
\(a\) to \(t\), with one such equation on a neighborhood
of each closed subinterval, for which the sum of the
integrated logarithmic radii is at most
\[
                            \int_a^t\log r(u)\,du+\epsilon.
 \tag{10}
\]
To make this construction explicit, first isolate the finite
discontinuity set. On its compact complement \(r\) is uniformly
continuous; use neighborhoods where (6) and its variation
are each at most a prescribed tolerance. A sufficiently fine
subdivision lies in this cover. At a transition use the
overlap of the adjacent open neighborhoods. Shrink the
discontinuity neighborhoods only after their finitely many
bounded certificates have been chosen. This proves (10).

Apply the regular-arc theorem successively on these
subintervals. Each chart extends beyond its right endpoint,
so it supplies a fixed positive right buffer; at the final
endpoint use the still available interval before \(b\).
The first packet has rate at most \(A(a)\).
At a later subdivision endpoint, the preceding estimate
bounds every fixed number of neighboring physical rows,
including their first normals: each is a sequence tending
to that same endpoint. Hence it bounds the complete finite
packet required by the next equation. The finitely many
limsup estimates can be combined. The higher-order fast
terms are suppressed using the actual source ceiling on
each right buffer, not by imposing an auxiliary boundary
condition.

The resulting upper rate is \(A(a)\) plus (10).
All charts, orders and buffers are fixed before \(N\) tends
to infinity. Then let \(\epsilon\) decrease to zero.
Definition (8) bounds the first packet for every finite
order which occurs in this process. This proves (9).
\(\square\)

## 5. Scope and direct sources

The arbitrary extra roots of one monic certificate no longer
enlarge the integral in (9). Roots which belong to the full
principal presentation remain. In particular this theorem
does not discard a branch because its source coefficient
has not yet been computed. An upper envelope of several
branches need not equal one geometric action.

The local argument also tolerates isolated finite fiber
components: their bounded neighborhoods contribute
arbitrarily little to the integral. It does not tolerate
shift infinity over a physical color. The resonant set,
endpoint transport, initial budget, comparison with the
filled geometric action, and noncancellation remain open
in the general partially filled problem.

The generic gcd reduction and the adjugate lift were
already present in the regular-arc note. The additional
step here is removing roots by a local holomorphic ideal
identity, preserving the actual ideal at special fibers,
and then patching the upper estimates. No literature
novelty claim is made.
