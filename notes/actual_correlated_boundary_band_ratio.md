## 4. Correlation survives the small odd-source denominator

Apply Cauchy's formula to the SAME R_N in (7) on the delta disk.
For |delta|<=2 it gives |D R_N|<=C/N^2. Therefore

    S_N(+1)/S_N(-1)
       =[B_N(+1)/B_N(-1)](1+O(N^-2)).                 (9)

The improved error is a correlation statement. Two unrelated relative
O(N^-1) estimates would not prove it. All source and model quotients
are nonzero here for sufficiently large N.

Let t range in a fixed real compact subset of (0,1), and retain the

    kappa_N(t)=1-q-t^d+q(1-t)^d.

    [S_N(+1)-q S_N(-1)]/[kappa_N S_N(-1)]
       =[B_N(+1)/B_N(-1)-q]/kappa_N + O(N^-1).        (10)

In particular this theorem retains the exact q, rather than replacing
it by one before a cancellation of order N^-1. The entire finite
model quotient, every cup character, internal channel label, clock
correction and half-index offset remains on the right of (10).

The constants are uniform for bounded total deficit, both parities,
and also in a sufficiently small fixed near-fold label neighborhood
0<=s_i<=eta N. Thus they apply to the shrinking window
sum_i s_i log N=o(N). This is a ROOT ratio result and does not expand
the previously proved generic-normal window.

No assertion is made that the right side of (10) has a nonzero limit,
or even stays bounded. In particular replacing its finite label
dependence by the complete parabolic stationary value would require
an additional argument. Formula (10) supplies an explicit model with
an absolute O(N^-1) error relative to S_N(-1), even when its leading
finite quotient has not been evaluated.
