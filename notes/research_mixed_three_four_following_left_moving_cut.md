# A calibrated moving lower cut on the following-family left taper

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The lower physical transform
sector of the actual following-family three/four prefix is paid by a
moving real cut whose final value is the original ordinary cutoff
\(1/8\). The complete first-cap source remains coupled to the literal
conditional row. This resolves the lower real-face mismatch with the
older \(3/20\) transport, without identifying the two faces.

The same rectangle has uniform strict horizontal concavity and
vertical convexity. These curvature statements do not by themselves
pay the remaining horizontal aliases or the endpoints of all vertical
currents. No complete left-taper source theorem is asserted here.
No numerical evaluation is used.

## 2. Positive transform force on the moving real cut

Set \(B=e^{-2\pi h}\) and \(R_z=e^{2\pi h_z}\). The source
guards imply \(B>5/6\), \(R_z>1/2\). The first inequality is

[the following-domain lower-row lemma](research_mixed_four_upper_rows_following_domains.md),
Section 6. For the second,
\(.204\pi<2/3<\log2\), where the last inequality is the first
strict positive-term bound for \(2\operatorname{atanh}(1/3)\).
Direct differentiation of (4) gives the chirp-aware identity
\[
 e^{\Re Q_t(v;a,z)}
 =\frac{|B-e^{-2\pi i(x+v)}|\,
              |1-R_z e^{-2\pi i(Z+v)}|}
              {(2\sin\pi v)^3}.
 \tag{9}
\]
For \(.11\le v\le.125\), the first shifted real part is in
\([.21,.345]\), so its cosine is less than \(1/4\).
The squared first modulus in (9), minimized over \(B\ge5/6\),
is greater than \(23/18>(11/10)^2\). The second shifted
real part is in \([.35,.485]\), so its cosine is less than
\(-4/7\). Its squared modulus is greater than
\[
                 1+\frac14+\frac47=\frac{51}{28}>\frac{16}{9}.
\]
Here radial monotonicity is valid because this cosine is negative.
Finally \(2\sin(\pi/8)<4/5\). Consequently
\[
 e^{\Re Q_t}>\frac{(11/10)(4/3)}{(4/5)^3}
             =\frac{275}{96}>e,\qquad \Re Q_t>1 .
 \tag{10}
\]
For an elementary exponential witness,
\(e<5/2+(1/6)/(1-1/4)=49/18<11/4<275/96\).

If \(\lambda\ge0\) and \(x\ge.18\), then \(B\ge1\),
\(v=s(x)\ge.12\), and \(x+v\ge.30\). The first modulus

The last bound uses \(\log2>2/3\).

For clarity, the small trigonometric guards above are purely

For \(\cos(.42\pi)<1/4\), write it as \(\sin(.08\pi)\),

The same bounds give \(\sin(.08\pi)>6/25\) by

## 5. The complete lower physical sector

Set \(L_N(x)=\lceil Ns(x)\rceil\) and keep the exact partial sum
\[
 C^{\rm lo}_{1,N}(X,Y;x)
      =\sum_{j=0}^{L_N(x)-1}
                  X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)/P_j^3 .
 \tag{28}
\]
Every index in (28) has \(j/N<s(x)\le1/8\). Lemma 2 of
[the following-domain row theorem](research_mixed_four_upper_rows_following_domains.md)
proves the real row rate is strictly increasing on \(0<t\le.15\)
throughout the present incoming and outgoing domains. Its continuous
value at \(t=0\) is zero. The proof retains the chirp, all numerator
zeros and every initially or finally revived row; no triangular
support assumption is made. Consequently, for every \(\tau>0\),
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{L_N(x)-1}
       \left|X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)/P_j^3\right|
 \le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
              e^{N\{\Re Q(s(x);a(x),z)+\tau\}} .
 \tag{29}
\]
This follows from the same uniform zero-safe product estimates and
denominator error \(O(\log N)\) as its equation (23).
The moving cut never requires differentiating \(L_N(x)\).
At its finitely many jumps, either adjacent physical partition
satisfies (29).

Combining (27)--(29) with the complete cap source proves:

**Theorem 2.** Uniformly for \(c_0\in K\), both parities, and
\(.1\le x\le.22\), the whole original cap multiplied by the
complete lower physical sector has a strict normalized exponential
gap. More precisely, after fixing sufficiently small neighborhoods
of \((w,z)=(-2,q(c_0))\), the taper (5) is held fixed at its
base reference, and
\[
 \left|
 e^{-N\mathcal P(w,z)}
 U_{0,N}(w,e^{-2\pi ia(x)})
 C^{\rm lo}_{1,N}
     (e^{-2\pi ia(x)},e^{-2\pi iz};x)
 \right|
 \le C N^B e^{-N/4000}.
 \tag{30}
\]
The neighborhoods and constants can be chosen uniformly over
each compact \(K\). The same assertion holds for every prescribed
fixed normalized mixed derivative in \(w,z\), on smaller
neighborhoods, and for the exact integral over any fixed subinterval
with the original prefactor \(N\) and taper Jacobian \(a'(x)\).

Indeed (27) has raw gap \(1/2000\); choose all source and
parameter tolerances strictly below its unused half. For each
fixed \(x\), (28) is a polynomial in \(Y\), and the cap is the
literal whole polynomial in \(w\). The zero-safe finite bounds
extend to a fixed small parameter neighborhood before Cauchy's
formula is applied. Compactness gives uniform neighborhoods;
polynomial factors absorb the sum over all rows, fixed jets and
the original prefactor \(N\). This is an absolute estimate for
the finite source, with no division by \(C_1\), \(H_N\), or a
possibly vanishing source row.

## 6. Uniform curvature on the central rectangle

There is a useful additional geometric estimate on the same
source domain. Write \(C(w)=\Re\cot\pi w\).

**Lemma 3.** For \(x\in[.1,.22]\), arbitrary incoming height
\(h\), \(Z\in[.24,.36]\), \(h_z\in[-.102,-.001]\), and
\[
                    .11\le v\le.5,\qquad |\eta|\le.12,
\]
the original phase (4) satisfies
\[
 -\Re Q_{tt}(v+i\eta;x+ih,z)
  =\pi\{3C(v+i\eta)-C(x+ih+v+i\eta)-C(z+v+i\eta)\}
  >\pi/84 .
 \tag{31}
\]

*Proof.* Since \((2\pi\eta)^2<3/5\),
\[
 \cosh(2\pi\eta)
 \le1+\frac{(2\pi\eta)^2/2}{1-(2\pi\eta)^2/12}
 <25/19<4/3 .
 \tag{32}
\]
For a shifted real coordinate \(r\in(0,1/2)\),
\(C(r+iy)\le\cot(\pi r)\), at every height. For
\(r\ge1/2\) in the present regular strip it is nonpositive.
In particular a negative real cotangent is never substituted
as an upper bound at an arbitrary height.

On \([.11,7/40]\), the own sine exceeds \(3/5\), its
cosine exceeds \(2/5\), and hence \(3C>27/14\).
The two shifted upper bounds are \(4/3\) and \(7/12\),
since their real coordinates are at least \(.21,.35\).
Thus the difference is greater than \(1/84\).
The first guard follows from
\(\tan y\ge y+y^3/3\) at \(y>.21(25/8)=21/32\),
which gives \(\tan(.21\pi)>3/4\).
For the second, \(\cot(.35\pi)=\tan(.15\pi)\) and
\[
 \tan(.15\pi)<
 \frac{33/70}{1-(33/70)^2/2}
       =4620/8711<7/12 .
 \tag{33}
\]

On \([7/40,1/4]\), the own sine exceeds \(4/5\),
its cosine is nonnegative, and \(3C>9/5\).
The shifted upper bounds are \(1\) and \(7/12\).
Their difference is greater than \(13/60\).

On \([1/4,2/5]\), the own sine exceeds \(1/2\) and
its denominator is less than \(7/3\), so \(3C>9/14\).
The shifted upper bounds are \(7/12\) and \(1/25\):
the second uses real coordinate at least \(.49\) and
\(\cot(.49\pi)=\tan(\pi/100)<1/25\).
The difference is greater than \(41/2100>1/84\).

On \([2/5,1/2]\), the own cotangent is nonnegative
and the first shifted cotangent is nonpositive.
The second shifted real coordinate lies in \([.64,.86]\);
its sine has magnitude greater than \(1/2\).
Its height has magnitude at most \(.222\). Now
\((2\pi\cdot.222)^2<2\), so the same cosh series bound
gives \(\cosh(2\pi\cdot.222)<11/5\). Its cotangent
denominator is less than \(16/5\), and therefore its
negative real cotangent is less than \(-5/32\).
The remaining difference exceeds \(5/32>1/84\).
This proves (31). \(\square\)
