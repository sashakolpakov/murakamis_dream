# The original turn applied to transported Gaussian currents

Private written proof, 15 November 2026.

The first-order Gaussian transport has polynomial cost in the full
physical jet norm. The original turn does not have such a bound in
that norm, even on the currents generated by the Gaussian endpoint
defect. This note gives the exact turn extension needed after a
one-sided shift, including its odd-level correction, and proves an
exponential lower bound on the turned current. Its ordinary closing
is nevertheless exactly zero. The result concerns the estimate
needed for an attached component, not failure of a knot asymptotic.

## 1. The turn with the original closing retained

Use the conventions of the
[first-order transport theorem](research_first_order_gaussian_jet_transport.md):
\[
 D=N-1,\quad q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad
 \eta=(-1)^D,\quad
 \mathcal B_N=\mathbb C[w,w^{-1}]/((w^N+1)^2),
 \quad \delta=w^N+1.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(Uh(w)=h(Qw)\), \(\iota h(w)=h(w^{-1})\), and
\(\mathcal A_N=\mathcal B_N^\iota\) is the original Casimir
algebra under \(z=w+w^{-1}\). Put \(P=(1+\iota)/2\).
At \(w_j=q^{2j+1}\), let \(\dot h_j\) denote the first
logarithmic jet. This is a jet evaluation, not a derivation on
the quotient. The physical norm is
\[
 \|h\|_N=\max_j\left(|h(w_j)|+|\dot h_j|/N\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
Let \(p_j\) have value one at node \(j\), zero at every other
node, and zero first jets everywhere. Thus
\(\dot{(\delta p_j)}_a=-N\mathbf1_{a=j}\).

The two perfect closing extensions \(\Lambda_\pm\) have average
\(\Lambda\), with
\[
 \Lambda(h)=-\frac\eta2\sum_j\dot{((w-w^{-1})h)}_j.
 \tag{3}
\]
They agree with the original \(\phi_N\) on \(\mathcal A_N\).
At even levels they equal \(\Lambda\). At odd levels, with
\(*=(N-1)/2\),
\[
 \Lambda_\pm(h)=\Lambda(h)\mp\frac\eta2\dot h_*.
 \tag{4}
\]

The [original source compiler](research_arbitrary_power_source_compilation.md),
Section 1, gives the exact turn kernel
\[
 K_N(z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^D e_j(z)e_j(y),\qquad
 (\mathsf T f)(z)=\phi_{N,y}(K_N(z,y)f(y)),
 \quad \mathsf T e_j=e_{D-j}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Define maps from the doubled algebra to the physical algebra by
\[
 (\mathscr T_\pm h)(z)=
       \Lambda_{\pm,w}(K_N(z,w+w^{-1})h(w)).
 \tag{6}
\]
These definitions retain the original kernel and the chosen closing
extension. They do not declare an additional geometric turn on all
of \(\mathcal B_N\).

**Proposition 1.** At even levels \(\mathscr T_\pm=\mathsf T P\).
At odd levels,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathscr T_\pm h=\mathsf T P h
       \mp\frac\eta2K_N(z,-2)\dot h_* .\quad}
 \tag{7}
\]
In both cases their average is \(\mathsf T P\), and their
restriction to \(\mathcal A_N\) is the original \(\mathsf T\).
For every integer \(k\), the exact shifted formula is
\[
 (\mathscr T_\pm U^k h)(z)=
 \Lambda_\pm\left(h\,V_{\pm,k}
                 [K_N(z,w+w^{-1})]\right),
 \qquad V_{\pm,k}=t_\pm^{-1}U^{-k}t_\pm,
 \tag{8}
\]
where \(t_\pm\) are the units of the first-order theorem.

**Proof.** Both the kernel and \(\Lambda\) are inversion-invariant.
Consequently \(\Lambda(K_Nh)=\Lambda(K_NPh)=\mathsf TPh\).
At the odd fixed node \(w=-1\), the kernel's logarithmic jet is
zero: \(w-w^{-1}=0\). Applying (4) to its product with \(h\)
therefore gives precisely (7). An invariant \(h\) has
\(\dot h_*=0\), proving the restriction statement. Equation (8)
is the exact adjoint identity of the first-order theorem applied
to the displayed kernel. All products and first jets are retained.
\(\square\)

The same adjoint identity applies when the kernel is multiplied by
the other factors at an attachment. Its bound
\(\|V_{\pm,k}\|\le3N\) is independent of \(k\), but is relative
to the norm of that full spectator. Formula (8) does not estimate
the spectator. At odd levels the two extensions cannot be exchanged
after a shift without retaining (7).

## 2. An exponential response inside the actual current orbit

Let
\[
 b=\lfloor N/2\rfloor-1,\qquad
 c_b=2\cos((2b+1)\pi/N),\qquad
 s_b=2\sin((2b+1)\pi/N).
 \tag{9}
\]
This is a double Casimir node at both parities. Its two Laurent
nodes have indices \(b,D-b\), which are distinct. Let \(\Xi_b\)
have derivative one at \(c_b\), zero derivatives at all other
double nodes, and value zero at every physical node.

**Proposition 2.** The physical element
\[
 f_b=-\delta(p_b-p_{D-b})
     =\frac{N}{i s_b}\Xi_b
     =-2P U^{D-b}(\delta p_D)
 \tag{10}
\]
has norm exactly one, and
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathsf T f_b=-i\eta N s_b K_N(\,\cdot\,,c_b).
 \quad}
 \tag{11}
\]
In particular, if \(G=\sum_{r\ge0}(-1)^r/(2r+1)^2\) is
Catalan's constant, then at both level parities
\[
 \|\mathsf T\|_{\mathcal A_N,\|\cdot\|_N}
 \ \ge\ \|\mathsf T f_b\|_N
 \ \ge\ \exp\left\{\frac{4G}{\pi}N-O(\log N)\right\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Yet its original closing satisfies
\[
                         \phi_N(\mathsf T f_b)=0.
 \tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** The logarithmic jets of the first expression in (10)
are \(+N,-N\) at the two indicated Laurent nodes; all values
and other jets vanish. Since the logarithmic derivatives of
\(z=w+w^{-1}\) there are \(+i s_b,-i s_b\), the second expression
and \(\|f_b\|_N=1\) follow. Also
\(U^{D-b}p_D=p_b\), \(\iota p_b=p_{D-b}\), and
\(\iota\delta=-\delta\), proving the last expression in (10).

The original trace at a double node is
\(\eta[(4-c_b^2)h'(c_b)-c_bh(c_b)]\). In (5), the product
\(K_N(z,y)f_b(y)\) has zero values at every node and only one
nonzero normal. Thus (5) gives
\[
 \mathsf T f_b
 =\eta s_b^2\frac{N}{i s_b}K_N(z,c_b),
\]
which is (11). Evaluating at \(c_b\) and retaining one nonnegative
square gives, for \(r=\lfloor N/4\rfloor\),
\[
 \|\mathsf T f_b\|_N
 \ge N s_b K_N(c_b,c_b)
 \ge N s_b e_r(c_b)^2.
 \tag{14}
\]
Here \(r\le b\) for all \(N\ge2\). The original Newton formula
and \(c_k-c_b=4\sin((b+k+1)\pi/N)\sin((b-k)\pi/N)\)
give the exact positive expression
\[
 e_r(c_b)=\frac{S_{b+r}}{S_{b-r}S_r^2},\qquad
 S_j=\prod_{k=1}^j2\sin(k\pi/N).
 \tag{15}
\]

For completeness, the required factorial estimate is elementary.
Put \(J(x)=\int_0^x\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt\). On any interval
\([0,a]\) with \(a<1\), the difference
\(\log(2\sin\pi t)-\log t\) extends smoothly to zero.
Its Riemann-sum error is bounded. Comparing the increasing function
\(\log t\) with its integral gives
\(\log(j!)-j\log j+j=O(\log(j+1))\). Hence
\[
             \log S_j=NJ(j/N)+O(\log N)
 \tag{16}
\]
uniformly for \(j\le aN\). Integrating the Abel-regularized
series for \(\log|1-e^{2\pi it}|\), and then letting the Abel
parameter increase to one, gives
\[
 J(x)=-\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{k\ge1}
                         \frac{\sin(2\pi kx)}{k^2}.
\]
The integrated series is absolutely convergent; the logarithmic
endpoint singularity is integrable. Therefore
\(J(1/4)=-G/(2\pi)\) and \(J(3/4)=G/(2\pi)\).
The three arguments in (15) tend to \(3/4,1/4,1/4\), with
errors \(O(N^{-1})\). Equations (15)--(16) imply
\[
             \log e_r(c_b)=2GN/\pi+O(\log N).
\]
Finally \(4\le N s_b\le2N\), by the nonfixed-node estimate in
the first-order theorem. This proves (12).

For (13), the original pairing gives
\(\phi_N(e_j)=\delta_{j,D}\), so
\(\phi_{N,z}(K_N(z,c_b))=e_D(c_b)=0\).
The last equality follows directly from the factor \(c_b-z\)
in \(e_D\), since \(b<D\). Apply (11). \(\square\)

## 3. Consequence for attachment estimates

For every nonzero even \(m\), the actual Gaussian endpoint defect
is \(m\delta p_D\). Thus (10) lies in its shifted current module
after invariant projection. The exponential response in (12)
already occurs on that module; it is not inferred from an arbitrary
matrix example. If another norm is polynomially equivalent to
\(\|\cdot\|_N\), its turn operator has the same exponential lower
rate, since the equivalence changes (14) by only a polynomial factor.

This rules out extending the polynomial first-order estimate
through arbitrary original turns by bounding each current separately
in this norm. It does not rule out an estimate that keeps their
joint contraction with the attached exterior. Equation (13) makes
the distinction exact: a large full-Hermite turn response can have
zero original cap closing. No assertion is made that \(f_b\) is the
uninserted state of a fixed knot, or that a geometric coefficient
vanishes. The kernel identity (5) and the first-order adjoint (8)
are existing source identities; the additional conclusion is their
explicit action and exponential norm cost on the generated currents.
