## 2. A sharper horizontal transport cost

The exact marginal at the fixed real private point is
\[
             \Psi_{0,z}(b,y+ic)=g'(b+y+ic)-g'(y+ic).           \tag{6}
\]
Here \(x\le y\le a_*<.391\).
The original height range gives
\(r=e^{2\pi c}<23/20\), so the numerator modulus is less than
\(43/20\).

For \(.1\le y\le.25\), minimizing the denominator modulus
over positive radii gives
\[
 |1-re^{-2\pi iy}|\ge\sin(2\pi y)
                         \ge\sin(\pi/5)>\frac{29}{50}.
\]
For \(.25\le y\le.391\), its angular cosine is nonpositive,
so this denominator is at least one. Consequently
\[
 \Re\Psi_{0,z}(b,y+ic)<
 \begin{cases}
 4/3,&.1\le y\le.25,\\
 4/5,&.25\le y\le.391.
 \end{cases}                                                  \tag{7}
\]
The logarithmic witnesses are elementary:
\[
 e^{4/3}>\frac{911}{243}>\frac{215}{58},\qquad
 e^{4/5}>\frac{827}{375}>\frac{43}{20}.                        \tag{8}
\]
The displayed lower bounds are the first five and first four
positive exponential terms, respectively.
For the sine bound, use
\(\sin^2(\pi/5)=(5-\sqrt5)/8>11/32>(29/50)^2\).
The radius bound is the one already proved in the early cap
half-stencils.

Integrating (7) from \(a_*\) back to \(x\), using (3), proves
\[
 \Re\Psi_0(b,x+ic)>1-C(x),\qquad
 C(x)=\frac43(.25-x)+\frac45(.391-.25)
                      =\frac{1673}{3750}-\frac43x.            \tag{9}
\]
The reference satisfies \(a_*>.25\), so the two subintervals
used for this bound are legitimate. The final upper endpoint

## 3. The actual height change and the true-end reserve

At the fixed real \(x\), put
\[
 N=1-re^{-2\pi i(b+x)},\qquad D=1-re^{-2\pi ix},
\]
now with \(r=e^{2\pi y}\) on the vertical segment between
\(c\) and \(h\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \Im(N\overline D)
   =2r\sin\pi b\{\cos\pi(2x+b)-r\cos\pi b\}<0.                \tag{10}
\]
Indeed \(.576<2x+b<.878\) and \(0<b<1/2\).
Both original factors have positive real part.
The numerator argument has real coordinate
\(.476<b+x<.658\), where its angular cosine is negative.
For the denominator use
\(r\le23/20\) and
\(\cos(2\pi x)\le\cos(\pi/5)<13/16\);
thus \(r\cos(2\pi x)<299/320<1\).

Their original argument difference lies in \((-\pi,\pi)\).
Equation (10) therefore gives, with no logarithmic lift change,
\[
 0<\partial_y\Re\Psi_0(b,x+iy)
        =-\Im\{g'(b+x+iy)-g'(x+iy)\}<\pi.                    \tag{11}
\]
If \(c\le0\), raising the target height to \(h\) increases
the bare phase. If \(c\ge0\), lowering it costs less than

\[

 \begin{cases}

 \end{cases}                                                  \tag{12}

The complete literal cap theorem for

\[

For \(c\ge0\), retain the correlation in (4):
\[

Then
\[
 \begin{split}

 &>\frac{193}{500}-C(x)-\pi c-\pi h\\

This is why an independent positive-height enlargement would lose

**Theorem 1.** On every actual source family (1)--(4),
