## 2. First main and real-half profiles

The accepted

pays every first main \(s=v+ih_s\), \(1/8\le v\le1/2\),
paired with every next main, real low, real half, or last profile,
with reserve \(1/100\), for both (5).
The [complete upper-row theorem](research_mixed_next_three_selected_left_upper_rows.md)
gives the same continuous reserve for every positive upper

Sections 4--5 of
[selected-left bounded-prefix attachment](research_mixed_next_three_selected_left_bounded_prefix.md)
reduce the remaining next pieces to those same profiles. Those
current comparisons are pointwise in the outside factors; no
bounded-prefix hypothesis is needed to use the comparisons themselves.

The first real half profile costs very little relative to its
actual negative-height half profile. At \(s=1/2+i\sigma\),
\(h_s\le\sigma\le0\), write
\[
J_1(\sigma)=\Im Q_{1,s}(1/2+i\sigma;a,y).
\]
The incoming argument is at least \(-\pi x\), since
\(h_a+\sigma<0\); its chirp contributes \(2\pi x\), and
the own argument is zero. If \(\sigma\le-H\), the outgoing
argument is at least \(-\pi q\). Therefore
\[
J_1(\sigma)\ge\pi(x-q)\ge0\qquad(\sigma\le-H).
\tag{8}
\]
For \(-H\le\sigma\le0\), put \(k=H+\sigma\). The exact
outgoing argument is
\[
\arg(1+e^{2\pi k-2\pi iq})
 =-\pi q-\arctan\{\tanh(\pi k)\tan(\pi q)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(q\le.22<1/4\), the last arctangent is less than
\(\pi H\). Thus \(J_1\ge-\pi H\), and integration gives
\[
\Re Q_1(1/2;a,y)
 <\Re Q_1(1/2+ih_s;a,y)+\pi H^2
 <\Re Q_1(1/2+ih_s;a,y)+1/3000.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(h_s>-H\), only a shorter subinterval of (9) is used.
All next factors are held fixed in this comparison. The first
real half is consequently paid against every next controlling
profile and upper physical row, with reserve greater than
\(1/100-1/3000>1/125\).

## 5. Every first physical row

For a real first index \(0<v\le1/8\), the folded incoming
modulus is at least \(2\sin(11\pi/50)>5/4\), since
\(x+v\in[.22,.575]\) and its folded radius is at least one.
The selected outgoing modulus is at least \(2\sin\pi v\),
since \(R>1\) and \(q+v\in[v,.345]\). Also
\(2\sin(\pi/8)<4/5\). The real index force consequently
exceeds \(125/64>1\). All first low physical rows increase
to the real cut in Section 3, including the exact zero row.

For upper rows let \(E_1\) be the original first real rate
and \(\Phi(v)=E_1(1-v)-L_1\). Exact reflection gives
\[
\Phi(v)=3J(v)
 -\int_0^v\log|T_a-e^{-2\pi i(x-b)}|\,db
 -\int_0^v\log|1-Re^{-2\pi i(q-b)}|\,db,
\quad T_a=e^{-2\pi h_a}<8/5.
\tag{19}
\]
This retains the outgoing mean \(\log R\) inside (16).
For \(v\le1/5\), the first arc lies in \([0,1/2]\)
and is at least \(J(v)\). The second is at least its
unit-radius centered arc \(2J(v/2)\), since \(R\ge1\)
increases each modulus above its unit chord. The original
Jensen inequality \(2[J(v)-J(v/2)]<0\) for \(0<v\le1/5\) proves
\(\Phi(v)\le0\).

For \(1/5\le v\le1/2\), the incoming modulus on the
complementary integration interval has square less than
\(21/5\), exactly as in Section 2 of
[the own-four upper-row theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_own_four_upper_rows.md).
Its logarithm is less than \(3/4\). The outgoing modulus
is at most \(1+R<31/15<e^{3/4}\); the last comparison
follows from the exponential polynomial through degree three.
Therefore
\[
\Phi(v)-\Phi(1/2)\le\tfrac32(1/2-v)+3J(v).
\tag{20}
\]
This upper bound is convex. It vanishes at \(1/2\), whereas
at \(1/5\) it is less than \(-3/1144\): the original
written witnesses give
\[
-J(1/5)>63/440+(1/20)\log(7/6)>63/440+1/130,
\quad3(63/440+1/130)-9/20=3/1144.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the complete first positive upper block reduces to its
real-half and last rates. The zero-safe uniform numerator and
sine-factorial estimates preserve this reduction at every
literal index, including both half-grid parities and \(j=D\).

## 6. All first currents and joins

Use the original canonical interpolation, cuts \(1/8,1/2\),
main height \(h_s\), and alias heights \(-1/5,+4\).
Write \(\mathcal A(v,\sigma)=\arg(1-e^{2\pi\sigma-2\pi iv})\)
on the original strip. For \(1/8\le v\le1/2\),
\[
J_1(v,\sigma)=\Im Q_{1,s}(v+i\sigma;a,y)
=\mathcal A(x+v,h_a+\sigma)+\mathcal A(q+v,H+\sigma)
 -3\mathcal A(v,\sigma)+2\pi x.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(q\le x\), splitting at the two shifted half-lines
gives the following bounds, all divided by \(\pi\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&x+v<1/2&q+v<1/2\le x+v&1/2\le q+v\\\hline
\text{upper, all heights}&2-4v-2q&1-2v-2q+2x&2x\\
\text{lower, }\sigma\le0&-3/2+3v+2x&-1+x+2v&x-2q\\
\text{lower, }\sigma\ge0&-5/2+5v+2x-q&-3/2+3v-q&-1+2v-2q.
\end{array}
\tag{23}
\]
For the last line use \(H+\sigma>0\) in the outgoing
argument; the incoming argument uses its all-height lower
bound. For the middle line \(h_a+\sigma<0\), while the
outgoing argument retains its all-height lower bound.
Consequently
\[
J_1<33\pi/20,\qquad
J_1>-137\pi/200\ (\sigma\le0),\qquad
J_1>-331\pi/200\ (\sigma\ge0).
\tag{24}
\]
Both weighted sides at both cuts decrease away from height
zero. Integrating these bounds from the actual main height
shows that the lower horizontal alias loses more than
\(7\pi/100\), and the upper one loses more than \(\pi\):
\[
8-(331/200)4-(137/200)(51/500)
 =131013/100000>1.
\tag{25}
\]

The unweighted joins are also retained. Put
\[
C(v,\sigma)=\Re\cot\pi(v+i\sigma),\qquad w=\cot\pi v.
\]
For \(h_s\le\sigma\le0\), the bound
\(\cosh(2\pi\sigma)<5/4\) from the
[complete-current source, Section 5](research_mixed_next_lower_left_complete_currents.md)
applies. For \(v<1/2\), since \(\cos2\pi v\le\sqrt2/2<3/4\),
\(C(v,\sigma)\) decreases in its real argument on
\([v,1/2]\), and \(2C(v,\sigma)>w\).
The incoming positive cotangent is at most \(C(v,\sigma)\),
because its height is more negative. The outgoing positive
cotangent is at most \(\cot\pi(q+v)\le w\), regardless
of its height. Hence
\[
3C(v,\sigma)-C(x+v,h_a+\sigma)-C(q+v,H+\sigma)>0.
\tag{26}
\]
At the half cut the incoming shifted cotangent gives strictness
directly. Equation (26) is convexity of the first real phase in
the private height, so both unweighted joins are bounded by
their original main and real-cut endpoint profiles.

## 7. Literal finite assembly and the seam

First fix \(\epsilon>0\) and \(\epsilon\le q\le q_0\).
For each conditional factor put
\[
L_N=\lfloor N\ell\rfloor+1,\quad M_N=\lceil N/2\rceil,
\quad\ell_N=(L_N-1/2)/N,\quad b_N=(M_N-1/2)/N,
\tag{27}
\]
with \(\ell=1/8\) or \(3/20\). Keep the low indices
\(j<L_N\), middle indices \(L_N\le j<M_N\), and upper
indices \(M_N\le j<N\). The exact cotangent decomposition
of each middle sum has its original \(+N\) main measure,
two horizontal aliases and four weighted vertical halves.
Moving its main line to the reference height retains both
oriented unweighted joins.

Sections 2--6 and the accepted next-current theorem cover
every pair of these pieces before integration. Both next
weight alternatives remain, and their full interpolation
remainder is bounded by the original absolute Fourier theorem.
All shifted arguments are in regular strips. Half-grid
excursions of size \(O(1/N)\) consume an arbitrarily small
uniform tolerance in the strict comparisons. The physical
estimates retain every numerator zero and every end row.
The complete cap supplies \(B_U(x)\) for all real ordinary
colors, with its own arbitrary small tolerance.

The common continuous profile reserve is greater than \(1/125\).
Choose all finite-factorial, interpolation, cap and half-grid
losses together below \(1/1000\). Finite sums, bounded path
lengths and original factors of \(N\) cost only a polynomial.
Thus the complete product has rate at most
\(\Lambda_{123}-7/1000\) on this compact q interval.

To cover \(q=0\), use the complete finite product rather
than any canonical seam symbol. Its Laurent support in \(Y\)
is contained in
\[
[-D-N,\,2D-N],
\tag{28}
\]
because the first kernel has outgoing degree \(D\), the
Fourier polynomial has degree \(D\), and the next incoming
band is \([-D,0]\). All frequencies have modulus less
than \(2N\). The fixed short-arc Chebyshev extension in
Section 6 of the selected-left bounded-prefix proof therefore
applies verbatim: choose the arc and ellipse for loss less
than \(1/1000\), then choose its left endpoint \(\epsilon\)
inside the resulting fixed extension width. This covers

The residual gap exceeds \(6/1000>1/200\), proving (6).

At the actual output,
\(\Lambda_{123}(z_*)=\Re\mathcal P_0^+(-2,z_*)\).
After the complete first source is assembled, its color and
outgoing polynomial degrees are at most \(D\). The original
polynomial/arc continuation and Cauchy argument consequently
give all fixed normalized local mixed jets of (6)--(7), with
the weaker gap \(1/1000\), on sufficiently small fixed
ordinary-color and actual-output neighborhoods. All reference
heights, cutoffs, paths and labels are held fixed. There is no
division by a Fourier factor or by an individual coefficient.

This proves only the central first-incoming part on the selected
left shared interval. The initial, tapered, right-horizontal,
corner and far-real incoming pieces require their own source
bounds. The old filter is unchanged; its separately paid
correction does not supply those missing pieces. No complete
Fourier coefficient or noncancellation conclusion follows here.
