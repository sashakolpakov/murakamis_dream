# Fixed second height and the remaining first outer join

Private source-scope deduction, 10 November 2026. The second retained
height can be kept at its actual reference value in an exact first
outer contour. The existing complete first-collar theorem permits
that choice. Its first-angle domain, however, does not reach the
early cut at one tenth. This note records the exact remaining face
estimate and an algebraic obstruction to inferring it from bounds
for two different Fourier alternatives. It proves no new bound for
that face.

## 1. The second height need not move

Fix an actual positive three/four open word, with its original
reference \(r_j=a_j+ic_j\), marking, scalar and ordinary boundary
variables. Write its first flags as \(\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}\).
The literal allocated first pair is
\[
 \mathcal P_{\alpha\beta\sigma,N}(w;a,b)
 =U_{\alpha,N}(w,X)C_{\beta,N}(X,Y)
       d_N(X)^\alpha X^{-N\sigma},\qquad
 X=e^{-2\pi ia},\quad Y=e^{-2\pi ib},\quad
 \sigma\in\{0,\alpha\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Its outgoing allocation is the nonnegative modulus
\(|d_N(Y)|^{\beta/2}\). The next incidence retains the other half.
In particular, no half of the first \(d_N(X)\) may be omitted.

The exact torus composition theorem, Sections 1--3, makes the
complete density a Laurent polynomial in every retained variable.
Keep \(b=z+ic_2\), with \(z\in P=[11/50,9/20]\), fixed while the
first coordinate follows
\[
 0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow L+ih
 \longrightarrow R+ih\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow1.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(0<L<R<1\), this is an exact constant-term cycle. Indeed every
nonconstant Fourier monomial has an entire periodic primitive, and
the integral of \(da\) along (2) is one. The same proof applies
with the other retained variables on their original cycles and
with fixed ordinary-color derivatives. All vertical pieces and
both of their orientations remain present. The original outside
scalar is unchanged.

Thus moving the second height is not imposed by the exact source.
It is a feature of the older particular hybrid graph. Keeping
\(c_2\) fixed avoids its additional second-incidence height debit,
which is explicitly retained in the two-incidence reduction,
Section 4. It does not by itself bound the new first-coordinate
vertical pieces.

## 2. What the complete real collar already permits

The full-plateau second-target collar theorem, Section 1, permits
the second unselected height \(k\) to be any point between \(c_2\)
and zero. In particular \(k=c_2\) is admissible, for every
\(z\in P\). Its Section 5, equation (23), is a complete finite
prefix estimate, including all first selections, both parities,
ordinary-color neighborhoods and fixed jets. Its original first
pair reference is
\[
 M_{12}=\Re\{\mathcal T_\alpha(r_1,-2)
       +\phi_{0\beta}(r_1,r_2)-\alpha g(r_1)\},\qquad
 p_2=\partial_b\phi_{0\beta}(r_1,r_2).
\tag{3}
\]
The real marginal \(\Re p_2(z-a_2)\) cancels against the next
incidence exactly as before. No replacement reference is required.
This theorem has \(m\ge4\) and an ordinary cut \(3\le h\le m-1\);
its protected-middle application retains its stated chart and
safety conditions.

The first angle in that theorem is only
\[
                         0\le x_1\le\rho,\qquad
                         0<\rho\le1/1000.
\tag{4}
\]
The explicit source cost is
\[
 C_\alpha(\rho)=2\Delta_\rho+\alpha\omega_\rho,\qquad
 \Delta_\rho=\rho\log2-J(\rho),\qquad
 J(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi s)\,ds,
\tag{5}
\]
with the collar chosen sufficiently small that
\(C_1(\rho)<1/200\). Here \(\omega_\rho\) is a nonnegative
true Fourier upper allowance. Both copies of \(\Delta_\rho\)
come from literal finite products. The first selection has unit
modulus on the real collar, so it introduces no height penalty
there.

The selected-four collar profile, Section 4, equation (11), retains
the same restriction (4). The real zero-collar extension enlarges
the *other* angle to \([.1,.5]\); its low angle still lies in
\([0,.001]\), and both heights are zero. Neither result supplies
the first point \(a=.1\) with its neighbor at \(c_2\).

There is also an explicit limitation to enlarging (5) without
changing its proof. Since \(\sin\pi s\le\pi s\),
\[
 \Delta_{1/10}\ge
 \frac1{10}\{1+\log(10/\pi)\}>\frac15.
\tag{6}
\]
For the strict inequality use \(\pi<10/3\) and \(\log3>1\);
the latter follows, for example, from
\(e=\sum_{n\ge0}1/n!<3\).
Thus the cost \(2\Delta_{1/10}\) alone exceeds \(2/5\),
whereas the inherited comparison requires \(C_1(\rho)<1/200\).
This is an obstruction to extending that particular collar
estimate, not a lower bound on the actual coherent pair.

Choosing \(L=\rho\) in (2) therefore leaves the horizontal interval
\((\rho,.1)\) at its new fixed height unpaid. Choosing \(L=.1\)
instead leaves both the real segment \((\rho,.1)\) and a genuinely
new vertical face unpaid. In particular, a bound for that face
alone would not complete the left path. One cannot remove either
piece by retaining only the already bounded horizontal segments.

## 3. The precise same-source face estimate

For a selected first type four, the natural left face is
\[
 a=\frac1{10}+ih,\quad 0\le h\le H,\qquad
 b=z+ic_2,\quad z\in P,\qquad \sigma=1,
\tag{7}
\]
with the prescribed selected height, for example \(H=7/625\)
for the completed selected first43 theorem. The exact next
sufficient finite-source inequality is a strict bound
\[
 |\mathcal P_{1\beta1,N}(w;a,b)|\,|d_N(Y)|^{\beta/2}
 \le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
  \exp N\{M_{12}+\Re p_2(z-a_2)-\delta+\tau\},
\tag{8}
\]
uniformly on (7), for some fixed \(\delta>0\), with both parities,
all original rows and forcing, and a fixed ordinary-color
neighborhood chosen before \(N\). The analogous unselected face
uses \(\sigma=0\) and \(h\) between \(0\) and \(c_1\).
The later complete plateau planes then cancel the marginal in
(8), retaining their usual halfweights. These are one-height
faces; no second-height transport estimate is needed. The real
segment \((\rho,.1)\) remains a separate source obligation even
after (8), as do any other unestimated pieces of the full cycle.

The completed selected first43 early theorem proves (8) only at
the endpoint \(h=H\), with its stated gap. The collar theorem
does not prove its other endpoint \(h=0\) at \(x_1=.1\).
An estimate for the entire finite modulus at the two endpoints
would still not imply (8) by pointwise height convexity:
the modulus of a Laurent polynomial need not have that property.

The existing convex-height atlas gives the valid alternative.
One must exhibit a *common complete finite-source envelope*
on the whole height segment, including every cap current,
ordinary real tail, join, alias and both Fourier upper branches.
If each of its calibrated profiles is convex in this height,
bounding those same profiles at \(0,H\) pays the face.
This is the already proved atlas criterion, not a new assertion
that its hypotheses or the endpoint signs hold here.
Horizontal concavity of the main critical phase alone does not
supply those hypotheses. Nor can the first cap alone be moved
up for free: the original first-cap height-tangent theorem,
Proposition 1, proves an actual local increase.

For comparison, the exact first34 and first343 patches remove
respectively one and two complete outer periods, with their
original factors \(N\) and \(N^2\). Their retained next coordinate
is restricted to a fixed local interval \(J_2\) or \(J_3\).
Their coefficient-preservation theorems do not replace (8) for
all \(z\in P\), and do not supply a general next-period contraction.

## 4. Different Fourier alternatives cannot be interpolated

The following elementary example concerns an inference, not the
actual knot source. Fix \(c<0<H\), a real ceiling \(M\), and
\[
 t=\frac{H}{H-c}\in(0,1),\qquad
 0<\delta<\frac{2\pi(-c)H}{H-c},\qquad
 k_N=\lfloor Nt\rfloor.
\]
Define the polynomial of degree less than \(N\)
\[
 F_N(X)=
 \exp\{N(M-\delta)-2\pi k_Nc\}\,X^{k_N}.
\tag{9}
\]
Uniformly on the entire circle at height \(c\),
\[
             |F_N(e^{-2\pi i(x+ic)})|=e^{N(M-\delta)}.
\tag{10}
\]
Uniformly on the entire circle at height \(H\), its selected
alternative satisfies
\[
 \frac1N\log|X^{-N}F_N(X)|
 =M-\delta+2\pi\frac{k_N}{N}(H-c)-2\pi H
 \le M-\delta.
\tag{11}
\]
At height zero, the selected and unselected moduli coincide,
but
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|F_N(e^{-2\pi ix})|
 =M-\delta+\frac{2\pi(-c)H}{H-c}>M.
\tag{12}
\]
This happens uniformly in \(x\). The example is already a
Laurent polynomial inside the allowed general degree bands.
It even satisfies full-circle endpoint bounds, so replacing
pointwise interpolation by three-circle log-convexity does not
repair the comparison of *different* Fourier alternatives.

Nothing in this example realizes the special factorization
\(U_\alpha C_\beta d_N^\alpha\), its forced recurrence or its
actual geometric point. It is not a counterexample to (8) or
to a knot theorem. It shows precisely why a paid unselected
source at \(c_1\) and a paid selected source at \(H\) cannot,
without an additional source estimate, pay their zero-height
face. The complete first-coordinate Fourier object and its
chosen monomial must remain the same throughout any proposed
height interpolation.
