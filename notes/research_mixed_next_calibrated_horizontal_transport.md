# Calibrated horizontal transport at the actual incoming height

Research deduction, 8 November 2026. The complete selected first
cap--conditional product retains its original following affine gap
on the horizontal segment from \(1/2+ih_a\) to \(13/20+ih_a\).
The incoming height is the actual canonical reference height.
The proof transports every fixed cap current through its possible
numerator wall using the original zero-safe factorial envelope.
It handles the omitted physical rows separately, and uses the
already complete conditional source on the displaced rectangle.

No numerical evaluation or new reference family is used. This pays
the horizontal segment; the vertical segment at its new right end
is a separate contour component.

## 2. The fixed cap currents through their numerator walls

Use the literal finite cap and the same retained interpolation:
\[

 \end{split}
 \tag{7}
\]
At \(e=j/N\), the second line is exactly the corresponding
summand in the first, by
[the original outer-cap identity](research_mixed_cap_outer_contour_reduction.md),
Section 1. The complete endpoint coefficient \(C_N\) is never

 \tag{8}

For clarity, use the exact continuous factorial upper envelope

[the displaced conditional source theorem](research_mixed_conditional_nonnegative_height_complete_source.md):
\[
 \mathfrak p(q)=
 \begin{cases}

   \Re g(q-1)+2\pi(\Im q)_+,&1\le\Re q\le6/5.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{10}
\]
The two branches agree continuously on their common boundary.
They retain both terms of the exact identity
\[
             \Pi_N(q+1)=(1-e^{-2\pi iNq})\Pi_N(q).
 \tag{11}
\]
The midpoint and reciprocal-midpoint estimates in that source

Since \(g(e)=J(e)+i\pi(e-e^2)/2\), the resulting upper rate of

 \tag{13}
\]
Thus (12) preserves the original cap carrier and additive constant.
The all-real endpoint bound has this same complete-color upper

**Lemma 1.** At fixed \(e\in\mathcal R\) and fixed incoming height,
the continuous rate (12) decreases strictly as \(x\) moves from
\(1/2\) to \(13/20\). More precisely, with
\(\chi=\tfrac12\log(144/139)>0\),
\[
 \widehat\Psi(e,x+ih_a)
 \le \widehat\Psi(e,1/2+ih_a)-\chi(x-1/2).
 \tag{14}
\]

*Proof.* Put \(p=e^{2\pi ie}\) and \(R=|X|\). The actual depth gives
\[
                5/8<R\le1,\qquad |p|^{-1}<4/3.
 \tag{15}
\]
The first inequality follows from
\(\log(8/5)>6/13>18\pi/125\); the second from
\(2\pi/25<\log(4/3)\).
The arguments satisfy
\[
 \Arg X\in[7\pi/10,\pi],\qquad
 \Arg p\in[27\pi/50,49\pi/50].
 \tag{16}
\]
Their difference lies in \([-7\pi/25,23\pi/50]\), strictly
inside \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\). Consequently
\[
 \begin{split}
 |1-X/p|^2&<1+\frac{16}{9}R^2,\\
 |1-X|^2&>1+R^2+R.
 \end{split}
 \tag{17}
\]
The second inequality uses \(\cos\Arg X<-1/2\).
The difference between the actual denominator and numerator
squares is therefore greater than
\[
                 R-\frac79R^2\ge\frac29R>\frac5{36}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The denominator square is at most four. Hence
\[
             \left|\frac{1-X/p}{1-X}\right|^2<\frac{139}{144}.
 \tag{19}
\]

On either branch of (10), differentiation with respect to the
incoming real coordinate gives
\[
 \partial_x\widehat\Psi(e,x+ih_a)
      =\log\left|\frac{1-X/p}{1-X}\right|<-\chi.
 \tag{20}
\]
The additional term in the second branch is independent of \(x\).
At a real numerator zero, the derivative has its integrable
logarithmic singularity. Integrating on both sides and using
continuity of (12) gives the same inequality across that point.
This proves (14). \(\square\)

All fixed cotangent-kernel penalties depend on \(e,N\), not on
the incoming coordinate. Thus (14) transports the rate of every
point on every retained current from its calibrated initial value.
There is no need to assert vertical convexity after a numerator

To retain the exact inventory, choose

 \tag{21}

Half-grid shifts in (21) lie in a fixed neighborhood of (8);

## 3. Every omitted physical private row

For a compact physical private index \(e=j/N\), the complete
coefficient and uniform product bounds give the all-real-color rate
\[
 \lambda(e)+I_{x,R}(e),\qquad
 I_{x,R}(e)=\int_0^e\log|1-Re^{-2\pi i(x+s)}|\,ds,
 \quad \lambda(e)=\Re S(e).
 \tag{22}
\]
This is the bound from
[all-real complementary completion, Section 2](research_mixed_three_four_complementary_real_colors.md).
It bounds the whole coefficient \(H_N\), including its original
ordinary normal.

For \(0\le e\le7/10\), \(1/2\le x\le13/20\), and \(R\le1\),
\[
 \partial_x I_{x,R}(e)
   =\log|1-Re^{-2\pi i(x+e)}|
                      -\log|1-Re^{-2\pi ix}|\le0.
 \tag{23}
\]
Indeed \(x\le x+e\le2-x\), since \(2x+e\le2\). Throughout
this interval
\(\cos(2\pi(x+e))\ge\cos(2\pi x)\). This gives (23)
directly from the squared moduli. As before, integration and
continuity include \(R=1\) and all numerator zeros.

Every omitted compact physical row with \(e\le7/10\) therefore
has no larger rate than the same row at \(x=1/2\). The initial
cap source theorem bounds each of those omitted-row rates by
\(B_*\), using its original real-tail reduction. This step is
applied only to physical rows outside (21); no signed retained
sum is used as a row bound.

It remains to bound \(7/10\le e<1\). The radial comparison in
[the far-half cap extension](research_mixed_three_four_far_half_cap_radial_extension.md),
equation (9), gives \(I_{x,R}(e)\le J(e)\) for \(e\ge1/2\).
Put
\[
                         h(e)=\lambda(e)+J(e).
 \tag{24}
\]
The exact original identity in
[the cap seam source bounds, Section 2](research_mixed_cap_seam_source_bounds.md)
gives, for \(e=1/2+s\), \(0\le s<1/2\),
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 h'(e)=-\operatorname{arctanh} b,\qquad
 q=\sqrt{1-b^2},\quad b\ge0,\\
 q(1+q)=2\cos^2\pi s,\qquad h(1/2)<101/125.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{25}
\]
In particular \(h\) decreases above one half.
For \(0\le s\le1/5\), the pentagon identity gives
\(\cos\pi s\ge\cos(\pi/5)>4/5\). Hence \(q>2/3\), because
\(2(4/5)^2> (2/3)(1+2/3)\). The relation in (25) also gives
\[
 b^2
 =2\sin^2\pi s\,\frac{1+q}{2+q}
 >\frac54\sin^2\pi s,
 \qquad
 b>\frac{11}{10}\sin\pi s
 \quad(0<s\le1/5).
 \tag{26}
\]
Since \(\operatorname{arctanh}b\ge b\), integration yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 h(7/10)
 &<\frac{101}{125}
       -\frac{11}{10\pi}\bigl(1-\cos(\pi/5)\bigr)\\
 &<\frac{101}{125}-\frac{21}{320}
   =\frac{5939}{8000}<\frac{149}{200}<B_*.
 \end{split}
 \tag{27}
\]
Here \(\cos(\pi/5)=(1+\sqrt5)/4<13/16\), using
\(\sqrt5<9/4\), and \(\pi<22/7\). The strict difference between
the last two rational ceilings is \(21/8000\).
Thus all compact upper physical rows are paid, with their entire
coherent coefficients.

## 4. The complete selected product keeps its affine gap

Let \(\Theta(a,y)\) be the actual conditional critical action.
[Horizontal contraction of the complete conditional source](research_mixed_conditional_horizontal_contraction.md)
proves
\[
 \Re\Theta(x+ih_a,y)\le\Re\Theta(1/2+ih_a,y)
                         \qquad(1/2\le x\le13/20).
 \tag{29}
\]
Its source consequence is the complete original asymptotic
\[
 C_{1,N}(X,Y)
    =N^{-1}\mathcal B(a,y)e^{N\Theta(a,y)}
                  (1+O_{\rm hol}(N^{-1})),\qquad\mathcal B\ne0,
 \tag{30}
\]
uniformly on the full displaced rectangle. Thus (29) is an
upper bound for the complete conditional polynomial, including
all its physical low, wall and upper rows, both parities, all
weighted currents, and every unweighted connector. None must
be inferred from the sign of a formal critical derivative.

For completeness, its use with (6) is sourcewise. Let \(R_2\)
be the maximum of the original first-conditional main and real
cut profiles at \(a=1/2+ih_a\), also including the half and last
profiles as in the right-segment proof. That proof bounds the
whole \(C_1\) by rate \(R_2\). The nonzero expansion (30) then
implies
\[
                    \Re\Theta(1/2+ih_a,y)\le R_2.
 \tag{31}
\]
If (31) failed at a parameter, the nonzero leading coefficient
in (30) would contradict that complete upper bound for large
\(N\). No bound on a constituent is inferred from a signed sum.
The original calibration (6) holds for each first profile and
each third-factor profile, with both selected-weight alternatives;
it therefore also holds with their first maximum \(R_2\).
Replacing \(R_2\) by (31), and then using (28)--(29), preserves it.

The original third-factor source, its outgoing marginal and
halfweight, and both full selected-weight alternatives are
independent of \(x\). Consequently the following statement
retains the actual correlation of the reference coordinates.

**Theorem 3.** For every \(\tau>0\), the full original density (3)

\[

uniformly on (1)--(2), at both \(\beta=0,1\), both parities,

The cap proof uses the complete all-real endpoint bounds on the

At each reference base point keep all cuts and paths fixed.
