# Hyperbolic volume of the whole cylindrical positive Conway source

29 October 2026. The positive source's volume problem has an actual
geometric realization. Every retained CYLINDRICAL factor in the robust
Conway class is the complement of its literal compiled augmented link,
with a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric, and its WHOLE original
quantum limit equals that metric's volume.

The distinction between cylindrical factors and cap products matters.
The [cap-sum reduction](actual_conway_cap_sum_reduction.md) gives exact
componentwise connected sums, which generally are not hyperbolic. First
apply that boundary reduction. The theorem below applies to every
remaining factor whose closure flag is C. It does not assert that an
arbitrary unreduced Conway expression is hyperbolic.

The construction is elementary and source-specific: leaf pyramids,
multiplication prisms, actual ordered quadrilateral ports, the original
crossing-disk cut, and canonical cusp horospheres. No polyhedron
existence theorem or identification inferred from a numerical volume
is used.

## 1. The exact class and result

Use the [literal physical compiler](actual_positive_complete_circle_conway_trees.md):
h is the genuine quarter-turn of the complete crossing-circle box,
A=h^3, and robust expressions are generated by

    E ::= A | R E | E E.

R is the actual quarter-turn with its original cups and caps; product
is the actual identity-wire stack. Write C(E) for the original marked
cylindrical closure phi(E), and D(E)=C(RE) for the cap closure.
Every auxiliary circle remains a complete original-color component.

A retained cylindrical factor means C(A), or C(FG) with F,G robust
expressions, with their entire internal turns and products retained.
Initial turns at the closure can be removed by the exact C/D boundary
rules; a D product splits by the cap-sum theorem. This produces the
stated factors without pretending that they exhaust arbitrary knots.

Let L_E be the ACTUAL unframed link of such a cylindrical factor,
and Z_(E,N)>0 its original raw one-cut source for N>=3. Then

    lim_(N -> infinity) (2pi/N)log Z_(E,N)
         =2 max_(P_E) V_E
         =Vol(S^3 minus L_E).                       (1)

The first equality is the proved whole-source variational theorem,
with all colors, all repeated-root normals, both parities and all
composite levels. This note proves the second equality and supplies
the actual complete metric. The recorded conversion from raw source
to the original enhanced reduced invariant is a unit framing phase;
the one-cut modified normalization has its recorded additional 1/N.
Neither changes (1). No framing or peripheral character is suppressed
inside the finite-level source identity.

There is also an open version. For every robust E and every b in

    v_E(b)=max_(P_E(b)) V_E

is the volume of a convex ideal polyhedron P_E(b) with ONE ordered

The original coefficient asymptotic and its algebraic boundary branch

The port is a geometric ideal quadrilateral, not a meridian eigenvalue
or an automatically totally geodesic four-punctured boundary sphere.

## 2. The source maximum is strict in every unforced cell

Expand every A into three h leaves and two products. A leaf output
x has 0<=x<=1/2. A product (x,y)->z has

    p=z-x>=0, q=z-y>=0, r=x+y-z>=0, w=1-x-y>=0.

The source atoms are the two leaf tetrahedra with angles
(x,1/2-x,1/2), and the three product tetrahedra

    (y,x,w), (p,q,2r+w), (p+q,r,r+w).              (2)

Turns impose output 1-input and add no atom.

First there is a strict feasible assignment for EVERY proper robust
output b in (0,1). At a product choose both child outputs in
(b/2,min(b,1/2)); then p,q,r,w are all positive. At a turn use 1-b
for the child. For the seed h^3, choose its last leaf c with

    0<c<min(2b/3,1-b),   t=b-c/2,

and choose both first leaves a in (t/2,min(t,1/2)). Their first
product has output t and the second has output b. All leaf and
product inequalities are strict. This proves feasibility by induction.

For a closed C(FG), the last product is forced to output 1, so its
inputs obey x+y=1 and its three atoms are identically flat. DELETE
ONLY this forced final gate from the volume list and eliminate its
forced wires. Choosing x=y=1/2 and applying the preceding construction
to F,G gives a strict feasible point of the reduced problem. For
C(A)=C((hh)h), choose last leaf c in (0,1/2), first-product output
1-c, and its two leaves in ((1-c)/2,1/2). Again every retained cell
is strict. The finite source has not been changed: the final exact
multiplication coefficient is one on its forced support.

Here is the barrier which rules out any other boundary maximum.
Along a segment from a boundary point toward the constructed strict
point, an ideal tetrahedron with angles (0,a,1-a), 0<a<1, has volume
directional derivative +infinity. This follows from
Lambda(t)=-t log t+O(t) near zero, or directly from its log-sine
derivative. A vertex-flat tetrahedron (0,0,1) has only a finite
one-sided derivative: the t log t terms cancel, leaving the finite
entropy expression in the two positive angle speeds. A positive
tetrahedron has finite derivative. Thus no other atom can cancel
a +infinity derivative by contributing -infinity.

Any proper wire equal to 0 or 1 forces such a nonvertex-flat atom
somewhere below it. A turn exchanges 0 and 1. A product with output
0 has both inputs 0. A product with output 1 has inputs summing to
1: either both are strictly between 0 and 1, giving the atom
(y,x,0), or one child is an endpoint and the argument continues
downward. A finite descent ends at a leaf, whose only possible
endpoint in {0,1} is 0; its leaf atom is (0,1/2,1/2). The sole
excluded root gate is never used in this descent. Therefore no
maximum has a proper endpoint wire. A leaf at 1/2 likewise has
the nonvertex-flat atom (1/2,0,1/2).

Now all proper wires lie in (0,1). If w=0 at a retained product,
its first atom is (y,x,0). If r=0 its third atom is (z,0,1-z).
If p=0 and q>0 its second atom is (0,q,1-q), with 0<q<1; the
q=0 case is symmetric. If p=q=0, the third atom is (0,x,1-x).
Each is a nonvertex-flat atom. All boundary cases are therefore
excluded by the same strict feasible segment. Every maximizing
leaf and retained prism is STRICT.

The maximum is also unique. Each positive tetrahedron has negative
definite angle Hessian, and the product's three angle maps recover
x,y,z. The leaf angle recovers its wire as well. Thus a tangent
annihilating every retained angle map annihilates every leaf and
product wire; turns then annihilate their wires too. The only deleted
gate had its output fixed and its inputs already belong to the two
children. The negative Hessian on the reduced tangent space is
positive definite. Strict concavity gives uniqueness.

For fixed proper output b this argument applies on every slice.
The implicit-function theorem consequently gives one real analytic
interior maximizing branch throughout b in (0,1). No hidden change
of maximizing face is needed for this robust class.

## 3. Literal local cells and their ordered port maps

The [local ideal-cell theorem](actual_newton_ideal_prism_and_associativity.md)
constructs the following actual convex cells and proves their volumes
and entire ordered face metrics.

A leaf is an ideal quadrangular pyramid. Its apex O is infinity;
its base corners C0,C1,C2,C3 lie successively at

    1, exp(2pi i x), -1, -exp(2pi i x).

Its base-edge angles on C0C1,C2C3 are pi x; on C1C2,C3C0 they
are pi(1/2-x). Its four apex edges are right angled. Its volume
is pi g(x), exactly the two leaf atoms. Color the two triangles
(O,C1,C2) and (O,C3,C0) SHADED; color the other two side triangles
WHITE. The base is an uncolored output PORT.

Call the output edges 01,23 FREE and the other pair CAP. A completed
product has FREE angles pi z and CAP angles pi(1-z). A genuine
turn rotates its port once, exchanges these pairs, and replaces its
label z by 1-z. The white and shaded faces elsewhere are carried
with the actual box.

A product is the explicit six-vertex ideal prism of the local theorem,
with vertices infinity,P,v0,v1,v2,v3. Its two triangular faces are
WHITE. Its input quadrilaterals and output are

    Fx=(infinity,v0,v1,P), Fy=(infinity,v3,v2,P),
    Fz=(v0,v1,v2,v3).

The actual stack maps are

    lower F: (C0,C1,C2,C3) -> (v0,v1,P,infinity),
    upper G: (C0,C1,C2,C3) -> (infinity,P,v2,v3).   (3)

Label C0 bottom-left, C1 bottom-right, C2 top-right, C3 top-left.
Then the lower top-left is infinity, the same as the upper
bottom-left; both right interface corners are P. The four remaining
outer corners are v0,v1,v2,v3 in exactly their original order.
Thus (3) glues the actual identity stack, with no Conway mutation.

For an ordered ideal quadrilateral define

    kappa(C0,C1,C2,C3)
      =(C0-C1)(C2-C3)/((C0-C3)(C1-C2))>0.

The local theorem proves kappa_leaf=exp(-g'), and
kappa(Fx)=exp(-m_x), kappa(Fy)=exp(-m_y), kappa(Fz)=exp(-m_z).
Both input maps (3) invert kappa. Stationarity at each actual
internal wire says that its child and parent action derivatives
sum to zero; hence kappa_child kappa_parent=1. These are precisely
the ordered ideal-quadrilateral isometry conditions. A turn inverts
kappa and changes the derivative sign, so the same conclusion holds
through any number of actual turns.

## 4. Convexity and the colored boundary survive every product

Attach the two child cells to their prism by the isometries (3), with
interiors on opposite sides of each attaching face. A child CAP edge
meets a prism edge with angle pi x or pi y. For a leaf child the sum
is pi/2, separating its shaded triangle from a prism white triangle.
For a completed child it is pi, so two WHITE faces merge flatly.

The edge shared by both inputs has total angle

    pi(w+x+y)=pi.

Its incident faces are white and merge. The two other longitudinal
edges on the output acquire angles pi(p+x)=pi z and pi(q+y)=pi z.
The other output pair retains pi(1-z). This proves the claimed
FREE/CAP output pattern. All nonport edges that remain are right
angled and separate one white and one shaded face.

Convexity can be checked without a global polyhedron theorem.
When two convex cells are put on opposite sides of their common
supported face, their intersection is exactly that face. Their union
is locally convex at its finite seam points whenever the incident
boundary angles add to at most pi. For the first child attachment
the sums are pi or pi/2 on CAP edges, pi z on one FREE edge and
pi(1-y) on the other; the second attachment makes the common edge
flat and gives the stated output angles. All are at most pi.

Here local convexity implies convexity directly. The union is closed
in hyperbolic space and connected by finite-length paths. A minimizing
sequence of paths between two finite points, with lengths bounded by
one competitor, stays in a compact hyperbolic ball. Constant-speed
parametrization and compactness give a shortest path. Local convexity
forces it to be an ambient geodesic locally, including at finite
seam edges. It is therefore the unique hyperbolic geodesic segment.
The entire segment lies in the union. Ideal vertices are outside
hyperbolic space and cause no extra finite seam exception.

Thus the inductive union is an actual convex ideal polyhedron.
Its output quadrilateral remains a genuine supported face because
its two edge angles lie strictly between zero and pi. Distinct
vertices not in the attaching face cannot accidentally be identified:
they lie on opposite supported sides. The cell adjacency graph is a
tree, and all volume contributions add with degree one.

The colored vertex pattern is equally explicit. A leaf apex is
four-valent with alternating shaded and white faces. A leaf port
corner is three-valent. After a product, each output corner has the
cyclic pattern PORT,WHITE,SHADED,WHITE, with its two port angles z
and 1-z and its other two angles right. The input-interface vertices
become four-valent, with alternating shaded/white faces, after the
just-listed flat white seams are merged. The same pattern is preserved
under a turn. In particular every leaf retains its TWO shaded ideal
triangles, meeting only at its original apex O.

The resulting open cell P_E(b) has volume exactly v_E(b). Its ordered
port metric is

    kappa_E(b)=exp(-v'_E(b)/pi),                    (4)

by the envelope identity and the local derivative formulas. Equation
(4) assigns a literal geometric quadrilateral modulus to the source
boundary conjugate variable. It does not rename that modulus as a
knot longitude or meridian.

## 6. The actual cylindrical closure makes a right-angled polyhedron

For C(FG), delete the final forced-flat product and take the two
already constructed child cells at outputs x and y=1-x. Their
ordered port metrics agree: stationarity in x gives
v'_F(x)=v'_G(1-x), hence kappa_F=kappa_G.

The actual closed port map is

    F:C_j -> G:C_(3-j),   j modulo 4.              (5)

It sends the lower top-left to the upper bottom-left and the lower
top-right to the upper bottom-right, giving the stack interface. It
sends the remaining lower bottom-left/right to the upper top-left/
right, giving the two original outside cylindrical closing wires.
It preserves the FREE and CAP edge pairs and preserves kappa.
Thus (5) is the required orientation-reversing ideal-face isometry.

Glue the child polyhedra on opposite sides of this face. FREE angles
sum to pi. If both children are completed cells, CAP angles also
sum to pi. If one child is a leaf, as in the last product of A=h^3,
the CAP sum is pi/2 instead. Two leaf children cannot satisfy the
closed constraint strictly. In every case the previous convexity
argument applies, flat white seams merge, and the result is a convex
ideal RIGHT-ANGLED polyhedron P_E with all boundary edges separating
white from shaded. Every ideal vertex is four-valent with alternating
white/shaded faces. Its shaded faces are the two triangles of each
original retained crossing circle, with their actual disk labels.

## 7. Restore the original link and verify complete cusps

Take two oppositely oriented copies P_E^0,P_E^1. Glue each white
face across the copies by its original projection label. For each
leaf glue its two shaded triangles WITHIN each copy, fixing its
apex and matching the two pieces of each original main arc. In
the leaf labels this matches C1 to C0 and C2 to C3. These are
exactly the two sides of the crossing disk cut in section 5.
The port maps and turns have carried these labels unchanged.
Reversing all cuts therefore gives S^3 minus the ACTUAL compiled
link L_E, with no component deletion and no change of ribbon source.

The shaded faces are ideal triangles, so each prescribed vertex
bijection is an isometry. Every finite edge cycle alternates its
white gluing with its shaded gluing and has FOUR right angles.
The two shaded faces of one disk meet only at their apex, so no
premature edge identification shortens this four-step cycle.
The cycle returns pointwise on the edge: the white maps identify
identical copies and the two shaded maps are inverse vertex-labeled
triangle isometries. There is consequently no residual translation
along the edge in addition to the angle check.
All finite edge neighborhoods therefore have angle 2pi, not a
multiple-winding or cone singularity.

Completeness follows from a simultaneous explicit decoration. At
every ideal vertex of P_E the horospherical link is a Euclidean
rectangle. Its two opposite shaded sides have equal lengths. Scale
that vertex's horosphere so that this common length is one. Every
shaded triangle now has corner horocycle segments of length one at
all three vertices, its canonical mutually tangent decoration.
Thus EVERY shaded gluing preserves these horospheres. White gluing
joins identical labeled copies and preserves them as well.

Uniformly shrink the horospheres to make them disjoint. Their links
glue without dilation to compact nonsingular Euclidean surfaces;
the reversible link cut identifies those surfaces as the actual
link's cusp tori. The complement of the cusp neighborhoods is
compact. These are complete finite-volume hyperbolic ends, and
the entire assembled metric is complete. Its volume is

    2 Vol(P_E)=2 max V_E.

Together with the original full-source limit this proves (1).

## 8. Algebraic classes, generality and the remaining knot problem

The selected stationary shapes are algebraic by the existing
[algebraic maximum theorem](positive_source_algebraic_volume_period.md).
The local cell theorem identifies the conjugate-antisymmetric class
of the three source product atoms with that of an actual prism
triangulation, by an exact five-term calculation. The leaf atoms
are already the pyramid triangulation. This gives an equality of
RATIONAL GEOMETRIC BLOCH CLASSES, not only equality of regulators.
Here B_E is the source-selected sum
sum_j([z_j]-[bar z_j]) over all its nonflat source atoms, as defined
in the [source Bloch theorem](actual_positive_source_bloch_regulator.md).

Here is its precise field and orientation convention. Choose a number
field K stable under complex conjugation and containing all source
phases and shapes, all local AND glued ideal vertex coordinates, and
all shapes of a geometric ideal triangulation of P_E. In particular
every auxiliary five-term/coning cross ratio lies in K. Such a finite
extension exists:
the local coordinates are algebraic expressions in the source phases;
an ordered ideal-face isometry is a Mobius or anti-Mobius map fixed
by three algebraic vertices. Each finite successive gluing therefore
introduces only algebraic coordinates. No identification of K with
the source shape field or a manifold trace field is asserted.

Write A(z)=[z]-[bar z]. The local five-term identities and ideal-chain
triangulation independence give

    image_K(B_E)=sum_(T in a geometric triangulation of P_E) A(z_T)
                    in P(K)_Q.                     (6)

For clarity, this comparison does not presume that arbitrary choices
of cell-face diagonals already match. A mismatch on a planar ideal
face is filled by a coplanar tetrahedral chain, whose shapes are real
and whose A-symbols vanish. After correcting those face chains, the
difference between the cell sum and a triangulation of P_E is a
3-cycle in the full abstract simplex on their finite ideal vertex
set. Coning that cycle gives a 4-chain; every 4-simplex boundary is
a five-term relation. This is the exact triangulation argument in
the local cell theorem, applied to the actual degree-one polyhedron.

One geometric ideal triangulation of P_E is obtained by coning its
nonincident faces to a chosen ideal vertex, triangulating those
faces by fans. The incident faces use the induced fan at that vertex.
Use IDENTICAL face triangulations on the oppositely oriented second
copy. White faces then match triangle by triangle under their labeled
copy maps; shaded faces were already triangles. Thus the two oriented
tetrahedral chains form a degree-one ideal fundamental chain for the
ACTUAL link complement from sections 5--7.

Normalize the first copy so its positively oriented tetrahedron has
shape z_T. Choose mirrored coordinates for the second copy by complex
conjugation and give it the opposite abstract orientation, as required
by the white-face gluing. The inherited vertex ordering of its mirrored
tetrahedron has shape bar z_T and coefficient -1 in this fundamental chain.
Its contribution is therefore -[bar z_T], not +[bar z_T]. In
particular its regulator contribution is +D(z_T), consistent with
the positive metric volume of the second copy. Consequently the
actual oriented geometric fundamental chain has symbol sum

    beta_geom(L_E;K)=sum_T([z_T]-[bar z_T]).         (7)

Equations (6),(7) prove

    image_K(B_E)=beta_geom(L_E;K) in B(K)_Q,
    R_K(beta_geom)=Vol(S^3 minus L_E).              (8)

The first equality is already an equality in the rational pre-Bloch
group; the source theorem proves that its boundary is zero. The
geometric construction supplies the actual degree-one fundamental
class which the earlier abstract source-regulator theorem did not
identify. We compare the images in this COMMON number field; no
injectivity of Bloch groups under field extension is assumed.
Integral flattenings, integral torsion, preferred logarithmic
peripheral lifts and a generic-meridian knot A-operator remain outside
this statement.

The class allows arbitrary finite branching and turn depth in the

For an arbitrary robust closed expression, first use the exact
