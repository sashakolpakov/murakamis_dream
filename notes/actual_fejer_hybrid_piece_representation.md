## 2. Each piece has one actual signed circle development

Apply the [cooriented developing-disk argument, §2](actual_fejer_marked_signed_development.md)
to each piece, with the following explicit multigraph and boundary adaptations.
Its new protected edge need not be MAIN. If it is MAIN, the two adjacent

edge was a MATCHING cut, its two incident triangles are the retained
CROSS halves and are again positive. Their new protected outer loads
are zero, so each is the fixed two-line triangle (0,0,pi). Thus in both
cases the two marked triangles supply the genuine common strip.

At a matching cut, formally pair the two boundary CROSS half-triangles
along the new matching edge. This retains the cubic perfect-matching

matching edge between the new protected vertices would have both outer
loads zero, hence be a pure-middle port already removed. Every other
parallel marked edge gives an allowed MAIN/MAIN or CROSS MATCHING/MATCHING
cut. Exhaustive splitting therefore makes the remaining marked
line/line edge unique.

Remove one positive marked triangle. The remaining abstract complex is
a triangulated disk. A finite/finite edge fixes an orientation-preserving
Euclidean gluing by its two distinct ordered centers; a line/finite edge
fixes it by its COORIENTED line and center. The unique line/line edge is
on the boundary and is never used for an underdetermined gluing. Around
each interior finite vertex the holonomy fixes its center and rotates
by its signed star angle, either zero or 2pi, so it is the identity.
These vertex loops generate the dual disk's cycles. Boundary fans
propagate the SAME two cooriented lines. The final finite circle touches
both inward sides, forcing its radius to be half their shared distance
and closing the omitted triangle. This argument uses disk topology and
nondegenerate edge data, not simplicity of the remaining finite-edge
graph. Double-triangle pieces have zero entropy and zero volume and are
represented by two lines and one tangent circle.

Every piece therefore gives its ONE family of oriented white planes,
