# The next-four last row paired with the complete first lower half

Private research proof, 8 November 2026. On the entire lower-left
shared interval, the next own-four last-row alternative is paid
after multiplication by the complete first conditional lower half.
For deeper shared heights the joint profiles transport to the old
face. For shallower heights the next last row is below its original
real low cut. No numerical value is assigned to the old last-row
certificate margin.

Together with the complete current and upper-row theorems, this
gives a finite bound with the **whole next type-four kernel** and
the first kernel restricted to its literal rows below one half.
The first upper physical block, next own type three and noncentral
first-incoming pieces remain separate.

## 1. Original source and the last-row allocation

Use the actual ordinary-next references and notation of
[the complete lower-left current theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_complete_currents.md).
Here the next own flag is \(\beta=1\), so
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=x+ih_a,\quad x\in P=[.22,.45],\qquad
y=q+ih_y,\quad 0\le q\le.22,\qquad z=u+ih_z,\quad u\in P,\\
-.072<h_a<0,\qquad -.102<h_s,h_y<-.001,
\qquad -.108\le h_z,h_t<0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
All coordinates retain their actual reference correlations. Set
\[
R=e^{2\pi h_y},\qquad \delta_y=-\log R=-2\pi h_y,
\qquad Q(s;a,y)=g(a+s)+g(y+s)-3g(s)-g(a)-g(y)+2\pi ias.
\tag{2}
\]
The next literal last-row rate is \(\delta_y\), since both
of its outside heights are negative. After the two shared
halfweights and the outgoing halfweight are attached, a first
index profile has total rate
\[
\mathcal L(s;x,q,u)=B_U(x)+
 \Re\{Q(s;a,y)-g(y)-\tfrac12g(z)\}+\delta_y.
\tag{3}
\]
This keeps the full shared allocation \(-g(y)\). The complete
cap plane \(B_U\) and the actual target \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\)
are exactly those of
[the joint profile theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_joint_main_profiles.md).

At \(q_0=11/50\), the original first main, real low and real
half profiles have their ordinary supporting planes. The original
next last-row theorem gives a strict inequality below its own
ordinary plane. It asserts no numerical value for that strict
last-row margin; see
[typed last-row completion, Theorem (4)](research_mixed_typed_last_row_support_completion.md).
Adding the complete cap and canceling the original marginals
still retains the two quadratic losses in the shared coordinate:
\[
\mathcal L(s;x,q_0,u)
<\Lambda_{123}(z)-2\kappa(\alpha-q_0)^2
<\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-10},
\qquad \kappa=1/2500000,\quad \alpha>.24.
\tag{4}
\]
For a real cut the already proved cut deficit only improves this
bound. Equation (4) uses the old last-row theorem only for its
sign. Indeed \(2\kappa(.02)^2=1/3125000000>10^{-10}\).

## 2. Joint transport when \(R\le7/10\)

For a first main profile, the existing lower and upper clippings
increase its value until its real index is in
\([1/6,5/12]\), without changing its original height \(h_s\).
They are Sections 3 and 6.1 of the joint profile theorem and
hold on the full interval in (1). The next last-row carrier
and the weights are independent of this first index, so the
same clippings apply to (3).

Put \(r_s=e^{2\pi h_s}\in[1/2,1]\). At a fixed clipped
index the exact derivative, including the original shared
weight, is
\[
\partial_q\mathcal L
=\log|1-Rr_se^{-2\pi i(q+s_R)}|
             -2\log|1-Re^{-2\pi iq}|.
\tag{5}
\]
The cap, the next carrier and the outgoing allocation have
zero \(q\) derivative. We prove that (5) is strictly positive
when \(1/2\le R\le7/10\).

Let \(D=|1-Re^{-2\pi iq}|^2\), and let \(U\) denote the
square of the numerator modulus in (5).
If \(0\le q\le1/10\), then
\[
D\le1+R^2-\tfrac85R\le\tfrac9{20},\qquad U\ge\tfrac34>D^2.
\tag{6}
\]
For the first bound use \(\cos(\pi/5)>4/5\).
For the second, the shifted real part is in
\([1/6,31/60]\); if its cosine is positive, minimize the
modulus over its radius and use sine at least \(\sqrt3/2\).
Otherwise the modulus is at least one.

For the two remaining ranges set
\[
D_a=1+R^2-aR.
\]
Sufficient bounds are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
q\text{ range}&D\le&U\ge\\\hline
[1/10,1/5]&D_{3/5}&1+R^2/4+R/10\\
[1/5,11/50]&D_{9/25}&1+R^2/4+13R/20.
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]
The own cosines satisfy \(\cos(2\pi q)>3/10\) and
\(\cos(2\pi q)>9/50\), respectively. In the first range,
\(q+s_R\in[4/15,37/60]\), so its cosine is less than
\(-1/10\). For example
\(\sin(\pi/30)>\sin(5/48)>5/48-(5/48)^3/6>1/10\).
In the second range its cosine is less than \(-13/20\),
by the written witness in the joint profile theorem, Section 6.2.
The resulting squared moduli increase with their radius, so
their minimum uses \(r_s=1/2\), giving (7).

Each difference between the last column of (7) and the square
of its middle column is strictly concave in \(R\in[1/2,7/10]\).
Indeed
\[
(D_a^2)''=12R^2-12aR+4+2a^2>4
\quad(a=3/5,9/25),
\tag{8}
\]
whereas the two numerator second derivatives are \(1/2\).
Their endpoint differences, at \(R=1/2,7/10\), are
\[
(21/100,119/2500),\qquad
(1213/5000,5607/125000),
\tag{9}
\]
respectively. Thus \(U>D^2\), proving the positive derivative.

For the first real low cut \(s=1/8\), the same derivative
(5) has \(r_s=1\). On \(q\le1/10\), its numerator square
is at least \(1/2>D^2\). On \([1/10,1/5]\) use
\[
U\ge1+R^2-\tfrac25R,
\tag{10}
\]
since \(\cos(2\pi(q+1/8))<1/5\). The difference of (10)
and \(D_{3/5}^2\) is strictly concave by (8); its endpoint
values are \(59/400,651/10000\). On \([1/5,11/50]\),
the sharper cosine bound \(-9/20\) from the joint profile
theorem gives \(U\ge1+R^2+9R/10\), which is larger than
the second numerator in (7). Thus the low real profile
also transports strictly upward to \(q_0\).

The first real half profile is bounded by its main-height
half profile at the same \(q\). This is Section 6.4 of the
joint profile theorem: along its original negative vertical
join, \(\Im Q_s\ge\pi(x-q)\ge0\). It may then be clipped
as above. Therefore every controlling first main or real-cut
profile in (3) is bounded by an eligible old profile at
\(q=q_0\). Equation (4) proves
\[
\mathcal L<\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-10}
                      \qquad(1/2\le R\le7/10).
\tag{11}
\]

## 3. Domination by the next low cut when \(R\ge7/10\)

Now use the original next real cut \(\ell=1/8\). Write
\[
I_b(\ell)=\int_0^\ell\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(b+v)}|\,dv.
\]
Because \(q+\ell<1/2\), even-arc monotonicity and the
radial primitive bound give \(I_y(\ell)\ge J(\ell)\).
Consequently its exact own-four row rate satisfies
\[
E_1(\ell;y,z)-\delta_y
\ge -2J(1/8)+I_z(1/8)-\tfrac78\delta_y.
\tag{12}
\]
This includes the folded incoming carrier.

All actual next-four output radii obey
\(e^{2\pi h_z}>1/2\), since \(.216\pi<\log2\).
For \(u\in[.22,.45]\), the outgoing factor has the following
lower bounds along this cut:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
v\text{ range}&\cos(2\pi(u+v))\text{ upper bound}
 &\log|1-e^{2\pi h_z-2\pi i(u+v)}|\text{ lower bound}\\\hline
[0,3/100]&1/5&1/41\\
[3/100,2/25]&0&1/9\\
[2/25,1/8]&-3/10&11/51.
\end{array}
\tag{13}
\]
For each row, the squared modulus increases with its radius
above \(1/2\). At radius \(1/2\) its lower bounds are
\(21/20,5/4,31/20\), respectively. The inequality
\(\log t>2(t-1)/(t+1)\), for \(t>1\), proves the last
column. The angle witnesses are
\(\cos(11\pi/25)<1/5\), \(u+v\ge1/4\) in the middle
range, and \(\cos(3\pi/5)<-3/10\) in the last range;
the other interval endpoints have smaller cosines.
Thus
\[
I_z(1/8)>
\frac3{4100}+\frac1{180}+\frac{33}{3400}
=\frac3{200}+\frac{623}{627300}
>\frac3{200}+\frac1{1100}.
\tag{14}
\]

The sine upper bound gives
\[
-J(1/8)\ge\tfrac18\{1-\log(\pi/4)\}>\frac3{20},
\tag{15}
\]
because \(\pi/4<4/5\) and \(\log(4/5)<-1/5\).
Also
\[
\delta_y\le\log(10/7)<9/25.
\tag{16}
\]
For the last witness, the first four exponential terms at
\(9/25\) sum to \(22384/15625>10/7\).
Equations (12)--(16) give the strict, same-point comparison
\[
\boxed{\qquad E_1(1/8;y,z)>\delta_y+1/1100,
                       \qquad R\ge7/10.\qquad}
\tag{17}
\]
No reference action or root was evaluated. Since every paired
first main/real-cut profile with this next real cut is already
paid by the joint profile theorem, (17) pays (3) throughout
this remaining range of radii. Together with (11), it covers
the entire actual depth interval.

## 4. Full finite consequence and remaining scope

Let
\[
C_{1,N}^{<1/2}(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil N/2\rceil-1}
              \frac{X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)}{P_j^3}.
\tag{18}
\]
The complete first middle-current comparisons are at the same
\(q\) and preserve every index-independent factor. They
therefore apply with the next last-row alternative attached:
both horizontal remainders, all four weighted side pieces and
both unweighted joins reduce to the first profiles paid above.
The first positive low block reduces to its original cut by
[the low-block theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_low_physical_blocks.md).
The distinct seam factorial symbol and complete forced Fourier
envelope are retained by the complete-current theorem, including
\(q=0\). No relative approximation for \(d_N(Y)\) at that
seam is required.

The whole next own-four upper block is bounded positively by
its real half and last profiles, by
[the own-four upper-row theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_own_four_upper_rows.md),
Section 3. Its half alternative is already paid by the joint
profile/current theorems; its last alternative is paid by
(11),(17). The next low and middle blocks are fully paid by
the complete-current theorem. Sharing the finite tolerances
inside the strict profile reserve proves
\[
\boxed{\quad
|U_{0,N}(w,X) C_{1,N}^{<1/2}(X,Y)d_N(Y)C_{1,N}(Y,Z)|
\,|d_N(Z)|^{1/2}
\le C N^B e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11}\}}.
\quad}
\tag{19}
\]
This retains every next physical row and every first row in
(18), both parities, all real ordinary colors, and the original
first-integration measure \(+N\,da\). Fixed real-color
derivatives have polynomial cost. The original integer-weight
holomorphic square, fixed base paths and smaller complex
neighborhoods give normalized local mixed jets with deficit
\(10^{-12}\). The strict overlap at \(R=7/10\) permits the
same local argument there.

The first upper physical block is not included in (19).
In particular this proof does not pay first-last by next-main,
first-last by next-last, or noncentral first-incoming pieces.
It makes no assertion for a next own-three kernel, whose
positive outgoing height and smaller denominator require
separate bounds. No complete next-transfer or new knot-family
conclusion follows.
