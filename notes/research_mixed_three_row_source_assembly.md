# Three-row assembly for ordinary sources and a contracted boundary sector

Research theorem, 5 November 2026. Every actual ordinary conditional
factor has its original supporting-plane upper rate on the full
independent horizontal plateau. The completed left-, half- and last-row
comparisons control every real row in the original finite contour.
The half- and last-row records have both passed their complete 320-bit
saved replays.

This removes the row excesses from all four first-pair prefix bounds.
Together with the terminal-transform end tails, and the high and low
Newton indices for terminal type three, it gives strict first-collar estimates
for the specified sectors of the exact contracted source. The
remaining central terminal transform, other sectors and arbitrary
short fillings are separate.

## 2. Complete finite support for every actual ordinary factor

Let the own type be \(3+\epsilon\), the first external type
\(3+\eta\), and \((s_*;a_*,b_*)\) either coupled orientation
from (3). The original allocated phase is
\[
\begin{split}
 F_\epsilon(v;a,b)
  ={}&g(v+a)+g(v+b)-(2+\epsilon)g(v)+2\pi iav\\
     &-(1+\eta/2)g(a)-(1+\epsilon/2)g(b),\\
 \phi(a,b)&=F_\epsilon(s(a,b);a,b).
\end{split}
 \tag{4}
\]
All roots, primitives and logarithmic lifts are the original ones.
The type of the other external input changes its parameter domain,
not the own flag in (4).

Write \(Z_*=(a_*,b_*)=U+iH\), let \(X=(x,z)\in P^2\),
\(P=[.22,.45]\), and put \(\kappa_*=1/2500000\). Define
\[
 B_{Z_*}(X)=
 \Re\phi(Z_*)+\Re\phi'(Z_*)\cdot(X-U)
                         -\kappa_*\|X-U\|^2 .
 \tag{5}
\]
The targets \(x+iH_1,z+iH_2\) retain the actual reference heights.
They are independent real plateau coordinates; they need not be
the outputs of a moving critical map.

**Theorem 1.** For every \(\tau>0\),
uniformly on all the actual ordinary reference families and \(P^2\),
both parities satisfy
\[
\begin{split}
 &|C_{\epsilon,N}(e^{-2\pi i(x+iH_1)},
                         e^{-2\pi i(z+iH_2)})|\,
 |d_N(e^{-2\pi i(x+iH_1)})|^{\eta/2}
 |d_N(e^{-2\pi i(z+iH_2)})|^{\epsilon/2}\\
 &\hspace{20mm}\le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
                       \exp N\{B_{Z_*}(X)+\tau\}.
\end{split}
 \tag{6}
\]
This is the complete finite conditional source with its two unselected
halfweights, not only its central integral.

*Proof.* The full-reference ordinary image bounds put the index
\(s_*\) in
\[
 .23\le\Re s_*\le.45,\qquad -.108\le\Im s_*\le.068 .
 \tag{7}
\]
Every retained actual target height has absolute value at most
\(.108\). Whenever its type is four its height is negative, by (2)
and the ordinary output bounds. These are precisely the physical
height hypotheses used by the fixed-contour estimates. In particular,
the larger auxiliary heights allowed by the half-row comparison are not used as physical
Fourier branches.

The [asymmetric fixed-contour theorem](research_mixed_horizontal_asymmetric_contour.md),
the [half-index extension](research_mixed_half_index_contour.md)
and the [own-four left-cut extension](research_mixed_four_left_cut_extension.md)
give the complete upper rate
\[
 \max\{B_{Z_*}(X),\mathcal R_\epsilon(\ell_\epsilon),
          \mathcal R_\epsilon(1/2),\mathcal R_\epsilon(1)\},
 \qquad \ell_0=3/20,\quad\ell_1=1/8 .
 \tag{8}
\]
Here \(\mathcal R_\epsilon\) is the original allocated full real-row
rate. The [right-tail endpoint theorem](research_mixed_right_tail_endpoint_bound.md)
reduces every intervening right row to the last two entries in (8)
on the actual height range. The left tail is increasing up to its
cut. All zero-mode joins, tall horizontal aliases, vertical aliases,
revived rows and physical endpoints have already been included in
the cited complete-contour maximum.

The left certificates bound their entries strictly below (5).
The completed half-row comparison does the same for \(1/2\).
The completed last-row comparison controls all four carriers at \(1\),
hence their maximum. Their domain intersections were verified in
Section 1. Consequently (8) equals \(B_{Z_*}(X)\).
The contour and finite-product tolerances are chosen to sum to the
single prescribed \(\tau\), proving (6). \(\square\)

The [complex-extension theorem](research_mixed_horizontal_complex_extension.md)
applies to the holomorphic Laurent polynomial
\[
 C_{\epsilon,N}(X_1,X_2)^2
                       d_N(X_1)^\eta d_N(X_2)^\epsilon .
 \tag{9}
\]
Its degree bands are \(O(N)\), the reference labels range over compact
families, and (5) is continuous. Hence (6), after squaring, extends
to a fixed complex neighborhood with any prescribed extra rate
tolerance and every fixed derivative of (9). No Fourier factor is
divided out. This is not a derivative assertion for a separately
weighted \(C_{\epsilon,N}\) at a zero of \(d_N\), and it supplies
no new ordinary-color dependence to a kernel which has none.

Theorem 1 includes the ordinary middle block of a length-three word
with two attached external inputs. It excludes the terminal
endpoint graph and any selected or independently attenuated later
height unless a separate transport theorem supplies that change.

## 3. All four first-collar prefixes with no row excess

Take \(m\ge4\), an ordinary cut
\(3\le h\le m-1\), and first types \((3+\alpha,3+\beta)\).
Use the full first collar \(x_1\in[0,\rho]\) and
\[
 y_2\in I_{3+\beta},\qquad
 I_3=[.267,.433],\quad I_4=[.24,.363],\qquad
 y_3,\ldots,y_h\in P .
 \tag{10}
\]
The later coordinates are unselected and retain their reference
heights. At coordinate two retain its physical height path:
between \(c_2\) and zero for an unselected coordinate, and the
proved selected path when \(\beta=1\). The first selection is also
retained. These are exactly the height and selection hypotheses of
the two cited prefix theorems.

Let \(\mathcal L_{h,N}\) be the literal prefix in
[ending three, equation (15)](research_mixed_first_collar_fixed_reference_completion.md)
or [ending four, equation (25)](research_mixed_four_enlarged_contour_transport.md).
It includes every full internal weight and exactly one cut halfweight.
Let \(d_j=y_j-\Re r_j\), and retain the original reference action
\(M_h\) and last allocated marginal \(P_{h,b}\). Theorem 1 sets
every ordinary excess \(D_j\) in those results to zero. Their exact
height transports on edge three and first-pair tangent cancellation
then give
\[
\begin{split}
\frac1N\log\mathcal L_{h,N}
 \le{}&M_h+\Re P_{h,b}d_h-\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\tau\\
 &-\kappa_*\left(d_2^2+
                 2\sum_{j=3}^{h-1}d_j^2+d_h^2\right)
       +O_h(\log N/N),\\
 \delta_{00}&=3/100,\qquad
 \delta_{10}=3/125,\qquad
 \delta_{01}=\delta_{11}=4/125 .
\end{split}
 \tag{11}
\]
In particular no small real neighborhood of coordinates two or three
is required, and no moving conditional root at their targets is
assumed. The ending-four transport pays its fixed \(9/500\)
allowance once, on edge three, from the first-pair \(1/20\) reserve.
The ending-three height allowance is already paid by its first pair.
There is no fixed loss charged at every later edge.

For a fixed word, choose its individual source tolerances to sum to
\(\tau\). The original cap supplies a fixed ordinary-color disk.
Squaring the whole prefix makes the cut weight integral, so the same
complex-extension and product-derivative conclusion applies to
(11), treating the transported second height as a compact label.
This does not extend the permitted selected-height paths to arbitrary
later selections.

## 4. Joining the terminal-transform end tails

Set \(h=m-1\), and use the exact contracted representation
[reversed-cap assembly, equation (13)](research_mixed_reversed_cap_assembly.md).
The terminal coordinate has already been summed coherently into
\(\mathcal V_{\epsilon_m,N}(w_R,X_h)\). Split it into its physical
transform-index end tails and central part, exactly as in
[the terminal-transform tail theorem](research_mixed_terminal_transform_tails.md).
These are end indices of the contracted transform; they are not a
claim about an arbitrarily chosen region of the former private
\(t_m\) contour.

Let \(a_*=r_h\), \(\nu=\epsilon_h\), \(\epsilon=\epsilon_m\),
and
\[
 \Pi(a_*)=P_\epsilon(a_*)-\frac{\nu}{2}g(a_*).
 \tag{12}
\]
The proved tail theorem gives, on the whole \(y_h\in P\),
\[
\begin{split}
 &|\mathcal V_{\epsilon,N}^{\rm end}
        (w_R,e^{-2\pi i(y_h+i\Im a_*)})|\,
       |d_N(e^{-2\pi i(y_h+i\Im a_*)})|^{\nu/2}\\
 &\qquad\le CN^A\exp N\left\{
       \Re\Pi(a_*)+\Re\Pi'(a_*)d_h
                      -\kappa_*d_h^2-\frac1{50}\right\}.
\end{split}
 \tag{13}
\]
It keeps the other cut halfweight. Its own terminal Fourier alternatives
were summed in the exact transform; no individual terminal alternative
is estimated by (13).

The two reference actions and cut derivatives satisfy the literal
allocated identities
\[
 M_h+\Re\Pi(a_*)=M_{\rm open},\qquad
                    P_{h,b}+\Pi'(a_*)=0 .
 \tag{14}
\]
Thus the product of (11) and (13) carries the full remaining cut
weight once and cancels its tangent. In the conditions (10), it has
the rate bound
\[
\begin{split}
 M_{\rm open}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\frac1{50}+\tau
       -\kappa_*\left(d_2^2+2\sum_{j=3}^{m-1}d_j^2\right)
       +O_m(\log N/N).
\end{split}
 \tag{15}
\]
Because \(\min\delta_{\alpha\beta}=3/125\), a total source
tolerance less than \(1/250\) leaves a common strict rate loss
\(1/25\), with the polynomial prefactor retained.
One may reserve a smaller part of this tolerance for the fixed complex
neighborhood rather than spending it twice.

Consequently, the full first collar with the
independent unselected plateau suffix in (10) has an exponentially
small end-tail contribution in the exact contracted source.
Integration over this fixed contour sector and multiplication by
the original scalar
\(\kappa_N^{m+k_4}N^{m+k_4-1}\) change only the polynomial
prefactor. The estimate is uniform in a fixed pair of ordinary-color
disks, and Cauchy gives every fixed ordinary boundary derivative on
smaller disks. For transverse derivatives, the asserted holomorphic
objects are the squared integer-allocated products, with an explicitly
reserved rate tolerance.

This sector allows both first selections and the proved second
type-four selection. Coordinates \(3,\ldots,m-1\) are unselected.
The entire terminal inner coordinate has been summed, but only its
end-index contribution in the transform is covered. The central
\(\mathcal V\) sum, later selected aliases, nonplateau regions,
lengths requiring a terminal edge in place of edge three, and global
ordinary-source dominance remain open obligations. The completed
ordinary row comparisons therefore do not alone prove the all-but-finitely-many result:
arbitrary short partial fillings and branching still require their
own arguments.

## 5. Two negligible ranges of Newton indices for a terminal type-three block

Use the same first-collar and plateau
hypotheses as Section 4, with terminal type three. First remove the
full transform-index end tails. In the remaining middle transform
indices, retain the two disjoint ranges
\(l\ge\lceil N/2\rceil\) and \(l/N\le1/200\) of the exact
[type-three Newton representation](research_mixed_terminal_three_newton_reduction.md).
This order makes all three discarded contributions disjoint.

The [high-Newton theorem, equation (23)](research_mixed_terminal_three_high_newton.md)
and [low-Newton theorem, equation (13)](research_mixed_terminal_three_low_newton.md)
bound each of these two contributions by the right side of (13) with
\(\epsilon=0\) and loss \(1/100\) in place of \(1/50\).
The restriction to middle transform indices is legitimate because
the theorem bounds the full positive double-sum majorant. Its
ordinary-color disk, both parities and fixed ordinary derivatives
are retained. The same allocated action \(\Pi\), cut halfweight
and exact tangent cancellation (14) apply.

Multiplying by the prefix (11) therefore gives the rate
\[
 M_{\rm open}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\frac1{100}+\tau
       -\kappa_*\left(d_2^2+2\sum_{j=3}^{m-1}d_j^2\right)
       +O_m(\log N/N).
 \tag{16}
\]
Since \(\min\delta_{\alpha\beta}=3/125\), total source
tolerance \(\tau<1/250\) leaves a common loss \(3/100\).
The end-tail contribution from Section 4 already has the stronger
loss \(1/25\), so their sum obeys the common \(3/100\)
bound, with its polynomial prefactor. The same ordinary-color
derivative and integer-allocated complex-extension qualifications
continue to apply, reserving any additional tolerance once.

For terminal type three, these discarded-region estimates leave
the coherent finite region
\[
 1/200<l/N<1/2,\qquad
 \delta<i/N<1-\delta,\qquad i+l<N.
 \tag{17}
\]
They do not estimate its coherent contribution, provide the analogous
Newton bounds for terminal type four, or remove the other sector
and filling obligations stated above.
