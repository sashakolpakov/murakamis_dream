# The complete positive transform alias on the ordinary left taper

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The original upper transform
alias has enough fixed reserve to pay its possible unfavorable
incoming derivative on the entire early taper. This avoids requiring
each profile to increase. The original lower transform cut is moved
as in the preceding boundary theorem; its additional positive-alias
horizontal segment is paid by the proved all-height transform force.
No new source evaluation or saved comparison is used.

 a=x+ih,\quad h=c(x-.1)/.12,\quad -.072<c<.021,\quad
 \lambda=-c/.12,\quad .1\le x\le.22,\quad s(x)=x-.07 .
                                                               \tag{1}

The terminal transform coordinate on the upper horizontal alias is
\(t=v+.12i\), with its original penalty \(2\pi(.12)=.24\pi\).

The other regular profiles have \(e=u+ik\),

 \mathcal H(\eta)=

The right endpoint action and private penalty are independent of

## 2. The shorter real-transform interval at height zero

**Lemma 1.** Uniformly for \(.15\le v\le.22\) and every
actual-private-height regular profile,
\[
                         \mathcal H(0)>-1/5.             \tag{3}
\]

*Proof.* Apply the exact \(k\)-monotonicity and real-private
endpoint reduction of
[the private-height transport](research_mixed_three_four_private_height_left_transport.md).
The \(v\)-derivative still has only downward crossings:
its numerator is
\(\lambda(1-r\cos\theta)+r\sin\theta\), where
\(r=e^{2\pi h}\), \(\theta=2\pi(v+x)\) and
\(\cos\theta\le0\).
At a zero its derivative is
\(r(\cos\theta-r)/(1-r\cos\theta)<0\).
Thus minimize at \(v=.15,.22\), \(u=.27,.49\), \(k=-.019\).

The case \(v=.22\) already has \(\mathcal H(0)>1/100\).
For \(v=.15\), the same fixed-radius real-part reduction takes
its lower bound to \(x=.22\).
When \(\lambda\ge0\), the first squared modulus ratio exceeds
\(4/3\); the second is at least \(270/143\), using
\[
       -\cos(.74\pi)=\cos(.26\pi)>2/3 .
\]
This elementary guard follows from
\(\cos z\ge1-z^2/2+z^4/24-z^6/720\) at
\(z=2\pi(13/100)<143/175\); substitution of this rational
upper endpoint gives a value greater than \(2/3\).
The same-radius quotient is minimized at \(r=3/5\) on the
original range \(3/5\le r\le1\), giving \(270/143\).
Consequently
\[
 \Re D>\tfrac12\log(360/143)
   =\operatorname{atanh}(217/503)
   >3/7+(3/7)^3/3=156/343>9/20.                         \tag{4}
\]
For \(u=.49\), the negative private height improves the original
argument lower bound \(\Im D\ge\pi(v-u)\).
For \(u=.27\), its loss is less than \(.019\pi\);
\(.27+.019<.49\).
Thus the imaginary debit is at most
\((3/5)(.49-.15)\pi<13/20\).
This proves (3).
For \(\lambda\le0\), the original upper argument bound plus that
private-height correction gives
\(\Im D<\pi(.22-.27+.019)<0\).
Both modulus ratios exceed one, so \(\mathcal H(0)>0\).
\(\square\)

On the rest of the old interval,
\[
              \mathcal H(0)>1/100\qquad(.22\le v\le.5).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
The interval through \(.49\) is the private-height theorem.
On \([.49,.5]\), the same downward-crossing argument reduces to
its two endpoints; the endpoint \(v=.5\) has slope \(>1/10\) by
[the calibrated upper-triangle theorem](research_mixed_three_four_left_upper_triangle_completion.md).

## 3. The positive-height cost is bounded before transport

Put \(q=e^{2\pi(h+\eta)}\), \(C=\cos2\pi(v+x)\) and
\(S=\sin2\pi(v+x)\). Exact differentiation, including the
ordinary chirp, gives
\[
 \partial_\eta\mathcal H
 =-2\pi\,\frac{1-qC-\lambda qS}{1+q^2-2qC}.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
If \(.15\le v\le.22\), then \(C\le0\) and \(S\ge0\).
For \(\lambda\ge0\), discard the favorable term
\(-\lambda qS\). Since \(q\ge r>3/5\),
\[
 \frac{1-qC}{1+q^2-2qC}\le25/34.
\]
To check this, when \(q\le1\) the quotient increases with \(C\)
and is at most \(1/(1+q^2)\le25/34\); when \(q\ge1\)
it is at most \(1/2\).
For \(\lambda\le0\), one has \(q\ge r\ge1\), and
\[
 \frac{1-qC-\lambda qS}{1+q^2-2qC}
       \le\frac12+\frac7{80}=\frac{47}{80}<\frac{25}{34}.
\]
Here \(q/(1+q^2)\le1/2\), \(S\le1\), and
\(-\lambda\le7/40\). Hence
\[
        \partial_\eta\mathcal H\ge-25\pi/17
                  \quad(.15\le v\le.22,\ 0\le\eta\le.12).
                                                               \tag{7}
\]

If \(.22\le v\le.5\), then \(C<-9/50\).
Thus \(q-C>3/5+9/50>|\lambda|\) in the
\(\lambda\ge0\) case; in the other case \(q\ge1\) is stronger.
It follows that
\(1-qC-\lambda qS\le1+q^2-2qC\), giving
\[
        \partial_\eta\mathcal H\ge-2\pi
                  \quad(.22\le v\le.5,\ 0\le\eta\le.12).
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
The cosine guard at the upper end is
\(\cos(1.44\pi)=-\sin(.06\pi)<-9/50\).
All heights in this argument retain the same original incoming
coefficient/radius correlation.

The cap true-end alternative has incoming derivative nonnegative
at \(\eta=0\) throughout \(.15\le v\le.5\).
Indeed its conditional modulus ratio exceeds one, and the rotated
argument gives \(\Im D_{\rm ord}\ge\pi v\) if \(\lambda\ge0\).
If \(\lambda\le0\), then
\(\Im D_{\rm ord}\le\pi v<2\pi\) and
\(E_{\rm end}'=-2\pi\lambda\) pays the possible debit.
Its \(\eta\)-derivative is still exactly (6), so (7)--(8)
apply to this alternative as well.

## 4. The original alias reserve pays the full incoming interval

Integrate (7) or (8) from height zero to \(3/25\), then integrate
the incoming derivative from \(x\) to \(.22\).
The maximum possible loss is bounded, respectively, by
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 3/125+9\pi/425<23/250,&\text{regular profiles, }v\le.22,\\
 18\pi/625-3/2500<23/250,&\text{regular profiles, }v\ge.22,\\
 9\pi/425<23/250\ \text{or}\ 18\pi/625<23/250,
                                      &\text{true-end profiles}.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{9}
\]
These are direct rational consequences of \(\pi<22/7\).
For example \(198/2975<17/250\) proves the first comparison;
\(396/4375<23/250\) proves the last.
No differentiability of a maximizing index is required: integrate
each fixed profile first and take the maximum afterward.
The private cap aliases stay below the actual-height main pointwise.

Subtracting (9) from the original raw reserve \(1/10\) gives
\[
             \text{coupled positive-alias rate}<M-1/125
                   \quad(.15\le v\le.5).                \tag{10}
\]
All right-private sampling penalties remain unchanged, so (10)
covers both upper single-alias choices and both upper
double-alias choices.

For the added segment \(s(x)\le v\le.15\), apply
[the all-height low-transform force](research_mixed_three_four_left_low_transform_force.md).
It transports the phase at \(\Im t=.12\) to \(v=.15\)
at the same incoming \(a\), with a favorable strict decrease.
The complete cap and all sampling penalties are independent of
this real transform motion. Equation (10) therefore holds on
the entire new upper horizontal alias, \(s(x)\le v\le.5\).

Use the original exact retained row and half-grid conventions,
with the whole endpoint coefficient. Every numerator and reciprocal
argument on these bounded contours stays in its regular strip.
The upper-alias kernels retain their original exponential penalties.
The finite source estimates and small ordinary-color perturbations
therefore give a rate \(M-1/250\), at both parities and for all
fixed ordinary-color derivatives.
Private joins within this upper-alias group are controlled by their
original private-height convexity; on the added segment this also
follows from (12) of the moving-left-boundary theorem.
Indeed the shifted estimate
\(C(b+t)\le\cot\pi(\Re b+\Re t)\), when that real sum is
below \(1/2\), holds at every imaginary height; when the real
sum is at least \(1/2\), its sign is nonpositive. Thus that
private-variable proof applies unchanged at \(\Im t=.12\).

No convexity in the transform height on the new small-\(v\) region
has been asserted. In particular this theorem does not yet pay
the transform vertical sides joining the moving cut to the upper
alias, or the unpenalized transform joins. Those are separate
complete-current obligations, as are the remaining physical sectors
and the full incoming circle.
