# Transport of the original type-four vertical source bounds

Research deduction, 5 November 2026. The original real complement and
horizontal aliases can be bounded along the full second-four height
path without a new row cover. The comparison ceiling is the **fixed
original reference action**, not the moving coherent action. This
distinction retains the unused first-pair reserve. The central
zero-mode contour is a separate obligation; this note alone is not a
complete finite-kernel theorem.

## 1. Literal allocation and the reference source inputs

Use the path and original logarithmic branch of
[the vertical phase theorem](research_mixed_four_first_vertical_phase.md):
\(a(k)=x+ik\), \(.24<x<.363\), \(-.102<c<-.001\),
and \(c\le k\le H=7/500\). The unchanged second argument is
the actual next reference \(b\), of type \(3+\epsilon\).
The two actual domains for \(b\) are those in that theorem's (10).
In particular both arguments stay in the old universal strip
\(\mathcal Q\).

Write \(g(z)=(\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-2\pi iz})-\pi^2/6)/(2\pi i)\),
\(J(t)=\Re g(t)\), and
\[
 Q(w;a,b)=g(w+a)+g(w+b)-(2+\epsilon)g(w)+2\pi iaw.
 \tag{1}
\]
For the literal bare conditional kernel, the complete real-row rate is
\[
 R(v;k)=I_{a(k)}(v)+I_b(v)-(2+\epsilon)J(v)-2\pi kv,
 \qquad I_z(v)=\int_0^v\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(z+t)}|\,dt.
 \tag{2}
\]
Every \(0\le v\le1\), including the revived endpoint, remains in
this expression. The allocated other halfweight is the constant
\(K_b=-\epsilon\Re g(b)/2\). The first halfweight is
\[
 q_0(k)=-\tfrac12\Re g(a(k))\quad(c\le k\le0),\qquad
 q_1(k)=\max\{-\tfrac12\Re g(a(k))-\pi k,\,0\}
                                      \quad(0\le k\le H).
 \tag{3}
\]
These are the exact two Fourier alternatives, including the selected
monomial. Define the fixed reference ceiling
\[
 F_* =\Re Q(s_c;a(c),b)-\tfrac32\Re g(a(c))
                         -(1+\epsilon/2)\Re g(b).
 \tag{4}
\]

The [reduced-disk source theorem](research_mixed_reduced_disk_conditional_packets.md)
supplies the reference conditional packet when the next vertex is
ordinary. If it is the private last vertex, use the
[terminal conditional packet](research_mixed_terminal_conditional_packet.md).
Their original complete-contour certificates give, at \(k=c\),
\[
 R(v;c)+q_0(c)+K_b < F_*-1/1000
 \quad(v\in[0,.15]\cup[.49,1]).
 \tag{5}
\]
This uses the actual reference coupling. It does not assert (5) on
the independent rectangular hull of the displayed height intervals.

The saved lower endpoints of the same accepted author and replay
records also give the following common horizontal-alias gaps. Here
the first argument's type selects the row of the table; the entries
include all own types, both ordinary orientations and both terminal
families.

| First type | Lower side \(w=t-.2i\) | Upper side \(w=t+.2i\) |

## 2. The whole real complement by a Poisson-kernel identity

For \(0<r<1\), put
\[
 P_r(t)=\frac{1-r^2}{|1-re^{-2\pi it}|^2},\qquad
 \int_0^1P_r(t)\,dt=1.
 \tag{6}
\]
The normalization follows, for example, by integrating the absolutely
convergent Fourier series of the Poisson kernel. Direct differentiation
of (2), with the other argument fixed, gives
\[
 \partial_kR(v;k)=
 \begin{cases}
 -\pi v-\pi\displaystyle\int_x^{x+v}P_{e^{2\pi k}}(t)\,dt,&k<0,\\
 -\pi v+\pi\displaystyle\int_x^{x+v}P_{e^{-2\pi k}}(t)\,dt,&k>0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{7}
\]
The second formula also follows from
\(I_{x+ik}(v)=2\pi kv+I_{x-ik}(v)\).
The integral in (7) is between zero and one, even when the interval
wraps across an integer. Thus no revived or wrapped row is discarded.
The formulas integrate to \(k=0\) by continuity of the logarithmic
integrals, including their integrable zeros.

On this real-part range the original logarithm obeys
\[
 0<\Im g'(x+ik)\le\pi(1/2-x)\quad(k\le0),\qquad
 \Im g'(x+ik)<\pi(1-2x)\quad(k\ge0).
 \tag{8}
\]
Indeed the argument of \(1-re^{-2\pi ix}\) increases with \(r>0\),
has value \(\pi(1/2-x)\) at \(r=1\), and tends to
\(\pi(1-2x)\) at infinity.

For \(v\ge.15\), (7)--(8) imply
\[
 \partial_k(R+q_0)\le\pi(1/4-x/2-v)<0\quad(c<k<0).
 \tag{9}
\]
For the selected factorial branch at positive height,
\[
 \partial_k\{R-\Re g(a)/2-\pi k\}
 \le\pi(1/2-x-v)\le11\pi/100.
 \tag{10}
\]
Its increase from zero to any \(k\le H\) is less than
\(77\pi/50000<121/25000<1/200\).
The forced selected branch has increase at most
\(\pi H(1-v)\le119\pi/10000<187/5000\).
It starts below the factorial branch by \(-J(x)/2\). This credit
is uniformly larger than \(137/3500\):
\[
 -J(x)/2\ge\tfrac12\int_{.363}^{.5}\log(2\sin\pi t)\,dt
 >\frac{137}{3500}>\frac{187}{5000}.
 \tag{11}
\]
For the strict estimate, \(\sin(.363\pi)=\cos(.137\pi)>9/10\)
follows from \(\cos t\ge1-t^2/2\) and \(\pi<22/7\);
\(\log(9/5)=2\operatorname{atanh}(2/7)>4/7\).
Also \(J\) is increasing on \([.24,.5]\).
Consequently the forced branch never exceeds its original reference
row rate.

Combining these facts with (5) proves
\[
 R(v;k)+q_\sigma(k)+K_b <F_*+1/250
 \quad(v\in[.49,1])
 \tag{12}
\]
on both physical height intervals. For \(v\in[0,.15]\), the old
left-tail derivative \(\partial_vR>1/2\) is unchanged: both
arguments still lie in \(\mathcal Q\), and the halfweights are
independent of \(v\). Its maximum is therefore the .15 cut, to
which the same calculation applies. Hence (12) holds on the entire
real complement. The sharper negative-height statement retains the
full original \(1/1000\) gap. No quartic/sextic tail-shape test on
the moved parameters is required.

## 3. The revived endpoint separately

At \(v=1\), the bare rate is exactly
\(L(k)=2\pi(-k)_++2\pi(\Im b)_+\).
On \([c,0]\) the derivative of its allocated rate is
\(-2\pi+\Im g'(a)/2<0\). On \([0,H]\) the selected
factorial derivative is \(\Im g'(a)/2-\pi<0\), and the
forced selected rate is constant. At zero the factorial branch is
larger since \(J(x)<0\). Thus the revived endpoint itself
retains its original strict gap relative to \(F_*\) throughout
the path, including references with \(c>-.014\).

For comparison, the bare moving-saddle last-row gap has derivative
\(2\pi-\theta\) below zero and \(-\theta\) above zero,
where \(0<\theta<\pi\). That less useful comparison can lose a
margin for thin negative reference heights. It is not needed here.

## 4. Horizontal aliases along the original path

For fixed \(w=t\pm.2i\), \(.15\le t\le.49\), put
\(C=\cot\pi w\), \(P=\cot\pi a\). On the original lift,
\[
 Q_a(w;a,b)-g'(a)=\log_{\rm R}(C+P)-\log_{\rm R}(C-i).
 \tag{13}
\]
All these logarithms have right-half-plane arguments. Equality is
calibrated on the real strip and continued there; neither the other
argument nor its height enters (13). Let \(\theta_w\) be the
imaginary part. Always \(-\pi<\theta_w<\pi\). On the upper
side, \(\Im C<0\), and the ratio's imaginary numerator is
\[
 \Re C(1+\Im P)+\Re P(1-\Im C)>0,
 \tag{14}
\]
since \(\Im P>-.1\); hence \(0<\theta_w<\pi\).
For the original moving critical branch write \(\theta\in(0,\pi)\)
as in the vertical phase theorem. The alias gap
\(G_w=\Re Q(s(k);a,b)-\Re Q(w;a,b)+2\pi/5\) has derivative
\(G_w'=\theta_w-\theta\). The total length is at most
\(H-c<29/250\). Its loss is therefore less than
\(29\pi/250\) on the upper side and \(29\pi/125\) below.
The extracted reference floors give
\[
 G_{t+.2i}>191/500-29\pi/250>1/60,
 \qquad
 G_{t-.2i}>989/1000-29\pi/125>1/4.
 \tag{15}
\]
The same halfweight is added to the critical and alias rates in
each branch. The vertical phase theorem puts both allocated critical
rates below \(F_*\); therefore all horizontal alias rates remain
strictly below \(F_*\), with the margins (15).
This argument uses the continued critical branch, but does not assert
that it lies inside the old row contour.

## 5. What remains, and the exact first-pair allocation

The transported real complement has excess less than \(1/250\),
and the horizontal aliases have strict reference gaps. To conclude a
finite upper bound, the original zero-mode central contour and its
vertical joins still have to be bounded by a reference ceiling.
Neither cotangent-strip continuation alone nor the phase theorem
proves that assertion. A fixed original contour can be used for an
upper bound if its own rate is controlled; a new moving saddle is
not logically required.

Suppose those remaining currents have allocated rate at most
\(F_*+E\), with \(E\ge1/250\). The unchanged finite contour
argument then yields the literal next-edge upper rate
\(F_*+E+\eta\), uniformly with all rows and both Fourier branches.
The ending-four first-pair theorem gives its own rate
\(M_\alpha-1/20\), before adding a supporting tangent. Thus
the two-factor rate is at most
\[
 M_\alpha+F_*-1/20+E+\eta.
 \tag{16}
\]
Equivalently, if \(P_2\) is the first pair's second marginal and
\(\Delta=k-c\), its reference-plane deficit includes
\(\Delta(-\Im P_2)\); the next reference plane changes by
\(\Delta\Im P_2\), since its marginal is \(-P_2\).
These two terms cancel exactly. There is no additional tangent
debit hidden in (16). In particular a complete source allowance
\(E\le1/100\) would leave \(1/25\) before one final tolerance.
This is a sufficient assembly statement, not an assertion that the
remaining central-current bound has been proved here.
