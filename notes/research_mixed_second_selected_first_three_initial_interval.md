# The complete first real interval for the second-selected first-three product

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The literal second-selected product
after an initial type-three cap is exponentially below its original
affine plane on the entire first incoming real interval \([0,1/10]\).
The selected second coordinate ranges over the full plateau, not only
the ordinary type-four image interval. Every conditional row and both
selected-weight alternatives remain.

The proof combines a full-plateau seam comparison, profilewise transport
to the already paid point \(a=.1\), and a separate all-real-color bound
for the true first-cap index ends. No compact physical-index estimate
is promoted to an off-grid endpoint bound. No numerical evaluation,
certificate replay or new reference is used.

## 1. The original finite product

Keep the canonical ordinary-next family and definitions (1)--(5) of

The first flags are \((0,1,\beta)\), \(\beta=0,1\); the indices

In particular
\[
 \Im a_*<0,\qquad
 z_*\in O_4,\quad u_*\in O_4,\quad v_*,b_*\in O_{3+\beta}.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
These are the actual correlated canonical map outputs. The negative
first height is the full-disk half-chain guard already used in the
selected left and right theorems.

The new targets are
\[
 a=x\in[0,.1],\qquad z=y+iH,\quad H=1/100,\qquad
 b=w+i\Im b_*,\qquad y,w\in P=[.22,.45].
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
With \(X=e^{-2\pi ia},Y=e^{-2\pi iz},Z=e^{-2\pi ib}\), consider
\[
 \mathcal B_N=
 |U_{0,N}(\omega,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)C_{\beta,N}(Y,Z)
                         d_N(Y)Y^{-N}|\ |d_N(Z)|^{\beta/2}.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
The definitions of \(U_0,C_\epsilon,p_j,P_j\), the original chirps,
and the affine plane \(\Lambda_{123}(b)\) are unchanged.
Write \(J(t)=\Re g(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi s)\,ds\) on
\([0,1]\), with its continuous endpoint values.
There is no incoming halfweight. At \(\beta=0\), the last factor
is one, including at zeros of \(d_N\).

**Theorem.** For every prescribed \(\tau>0\),
\[
 \mathcal B_N\le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
       e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(b)-1/400+\tau\}}                    \tag{4}
\]
uniformly on (1)--(2), at sufficiently large levels of both parities.
It holds for every real ordinary color and every fixed ordinary-color
derivative with polynomial costs, and on a sufficiently small fixed
complete-color neighborhood with its prescribed extra tolerance.
One common sufficiently small tolerance leaves finite loss \(1/800\).
The same rate bounds the integral over this fixed first real interval
with its original contour density and polynomial prefactors.

The third target remains on its ordinary plateau. This theorem
does not estimate the nonplateau pieces of the selected second
coordinate or the entire selected Fourier coefficient.

## 3. The cap envelope on the remaining real interval

Fix the preceding \(\rho\) and work on \([\rho,.1]\).
For a real private index \(e\), set
\[
 R_0(e,x)=\lambda(e)+I_x(e),\quad
 \lambda(e)=\Re S(e),\quad
 I_x(e)=\int_0^e\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(x+s)}|\,ds .
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
[The real small-angle cap proof](research_mixed_three_four_real_small_angle_source.md),
Sections 2--3, proves the exact physical tail comparisons
\[
 R_0(e,x)\le R_0(.27,x)\quad(e\le.27),\qquad
 R_0(e,x)\le R_0(.5,x)\quad(e\ge.5).
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
It therefore retains, in addition to the five original central
cap profiles, the real physical profile
\[
                    \max_{.49\le e\le.5}R_0(e,x).              \tag{16}
\]
At \(x=.1\), this extra profile is below \(R_0(.49,.1)\).
This endpoint comparison is proved in that source; it is not
asserted at smaller \(x\). The central cap contour is unchanged,
with private main height \(\Im e_*\), real cuts \(.27,.49\),
and original aliases \(-.055,.04\).
Its strict private vertical convexity holds on the whole new
real interval, so these five profiles retain all joins.

For small complete-color disks, the whole-index polynomial theorem
used in that proof supplies (14) through both index ends.
For all real colors we instead remove two fixed extreme cap
layers at the level of the literal sum. The
[real-circle index-end theorem](research_mixed_cap_real_circle_index_ends.md)
gives, for some fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
 U_{\rm end,N}^{\rm abs}(\omega,X)
  :=\sum_{j/N\le\delta\ {\rm or}\ j/N\ge1-\delta}
       |H_N(\omega,r^{-j})p_j(X)/P_j|
       \le C N^B e^{3N/5},\qquad |X|=1 .
                                                               \tag{17}
\]
The remaining physical tails lie on compact index intervals,
where the complete \(H_N\) all-real-color bound in
[endpoint real-color decay](research_mixed_endpoint_real_color_decay.md)
has exactly the rate used in (14). On the central complex
private rectangle its whole \(C_N(e,\omega)\) estimate preserves
every original cap profile. Thus (15)--(16) bound all remaining
cap rows for all real colors, while (17) is kept separate.
No continuum-arc consequence is inferred from a bound at physical
indices, and no endpoint coefficient is split into Newton terms
in the central part.

## 4. Every central conditional current transports to \(x=.1\)

Use the fixed real transform cuts \(.11,.5\), original main
height \(h_u\), and the two heights \(-1/5,8\) of the selected
left-transition theorem at its endpoint \(x=.1\).
For each regular cap state, each conditional point
\(t=v+i\eta\) on these currents, and \(x\in[\rho,.1]\),
the exact incoming derivative is
\[
 \frac d{dx}\Re\{\Psi_0(e,x)+Q(t;x,z)\}
 =\log\frac{
 |1-e^{-2\pi i(e+x)}|\,
 |e^{-2\pi\eta}-e^{-2\pi i(x+v)}|}
 {(2\sin\pi x)^2}
 >\log(3/2).
                                                               \tag{18}
\]
The real part of \(e+x\) is in \([.27,.59]\), so the first
modulus exceeds one at every private height.
The second modulus exceeds \(3/5\): \(x+v\in[.11,.6]\);
when its cosine is positive, minimizing over its nonnegative
radius gives a sine at least \(\sin(.22\pi)>3/5\), and
when its cosine is nonpositive the minimum is one.
Finally
\[
              (2\sin\pi x)^2\le(3-\sqrt5)/2<2/5.
                                                               \tag{19}
\]
All sampling penalties are independent of \(x\).
The derivative is independent of \(z\); its incoming chirp is
essential for the reciprocal radius in (18).

For the extra real profile (16), the same formula holds with
\(\Re\Psi_0\) replaced by \(R_0(e,x)\); its first modulus
also exceeds one because \(e+x\in[.49,.6]\).
Thus every central-cap/conditional-current pair is bounded by
its value at \(x=.1\). There (16) clips to the old private
right cut, and the selected left-transition proof pays all
these individual current profiles with raw loss \(1/400\),
including both selected weights and every third profile.
This uses its profilewise proof, not an inference from a
bound on a complete signed source.

The same statement includes all four conditional vertical aliases
and both zero-mode joins: (18) holds at every intermediate
height on their actual paths. The cap's original five-profile
envelope already includes its own vertical sides.
All factorial arguments remain in regular strips on each fixed
\([\rho,.1]\); the exact half-grid identity keeps density \(+N\).
No new index convexity statement is needed here.

The physical low conditional rows also reduce to this anchor.
For \(0<t\le.11\) the exact real force is
\[
 e^{\partial_t\Re Q(t;x,z)}
 =\frac{2\sin\pi(x+t)\,
                |1-e^{-2\pi i(z+t)}|}{(2\sin\pi t)^3}
       >\frac1{(2\sin\pi t)^2}>1 .                            \tag{20}
\]
Here \(t\le x+t\le.21<.5\), and \(y+t\in[.22,.56]\),
so the second modulus exceeds one at its radius greater than one.
The continuous rate at \(t=0\) is zero. Uniform physical product
bounds therefore pay all rows below the left half-grid cut by
the real face \(t=.11\), then (18) transports that face to
the paid endpoint.

## 5. Every physical high conditional row reduces to half or last

Let \(E(t;x,z)\) be the real physical row rate in equation (20)
of the selected left-transition theorem, now with \(h=0\).
Its exact last-row value is
\[
                            E(1)=2\pi H=\ell=\pi/50 .
                                                               \tag{21}
\]
Put \(R_b=e^{-\ell}>14/15\). For \(v=1-t\), the exact
reflection including the positive-height outgoing radial drift is
\[
 F(v):=E(1-v)-E(1)
 =-\int_{x-v}^x f_1(s)\,ds-\int_{y-v}^y f_{R_b}(s)\,ds
                         +3J(v)-\ell v ,
 \quad f_R(s)=\log|1-Re^{-2\pi is}|.
                                                               \tag{22}
\]
The guard follows from \(\ell<1/15\) and
\(e^{1/15}<(1-1/15)^{-1}=15/14\).

For \(0<v\le11/50\), \(v\le y\le1/2\). The same
centered-arc and radial-primitive inequalities as in the
selected-left proof give
\[
 F(v)\le v\{\log(4\pi v)-1-\ell\}<0.
                                                               \tag{23}
\]
The last sign includes the larger endpoint \(v=.22\):
\[
 4\pi(11/50)<484/175<212/75
        <e^{1+\pi/50},
                                                               \tag{24}
\]
using \(e>8/3\), \(\pi>3\), and \(\pi<22/7\).
Thus the entire short reflected interval lies below the last row.

For \(11/50\le v\le1/3\), the incoming modulus is at most
\(S(v)=2\sin\pi v\), because \(0\le x\le.1\le2v\).
The outgoing distance is at most \(.45-v\). The radial
comparison (24) in the selected-left proof bounds its modulus
by \(S(.45-v)\), since \(R_b>14/15\) and
\(.45-v\ge7/60>1/10\).
Consequently
\[
 e^{F'(v)}
 \ge R_b\,\frac{S(v)^2}{S(.45-v)}
 >\frac{14}{15}\frac{8/5}{10/7}
                         =392/375>1 .
                                                               \tag{25}
\]
The ratio increases in \(v\) on this interval. At its left
endpoint \(S(.22)^2>8/5\), since \(\cos(.44\pi)<1/5\);
the denominator \(S(.23)<\sqrt2<10/7\).
For \(1/3\le v\le1/2\), the first modulus is still at
most \(S(v)\), the second is less than \(8/5\), and \(S(v)^2\ge3\).
Hence
\[
                         e^{F'(v)}>7/4>1 .                    \tag{26}
\]
The second-modulus bound uses \(|y-v|\le.28\), exactly as
in the selected-left proof. Integrable logarithmic zeros are
handled by limits. Equations (23), (25), (26) prove
\[
       \max_{1/2\le t\le1}E(t)=\max\{E(1/2),E(1)\}.
                                                               \tag{27}
\]
The zero-safe finite product bounds include the entire high
sum, both parities, the even half row and every revived term.

The half branch is already covered by (18) at \(v=.5,\eta=0\).
The last branch is constant in \(x\). Each cap profile has
positive incoming derivative on \([\rho,.1]\), since its
shifted numerator modulus exceeds one and \(2\sin\pi x<1\).
The same holds for (16). Thus each coupled cap/last-row profile
increases to \(x=.1\), where the selected-left proof pays it.
This completes every high physical row without substituting
physical support for a contour arc.
