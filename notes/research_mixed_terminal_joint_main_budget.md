# The coupled main current at the last selected coordinate

Research deduction and fixed diagnostic protocol, 6 November 2026.
The original ordinary and terminal phases have a common stationary
shared leg. Keeping that stationarity removes the linear height fee
introduced by the separate horizontal terminal plane. Their joint main
comparison has an exact three-force upper bound, with no terminal
private-action evaluation. This note also reduces the complete terminal
alphabet from 378 to 54 profiles on an explicit incoming-height subclass.
No new comparison sign or numerical evaluation is asserted.

## 1. The complete weight and the joint phase

For a last retained selection in a word of length at least three, put
\(\alpha=n_{m-1}-3=1\), \(\beta=n_{m-2}-3\),
\(\epsilon=n_m-3\), with \(\beta,\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\).
Write \(b=z_{m-2}\), \(a=z_{m-1}\), and fix
\(H=1/100\), \(P=[11/50,9/20]\).
The literal allocated modulus from the
[complete terminal envelope](research_mixed_terminal_concave_profile_envelope.md),
Section 5, is
\[
 \mathcal K_N=
 |C_{\alpha,N}(X_b,X_a)\mathcal V_{\epsilon,N}(w,X_a)
              d_N(X_a)^\alpha X_a^{-N}|\,|d_N(X_b)|^{\beta/2},
 \qquad X_z=e^{-2\pi iz},\quad\alpha=1.
 \tag{1}
\]
The terminal source already contains its own full terminal diagonal
and Fourier polynomial. No extra \(\epsilon\)-multiple of the
shared weight is inserted. If the shared weight in (1) is split for
an estimate, its two exponents are \(\alpha/2+\alpha/2=1\),
and its selected monomial has exponents \(1/2+1/2=1\).
The incoming exponent \(\beta/2\) is a modulus allocation to be
matched by the adjoining window, not a holomorphic square root.

Use the original \(g\) and
\[
 Q_1(s;b,a)=g(s+b)+g(s+a)-3g(s)-g(b)-g(a)+2\pi ibs.
 \tag{2}
\]
Let \(\Phi_\epsilon(a;t,v)\) be the two regular terminal phases
in equations (2)--(3) of the terminal envelope. Here \(v=\xi\)
for \(\epsilon=0\), and \(v=e\) for \(\epsilon=1\).
The real phase of the main/main alternative is
\[
 f_\nu=\Re\{Q_1(s;b,a)+\Phi_\epsilon(a;t,v)
             -\nu g(a)-\tfrac\beta2g(b)\}-2\pi\nu H,
 \qquad \nu\in\{0,1\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
**The factorial alternative is \(\nu=1\); the forced alternative
is \(\nu=0\).** These labels refer to the complete selected weight.
For every positive tolerance its original additive estimate is
\[
 |d_N(X_a)X_a^{-N}|\le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
      \{e^{N(w_H(\Re a)+\tau)}+e^{N\tau}\},\qquad
 w_H(x)=-\Re g(x+iH)-2\pi H.
 \tag{4}
\]
The [branch-domain theorem](research_mixed_selected_weight_branch_domains.md)
proves \(w_H(x)>1/875\) for \(x\in P\), \(x\le2/5\).
At each such target the factorial rate strictly dominates the forced
rate, with all other profile terms unchanged. Therefore the complete
upper envelope is obtained using
\[
 I_1=P,\qquad I_0=[2/5,9/20]
 \tag{5}
\]
for the real part of \(a\). Equivalently, for any common profile
\(R\), its full supremum of \(R+\max(w_H,0)\) equals the
maximum of the factorial supremum on \(P\) and the forced
supremum on \(I_0\). No physical summand is deleted, the two
rates are not replaced by their minimum, and the factorial domain
has not been shortened.

## 2. Original reference and horizontal concavity

Keep an original coupled reference
\((b_*,s_*,a_*,t_*,v_*)\), with the original zero logarithmic
lifts. For type three, the
[off-shell elimination identity](research_mixed_terminal_three_critical_curve.md),
equations (17)--(20), gives \(v_*=\xi_*\) and
\(\Phi_0(a_*;t_*,v_*)=P_0(a_*)\), including its absolute
complex constant. For type four, the
[coherent cap elimination](research_mixed_terminal_four_reduced_currents.md)
gives the same identity with \(v_*=e_*\) and \(P_1\).
In both cases the terminal internal derivatives vanish. Set
\[
 \begin{split}
 M_*&=Q_1(s_*;b_*,a_*)+P_\epsilon(a_*)
                         -g(a_*)-\tfrac\beta2g(b_*),\\
 p_*&=g'(b_*+s_*)-(1+\beta/2)g'(b_*)+2\pi i s_*,\\
 D_\nu&=f_\nu-\Re\{M_*+p_*(b-b_*)\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{6}
\]
The two remaining internal equations are exactly
\[
 Q_{1,s}(s_*;b_*,a_*)=0,\qquad
 g'(s_*+a_*)+g'(t_*+a_*)-3g'(a_*)+2\pi it_*=0.
 \tag{7}
\]
The second is the shared-leg equation, not a new terminal plane.

The full target product uses the original fixed heights and intervals
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{coordinate}&\text{target}\\ \hline
 b&P+i\Im b_*\\
 s&[1/8,1/2]+i\Im s_*\\
 a&I_\nu+iH\\
 t&[3/20,1/2]+i\Im t_*\\
 v,\ \epsilon=0&[1/10,2/5]+i\Im\xi_*\\
 v,\ \epsilon=1&[27/100,49/100]+i\Im e_* .
 \end{array}
 \tag{8}
\]
Assume \(\Im a_*\in[-.108,-.001]\) and the other original heights
lie in the products of the terminal
envelope and the ordinary compiler: \(|\Im b_*|\le.108\),
\(\Im s_*\in[-.102,-.001]\),
\(|\Im t_*|\le.12\), and respectively
\(|\Im\xi_*|\le.12\) or
\(\Im e_*\in[-.055,.04]\). Original references are interior
to the displayed real intervals after replacing \(I_\nu\) by \(P\).

**Lemma 1.** Each \(D_\nu\) is jointly horizontally concave on
the larger product obtained from (8) by replacing \(I_\nu\)
by \(P\). The same holds throughout the vertical segment from
\(\Im a_*\) to \(H\).

*Proof.* The [ordinary compiler](research_mixed_plateau_window_concave_profiles.md),
equation (9), gives \(-\Re Q_1''\succ(1/1250000)I_3\)
on its full three-variable product. The terminal envelope gives
\(-\Re\Phi_\epsilon''\succ(\pi/100)I_3\) on its full
three-variable product. The two forms share the \(a\)-coordinate;
adding them keeps all simultaneous motions. For \(x\in P\),
\(\Re g''(x+ih)=\pi\Re\cot\pi(x+ih)>0\).
Thus the additional terms \(-\nu g(a)-\beta g(b)/2\) only
increase the negative real Hessian. Affine subtraction has no
Hessian. All relevant heights stay inside the stated common tubes.
\(\square\)

## 3. An exact three-force budget with stationary height response

Write \(a_*=A+ic\), \(\Delta=H-c\), and
\(\bar a=A+iH=a_*+i\Delta\). Keep every other coordinate
at its reference. For a regular argument \(z\), define
\[
 \mathcal B_g(z;i\Delta)
       =g(z+i\Delta)-g(z)-i\Delta g'(z),\qquad
 \mathcal W=\Re\{\mathcal B_g(s_*+a_*;i\Delta)
       +\mathcal B_g(t_*+a_*;i\Delta)
       -3\mathcal B_g(a_*;i\Delta)\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Also put
\[
 \begin{split}
 L_s&=g'(s_*+\bar a)-g'(s_*+a_*),\quad
 L_t=g'(t_*+\bar a)-g'(t_*+a_*),\\
 L_a&=g'(\bar a)-g'(a_*),\\
 T_\nu&=\mathcal W+(1-\nu)\Re g(\bar a)-2\pi\nu H,\\
 A_s&=\Re L_s,\quad A_t=\Re L_t-2\pi\Delta,\\
 A_{a,\nu}&=\Re\{L_s+L_t-3L_a+(1-\nu)g'(\bar a)\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{10}
\]
For a real interval \(I=[L,R]\), a real force \(d\), and
any real center \(x_*\), define the signed affine support
\[
 \mathfrak s_I(d;x_*)=\max\{d(L-x_*),d(R-x_*)\}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The center need not belong to \(I\); the support is not replaced
by its maximum with zero.

**Proposition 2.** On the whole branch-specific target product (8),
\[
 \sup D_\nu\le T_\nu
 +\mathfrak s_{[1/8,1/2]}(A_s;\Re s_*)
 +\mathfrak s_{I_\nu}(A_{a,\nu};A)
 +\mathfrak s_{[3/20,1/2]}(A_t;\Re t_*).
 \tag{12}
\]
This is a three-scalar upper bound, although all five target
coordinates in (8) remain free.

*Proof.* At the lifted reference the value of \(D_\nu\) is
exactly \(T_\nu\). In fact its only changing primitive terms
are \(g(s_*+a)\), \(g(t_*+a)\), \(-3g(a)\), and
the terminal chirp \(2\pi iat_*\). Their linear height terms
sum to zero by (7). The forced phase adds \(g(\bar a)\)
and removes the selected factorial penalty. This proves the value
formula without evaluating \(S(e_*)\), \(P_\epsilon(a_*)\),
or either eliminated private action separately.

Differentiate the same original expressions before taking real
parts. In the coordinate order \((b,s,a,t,v)\), the exact
real gradient of \(D_\nu\) at this point is
\[
                 (0,A_s,A_{a,\nu},A_t,0).
 \tag{13}
\]
The first zero uses the original incoming marginal in (6).
The last zero follows because the change in \(a\) affects only
\(g(t+a)-g(a)+2\pi iat\) in either terminal phase.
The term \(-2\pi\Delta\) in \(A_t\) is the retained
chirp derivative \(2\pi i(\bar a-a_*)\).
Lemma 1 bounds \(D_\nu\) by this affine tangent. Maximizing
it over (8) yields (12). Even when \(A<.4\), the forced
support is valid: the trial belongs to the containing full-\(P\)
concavity domain, and every segment to its restricted target lies
there. The signed forced support must then be retained. \(\square\)

For the factorial phase, the non-affine fixed-reference height cost
really starts at second order:
\[
 \mathcal W=\int_c^H(H-k)
 \left[-\Re\{g''(s_*+A+ik)+g''(t_*+A+ik)
                                  -3g''(A+ik)\}\right]dk.
 \tag{14}
\]
It is nonnegative by Lemma 1. This does not make it small enough
for a negative bound in (12). It does show precisely which shared
response was lost in the
[horizontal-envelope obstruction](research_mixed_terminal_horizontal_envelope_obstruction.md):
there is no remaining first-order term
\((H-c)\Im(P_\epsilon-g/2)'(a_*)\) in the joint main phase.
The horizontal displacement costs in (12) remain to be paid.

**Corollary 2a.** At the original strong reference images one has
\(A_s>0\) and \(A_t<0\). Thus the first and third supports
in (12) use their right and left endpoints, respectively.

*Proof.* The original ordinary and endpoint image theorems cited in
the [saved reference protocol](research_mixed_terminal_horizontal_envelope_diagnostic.md)
give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \Re a_*,\Re s_*&\in(.24,.36),\\
 t_*&\in(.278,.391)+i(-.072,.021)\quad(\epsilon=0),\\
 t_*&\in(.246,.350)+i(-.108,-.020)\quad(\epsilon=1).
 \end{split}
 \tag{15}
\]
Along \(c\le k\le H\), the real part of \(s_*+A+ik\)
lies in \((.48,.72)\), so its doubled-angle cosine is negative.
For \(t_*+A+ik\) the real ranges are \((.518,.751)\) and
\((.486,.710)\), respectively. The second has negative cosine.
For the first, which slightly exceeds \(.75\), the cosine is
at most \(\sin(.002\pi)<1/100\), while its imaginary part
has absolute value at most \(.18\). Hence
\(e^{-2\pi|\Im(t_*+A+ik)|}>e^{-2}>1/9\), using
\(\pi<22/7\) and \(e<3\).
The elementary identity
\(\Im\cot\pi(x+iy)=-\sinh(2\pi y)/
 (\cosh(2\pi y)-\cos(2\pi x))\)
therefore gives absolute imaginary cotangent less than one for
both summed arguments. Since \(g''=\pi\cot\pi z-\pi i\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 A_s&=\pi\int_c^H[1-\Im\cot\pi(s_*+A+ik)]\,dk>0,\\
 A_t&=-\pi\int_c^H[1+\Im\cot\pi(t_*+A+ik)]\,dk<0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{16}
\]
Here \(H-c>0\). No sign of the remaining force or of the total
budget is inferred. \(\square\)

## 4. A completed reduction of the ordinary labels

**Proposition 3.** Suppose in addition
\(\Im b_*\in[-.078,.021]\). At height \(H=.01\), every
ordinary label can be bounded by the main ordinary maximum, with
the same terminal profile and Fourier alternative. Consequently
the complete source comparison needs at most
\(27\cdot2=54\) labeled terminal/Fourier comparisons in this
subclass, rather than 378.

*Proof.* The three completed pointwise comparisons in Section 2 of
[the four-profile reduction](research_mixed_second_selected_four_profile_reduction.md)
give the ordinary last-row, real left-cut, and real half-row rates
below the main maximum by \(1/50,1/100,1/30\), respectively.
Their proofs use the fixed main trial \(s=.3+i\Im s_*\),
retain the original incoming chirp, and are uniform in both outer
real targets in \(P\). The full last-row maximum dominates each
of its four affine carriers. The other factor, the selected weight,
and the incoming allocation are independent of \(s\), so they
are unchanged in every replacement. Each removed ordinary label
is therefore dominated by the corresponding main-ordinary term.
The branch restriction (5) commutes with this pointwise replacement.
Apply the original finite-profile envelope with one total positive
tolerance. \(\square\)

This proposition applies to all four already certified canonical
inputs \(b_0=.35-.01i\), \(b_1=.30-.052i\). It is not a
claim that every arbitrary-word incoming height lies in the narrower
interval. The incoming last-row comparison alone permits the wider
interval \([-.108,.068]\); the cited left/half-row comparisons
must not be enlarged on that basis. Nor does Proposition 3 prove
any of the 54 remaining signs. In particular the 52 non-main
terminal/Fourier profiles remain after the two main bounds (12).
