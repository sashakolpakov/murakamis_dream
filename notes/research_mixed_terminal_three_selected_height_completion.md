# Selected type-four input heights of the complete terminal-three source

Research proof, 5 November 2026. The whole terminal-three polynomial has
its allocated horizontal supporting-plane upper bound after an actual
type-four incoming coordinate is moved to a selected height. This is an
upper source theorem, not a selected-sector Gaussian or an ordinary
closure theorem. The original imaginary tangent is retained explicitly.
No root or source comparison is recomputed.

## 1. Reference, weight, and statement

Let \(a_*=A+ic\) be the actual type-four incoming leg of a type-three
terminal vertex in a fixed word of length \(m\ge2\). Use its original
action \(P_0\), marginal
\(p=(P_0-g/2)'(a_*)\), and
\[
 B_1(a_*;y)=\Re(P_0-g/2)(a_*)+\Re p\,(y-A)
                         -\kappa(y-A)^2,\qquad \kappa=1/2500000.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(y\in P=[.22,.45]\), \(-.108<c\le-.001\), and
\(a_h=y+ih\), \(0\le h\le7/500\).
The selected halfweight is the literal quantity
\[
 e^{-\pi Nh}|d_N(e^{-2\pi ia_h})|^{1/2},
 \quad\text{with upper rate}\quad
 q_1(h)=\max\{-\Re g(a_h)/2-\pi h,\,0\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Both Fourier alternatives are kept.

**Theorem 1.** For every \(\tau>0\), on a sufficiently small fixed
ordinary-color disk and for all sufficiently large integer levels of
either parity,
\[
 |\mathcal V_{0,N}(w,e^{-2\pi ia_h})|\,
 e^{-\pi Nh}|d_N(e^{-2\pi ia_h})|^{1/2}
 \le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}e^{N(B_1(a_*;y)+\tau)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Each fixed ordinary-color derivative satisfies the same rate on a
smaller disk. All original transform and Newton indices remain in the
whole polynomial.

Relative to the original complex affine plane at the moved height,
the right side retains the fee
\[
 B_1(a_*;y)
 =\{B_1(a_*;y)-\Im p\,(h-c)\}+\Im p\,(h-c).
 \tag{4}
\]
No sign or cancellation of this last term is inferred here. A global
selected-sector estimate must retain the matching adjacent incidence.

## 3. The complex left strip can be compared without a moving saddle

Keep the original joint phase \(\mathcal T(a;t,u)\) and all original
contours of [whole terminal completion](research_mixed_terminal_three_full_completion.md).
Its incoming dependence is \(g(a+t)-g(a)+2\pi iat\).
The actual index heights obey
\[
 -.072<h_t<.021,\qquad .049<h_u<.077.
 \tag{10}
\]
The admitted complex transform rectangle is
\([.15,.5]+i[-.12,.12]\).

First improve the zero-cost transport threshold in
[the selected-row theorem](research_mixed_terminal_selected_height_rows.md).
Let \(v=\Re t\), \(\eta=\Im t\), and
\(A(x,k)=\arg(1-e^{2\pi k-2\pi ix})\).
On the negative incoming path the allocated height derivative is
\[
 F'(k)=\frac32A(y,k)-A(y+v,k+\eta)-2\pi v.
 \tag{11}
\]
If \(y+v\ge.5\), that theorem already proves \(F'(k)<0\).
If \(y+v<.5\), then \(y<.35\) and
\[
 F''(k)=\pi\{\tfrac32\Re\cot\pi(y+ik)
                     -\Re\cot\pi(y+v+i(k+\eta))\}>0.
 \tag{12}
\]
Indeed the first cotangent exceeds \(8/19\), from
\(\cosh(.216\pi)<1.3\), \(\sin(.7\pi)>.8\) and
\(\cos(.7\pi)>-.6\). The real cotangent decreases with the real
coordinate on \([.22,.35]\), since
\(1.3\cos(.44\pi)<1\).
The second is at most \(\cot(.37\pi)<1/2\).
Thus it suffices to bound \(F'(0)\).

For \(\eta\ge0\), \(F'(0)\le\pi(1/4-y/2-v)<0\) when \(v\ge.18\).
For \(\eta<0\), it is largest at \(\eta=-.12\). At that fixed height
\(A_x(x,-.12)>-\pi\), so \(F'(0)\) decreases in both \(y,v\).
At \(y=.22,v=.18\),
\[
 F'(0)=.06\pi-A(.4,-.12)<0.
 \tag{13}
\]
For a wholly elementary bound, \(r=e^{-.24\pi}>7/15\),
\(\sin(.2\pi)>.58\), and \(\cos(.2\pi)<.81\). Therefore
\[
 \tan A(.4,-.12)>\frac{406}{2067}>\frac{39}{200},
 \qquad
 A(.4,-.12)>
 \frac{39}{200}-\frac{(39/200)^3}{3}>.06\pi.
\]
The radial lower bound follows from
\(e^x\le1+x+x^2/[2(1-x/3)]\) at \(x=132/175\);
the trigonometric bounds follow from alternating Taylor bounds
and \(157/50<\pi<22/7\).
Both positive-height selected branches decrease by the selected-row
proof. Hence every admitted complex current with \(v\ge.18\) satisfies
\[
 \Re\mathcal T(a_h;t,u)+q_1(h)
 \le \Re\mathcal T(a_c;t,u)+q_0(c).
 \tag{14}
\]

For the remaining **zero-mode** points with \(.15\le v\le.18\),
\(0\le\Im u\le h_u\) and \(\eta\) between zero and \(h_t\),
one has
\[
                         \Re\mathcal T_t(a_h;t,u)>0.
 \tag{15}
\]
To prove this put \(r=e^{2\pi\eta}\), \(R=e^{-2\pi h}\).
The entire incoming path also obeys the bounds used below.
By (10), \(5/8<r<8/7\), and \(R>10/11\).
The square of the exponential of this derivative is
\[
 \frac{|R-r e^{-2\pi i(y+v)}|^2
       |1-r e^{2\pi\Im u}e^{-2\pi i(v+\Re u)}|^2}
      {|1-r e^{-2\pi iv}|^6}.
\]
Here \(y+v\in[.37,.63]\), \(v+\Re u\in[.25,.58]\).
Use \(\cos2\pi(y+v)<-2/3\),
\(\cos2\pi(v+\Re u)\le0\), and \(\cos2\pi v>2/5\).
The numerator is at least
\(\{100/121+25/64+25/33\}(89/64)\);
the denominator is at most \((341/245)^3\).
The former is strictly larger, by exact rational comparison.
This proves (15). All exponential/trigonometric endpoint bounds
follow from the same elementary Taylor inequalities above.

Thus the main horizontal zero-mode line and its Newton joins at
\(v<.18\) can be moved pointwise to \(v=.18\), keeping both index
heights fixed. This is an upper phase comparison, not a contour
replacement. Apply (14) there. At the original heights the joint
supporting-plane estimate bounds the main plane by \(B_1\); at
\(v=.18\) it has strict index displacement because \(\Re t_*>.278\).
Even the weaker joint Hessian \(\pi/100\) gives loss
\[
                  \frac{\pi}{200}(.278-.18)^2>1/10000.
 \tag{16}
\]

For a Newton join, first use separate height convexity at the
new incoming target. Its endpoints are the main horizontal plane
and the completed real-Newton flattened family. The latter, after
(14), retains its original strict gap. The same reasoning handles
the transform joins at \(v=.15,.5\): the endpoint with transform
height zero is a real-transform anchor and is controlled by
Proposition 1 of the selected-row theorem; the endpoint at \(h_t\)
is the main or Newton-join case just handled. Double joins reduce
by two successive convexity steps. At no step is convexity asserted
along a diagonal change of the two index heights.

These arguments put every constant-mode current below \(B_1\),
with a strict gap on its boundary currents. All variables remain
inside the original interpolation domains; only the incoming height
has changed, and it remains in \([-.12,.12]\).
