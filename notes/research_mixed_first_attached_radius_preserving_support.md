# Neighbor-force supports retaining the actual radius

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The original neighboring
force has simple concavity on each side of its shifted half-period.
Its chord and tangent supports preserve the existing endpoint
minimization while retaining the actual negative neighbor radius.
There are at most three first-ordinary index values and the two
proved following endpoint branches.

The containing-box witness where the earlier global affine
credit is negative is paid by this stronger support, with
margin greater than \(3/20\). No simultaneous canonical
eligibility of that witness is asserted, and no full boundary
sign follows from this local verification.

## 1. Original forces and the exact following endpoints

Keep the actual first-minimum domain and allocations of the
[neighbor-height theorem](research_mixed_first_attached_neighbor_height_credit.md).
Write
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 a=x-i\lambda_a d,\quad b=z-i\lambda_bd,\quad d=x-.1,\quad
 .1\le x\le.22,\quad x\le z\le.5,\\
 0\le\lambda_b\le17/20,\qquad
 h=-\lambda_bd,\quad r_b=e^{2\pi h},\qquad .22\le v\le.4 .
 \end{gathered}                                                \tag{1}
\]
Both first-cap coefficient ranges and the own-four \(m\ge3\)
restriction are unchanged. The first ordinary height has
already been raised to zero by its proved favorable derivative
reduction.

Let \(G_\pm(z,h)\) be the two expressions in
[the following-index elimination](research_mixed_first_attached_following_index_elimination.md),
and put \(G(z,h)=\min\{G_-,G_+\}\).
After that exact minimization, the remaining regular neighbor
force is
\[
 B_h(v)=A(v+z,r_b)-3A(z,r_b)+G(z,h),
 \qquad A(y,r)=\arg(1-re^{-2\pi iy}).
 \tag{2}
\]
Both following branches have been retained; the finite-source
eligibility of the following rectangle remains a hypothesis
of any application.
The joint lower comparison is
\[
                        H_\alpha(e,v)+\lambda_bB_h(v).
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(H_\alpha\) is the original first-coordinate derivative,
with its own radius \(e^{-2\pi\lambda_ad}\) and chirp unchanged.

## 2. Chords and tangents with admissible slopes

Put
\[
 I_h(y)=\Im\cot\pi(y+ih)
       =\frac{-\sinh(2\pi h)}
                    {\cosh(2\pi h)-\cos(2\pi y)}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Throughout \(h\le0\), \(I_h\ge0\), and
\[
 B_h'(v)=\pi\{I_h(v+z)-1\},\qquad
 B_h''(v)=
 \frac{2\pi^2\sinh(2\pi h)\sin(2\pi(v+z))}
      {[\cosh(2\pi h)-\cos(2\pi(v+z))]^2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(B_h\) is concave when \(v+z\le1/2\) and convex
when \(v+z\ge1/2\).
These properties include the affine \(h=0\) case.

If \(z\ge.28\), the whole interval \(v\in[.22,.4]\)
is convex. Its tangent at \(v_0=.22\) gives
\[
 B_h(v)\ge B_h(.22)
             -\pi\{1-I_h(.22+z)\}(v-.22).
 \tag{6}
\]
Since \(.22+z\in[.5,.72]\), its angular cosine is
nonpositive, and
\[
                         0\le I_h(.22+z)<1.
 \tag{7}
\]
The coefficient of \(v\) in \(\lambda_b\) times this
support is therefore \(-\pi\gamma\), with
\(\gamma=\lambda_b[1-I_h(.22+z)]\in[0,17/20]\).
The old endpoint lemma for \(H_\alpha(e,v)-\pi\gamma v\)
applies without change.
Consequently it suffices to compare the support (6)
at \(v=.22,.4\).
The actual \(r_b\) is retained in its value and slope.

If \(.1\le z<.28\), put \(v_*=.5-z\in(.22,.4]\).
The concave part is bounded below by its exact chord:
\[
 B_h(v)\ge
 B_h(.22)-\frac{A(.22+z,r_b)}{.28-z}(v-.22),
                          \qquad .22\le v\le v_*.
 \tag{8}
\]
For \(r_b\le1\) the original radial argument satisfies
\[
        0\le A(.22+z,r_b)\le\pi(.28-z),
 \tag{9}
\]
so its slope also belongs to \([-\pi,0]\).
The convex part has the tangent lower support
\[
 B_h(v)\ge
 B_h(v_*)-\pi(1-q_b)(v-v_*),
                 \qquad v_*\le v\le.4,\qquad
 q_b=\tanh(-\pi h)\in[0,1).
 \tag{10}
\]
Here \(I_h(1/2)=q_b\), and the first argument in
\(B_h(v_*)\) is exactly zero:
\[
                         B_h(v_*)=-3A(z,r_b)+G(z,h).
 \tag{11}
\]
Multiplication by \(\lambda_b\) again gives coefficients
in the old endpoint lemma's interval. Applying that lemma
on each subinterval shows that only
\[
                         v=.22,\quad v_*=.5-z,\quad v=.4
 \tag{12}
\]
are needed for these conservative supports.
At \(z=.1\) the final interval is a singleton.
At \(z=.28\), its coinciding endpoints have the tangent
description (6); there is no division by zero in (8).

The private \(e\)-endpoint argument is unchanged, since
every support above is independent of \(e\).
Use \(e=.27,.49\) for own three and \(e=.27,.4\)
for own four.
These reductions hold at each actual \(x,\lambda_a,\lambda_b\).
The old monotonicity in \(x\) proved for a different affine
credit is not being imported into (6)--(12).

## 3. The earlier negative lower-test witness is paid

Take the same containing-box point used in Section 3 of the
following-index note:
\[
 x=.22,\quad z=.28,\quad
 \lambda_a=0,\quad\lambda_b=3/4,\quad
 e=.49,\quad v=.4.
 \tag{13}
\]
Here \(h=-.09\), \(r_b=e^{-.18\pi}\).
For every permitted following height,
\(h+\zeta\le-.022<0\). Thus the following-index
derivative (5) in the elimination note is positive in \(w\);
its minimum is \(w=.22\).
At that endpoint \(w+z=.5\), so the argument vanishes
at every following height:
\[
                              G(z,h)=.44\pi .
 \tag{14}
\]
In particular the second following branch is accounted for
by monotonicity, rather than discarded.

Put \(q=\tanh(.09\pi)\) and \(c=\cot(.28\pi)\).
The original branch factorization gives
\[
 A(.28,r_b)=.22\pi-\arctan(qc).
 \tag{15}
\]
It follows either by the same logarithmic factorization
as equation (12) of the
[own-four moving comparison](research_mixed_first_four_moving_height_comparison.md),
or by rotating the original factor with its positive-real
last factor. No integer logarithmic lift is introduced.

Equations (6), (14)--(15) imply
\[
 B_h(.4)\ge
        -\frac{2\pi}{5}+3\arctan(qc)+\frac{9\pi}{50}q .
 \tag{16}
\]
The original first derivative in (13) is
\[
 P_0=\log\frac{\cos(.21\pi)\cos(.12\pi)}
                             {\sin^2(.22\pi)}>\frac7{12}.
 \tag{17}
\]
For an exact lower witness, the original rational trigonometric
bounds give
\[
 \cos(.21\pi)>\frac{7899}{10000},\quad
 \cos(.12\pi)>\frac{28447}{30625},\quad
 \sin(.22\pi)<\frac{319}{500}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The first and third are the polynomial witnesses in
Section 3 of the cited own-four comparison; the second
is \(1-(66/175)^2/2\).
The product of the first two lower bounds exceeds
\((9/5)(319/500)^2\) by
\(319848/306250000>0\).
Thus the ratio in (17) exceeds \(9/5\), and
\[
 \log(9/5)>
       2\left(\frac27+\frac{(2/7)^3}{3}\right)
                       =\frac{604}{1029}>\frac7{12}.
 \tag{19}
\]

Also \(q>1/4\) and \(c>3/4\).
Indeed \(.09\pi>27/100\) and
\(\tanh t\ge t-t^3/3\) give the first bound.
For the second use
\(c=\tan(.22\pi)\),
\(.22\pi>33/50\), and
\(\tan u>u+u^3/3\).
Consequently
\[
 \arctan(qc)>\arctan(3/16)>
            \frac3{16}-\frac{(3/16)^3}{3}=\frac{759}{4096}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Combining (16)--(20), the actual regular comparison for
this prescribed \(e,v\), uniformly over the full following
rectangle, is bounded below by
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_0+\frac34B_h(.4)
 &>\frac7{12}-\frac{33}{35}
                       +\frac{6831}{16384}+\frac{81}{800}\\
 &>\frac7{12}-\frac{33}{35}+\frac5{12}+\frac{81}{800}
                      =\frac{887}{5600}>\frac3{20}.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{21}
\]
Only \(\pi<22/7\), \(\pi>3\) and the displayed exact
elementary inequalities are used here.
This is a mathematical comparison at a containing-box point,
not a new numerical or analytic-function evaluation.

## 4. Scope of the improvement

The witness (21) identifies a concrete loss in the global
affine credit: preserving the actual neighbor radius pays
the same regular profile which that lower test could not
certify. It does not establish simultaneous canonical
reference eligibility of (13), nor replace the remaining
uniform comparison by that one point.

Equations (6)--(12), in contrast, hold on the entire actual
first-minimum domain and retain both following branches.
They provide sufficient comparisons at two or three
ordinary index values with no new partition or source
evaluation.
The first-coordinate interval is still present;
the previous \(x=\min(z,.22)\) conclusion applies only
to its separately proved affine lower expression.

The actual coefficient-radius correlations, full following
source eligibility, original middle compensation and
relative pinning fee remain unchanged. True ordinary
last rows and other high physical-tail profiles have
their distinct formulas. Their signs, and the complete
calibrated boundary sign, are not asserted here.
