# Relative Stokes transport of the actual low-wire fold

Private written proof, 17 November 2026.

The finite low-Newton source has the oriented cubic half-period
proved in [F]. Its endpoint is part of the source. We compute its
relative descending cycles, including that endpoint, and their
integer transition matrices. A growing complex overlap identifies
these modes in explicit combinations of the actual finite kernels.
The positive stationary-wire coefficient changes from one to zero
across either of two walls, while all eight original edge shapes
remain positive. This is a local low-kernel assertion; neither the
high radical nor an original closing functional is removed.

## 1. The finite source and its three rotated periods

Fix \(p\) in a compact interval \(P\subset(0,\infty)\). Retain
the actual polynomials, parity cutoff and normalization of [F]:
\[
 \epsilon=N^{-2/3},\quad L=N-1-\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\quad
 w=-2-4p,\quad p'=p^{-1}e^{-\epsilon\zeta},\quad w'=-2-4p',
\]
\[
 {\cal S}_N(p,\zeta)
 =\epsilon\,\frac{K_N^{\rm low}(w,w')}{e_L(w)e_L(w')},
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\pi^2}{3}(2+p+p^{-1})>0.
 \tag{1}
\]
This finite expression is holomorphic on the growing domains used
below. We do not assert it is entire after dividing by the two
displayed polynomials.

Let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\), and orient \(\Gamma_k\) from zero
to infinity along the ray \(\omega^k[0,\infty)\). Set
\[
 J_k(\zeta)=\int_{\Gamma_k}e^{\zeta s-\kappa s^3}\,ds
       =\omega^k I_p(\omega^k\zeta),\qquad
 I_p(\zeta)=\int_0^\infty e^{\zeta s-\kappa s^3}\,ds,
 \quad k\in\mathbb Z/3.
 \tag{2}
\]
Every \(J_k\) is entire and obeys
\[
                 3\kappa J_k''-\zeta J_k=1.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the integral of
\(\partial_s e^{\zeta s-\kappa s^3}\) is \(-1\) on
each of these oriented half-rays. Their differences obey the
homogeneous equation. In particular
\[
 (J_0-J_1)+(J_1-J_2)+(J_2-J_0)=0.
 \tag{4}
\]
The endpoint boundary of every \(\Gamma_k\) is the same
\(-[0]\); it cancels only in differences.

Here and below a cycle ending at infinity may move inside its
specified cubic decay sector. Joining arcs there have vanishing
integrals. The plane has no finite poles, so a relative cycle
with one end at zero is determined by its chosen decay end,
and a homogeneous cycle by its oriented pair of decay ends.
Equivalently, compactify to a disk with three allowed boundary
arcs and retain the interior point zero. The relative first
homology is free of rank three, generated by \(\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1,
\Gamma_2\); its endpoint-free subgroup has rank two. No relation
setting the sum of the three half-rays to zero is imposed.

## 2. Elementary descending-contour identification

Use the principal argument \(-\pi<\theta=\arg\zeta<\pi\),
away from zero and the rays specified below. Write
\[
 s_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\zeta/(3\kappa)},\qquad
 {\cal A}=\frac{2\zeta^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{3\kappa}}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The critical values are \({\cal A},-{\cal A}\), and the
endpoint value is zero. The positive stationary wire of [F]
corresponds to \(s_+\).

We identify the descending contours directly. Rescale
\(t=(3\kappa)^{1/3}s,\ z=\zeta/(3\kappa)^{1/3}\), and put
\[
 f(t)=zt-t^3/3,\quad a=\sqrt z,\quad S=2a^3/3.
 \tag{6}
\]
The two branches through \(a\) of
\[
 S-f(t)=\frac{(t-a)^2(t+2a)}3=u^2,\qquad
 u\in\mathbb R,\qquad t'(0)=a^{-1/2},
 \tag{7}
\]
form the oriented positive-saddle descent contour. The only
other critical value of the polynomial on the left is \(2S\),
attained at \(-a\). For \(0<\theta<\pi\), \(2S\) never lies
on \([0,\infty)\). Thus the branches in (7) continue for every
real \(u\); polynomial properness prevents escape at finite \(u\).
Their two decay ends cannot change on this whole angular interval.

For small positive \(\theta\), the right branch ends at
\(\Gamma_0\). To identify the left branch, let \(\theta\)
decrease toward zero. Near the would-be encounter with \(-a\),
(7) reads
\[
              (t+a)^2=(2S-u^2)/a+\text{higher terms}.
\]
The branch which had \(t+a>0\) passes through the upper half-plane,
since \(2S\) approaches the positive real axis from above.
It ends at \(\Gamma_1\). Hence (7), oriented by increasing
\(u\), is \(\Gamma_0-\Gamma_1\) for every \(0<\theta<\pi\).
Complex conjugation gives \(\Gamma_0-\Gamma_2\) for
\(-\pi<\theta<0\). This also fixes the Gaussian orientation,
rather than choosing its sign after evaluating the integral.

The endpoint descent contour is the inverse branch
\[
                    f(t)=-v,\qquad v\ge0,\quad t(0)=0.
 \tag{8}
\]
Its initial tangent is \(-1/z\). A finite branch collision
can occur only when \(-v=\pm S\), namely on
\(\theta=0,\pm2\pi/3\). For small positive \(\theta\),
the same local square-root test at \(-a\) makes (8) end at
\(\Gamma_1\). For \(\theta\) near \(\pi\), it ends at
\(\Gamma_0\): at negative real \(z\), (8) is simply the
positive real half-line. Therefore its end is \(\Gamma_1\)
through \(0<\theta<2\pi/3\), and \(\Gamma_0\) through
\(2\pi/3<\theta<\pi\). Reflection gives the lower sectors.
At \(2\pi/3\), the endpoint contour meets the positive saddle,
whose value is negative real. This endpoint event must be kept.

The negative-saddle contours follow by the same inverse construction,
or by rotating (7). For example, for \(\theta>0\), replace
\(z\) by \(\omega^2z\) with argument \(\theta-2\pi/3\),
rotate its contour by \(\omega^2\), and reverse orientation.
The positive saddle then becomes \(-a\). Below \(2\pi/3\)
this gives \(\Gamma_1-\Gamma_2\); above it gives
\(\Gamma_0-\Gamma_2\). The orientation has local tangent
\(i a^{-1/2}\). The corresponding lower rotation gives the
last two rows of the table:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{sector}&E\text{ (endpoint)}&A\text{ (positive saddle)}
                                      &B\text{ (negative saddle)}\\ \hline
0<\theta<2\pi/3&J_1&J_0-J_1&J_1-J_2\\
2\pi/3<\theta<\pi&J_0&J_0-J_1&J_0-J_2\\
-2\pi/3<\theta<0&J_2&J_0-J_2&J_1-J_2\\
-\pi<\theta<-2\pi/3&J_0&J_0-J_2&J_1-J_0
\end{array}
 \tag{9}
\]
Entries denote both the oriented cycle and its period.
These are actual descending contours of (6), not labels
assigned solely from a formal asymptotic expansion.

On closed angular subsectors of any row of (9), as
\(R=|\zeta|\to\infty\), their periods have uniform asymptotics
\[
 \begin{split}
 E&=-\zeta^{-1}+O(R^{-4}),\\
 A&=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{(3\kappa\zeta)^{1/4}}
                 e^{\cal A}(1+O(R^{-3/2})),\\
 B&=\frac{i\sqrt\pi}{(3\kappa\zeta)^{1/4}}
                 e^{-{\cal A}}(1+O(R^{-3/2})).
 \end{split}
 \tag{10}
\]
All roots here use the specified principal argument.
For completeness, (7) makes its integral
\(e^S\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-u^2}t'(u)\,du\).
The inverse expansion at \(a\) has
\[
 t'(u)=a^{-1/2}\{1+O(u/a^{3/2})+O(u^2/a^3)\}.
\]
Its first correction is odd and integrates to zero.
On a fixed small fraction of \(|a|^{3/2}\), the holomorphic
inverse expansion has uniform remainders. Outside that interval,
the inverse polynomial has only algebraic growth and its critical
values stay an angular distance from the integration ray;
the factor \(e^{-u^2}\) gives an exponentially small tail.
This proves the second line of (10). The rotations just used
give the third line, including its factor \(i\).
Similarly, (8) gives an \(e^{-v}\) integral. Its inverse at
zero is \(t=-v/z+O(v^3/z^4)\), proving the first line with
the same inverse-polynomial tail bound. These arguments are
uniform for \(p\in P\). No Airy connection formula is imported.

## 3. Exact relative transition matrices

Write the ordered cycle column as \({\bf C}=(E,A,B)^{\mathsf T}\).
Crossing \(2\pi/3\) outward from the upper inner sector gives
\[
 {\bf C}_{\rm out}=M_+{\bf C}_{\rm in},\qquad
 M_+=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Crossing \(-2\pi/3\) outward from the lower inner sector gives
\[
 {\bf C}_{\rm out}=M_-{\bf C}_{\rm in},\qquad
 M_-=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Crossing zero from the lower inner to the upper inner sector gives
\[
 {\bf C}_{\rm upper}=M_0{\bf C}_{\rm lower},\qquad
 M_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&-1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{13}
\]
These follow exactly by subtracting the half-rays in (9).
Reverse crossings use the inverse matrices. A column of
coefficients of a fixed cycle transforms by \(M^{-\mathsf T}\).

In either inner sector, the original half-period is
\[
                         J_0=E+A.
 \tag{14}
\]
In either outer sector it is \(J_0=E\). Its coefficient
column therefore changes from \((1,1,0)^{\mathsf T}\)
to \((1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}\) across either outward wall.
Across zero it remains \((1,1,0)^{\mathsf T}\).
Every endpoint period still has forcing one in (3); every
saddle period has forcing zero. Thus (11)--(13) preserve the
endpoint map, rather than silently replacing the source by a
homogeneous Airy solution.

The coefficient walls here are \(\theta=0,\pm2\pi/3\).
The equal-real-action rays between the endpoint and a saddle,
\(\theta=\pm\pi/3\) in the principal range, are different.
In particular \(A\) is already subdominant to the endpoint
before its coefficient disappears. This agrees with dominant
transport; it is not a loss of a strictly largest contribution.

## 4. A complex overlap for the actual finite kernels

There are constants \(C,B\), uniform for \(p\in P\), such that
for \(1\le R\le(\log N)^{2/3}\) and \(|\zeta|\le R\),
\[
 |{\cal S}_N(p,\zeta)-I_p(\zeta)|
       \le C\epsilon(1+R)^B e^{C R^{3/2}}.
 \tag{15}
\]
We prove this strengthened range directly from the finite ratios
in [F], rather than infer it from compact convergence.
With \(\nu=1\) at even levels and \(1/2\) at odd levels, put
\[
 a_j=\cos^2((j+\nu)\pi/N),\qquad
 b_j=\sin^2((j+\nu+1/2)\pi/N).
\]
The exact ratio and sum are
\[
 R_{N,j}=\frac{e^{\epsilon\zeta}a_j^2}
                  {(1+b_j/p)(1+p e^{\epsilon\zeta}b_j)},
 \qquad
 {\cal S}_N=\epsilon\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}
                              \prod_{j<\ell}R_{N,j}.
 \tag{16}
\]
For the present range the denominator factors have uniformly
positive real coefficients of \(b_j\). Thus, with \(s=\ell\epsilon\),
\[
 \left|\prod_{j<\ell}R_{N,j}\right|
                         \le e^{Rs-cs^3}.
 \tag{17}
\]
For \(s\le N^{1/12}\), the Taylor calculation in [F], now
retaining \(|\zeta|\le R\), gives
\[
 \log\prod_{j<\ell}R_{N,j}
 =\zeta s-\kappa s^3+E_{N,\ell},\qquad
 |E_{N,\ell}|\le C\epsilon(s+s^2+s^5+Rs^3).
 \tag{18}
\]
This error is uniformly \(o(1)\) on that whole truncated range.
Use \(|e^E-1|\le |E|e^{|E|}\), and then
\(Rs\le c_1s^3+C_1R^{3/2}\), choosing \(c_1\) smaller
than the available cubic decay constant. The scaled sum of
the resulting fixed polynomial in \(s,R\) is at most
\(C\epsilon(1+R)^B e^{C R^{3/2}}\).
The elementary Riemann error for
\(e^{\zeta s-\kappa s^3}\) is bounded by \(\epsilon\)
times the integral of the absolute derivative and satisfies
the same estimate. The tails beyond \(N^{1/12}\) in (17)
and in the model are \(O(e^{-cN^{1/4}})\), since \(R\)
is at most logarithmic on this scale. This proves (15).
The same proof works on a small fixed complex neighborhood
of \(P\). Buffered Cauchy estimates therefore give the
corresponding bounds for every fixed \(p,\zeta\) derivative.

Define three actual normalized finite kernels
\[
 J_{k,N}(p,\zeta)=\omega^k{\cal S}_N(p,\omega^k\zeta).
 \tag{19}
\]
Each uses its own literal \(w'_N(p,\omega^k\zeta)\) and
its own exact endpoint polynomial in (1). Apply the table
(9) to these three expressions to define \(E_N,A_N,B_N\).
Because the definitions are linear, (11)--(14) hold
**exactly** for these finite combinations. They are not
claimed to solve the cubic differential equation at finite
\(N\), or to be finite-level thimble integrals.

Now suppose
\[
 R_N=|\zeta_N|\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
                  R_N^{3/2}=o(\log N),
 \tag{20}
\]
and the arguments lie in a closed subsector of one row of
(9). Equations (10),(15) imply
\[
 E_N=E+N^{-2/3+o(1)},\quad
 A_N=A(1+o(1)),\quad B_N=B(1+o(1)).
 \tag{21}
\]
Indeed \(|A|,|B|\ge cR_N^{-1/4}e^{-C R_N^{3/2}}\)
there, whereas the error in (15) is \(N^{-2/3+o(1)}\).
It is therefore smaller than both exponential modes, even
when one is subdominant. The endpoint has size comparable
to \(R_N^{-1}\) and is resolved as well.

This specifies the finite-source meaning of the transition.
For example, above the real axis but below \(2\pi/3\),
\[
 \frac{{\cal S}_N-E}{A}\longrightarrow1;
 \qquad
 \frac{{\cal S}_N-E}{A}\longrightarrow0
             \quad\hbox{above }2\pi/3,
 \tag{22}
\]
where \(E\) is the descending endpoint period of the
respective row. The lower sectors have the same statements.
Equivalently, use the exact finite modes in (19): the
coefficients are exactly one and zero, and (21) identifies
those modes to relative accuracy with the descending periods.
The assertion is thus stronger than a leading algebraic
endpoint estimate. It does not require selecting an
exponentially small remainder by an unspecified summation
convention: the three oriented periods (2) specify it.

## 5. Original stationary normalization and positive shapes

The saddle mode keeps the normalization of [F]. Put
\[
 C_0=2+p+p^{-1},\qquad
 q=\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{-\epsilon\zeta}}{(1+p)(1+p')}},
 \quad
 x_*=\frac12-\frac1\pi\arctan q,
 \tag{23}
\]
using the branch continued from positive \(\zeta\).
Continue the original phase from that same branch by
\[
 \Delta F=\int_{1/2}^{x_*}
 \Log\frac{(p+\cos^2\pi x)(p'+\cos^2\pi x)}
                    {\sin^4\pi x}\,dx,\qquad
 H_e=2\pi(1+p)(1+p')q(1+q^2)^2 .
 \tag{24}
\]
The arguments in this local integral stay near nonzero
positive numbers, so its logarithm is fixed without
resetting the source constant. The estimates in [F](35)
continue holomorphically and give
\[
 N\Delta F={\cal A}+O(\epsilon R^{5/2}),\qquad
 H_e=2\pi N^{-1/3}\sqrt{C_0\zeta}
                                  (1+O(\epsilon R)).
 \tag{25}
\]
Consequently, in (20), the original saddle mode is
\[
 \frac{e_L(w)e_L(w')}{\epsilon}\,A_N
 =e_L(w)e_L(w')\,e^{N\Delta F}
             \sqrt{\frac{2\pi N}{H_e}}\,(1+o(1)).
 \tag{26}
\]
The square root is the positive Gaussian normalization
continued from real positive detuning. Formula (26) holds
also where this mode is subdominant or absent from the
decomposition of the particular source \({\cal S}_N\).

The walls in (11),(12) lie inside the positive edge domain
at these actual detunings. Set
\[
 t=-i\operatorname{arcosh}(1+2p),\qquad
 u=-i\operatorname{arcosh}(1+2p'),\qquad
 a=\tan(t/2),\quad c=\tan(u/2).
 \tag{27}
\]
Here \(a\) is purely imaginary and \(\Re c=O(\epsilon R)\).
Uniformly for \(|\arg\zeta|\le\pi-\delta\), \(R\ge1\),
\[
 q=\sqrt{\epsilon\zeta/C_0}\,(1+O(\epsilon R)),
 \qquad
 \Re q\ge c_\delta\sqrt{\epsilon R}
       >\sqrt{(\Re a)^2+(\Re c)^2}
 \tag{28}
\]
for sufficiently small \(\epsilon R\). By the exact
original-shape criterion [P](6), all eight edge tetrahedra
are positive and regular. This includes neighborhoods
on both sides of \(\arg\zeta=\pm2\pi/3\). The stationary
wire (23) and Hessian (24) are those of [U](25)--(28).
Thus positivity of these local shapes does not fix the
positive-saddle coefficient of this actual low half-cycle.
The detunings are not asserted to solve any filling equations.

## 6. Scope and direct sources

The generic dominant-saddle transport note already explains
how coefficients of lower saddles can change, and gives a
homogeneous cubic example. The result here is the explicit
endpoint-relative matrix for the half-period selected by the
original finite low-Newton sum, together with an error range
that resolves both its exponential modes. No homogeneous
replacement of the forcing in (3) is used.

The high radical, its original cap/value/normal contractions,
angular horizontal and vertical currents, aliases and final
co-source have not been estimated here. In particular a zero
coefficient in (22) is not a zero geometric coefficient of a
closed knot source. Unscaled physical derivatives retain the
factors \(N^{2/3}\) and the endpoint-polynomial derivatives
specified in [F](27); (15) does not delete them or bound an
arbitrarily growing attachment.
