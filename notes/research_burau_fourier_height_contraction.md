# Fourier coordinates with a height increasing under transport

22 November 2026. A finite Fourier transform puts the negative
crossing into an orthonormal basis in which its nilpotent generator
strictly increases a height. Together with the positive crossing
basis, this makes the full finite Burau operator strictly increase
total height.

An explicit diagonal weight then makes that operator a contraction.
The scalar source has a quadratic minimization with Euclidean Hessian
condition number at most ten. The weight has exponential size; the
geometric source estimate is still required.

## 1. The original source and inner product

Fix a braid on \(b\ge2\) strands with \(k\ge1\) crossing occurrences
and knot closure. Use the reversed, sign-mirrored auxiliary word of
the [marked source bridge](research_burau_cyclic_marked_source_bridge.md).
At level \(N\ge2\), put
\[
 d=N-1,\qquad Q=e^{2\pi i/N},\qquad
 L=kd+1,\qquad \zeta=Q^{(b-1+w)/2},
\]
where \(w\) is the physical writhe. The
[orthogonal row model](research_burau_orthogonal_marked_row_model.md)
gives the exact source
\[
 K_N=\zeta\,\mathfrak e^*(I-\widehat C)^{-1}H,
 \qquad
 \widehat C^L=0,\quad
 \|\mathfrak e\|=1,\quad\|H\|=N^k.
 \tag{1}
\]
All spaces below carry that model's Euclidean inner product.
In particular a Fourier change of basis is unitary in the specified
physical observation space, rather than in a redundant polynomial
expansion.

For a positive auxiliary occurrence its orthonormal rows \(f_{r,l}\)
already satisfy
\[
 f_{r,l}a_+=\rho_l f_{r,l+1},\quad
 f_{r,d}a_+=0,\quad
 f_{r,l}b_+=Q^{-2r}f_{r,l},\quad
 f_{r,l}c_+=Q^l f_{r+1,l}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here \(r\) is cyclic, \(0\le l\le d\), and \(0<\rho_l\le2\).
Thus \(l\) is a height increased by \(a_+\) and preserved by
\(b_+,c_+\).

## 2. A Fourier transform for the negative occurrence

Write \(z=Q^{-1}\). Its orthonormal point rows in the original
negative model are
\[
 \xi_{r,h}f=f(zQ^{-r},zQ^h,Q^r),
 \qquad r,h\in\mathbb Z/N.
\]
Their actual right actions are
\[
 \xi_{r,h}a_-=\xi_{r+1,h+1}-Q^{r+1}\xi_{r+1,h},\quad
 \xi_{r,h}b_-=Q^{2r}\xi_{r,h},\quad
 \xi_{r,h}c_-=Q^{1-h}\xi_{r+1,h}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Apply a normalized Fourier transform only in \(h\), and then relabel:
\[
 f^-_{r,p}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{h=0}^{N-1}Q^{ph}\xi_{r,h},
 \qquad
 F_{p,j}=f^-_{j-p,p},\qquad 0\le j\le d.
 \tag{4}
\]
The labels \(p,r\) are cyclic. The representative of \(j=r+p\)
is always chosen in \(0,\ldots,d\). This is a unitary basis at
every level, with no inverse of two or primality hypothesis.

**Lemma 1.** In this basis the actions are
\[
 \begin{split}
 F_{p,j}a_-&=Q^{-p}(1-Q^{j+1})F_{p,j+1}\quad(j<d),
       & F_{p,d}a_-&=0,\\
 F_{p,j}b_-&=Q^{2(j-p)}F_{p,j},
       & F_{p,j}c_-&=QF_{p-1,j}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{5}
\]
The marked initial row and terminal column are
\[
 \xi_{0,0}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}F_{p,p},
 \qquad
 H^-_{\mathrm{new}}(p,j)=\sqrt N\,\delta_{p,0}.
 \tag{6}
\]

**Proof.** Formula (3) first gives
\[
 f^-_{r,p}a_-=(Q^{-p}-Q^{r+1})f^-_{r+1,p},\quad
 f^-_{r,p}b_-=Q^{2r}f^-_{r,p},\quad
 f^-_{r,p}c_-=Qf^-_{r+1,p-1}.
\]
Substitute \(j=r+p\). The first coefficient is
\(Q^{-p}(1-Q^{j+1})\); at \(j=d\) it is zero, so it never
crosses the height boundary. The last action preserves \(j\).
Fourier inversion at \(r=h=0\) proves the initial row in (6).
Finally each original point row takes the value one on the constant
function, so its Fourier transform has value \(\sqrt N\delta_{p,0}\).
\(\square\)

The negative initial row is therefore a specified diagonal sum,
not the single row \(F_{0,0}\). Its coefficients and the endpoint
in (6) are part of the source identity.

## 3. An orthogonal grading of the full operator

Let \(U\) be the tensor product of (4) at all negative occurrences
and the identity at positive occurrences. Set
\[
 C=U\widehat C U^*,\qquad E=U\mathfrak e,\qquad H_1=UH.
\]
Then exactly
\[
 K_N=\zeta E^*(I-C)^{-1}H_1,\qquad
 \|E\|=1,\quad\|H_1\|=N^k.
 \tag{7}
\]
On each tensor basis row define its total height \(s\) as the sum
of the positive \(l\)'s and negative \(j\)'s. Thus \(0\le s\le kd\).
Both signs' \(a\)-generators increase \(s\) by one; every \(b,c\)
preserves it. All elements of their generated algebra consequently
preserve or increase height.

The bridge proves that its entire operator lies in the two-sided
ideal generated by the \(a\)'s. Compression and the unitary change
of basis preserve this assertion. It follows that \(C\) strictly
increases height. In a row evolution \(v\mapsto vC\), the only
possible blocks are
\[
 C_{s,t}\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1\le t-s\le k(b-1).
 \tag{8}
\]
For the upper bound, each entry of the product of \(k\) local Burau
blocks has letter degree at most \(k\). A term in a principal minor
of size \(j\le b-1\) has degree at most \(kj\). Only \(a\)'s change
height, proving the bound. Thus the exact row solve
\[
 v=E^*+vC,\qquad
 v_t=E_t^*+\sum_{s<t}v_s C_{s,t}
 \tag{9}
\]
is causal in total height, with the bounded width (8). The height
layers still have large internal dimensions.

## 4. A specified diagonal contraction

We first need a bound independent of the level. Define
\[
 B_{b,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}\binom{b-1}{j}\,j!\,4^{kj}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Each local generator has norm at most \(2,1,1\), respectively.
An embedded local Burau block has norm at most four; hence every
entry of the full Burau product has norm at most \(4^k\).
The column-ordered quantum minor of size \(j\) has \(j!\) terms,
each a product of \(j\) such entries. Its scalar coefficients have
modulus one. The defining sum of principal minors therefore gives
\(\|C\|\le B_{b,k}\).

Let \(\mathcal H\) be the diagonal total-height matrix. Decompose
\(C=\sum_{m\ge1}C_m\) by its height increase \(m\). Each term obeys
\[
 C_m=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
       e^{im\theta}e^{i\theta\mathcal H}C
                         e^{-i\theta\mathcal H}\,d\theta,
 \qquad \|C_m\|\le B_{b,k}.
 \tag{11}
\]
This is a finite-dimensional Fourier coefficient of unitary
conjugations, so no entrywise absolute-value estimate is needed.

Put
\[
 R=2B_{b,k}+1,\qquad D=R^{\mathcal H},\qquad C_R=DCD^{-1}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
 C_R=\sum_{m\ge1}R^{-m}C_m,\qquad
 \|C_R\|\le B_{b,k}\sum_{m\ge1}R^{-m}=\tfrac12.
 \tag{13}
\]
The exact source becomes
\[
 K_N=\zeta u^*(I-C_R)^{-1}v,\qquad
 u=D^{-1}E,\quad v=DH_1.
 \tag{14}
\]
Both vectors are explicit. The bounds
\[
 \|u\|\le1,\qquad
 N^k\le\|v\|\le R^{kd}N^k,\qquad
 \operatorname{cond}(D)=R^{kd}
 \tag{15}
\]
show where an exponential cost remains. In particular (13) does
not imply a polynomial bound on the original source.

The norms have closed formulas. If \(k_-\) is the number of negative
auxiliary occurrences, then
\[
 \|v\|^2=\left(N\sum_{j=0}^{d}R^{2j}\right)^k,\qquad
 \|u\|^2=\left(\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{d}R^{-2j}\right)^{k_-},
 \qquad u^*v=1.
 \tag{15a}
\]
Indeed each positive terminal has squared mass \(N\) at every
height, by the exact Parseval identity of the row model. The negative
terminal in (6) has the same height masses. Each negative initial
row has squared mass \(1/N\) at every height; each positive initial
row is at height zero. Tensor products give both norm formulas.
Finally \(u^*v=E^*H_1=\mathfrak e^*H=1\), since the original marked
evaluation takes value one on the constant function.

## 5. A quadratic with ordinary Euclidean conditioning

For \(\tau\ge1\), define
\[
 A=I-C_R^*,\qquad \beta=4\tau\|v\|^2,\qquad
 J_\tau(x)=\beta\|Ax-u\|^2+|v^*x|^2,\qquad
 \mu_\tau=\min_xJ_\tau(x).
 \tag{16}
\]
All norms in this section are unweighted Euclidean norms. There is
no history matrix in (16).

**Proposition 2.** At every level,
\[
 \mu_\tau=\frac{|K_N|^2}{1+\eta_\tau},\qquad
 \eta_\tau=\frac{\|A^{-*}v\|^2}{\beta}\le\tau^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mu_\tau\le |K_N|^2\le(1+\tau^{-1})\mu_\tau.
 \tag{17}
\]
The minimum is unique. Its real Hessian has condition number at most
\(9+1/\tau\le10\), in the unweighted coordinates of (16).

**Proof.** From (13),
\[
 \tfrac12\le\sigma_{\min}(A)\le\|A\|\le\tfrac32,\qquad
 \|A^{-1}\|\le2.
\]
Thus the stated bound on \(\eta_\tau\) follows from (16).
Substitute \(r=Ax-u\) into the objective. With
\(s=v^*A^{-1}u=\zeta\overline{K_N}\), completing its quadratic
square gives \(\mu_\tau=|s|^2/(1+\eta_\tau)\).

The complex quadratic coefficient matrix and its bounds are
\[
 P=\beta A^*A+vv^*,\qquad
 \tfrac\beta4 I\preceq P
       \preceq\frac{\beta}{4}(9+1/\tau)I.
 \tag{18}
\]
The real Hessian is twice the real representation of \(P\).
It has the same condition number. \(\square\)

In particular \(\tau=N\) gives the finite relative factor \(1+1/N\).
The minimizer solves the single positive definite linear system
\[
 Px_*=\beta A^*u.
\]
For an arbitrary trial \(x\), define its complex half-gradient
\[
 g(x)=\beta A^*(Ax-u)+v(v^*x).
\]
Exact quadratic completion and (18) give
\[
 \max\{0,J_\tau(x)-4\|g(x)\|^2/\beta\}
       \le |K_N|^2\le(1+\tau^{-1})J_\tau(x).
 \tag{19}
\]
Indeed \(J_\tau(x)-\mu_\tau=g(x)^*P^{-1}g(x)\le4\|g(x)\|^2/\beta\).
This certificate uses no inverse history metric.

## 6. Consequences and remaining estimate

The Fourier transform gives an exact orthogonal description of
transport by increasing height, with the original initial and final
rows retained. The diagonal weight turns its finite Green solve into
a contraction. Its coefficients, weight, endpoints and quadratic
certificate are all specified from the fixed word.

The contraction is obtained with the exponentially conditioned
diagonal \(D\). Its cost appears in \(v\) and \(\beta\). Good
Euclidean conditioning of the quadratic does not give a relative
source error bound from a fixed absolute state error. A state error
of size \(\varepsilon\) can still change the closing by
\(\|v\|\varepsilon\). To compare with \(|K_N|\), that error must be
controlled at the corresponding relative scale.

The transport coefficients also retain their complex phases. For
example the two positive-register paths \(a_+c_+\) and \(c_+a_+\)
have ratio \(Q\) wherever both are nonzero. A scalar diagonal gauge
does not change a ratio between paths with the same endpoints.
The increasing height is therefore not an assertion of positive
path weights or of absence of cancellation.

Nor does contraction alone justify shortening the finite source.
For example its generic observed Neumann-tail bound after \(s\)
terms is \(2^{1-s}\|u\|\|v\|\). The exponential endpoint norm in
(15a) must still be compared with the desired source scale.

The construction supplies neither a geometric trial with the volume
energy rate nor a positive lower certificate at that rate after
arbitrary short partial fillings. The finite-exception theorem is
still open. The purpose of (16)--(19) is to make the required estimate
available in explicit Euclidean coordinates without a nonlocal
history metric.
