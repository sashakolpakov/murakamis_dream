\(t=x-7/100\) connects the early taper to the original paid face
\(t=.15\) at \(x=.22\). Its complete first-cap envelope, multiplied
by each original central-private terminal boundary profile,

boundary at the original geometric action. The other horizontal
and physical sectors remain separate. No numerical evaluation is used.

## 2. The real transform force

**Lemma 1.** Uniformly on (1), for every \(b\in E_c\),
\[
 \Re\Phi_t>
 \begin{cases}
   \log3>1,&.1\le x\le.16,\\
   \log(75/32)>\log2>2/3,&.16\le x\le.22 .
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{4}
\]

*Proof.* The original chirp gives exactly
\[
 e^{\Re\Phi_t}
 =\frac{|R-e^{-2\pi i(x+t)}|\,|1-e^{-2\pi i(b+t)}|}
             {(2\sin\pi t)^3},\qquad R=e^{-2\pi h}>4/5.          \tag{5}
\]
The last bound follows from \(h\le.021\) and
\(e^{-.042\pi}>1-.042\pi>4/5\).
If \(x\le.16\), then \(t\le.09\) and
\(.13\le x+t\le.25\). Minimizing the first modulus over nonnegative
radii gives \(\sin(2\pi(x+t))\ge\sin(.26\pi)>7/10\).
For a witness, \(.26\pi>.81\) and
\(\sin(.81)>.81-.81^3/6>7/10\).
The real part of \(b+t\) is in \([.30,.58]\), so its cosine is
negative and the second modulus exceeds one. Also
\(2\sin(.09\pi)<3/5\). Thus (5) exceeds \(175/54>3\).
The elementary exponential series gives \(e<3\).

If \(x\ge.16\), then \(.25\le x+t\le.37\).
The first modulus has square greater than
\(1+(4/5)^2>(5/4)^2\).
Now \(.36\le\Re(b+t)\le.64\), whose angular cosine is below
\(-3/5\), and \(e^{2\pi\Im b}>7/10\). The second modulus
has square greater than \(1+49/100+21/25>(3/2)^2\).
The old witness \(2\sin(.15\pi)<23/25\) has cube less than \(4/5\).
Thus (5) exceeds \(75/32\). Finally
\(\log2=2\operatorname{atanh}(1/3)>2/3\).
The radius and cosine guards are the elementary ones in
[the right moving-cut proof](research_mixed_three_four_moving_transform_cut.md).
\(\square\)

## 3. The coupled moving derivative is positive

**Lemma 2.** For \(e=u+ik\) with
\(.27\le u\le.49\), \(-.019\le k\le.037\), the right side
of (3) is greater than \(1/20\). The same holds with the cap
true-index alternative in place of \(\Re\Psi_0\).

*Regular profiles.* The exact \(k\)-monotonicity and private
endpoint reduction in Sections 2--4 of
[the private-height transport](research_mixed_three_four_private_height_left_transport.md)
remain valid at real \(t\in[.03,.15]\): both depend only on \(e+a\).
Minimize at \(k=-.019\), \(u=.27,.49\). Keep
\[
 3/5<r=e^{2\pi h}<23/20,\quad -7/40<\lambda<3/5,\quad
 \lambda\ge0\Rightarrow r\le1,\quad
 \lambda\le0\Rightarrow r\ge1.                                \tag{6}
\]
If \(\lambda\ge0\), the first squared modulus ratio in \(D\)
exceeds \(4/3\), by the same explicit quadratic witness (11) of
that proof. Its hypotheses
\(\cos2\pi(u+x)<-6/25\), \(e^{2\pi k}>22/25\), and
\(\cos2\pi x>9/50\) are unchanged.
The second ratio exceeds one, because
\(0<x<x+t<1/2\) and its two factors have the same radius.
Hence \(\Re D>\tfrac12\log(4/3)>1/7\).

The original argument identity at \(k=0\) gives
\(\Im D_0\ge\pi(t-u)\).
At \(u=.49\), the negative \(k\) only increases this imaginary
part. At \(u=.27\), it decreases it by less than \(.019\pi\).
Since \(.27+.019<.49\), this proves
\[
                  \mathcal H>1/7-(3/5)\pi(.49-t).              \tag{7}
\]
If \(\lambda\le0\), the opposite argument bound and the same
private-height correction give
\(\Im D<\pi(.15-.27+.019)<0\).
The first ratio is now greater than \(13/10\), and the second
still exceeds one. Therefore \(\mathcal H>0\) in this case.

For \(x\le.16\), (7) implies
\(\mathcal H>-69\pi/250>-759/875\).
For \(x\ge.16\), it implies \(\mathcal H>1/7-6\pi/25\).
Adding (4), the respective strict lower bounds are
\[
           116/875>1/20,\qquad
           2/3+1/7-132/175=29/525>1/20.                        \tag{8}
\]

*True-index alternative.* Keep exactly
\(E_{\rm end}(h)=3/5+2\pi h_++(\log4)/100\).
At fixed real \(t\), set
\(D_{\rm ord}=g'(a+t)-g'(a)+2\pi it\).
Its real part is positive by the same-radius cosine comparison.
If \(c\le0\), then \(\lambda\ge0\),
\(\Im D_{\rm ord}\ge\pi t>0\), and \(E_{\rm end}'=0\).
If \(c\ge0\), then \(\lambda\le0\),
\(\Im D_{\rm ord}\le\pi t\), while
\(E_{\rm end}'=-2\pi\lambda\).
Thus \(\lambda(\Im D_{\rm ord}-2\pi)\ge0\).
The limiting case \(c=0\) agrees. The true-end alternative plus
the conditional incoming phase is therefore nondecreasing at
fixed \(t\). Adding (4) proves the assertion. \(\square\)
