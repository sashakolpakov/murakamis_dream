# A paid shallow taper at the compensated left boundary

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The compensated left boundary
comparison remains nonpositive on a fixed open enlargement of its
unselected plateau, using the actual hybrid heights. Thus finitely
many physical boundary coordinates may cross either plateau endpoint
and reset their neighbors' heights slightly. This conclusion uses
the complete finite prefix polynomial and the already proved relative
pinning fee. It does not transport a bare cap plane at zero cost.

The enlargement has a fixed positive width before the level tends
to infinity. No numerical value for that width, new source evaluation,
or deeper taper comparison is asserted.

## 1. The exact left component and its plateau reserve

Use either an actual ordinary-ended reference, or the actual
Fourier-ended reference of
[the Fourier boundary-window reduction](research_mixed_fourier_ended_boundary_windows.md).
Fix its left cut \(p\), a permitted good pinning block, and its
first pinned coordinate \(\ell\). The original three consecutive
pins and all protected strips remain in place. All variables in this
note are unselected. Set
\[
 P=[.22,.45],\qquad r_j=a_j+ic_j,\qquad
 \kappa_* =1/2500000,\qquad
 a_d=1000(99/101)^{704}<1/1296.
 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(u\) denote the outer Dirichlet coordinate of the left good
block. It lies in the safe coherent extension, after the literal
prefix and before \(\ell\), as proved in Section 4 of
[contracted boundary collars](research_mixed_contracted_boundary_collar_completion.md).
The left comparison to be bounded is exactly
\[
 \mathcal C_{L,N}=B_{L,N}-D_L+a_d(x_u-a_u)^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
This is not the separately closed relaxation \(B_{L,N}\).

The literal prefix modulus is
\[
 L_N=|U_{\epsilon_1,N}(w,X_1)|
       \prod_{j=2}^{p-1}|C_{\epsilon_j,N}(X_{j-1},X_j)|
       \prod_{j=1}^{p-2}|d_N(X_j)|^{\epsilon_j}
                        |d_N(X_{p-1})|^{\epsilon_{p-1}/2}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Suppose first that every free real coordinate belongs to \(P\),
at its reference height. The complete first-cap and ordinary-factor
planes in [the unselected plateau theorem](research_mixed_full_unselected_plateau.md),
equation (5), apply to (3). For Fourier-ended references their
first-cap source eligibility is the one proved in
[the coherent main-sheet theorem](research_mixed_fourier_prefix_coherent_main_sheet.md),
Section 1; no periodic reference is substituted.

The six-edge closing terms cancel exactly. Since \(z_\ell=r_\ell\),
the identity from the finite-boundary proof, equation (16a), is
\[
 B_{L,N}-D_L
 =N^{-1}\log L_N-M_L+
       \sum_{j=p}^{\ell}\Re\{
       \phi_j(z_{j-1},z_j)-\phi_j(r_{j-1},r_j)\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
All original internal complex marginals cancel. The literal prefix's
cut marginal cancels the first term of the extension; the last cut
has zero displacement. Only after these cancellations is (4) used.

Each safe coherent extension edge has negative real Hessian at least
\(I/5\) on this plateau. It therefore contributes at least
\(1/10\) of the square displacement of each endpoint. The
edge identified in the contracted-collar proof pays the fee in (2),
and still retains more than \(\kappa_*\) at its fee endpoint,
because \(1/10-a_d>\kappa_*\). The other complete prefix
quadratics and coherent extension costs are not discarded. Thus,
for every aggregate source tolerance \(\tau>0\),
\[
 \mathcal C_{L,N}\le
       -\kappa_*\sum_{j<\ell}(x_j-a_j)^2
                    +\tau+O(\log N/N)
                  \qquad(\mathbf x\in P^{\ell-1}).
 \tag{5}
\]
The constants may depend on the fixed boundary width and a compact
admissible reference family, but not on the level. The original
finite-window reduction has finitely many such bounded-width type
families. The same source tolerances can be chosen uniformly on a
compact family where the original source comparisons apply.

Every reference real coordinate appearing here has distance greater
than
\[
                         r_0=17/1000
 \tag{6}
\]
from \(\partial P\). For the attached endpoint this follows
from its real image in \((.246,.391)\); for the ordinary legs
use \((.267,.433)\) for type three and \((.24,.36)\)
for type four. These are actual full-input images, not the narrower
cyclic rectangles. The Fourier-ended version is also recorded in
the coherent main-sheet theorem.

## 2. Continue the complete squared prefix, including every zero

The square of (3) is the modulus of a holomorphic Laurent polynomial:
\[
 \mathcal P_N=
 U_{\epsilon_1,N}(w,X_1)^2
 \prod_{j=2}^{p-1}C_{\epsilon_j,N}(X_{j-1},X_j)^2
 \prod_{j=1}^{p-2}d_N(X_j)^{2\epsilon_j}
                        d_N(X_{p-1})^{\epsilon_{p-1}}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Every separate exponent band is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(N\).
The exact kernel bands are \([-N+1,0]\) in the incoming
variable and \([0,N-1]\) in the outgoing variable, and both
\(U\) and \(d_N\) have degree less than \(N\).
There is no choice of a holomorphic square root: simply
\(|\mathcal P_N|=L_N^2\), including at zeros.

Apply [horizontal complex extension](research_mixed_horizontal_complex_extension.md),
Theorem 1, to (7), with the continuous upper rate obtained by
summing the original prefix planes. For every \(\zeta>0\),
there is a fixed \(\delta>0\) such that moving its logarithmic
arguments at most \(\delta\) away from a point of the reference-height
plateau raises the proved rate of \(L_N\) by at most \(\zeta\),
apart from an arbitrarily small final source tolerance and a polynomial
factor. Use rate tolerance \(2\zeta\) for (7), then take moduli
and square roots. This is a bound for the whole finite product, not
continuity of its value at one level.

The theorem is applicable uniformly with the cut coordinate held
fixed in \(P\), with all original reference parameters, and with
the ordinary-color parameters for which the prefix plane is uniform.
It needs no new primitive evaluation or nonvanishing assumption.
Its neighborhood includes the real endpoints of \(P\).

## 3. The actual shallow hybrid taper

Allow only the first physical boundary coordinates to leave the
plateau:
\[
 x_j\in[.22-\varepsilon,.45+\varepsilon]\quad(j\le p-3),
 \qquad x_j\in P\quad(p-2\le j<\ell),
 \tag{8}
\]
and keep all selections zero. The last two prefix coordinates and
the whole synthetic extension are protected. Let
\(\bar x_j=\operatorname{clamp}_P x_j\). On this small range
the original unselected hybrid height is exactly
\[
 h_j(\mathbf x)=c_jR(x_j)
      \min\{L(x_{j-1}),L(x_j),L(x_{j+1})\},
 \quad L(x)=\min\{1,(x-.1)/.12\},
 \tag{9}
\]
with the missing first neighbor assigned cutoff one. The upper taper
is \(R(x)=1\) up to \(.45\), then \(400(.5-x)^2\).
The original near-zero repair is not encountered.

For \(0<\varepsilon<.01\), all factors in (9) belong to
\([0,1]\), and
\[
 1-R(x_j)\le40\varepsilon,\qquad
 1-\min L\le(25/3)\varepsilon.
 \tag{10}
\]
Since \(|c_j|<.12\), the product rule for these two deficits
gives
\[
 |h_j(\mathbf x)-c_j|<\frac{29}{5}\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 |x_j+ih_j(\mathbf x)-(\bar x_j+ic_j)|<7\varepsilon.
 \tag{11}
\]
These estimates allow simultaneous lower and upper crossings, not
only one changed coordinate. At the cut \(p-1\) and throughout
the synthetic extension all neighborhoods in (9) lie in \(P\).
Their heights are therefore unchanged. Only arguments of the literal
prefix polynomial (7) move. In particular no raw/coherent switch in
\(D_L\) is being replaced by a coherent phase outside its domain.

Choose the extension rate loss
\(\zeta<\kappa_*r_0^2/4\), and then choose a fixed
\(\varepsilon>0\) small enough that \(7\varepsilon<\delta\)
for the neighborhood in Section 2. If some coordinate in (8) lies
outside \(P\), its clamped value is an endpoint of \(P\), so
\[
                    \sum_{j<\ell}(\bar x_j-a_j)^2>r_0^2.
 \tag{12}
\]
The extension and pinning fee in (4) are identical at the actual
point and its clamped comparison point. Apply the complete polynomial
bound only to \(L_N\), and retain all plateau quadratics in (5).
For any final tolerance \(\tau>0\), this proves
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal C_{L,N}\le
       -\frac{\kappa_*}{2}
           \sum_{j<\ell}(\bar x_j-a_j)^2
                    +\tau+O(\log N/N)
                    \quad\hbox{throughout (8).}\quad}
 \tag{13}
\]
When every coordinate belongs to \(P\), use (5) directly rather
than spend a fixed continuation loss near the saddle. Otherwise
(12) pays the loss \(\zeta\), with room to spare. This distinction
is essential to a statement nonpositive at the reference.

The new nonplateau part of (8) consequently has common raw reserve
\(\kappa_*r_0^2/2\). After one smaller total tolerance it retains,
for example, the positive finite rate reserve
\[
                \frac{\kappa_*r_0^2}{4}
                         =\frac{289}{10^{13}}.
 \tag{14}
\]
All bounds retain the literal source, both parities, and its existing
ordinary-color scope. Fixed color jets follow on smaller complex
color neighborhoods, as in the starting source bounds. No derivative
of a modulus or division by \(d_N\) is used.

## 4. What has and has not been paid

Equation (13) is an actual compensated boundary estimate on an open
enlargement of the unselected plateau. It is not merely a new test
whose sign remains unknown. It includes shallow physical taper states
at several left-boundary positions, their neighbor-height changes,
the original closing terms and the relative pinning fee. The cap
need not have a favorable independent vertical derivative.

The width \(\varepsilon\) is fixed and positive, but not quantified
by a saved numerical certificate. The proof supplies no comparison
for deeper early targets, a seam entering this short physical prefix,
arbitrary right targets, or unproved boundary selection patterns.
Nor may the protected cut strip be removed: its purpose is to keep
the synthetic extension in the already proved coherent plateau.
Those remaining states still require a genuine joint cap and
ordinary-source comparison. The new right Fourier boundary cost is
independent of this left conclusion and remains as in the boundary
reduction. No whole-source or knot-class enlargement is inferred.
