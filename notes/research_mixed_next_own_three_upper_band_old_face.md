# An enlarged own-three physical band ending at the old face

Private research proof, 8 November 2026. The moving physical-row
comparison can stop at the already calibrated shared face
\(q=11/50\), before the next row reaches one half. A correlated
outgoing-modulus bound makes the entire path increasing when
the reflected index satisfies \(v-q\ge1/5\).

The complete first factor and every next physical row in the
stated band are retained. The result enlarges a positive
finite-row estimate; it makes no inference about cancellation
in the remaining higher rows. No numerical evaluation or
modification of a frozen input is used.

\tag{1}

\quad R=|Y|\in(13/25,1),\quad \rho=|Z|<L:=43/28.

\(P_j=(r;r)_j\), and \(p_j(W)=(rW;r)_j\).

\[
C_{1,N}(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{D}
               \frac{X^{-i}p_i(X)p_i(Y)}{P_i^3}.

Define the literal positive next upper band

\mathcal U_{0,N}^{\rm band}(Y,Z;q)

       \left|\frac{Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)}{P_j^2}\right|.

\[
\boxed{\quad

\]
Arbitrary subsets of the positive band obey the same bound.
The complete shared Fourier polynomial \(d_N(Y)\) remains
attached; the next own flag is zero, so no outgoing halfweight
is present. The actual central first incoming integral retains
its original measure \(+N\,da\) and the same exponential gap.
Fixed-base signed sums and integrals have normalized local
mixed jets with gap \(10^{-12}\).

Together with the complete-first lower-half theorem, (4)
also pays the exact signed next truncation

\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor N(4/5-q)\rfloor}
                 \frac{Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)}{P_j^2}
\tag{5}

## 3. A correlated outgoing-modulus bound

Write \(S(v)=2\sin\pi v\). We prove
\[
|1-\rho e^{2\pi i(v-u)}|<\frac{S(v)^2}{2}
\quad
\left(3/10\le v\le1/2,\quad u\in P,\quad 0\le\rho\le L\right).
\tag{14}
\]
For fixed \(v,\rho\), the largest squared modulus over \(u\in P\)
occurs at an endpoint of \(P\): the relevant differences lie
in \([-3/20,7/25]\), and cosine decreases with the absolute
value of this difference. For either endpoint \(u\), the
squared modulus is convex in \(\rho\). It therefore suffices
to take \(\rho=0,L\). The zero-radius case is immediate.

Set \(c=\cos2\pi v\), so \(-1\le c<-3/10\) and
\(\sin2\pi v=\sqrt{1-c^2}\). At \(u=11/50\), use
\[
\cos(11\pi/25)<1/5,\qquad \sin(11\pi/25)>49/50.
\]
The desired squared difference is bounded below by
\[
G_1(c)=c^2-(2-2L/5)c-L^2
                    +\frac{49L}{25}\sqrt{1-c^2}.
\tag{15}
\]
This function is strictly concave on \((-1,-3/10)\),
since its second derivative is
\(2-(49L/25)(1-c^2)^{-3/2}<0\).
At its endpoints,
\[
G_1(-1)=3-\frac{2L}{5}-L^2=\frac{107}{3920}>0,
\]
and, using \(\sqrt{91/100}>19/20\) and \(3/2<L<8/5\),
\[
G_1(-3/10)>
\frac{69}{100}+\frac{871}{500}L-L^2
>\frac{743}{1000}>0 .
\tag{16}
\]
Concavity proves positivity on the whole interval.

At \(u=9/20\), the elementary bounds
\[
\cos(9\pi/10)<-19/20,\qquad \sin(9\pi/10)>3/10
\]
instead give the lower difference
\[
G_2(c)=c^2-(2+19L/10)c-L^2
                       +\frac{3L}{5}\sqrt{1-c^2}.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(c\le-1/2\), the polynomial part decreases as \(c\)
increases, and hence
\[
G_2(c)\ge\frac54+\frac{19L}{20}-L^2
>\frac54+\frac{57}{40}-\frac{64}{25}
=\frac{23}{200}>0 .
\tag{18}
\]
On \([-1/2,-3/10]\), \(G_2\) is strictly decreasing.
Indeed its only positive derivative term is at most
\((3L/5)/\sqrt3< (24/25)(3/5)<1\), whereas its negative
constant term is already less than \(-2\).
At the right endpoint,
\[
G_2(-3/10)>
\frac{69}{100}+\frac{57L}{50}-L^2
=\frac{1613}{19600}>0 .
\tag{19}
\]
This proves (14).

All trigonometric witnesses here are elementary. For example
\(\sin(\pi/10)>3/10\) follows from the cubic sine lower
bound at \(5/16<\pi/10\), and
\(\cos(\pi/10)>19/20\) from
\(\pi/10<11/35\) and the quadratic cosine lower bound.
The bounds at \(11\pi/25\) use
\(\cos(11\pi/25)=\sin(3\pi/50)<1/5\) and
\(\cos(3\pi/50)>49/50\).
They also justify \(c<-3/10\) at \(v=3/10\).

We will also use the simpler bound
\[
|1-Ze^{2\pi iv}|<2
                  \qquad(1/5\le v\le1/2,\ u\in P).
\tag{20}
\]
Here \(|v-u|\le7/25\), so its cosine exceeds \(-1/5\).
The squared modulus is less than
\[
1+L^2+\frac25L=\frac{15573}{3920}<4,
\]
including both endpoints of its radial interval.

## 4. Positive transport of the first main and real cuts

Put \(D_q=|1-Y|^2\).

q\text{ interval}&R/D_q^{3/2}&|1-Y/S_s|\\ \hline
[0,1/10]&>5/3&>7/10\\

\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]
These bounds concern just \(q,R\) and the first profiles;
they do not require the previous larger value of \(v-q\).

On \(0\le q\le1/20\), improve the radial bound to
\[
                            R/D_q^{3/2}>3.
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed \(\cos2\pi q>19/20\). The radial function
\(R/(1+R^2-2cR)^{3/2}\) has its minimum at an endpoint

\(1-2R^2+cR\). At \(R=13/25\) the denominator before
the power \(3/2\) is \(353/1250<3/10\), and the squared
ratio is greater than \(1352/135>9\). At \(R=1\) the
denominator is \(1/10\), giving a still stronger bound.

On this initial interval (8) gives \(v\ge1/5\), so
\[
S(v)^2\ge2-2\cos(2\pi/5)>4/3.
\tag{23}
\]
For the last witness use
\(\cos(2\pi/5)=\sin(\pi/10)<11/35<1/3\).
Equations (10),(20)--(23) make the multiplier greater than
\[
                    3\cdot\frac7{10}\cdot\frac{4/3}{2}
                    =\frac75>1.
\]
On \(1/20\le q\le1/10\), one has \(v\ge1/4\) and
\(S(v)^2\ge2\); (20)--(21) give a multiplier greater
than \(7/6\).

Finally, for \(q\ge1/10\), (8) gives \(v\ge3/10\).
Use (14), so that \(S(v)^2/|1-Ze^{2\pi iv}|>2\).
The three remaining lines of (21) make the multiplier

Thus (7) increases on the entire path (9).
Its endpoint calibration in Section 2 proves
\[
                  \mathcal B_s(q,v)
                      <\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-10}
\tag{24}
\]
for every first main or real-cut profile and every (8).
