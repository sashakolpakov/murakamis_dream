# The first last-row alternative and the remaining double-last rate

Private research proof, 8 November 2026. For a next own-four block,
the first last-row alternative is paid against every next main,
real low and real half profile on the full lower-left interval.
All corresponding finite currents are included. Combined with the
previous complementary estimate, the complete central two-kernel
source reduces to one explicit double-last rate at deeper shared
heights. At shared radius at least \(7/10\), that final alternative
is also paid by its original next low cut. The remaining deeper
rate is retained, not declared small.

No numerical evaluation or modification of a frozen input is used.

## 1. Original source and old-face calibration

Keep exactly the actual references, heights, complete cap plane
\(B_U(x)\) and next action plane \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\) of
[the next-four last-row theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_next_four_last_row.md).
In particular
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=x+ih_a,\quad z=u+ih_z,\quad x,u\in P=[.22,.45],\\
y=q+ih_y,\quad 0\le q\le.22,\quad
-.072<h_a<0,\quad -.102<h_y<-.001,\quad
-.108\le h_t,h_z<0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Write \(R=e^{2\pi h_y}>13/25\),
\(\delta_a=-2\pi h_a\), \(\delta_y=-2\pi h_y\), and
\[
Q_1(t;y,z)=g(y+t)+g(z+t)-3g(t)-g(y)-g(z)+2\pi iyt.
\]
After replacing the first raw row by its last-row alternative,
the allocated profile is
\[
\mathcal A(t;x,q,u)=B_U(x)+\delta_a+
             \Re\{Q_1(t;y,z)-g(y)-\tfrac12g(z)\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Every original shared weight and chirp is present.

At \(q_0=11/50\), the first last-row theorem supplies its
strict supporting-plane inequality, with no quantitative
margin assigned to that certificate. The next main plane
and the two original quadratic losses give
\[
\mathcal A(t;x,q_0,u)<\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-10}
\tag{3}
\]
for its original main height and index range. At either
real cut \(t=1/8,1/2\), the next certificate instead gives
the stronger loss \(1/2000\). These are exactly the
ordinary independent-target source bounds at \(q=q_0\).
The quantitative main loss in (3) comes from
\(2\kappa(\alpha-q_0)^2>1/3125000000\), not from an
unreported last-row margin. The sources and marginal
cancellation are those traced in Section 1 of the cited
next-four last-row theorem.

## 2. The next main clips below \(9/20\)

The existing lower clipping raises a next main index below
\(1/6\) to \(1/6\), increasing its value. We now prove
that any index above \(9/20\) may be lowered to that point.
Let \(r_t=e^{2\pi h_t}\in[1/2,1]\), \(T=R^{-1}<25/13\),
and \(9/20\le v\le1/2\). The exact squared horizontal
force has the upper bound
\[
|e^{Q_{1,t}(v+ih_t;y,z)}|^2
<\frac{(25/13+r_t)^2(1+r_t^2+r_t)}
        {(1+r_t^2+19r_t/10)^3}.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed the folded incoming numerator is at most \(T+r_t\).
For the outgoing numerator, \(u+v\in[.67,.95]\), whose
cosine exceeds \(-1/2\); its radius is at most \(r_t\),
so its squared modulus is at most \(1+r_t^2+r_t\).
The own cosine is less than \(-19/20\).

Put \(D=1+r_t^2+19r_t/10\), \(E=1+r_t^2+r_t\). Then
\[
\begin{split}
D-\tfrac54E&=\tfrac{13}{20}r_t-\tfrac14(1+r_t^2)>0,\\
D-\tfrac9{10}(25/13+r_t)&=r_t^2+r_t-19/26\ge1/52>0.
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
The first concave quadratic has endpoint values \(1/80,3/20\).
Thus \(D^3>(81/80)(25/13+r_t)^2E\), and (4) is less than
one. This proves the clipping. No moving critical point is
used. Both clippings hold pointwise at the same current \(q\),
with the first last-row alternative kept attached.

It remains to transport a fixed next main in
\(1/6\le t_R\le9/20\). Its exact derivative is
\[
\partial_q\mathcal A
=\log|r_t^{-1}-Re^{-2\pi i(q+t_R)}|
              -2\log|1-Re^{-2\pi iq}|.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(r_t^{-1}\ge1\), the numerator modulus is at least
\(|1-Re^{-2\pi i(q+t_R)}|\).
Put \(D_q=|1-Re^{-2\pi iq}|^2\), and let \(U\) be the
square of this latter numerator. On \(q\le1/10\),
\[
D_q\le9/20,\qquad U\ge3/4>D_q^2.
\tag{7}
\]
On the other ranges, sufficient bounds are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
q\text{ range}&D_q\le&U\ge\\\hline
[1/10,1/5]&1+R^2-3R/5&1+R^2+R/5\\
[1/5,11/50]&1+R^2-9R/25&1+R^2+9R/10.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]
The first shifted range is \([4/15,13/20]\), with cosine
less than \(-1/10\). The second is \([11/30,67/100]\),
with cosine less than \(-9/20\): its upper endpoint gives
\(\sin(4\pi/25)>\sin(1/2)>23/48>9/20\).
The own cosine bounds are those in the preceding last-row note.

Each difference of the last column and the square of the
middle column is strictly concave for \(1/2\le R\le1\).
The second derivative of \((1+R^2-cR)^2\), for
\(c=3/5,9/25\), is greater than four on this interval.
The endpoint differences in (8) are respectively
\[
(179/400,6/25),\qquad (5551/10000,263/1250),
\tag{9}
\]
all positive. Equations (7)--(9) prove that (6) is positive.
Every original main profile is therefore bounded by an
eligible old main profile at \(q_0\), proving (3) on the
whole interval.

## 3. The real low cut transports without loss

At \(t=1/8\), (6) has \(r_t=1\). For \(q\le1/10\),
the numerator square is at least \(1/2>D_q^2\).
For \(1/10\le q\le3/20\), use
\[
D_q\le1+R^2-R\le1,
\qquad U\ge1+R^2-2R/5>1.
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(\cos(2\pi q)>1/2\) and
\(\cos(2\pi(q+1/8))<1/5\).
For \(3/20\le q\le1/5\), the shifted cosine is less
than \(-3/20\), giving
\[
U\ge1+R^2+3R/10,\qquad D_q\le1+R^2-3R/5.
\tag{11}
\]
The first witness follows from
\(\sin(\pi/20)>\sin(5/32)>5/32-(5/32)^3/6>3/20\).
The difference in (11) is strictly concave, with endpoint
values \(199/400,17/50\). For \(1/5\le q\le11/50\)
use the second line of (8), which also holds at this cut.
Thus \(\partial_q\mathcal A(1/8)>0\) everywhere, and its
old real-cut deficit \(1/2000\) is preserved.

## 4. The real half cut has only a smaller explicit overshoot

At \(t=1/2\), the derivative is exactly
\[
f_R(q)=\tfrac12\log(1+R^2+2R\cos2\pi q)
                       -\log(1+R^2-2R\cos2\pi q).
\tag{12}
\]
For \(q\le16/75\), its cosine exceeds \(2/9\).
Indeed at the right endpoint it is
\(\sin(11\pi/150)>11/48-(11/48)^3/6>2/9\).
At cosine \(2/9\), the numerator square minus the own
fourth power is strictly concave in \(R\in[1/2,1]\),
with endpoint values \(539/1296,2/81\). Hence
\(f_R(q)>0\) throughout this initial range.

For the full range \(q\le11/50\), use its cosine
\(c>9/50\). The logarithmic ratio in (12) increases with
\(c\). At fixed \(c=9/50\), its derivative in \(R\)
has a positive denominator and numerator strictly bounded above by
\[
-R^3-3cR^2+(4c^2-1)R+3c.
\tag{13}
\]
The exact numerator is (13) minus \(2c^2R>0\).
This polynomial is decreasing for \(R\ge1/2\), and its
value at \(1/2\) is \(-97/625\). Thus
\[
f_R(q)>\log(5\sqrt{59}/41)>-1/15.
\tag{14}
\]
For the last comparison,
\(e^{2/15}>257/225>1681/1475\); the second cross-product
difference is \(850\).
Only an interval of length
\(11/50-16/75=1/150\) can have a negative derivative.
Therefore
\[
\mathcal A(1/2;x,q,u)
\le\mathcal A(1/2;x,q_0,u)+1/2250
<\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/18000.
\tag{15}
\]
The final reserve is \(1/2000-1/2250\). It spends the
actual next **half-row** certificate margin only. The first
last-row comparison is again used only for its strict sign.

## 5. Actual finite blocks and the one remaining rate

Every controlling next main and real-cut profile with the
first last-row alternative attached is now paid. The complete
next-current theorem applies at the same \(q\) and retains
this index-independent first factor. Thus all next horizontal
aliases, all four weighted vertical sides, and both unweighted
joins are paid, together with the entire positive next low
block. The original factorial seam continuation and full forced
Fourier envelope cover \(q=0\) without a weight substitution.

By [the own-four upper-row theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_own_four_upper_rows.md),
the entire first upper physical block is bounded positively by
its real half or its last profile. Its half profile is already
paid against all next low/middle pieces by the joint-profile
and complete-current theorems. Its last profile has just been
paid against those pieces. Hence the actual product of the
first upper physical block and the complete next below-half
sum has the finite deficit \(10^{-11}\), with the complete
cap and both Fourier allocations attached.

For a compact statement, put
\[
\mathcal B_{\rm LL}(x,q,u)=B_U(x)+\delta_a+\delta_y
                             -\Re g(y)-\tfrac12\Re g(z).
\tag{16}
\]
The complementary theorem already pays the whole next kernel
when the first factor is restricted below one half. In the
upper-by-upper product, the two endpoint reductions give
four profile pairs. The half-by-half, half-by-last and
last-by-half pairs were paid above or in that complementary
theorem. Only last-by-last gives (16).
Consequently, for every prescribed small \(\tau>0\),
\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
&|U_{0,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(Y)C_{1,N}(Y,Z)|
                         |d_N(Z)|^{1/2}\\
&\quad\le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
 \left\{e^{N(\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11})}
              +e^{N(\mathcal B_{\rm LL}(x,q,u)+\tau)}\right\}.
\end{split}\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
For \(R\ge7/10\), even that last alternative is paid:
the pointwise next-low domination in Section 3 of the preceding
last-row theorem can be multiplied by the first last-row
alternative. The resulting first-last by next-low profile was
paid in Section 3 here. Thus on this entire shallow subdomain,
\[
|U_{0,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(Y)C_{1,N}(Y,Z)|
                         |d_N(Z)|^{1/2}
\le C N^B e^{N(\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11})}.
\tag{18}
\]
Only \(R<7/10\) requires the remaining second term of (17).

This is a complete finite central-source bound: no physical
row, forcing term or contour current is omitted. Both parities,
all real ordinary colors and the original first measure
\(+N\,da\) are retained. Fixed ordinary-color derivatives
have polynomial costs. For fixed local mixed jets use the
original integer-weight holomorphic square, fixed base paths
and local suprema of the two displayed rates. The paid first
term retains a normalized deficit \(10^{-12}\) on smaller
neighborhoods; the second term remains present.

No inequality \(\mathcal B_{\rm LL}<\Lambda_{123}\) is
asserted on the remaining deeper subdomain. This note does not
pay the central product at all actual depths, does not extend
to next own type three, and does not estimate noncentral
first-incoming or other outgoing pieces.
