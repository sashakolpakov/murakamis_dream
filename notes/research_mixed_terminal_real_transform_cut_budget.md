# Direct budgets for real transform cuts and entropy profiles

Research deduction, 6 November 2026. A real transform cut has an
exactly separable dependence on the selected input. The same is true
of both physical entropy profiles. An original bare-source bound at
the reference input therefore eliminates every terminal private
target without evaluating its action. Only two ordinary/selected
scalar gains remain. The fixed real cut has height zero throughout;
no main-height optimizer restriction is assigned to it.

This gives a new sufficient criterion for the five comparisons
isolated in the [source-cell outcome](research_mixed_terminal_source_cell_outcome.md).
Later separate estimates may pay some of those profiles without
changing this reduction.
No new sign, logarithm, primitive, reference or optimizer is evaluated
here.

## 1. Exact elimination of the terminal targets

Keep the external flag \(\beta=1\), the preceding ordinary type four,
and terminal flag \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\). Let
\[
 r_*=(b_*,s_*,a_*,t_*,v_*),\qquad
 a_*=A+ic,\qquad \bar a=A+iH,\qquad
 H=1/100,\qquad \Delta=H-c>0
\tag{1}
\]
be the original canonical reference on the actual \(K_4\) family.
The [reference and main theorem](research_mixed_terminal_uniform_main_comparison.md)
supplies its original zero lifts and the verified height domains.
Write
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q_1(s;b,a)&=g(s+b)+g(s+a)-3g(s)-g(b)-g(a)+2\pi ibs,\\
 F_\epsilon&=Q_1+\Phi_\epsilon-g(a)-\tfrac12g(b),\\
 M_*&=F_\epsilon(r_*),\qquad p_*=(F_\epsilon)_b(r_*),\\
 P_*&=\Phi_\epsilon(a_*;t_*,v_*).
 \end{split}
\tag{2}
\]
In particular \((F_\epsilon)_s=(F_\epsilon)_a=0\) at \(r_*\).
The original complex marginal \(p_*\) is retained.

For either \(\tau=3/20\) or \(\tau=1/2\), define the holomorphic
incoming expression
\[
                \Xi_\tau(a)=g(\tau+a)-g(a)+2\pi i\tau a .
\tag{3}
\]
The complete regular terminal phases in
[the original envelope](research_mixed_terminal_concave_profile_envelope.md),
equation (2), give the literal identity
\[
 \Re\Phi_\epsilon(a;\tau,v)
                 =\Re\Xi_\tau(a)+E_{\epsilon,\tau}(v),
\tag{4}
\]
where \(E_{\epsilon,\tau}\) is independent of \(a\). All private
actions, constants, logarithmic lifts and the term \(-3g(\tau)\)
are included in \(E_{\epsilon,\tau}\). The cut \(\tau\) is real,
while the private target \(v\) retains its original main height and
full original interval.

More generally let an original terminal profile have the form
\[
                   T_\tau(a;y)=\Re\Xi_\tau(a)+E(y).
\tag{5}
\]
Suppose its already proved bare-source bound at the same reference is
\[
                   T_\tau(a_*;y)-\Re P_*\le U
                         \quad\hbox{for every original }y .
\tag{6}
\]
Then, identically for every allowed complex target \(a\),
\[
 T_\tau(a;y)\le
       \Re P_*+U+\Re\{\Xi_\tau(a)-\Xi_\tau(a_*)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
This applies a source bound only at \(a_*\). It does not transport
that bound to another height by assumption. Instead (3) accounts
exactly for the change in \(a\). The real displacement and height
change from \(t_*\) to the real cut, and optimization over the
private target, are already included in the bare bound (6).

The two entropy profiles also satisfy (5) with \(\tau=1/2\).
Indeed equation (8) of the envelope is exactly
\[
 R_\epsilon(a;y)
 =\Re\{g(a+1/2)-g(a)+\pi ia\}+E_\epsilon(y).
\tag{8}
\]
The full closed entropy interval or triangle remains in (6).
No entropy concavity or optimizer localization is needed for (7).
The normalization \(J(1/2)=0\) gives \(\Re g(1/2)=0\), so this
identification drops no real half-row constant.

The immutable bare-source extraction supplies (6), either with its
uniform upper \(U\) or with the upper from the same original
reference cell. At the target real part \(a_R=A\) and original
height \(c\), its supporting reference plane equals \(\Re P_*\).
The [source-domain theorem](research_mixed_terminal_canonical_source_domain.md)
supplies the source hypotheses throughout the actual \(K_4\)
family. An independently enlarged reference rectangle is not being
declared source-feasible.

## 2. Exact height correction and two forces

Keep the lifted-main quantities \(T_\nu,A_s,A_{a,\nu}\) of
[the original joint-main budget](research_mixed_terminal_joint_main_budget.md),
equations (9)--(10), with both alternatives on all
\(P=[.22,.45]\). For clarity, set
\[
 \begin{split}
 D_\tau(z)&=g'(\tau+z)-g'(t_*+z)+2\pi i(\tau-t_*),\\
 C_\tau&=\Re\{g(\tau+\bar a)-g(\tau+a_*)
             -g(t_*+\bar a)+g(t_*+a_*)
                 +2\pi i(\tau-t_*)i\Delta\}\\
        &=-\int_c^H\Im D_\tau(A+iy)\,dy,\\
 d_\tau&=\Re D_\tau(\bar a).
 \end{split}
\tag{9}
\]
All \(g'\) are on the original regular logarithmic strip. In
particular the chirp \(2\pi i(\tau-t_*)\) is not discarded or
replaced by a principal modulus logarithm.

Apply (7) to the literal joint defect
\[
 \Re Q_1(s;b,a)+T_\tau(a;y)-\nu\Re g(a)-\tfrac12\Re g(b)
       -2\pi\nu H-\Re\{M_*+p_*(b-b_*)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
After removing the additive upper \(U\), call the resulting
three-variable upper function \(\mathcal R_{\tau,\nu}(b,s,a)\).
At the lifted reference \((b_*,s_*,\bar a)\), its value and real
gradient are exactly
\[
 \mathcal R_{\tau,\nu}=T_\nu+C_\tau,\qquad
 \nabla_{\!(b_R,s_R,a_R)}\mathcal R_{\tau,\nu}
                     =(0,A_s,A_{a,\nu}+d_\tau).
\tag{11}
\]
There is no terminal-index gain or private-variable gain: those
variables have been eliminated by (6), not assumed stationary at
the cut. The incoming zero is supplied by the same original \(p_*\).

Original shared-leg stationarity also gives a useful direct expression
for the last force:
\[
 A_{a,\nu}+d_\tau
  =\Re\{g'(s_*+\bar a)+g'(\tau+\bar a)
                    -(2+\nu)g'(\bar a)+2\pi i\tau\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Although its last term is purely imaginary for real \(\tau\), it
is retained in this original holomorphic derivative. Formula (12)
can preserve correlation better than separately enclosing
\(A_{a,\nu}\) and \(d_\tau\).

There is also an exact cancellation at the level of the trial value.
Write
\(\mathcal B_g(z;i\Delta)=g(z+i\Delta)-g(z)-i\Delta g'(z)\).
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_\nu+C_\tau={}&-\Delta\Im D_\tau(a_*)\\
 &+\Re\{\mathcal B_g(s_*+a_*;i\Delta)
       +\mathcal B_g(\tau+a_*;i\Delta)
                       -3\mathcal B_g(a_*;i\Delta)\}\\
 &+(1-\nu)\Re g(\bar a)-2\pi\nu H,\\
 D_\tau(a_*)&=g'(\tau+a_*)+g'(s_*+a_*)-3g'(a_*)+2\pi i\tau .
 \end{split}
\tag{12a}
\]
The old \(t_*+a_*\) remainder cancels before bounding: the real
part of
\(i\Delta D_\tau(a_*)+\mathcal B_g(\tau+a_*;i\Delta)
-\mathcal B_g(t_*+a_*;i\Delta)\) is exactly \(C_\tau\).
Thus the value and force in the reduced budget can use only the
same two reference coordinates \(s_*,a_*\), the fixed cut, and the
original selected primitive. Their dependence on the original
canonical family is unchanged.

Equations (9)--(12) follow by subtracting the two incoming expressions
\(\Xi_\tau\) and \(\Xi_{t_*}\). They require no new evaluation of
\(S\), \(P_\epsilon\), or the private phase. They also retain the
actual height-zero cut; the old three-cost main comparison is not
being restricted from height \(\Im t_*\) to height zero.

## 3. Curvature of the remaining selected variable

Let \(g_H(x)=\Re g(x+iH)\), and put
\[
 V(z,w)=g(z+w)-g(z)-g(w),\qquad
 \sigma_{3/20}=\tfrac35,\quad \sigma_{1/2}=\tfrac53.
\tag{13}
\]
On every \(x\in P\), at \(H=1/100\),
\[
 -\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\Re V(\tau,x+iH)
       =g_H''(x)-g_H''(x+\tau)
                    \ge\sigma_\tau g_H''(x)>0 .
\tag{14}
\]

For \(\tau=1/2\), set \(C=\cosh(2\pi H)\) and
\(X=\cos(2\pi x)\). The exact ratio is
\[
 \frac{-g_H''(x+1/2)}{g_H''(x)}
                         =\frac{C-X}{C+X}>\frac23.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed \(C\ge1\), \(X\le\cos(.44\pi)=\sin(.06\pi)<1/5\),
and \(C+X>0\). The inequality is equivalent to \(C>5X\).
This argument works for every regular real height \(H\), although
only \(H=.01\) is used here.

For \(\tau=3/20\), the assertion is immediate if
\(x+\tau\ge1/2\), since then \(g_H''(x+\tau)\le0\).
Otherwise \(x\in[.22,.35)\). Put
\[
 \theta=2\pi x,\quad \varphi=3\pi/10,\quad
 X=\cos\theta,\quad Y=\sin\theta,\quad
 c_\varphi=\cos\varphi,\quad s_\varphi=\sin\varphi.
\tag{16}
\]
We use
\[
 1\le C<101/100,\quad X<1/5,\quad Y>4/5,\quad
 1/2<c_\varphi<59/100,\quad 4/5<s_\varphi<41/50.
\tag{17}
\]
For explicit elementary witnesses, \(2\pi H<1/10\), and
\[
 \begin{split}
 \cosh(1/10)&\le1+
       \frac{(1/10)^2}{2(1-(1/10)^2/12)}
       =1+\frac6{1199}<\frac{101}{100},\\
 \cos\varphi&<
       1-\frac{(471/500)^2}{2}
                     +\frac{(471/500)^4}{24}<\frac{59}{100},\\
 \sin\varphi&>
             \frac{471}{500}-\frac{(471/500)^3}{6}>\frac45,\\
 \sin\varphi&<
       \frac{19}{20}-\frac{(19/20)^3}{6}
                     +\frac{(19/20)^5}{120}<\frac{41}{50}.
 \end{split}
\tag{18}
\]
Here \(471/500<\varphi<33/35<19/20\), and
\(c_\varphi>1/2\) follows from \(\varphi<\pi/3\).
Also \(X\le\sin(.06\pi)<33/175<1/5\). On
\(\theta\in[.44\pi,.7\pi]\), the minimum of \(\sin\theta\)
is \(\sin(.3\pi)=s_\varphi>4/5\).

After multiplying by the positive cotangent denominators, the sign
of \(5g_H''(x+.15)-2g_H''(x)\) is the sign of
\[
 \mathcal E=
 C(5c_\varphi-2)Y
 +X(5Cs_\varphi-3c_\varphi Y)
 -s_\varphi(2+3X^2).
\tag{19}
\]
If \(X\le0\), the bracket multiplying \(X\) is positive:
it exceeds \(4-177/100>0\). Equations (17) imply
\[
 \mathcal E<
 \begin{cases}
 1919/2000-8/5=-1281/2000,&X\le0,\\
 1919/2000+\frac15(\frac{4141}{1000}-\frac65)-\frac85
                         =-523/10000,&X\ge0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{20}
\]
Thus \(g_H''(x+.15)<(2/5)g_H''(x)\), proving (14).
This is a scalar fixed-coordinate argument;
it does not apply the symmetric pair theorem outside that theorem's
two-variable real domain.

The ordinary index barrier remains unchanged, because the terminal
profile is independent of \(s\). It restricts a joint maximum to
\(s_R\in[.22,.5]\). Apply the
[symmetric pair lemma](research_mixed_terminal_symmetric_pair_costs.md)
to \(V(s,b)\) and \(V(s,a)\), retaining the original
\(-g(s)\), \(-\nu g(a)\), incoming halfweight and chirps.
Equation (14) then gives, on the entire containing product,
\[
 -\mathcal R_{\tau,\nu}''
 \succeq\operatorname{diag}
   \left(g_{h_b}'',\,2g_{h_s}'',\,
                 \kappa_{\tau,\nu}g_H''\right),
 \qquad
 \kappa_{\tau,\nu}=\nu+\tfrac12+\sigma_\tau.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus \(\kappa_{3/20,\nu}=\nu+11/10\), whereas
\(\kappa_{1/2,\nu}=\nu+13/6\). The latter extra cost is
available for both the real right cut and the entropy profiles.
No curvature of the eliminated private or entropy variables is
needed.

## 4. Direct sufficient tests for the real cuts and entropy profiles

Let
\[
 \phi_h(x;x_*)=g_h(x)-g_h(x_*)-g_h'(x_*)(x-x_*).
\tag{22}
\]
Integrating (21) from the lifted reference, retaining the exact
forces (11), and discarding only the nonnegative incoming cost with
zero force gives the following theorem.

**Theorem.** Under the original source bound (6) and reference
hypotheses above, uniformly over the original independent targets,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sup D_{\tau,\nu}\le U+T_\nu+C_\tau
 &+\sup_{x\in[.22,.5]}
    \{A_s(x-\Re s_*)-2\phi_{h_s}(x;\Re s_*)\}\\
 &+\sup_{x\in P}
    \{(A_{a,\nu}+d_\tau)(x-A)
                     -\kappa_{\tau,\nu}\phi_H(x;A)\}.
 \end{split}
\tag{23}
\]
Both Fourier alternatives retain all \(P\). In particular no
forced optimizer is discarded.

The verified canonical height bounds and the
[cubic-cost theorem](research_mixed_terminal_cubic_cost_and_response_bounds.md)
give the explicit stronger-than-affine test
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 U+T_\nu+C_\tau
 +\mathcal G_{88/5}(A_s;\Re s_*,[.22,.5])
 +\mathcal G_{(49/5)\kappa_{\tau,\nu}}
                 (A_{a,\nu}+d_\tau;A,P)<-\gamma .
 \quad}
\tag{24}
\]
Here \(\mathcal G_K\) is the signed cubic upper bound for the
corresponding exact scalar supremum; it is not a new optimization
over a reference rectangle. A uniform proof of (24) gives the same
raw phase gap \(\gamma\) for that original source profile.

The five branch comparisons isolated in that outcome reduce to three choices of
reference family and incoming expression:

| \((\epsilon,\tau)\) | Original profile | Alternatives addressed |
|---|---|---|
| \((0,1/2)\) | Type-three real right cut | Factorial |
| \((0,1/2)\) | Type-three physical entropy | Factorial |
| \((1,3/20)\) | Type-four real left cut | Factorial and forced |
| \((1,1/2)\) | Type-four physical entropy | Factorial |

The first two rows share \(T_\nu,C_\tau\), the force and both scalar
gains; only their own bare upper \(U\) changes. The same reference
cell must be used if \(U\) and the other quantities are restricted
to cells. A bound valid on the whole canonical family may instead
be combined with the uniform bare upper.

Compared with the existing main budget, (24) removes the terminal
gain, preserves or strengthens the selected cost, and inserts the
literal height correction and altered selected force. A sign for
\(C_\tau\) or a force-contraction inequality is not asserted.
The completed main sign alone does not prove (24).

## 5. Exact additional data and enclosure requirements

The only new incoming argument is \(\tau+A+iy\),
\(c\le y\le H\). It remains in the original regular strip:
its real part is in \([.37,.60]\) for \(\tau=.15\), and
in \([.72,.95]\) for \(\tau=.5\). The other arguments
\(t_*+A+iy\), \(s_*+A+iy\), and \(A+iy\) already occur in
the original height integrals. Thus (9) can be bounded by a fixed
height integral of original logarithms, or by a center value and
cotangent remainder, without any private-action evaluation.
All coefficients should retain the same canonical parameter and
posterior errors.

For a nonstationary enclosing point \(r\), the exact identity is
\[
 D_\tau(a)=g'(\tau+a)+g'(s+a)-3g'(a)+2\pi i\tau-F_a(r),
\tag{25}
\]
where \(F_a(r)=g'(s+a)+g'(t+a)-3g'(a)+2\pi it\).
Consequently the cancellation leading to (12) may be used at the
actual reference, but it must not be imposed as a new equation on
every point of an enclosing rectangle. Keeping the literal (9),
or retaining the residual in (25), supplies a valid full-box
enclosure. The midpoint of such a box is not presumed stationary.

No such new logarithmic or cotangent enclosure is produced here.
The present result is the exact reduction (23)--(24), including
the sharper fixed-half-row curvature. Any later computation must
use a fixed reviewed protocol, preserve the source associations
in (6), retain both alternatives where required, and record an
inconclusive outcome if the upper remains nonnegative.

A proved test may replace the upper for its own profile in the
[conditional source-cell assembly](research_mixed_terminal_source_cell_outcome.md).
Every remaining profile must still be paid, and the final common
gap must be the minimum of the actual available gaps, including
any obtained from separate estimates. No test sign, complete
terminal kernel, nonplateau contour estimate, or whole-word
asymptotic is claimed in this note.
