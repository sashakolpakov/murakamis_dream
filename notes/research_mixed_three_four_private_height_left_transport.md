# Coupled left transport with the original first-cap private height

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The central and real-cut
comparison beside the first type-three cap extends from a real private
index to its entire actual private-height interval. Both private
aliases are below the main profile at that actual height. Thus the
early-taper transport need not reset the private main contour to zero.
The new conditional real cuts are still not identified with the old
terminal calibrated faces. No numerical evaluation is used.

and [the private-target finite envelope](research_mixed_three_four_terminal_private_taper.md).

 s=v+i\eta,\quad .22\le v\le.49,\quad \eta\le0 .
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Finite source statements use a fixed compact range of \(\eta\);
the actual terminal main height belongs to \((-.108,-.020)\).
The \(k\)-interval is the complete actual own-three private range
from [the original endpoint rectangles](research_mixed_open_word_endpoint_rectangles.md).
It also contains the real private cuts. No new critical point is used.

For the fixed right private target \(b\in E_c\), the coupled phase is
the original bare first-cap phase plus the original conditional phase:
\[
 \mathcal J(e,s;a,b)
 =\Psi_0(e,a)+
   g(s+a)+g(s+b)-3g(s)+2\pi ias-g(a)-g(b).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
The full right endpoint coefficient and the remaining private

\end{split}                                                     \tag{3}

The two shared allocations and the chirp have already been combined
in (3); no gauge or marginal is discarded.

**Theorem 1.** Throughout (1),
\[
                         \mathcal H>\frac1{100}.         \tag{4}
\]
In particular this holds on the whole actual private-main height,
both real private cuts, and the stated central or real-cut adjoining

## 2. Reductions preserving the actual incoming radius

Put \(r=e^{2\pi h}\). The original elementary witnesses give
\[
 \frac35<r<\frac{23}{20},\qquad
 -\frac7{40}<\lambda<\frac35,\qquad
 \lambda\ge0\Rightarrow r\le1,\quad
 \lambda\le0\Rightarrow r\ge1.                            \tag{5}
\]
These correlations remain in the argument.

First, \(\mathcal H\) strictly decreases as \(\eta\) increases
to zero, by the exact derivative in Lemma 1 of
[the moving-height reduction](research_mixed_first_attached_moving_height_reduction.md).
That derivative is independent of the first private coordinate \(e\).
It suffices to take \(\eta=0\).

Next put \(\rho=e^{2\pi k}\), \(r_e=\rho r\) and
\(\theta=2\pi(u+x)\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_k\mathcal H
 =\frac{2\pi r_e\{r_e-\cos\theta+\lambda\sin\theta\}}
              {|1-r_e e^{-i\theta}|^2}>0.               \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(\cos\theta<-6/25\) on the present interval.
Also \(\rho>22/25\): the elementary exponential witness
for \(e^{-\pi/25}>22/25\) in Section 2 of
[the private-alias payment](research_mixed_cap_alias_half_and_private_payment.md)
applies since \(2\pi k\ge-.038\pi>-\pi/25\).
If \(\lambda\ge0\), the braces exceed
\((22/25)(3/5)+6/25-3/5>0\).
If \(\lambda<0\), they exceed \(r_e+6/25-7/40>0\).
Thus the minimum in \(k\) is at \(k=-19/1000\).

At this fixed \(k\), the old endpoint-minimum argument in \(v\)
is unchanged. The \(u\) derivative now has the sign of
\[
       \sin\theta+\lambda(\cos\theta-\rho r).
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
At a zero its derivative in \(\theta\) equals
\((\rho r\cos\theta-1)/(\rho r-\cos\theta)<0\).
Every interior critical point is therefore a strict maximum;
the same argument as in the cited reduction puts each minimum
at a real endpoint. It suffices to treat
\[
      k=-19/1000,\quad \eta=0,\quad
      u\in\{.27,.49\},\quad v\in\{.22,.49\}.             \tag{8}
\]

## 3. Uniform modulus bounds at the four real endpoint pairs

For fixed \(r\), the original own-cotangent estimate gives
\(\Re\cot\pi a>1\) on \(.1\le x\le.22\).
It holds on the entire range (5), since
\((r+r^{-1})/2\le17/15\); see Section 2 of
[the own-three comparison](research_mixed_first_three_moving_height_comparison.md).
Both shifted angular cosines are negative, so their real cotangents
are less than one, including the extra private height \(k\).
Consequently \(\Re D\) decreases as \(x\) increases with the radii
held fixed. This compares only its real part, not the full moving
derivative. Its lower bound may therefore be taken at \(x=.22\).

Use the original rational trigonometric bounds
\[
 \cos(.44\pi)>\frac9{50},\quad
 -\cos(1.42\pi)>\frac6{25},\quad
 -\cos(.88\pi)>\frac{23}{25},\quad
 -\cos(.98\pi)>\frac{23}{25}.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
The last follows from the preceding bound by cosine monotonicity.
Define \(D_r=1+r^2-(9/25)r\). The squared first and second
numerator-to-denominator ratios in \(D\) have the following lower
bounds:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{first shifted index}&\text{squared ratio lower bound}\\ \hline
 u=.27&9/5\quad(3/5\le r\le23/20)\\
 u=.49&13/10\quad(3/5\le r\le23/20)\\
 u=.49&4/3\quad(3/5\le r\le1)
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{second shifted index}&\text{squared ratio lower bound}\\ \hline
 v=.22&308/143\\
 v=.49&7/5 .
\end{array}                                                     \tag{10}
\]
Every inequality is strict. Here is an exact algebraic check.
Replace the first shifted radius \(\rho r\) by \((22/25)r\);
the modulus increases with that radius because its cosine is negative.
The relevant numerator polynomials are
\[
 1+(22r/25)^2+(46/25)(22r/25)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 1+(22r/25)^2+(12/25)(22r/25).
\]
Subtract the proposed multiple of \(D_r\).
Each resulting quadratic is concave. Its values at the two interval
ends are respectively
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{proposed ratio}&r=3/5&r=23/20\ \text{or }1\\ \hline
 9/5&2986/15625&112731/250000\\
 13/10&1407/31250&14427/500000\\
 4/3&323/46875&19/1875 .
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{11}
\]
Thus all three first-ratio bounds follow without a radius reset.
For the second ratio at \(v=.49\), subtracting \((7/5)D_r\)
from \(1+r^2+(12/25)r\) gives a concave quadratic with values
\(29/625,1013/5000\) at the two ends.
For \(v=.22\), the same-radius quotient increases up to \(r=1\)
and decreases afterward, and is invariant under \(r\mapsto1/r\).
Its minimum on (5) is therefore bounded by its value at \(r=3/5\),
namely \(308/143\), exactly as in the cited own-three proof.

## 4. Original arguments and the actual moving coefficient

Let \(D_0\) denote (3) at \(k=\eta=0\), with the same \(r,x,u,v\).
The original rotated-ratio identity gives
\[
 \Im D_0\ge\pi(v-u)\quad(r\le1),\qquad
 \Im D_0\le\pi(v-u)\quad(r\ge1).                         \tag{12}
\]
Its hypotheses hold for both shifts: \(0<\pi w<2\pi x+\pi w<\pi\).
There is no logarithmic-lift change.

The change from \(k=0\) to \(k=-19/1000\) satisfies
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 -.019\pi<\Im D-\Im D_0<0,&u=.27,\\
 0<\Im D-\Im D_0<.019\pi,&u=.49.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{13}
\]
Indeed \(\partial_k\Im g'(e+a)=\pi\Re\cot\pi(e+a)\).
For \(u=.27\), the shifted real part is in \([.37,.49]\);
for \(u=.49\), it is in \([.59,.71]\).
The real cotangent has respectively positive and negative sign,
and absolute value less than one at every intermediate height.
Integrating over the actual negative \(k\) proves (13).

Consider first the only tight pair \(u=.49,v=.22\).
If \(\lambda\ge0\), (10) gives
\[
 \Re D>\frac12\log\frac{1232}{429}
       =\operatorname{atanh}\frac{803}{1661}>\frac{13}{25}.
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
For the last inequality, \(803/1661>12/25\), and the first three
positive terms of the atanh series exceed
\[
 \frac{12}{25}+\frac9{250}+\frac1{200}
 =\frac{521}{1000}>\frac{13}{25}.
 \quad
 \left(\frac{(12/25)^3}{3}>\frac9{250},\
       \frac{(12/25)^5}{5}>\frac1{200}\right)
\]
Equations (12)--(13) give \(\Im D\ge-.27\pi\).
Its possible debit is at most
\((3/5)(27/100)\pi<891/1750<51/100\).
Thus \(\mathcal H>1/100\).
If \(\lambda\le0\), (12)--(13) instead give
\(\Im D<-.251\pi<0\), so its contribution is nonnegative.
The real part exceeds
\(\tfrac12\log(13/5)>4/9\), by the first atanh term.

For the other pairs, (10)--(13) give ample strict bounds:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 (u,v)&\text{real-part lower bound}
                  &\text{largest possible imaginary debit}\\ \hline
 (.27,.22)&\tfrac12\log(18/5)>13/23
                  &(3/5)(69/1000)\pi<207(22)/(35000)\\
 (.27,.49)&\tfrac12\log(63/25)>19/44
                  &(7/40)(11/50)\pi<121/1000\\
 (.49,.49)&\tfrac12\log(91/50)>41/141
                  &(7/40)(19/1000)\pi<209/20000 .
\end{array}                                                     \tag{15}
\]
In the first row the debit is needed only when \(\lambda\ge0\);
in the other two rows it is needed only when \(\lambda\le0\).
All lower bounds in (15), after the displayed debits, exceed \(1/100\).
This proves Theorem 1, with the original coefficient/radius sign
correlation preserved.

## 5. The private aliases and the complete cap envelope

For own three, the original argument proof in
[the private-alias payment](research_mixed_cap_alias_half_and_private_payment.md)
gives the sharper unallocated strip
\[
             -21\pi/20<\Im\Psi_{0,e}<59\pi/50
                     \quad(e\in E_c,\ .1\le\Re a\le.22).
                                                               \tag{16}
\]
It is uniform at the actual target heights. Let \(k_*\in(-.019,.037)\)
be the original first-cap private height. Integrating (16) at fixed
real private coordinate \(u\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \Re\Psi_0(u-.055i,a)-.11\pi
 &<\Re\Psi_0(u+ik_*,a)-36\pi/25000,\\
 \Re\Psi_0(u+.04i,a)-.08\pi
 &<\Re\Psi_0(u+ik_*,a)-361\pi/20000 .
\end{split}                                                     \tag{17}
\]
For the lower alias use a height distance at most \(.092\) and
the upper bound \(59\pi/50\). For the upper alias use at most
\(.059\) and the lower bound \(-21\pi/20\).
The two reserves exceed \(1/250\) and \(1/20\), respectively.
They remain unchanged after adding the identical neighboring phase.
Thus both aliases are inactive relative to the actual-height main
profile in [the complete early five-profile envelope](research_mixed_cap_early_taper_complex_profiles.md).

The remaining cap profiles are its main at \(k_*\), its two real
cuts, and its explicit true-index alternative.
Theorem 1 covers the first three coupled with every regular adjoining
profile in (1). The true-index alternative has moving derivative
greater than \(1/20\), by Proposition 2 of the cited moving-height
reduction; that derivative is independent of the private target \(b\).
Consequently their maximum obeys
\[
 \mathcal E(x_0)\le
 \mathcal E(x_1)-\frac{x_1-x_0}{100},
                 \qquad .1\le x_0<x_1\le.22,            \tag{18}
\]
with \(k_*,\eta,b\) fixed and \(h\) varying as in (1).
Integration is performed before maximization, so no smooth maximizing
index is assumed. The angular loss, where present, is unchanged.

Equation (18) is still not a whole-terminal calibrated estimate.
In particular the conditional envelope's cuts \(.22,.49\) are
not the original terminal real-transform faces \(.15,.5\).
Their height-zero rates at \(x=.22\) cannot be replaced by the
completed terminal supporting plane without a further comparison.
The positive adjoining alias, the remaining high physical profiles,
the outer private summation and incoming-height connectors must retain
their own source arguments. No original one-circle normalization,
ordinary marginal, observation, or long-filling remainder is changed.
