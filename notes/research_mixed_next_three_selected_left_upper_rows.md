# The complete upper physical block on the selected left interval

Private research proof candidate, 8 November 2026. Every upper
physical row of the next own-three factor is paid against each
original first main profile on the selected left interval. The
actual type-three outgoing real coordinate is essential in the
arc comparison. Both selected Fourier-weight alternatives and
the full forced polynomial are retained.

This is a finite positive-block bound. It does not by itself
estimate the next canonical currents, non-main first groups, or
the complete upper-left shared contour. No numerical evaluation
or modification of a frozen source is used.

## 1. Actual domain and the original finite block

Use the reference, cap plane, phases and allocation of
[selected left main profiles](research_mixed_next_three_selected_left_main_profiles.md),
for actual complete original words in powers three and four
beginning \(343\), with length \(m\ge5\). Write
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=x+ih_a,\quad x\in P=[11/50,9/20],\qquad
y=q+iH,\quad 0\le q\le q_0=11/50,\quad H=1/100,\\
z=u+ih_z,\qquad 3/10\le u\le39/100,\qquad
-3/100<h_z<1/50,\\
s=s_R+ih_s,\qquad 1/8\le s_R\le1/2,
\qquad r_s=e^{2\pi h_s}>14/25,\quad h_s<0.
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The smaller outgoing real interval is the original \(j=3\)
type-three window of
[open-word inheritance, Theorem 1](research_mixed_open_word_window_inheritance.md).
It contains the actual \(u=\Re t_3^*\) for every word in this
scope. It is not asserted for an independent target throughout
the larger interval \(P\).

Put
\[
R=e^{2\pi H},\quad T=R^{-1},\quad
Y=Re^{-2\pi iq},\quad Z=\rho e^{-2\pi iu},\quad
\rho=e^{2\pi h_z}.
\]
The exact bounds already proved in the profile source are
\[
53/50<R<16/15,\qquad 15/16<T<1,\qquad 4/5<\rho<8/7.
\tag{2}
\]
Retain \(g\), \(J(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi b)\,db\),
and
\[
Q_1(s;a,y)=g(a+s)+g(y+s)-3g(s)-g(a)-g(y)+2\pi ias.
\tag{3}
\]
All phases have their original logarithmic lift and constant.
Let \(B_U(x)\) and \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\) be the unchanged
cap supporting rate and final affine comparison.

For \(D=N-1\), \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\),
\(P_j=(r;r)_j\), and \(p_j(V)=(rV;r)_j\), define the
literal positive upper block
\[
\mathcal U_N(Y,Z)=
\sum_{j=\lceil N/2\rceil}^{D}
       \left|\frac{Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)}{P_j^2}\right|.
\tag{4}
\]
An empty sum is zero. No physical row is replaced by a
noninteger index. Keep the complete polynomial \(d_N\), with
\(d_N(r^b)=P_b^{-1}\) and degree at most \(D\).

**Theorem.** On (1), for every sufficiently large integer level
of either parity,
\[
\boxed{\quad
e^{N\{B_U(x)+\Re Q_1(s;a,y)\}}
 |d_N(Y)Y^{-N}|\,\mathcal U_N(Y,Z)
 \le C N^B e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/200\}}.
\quad}                                                         \tag{5}
\]
The complete cap may therefore retain its stated supporting
envelope, and any first-main amplitude with polynomial cost and
a sufficiently small prescribed exponential tolerance may be
attached. Equation (5) is not a bound for the entire first
conditional sum. The positive block has no additional factor
\(N\); the original \(+N\) is inserted only when an actual
first incoming integral or constant-mode index integral is taken.

## 2. Exact reflection and a small-index arc bound

The physical rate, with its incoming carrier, is
\[
E(t)=\int_0^t\log|1-Ye^{-2\pi ib}|\,db
 +\int_0^t\log|1-Ze^{-2\pi ib}|\,db-2J(t)-t\log R.
\tag{6}
\]
Set \(v=1-t\), \(F(v)=E(1-v)\), and
\(L_H=\log_+\rho\). The full-period means give
\(E(1)=\log R+\log_+\rho-\log R=L_H\).
Reflection consequently gives exactly
\[
\begin{split}
F(v)-L_H={}&2J(v)
-\int_0^v\log|T-e^{2\pi i(q-b)}|\,db\\
&-\int_0^v\log|1-\rho e^{2\pi i(u-b)}|\,db,\\
F'(v)&=\log\frac{S(v)^2}{A(v)B(v)},\qquad S(v)=2\sin\pi v,\\
A(v)&=|T-e^{2\pi i(v-q)}|,\qquad
B(v)=|1-\rho e^{2\pi i(v-u)}|.
\end{split}                                                      \tag{7}
\]
In particular the positive outgoing-height carrier remains in
\(L_H\). Integrable logarithmic zeros are interpreted by
continuity of the integrated rates.

For \(0\le v\le1/5\), every arc of length \(v\) has incoming
integral at least its centered arc. Indeed its log modulus is
even and increases with distance from the nearest integer.
Radial minimization gives
\(|T-e^{2\pi ib}|\ge\sin(2\pi b)\) for \(0\le b\le v/2\).
Thus
\[
\int_0^v\log|T-e^{2\pi i(q-b)}|\,db
\ge2\int_0^{v/2}\log\sin(2\pi b)\,db
=J(v)-v\log2.
\tag{8}
\]
For the other factor use the exact inequality
\[
|1-\rho e^{2\pi ib}|^2
       =\rho(2\sin\pi b)^2+(1-\rho)^2
       \ge\rho(2\sin\pi b)^2.
\]
The interval \([u-v,u]\) lies in \([1/10,39/100]\).
Since \(\log(2\sin\pi b)\) increases there, (1)--(2) give
\[
\int_0^v\log|1-\rho e^{2\pi i(u-b)}|\,db
\ge J(3/10)-J(3/10-v)+\tfrac v2\log(4/5).
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently \(F(v)-L_H\le H_0(v)\), where
\[
H_0(v)=J(v)+v\log2-J(3/10)+J(3/10-v)
                         +\tfrac v2\log(5/4).
\tag{10}
\]
This function is continuous at zero, \(H_0(0)=0\), and
\[
H_0''(v)=\pi\cot\pi v+\pi\cot\pi(3/10-v)>0
                                      \quad(0<v\le1/5).
\tag{11}
\]
Its other endpoint has a strict written bound. The inequality
\(2\sin\pi b\le2\pi b\) yields
\[
J(1/10)\le\tfrac1{10}\{\log(\pi/5)-1\},
\]
and the original pentagon witness \(2\sin(\pi/5)>7/6\) yields
\[
\int_{1/5}^{3/10}\log(2\sin\pi b)\,db
                             >\tfrac1{10}\log(7/6).
\]
After combining the logarithms in (10), these imply
\[
H_0(1/5)<\tfrac1{10}\{\log(6\pi/7)-1\}
       <-\frac{17}{31765}<-\frac1{2000}.
\tag{12}
\]
For the middle inequality use \(6\pi/7<132/49\),
\(e>1+1+1/2+1/6+1/24=65/24\), and
\[
\log\frac{3185}{3168}>\frac{34}{6353}.
\]
The last logarithmic bound is \(\log x>2(x-1)/(x+1)\).
Convexity between the two endpoints in (11)--(12) proves
\[
\boxed{\qquad F(v)\le L_H-v/400
                                  \quad(0\le v\le1/5).
\qquad}                                                        \tag{13}
\]
This comparison is independent of the first private profile.

## 3. Increasing upper rows and the diagonal transport

For \(1/5\le v\le1/2\), the actual outgoing interval gives
\(|u-v|\le1/5\), so \(\cos(2\pi(u-v))>3/10\).
Convexity in \(0\le\rho\le8/7\) therefore gives
\[
B(v)^2<\max\{1,1+64/49-24/35\}
             =397/245<81/49,
\qquad B(v)<9/7.
\tag{14}
\]
The cosine witness follows from \(\sqrt5>11/5\).

If \(v\ge3/10\), then \(A(v)<2\) and
\[
S(v)^2\ge S(3/10)^2=(3+\sqrt5)/2>18/7,
\tag{15}
\]
using \(\sqrt5>15/7\). Equations (7), (14)--(15) give
\(F'(v)>0\). Hence these rows are bounded by the real half
profile \(F(1/2)\) at the same \(q\).

If \(1/5\le v\le3/10\) and \(q\ge3/20\), then
\(|v-q|\le3/20\). The original witness
\(\cos(3\pi/10)>7/12\) and convexity in
\(T\in[15/16,1]\) give
\[
A(v)^2<\max\{201/256,5/6\}=5/6<1.
\tag{16}
\]
Also \(S(v)^2\ge S(1/5)^2=(5-\sqrt5)/2>11/8\), using
\(\sqrt5<9/4\). Thus \(F'(v)>\log(77/72)>0\) in this
region. Increasing to \(v=3/10\) and then using (15) again
bounds it by the same-coordinate real half profile. These
monotonicity statements hold across any isolated outgoing
logarithmic zero: the derivative is locally integrable and has
the indicated positive sign on either side.

It remains to treat \(q<3/20\), \(1/5<v<3/10\).
Clip the first main at this \(q\), as in Section 2 of the
accepted profile theorem, to
\(1/6\le s_R\le5/12\). This only increases its rate and
does not change \(h_s\). Hold that clipped index fixed while
increasing \(q,v\) at equal speed. Stop at \(q=3/20\) or
\(v=3/10\).

Write
\[
D_q=|1-Y|^2,\qquad I_s(q)=|1-Rr_s e^{-2\pi i(q+s_R)}|,
\quad W_{\rm fac}=-\Re g(y)-2\pi H,\quad W_{\rm force}=0.
\]
The exact derivatives of the two allocated joint rates are
\[
\begin{split}
\exp\frac d{dq}\{B_U+\Re Q_1+F(q+d)+W_{\rm fac}\}
 &=\frac{R I_s(q)S(v)^2}{D_q^{3/2}B(v)},\\
\exp\frac d{dq}\{B_U+\Re Q_1+F(q+d)+W_{\rm force}\}
 &=\frac{R I_s(q)S(v)^2}{D_q B(v)},\qquad d=v-q.
\end{split}                                                      \tag{17}
\]
These retain the incoming carrier in (6) and the selected factor
exactly once. In particular the forcing branch has a different
power of \(D_q\).

Table (18) of the accepted profile theorem gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
q\text{ interval}&D_q\text{ upper bound}&I_s(q)>\\ \hline
[0,1/10]&1/2&6/7\\
[1/10,3/20]&1&1.
\end{array}
\tag{18}
\]
In the first line it gives \(I_s\ge\sqrt3/2>6/7\).
Both powers of \(D_q\) in (17) are no larger than \(D_q\)
on these intervals. With (14) and \(S(v)^2>11/8\), the two
multipliers are respectively greater than
\[
\frac67\frac{11}{8}\,2\frac79=\frac{11}{6},
\qquad \frac{11}{8}\frac79=\frac{77}{72}.
\tag{19}
\]
The joint rates therefore increase until the stopping point.
At either stopping edge the same-coordinate comparisons just
proved reduce the next rate to its real half profile. Theorem
(8) of the accepted profile source pays that endpoint, for both
selected alternatives, with deficit greater than \(1/100\).

Together with the last-profile reduction (13), these cases prove
the complete continuous upper-row comparison
\[
B_U(x)+\Re Q_1(s;a,y)+E(t;y,z)+W_\nu(y)
     <\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/100,
\quad 1/2\le t\le1,\quad \nu\in\{{\rm fac},{\rm force}\}.
\tag{20}
\]
The first main on the left is the original one, before its
optional clipping. Every endpoint equality in the partition is
covered by one of the adjacent comparisons.

There is also a version independent of the first factor. Define
the explicit endpoint envelope
\[
M_\nu(z)=\max_{0\le q'\le q_0}
 \max\{L_H+W_\nu(q'+iH),
                E(1/2;q'+iH,z)+W_\nu(q'+iH)\}.
\tag{20a}
\]
All its phases use the same fixed output and the original
continuous upper-side lift. Then
\[
 E(t;q+iH,z)+W_\nu(q+iH)\le M_\nu(z),
              \qquad 1/2\le t\le1,\quad0\le q\le q_0.
\tag{20b}
\]
For the only comparison in Section 3 that moved \(q\), omit
the first profile in (17). The two exact multipliers become
\[
 \frac{R S(v)^2}{D_q B(v)},\qquad
 \frac{R S(v)^2}{\sqrt{D_q}B(v)}.
\tag{20c}
\]
Both are greater than \(77/72\), since \(D_q<1\) throughout
\(q\le3/20\), \(R>1\), and (14), (16) give
\(S(v)^2/B(v)>77/72\) for \(1/5<v<3/10\).
Every other upper-row comparison was already independent of
the first profile. Its endpoint is a half or last profile at
a permitted \(q'\), proving (20b). In particular any constant
preceding rate may be attached to this endpoint envelope;
no transport of such a factor is required.

## 4. The finite sum, forcing and the positive-height seam

The original zero-safe numerator estimates and endpoint-uniform
sine-factorial estimate give, for every \(\tau>0\), uniformly
over all actual rows in (4),
\[
\left|Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)/P_j^2\right|
       \le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
                   e^{N\{E(j/N;y,z)+\tau\}}.
\tag{21}
\]
These are the finite estimates used in
[the reflected upper-block theorem, Section 4](research_mixed_next_lower_left_own_four_upper_rows.md)
and the original row sources it cites; here their denominator
multiplicity is the literal two in (6). No endpoint-maximization
claim from that own-four theorem is imported.

On any fixed \(\epsilon\le q\le q_0\), the full absolute
Fourier-weight bound in
[weight continuation, Section 5, the absolute bound following (19)](research_mixed_fourier_weight_continuation.md)
gives
\[
|d_N(Y)Y^{-N}|
 \le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
       e^{N\{\max(W_{\rm fac}(y),W_{\rm force}(y))+\tau\}}.
\tag{22}
\]
This keeps the interpolation remainder and the forcing. Apply
(20) to both branches, then (21)--(22), and sum the at most
\(N\) rows. Their count costs only a polynomial.

Here is an explicit finite extension to \(q=0\), without
assuming a canonical factorial symbol there. By
[upper radial seam, equations (4)--(6)](research_mixed_next_upper_radial_seam.md),
\[
R^{-N}|d_N(R)|\le1,\qquad
d_N(Y)=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{D}p_j(Y)/P_j.
\tag{23}
\]
The same source's finite product inequality gives, for
\(|Y-R|\le\varepsilon\),
\[
|p_j(Y)|\le |p_j(R)|e^{N\Omega(\varepsilon)},\qquad
\Omega(\varepsilon)=\log(1+\varepsilon/2)
 +\frac\varepsilon2\log(1+2/\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{24}
\]
For the circular arc \(Y=Re^{-2\pi iq}\), its radius is
unchanged. Using the positive representation in (23), rather
than dividing by \(d_N(R)\), gives
\[
R^{-N}|d_N(Y)|\le e^{N\Omega(\varepsilon)},\qquad
\mathcal U_N(Y,Z)\le e^{N\Omega(\varepsilon)}\mathcal U_N(R,Z).
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed \(R^{-N}N^{-1}\sum_j|p_j(R)/P_j|\le1\) is part of
the proof of (23). The first-main rate is uniformly continuous
as \(q\downarrow0\), with the original one-sided upper
logarithmic lift. The positive height \(H\) is fixed and
nonzero; every shifted real index stays inside its regular
strip. Therefore (20)--(21) at \(q=0\), together with
(25), cover a fixed sufficiently short initial arc, spending
an arbitrarily small part of the \(1/100\) reserve. They
overlap the compact-strip argument from (22).

Choose the total uniform finite tolerances, the two costs in
(25), and the first-profile continuity cost below \(1/200\).
This proves (5). At the final physical row \(j=D\), the
reflected index is \(1/N\), so the extra debit in (13)
vanishes at the rate scale; the uniform margin in the paid
last profile (20) supplies the required bound. The even half
row and the odd-level first upper row are both included in
(4), with their exact endpoint conventions. Outgoing numerator
zeros are retained by the upper estimate (21).

The same finite argument, applied to (20b), proves the
prefix-independent positive-block estimate
\[
 |d_N(Y)Y^{-N}|\,\mathcal U_N(Y,Z)
 \le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
       e^{N\{\max(M_{\rm fac}(z),M_{\rm force}(z))+\tau\}}
                                                    \tag{26}
\]
for every \(\tau>0\), uniformly on the closed \(q\) interval.
At its seam endpoint the forcing envelope includes \(W_{\rm
force}=0\), so (23)--(25) give precisely the required endpoint
extension. Formula (26) is a finite-source reduction to the
explicit half/last envelope, not an action comparison for an
arbitrary preceding factor.

No outside derivative or complete first-source conclusion is
inferred from the exponential first-profile factor in (5).
Those require the corresponding canonical amplitudes, all
remaining currents, and the other first incoming pieces.
The next lower physical block and middle canonical sum also
remain separate here. The old filter and the three signed
shared-contour contributions are unchanged.
