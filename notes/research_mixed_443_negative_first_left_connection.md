# The negative first left connection with a real second coordinate

Private written proof, 11 November 2026. The first unselected
contour can be continued from real coordinate \(11/50\) to
\(1/10\) at its original negative height and then vertically
to the real axis, while the second coordinate is real between
\(1/2\) and \(18/25\). The cap and both conditional kernels
remain complete finite sums.

The new comparison concerns the first upper sampling correction.
Its private height is finite. A direct radial inequality transports
it together with every cap profile, including the entire cap
private rectangle. The high physical rows retain their nonzero
reflected drift. Their last carrier is paid by a separate real
left-cut inequality.

Author-frozen after full proof and direct-source readback; not
adopted pending separate full proof and source review. Frozen sources
and public documents are unchanged. This is written mathematics only.

## 1. Original source and statement

Fix an actual positive three/four word of length at least four
beginning with \(4,4,3\), with precisely the reference, primitive,
markings and affine action of
[the complete core-pair theorem](research_mixed_443_core_first_complete_right_pair.md).
In particular
\[
 c=c_1\in(-39/500,-1/50),\quad
 h_e\in(-51/1000,-19/1000),\quad
 h_2\in(-51/500,-1/1000),
\]
\[
 P=[11/50,9/20],\quad z=b+ic_3,\quad b\in P,\quad
 -13/200\le c_3,h_3\le17/250,\quad
 q\in[1/2,18/25].
\]
The heights belong to one unchanged actual word. They are not
chosen independently to create a new critical point.

Put \(X=e^{-2\pi ia}\), \(Y=e^{-2\pi iq}\), \(Z=e^{-2\pi iz}\).
For \(\zeta=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), retain
\[
 P_j=(\zeta;\zeta)_j,\quad p_j(V)=(\zeta V;\zeta)_j,
\]
\[
 U_{1,N}(w,X)=\sum_{i<N}H_N(w,\zeta^{-i})p_i(X)/P_i^2,\qquad
 C_{\epsilon,N}(X,Y)=\sum_{j<N}
        X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)/P_j^{2+\epsilon}.
\]
Here \(H_N\) is the original complete endpoint coefficient.
The polynomial \(d_N\) has degree less than \(N\) and interpolates
\(d_N(\zeta^j)=P_j^{-1}\). Define
\[
 {\cal K}_{\mu,N}
 =U_{1,N}(w,X)d_N(X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)
                  d_N(Y)Y^{-\mu N}C_{0,N}(Y,Z),
                     \qquad\mu=0,1.                         \tag{1}
\]
The first Fourier choice is unselected. There is no outgoing
halfweight because the third type is three.

Use the original phases
\[
 \Psi(e,a)=S(e)+g(e+a)-g(a)-g(e)-\frac{\pi i}{2}(e-e^2),
\]
\[
 Q_\epsilon(s;a,q)=g(s+a)+g(s+q)-(2+\epsilon)g(s)
                              -g(a)-g(q)+2\pi ias,
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
where \(g'=\operatorname{Log}(1-e^{-2\pi i u})\) on its original
marking. Write \(J(t)=\Re g(t)\) on the real axis, continued
periodically by its continuous real primitive, and
\(G_h(t)=\Re g(t-ih)\), \(h\ge0\).
Let \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\) and
\[
 \delta=\min\{(9/20-\Re r_2)^2/250000000,10^{-6}\}>0
\]
be exactly the action and reserve in the core-pair source.

**Theorem.** Uniformly on
\[
 a\in[1/10,11/50]+ic
       \quad\hbox{or}\quad a=1/10+ih,\quad c\le h\le0,
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
both second choices, both parities, and a sufficiently small fixed
ordinary-color disk about \(-2\) obey
\[
 |{\cal K}_{\mu,N}|
       \le C N^A\exp N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-\delta/64\}.           \tag{4}
\]
Fixed derivatives in that ordinary color satisfy the same rate on
smaller disks. Integrals over (3) keep the original density
\(+N\,da\) and their chosen orientation.

The theorem does not include the real first arc after \(1/2\)
or later second coordinates. In combination with the already proved
real interval from zero to \(1/10\), it closes the left end of the
unselected first path on this second-coordinate interval. It is
not a complete first-period or knot-volume theorem.

## 2. The exact comparison available at the old first face

At \(a_+=11/50+ic\), the core-pair proof supplies bounds for
the complete first conditional contour with lower height \(-1/5\),
actual main height \(h_2\), upper height
\[
                 h_U=\frac{\log2}{4\pi},
\]
left cut \(1/8\), and a fixed right cut \(r_0<1/2\) sufficiently
close to one half. It retains both zero-mode joins, all four
weighted vertical halves, and every physical row.
Its third factor is the complete source in
[the paired-upper theorem](research_mixed_443_paired_upper_prewall_currents.md).

We use those proofs before summing the finite currents.
The main and real-cut calibrations at \(q=1/2\), with any cap
alternative and every third profile, have more than \(20\delta\)
of common margin by Sections 2 and 6 of
[the midpoint theorem](research_mixed_443_complete_second_half_face.md).
Section 2 of the core-pair proof traces the additional first-half
margin \(1/30\) at this same real second face.
The first upper correction has its independent reserve \(1/162\)
before the fixed-cut allowances. Its subsequent paired transport
retains a positive fixed reserve.

On this unselected first plateau the cap's original supporting
plane, with its original half allocation, may be retained as a
single preceding ceiling. This is explicit in Section 2 of the
midpoint proof. Its further second-coordinate transports do not
differentiate that ceiling. The complete core-pair argument therefore
also applies with this plane in place of an individual cap profile.

For the true cap end the resulting extra reserve is greater than
\(7/40\). Section 3 of
[the joint low/half proof](research_mixed_early_joint_low_tail.md)
establishes it at \(x=11/50\): the bare cap plane exceeds \(79/100\),
the true-end ceiling is below \(123/200\), and retaining the
allocated halfweight makes the comparison at least as favorable.
The other first halfweight, when attached, is common to both sides.
At the selected old second face the same allocated-plane comparison
is explicitly equation (9) of
[the earlier negative-left proof](research_mixed_complete44_second_selected_negative_left_connection.md):
its true-end version retains this \(7/40\) separately from the
conditional row reserves. The midpoint proof's second-coordinate
transports keep that preceding ceiling fixed. Thus the use of the
plane here does not replace a cap profile by an uncalibrated larger
number during the shared-height comparison.

All these are comparisons of individual rates and supporting planes,
not deductions from the modulus of a cancelling finite sum.

## 3. Transport at nonpositive private heights

The [independent-height cap theorem](research_mixed_cap_early_fixed_height_full_source.md)
gives the complete cap envelope on (3). At the negative horizontal
it consists of the five regular alternatives
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 A_0(a)=\max_{.27\le u\le.49}\Re\Psi(u+ih_e,a),&
 A_L(a)=\Re\Psi(.27,a),\quad A_R(a)=\Re\Psi(.49,a),\\
 A_-(a)=\max_u\Re\Psi(u-.055i,a)-.11\pi,&
 A_+(a)=\max_u\Re\Psi(u+.04i,a)-.08\pi
\end{array}
\]
and
\[
                   A_*=\frac35+\frac{\log4}{100}<\frac{123}{200}.
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
There is no additional upper-row allowance for own type four.

The full first Fourier weight has rate \(-\Re g(a)\).
Indeed \(a=x+ic\) is in the lower half-plane, and
\(J(x)\le G_{|c|}(x)<0\) for \(0<x<1/2\); its forcing
alternative zero is no larger. The full second Fourier weight
has rate zero for real \(q\ge1/2\), and both second monomials
have unit modulus.

Put \(r=e^{2\pi c}\), and fix a cap private point
\(e=u+i\xi\) in its entire rectangle. For a first private point
\(s=t+i\eta\), set
\[
 N_e=|1-r e^{2\pi\xi}e^{-2\pi i(u+x)}|,\quad
 N_s=|e^{-2\pi\eta}-r e^{-2\pi i(t+x)}|,\quad
 D=|1-r e^{-2\pi ix}|.
\]
Exact differentiation, including the chirp and whole first weight,
gives
\[
 \partial_x\{\Re\Psi(e,a)+\Re Q_1(s;a,q)-\Re g(a)\}
                         =\log(N_eN_s/D^3).                  \tag{6}
\]
For \(\eta\le0\), \(1/8\le t\le1/2\), this derivative is
strictly positive. This is the pointwise inequality of Section 2 of
[the complete early central source](research_mixed_early_joint_central_source.md).
Its proof uses the full cap rectangle and no outgoing coordinate:
the incoming factor \(e^{-2\pi\eta}\ge1\) only improves the
real-private-index inequality in the joint low/half proof.

Consequently every regular cap profile times a first main, lower
correction, real cut, or nonpositive-height joining point transports
to \(a_+\) without increase. All sampling penalties are constant
in this transport. The shifted \(q\)-numerator may cross a period,
but its multiplier is independent of \(a\), so (6) is unchanged.

For the true-end alternative the derivative is
\(\log(N_s/D^2)>-\log2\). Indeed at \(\eta=0\) its numerator
exceeds \(D\), since \(t+2x<1\); increasing
\(e^{-2\pi\eta}\) only increases it. Also \(D<2\).
The backward cost is less than
\[
                \frac3{25}\log2<\frac{21}{250}.               \tag{7}
\]
It is paid by the \(7/40\) reserve in Section 2, leaving
\(91/1000\). Thus true ends are included in every such transport.
In particular the first half carrier at \(q=1/2\) retains the
core proof's additional \(1/30\) margin.

## 4. A radial inequality for the finite upper correction

For the actual first height,
\[
                         \frac35<r<\frac9{10}.                \tag{8}
\]
The upper bound follows from \(c<-1/50\). For the lower bound,
\(2\pi|c|<1/2\) and \(e^{1/2}<5/3\), the latter following
from the positive exponential-series bound
\(e^v\le1+v+v^2/[2(1-v/3)]\) at \(v=1/2\).
The whole cap rectangle has \(R=e^{2\pi\xi}>7/10\), as proved
by the same series in Section 3 of
[the old zero-face proof](research_mixed_selected_four_four_zero_height_face.md).

The first upper maximum occurs at \(t\le2/5\).
For \(2/5\le t\le1/2\), the folded incoming square is less
than \((5/3+3/2)^2<11\), the outgoing square is less than
\(3\), since \(q+t\in[9/10,61/50]\), and the own square
\[
                    3-2\sqrt2\cos2\pi t
\]
exceeds \(5\). Its squared private force is therefore less
than \(33/125<1\). This remains its derivative after the exact
period multiplier has canceled the upper sampling penalty.
Thus the entire upper line is bounded by its restriction to
\(1/8\le t\le2/5\).

We prove (6) positive on that restriction, and also on the
upper left vertical. More generally take
\[
 1/8\le t\le2/5,\qquad 0\le\eta\le h_U,\qquad
 p=e^{-2\pi\eta}\in[1/\sqrt2,1].
\]
Every fixed cap private point in the full rectangle is allowed.
The derivative in (6) decreases with \(x\), because its next
derivative divided by \(\pi\) is
\[
 \Re\cot\pi(e+a)+\Re\cot\pi(s+a)-3\Re\cot\pi a<0.               \tag{9}
\]
The first two terms are less than \(3/5\) and \(5/4\):
their real parts are at least \(.37\) and \(.225\), and a
positive shifted real cotangent is at most the real cotangent.
For the second witness use
\(\cot(9\pi/40)<13/11<5/4\), from
\(\tan(\pi/40)<1/12\).

Here \(\cosh(2\pi c)<8/7\), by the cosh series and
\(2\pi|c|<1/2\). Its product with
\(\cos(\pi/5)<13/16\) is less than one, so
\(x\mapsto\Re\cot\pi(x+ic)\) decreases on the stated interval.
At \(x=11/50\), the numerator exceeds \(6/7\), while the
denominator is less than one: use
\(\cos(11\pi/25)>37/200>1/7\).
Thus \(\Re\cot\pi a>6/7\), proving (9).
All these trigonometric witnesses follow from the sine and
cosine inequalities already given in Section 3 of
[the selected early proof](research_mixed_443_selected_early_first_right_pair.md).

It remains to check (6) at \(x=11/50\).
Since \(u+x\in[.49,.71]\) and \(t+x\in[.345,.62]\),
\[
 \cos2\pi(u+x)<-6/25,\quad
 \cos2\pi(t+x)<-1/2,\quad \cos2\pi x>37/200.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac{N_e^2}{r}>
 f(r):=\frac1r+\frac{49}{100}r+\frac{42}{125},\qquad
 \frac{N_s^2}{r}>\sqrt2+\frac7{10}>\frac{211}{100},
\]
\[
 \frac{D^6}{r^2}<
 d(r):=\frac{(1+r^2-37r/100)^3}{r^2}.                         \tag{10}
\]
The numerator bound is smallest at \(p=1/\sqrt2\);
then use \(1/(2r)+r\ge\sqrt2\).
The strict witness \(\sqrt2>141/100\) follows by squaring.

On \(3/5\le r\le2/3\), \(f(r)>2\).
The sign of \(d'(r)\) is that of
\(4r^2-37r/100-2\), which is negative there. Hence
\[
 d(r)\le d(3/5)=\frac{184220009}{45000000}<\frac{21}{5}
                <f(r)\frac{211}{100}.                        \tag{11}
\]
On \(2/3\le r\le9/10\), \(f\) decreases and
\[
 f(r)\ge\frac{10}{9}+\frac{777}{1000}>\frac{151}{80}.
\]
The polynomial determining the sign of \(d'\) increases on
this interval. Thus \(d\) has no interior maximum, and
\[
 d(2/3)=\frac{156590819}{40500000}<\frac{199}{50},\qquad
 d(9/10)=\frac{3222118333}{810000000}<\frac{199}{50}.
\]
Finally
\[
             \frac{151}{80}\frac{211}{100}
                    =\frac{31861}{8000}>
                      \frac{199}{50}=\frac{31840}{8000}.      \tag{12}
\]
Equations (10)--(12) prove \(N_e^2N_s^2>D^6\).
Together with (9), they prove (6) positive throughout the
early first interval.

This transports every regular cap profile times the upper horizontal
or upper-left vertical to its paid value at \(a_+\).
No restriction on the cap maximizer was made.
For a true cap end, \(D^2<3/2\) and \(N_s>4/5\), so
\[
                         \log(N_s/D^2)>-\log2.
\]
To verify the latter bound, the smallest possible square is bounded
below using \(p\ge1/\sqrt2,r\ge3/5\) and cosine below \(1/6\):
\(p^2+r^2-pr/3>71/100>16/25\).
Both partial derivatives of this quadratic are positive on those
radius intervals; at their lower endpoints use \(1/\sqrt2<3/4\).
The same cost (7) is therefore paid from the true-end reserve.

At the limiting right private cut \(t=1/2\), every upper weighted
vertical rate is bounded by its real cut. After retaining the period
multiplier, the outgoing argument is nonnegative, and the incoming
argument plus the original chirp \(2\pi x\) is nonnegative.
These are exactly the signs in Section 5 of the core-pair proof;
they use \(0<x\le1/2\), with no lower bound \(x\ge11/50\).
Uniform continuity of the integrated rates allows the fixed cut
\(r_0<1/2\), with an arbitrarily small allowance.
The real half profile was already transported in Section 3.

## 5. Every first physical row, including its last carrier

For the first physical rate retain
\[
 E_2(t;a,q)=I_a(t)+J(q+t)-J(q)-3J(t)-2\pi ct,\quad
 I_a(t)=\int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(a+v)}|\,dv.                  \tag{13}
\]
All zeros are integrated, including after a numerator wraps.

The low rates increase to \(t=1/8\). As in the midpoint proof,
split at \(t=1/10\): the folded incoming modulus exceeds
\(1/2\) and \(9/10\), respectively, while the own denominator
cube is below \(1/4\) and \((4/5)^3\).
On the present second interval the outgoing modulus exceeds \(4/5\).
Both products strictly exceed the own denominator cube.
These comparisons include the index zero by continuity.

Put \(t=1-v\), \(d=1-q\), and \(T=-2\pi c>0\).
The exact reflected expression is
\[
 E_2(1-v;a,q)=G_{|c|}(x-v)-G_{|c|}(x)
       +J(d)-J(d+v)+3J(v)+T(1-v).                            \tag{14}
\]
For \(v\le1/10\le x\), monotonicity of \(G_{|c|}'\) on
\([0,1/2]\) and \(G_{|c|}(v)\ge J(v)\) give a first difference
at most \(-J(v)\). The second difference is at most
\(-v\log(3/2)\). Thus this range is bounded by \(T\).

For \(1/10\le v\le1/2\), the
[all-angle leg inequality](research_mixed_all_angle_high_row_sign_class.md)
gives, with \(H_2(a)=E_2(1/2;a,1/2)\),
\[
 E_2(1-v;a,q)\le H_2(a)+F_d(v)+J(v)+\pi|c|(1-2v),
 \qquad F_d(v)=J(d)+J(v)-J(d+v).
\]
The paired-upper proof has
\(F_d(v)\le-9(1-2v)/40\).
Also \(J(1/10)<-1/10\), and convexity of \(J\) up to one
half gives \(J(v)\le-(1-2v)/8\).
Since \(\pi|c|<1/4<7/20\), the remaining sum is nonpositive.
Consequently
\[
                E_2(t;a,q)\le\max\{H_2(a),T\},
                                \qquad1/2\le t\le1.          \tag{15}
\]
This includes all revived rows and the final continuous carrier.

The half carrier is paid by the transported extra \(1/30\).
For the last one we prove the following direct bound:
\[
                       E_2(1/8;a,1/2)>T+\frac1{50}
                           \qquad(1/10\le x\le11/50).        \tag{16}
\]
Write its incoming numerator with the chirp folded in, as
\(|r^{-1}-e^{-2\pi i(x+v)}|\).
The denominator and outgoing half-input integrals satisfy
\[
             -3J(1/8)>93/200,\qquad
       \int_0^{1/8}\log(2\cos\pi v)\,dv>1/14.
\]
For the first bound use \(\sin\pi v\le\pi v\),
\(\pi<22/7\), and \(\log(14/11)>6/25\).
For the second use \(2\cos(\pi/8)>9/5\) and
\(\log(9/5)>4/7\).

If \(r\ge2/3\), the incoming folded modulus exceeds \(3/5\),
so its integral exceeds \(-3/40\).
The positive atanh series gives
\[
 T\le\log(3/2)
   <\frac25+\frac23\sum_{j\ge1}(1/5)^{2j+1}
   =\frac{73}{180}<\frac{41}{100}.
\]
The difference in (16) is therefore greater than
\(1/14-1/50=9/175>1/50\).

If \(r\le2/3\), put \(R_0=r^{-1}\ge3/2\).
Since \(x+v\in[.1,.345]\) and \(\cos(\pi/5)<13/16\),
\[
 |R_0-e^{-2\pi i(x+v)}|^2
    >R_0^2+1-\frac{13}{8}R_0\ge\frac{13}{16}>
                                               \left(\frac9{10}\right)^2.
\]
Its logarithmic integral exceeds \(-1/72\), because
\(\log(9/10)>-1/9\). Also \(T<1/2\).
The difference is now greater than
\[
       \frac{93}{200}-\frac1{72}+\frac1{14}-\frac12
                      =\frac{71}{3150}>\frac1{50}.
\]
This proves (16), without transporting a last-row bound from
an outgoing-angle range which does not include the real half point.

Every third main or lower maximum increases by less than \(1/4240\)
from its half-face value; the upper maximum never increases.
The third real-left, half and last alternatives are constant or
smaller. These are the complete third-source comparisons of the
core-pair proof.
The half reserve \(1/30\) and last reserve \(1/50\) therefore
pay every pairing in (15), with strict room remaining.
For the last carrier use the original real-left calibration at
\(q=1/2\), which includes the whole early first path.
No value of a finite half or last row is divided out.

## 6. Finite assembly on the negative horizontal

Use inward half-grid cuts tending to \(1/8,r_0\), with actual
height \(h_2\), lower height \(-1/5\), and upper height \(h_U\).
The exact first row interpolant is
\[
 T_{1,N}(s;a,q)=e^{2\pi iNas}\Pi_N(s+a)\Pi_N(s+q)
                           E_N(a)E_N(q)E_N(s)^3,
                    \qquad E_N=1/\Pi_N.
\]
Its split-cotangent identity retains the \(+N\) zero-mode
horizontal, both unweighted joins, the lower and upper corrections
with their original \(N/(2i)\) coefficients and orientations,
and all four weighted vertical halves.

The nonpositive-height pieces are paid by Section 3.
Section 4 pays the upper horizontal, the upper left side,
and the limiting right-side comparison.
All remaining physical indices are in the low block, a fixed
collar below one half, or the high block in Section 5.
The collar has an arbitrarily small cost by continuity.

The exact continuation
\[
               \Pi_N(u+1)=(1-e^{-2\pi iNu})\Pi_N(u)
\]
is retained across the shifted second numerator wall.
For positive private height its multiplier cancels the
corresponding upper penalty in precisely the same way as in the
core-pair proof. Its finite one-step product argument gives uniform
upper rates through that wall and height zero.
The incoming shifted numerator \(s+a\) remains in its regular
real strip; all denominator strips stay compact and regular.
The physical-row bounds follow from the
[zero-safe product argument](research_mixed_conditional_contour_allowance.md),
with arbitrary fixed allowances and no division by a numerator zero.

The complete third kernel is unchanged and independent of this
first-coordinate transport. Its physical rows, three horizontals,
two zero joins and four weighted halves are exactly those already
retained in the paired-upper source.
Each cap alternative and the full first and second Fourier
weights has been included before summing those finite pieces.
The common raw margins exceed \(\delta\); the independent
upper, half, last and true-end reserves pay the displayed costs.
All fixed cuts and collars can therefore be chosen before \(N\)
so that a positive fraction of that margin remains.

## 7. The negative first vertical

Keep one common complete envelope for
\(a=1/10+ih\), \(c\le h\le0\).
Its cap alternatives are the same five profiles and the constant
true-end ceiling (5).
Its first conditional terms are every fixed private current
just used, the real left cut, the half carrier
\(E_2(1/2;a,1/2)\), and the last carrier \(-2\pi h\).
All third profiles and the whole second weight are independent
of \(h\).

Each regular cap and first current is convex in \(h\).
Indeed
\[
 \Re\cot\pi(1/10+ih)>
       \frac{7/12}{\,8/7-4/5\,}=\frac{245}{144}>\frac32,
\]
whereas every shifted real cotangent is at most \(3/2\):
the cap shifted real parts lie in \([.37,.59]\), and the
first conditional ones in \([.225,.6]\).
The all-radius cotangent estimate is Section 4 of
[the positive-join envelope](research_mixed_first_positive_join_convex_envelope.md).
Thus
\[
 \partial_h^2\Re\Psi(e,a)
     =\pi\{\Re\cot\pi a-\Re\cot\pi(e+a)\}>0,
\]
\[
 \partial_h^2\Re Q_1(s;a,q)
     =\pi\{\Re\cot\pi a-\Re\cot\pi(s+a)\}>0.
\]
The chirp has zero second derivative. The second-numerator period
multiplier is independent of \(h\).
The half carrier has the same formula with \(s=1/2\);
the last carrier is affine. The full unselected first weight
has convex rate \(-\Re g(a)\), and the cap end is constant.
Sums and maxima therefore give a convex complete envelope.

This envelope bounds the finite source at every intermediate
height. The independent-height cap theorem applies throughout;
the same low-row argument and the reflected proof (14)--(15)
apply with \(c\) replaced by \(h\).
Every private current is retained explicitly, so no new private
curvature assertion is needed.
At \(h=c\) Section 6 pays the envelope.
At \(h=0\) Section 3 of
[the positive first left-connection theorem](research_mixed_443_positive_first_left_connection.md)
pays precisely these complete profiles. At height zero the two
first Fourier monomials have the same modulus and upper rate.
Choose identical sufficiently near-half cuts and sufficiently small
allowances in the two endpoint constructions.
Convexity pays the whole negative vertical.

Finally choose the finite-source, fixed-cut and ordinary-color
allowances within the common raw margin, leaving the gap
\(\delta/64\) in (4). The cap estimates hold for every real
ordinary color in \([-2,2]\). Its exact degree is less than
\(N\), while all other factors are color-independent.
The same interval polynomial continuation used by the direct
sources gives a fixed disk with arbitrarily small rate cost,
and Cauchy's formula gives fixed derivatives on smaller disks.
Finite current lengths, densities and row counts cost only
polynomial factors. Both parities and the literal last index
remain included.
