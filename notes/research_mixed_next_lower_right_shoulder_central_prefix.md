# The complete central first block on the lower right shoulder

Research proof, 8 November 2026. The complete first cap times the
complete type-four conditional kernel has its original allocated
supporting plane when the first incoming coordinate stays on its
central interval and the outgoing real coordinate extends from
\(9/20\) to \(1/2\), at the actual negative height. Every conditional
physical row, alias and joining current is retained.

This is a complete finite block estimate, not a claim about the other
first-incoming paths or the next kernel. No numerical evaluation or
modification of a frozen source is used.

\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{1}

For \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(r;r)_j\), \(p_j(V)=(rV;r)_j\),

The attached stationary equation is \(T_0'(a_*)+p_L=0\).
The complete all-real-color cap bound with rate \(B_U(x)\) is
the original theorem traced in
[the right-tail proof, Section 1](research_mixed_next_unselected_incoming_right_tail.md).
It already includes every private index and current.

\boxed{\quad

The same rate, with polynomial cost, bounds the central first-incoming

real-color derivative. The corresponding integer-weight holomorphic
product has local fixed mixed-derivative bounds after shrinking
outside neighborhoods within the strict reserve. There is no division

## 2. Main support and the smaller index strip

The original bare ordinary curvature theorem and its smaller-cut and
half-index extensions give
\[

[the smaller own-four cut](research_mixed_four_left_cut_extension.md),
and [the full concavity deduction, Section 2](research_mixed_second_selected_horizontal_concavity.md).

There is a negative semidefinite extension to \(a_R\in P\),
\(s_R\in[1/8,.5]\), \(y_R\in[.45,.5]\). Indeed, with
\(V(s,z)=g(s+z)-g(s)-g(z)\),
\[
F=V(s,a)+V(s,y)-g(s)-\tfrac12g(y)+2\pi ias.                     \tag{7}
\]
For \(s_R\ge.15\), the original pair theorem keeps
\(-\Re V(s,a)''\) positive on its active variables. The outgoing
pair has \(C_s,C_y\ge0\) and \(C_{sy}\le0\), where
\(C_z=\Re\cot\pi z\), because \(s_R+y_R\in[.575,1]\).
Its negative real Hessian is therefore positive semidefinite.
The remaining own terms in (7) add nonnegative diagonals.
For \(1/8\le s_R\le.15\), the smaller-cut Schur proof gives
a positive incoming diagonal and incoming Schur cost less than
\(187/120\). The outgoing cost is nonpositive since \(C_{sy}\le0\);
its diagonal is positive here. The index cotangent exceeds \(21/20\),
so the index Schur complement is greater than
\[
3(21/20)-187/120=191/120>0.                                    \tag{8}
\]
At \(s_R=y_R=.5\), use the continuous lower-half boundary value.
Only semidefiniteness is asserted at this corner.

A straight real segment from the actual reference first stays in
(6), then in this extension. If
\(L=(.45-\alpha)/(q-\alpha)\), its first fraction \(L\) supplies,

At \(q=.45\) use \(L=1\). The original index equation and the cap

\le A_*+p(q-\alpha)-3\cdot10^{-9},

\]

The first factor is at least the own denominator modulus, since

than one because \(\cos2\pi u>1/2\) and \(0<r_s\le1\).
Thus the additional main strip increases to \(u=1/8\), where

## 4. Height-uniform curvature on the retained interval

For all targets (1)--(2),
\[
\Re Q_{ss}=\pi\{C(a+s)+C(y+s)-3C(s)\}<0,
\quad \ell(q)\le u=\Re s\le1/2,\quad h_s\le\sigma=\Im s<\infty .
                                                               \tag{21}
\]
At a numerator boundary use one-sided regular limits.

Here is a direct proof. For \(u\ge.28\), both shifted real
coordinates are in \([.5,1]\), so their cotangents are
nonpositive, whereas the own cotangent is nonnegative.
At \(u=.5\) the incoming shifted cotangent is strictly negative.

For \(.075\le u\le.125\), the following guards apply:
\[
\sin2\pi u>9/20,\quad \cos2\pi u>7/10,\quad
\cos2\pi(x+u)<-1/4,\quad -\sin2\pi(q+u)>9/20.                   \tag{22}
\]
The third is needed only when the incoming cotangent is positive.
For instance
\(\sin(.15\pi)>\sin(15/32)>15/32-(15/32)^3/6>9/20\);
and \(\sin(.09\pi)>\sin(9/32)>1/4\).
For \(h_s\le\sigma\le.102\), \(\cosh(2\pi\sigma)<5/4\).
A positive incoming shifted cotangent is less than its real value,
which is less than one. The outgoing cotangent is nonpositive, and
\(3C(s)>27/11>1\).

For \(\sigma\ge.102\), set \(A_\sigma=\cosh(2\pi\sigma)\).
The actual incoming radius bound and the negative outgoing height give
\[
\cosh2\pi(\sigma+h_a)>(5/8)A_\sigma,\qquad
\cosh2\pi(\sigma+h_y)\le A_\sigma .
\]
Thus, if the incoming cotangent is positive,
\[
C(a+s)<\frac1{(5/8)A_\sigma+1/4},\quad
-C(y+s)>\frac{9/20}{A_\sigma+1},\quad
C(s)>\frac{9/20}{A_\sigma-7/10}.                               \tag{23}
\]
The desired comparison follows from
\[
\frac{27/20}{A_\sigma-7/10}+\frac{9/20}{A_\sigma+1}
>\frac1{(5/8)A_\sigma+1/4}.                                   \tag{24}
\]
After multiplying positive denominators, its difference is
\[
\tfrac18 A_\sigma^2+\tfrac{51}{64}A_\sigma+\tfrac{767}{800}>0.
                                                               \tag{25}
\]

For \(.125\le u\le.28\), retain only positive incoming cotangents.
Here \(\sin2\pi u>7/10\), \(\cos2\pi u>-1/5\), and
\(\cos2\pi(x+u)<-1/2\). For \(\sigma\le.102\), they give
\(3C(s)>42/29>1>C(a+s)\). For \(\sigma\ge.102\), use
\[
3C(s)>\frac{21/10}{A_\sigma+1/5}
>\frac1{(5/8)A_\sigma+1/2}>C(a+s).                             \tag{26}
\]
The middle inequality is immediate after multiplication by
the positive denominators. The outgoing cotangent remains
nonpositive. This proves (21).

## 5. The two aliases and all joining currents

The original argument identity is
\[
\Im Q_s=\arg(1-e^{-2\pi i(a+s)})+\arg(1-e^{-2\pi i(y+s)})
 -3\arg(1-e^{-2\pi is})+2\pi x.                               \tag{27}
\]
For \(\sigma\le0\), the outgoing argument is nonpositive,
the own argument is nonnegative, and the incoming argument

\[
\Im Q_s<\max\{\pi-2\pi u,2\pi x\}\le9\pi/10.                  \tag{28}
\]
For \(\sigma\ge h_s\), (21) makes \(\Im Q_s\) decreasing
with \(\sigma\). Its original upper-height limit is
\[
-\pi-2\pi q+2\pi u\ge-37\pi/20,                               \tag{29}
\]
so this is a lower bound at every finite height in that range.
No argument is reset modulo \(2\pi\).

Keep auxiliary heights \(-1/5\) and \(8\). For each fixed \(u\),
(28)--(29) give, relative to the main height \(h_s\),
\[
\begin{split}
\Re Q(u-i/5)-2\pi/5
 &\le\Re Q(u+ih_s)-11\pi/50,\\
\Re Q(u+8i)-16\pi

\end{split}                                                   \tag{30}

For the second inequality use \(-h_s<51/500\).
Both losses are strict and fixed. When \(u<1/8\), first use

The cap and the outgoing halfweight allocation are index-independent.

The two lower weighted vertical sides decrease away from their

\(h_s\) to zero, (21) gives vertical convexity, so their maxima
are at their two paid endpoints. All six vertical and joining

## 6. Exact finite contour, numerator wall and derivatives

Put \(L_N(q)=\lceil N\ell(q)\rceil\), \(M_N=\lceil N/2\rceil\).
The exact middle rows use the original interpolation
\[
T_N(s;a,y)=e^{2\pi iNas}
 \Pi_N(a+s)\Pi_N(y+s)E_N(a)E_N(y)E_N(s)^3.                      \tag{31}
\]
At \(s=j/N\) it is the literal summand (3).
The half-grid cotangent decomposition has the original \(+N\)
main density, two horizontal remainders, four weighted vertical
sides and two unweighted joins. All nine pieces were bounded above.
The physical rows outside that middle range were bounded in Section 3.

The closed endpoint \(q=u=.5\) reaches the numerator wall \(y+s=1\).
Only a zero-safe upper bound is used there. Exact reflection and
the reciprocal midpoint estimate in
[the canonical-row source, Section 3](research_repeated_two_block_canonical_row_source.md)
give
\[
|G_N(Nw)|\le C N^B e^{-N\Re J_{\rm side}(1-w)}
\quad\hbox{on compact subsets of }0<\Re w<2.                  \tag{32}
\]
On the present prewall side the rate equals \(\Re J(w)\),
including its continuous value at \(w=1\). The original chirp
converts it to \(\Re g(w)\) for \(\Pi_N\). The exact reciprocal
argument is \(1-w-1/(2N)\), retaining both terms; half-grid
changes cost \(O(\log N/N)\) in the rate, also across the wall.
All denominator arguments in (31) stay in regular compact strips.
The one-sided argument inequalities (27)--(29) integrate to the
same continuous profile at the wall. The same source applicability
is detailed in
[the initial-shoulder wall audit](research_mixed_next_initial_right_shoulder_rows.md).
No relative expansion is asserted at the numerator zero.

The zero-safe finite estimates have arbitrary common rate
tolerances and polynomial prefactors. Choose them inside (9);
all other current reserves are larger. This proves (5), including
every parity, physical endpoint and original density factor.

Fixed real-color derivatives follow by Markov after the complete
ordinary-color polynomial is bounded. For local complex derivatives,
use the integer-weight holomorphic square
\((U_{0,N}C_{1,N})^2d_N(Y)\), fix the contours and cuts at their
base parameters, and shrink neighborhoods within the strict gaps.
Cauchy's formula then gives fixed mixed jets with polynomial cost.
No internal Fourier factor is divided out.

The theorem supplies the complete central first-incoming block
on the right shoulder. It neither substitutes that block for the
whole first contour nor estimates the next kernel by division.
