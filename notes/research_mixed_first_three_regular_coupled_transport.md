# A paid coupled regular transport for the type-three first cap

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. On the actual early first-minimum
taper, the first type-three cap and adjoining own-four central/cut
envelope have coupled derivative greater than \(1/100\) whenever the
changing neighbor has real part at least \(.28\). Both neighbor
incidences and the full following regular rectangle are retained.

The proof uses the real main line of the existing complete finite
envelope, with its original finite cuts unchanged. It keeps the
actual coefficient-radius relation. It does not import the
first-coordinate monotonicity of one lower support into a different
support.

Interior high physical rows, the lower-neighbor sector and the
complete following finite-source estimate remain separate. No
comparison with the calibrated boundary value is asserted.

## 1. Exact coupled source and derivative

Retain the actual first-minimum assumptions, allocations and following
regular profile of
[the two-incidence theorem](research_mixed_first_attached_two_incidence_reduction.md)
and [the coupled seam theorem](research_mixed_first_attached_coupled_seam_transport.md).
The first own type is three and the adjoining own type is four:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 d=x-.1,\quad .1\le x\le.22,\quad .28\le z\le.5,\\
 a=x-i\lambda_a d,\quad b=z-i\lambda_b d,\qquad
 -7/40\le\lambda_a\le3/5,\quad0\le\lambda_b\le17/20,\\
 t=w+i\zeta,\quad .22\le w\le.49,\quad
                         -.108\le\zeta\le.068 .
 \end{gathered}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The actual upper cutoff remains inside \(\lambda_b\), which is the
same coefficient in its height and its derivative. The following
regular profile must independently be valid on this target domain.

The complete own-three cap envelope has already had its true ends
absorbed pointwise by
[the early endpoint theorem](research_mixed_first_three_early_endpoint_payment.md).
Its remaining real private index is \(e\in[.27,.49]\).
Choose the real main line for the adjoining ordinary envelope.
Its real maximizing index is denoted by \(\widehat v\).
The [one-third barrier](research_mixed_first_attached_real_index_third_barrier.md)
gives \(.22<\widehat v<1/3\), without moving the original finite
contour or deleting physical rows.

For a fixed real ordinary profile \(s=v\), write
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_0(e,v;x,\lambda_a)
 &=\Re(1-i\lambda_a)
     \{g'(e+a)+g'(v+a)-2g'(a)+2\pi iv\},\\
 B(v,t;z,h)
 &=\Im\{g'(v+z+ih)+g'(t+z+ih)
                           -3g'(z+ih)+2\pi it\}.
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{2}
\]
The exact derivative of the coupled regular profile is
\[
              H_0(e,v;x,\lambda_a)+
                         \lambda_b B(v,t;z,-\lambda_bd).
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
The original two halves at \(b\), both chirps and the cap allocation
are precisely those in (2). No separate neighboring source plane is
substituted.

**Theorem 1.** For every (1), every admitted following regular
profile and every maximizing cap/ordinary central pair,
the derivative (3) is strictly greater than \(1/100\).
Consequently the corresponding coupled regular envelope increases
by more than \((x_1-x_0)/100\) along each such first-minimum segment
\(.1\le x_0<x_1\le.22\).

## 2. A coefficient split restricts the active real index

\[

\[

To prove it, put \(q=e^{2\pi\lambda_a d}\),
\(r_b=e^{-2\pi\lambda_bd}\). The exact original derivative at

\[
 e^{\Re Q_{1,s}(.3;a,b)}
 \le\frac{(1+q)(1+r_b)}{(2\sin(.3\pi))^3}.
                                                                    \tag{6}
\]
For \(\lambda_a\le0\), the numerator is at most \(4\).
For \(\lambda_a\ge0\), (4) implies \(r_b\le q^{-1}\).

\[
                    2+q+q^{-1}\le\frac{169}{40}.

The exact pentagon value is

The quotient in (6) is consequently less than \(845/846\).

In the complementary case,

## 6. Exact envelope and boundary scope

The private cap maximum and the real ordinary maximum have the
pointwise stationarity and strict concavity already proved on their
compact intervals. The derivative bound may therefore be integrated
for their maximum; equivalently one may use the standard elementary
envelope inequality for the uniformly differentiable fixed profiles.
The following regular index can be kept fixed during integration
and then maximized over its unchanged admitted rectangle.

The original real-line central envelope dominates its original real
cuts and paid alias profiles at every target. Thus this comparison
does not need an independent favorable transport derivative for each
inactive cut or alias. It does not change the finite contour or
remove the interior physical high-row values from the complete
adjoining source bound. The true ordinary last row has its separate
already proved coupled payment.

The actual first marginal and the relative-pinning fee are unchanged.
On a protected compensated first-minimum segment, the distant cut
and synthetic completion remain fixed, exactly as in the previous
coupled seam theorem. The estimate proved here is the local
regular-source increase needed on the sector \(z\ge.28\).
It does not identify the value of the resulting real-main envelope
with the original calibrated plateau value.
The sector \(z<.28\), first own type four, interior high physical
profiles, following whole-source eligibility and final compensated
boundary comparison remain open.
