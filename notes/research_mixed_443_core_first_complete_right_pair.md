# A complete conditional pair on the core first paths of an actual 443 word

Private written proof, 11 November 2026. This note keeps the first
private right cut fixed near one half. Its outgoing numerator may
cross one period; the exact factorial recurrence, including its
multiplier, is retained there. This permits the complete first
conditional kernel, rather than only its upper sampling correction,
to be paired with the complete type-three kernel on a substantial
real second interval.

Author-frozen after full proof and direct-source readback. Independent
full proof/direct-source review is required before adoption. Its two
finite-upper-current inputs are separately frozen and remain subject
to their own reviews. No frozen source or public document is changed,
and no mathematical numerical evaluation is used.

## 1. Exact source and scope

Fix the original reference of an actual positive three/four word of
length \(m\ge4\) beginning with \(443\). Use the original primitive,
cap, ordinary markings and affine action of the complete half-face
theorem. In particular
\[
 P=[11/50,9/20],\quad H=1/100,\quad
 -39/500<c_1<-1/50,\quad -51/500<h_2<-1/1000,
 \quad -13/200\le h_3,c_3\le17/250.
 \tag{1}
\]
Put \(z=b+ic_3\), \(b\in P\), and let
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \nu=0&a\in[11/50,1/2]+ic_1\ \text{or}\ 1/2+i[c_1,0],\\
 \nu=1&a\in[11/50,1/2]+iH\ \text{or}\ 1/2+i[0,H].
 \end{array}
 \qquad 1/2\le q\le18/25.                              \tag{2}
\]
Every height is fixed by that same word. Write
\[
 Q_\epsilon(s;a,b)=g(s+a)+g(s+b)-(2+\epsilon)g(s)
                    -g(a)-g(b)+2\pi ias,
 \quad g'(w)=\operatorname{Log}(1-e^{-2\pi iw}),\quad g(0)=0.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
For \(\zeta=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(\zeta;\zeta)_j\), and
\(p_j(V)=(\zeta V;\zeta)_j\), the literal kernels are
\[
 U_{1,N}(w,X)=\sum_{i<N}H_N(w,\zeta^{-i})p_i(X)/P_i^2,
 \qquad C_{\epsilon,N}(X,Y)=\sum_{j<N}
          X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)/P_j^{2+\epsilon}.             \tag{4}
\]
Set \(X=e^{-2\pi ia},Y=e^{-2\pi iq},Z=e^{-2\pi iz}\).
There is no outgoing halfweight, since the third type is three.
The original calibrated action is
\[
 \begin{split}
 F_{123}&=\Psi(e,z_1)+Q_1(s_2;z_1,z_2)+Q_0(s_3;z_2,z_3)
                                            -g(z_1)-g(z_2),\\
 \Lambda_{123}(z)&=\Re F_{123,*}
   +\Re\{[g'(s_3^*+r_3)-g'(r_3)](z-r_3)\}.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{5}
\]
No constant, shared tangent or Fourier alternative is reset. Let
\(\delta>0\) have exactly the definition in equation (5) of the
complete half-face source:
\[
 \delta=\min\{\kappa(9/20-\Re r_2)^2/100,10^{-6}\},
                    \qquad\kappa=1/2500000.             \tag{6}
\]

**Theorem 1.** On a sufficiently small fixed ordinary-color disk
about \(-2\), uniformly on (1)--(2), both parities, and both
\(\mu\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 &|U_{1,N}(w,X)d_N(X)X^{-\nu N}C_{1,N}(X,Y)
                         d_N(Y)Y^{-\mu N}C_{0,N}(Y,Z)|\\
 &\hspace{15mm}\le C N^A
       \exp N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-\delta/4\}.             \tag{7}
 \end{split}
\]
Fixed ordinary-color derivatives have the same assertion on smaller
disks with polynomial costs. Integrating any stated first or second
piece retains its original \(+N\) density and literal orientation.
All finite rows and all contour terms of both conditional kernels
are included.

The theorem concerns only the first paths (2). The first portions
with real angle below \(11/50\), the subsequent real first arcs,
and the full first polynomial are not conclusions of this note.
Nor does it assert a complete second period or a knot-volume result.

## 2. The half-face calibration and two extra reserves

At \(q=1/2\), the complete half-face proof supplies a strict raw
calibration for each cap alternative, each complete first Fourier
upper alternative, and each pair of first/third main, real-cut,
half and last profiles. Its prepath proof retains more than
\(20\delta\) in the common comparison. Only this raw statement
is used, not a bound on a cancelling sum.

We also need the extra reserves for the first real half and last
carriers. They are, respectively,
\[
                         1/30\quad\hbox{and}\quad1/350.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
Here is their full source trace on precisely (2).
At the old second face \(q=9/20+iH\), the four-profile theorem,
Sections 2 and 4, gives first half/last losses \(1/30,1/50\)
for both first heights on \(P\), before any third profile is
attached. The right-half connection proof, Section 4, transports
each physical half or last profile, with the same cap choice, by a
nonpositive derivative from first angle \(9/20\) to \(1/2\).
Its Section 5, equation (25), makes cap-plus-high-row profiles
nonincreasing below height zero and nondecreasing above it.
Thus the corresponding endpoint surplus is preserved on each
native first half-height segment. The true cap-end comparisons
have their own larger reserves; their costs do not use (8).

The complete selected443 shoulder then transports the second
angle from \(9/20\) to \(1/2\) at height \(H\).
Its Section 4, equation (15), makes the first half pairing with
any third main strictly decrease; other third profiles have no
larger cost. The half surplus \(1/30\) survives. For the last
carrier that source's Section 2 reserves \(1/100\), and its
Section 4 spends less than \(1/140\). Its remaining extra is
therefore greater than \(1/350\), not \(1/100\).
Finally, equations (26)--(28) of the complete half-face source
make a high-first/regular-third or high-first/high-third pair
nondecreasing as the second height moves from zero to \(H\).
The cap and first weight are fixed in this motion, and the native
selected second weight has rate zero. Thus the real second half
face inherits (8). This trace includes the first half-height
segments; it does not manufacture a reserve from their common
finite-sum bound.

The complete cap keeps its five original private profiles, their
two penalties, both zero joins, all weighted sides, compact rows
and the separate true-end ceiling on each piece. Its full source
and its color continuation are exactly those already licensed on
(2) by these sources. None depends on \(q\).

## 3. Joint transport of negative first profiles

Fix a right cut \(r_0<1/2\), sufficiently close to one half,
before \(N\). The first main interval will remain
\([1/8,r_0]\), even after its outgoing numerator crosses a
period. For \(\sigma<0\), define
\[
 B^2_\sigma(q)=\max_{1/8\le t\le r_0}
                   \Re Q_1(t+i\sigma;a,q).              \tag{9}
\]
Below the axis the numerator uses its original global lower
factorial branch. This branch is periodic in its real argument;
it is not obtained by resetting the action.

Retain the third prewall interval and maxima from the paired-upper
source:
\[
 I_q=[1/8,\min(r_0,1-q-\varepsilon)],\quad
 B^3_\eta(q)=\max_{t\in I_q}\Re Q_0(t+i\eta;q,z),
 \quad L=13/200.                                         \tag{10}
\]
The positive \(\varepsilon\) is fixed and sufficiently small.
For \(-L\le\eta\le17/250\), and also for \(\eta=1/5\),
\[
 B^2_\sigma(q)+B^3_\eta(q)
                \le B^2_\sigma(1/2)+B^3_\eta(1/2).
                                                               \tag{11}
\]
The same statement holds if \(B^2_\sigma\) is replaced by the
first real left-cut rate at \(t=1/8\).

To prove it, put \(D=|1-e^{-2\pi iq}|\) and
\(N_2=|1-\rho e^{-2\pi i(q+t)}|\), \(0\le\rho\le1\).
Since \(q\le18/25<3/4\), the interval \(q+t\),
\(1/8\le t\le1/2\), lies between \(q\) and \(2-q\).
Its cosine is at least \(\cos2\pi q\). Convexity in \(\rho\)
reduces to \(\rho=0,1\), proving \(N_2\le D\), including
the postwall part of the interval. When \(q\le21/40\),
\[
                 N_2^2\le2+\sqrt2<7/2<31/8.            \tag{12}
\]
Indeed the minimum shifted cosine on
\([5/8,41/40]\) is \(-1/\sqrt2\).
The third maximizing-index proof in Sections 2--3 of the
paired-upper source gives \(N_3^2<81/20\), \(D^2>159/40\)
on this first range. Its displayed product inequality with the
larger first bound \(31/8\) proves the nonpositive derivative
of the sum. On the remaining range it gives \(N_3\le D\).
For the upper third line \(\eta=1/5\), that last comparison
holds throughout. The maxima are locally Lipschitz. At a moving
third right endpoint its velocity is nonpositive and its private
derivative has the appropriate nonnegative sign, so its extra
term is nonpositive. This proves (11) by almost-everywhere
differentiation and integration. The first interval is fixed.

At the half face the first lower alias at height \(-1/5\)
has a reserve greater than \(\pi/5\) against the same-index
actual main at height \(h_2\). On this half-face prewall
interval the all-height curvature is negative. Indeed, if the
incoming shifted cotangent is positive then \(t<7/25\), and
the two bounds (16) below hold with \(1/8\) replaced by
\(t\): the own sine is greater than \(7/10\), its cosine
greater than \(-1/5\), and the positive incoming sine is
less than \(7/8\), with shifted cosine below \(-1/2\).
Otherwise both shifted cotangents are nonpositive. The limiting
imaginary derivative below is \(2\pi x\); hence
\(\Im Q_{1,s}<2\pi\Re a\le\pi\). Hence the possible
height cost is at most \(\pi(1/5+h_2)<\pi/5\), whereas
the lower sampling penalty is \(2\pi/5\). Equation (11)
therefore pays this entire first lower horizontal paired with
each third horizontal. The third lower and upper alias reserves
are respectively \(31\pi/1000\) and \(323\pi/2000\),
as proved at the half face in the paired-upper source.

We record one weaker but useful unpaired third estimate:
\[
 B^3_\eta(q)\le B^3_\eta(1/2)+1/4240,
       \quad -L\le\eta\le17/250.                        \tag{13}
\]
Only \([1/2,21/40]\) can contribute an increase. There
\(N_3^2/D^2<54/53\), so its derivative is less than
\(\tfrac12\log(54/53)<1/106\). The interval has length
\(1/40\). Afterward the derivative is nonpositive. For
\(\eta=1/5\) there is no increase anywhere.

## 4. The first upper correction through its numerator wall

Put \(h_U=\log2/(4\pi)\), so the upper private radius is
\(\sqrt2\). On the prewall part, the paired-upper theorem
already pays the first upper correction paired with the complete
third kernel, with gap \(1/324\). We extend the first upper
line to the fixed right cut \(r_0\).

The normalized original factorial satisfies exactly
\[
 \Pi_N(w)=G_N(Nw)e^{(\pi i/2)[N(w-w^2)-w]},\qquad
 \Pi_N(w+1)=(1-e^{-2\pi iNw})\Pi_N(w).                 \tag{14}
\]
For \(t\ge d:=1-q\), the multiplier from
\(\Pi_N(q+t+ih_U)\) has upper rate \(2\pi h_U\).
It cancels precisely the upper sampling penalty. The resulting
rate is
\[
 \Re\{g(s+a)+g(s+q-1)-3g(s)-g(a)-g(q)+2\pi ias\},
                  \qquad s=t+ih_U.                     \tag{15}
\]
It agrees continuously with the prewall weighted rate at \(t=d\).
No multiplier is discarded or divided out.

The rate (15) strictly decreases in \(t\) on
\([d,1/2]\). Its squared private force has denominator
\(D_s^6\), \(D_s^2=3-2\sqrt2\cos2\pi t\).
The incoming folded square is less than \(11\):
\(e^{-2\pi h}<5/3\), \(\sqrt2<3/2\), so it is
less than \((5/3+3/2)^2<11\). The radius assertion follows
from \(\log(5/3)>49/96>39\pi/250\), by the positive
atanh series and \(\pi<22/7\).
If \(d\le t\le7/20\), then \(0\le t-d\le7/100\),
\(\cos2\pi(t-d)>9/10\), and the outgoing square is
less than \(12/25\); also \(D_s^2>3\). Thus the squared
force is less than \(44/225\). If \(7/20\le t\le1/2\),
then \(D_s^2>4\) and the outgoing square is less than \(3\),
because \(0\le t-d\le11/50<1/4\). The squared force is
then less than \(33/64\). Both are less than one.

Consequently every added postwall upper rate is no larger than
its wall value. The short gap between the nested prewall cut
and that wall costs an arbitrarily small allowance by continuity
of the integrated rates. The same applies when the fixed right
cut is still before the wall. The first upper correction on the
entire fixed interval, paired with complete \(C_{0,N}\),
therefore retains any prescribed gap smaller than \(1/324\).

## 5. Both first zero joins and all four weighted vertical halves

The first lower height is \(-1/5\), its actual main height is
\(h_2<0\), and its upper height is \(h_U\). The left cut
remains \(1/8\).

At this left cut \(\Re Q_{1,ss}<0\) at every height.
The outgoing shifted cotangent is negative. If the incoming one
is positive, its shifted real coordinate lies between \(.345\)
and \(.5\). With \(A=\cosh2\pi\eta\), the elementary
cotangent formula gives
\[
 3\Re\cot\pi(1/8+i\eta)>\frac{21/10}{A+1/5},\qquad
 \Re\cot\pi(1/8+a+i\eta)
                      <\frac{7/8}{(3/5)A+1/2}.          \tag{16}
\]
Here \(e^{-2\pi|h|}>3/5\), the shifted cosine is below
\(-1/2\), and its positive sine is below \(7/8\).
Cross multiplication has positive coefficients. Thus the height
rate is convex. The unweighted left join is bounded by its real
cut or actual-height endpoint; each weighted left half is bounded
by its real cut or its horizontal alias endpoint, because its
penalty is affine on either half-axis. All those endpoints have
already been paid.

For the right side first take the limiting real private point
\(t=1/2\). This is only a comparison of continuous rates;
the actual contour still has \(r_0<1/2\).
Write \(I=\Im Q_{1,s}\) with the original continuation.
For \(h_2\le\eta\le0\), the own argument is zero,
the incoming argument lies in \([-2\pi x,0]\), and the
postwall outgoing disk argument is nonnegative. The chirp adds
\(2\pi x\). Hence \(I\ge0\), so the unweighted right
join is bounded by its actual-height endpoint.

On the lower weighted half, \(-1/5\le\eta\le0\), the
incoming argument is nonpositive, the outgoing disk argument is
at most \(\pi/2\), and the chirp is at most \(\pi\).
Thus \(I\le3\pi/2<2\pi\). Its weighted rate decreases
away from the real cut. On the upper half use (14). After its
multiplier cancels the upper penalty, the outgoing argument is
that at \(q-1/2+i\eta\), and is nonnegative. The incoming
argument plus chirp is again nonnegative. Thus this weighted
rate also decreases away from the real cut. At \(q=1/2\)
the upper right-limit outgoing argument is \(+\pi\), so
the same inequality holds. None of these signs uses an erroneous
arbitrarily tall upper rectangle at the left cut.

All these integrated modulus rates are uniformly continuous on
the compact family, including their numerator-wall limits.
Choosing \(r_0\) sufficiently near one half therefore pays
every right vertical rate by the actual first main maximum or
the real half profile, with an arbitrarily small fixed allowance.
The actual main endpoint at \(t=1/2\) is likewise within
that allowance of the maximum over \([1/8,r_0]\).

For completeness, there is a uniform finite source through those
walls, not just a continuous limiting phase. Near a shifted
numerator wall choose a small fixed \(d_0>0\) and an integer
\(k_N\) with \(k_N/N\to d_0\), so that
\(w-k_N/N\) stays in a compact original strip. Iterating
the original one-step recurrence gives exactly
\[
 \Pi_N(w)=\Pi_N(w-k_N/N)
       \prod_{j=0}^{k_N-1}(1-e^{-2\pi i(w-j/N)}).        \tag{17}
\]
The starting factorial has its original uniform upper expansion.
For the product, replace each logarithm only in the upper estimate
by \(\max(\log|1-e^{-2\pi iv}|,-M)\). At fixed \(M\)
these continuous functions have uniform Riemann sums on the compact
family. Their integrals decrease uniformly to the integrable
logarithmic primitive: a zero has uniformly integrable logarithmic
mass, and there are only finitely many possible zero locations.
Thus (17) has the claimed continuous rate with an arbitrary fixed
allowance, uniformly through the wall and height zero. Its lower
and upper continuations agree with the global lower factorial
and the exact multiplier (14), respectively. No zero is divided
out. All denominators remain in compact original regular strips.
This proves the finite estimates also on the four vertical halves,
including where their shifted numerators are zero.

## 6. Every first physical row

For \(0\le t\le1/2\) the real row rate is
\[
 E_2(t;a,q)=I_a(t)+I_q(t)-3J(t)-2\pi ht,\quad
 I_a(t)=\int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(a+v)}|\,dv,\quad
 J(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi v)\,dv.                    \tag{18}
\]
Its \(q\)-derivative is nonpositive for \(q\le3/4\).
Every low row \(0\le t\le1/8\) is bounded by the current
real left cut. Indeed its incoming folded modulus is greater
than one: \(e^{-2\pi h}\ge15/16\) and the shifted cosine
is less than \(1/5\). Its outgoing modulus is greater than
\(4/5\), since \(q+t\le169/200\) and
\(2\sin(3\pi/20)>4/5\). Its own denominator cube is
less than \((4/5)^3\). Hence the private real derivative
is strictly positive. This includes the zero index by continuity.
The fixed physical collar \([r_0,1/2]\) is arbitrarily close
in rate to its half row and is kept separately.

For the full high range put \(t=1-v\), \(0\le v\le1/2\),
\(d=1-q\), and \(G_{|h|}(u)=\Re g(u-i|h|)\).
The exact reflected modulus identity is
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_2(1-v;a,q)={}&G_{|h|}(x-v)-G_{|h|}(x)
       +J(d)-J(d+v)+3J(v)+2\pi(-h)_+(1-v).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{19}
\]
In particular its last carrier is \(T_2=2\pi(-h)_+\).
For \(v\le1/10\), the first difference in (19) is at
most \(v\log(3/2)\), since \(x-u\in[3/25,1/2]\)
has disk modulus greater than \(2/3\). The next difference
is at most \(-v\log(3/2)\), since \(d\ge7/25\).
The remaining terms show \(E_2(1-v)\le T_2\).

For \(1/10\le v\le1/2\), the all-angle leg inequality gives
\[
 E_2(1-v;a,q)\le H_2+F_d(v)+J(v)+\pi(-h)_+(1-2v),
                                                               \tag{20}
\]
where \(H_2=E_2(1/2;a,1/2)\) is the original real-half
profile, and \(F_d(v)=J(d)+J(v)-J(d+v)\).
The paired-upper proof, equation (33), gives
\(F_d(v)\le-9(1-2v)/40\). Also
\(J(1/10)<-1/10\), from
\(J(1/10)\le[\log(\pi/5)-1]/10\), and convexity of
\(J\) on \([1/10,1/2]\) gives
\(J(v)\le-(1-2v)/8\). Thus the total negative chord
coefficient is \(7/20\), while
\(\pi(-h)_+<39\pi/500<1/4\). Consequently
\[
           E_2(t;a,q)\le\max\{H_2,T_2\},
                       \qquad1/2\le t\le1.             \tag{21}
\]
This is a bound for every integrated physical row, not a scalar
reflection formula for the coherent conditional polynomial.

The finite zero-safe product argument of the conditional allowance
source applies (18)--(21) to every row of the low, collar and high
blocks, with polynomial factors and one arbitrarily small allowance.
It includes off-grid revived rows, both midpoint parities and the
actual last index. There is no division by a finite half or last row.

## 7. Pairing each first carrier with the complete third kernel

Use the complete third source in Section 6 of the paired-upper
theorem. We spell out how its comparisons are attached here, since
its printed theorem has a first upper correction as its other factor.

Its actual main, lower alias and upper alias are the three maxima
in (10), with their stated penalties. The first actual main,
first lower alias and first real left cut are paired with them by
(11). The first upper correction is paid by Section 4. All third
zero joins are convex in height and all four weighted vertical
halves are paid by their real cuts. The third incoming wall rate
\(I_z(1-q)-J(1-q)\) is no larger than the third actual-height
main value there, for either sign of \(h_3\); the choice
\(q\le18/25\) ensures \(b+1-q\ge1/2\).
The short real collar of its moving prewall cut is paid by uniform
continuity. Every postwall third middle row decreases from that
wall, with the source's squared ratio bound \(4573/9000<1\).
Its low rows reduce to the original third left profile, and all
its high rows reduce to the original third half and last carriers.
These latter three profiles are independent of \(q\). Each
first main, lower and real-left carrier is separately nonincreasing,
so their original calibrations pay these combinations too.

It remains to pair the first half and last carriers in (21), and
the first right-side comparisons of Section 5. For any third main
or lower-alias branch, (13) costs less than \(1/4240\).
The first-half extra \(1/30\) and first-last extra \(1/350\)
from (8) each pay this cost, with a strict remaining surplus.
The third upper alias never increases; its real-left, high-half
and last alternatives are constant or smaller. Every such Cartesian
pair is therefore paid. In particular no bound for the complete
\(C_0\) is inferred from only its main profile, and no signed
lower estimate is used.

## 8. Exact contour assembly, allowances, and exclusions

For the first central block choose inward half-grid cuts tending
to \(1/8,r_0\), lower height \(-1/5\), upper height
\(h_U\), and actual main height \(h_2\). Its exact row is
\[
 T_{1,N}(s;a,q)=e^{2\pi iNas}\Pi_N(s+a)\Pi_N(s+q)
                         E_N(a)E_N(q)E_N(s)^3,
                         \qquad E_N=1/\Pi_N.            \tag{22}
\]
The split-cotangent identity retains the \(+N\) zero-mode
current from left to right, its two unweighted joins, the lower
horizontal from left to right with \(N(\cot\pi Ns-i)/(2i)\),
the upper horizontal from right to left with
\(N(\cot\pi Ns+i)/(2i)\), and all four weighted vertical
halves. Equations (14), (17) keep (22) holomorphic across the
shifted numerator wall. Its three own denominators and the two
outer denominators stay in regular strips; no new pole or residue
has been introduced. The low and high physical groups, including
the fixed right collar, supply every remaining original index.

For the third central block use exactly the moving prewall contour
of the paired-upper source, with lower height \(-13/200\),
upper height \(1/5\) and actual height \(h_3\). Section 7
has paid every one of its corresponding contour pieces and every
physical row. Simultaneous first/third corrections are paid by
their paired rate bounds, before finite summation. The complete cap
inventory from Section 2 multiplies each of them.

On the real second interval, \(\Re g(q)\ge0\). The full
second Fourier bound is therefore
\(\max\{-\Re g(q),0\}=0\), and both \(Y^{-\mu N}\)
have unit modulus. Both first alternatives remain in the original
native source throughout (2). Finite current lengths, the exact
\(N\) densities, index counts, and the fixed external scalar
are unchanged. The fixed contours and collars are chosen before
\(N\). Allocate their continuity errors, the factorial/product
upper allowances, half-grid rounding and ordinary-color
continuation below a fixed fraction of the common raw reserve.
The proof of the half-face source retains more than \(20\delta\)
on these prepaths; every new loss above is paid by a separate
displayed reserve. Thus the remaining gap is at least
\(\delta/4\), proving (7). Cauchy's formula gives the fixed
ordinary-color derivatives on smaller disks.

This does not replace the original full first integral by its core
restriction. To infer a full-first or full-second theorem one would
still need the omitted first pieces with the same second interval,
and the other required second-contour pieces. Those assertions are
not consequences of (7).
