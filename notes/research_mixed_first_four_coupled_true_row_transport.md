# Uniform coupled true-row transport for an attached type-four cap

Research corollary, 7 November 2026. With its actual unattenuated
attached height, a first cap of type four and its adjoining
own-four true-last-row alternative have coupled transport
slope greater than \(1/2\) on the entire early first-minimum
taper. Both incidences of the changing neighbor and every
following regular index height are included.

The actual \(m\ge3\) source restriction and the unattenuated
first coefficient are essential. Interior high rows, following
source eligibility and the final calibrated boundary comparison
remain separate.

## 1. The original allocation and the radial comparison

Use the actual first-minimum notation of
[the own-three coupled true-row theorem](research_mixed_first_three_coupled_true_row_transport.md),
but now take a first cap of type four:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 d=x-.1,\quad .1\le x\le.22,\quad x\le z\le.5,\qquad
 a=x-i\lambda_ad,\quad b=z-i\lambda_bd,\\
 1/6<\lambda_a<13/20,\qquad 0\le\lambda_b\le17/20 .
 \end{gathered}                                                \tag{1}
\]
The first coefficient comes from the original coupled
\(-.078<c_a<-.020\) theorem for \(m\ge3\), without
additional attenuation. The neighbor retains its original
upper cutoff and coefficient. The following ordinary profile
has the unchanged full rectangle
\[
 t=w+i\zeta,\qquad .22\le w\le.49,\qquad -.108\le\zeta\le.068 .
 \tag{2}
\]
Its full finite-source eligibility is not supplied by this
derivative calculation.

The whole real-private cap envelope may use
\(e\in[.27,.4]\) by the
[private-index barrier](research_mixed_first_four_private_index_barrier.md).
The exact first derivative on the ordinary true row is
\[
 F_1(e;x)=\Re p_1+\lambda_a(\Im p_1+2\pi),
                  \qquad p_1=g'(e+a)-2g'(a).
 \tag{3}
\]
This is the allocation in equation (2) of
[the original true-row transport](research_mixed_first_attached_true_last_row_transport.md):
both first-leg halves, the actual last-row rate and its
\(2\pi(-\Im a)_+\) term are retained.
The full coupled derivative is
\[
                   F_1(e;x)+
                       \lambda_bB_{\rm end}(t;z,-\lambda_bd).
 \tag{4}
\]

Varying the first coefficient between zero and \(\lambda_a\)
is an analytic comparison within the already proved taper
strip, not a new reference selection.
The same imaginary-cotangent bounds used in Lemma 1 of the
own-three theorem give
\[
 \Re p_1\ge f_1(e,x)-2\pi d\lambda_a,\qquad
 f_1(e,x)=\log\frac{\sin\pi(e+x)}{2\sin^2\pi x}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed the shifted imaginary cotangent is nonnegative
and no greater than the own one, whose modulus is less
than one.

The original argument bounds also give
\[
                         \Im p_1+2\pi\ge6\pi/5 .
 \tag{6}
\]
To verify this directly, the own argument is at most
\(\pi(.5-x)\). If \(e+x\le.5\), the shifted argument
is nonnegative, so the left side of (6) is at least
\(\pi(1+2x)\ge6\pi/5\).
If \(e+x\ge.5\), its shifted argument is at least
\(\pi(1-2e-2x)\), giving instead the lower bound
\(2\pi(1-e)\ge6\pi/5\).
These are original logarithmic lifts, at the same radius.

Combining (3)--(6) and the unattenuated \(\lambda_a>1/6\),
\[
               F_1(e;x)>
                   f_1(e,x)+\frac\pi5-\frac\pi3d .
 \tag{7}
\]
The coefficient \(6/5-2d\) is positive throughout the
interval, so using the lower \(\lambda_a\) bound here
has the required direction.

## 2. Uniform real-profile tangent bounds

At fixed \(x\), the minimum of \(f_1(e,x)\) is at a
private endpoint \(e=.27,.4\), since its positive sine
numerator is concave in \(e\).
Both endpoint functions are strictly convex in \(x\).
Their endpoint value and derivative bounds are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 e&f_1(e,.22)&-\partial_xf_1(e,.22)\\ \hline
 .4&>1/8&>111\pi/40\\
 .27&>1/5&>71\pi/30 .
 \end{array}                                                  \tag{8}
\]
For explicit value witnesses use the earlier polynomial bounds
\[
 \cos(.12\pi)>116/125,\qquad
 \cos(.01\pi)>999/1000,\qquad
                  \sin^2(.22\pi)<51/125 .
 \tag{9}
\]
They follow respectively from \(1-(66/175)^2/2\),
the quadratic cosine bound, and
\(\sin(.22\pi)<319/500\).
The ratios in \(f_1\) are consequently greater than
\[
 \frac{58}{51}>\frac{17}{15},\qquad
 \frac{333}{272}>\frac{11}{9}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The inequality \(\log u>2(u-1)/(u+1)\) gives the
value column of (8).
For its derivative column use
\[
 \cot(.22\pi)>6/5,\quad
 \tan(.12\pi)>3/8,\quad
                         \cot(.49\pi)<1/30 ,
 \tag{11}
\]
all already proved elementary bounds on the original real
arguments.
Convexity now yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 f_1(.4,x)&>\frac18+\frac{111\pi}{40}
                                          \left(\frac3{25}-d\right),\\
 f_1(.27,x)&>\frac15+\frac{71\pi}{30}
                                          \left(\frac3{25}-d\right).
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{12}
\]

## 3. Payment of the full neighboring force

Section 3 of the own-three coupled theorem is independent
of the first cap type. It retains both following branches
and proves
\[
 B_{\rm end}(t;z,-\lambda_bd)\ge0\qquad(z\ge.28).
 \tag{13}
\]
For \(z<.28\), its correlated lower bound is
\[
 \lambda_bB_{\rm end}(t;z,-\lambda_bd)
 \ge \lambda_b\{\pi(2x-.56)+1/9\}
                         +(9\pi/10)\lambda_b^2d .
 \tag{14}
\]
The same actual \(\lambda_b\) occurs in both terms.
These bounds hold uniformly over (2); in particular they
do not require the following index height to be negative.

For \(z\ge.28\), equations (7), (12)--(13) already give
a slope exceeding \(1/8+4\pi/25>1/2\).
For \(z<.28\), insert (12) and (14) into (7).
At fixed \(\lambda_b\), each lower expression is affine
in \(d\); its minimum on the containing interval
\([0,3/25]\) is at an endpoint.

At \(d=0\), minimizing over \(0\le\lambda_b\le17/20\)
gives the two lower bounds
\[
 \frac18+\frac{17}{180}+\frac{227\pi}{1000},
 \qquad
 \frac15+\frac{17}{180}+\frac{89\pi}{500}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Both exceed \(1/2\), already using \(\pi>3\).
At \(d=3/25\), the neighbor quadratic is exactly the
one in (24)--(25) of the own-three theorem. Its loss is
less than \(1/16\). The smaller private value therefore
still gives
\[
 \frac18+\frac{4\pi}{25}-\frac1{16}
                >\frac1{16}+\frac{12}{25}
                         =\frac{217}{400}>\frac12 .
 \tag{16}
\]
This proves the following uniform statement.

**Theorem 1.** Under (1)--(2) and the original actual-source
hypotheses, the rate derivative in (4) is greater than
\(1/2\) throughout the whole early first-minimum taper.

The own-four cap's true-end exponents have already been
absorbed pointwise into its real-private envelope, and
the private-index restriction preserves that complete
finite-source bound. Adding the same row and following
profile preserves both facts.
Integrating the pointwise rate inequality before maximizing
over the unchanged profile intervals thus pays the entire
true-last-row alternative of the first adjoining source.

This result does not cover an attenuated first own-four
coefficient, a length-two endpoint configuration, an
unproved following finite-source domain, or the first
ordinary factor's interior high physical-tail maxima.
The regular central/cut comparisons and final calibrated
boundary value remain open.
All original complex marginals, middle compensation,
relative pinning fees, physical zeros and parity
conventions are retained.
