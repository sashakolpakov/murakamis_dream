# A complete conditional source on the terminal private contour

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The finite conditional source
beside the first type-three cap admits the entire private contour of
the ordinary terminal-four factor as its second target. This does not
require that private target to be an ordinary reference coordinate.
The low physical rows have an exact geometric bound, and the existing
paid first-cap moving derivative applies to the regular part of this
new source family. No numerical evaluation is used.

The full terminal polynomial is not estimated here: its remaining
private pieces, the conditional high-row profiles, and the calibrated
comparison at the end of the first taper are retained as separate
obligations. Frozen source notes and manuscripts are unchanged.

## 2. The private target supplies the needed cotangent and radius bounds

The actual first taper in (3) has
\(\Re\cot\pi a>0\) and \(|\Im\cot\pi a|<1\), by the
first-coordinate estimate in
[first-attached eligibility](research_mixed_first_attached_taper_root_eligibility.md).
For (4), \(\cos(2\pi z)<0\), and hence directly
\[
 \Re\cot\pi b>0,\qquad
 |\Im\cot\pi b|
 =\frac{|\sinh(2\pi h_b)|}
        {\cosh(2\pi h_b)-\cos(2\pi z)}<1.
 \tag{7}
\]
The same conclusions hold for \(s=v+i\eta\),
\(.2\le v\le.49\), \(-.108\le\eta\le0\). When the real
cosine is positive, use
\(\cos(2\pi v)\le\cos(2\pi/5)<1/3<e^{-.216\pi}\);
negative cosine gives the inequality immediately. Thus the original
triangle determinant proves strict horizontal concavity of
\(\Re Q(v+i\eta;a,b)\), uniformly on the compact domain.

Put \(R=e^{2\pi\eta}\), so \(1/2<R\le1\). The first numerator
bound in Lemma 1 of
[the raised low-index barrier](research_mixed_first_attached_index_barrier.md)
uses only (3), and therefore still gives
\[
 |e^{-2\pi h_a}-R e^{-2\pi i(x+.22)}|^2
          > A(R):=1+R^2+\frac{21}{25}R.
 \tag{8}
\]
For the new private target,
\[
 r_b=e^{2\pi h_b}>\frac7{10},\qquad
 \cos2\pi(z+.22)<-\frac6{25}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The second bound is the already proved
\(\sin(.08\pi)>6/25\), since \(.49\le z+.22\le.71\).
For the first, \(.11\pi<7/20\), and the positive exponential
series with its geometric remainder gives
\[
 e^{7/20}\le1+\frac7{20}
       +\frac{(7/20)^2}{2(1-(7/20)/3)}
       =\frac{3009}{2120}<\frac{10}{7}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Both terms involving \(r_b\) in the squared modulus increase with
\(r_b>0\). Consequently
\[
\begin{split}
 |1-Rr_b e^{-2\pi i(z+.22)}|^2
 &>1+\frac{49}{100}R^2+\frac{42}{125}R\\
 &>B(R):=1+\frac14R^2+\frac{21}{50}R.
\end{split}
 \tag{11}
\]
The last difference is
\(R(6R/25-21/250)>0\), because \(R>1/2\).
This replaces the ordinary neighbor's quadratic upper-height cutoff;
no such cutoff is imposed on the private contour (4).

The exact chirp-aware logarithmic ratio is
\[
 e^{2\Re Q_s(.22+i\eta;a,b)}
 =\frac{|e^{-2\pi h_a}-R e^{-2\pi i(x+.22)}|^2
             |1-Rr_b e^{-2\pi i(z+.22)}|^2}
            {|1-R e^{-.44\pi i}|^6}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Its denominator base is less than
\(D(R)=1+R^2-9R/25\). The unchanged exact polynomial proof
in the raised-barrier note, equations (19)--(21), establishes
\(AB-D^3>1/8\) and hence
\[
                  \Re Q_s(.22+i\eta;a,b)>\frac1{75}.
 \tag{13}
\]
In particular every fixed-height central maximum on \([.2,.49]\)
lies strictly to the right of \(.22\).

## 3. All low physical rows, with an exact finite constant

At height zero and \(0<u\le.22\), the first shifted real argument
is in \((0,.44]\), and the second is in \((.27,.71]\).
For a shifted real argument below \(1/2\), its real cotangent is
at most its real-height value and is strictly smaller than
\(\cot\pi u\). Above \(1/2\) that cotangent is nonpositive.
Therefore
\[
 \Re Q_{ss}(u;a,b)
 =\pi\Re\{\cot\pi(u+a)+\cot\pi(u+b)-3\cot\pi u\}<0.
 \tag{14}
\]
Together with (13), this gives
\(\Re Q_s(u;a,b)>1/75\) throughout that interval.

For \(L_N=\lfloor11N/50\rfloor\), the literal consecutive-row
ratio in (2) is exactly
\[
 \frac{|R_{j+1,N}|}{|R_{j,N}|}
   =\exp\Re Q_s((j+1)/N;a,b)>e^{1/75}
                         \qquad(0\le j<L_N).
 \tag{15}
\]
All these numerator arguments have real part strictly between zero
and one, so their factors are nonzero. The case \(L_N=0\) is
immediate. Summing the geometric series proves, at every \(N\ge2\),
\[
 \left|\sum_{j=0}^{L_N}R_{j,N}\right|
 \le\sum_{j=0}^{L_N}|R_{j,N}|<76|R_{L_N,N}|.
 \tag{16}
\]
Indeed \(e^{1/75}>76/75\). Multiplication by any common finite
source factor preserves the nonstrict bound with constant 76,
including every zero of that factor. This is an exact row estimate,
not an asymptotic replacement or a moving-root computation.

## 4. The complete bare conditional envelope

Define
\[
 B^0(a,b;h_0)=\max_{.22\le v\le.49}\Re F(v+ih_0;a,b).
 \tag{17}
\]
For real physical indices set
\[
 \mathcal R(u;a,b)=
 \int_0^u\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(a+t)}|\,dt
 +\int_0^u\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(b+t)}|\,dt
 -3J(u)-2\pi h_a u.
 \tag{18}
\]
It is continuous through the integrated logarithmic zeros and at
both endpoints. In particular
\[
          \mathcal R(1;a,b)=2\pi(-h_a)_++2\pi(h_b)_+.
 \tag{19}
\]
Let \(T(a,b)\) be the maximum of its values at \(.22,.49,1\)
and at every high-tail stationary point \(.49<u<1\).
The latter form a list of at most six: put \(w=-\cot\pi u\)
and retain every real admissible root of
\[
 \frac14 e^{2\pi(h_b-h_a)}
 |\cos\pi a-w\sin\pi a|^2
 |\cos\pi b-w\sin\pi b|^2(1+w^2)-1=0.
 \tag{20}
\]
No root is evaluated or omitted. A numerator zero cannot add a local
maximum, because the derivative tends to \(-\infty\) on both sides.

**Theorem 1.** Uniformly on (3)--(5), for each \(\tau>0\),
\[
 |C_{1,N}(e^{-2\pi ia},e^{-2\pi ib})|
 \le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
      \exp N\{\max(B^0(a,b;h_0),T(a,b))+\tau\}.
 \tag{21}
\]
It retains the entire original finite sum, both parities and all zeros.

*Proof.* Repeat the exact contour construction of
[the complete adjoining envelope](research_mixed_first_attached_complete_contour_envelope.md)
before its exterior Fourier-weight multiplication. Its argument-strip
bounds use only \(.1\le x,z\le.5\), \(.1\le x\le.22\),
and \(-.108\le h_a\le.021\), \(-.102\le h_b\le.068\);
(3)--(4) satisfy these bounds. Its central curvature requires the
cotangent conditions, supplied here by (7). The half-grid cuts may
be taken at limits \(.22,.49\), by (13) and (16).

On heights \(-1/5\) and 2 both complete horizontal aliases have the
original strict reserves; their argument bounds are unchanged on the
smaller central interval. Paid vertical pieces reduce to their real
cuts. The unpenalized joins between zero and \(h_0\) reduce to the
real-cut and main-profile maxima by vertical convexity. All factorial
real arguments lie in compact subsets of \((0,1)\): in particular
\(.32\le\Re(s+a)\le.71\) and
\(.49\le\Re(s+b)\le.98\). The prescribed factorial continuation
therefore applies at every fixed finite height used by the contour.
The original cotangent identity retains its exact \(+N\) zero mode.

The low physical rows obey (16); the high physical rows obey the
uniform zero-safe product bound (18), whose exhaustive maxima are
(20) and the endpoints. Cut shifts cost only the usual polynomial
factor. No source halfweight is divided out: all these statements
precede the weight-allocation step of the cited proof. This gives
exactly the bare phase (6) and proves (21). \(\square\)

## 5. Paid regular transport with the whole first-cap envelope

Let \(\mathcal C_0(a,\vartheta)\) be the complete early type-three
cap envelope, including its real-private maximum, angular loss and
true-index alternative, from
[complete early-cap transport](research_mixed_cap_early_taper_envelope_transport.md).
At any fixed \(b\in E_c\), multiplication of its original finite
upper bound and (21) gives a complete same-target bound for
\(U_{0,N}C_{1,N}\), with exponent
\[
             \mathcal C_0(a,\vartheta)+\max(B^0,T).
 \tag{22}
\]
The ordinary color and all inherited fixed ordinary-color jets retain
their original scope.

There is also a strict transport inequality for the regular part.
Set
\[
 K(a,b)=\max\{B^0(a,b;h_0),\Re F(.22;a,b),\Re F(.49;a,b)\}.
 \tag{23}
\]
The proof of
[the paid first-three moving comparison](research_mixed_first_three_moving_height_comparison.md)
uses its neighboring target only to establish the lower-index
restriction. After that restriction, its moving derivative is
independent of \(b\). Equations (7)--(13) supply the same restriction
on the present private-target family. Its exact derivative, for the
regular cap row \(e\) real, is still
\[
 \Re\left[(1-i\lambda)
  \{g'(e+a)+g'(s+a)-2g'(a)+2\pi is\}\right]>\frac1{20},
 \qquad \lambda=-c/.12,
 \tag{24}
\]
for \(.27\le e\le.49\), \(.22\le\Re s\le.49\),
\(-.108\le\Im s\le0\). The same cited theorem retains the
true-end cap alternative with the same lower slope.
Integration before taking any maximum thus yields
\[
 \mathcal C_0(a(x_0),\vartheta)+K(a(x_0),b)
 \le \mathcal C_0(a(x_1),\vartheta)+K(a(x_1),b)
                       -\frac{x_1-x_0}{20}
 \tag{25}
\]
whenever \(.1\le x_0<x_1\le.22\), with the same \(c,b,h_0\).
No smoothness of a maximizing row is assumed. Any further fixed
private multiplier in (1) contributes the same rate at both ends.

Equation (25) is a genuine coupled first-cap/terminal-profile
transport, with the original chirp and without an ordinary-neighbor
height fee. It does not put its value at \(x=.22\) below the original
geometric action: the real-private cap envelope cannot be identified
with the smaller actual-height supporting plane merely because both
bound the finite cap. The high profiles in (20) also remain in (22),
and the full private-index assembly of (1) must retain its physical
complement and every connector. No whole terminal or whole-circle
ordinary \((3,4)\) conclusion is inferred.
