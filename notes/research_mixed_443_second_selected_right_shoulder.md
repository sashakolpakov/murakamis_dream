# A complete ordinary-pair shoulder for the selected second coordinate of 443

Private written deduction, 11 November 2026. The selected second
coordinate moves from \(9/20\) to \(1/2\), at height \(1/100\),
while the first real coordinate stays on its old plateau. Both first
Fourier currents are allowed, at their original respective heights.
Every finite row of both ordinary kernels remains present.

The actual type-three private height bounds the growth of the forced
second Fourier alternative by less than the existing central reserve.
No dominance of the factorial alternative is assumed. This is a
restricted-first-coordinate theorem, not a bound on complete
\(f_{44,N}\), either second height connection, or the whole second period.

## 1. Source, original action, and statement

Fix an actual positive three/four open word of length \(m\ge4\)
beginning with \(443\). Keep its original coupled reference and put
\[
 P=[11/50,9/20],\quad H=1/100,\quad
 a=x+ih_\sigma,\quad y=q+iH,\quad z=v+ic_3,\qquad
 x,v\in P,\quad 9/20\le q\le1/2,
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(h_0=c_1=\Im r_1,\ h_1=H\). The actual bounds are
\[
 -39/500<c_1<-1/50,\quad
 -51/500<h_2:=\Im s_2^*<-1/1000,\quad
 -13/200\le h_3:=\Im s_3^*,c_3\le17/250.
 \tag{2}
\]
Both private indices are ordinary, also at length four.
With \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(r;r)_j\),
\(p_j(X)=(rX;r)_j\), and \(X=e^{-2\pi ia},Y=e^{-2\pi iy},
Z=e^{-2\pi iz}\), the literal product is
\[
\begin{split}
 U_{1,N}(w,X)&=\sum_{i<N}H_N(w,r^{-i})p_i(X)/P_i^2,\\
 C_{\epsilon,N}(X,Y)&=\sum_{j<N}X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)/P_j^{2+\epsilon},\\
 \mathcal B_{\sigma,N}
 &=|U_{1,N}(w,X)d_N(X)X^{-\sigma N}C_{1,N}(X,Y)
                       d_N(Y)Y^{-N}C_{0,N}(Y,Z)|.
\end{split}\tag{3}
\]
There is no outgoing halfweight at coordinate three. No Fourier
polynomial is divided out.

Use the original primitive \(g'(u)=\operatorname{Log}
(1-e^{-2\pi iu})\), normalized by the continuous value \(g(0)=0\),
the original cap phase \(\Psi\), and original constants:
\[
\begin{split}
 Q_\epsilon(s;a,b)&=g(s+a)+g(s+b)-(2+\epsilon)g(s)-g(a)-g(b)+2\pi ias,\\
 F_{123}&=\Psi(e,a)+Q_1(s_2;a,y)+Q_0(s_3;y,z)-g(a)-g(y),\\
 p_R&=g'(s_3^*+r_3)-g'(r_3),\\
 \Lambda_{123}(z)&=\Re F_{123,*}+\Re\{p_R(z-r_3)\}.
\end{split}\tag{4}
\]
Every star belongs to the same fixed word.

**Theorem 1.** For every prescribed \(\tau>0\), at both level
parities and all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \mathcal B_{\sigma,N}\le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
        e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/400+\tau\}},\qquad \sigma=0,1,
 \tag{5}
\]
uniformly on (1). The original fixed ordinary-color neighborhood
about \(-2\), and all fixed ordinary derivatives on smaller disks,
are retained with their prescribed allowances and polynomial factors.

For \(\sigma=0\) there is also the complete ordinary-pair estimate
\[
 |C_{1,N}(X,Y)C_{0,N}(Y,Z)d_N(Y)Y^{-N}|\,|d_N(X)|^{1/2}
 \le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
               e^{N\{\Lambda_{23}(a,z)-1/400+\tau\}},
 \tag{6}
\]
with the unchanged two-boundary affine term of the second-selected
plateau theorem. Its incoming halfweight has rate \(-\Re g(a)/2\).

Integration over the stated first plateau or second shoulder keeps
the original densities \(+N\,da,+N\,dy\), and changes only a
polynomial factor. The unselected first integral gives an
\(I_{44,P}\) contribution; it is not replaced by \(f_{44,N}\).
At \(m=4\) the following terminal incidence has type-three incoming
allocation, so no outgoing halfweight or fourth ordinary factor is
missing. No nonzero coefficient for the added shoulder is asserted.

\[
 R_0^2=\max_{1/8\le t\le1/2}\Re Q_1(t+ih_2;a,y),\quad

\]
For factor three use \(Q_0\), main height \(h_3\), left cut
\(3/20\), half row \(1/2\), and last rate \(R_1^3=2\pi(c_3)_+\).
Boundary values mean the continuous original one-sided action.
The finite contour below first keeps its right cut strictly below
\(1/2\).

The two upper rates of the full selected second weight are
\[
 W_2^{\rm fac}=-\Re g(q+iH)-2\pi H,\qquad W_2^{\rm force}=0.
 \tag{8}

The absolute Fourier continuation theorem supplies their maximum
throughout (1), including \(q=1/2\).

main/main reserve \(1/100\), main/half reserve \(1/20\), and

has raw cap/main/main and cap/main/half reserve \(1/100\), for

\end{array}\tag{10}
\]
These are individual rate-profile comparisons, not consequences
drawn from a bound on a cancelling finite sum. The cap profiles
and first Fourier rates are independent of \(q\).

## 3. The sharper type-three radius and its maximizing barrier

The actual bounds imply
\[
 13/20<e^{2\pi h_3},e^{2\pi c_3}<31/20,\qquad
                        T:=e^{-2\pi h_3}<151/100.
 \tag{11}
\]
The last estimate uses only \(h_3\ge-13/200\):
\[
 \log(151/100)=2\operatorname{artanh}(51/251)
 >102/251+1/200>143/350>13\pi/100.
\]
The cubic term in this positive series exceeds \(1/200\), while
\(143/350-102/251=193/87850<1/400\).
The other containing bounds follow similarly from
\(\log(20/13)>14/33+1/200>17\pi/125\) and
\(\log(31/20)>22/51>17\pi/125\).
Also \(\log(16/15)>2/31>\pi/50\), so
\(e^{2\pi H}<16/15<43/40\) and \(T_H>15/16\).

The third main maximum may be restricted to \(t\ge1/5\).
Indeed, for \(3/20\le t\le1/5\), put
\(\rho=e^{2\pi h_3},R=e^{2\pi c_3},T_H=e^{-2\pi H}>15/16\).
The exact squared private force is
\[
 e^{2\Re Q_{0,s}}=
 \frac{|T_H-\rho e^{-2\pi i(q+t)}|^2
             |1-R\rho e^{-2\pi i(v+t)}|^2}
            {|1-\rho e^{-2\pi it}|^4}.
 \tag{12}
\]
On these intervals
\[
 \cos2\pi(q+t)<-3/10,\qquad
 \cos2\pi(v+t)<-1/2,\qquad \cos2\pi t>3/10.
 \tag{13}
\]
The first and third use
\(\sin(\pi/10)=(\sqrt5-1)/4>3/10\).
Writing \(D_s^2=|1-\rho e^{-2\pi it}|^2\), the first
numerator square exceeds \(D_s^2\) by more than
\[
                         -31/256+(93/80)\rho>0.
 \tag{14}
\]
The other exceeds it by more than
\(\rho\{(R^2-1)\rho+R+3/5\}\).
If \(R\ge1\) this is positive; otherwise its braces exceed
\[
 ((13/20)^2-1)(31/20)+13/20+3/5=2839/8000>0.
 \tag{15}
\]
Thus (12) is greater than one on the whole excluded interval.

## 4. Transport of all profiles, with the forced alternative retained

Put \(r_H=e^{2\pi H}\) and \(D=|1-r_H e^{-2\pi iq}|\).
For every first main point or real cut write
\[
 N_2=|1-r_H\rho_2e^{-2\pi i(q+t_2)}|,\qquad
 1/8\le t_2\le1/2,\quad 0<\rho_2\le1.
\]
Then \(N_2\le D\). Indeed \(1-q\le q+t_2\le1\), so its
shifted cosine is at least \(\cos2\pi q\). Convexity of the
squared modulus in \(\rho_2\in[0,1]\) reduces this to the
endpoints; at zero use \(D>1\).

On the clipped third main line let
\(N_3=|T-r_H e^{-2\pi i(q+t_3)}|\), \(1/5\le t_3\le1/2\).
The elementary cosine bounds give
\[
 D^2\ge r_H^2+(19/10)r_H+1,\qquad
 N_3^2\le r_H^2+(453/250)r_H+22801/10000,\qquad N_3/D<8/7.
 \tag{16}
\]
For the last inequality the difference of 64 times the first
polynomial and 49 times the second is
\[
                  15r_H^2+(8203/250)r_H-477249/10000.
 \tag{17}
\]
It increases for \(r_H\ge1\), and is \(871/10000>0\) at one.
Here \(\cos2\pi q\le-19/20\) follows from
\(\cos(\pi/10)>19/20\), and
\(\cos2\pi(q+t_3)\ge-3/5\) follows from
\(\sin(\pi/5)<3/5\).

The first outgoing-leg derivative is \(\log(N_2/D)\le0\).
The third incoming-leg derivative, including its asymmetric chirp,
is \(\log(N_3/D)<\log(8/7)<1/7\).
The literal first last-row carrier is constant in \(q\).
Thus every paired rate with third profile 0 has one-sided
increment bounded by \( (q-q_0)/7\). Taking the maximum over
fixed private intervals preserves this bound.

For third profile \(L\), its private height is zero, and
\[
 N_3\le1+r_H,\quad
 (1+r_H)/D\le\sqrt{40/39},\quad
                  \log(N_3/D)<1/78.
 \tag{18}
\]
This uses \(D^2\ge(1+r_H)^2-r_H/10\) and
\(r_H/(1+r_H)^2\le1/4\).
For profile \(R\), the numerator is
\(|1+r_H e^{-2\pi iq}|<2/5<D\), since \(1<r_H<43/40\)
and its square is at most
\((3/40)^2+(43/40)/10<4/25\).
For profile 1 the third rate is constant. Hence paired rates
with either of the last two profiles do not increase.

The forced second rate is constant. The factorial alternative
has the additional derivative \(-\log D<0\); both alternatives
therefore obey the same bounds. Over length \(1/20\), (10)
retains respectively
\[
 1/100-1/140=1/350,\quad
 1/100-1/1560,\quad 1/100,\quad 1/400.
 \tag{19}
\]
Each is at least \(1/400\). The original affine calibration has
no free \(q\)-marginal.

## 5. The complete finite contour on the enlarged shoulder

This section verifies the source extension rather than applying an
old contour statement outside its printed target domain.
Write \(C(w)=\Re\cot\pi w\), \(A=\cosh(2\pi\eta)\).
For \(Q_1\), the shifted cotangent at \(q+t>1/2\) is nonpositive.
If the other shifted cotangent is positive, then \(t<7/25\),
\(x+t\ge69/200\), and
\[
 3C(t+i\eta)>\frac{21/10}{A+1/5},\qquad
 C(x+ih_\sigma+t+i\eta)<\frac1{(20/33)A+1/2}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Use \(\sin2\pi t>7/10\), \(\cos2\pi t>-1/5\),
\(\cos2\pi(x+t)<-1/2\), and
\(\cosh2\pi(\eta+h_\sigma)\ge(20/33)A\).
For the last bound,
\(2\pi|h_\sigma|<1/2\) and
\(e^{1/2}<1+1/2+(1/2)^2/[2(1-1/6)]=33/20\).
The first rational expression in (20) exceeds the second for
every \(A\ge1\). When both shifted cotangents are nonpositive,
the conclusion is immediate. Thus \(\Re Q_{1,ss}<0\) at
every finite height, for \(1/8\le t<1/2\).

For \(Q_0\) the incoming shifted cotangent is nonpositive.
When the outgoing one is positive, \(t<7/25\), \(v+t\ge37/100\),
and (11) gives
\[
 2C(t+i\eta)>\frac{8/5}{A+1/5},\qquad
 C(v+ic_3+t+i\eta)<\frac1{(13/20)A+1/2}.
 \tag{21}
\]
The first expression exceeds the second: the linear coefficient
after cross multiplication is \(26/25-1>0\), and the constant
difference is \(3/5>0\). Hence \(\Re Q_{0,ss}<0\) for
\(3/20\le t<1/2\), at every height.

The original strip logarithms and these signs show that
\(\Im Q_s\) decreases with private height. Its exact limits are
\(2\pi x,\ \pi(-1-2q+2t)\) for \(Q_1\), and
\(2\pi q,\ -2\pi v\) for \(Q_0\). Therefore
\[
 -7\pi/4<\Im Q_{1,s}<9\pi/10,\qquad
 -9\pi/10<\Im Q_{0,s}<\pi.
 \tag{22}
\]
Choose lower and upper alias heights \((-1/5,4)\) for \(C_1\)
and \((-1/5,1/5)\) for \(C_0\). Their exact period penalties,
compared with the respective actual main heights, leave gaps
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\text{lower horizontal}&\text{upper horizontal}\\ \hline
 C_1&11\pi/50&1643\pi/2000\\
 C_0&33\pi/250&323\pi/2000.
\end{array}\tag{23}
\]
Every weighted vertical half decreases away from its real cut.
Vertical convexity from (20)--(21) bounds the two unweighted
joins by their fixed-main and real-cut endpoints.

Both physical low blocks still increase to their original cuts.
For \(C_1\), including its chirp, the incoming numerator is
greater than \(49/50\) on \(0\le t\le1/8\), the outgoing
numerator greater than one, and the denominator is less than
\((4/5)^3\). For the only cosine-positive part of \(x+t\),
the first bound follows from
\(\sin(2\pi(x+t))\ge\sin(11\pi/25)>49/50\); in the
cosine-nonpositive part the modulus is at least one.
If the incoming radial parameter exceeds one its minimum is at
radius one. For \(C_0\), on \(0\le t\le3/20\), the incoming
numerator is greater than one, the outgoing numerator at least
\(49/50\), and the denominator less than \((19/20)^2\).
Thus each exact low-row ratio is uniformly greater than one,
including the transition from the zero row.

The adopted full high-row endpoint theorem already covers both own
types on \([.22,.5]^2\), with either height sign of modulus at
most .108. It bounds all high rows by their literal half and last
rates. No revived row is removed.

Take a fixed right cut \(r_0<1/2\) sufficiently close to \(1/2\).
The physical collar between it and \(1/2\) has rate at most the
half-row profile plus any prescribed positive allowance, by
uniform continuity of the zero-safe row integrals. All factorial
arguments on the remaining fixed contours have real parts strictly
between zero and one. The exact marked factorial interpolation
therefore gives the original cotangent residue decomposition,
with its \(+N\) main coefficient, both unweighted joins, both
horizontal aliases and all four weighted vertical halves.
The physical low, collar and high rows are retained separately.
The cut is fixed before \(N\); half-grid rounding, uniform symbols
and all finite errors spend one arbitrary total allowance.
This proves the required complete four-profile bound at both
parities on the new shoulder.

## 6. Original allocations and remaining scope

cap plane bounds
\[
 |U_{1,N}(w,X)|\,|d_N(X)|^{1/2}
 \le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
 e^{N\{\Re L(r_1)+\Re L'(r_1)(a-r_1)
                      -\kappa_*(x-\Re r_1)^2+\tau\}},
 \quad L=\mathcal T_1-g/2.
 \tag{24}
\]
Its marginal is \(-p_L\); multiplication cancels the original
incoming tangent and gives (5), retaining the cap quadratic.
The two halfweights restore the whole first diagonal factor.

For \(\sigma=1\), keep the complete original cap reduction to
\(A_0,A_L\) and both first Fourier rates. Its other currents and
true ends are below those two profiles independently of the following
target. The first target is unchanged. Sections 2--4 therefore
transport its individual double-selected comparisons and prove
(5) with the full first selected weight.

Only the cap depends on the ordinary color. Its existing fixed disk
and Cauchy estimates give the stated color and derivative scope.
No external \(\kappa_N\) scalar or integration density changes.
The complementary first contour, the complete \(f_{44,N}\)
remainder, the \(\beta=1\) shoulder, and the second height
connections remain separate.
