# The complete initial incoming interval on the lower boundary plateau

Research proof, 8 November 2026. The original first incoming real
segment \(0\le a\le1/10\) is below the actual ordinary-next
supporting plane on the full independent lower boundary plateau.
The proof transports the complete cap and conditional current
profiles to the already paid point \(a=1/10\). A reflected physical
row estimate supplies the upper conditional rows, including the
last row. Every coherent cap coefficient and both alternatives of
the full Fourier weight remain.

No numerical evaluation, saved-data replay, new reference, or
modification of a frozen source is used.

## 1. Literal source and scope

Use the notation, actual references, and allocations of
[the full-plateau left taper](research_mixed_next_unselected_incoming_left_taper.md),
Section 1. Thus the first flags are \((0,1,\beta)\),
\(\beta=0,1\), and the second and third vertices are ordinary.
Write
\[
 (a_*,s_*,y_*,t_*,z_*)=(r_1,s_2^*,r_2,s_3^*,r_3),
 \qquad h_y=\Im y_*,\quad h_s=\Im s_* .
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular
\[
 .278\le\Re a_*\le.391,\qquad -.072<\Im a_*<0,
 \qquad -.102<h_s,h_y<-.001.
 \tag{2}
\]
The physical targets in the present theorem are
\[
 a=x\in[0,1/10],\qquad
 y=q+ih_y,\quad z=u+i\Im z_*,
 \qquad q,u\in P=[.22,.45],
 \tag{3}
\]
with the last two real coordinates independent. Set
\(X=e^{-2\pi ia},Y=e^{-2\pi iy},Z=e^{-2\pi iz}\).
For \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(r;r)_j\), and
\(p_j(V)=(rV;r)_j\), the complete factors are
\[
\begin{split}
 U_{0,N}(w,X)&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}H_N(w,r^{-k})p_k(X)/P_k,\\
 C_{1,N}(X,Y)&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
                         X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)/P_j^3,\\
 C_{\beta,N}(Y,Z)&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
                         Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)/P_j^{2+\beta}.
\end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
The original degree-at-most-\(N-1\) Fourier polynomial satisfies
\(d_N(r^b)=P_b^{-1}\). There is no factor \(Y^{-N}\) here.

Keep the original bare and allocated conditional phases
\[
\begin{split}
 Q(s;a,y)&=g(a+s)+g(y+s)-3g(s)-g(a)-g(y)+2\pi ias,\\
 F(s;a,y)&=Q(s;a,y)-\tfrac12g(y),
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
with their original logarithmic lift. Let \(A_*,p,B_3(q,u)\)
and \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\) be precisely those of the left taper,
equations (5)--(7); in particular
\[
 A_*+p(q-\Re y_*)+B_3(q,u)\le\Lambda_{123}(z).
 \tag{6}
\]
No change to the action constant or to either incident halfweight
is made.

**Theorem.** At sufficiently large levels of both parities, uniformly
on (1)--(3) and for every real ordinary color \(w\in[-2,2]\),
\[
\begin{split}
 &|U_{0,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(Y)C_{\beta,N}(Y,Z)|
                                      |d_N(Z)|^{\beta/2}\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\le C N^B
             \exp\{N(\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11})\}.
\end{split}
\tag{7}
\]
For \(\beta=0\) the last factor is one, including at zeros
of \(d_N\). The same rate, with a polynomial prefactor, bounds
the integral over (3) with its literal orientation and measure
\(+N\,dx\), and every fixed real-color derivative.

Near the actual complete color and actual output \(z_*\), the
unweighted integral normalized by the original next action has
all fixed normalized mixed derivatives bounded with gap \(10^{-12}\).
The outgoing halfweight is removed only on that local neighborhood,
using its original nonzero packet. No division by it is made on
the independent plateau in (7).

This theorem is for ordinary-next references. It makes no terminal
reference extension and does not estimate either complementary
outgoing contour or the complete selected Fourier coefficient.

## 4. Transport of every central conditional current

At \(x=.1\), the left taper has real conditional cuts
\(.11,.5\), main height \(h_s\), and auxiliary heights
\(-1/5,8\). Use these same fixed currents for
\(\rho\le x\le.1\). For every retained cap state \(e\),
every conditional point \(s=v+i\eta\) on these paths, and
every intermediate height on their vertical sides,
\[
\begin{split}
 \frac d{dx}\Re\{\Psi_0(e,x)+Q(v+i\eta;x,y)\}
 &=\log\frac{|1-e^{-2\pi i(e+x)}|\,
       |e^{-2\pi\eta}-e^{-2\pi i(x+v)}|}
                  {(2\sin\pi x)^2}\\
 &>\log(3/2).
\end{split}
\tag{13}
\]
This is the exact incoming derivative in
[the selected initial proof, equation (18)](research_mixed_second_selected_first_three_initial_interval.md).
It is independent of \(y\). In particular its validity does
not use positive outgoing height.

For completeness, \(\Re(e+x)\in[.27,.59]\), so its first
modulus exceeds one at every original private height.
Also \(x+v\in[.11,.6]\). Minimizing the second modulus over
its nonnegative radius gives a lower bound greater than \(3/5\):
when its cosine is positive use
\(\sin(.22\pi)>3/5\), and otherwise use one.
Finally
\[
                (2\sin\pi x)^2\le(3-\sqrt5)/2<2/5.
 \tag{14}
\]
For the extra real profile (11), the identical derivative holds
with \(\Re\Psi_0\) replaced by \(R_0\), and its first modulus
still exceeds one since \(e+x\in[.49,.6]\).
Every sampling penalty is independent of \(x\).

Consequently each individual central-cap/conditional-current
profile is bounded by its value at \(x=.1\). There (11) clips
to the old real cap cut. The proof of the full-plateau left taper,
Sections 2 and 4--7, pays all of these endpoint profiles at the
actual negative height \(h_y\), before maximizing or summing them.
Its main deficit is at least
\[
 \kappa_0(\Re a_*-.22)^2
       \ge\frac{841}{625000000000}>10^{-9},
 \qquad\kappa_0=1/2500000;
 \tag{15}
\]
the real cuts and aliases have the larger deficits stated there.
The allocation \(-g(y)/2\), both weight alternatives, and every
third-factor profile are independent of \(x\) and stay attached.
This transport is therefore a sourcewise comparison, not an
inference from the magnitude of a signed endpoint sum.

It includes the two horizontal aliases, all four weighted vertical
pieces, both unweighted main joins, and the main segment itself:
(13) holds at each actual point of each current. The cap's own
five-profile envelope has already retained its vertical pieces.
No additional conditional curvature assertion at \(x<.1\) is
required. On every fixed \([\rho,.1]\), all real factorial
arguments remain in their original regular strips.

The low physical conditional rows also reduce to this endpoint.
Let \(R=e^{2\pi h_y}>1/2\); the bound follows from
\(.204\pi<2/3<\log2\). For \(0<t\le.11\),
\[
 e^{\partial_t\Re Q(t;x,y)}
 =\frac{2\sin\pi(x+t)\,
         |1-Re^{-2\pi i(q+t)}|}{(2\sin\pi t)^3}
       >\frac1{(2\sin\pi t)^2}>1 .
 \tag{16}
\]
Indeed \(t\le x+t\le.21<.5\), while \(q+t\in[.22,.56]\)
has cosine less than \(1/5\). Its squared numerator modulus
is greater than \(1+R(R-2/5)>1\).
The real rate has continuous value zero at \(t=0\).
Thus every low row is bounded by the real face \(t=.11\),
and then by its paid endpoint through (13). The exact zero
row is retained.

## 5. Every upper physical row reduces to half or last

This step uses the negative outgoing height directly.
Let \(E(t;x,y)\) be the literal real row rate of \(C_1\).
Because the incoming height is zero and \(R\le1\), both
numerator logarithms have zero full-period mean and the chirp
has modulus one. Hence its exact endpoint rate is
\[
                              E(1)=0.
 \tag{17}
\]
With \(f_R(s)=\log|1-Re^{-2\pi is}|\) and \(v=1-t\),
the original reflection gives
\[
 \mathscr F(v):=E(1-v)-E(1)
 =-\int_{x-v}^{x}f_1(s)\,ds
  -\int_{q-v}^{q}f_R(s)\,ds+3J(v).
 \tag{18}
\]
There is no discarded height drift in (18).

For \(0<v\le21/100\), we have \(v\le q\le1/2\).
The minimum-integral-arc argument of
[the upper-row theorem, Section 2](research_mixed_four_upper_rows_following_domains.md)
therefore gives
\[
 \mathscr F(v)\le2[J(v)-J(v/2)]
       \le v\{\log(4\pi v)-1\}<0.
 \tag{19}
\]
The first numerator's minimum arc is centered at an integer;
the second arc shifts to \([0,v]\), where its integral is
at least \(J(v)\). This argument places no lower bound on \(x\).
The final strict sign follows from
\(4\pi v\le21\pi/25<66/25<8/3<e\).

For \(21/100\le v\le1/2\), put \(S(v)=2\sin\pi v\).
Since \(0\le v-x\le v\le1/2\), the incoming modulus is
at most \(S(v)\). For \(0<R\le1\),
\[
 |1-Re^{-2\pi is}|\le\max\{1,2|\sin\pi s|\}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Away from numerator zeros, (18) consequently gives
\[
 e^{\mathscr F'(v)}
   \ge \frac{S(v)^2}{|1-Re^{-2\pi i(q-v)}|}>1 .
 \tag{21}
\]
Here are explicit witnesses for the final inequality:

* On \(21/100\le v\le1/4\), \(|q-v|\le1/4\), so the
  denominator is at most \(\sqrt2<10/7\). Meanwhile
  \[
    S(v)^2\ge S(21/100)^2
     =2-2\sin(2\pi/25)>262/175>10/7.
  \]
* On \(1/4\le v\le1/3\), again \(|q-v|\le1/4\).
  The denominator is at most \(\sqrt2\), and \(S(v)^2\ge2\).
* On \(1/3\le v\le1/2\), the denominator is at most two,
  and \(S(v)^2\ge3\).

Numerator zeros have integrable logarithmic limits, so integration
on complementary intervals proves strict increase in (21).
Together with (19) and \(\mathscr F(0)=0\), this proves
\[
       \max_{1/2\le t\le1}E(t;x,y)
             =\max\{E(1/2;x,y),E(1;x,y)\}.
 \tag{22}
\]
The endpoint-uniform zero-safe finite-product estimate turns
(22) into a bound for the complete upper physical sum, with
an arbitrarily small fixed rate tolerance and a polynomial
prefactor. It includes the even half row, the first odd row
above one half, and every revived or vanishing numerator row.

The half-row branch is a real conditional cut already transported
by (13). For the last-row branch retain the complete cap and use
(8) and (17). Its prefix ceiling is
\[
               \frac{101}{125}+\frac{23}{100}
                     =\frac{519}{500}<\frac{2383}{1500}.
 \tag{23}
\]
The actual marginal comparison in Section 2 pays this branch
with the full \(d_N(Y)\), complete third kernel, and outgoing
halfweight. No positivity of the coherent cap is assumed.

## 6. Finite contour assembly and derivatives

For the conditional sum put
\[
 L_N=\lceil11N/100\rceil,\qquad M_N=\lceil N/2\rceil.
 \tag{24}
\]
Sections 4--5 pay every physical index \(j<L_N\) and
\(M_N\le j<N\). On \(L_N\le j<M_N\), use the original
canonical interpolation
\[
 e^{2\pi iNas}
  \frac{\Pi_N(a+s)\Pi_N(y+s)}
       {\Pi_N(a)\Pi_N(y)\Pi_N(s)^3}
 \tag{25}
\]
on half-grid real cuts \((L_N-1/2)/N\) and
\((M_N-1/2)/N\). The exact finite cotangent decomposition
has its original \(+N\) main density, the two horizontal
Fourier remainders, four weighted vertical pieces, and two
unweighted main joins. All nine pieces are bounded in Section 4.
Their real arguments satisfy, up to vanishing half-grid changes,
\[
 .11+\rho\le\Re(a+s)\le.6,\qquad
 .33\le\Re(y+s)\le.95,\qquad .11\le\Re s\le.5 .
 \tag{26}
\]
Thus the fixed heights \(-1/5,8\) use the original factorial
branches throughout. No contour is inferred from a physical
row bound. The complete cap has its own exact decomposition,
all compact physical tails, the extra real profile (11), and
the separately paid true ends (12).

The original lower-strip Fourier and third-factor bounds are
exactly the left taper's equation (29). They supply the allocation
in (5) and then (6), retaining both Fourier alternatives.
All finite-product tolerances and half-grid displacements can
be chosen inside the difference between (15) and \(10^{-11}\).
The separate collar, end, and last-row branches have larger
reserves. This proves (7).

Integration uses the original positive orientation \(+N\,dx\).
The path has bounded length; the cap and conditional residue
density factors also cost only powers of \(N\).
Fixed real-color derivatives follow from Markov's inequality
for the exact color polynomial. At the actual outside parameters,
the strict bounds persist on sufficiently small fixed complex
neighborhoods, with all paths and index cuts held fixed at their
base values. The original nonzero outgoing Fourier packet removes
the outgoing halfweight only there. Shrinking the neighborhoods
and applying Cauchy's formula gives the normalized gap \(10^{-12}\)
for every fixed mixed jet.

The initial segment (3), the reviewed left taper, and the previously
paid remaining first incoming pieces now all have bounds on the
same independent lower plateau. The local geometric contribution,
the complementary outgoing contours, and the other Fourier
coefficient retain their separate exact roles. This theorem alone
does not suppress those remaining terms or enlarge a knot family.
