  2 sum_blocks Vol(Q_block)=2pi Phi_sm < Vol(Y_D)=2pi Phi_D. (1)

## 1. The common circle data can be assembled along the digon tree

Choose a root block. Each simple block has its proved positive circle
packing; a double-triangle block is represented by three mutually
tangent circles, with its two shaded triangles having the SAME three
contact points and zero three-dimensional volume. Normalize the latter,
for example, as two opposite lines and one disk tangent to both.

At a digon attachment its boundary vertices a,b are adjacent in each
closed block. There is an orientation-preserving Mobius map carrying
the child's ORDERED oriented tangent disks a,b to the already assigned
parent disks. To see this explicitly, send their common contact to
infinity. Both pairs become ordered opposite parallel halfplanes;
positive scaling and a translation match their width and position.
The remaining freedom is horizontal translation inside the strip.
Thus every shared white face receives exactly the same reflection on
both sides. Recursing along the actual cut tree assigns circles to all
original smoothed white faces. The free translations can avoid any
additional finite accidental equalities of distinct circles. A globally
embedded packing is NOT required here; only every block's packing and
the shared ordered circles are retained.

## 2. The two types of digon cuts have different actual pairings

The digon encloses 2v source vertices. Since each vertex has one
matching incidence, its number of crossing matching edges is even.
Thus the two crossed source edges are both MAIN or both MATCHING.

MAIN/MAIN: the matching pairs stay within the pieces. The same raw
physical head r_b r_a (or its literal inverse when the port is reversed)
appears at the two cut ports. Topologically the cut is the actual
two-main-arc sphere, or the corresponding meridional annulus after
deleting cusp neighborhoods. This is the familiar componentwise
connected-sum situation, with the actual meridian identified.

MATCHING/MATCHING: the two ORIGINAL shaded pairings cross the cut
pieces. They must not be replaced by gluing entire artificial cusp
tori. In particular no equality of the two blocks' cusp shapes is
required. The local algebra gives the needed actual collar directly.
The oriented boundary of the proper cut disk traverses its two cut
edges in opposite directions. Closing either side therefore records
one input and one output side. The original cross-piece pairing keeps
these opposite sides and its prescribed vertex permutation; it is not
an arbitrary input-to-input identification of two block triangles.

Let the common outer white pair be a,b and put P=r_b r_a=-I+H.
It is a noncentral parabolic: H^2=0. For either side write its ordered
raw main pair as (A,B), with BA=P and Tr A=Tr B=-2. Then

  Tr(H A)=4.

After H=[[0,h],[0,0]], this means A_21=4/h. Its trace and determinant
give A_22=-2-A_11 and A_12=-(A_11+1)^2/A_21. Hence the normalized
centralizer I+tH acts SIMPLY TRANSITIVELY on these ordered pairs.
For any original input/output triangles across a cut matching edge,
there is a unique C=I+tH with C A_in C^-1=A_out; it also transports B.

This C fixes the common a/b contact and sends the a/middle and
middle/b contacts to their recorded output contacts, because those
are the unique main parabolic eigenpoints. It is therefore exactly
the ordered three-point map of the two actual ideal shaded triangles.
This proves the contact/peripheral adapter; it does not infer one
from a scalar eigenratio.

For a retained half twist epsilon, use the literal primitive matrix

  M=C^-1 (I+H/2)^epsilon.

It has determinant1 and commutes with P. The original threading word
[2,1,1,2] followed by the recorded signed Hurwitz move transports the
input pair to the output pair, exactly as in the proved critical-scheme
and primitive-collar identities. The signs are the actual parabolic
specialization of (P^-1-I)/(-2), not a freely chosen peripheral shear.
Both P and M fix the same cusp point. MAIN cuts match their meridian;
MATCHING cuts instead retain BOTH original M matrices separately.

## 4. The ideal bigon collapse and the fundamental chain

The connected augmented projection has the usual TWO literal cut
balls obtained by cutting along the projection sphere and the recorded
crossing disks. This is a topological cut before any convexity claim.
Its white/shaded boundary cellulation is the rectified white graph;
the surviving half twists specify the same recorded shaded face maps.
For a loopless triangular sphere, the source cubic dual is bridgeless,
so these white faces have disk boundaries; degree-two faces are allowed.

In either cut ball, a digon CUT between white faces a,b bounds a proper
disk D whose boundary
has one arc in white face a, one in white face b, and whose two ideal
endpoints are the crossed source edges. Cutting the ball along D gives
two balls. Collapsing D in either boundary to a point gives exactly
the two closed block balls of the combinatorial digon decomposition.
All shaded triangles are unchanged; only the two cut ideal vertices
coalesce. Iterating constructs the actual block tree separately in
both cut balls. This does not claim that the doubled disk separates
the full exterior when its shaded pairings cross the cut.

Under the circle assignments of section1, BOTH cut ideal vertices
develop to the SAME contact p=a intersect b. Thus D is assigned a
constant ideal image p. Each remaining block ball, after collapsing
its cut disks, maps with degree ONE to its actual convex Q_block.
The double-triangle block maps into its single ideal triangle and
has volume zero.

Here is a finite-chain way to retain the multiplicity. First decorate
each tangency of an ordered white pair by its CANONICAL horosphere.
Send the contact to infinity and normalize the strip between the two
white planes to width1. The horosphere at height1 is independent of
the remaining horizontal translation, and pulling it back gives the
decoration. Swapping the ordered pair preserves the same horosphere.
Reflection in a boundary white plane preserves heights in this model.
An actual shaded face map carries each of its three adjacent white
planes: such a plane is the unique plane orthogonal to the shaded
plane along the specified ideal triangle edge. Therefore the actual
three-point shaded map, including its retained vertex permutation,
carries these pair decorations. All finite replacements can consequently
be chosen face-compatibly before passing to the quotient.
These are the finitely many INCIDENCE points and truncated corners of
a fundamental chain. They are not a single finite point assigned
equivariantly to an ideal cusp vertex: a nontrivial parabolic stabilizer
would forbid the latter. Translated corners on the same horosphere
are retained, with their actual pairing maps.

The TWO cut endpoints have the same white pair a,b, not merely the
same ideal image. In their common normalized strip choose every
replacement vertex of the collapsed disk at height T, with bounded
horizontal coordinates. Reflected white maps and the actual shaded
maps preserve the preceding decoration. Since the triangulation and
its face maps are finite, all coordinates stay bounded in their
respective normalized strips. In particular any two replacement
vertices on one cut disk have distance

  2 asinh(|z_1-z_2|/(2T))=O(1/T).

Complete the truncated chain with the original conical cusp tails.
Their boundary comparison is the previously proved horospherical
O(T^-2) flux estimate; no artificial finite cusp fixed point is used.

This specific common-height construction is essential: arbitrary
sequences of ideal vertices converging to the same p need not have
vanishing tetrahedral volume.

We use the following uniform elementary bound. If a possibly partly
ideal tetrahedron has two FINITE vertices at distance ell, then

  |Vol(tetrahedron)| <= 2pi integral_0^ell cosh(t)^2 dt
                     <= 2pi ell cosh(ell)^2.             (4a)

Indeed take the plane through one of these vertices and the other
two vertices. The tetrahedron lies in the one-sided distance-ell slab
over this plane. In Klein coordinates adapted to the plane, orthogonal
projection is the linear coordinate projection; its image is the
convex hull of at most four points in the plane and has area at most
2pi. Fermi coordinates have volume element cosh(t)^2 dA dt, proving
the bound. Degenerate plane choices follow by a finite approximation.
The estimate is uniform when either of the last two vertices is ideal.

Triangulate the proper disks and both adjacent balls compatibly.
Collapsing a boundary disk of a ball to a point again gives a ball,
with relative degree1. Refine against a vertex-fan triangulation of
Q_block. One can perform the refinement by coning triangulated disk
faces and their mapping cylinders; every additional tetrahedron in
the collapse region then has at least TWO disk replacement vertices.
By (4a) their total volume is O(1/T). The remaining block chain has
relative degree1 and tends to an ordinary ideal triangulation of its
actual Q_block. Thus its developed volume tends to Vol(Q_block).
This is the degree of an explicit ball quotient, not an assumed global
covering degree, and the vanishing of the correction is supplied by
(4a), not by a continuity claim at coincident ideal flags.

## 5. A uniform corridor for the actual untwisted external colors

Fix any set E of original UNTWISTED external circle ports. Let
A_N(N-h_E,N_else) denote the literal original mixed contraction, with
external color N-h_i at i in E and color N at every other port. The
other ports may carry their original signed half twists. Let F_N^E(h)
be its EXACT empty-subset cosine term from the
[external reflection identity](actual_external_circle_reflection_and_cusp.md),
with deficit zero at ports outside E. Put Phi_D=Vol(Y_D)/(2pi).
There exist delta(D)>0, gamma(D)>0 and a fixed power K(D) such that,
for every sufficiently large N of EITHER parity and every integer
0<=h_i<=delta(D)N,

  |A_N(N-h_E,N_else)-F_N^E(h)|
       <= N^K(D) exp[N(Phi_D-gamma(D))].               (5)

All constants are allowed to depend on the fixed labeled diagram;
there is no width-independent or twist-independent uniformity claim.
One can choose them simultaneously for all subsets E of its untwisted
ports. This is a near-complete REAL INTEGER color corridor, not an
off-grid identity or a genuine-q normal theorem.

Proof. Expand by the exact full-matrix reflection identity. A nonempty
subset S contains literal L_(h_i) at its ports; a term with h_i=0 is
zero. If its identity smoothing is connected, the
[simultaneous polynomial correspondence](actual_simultaneous_small_circle_face_correspondence.md)
bounds its full finite source by C(l)^sum h_i times the smoothed
positive-coefficient bound at ANY fixed positive shared radii l. This
retains the original mark, all carries and the actual tensor endpoints.

Equation (1), or the loop capacity -infinity, gives Phi_sm<Phi_D.
Choose fixed positive finite-face radii with marked radii zero making
the fold capacity strictly below Phi_D. Then choose both marked radii
small and POSITIVE, still below that value with a fixed margin. This is
permitted by continuity of the local fold entropy, with the limit
-infinity allowed when every local argument vanishes. The resulting
C(l) is finite; it need not be uniformly bounded as marked radii tend
to zero. The [all-channel determinant upper bound](actual_positive_face_channel_rate.md)
at these now strictly positive radii bounds every remaining folded
label by the same fold value, up to a uniform o(N) logarithmic error.
There are only polynomially many such labels. Multiplicities and
measures are polynomial, the remaining cosine factors have modulus at
most one, and the retained integer-label half-twist phases are units.

There are finitely many S. Fix one such radius choice for each connected
S and take the minimum positive capacity margin and maximum log C(l).
Choose delta small enough that C(l)^sum h_i consumes less than half
that margin. Absorb the uniform o(N) and polynomial factors by decreasing
gamma. For disconnected smoothing the exact correspondence instead
vanishes whenever 2 sum_(i in S)(h_i-1)<N-1. Choosing also
delta<1/(4 max(1,|E|)) guarantees this for all large N. Summing the
finitely many subsets proves (5), with the original subset signs intact.

This proves the missing proper-subset suppression in this corridor.
It does not bound colors outside the corridor or vary a signed circle
port: an odd half twist retained at a small port is not an identity
smoothing. Full filling-source transfer still requires those separate
inputs. No finite tensor product over the capacity blocks was used.
