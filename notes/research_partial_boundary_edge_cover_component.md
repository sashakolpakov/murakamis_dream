# The marked edge cover with some boundary angles fixed at zero

Research note, 15 November 2026.

The original circle-tree face construction has one square root for
each tree edge. When some auxiliary boundary angles are fixed at zero,
different edges can acquire the same radicand. The restricted cover can
then be reducible. This note identifies its component through the marked
complete structure, proves that its regular complex charts are connected,
and checks that the original face maps are rational on this component.

The conclusion concerns an explicit family of representations of the
original augmented exterior. It does not assert that every hyperbolic
short partial filling occurs in that family. A path in this family need
not preserve filling slopes, positive tetrahedra, logarithmic periods,
or the coefficient of a cycle in the quantum source.

## 1. The restricted cover and its components

Let \(\mathcal T=(V,E)\) be an original ordered circle tree with at
least one edge. Retain its page orders, disk frames, half-twist carriers,
and primitive peripheral curves, as in Sections 2--3 of the
[circle-tree geometry manuscript](../papers/circle-tree-geometry/main.tex).
Choose a nonempty subset \(S\subseteq V\), and write \(U=V\setminus S\).
The selected variables are \(a_v=\tan(t_v/2)\), for \(v\in S\);
set \(a_u=0\) for \(u\in U\). The equations are
\[
 q_e^2=1+a_v^2+a_w^2,\qquad e=vw\in E.
 \tag{1}
\]
At the marked complete point \(o\), every \(a_v=0\) and every
\(q_e=1\). Fixing \(a_U=0\) will be called the complete-parameter
slice. Away from the positive metric domain, this terminology specifies
parameters and peripheral multipliers, not a complete hyperbolic metric.

Partition the edges into \(E_S\), with both endpoints selected;
\(E_U\), with neither endpoint selected; and boundary edges, with one
endpoint in each set. For \(v\in S\), let \(k_v\) be its number of
boundary edges, and put \(B=\{v\in S:k_v>0\}\). Choose one reference
boundary edge \(e_v\) at each \(v\in B\). Set
\[
 f_e=1+a_v^2+a_w^2\quad(e=vw\in E_S),
 \qquad g_v=1+a_v^2\quad(v\in S).
 \tag{2}
\]

**Theorem 1.** The reduced affine cover (1) has
\[
 2^{\,|E_U|+\sum_{v\in B}(k_v-1)}
 \tag{3}
\]
irreducible components. Each has degree
\[
                         2^{\,|E_S|+|B|}
 \tag{4}
\]
over the selected parameter space. On the open set where every
\(q_e\ne0\), they are disjoint, and their labels are exactly
\[
 q_e=\sigma_e\quad(e\in E_U),\qquad
 q_e=\epsilon_e q_{e_v}
 \quad(e\ne e_v\text{ a boundary edge at }v),
 \tag{5}
\]
where every displayed sign is independently \(1\) or \(-1\).
The marked component \(X_S\), through \(o\), has every sign in (5)
equal to \(+1\). Thus (5) with these signs is an exact sheet test on
this open set. The absolute signs of the remaining independent roots
are not additional component labels.

**Proof.** Work over \(R=\mathbb C[a_v:v\in S]\). Each internal
polynomial \(f_e\) is irreducible: its homogenization is the rank-three
quadratic form \(z^2+a_v^2+a_w^2\), whereas a product of two linear
forms has rank at most two. Distinct tree edges give distinct primes.
For each \(v\in B\), the prime \(a_v-i\) divides \(g_v\) to order
one, divides no other \(g_w\), and divides no internal \(f_e\).
Consequently the classes
\[
                  \{f_e:e\in E_S\}\cup\{g_v:v\in B\}
 \tag{6}
\]
are independent in \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)^*/
\operatorname{Frac}(R)^{*2}\): take valuations first at the internal
primes and then at \(a_v-i\).

Independent square classes give a field extension of degree
\(2^{|E_S|+|B|}\). One elementary justification is induction: in a
multiquadratic extension, an element whose square belongs to the base
field is an eigenvector for every sign automorphism. Expanding it in
the root-monomial basis shows that it is a base-field multiple of one
root monomial. Thus a new base-field radicand becomes a square only
if its class is generated by the preceding ones.

It follows that
\[
 R[r_e,s_v:e\in E_S,\ v\in B]/
 (r_e^2-f_e,\ s_v^2-g_v)
 \tag{7}
\]
is a domain. Indeed it is free over \(R\) in the root monomials, and
these monomials are independent in that extension field. On \(q_e\ne0\),
the untouched roots have the constant signs in (5), and each quotient
of two boundary roots at the same vertex has square one. Splitting
by these signs leaves exactly (7), with the corresponding units
inverted. This proves the disjoint decomposition and both counts.

For completeness, the full algebra in (1) is free over \(R\) by
successively adjoining monic quadratics. Its generic algebra is a
product of the fields just described. Hence it is reduced: a nilpotent
would vanish generically and so would be \(R\)-torsion, contrary to
freeness. Its minimal components all dominate the base: a nonzero
element of \(R\) is a non-zero-divisor in this reduced finite algebra
and cannot lie in a minimal prime. The closures
of the components above are therefore all the components. Their
degrees sum to \(2^{|E|}\). Different closures can meet when a shared
boundary root vanishes, so the disjointness assertion was restricted
to \(q_e\ne0\). At \(o\), all signs are \(+1\), as claimed. \(\square\)

## 2. A regular open set and its connectedness

On \(X_S\) use the following convenient open set:
\[
 \mathcal R_S=
 \left\{\prod_{v\in S}(1+a_v^2)
              \prod_{e\in E}q_e(q_e^2+1)\ne0\right\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
This deliberately excludes the ramification locus \(q_e=0\), even
where an individual face expression might extend. It contains \(o\).
Its base is the complement of the hypersurfaces
\[
 g_v=0\ (v\in S),\quad
 f_e=0,\ f_e+1=0\ (e\in E_S),\quad
 g_v+1=0\ (v\in B).
 \tag{9}
\]
The map to that base is an unramified cover with all the independent
sign changes in (6) as deck transformations.

**Proposition 2.** The complex manifold \(\mathcal R_S\) is path
connected. Removing any further proper algebraic divisor from it
leaves a path-connected set. In particular any two surviving regular
points can be joined without crossing that divisor.

**Proof.** The base in (9) is path connected. On the complex line
through any two allowed points, none of its defining polynomials is
identically zero, since it is nonzero at those points. There are only
finitely many forbidden values on that line, and a path can detour
around them in its complex parameter plane.

A small loop about a generic smooth point of \(f_e=0\) changes the
sign of \(r_e\) alone. Such a point can be chosen away from all the
other hypersurfaces in (9). Likewise a loop about a generic point
of \(a_v=i\) changes the sign of \(s_v\) alone. If an internal edge
touches \(v\), its radicand there is \(a_w^2\), so it is nonzero at
a generic point of this divisor. The preceding loops generate every
deck sign. Conjugating them by base paths permits all these sign
changes at any one base point. Thus a lifted path between two base
points can be followed by loops that correct its endpoint sheet.
This proves connectedness of \(\mathcal R_S\).

Finally cover a path in \(\mathcal R_S\) by finitely many complex
coordinate balls. A proper algebraic divisor has empty interior in
this connected manifold: otherwise a local defining holomorphic
function, and hence the divisor, would contain the whole component.
Choose intermediate points outside the divisor in the successive
ball overlaps. In each ball, the complex line between its two chosen
points is not contained in the divisor. Its intersections with the
divisor are discrete; only finitely many lie near the compact line
segment under consideration. Detour around them inside the ball.
The resulting concatenated path proves the last assertion. \(\square\)

This argument also proves that the independent absolute root signs
are connected by complex continuation. It does not prove that they
can be changed within the positive tetrahedron domain.

## 3. The original face maps, not only their multipliers

Fix an edge with \(a=a_v\), \(b=a_w\), and \(q=q_e\). The coordinates
in Section 3 of the geometry manuscript become
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 E=\frac{q+i}{q-i},\qquad
 H_v=\frac{q-a}{q+a},\qquad H_w=\frac{q-b}{q+b},\\
 R=\frac{1+b^2}{q^2+1},\qquad R_1=1-R=\frac{1+a^2}{q^2+1},\\
 Z_v=\frac{q+a}{q-i},\qquad Z_w=\frac{q+b}{q-i},\qquad
 Q_v=\frac{1+ia}{1-ia}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{10}
\]
Here \(R\) is a face-coordinate ratio; it is unrelated to the ring
in the proof of Theorem 1. No square root in addition to those in
(1) has been introduced.

All denominators in (10) are units on (8). For the apex denominators,
the exact factorizations are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (q-a)(q+a)&=1+b^2,&(q-b)(q+b)&=1+a^2,\\
 Z_w-R_1&=\frac{(q+b)(b+i)}{q^2+1},&
 Z_w-ER_1&=\frac{(q+b)(b-i)}{(q-i)^2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
In particular the second apices in the two projection copies simplify
to \((b-i)/(q-i)\) and \((b+i)/(q+i)\), respectively.

The original eight tetrahedron shapes are
\[
 (A,B,-1/A,-1/B),\qquad(C,D,-1/C,-1/D),
 \tag{12}
\]
where, as in Section 1 of the
[positive edge note](research_positive_edge_global_monotonicity.md),
\[
 A=\frac{q+a}{b-i},\quad C=\frac{q-a}{b-i},\quad
 B=-\frac{q-b}{a+i},\quad D=-\frac{q+b}{a+i}.
 \tag{13}
\]
They are finite and nonzero on (8). None is \(1\) or \(-1\):
an equality of one of the four ratios to either value says that
\(q\) is a signed sum of \(a,b,i\); substituting in
\(q^2=1+a^2+b^2\) forces one of \(a\pm i,b\pm i\) to vanish.
Thus every shape in (12) avoids \(0,1,\infty\). The original ordered
face triples are consequently distinct. No positivity is asserted.

For clarity, the literal white matrices are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_0&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&E\end{pmatrix},&
 W_1&=\begin{pmatrix}1&E-1\\0&E\end{pmatrix},\\
 W_2&=\begin{pmatrix}E-R&R(1-E)\\E-1&1-ER\end{pmatrix},&
 W_3&=\begin{pmatrix}R&0\\1-E&ER\end{pmatrix}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
\]
The pure shaded map and the two maps used on the odd disk seam are
\[
 S_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&-ER\\1&-1-E\end{pmatrix},\quad
 A_0=\begin{pmatrix}R&-R\\1&ER-1-E\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B_0=\begin{pmatrix}R/E-1-1/E&R\\-1&R\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{15}
\]
The manuscript identifies each of these with its ordered face map,
including the actual half-twist permutation. Their determinants are
units on (8): those of (14) are, respectively,
\(E,E,E(1-R)^2,ER^2\), and those of (15) are
\(ER,ER(R-1),R(R-1)/E\).

Across pages, the doubled disk-side triangle ratios are
\((-Q_v^{-1},-Q_v,-Q_v^{-1})\) on the incoming boundary and
\((-Q_v,-Q_v^{-1},-Q_v)\) on the outgoing boundary. They depend only
on the shared vertex parameter. Normalizing one ordered triangle
then matches the full other triangle. The map between two ordered
distinct triples is rational in their coordinates. Consequently every
page identification and every finite product or inverse used to
develop the original cut is rational in \((a,q)\). Apparent poles
caused by an affine vertex moving to infinity can be handled by
homogeneous coordinates; they do not require a new field extension.

**Proposition 3.** On \(\mathcal R_S\), these maps give a rational
\(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) representation family of the original marked
augmented exterior. Its image has the same irreducible Zariski closure
as the marked complete germ with \(a_U=0\).

**Proof.** Near \(o\), the original cut construction, the full page
ratio table, and the pure and odd edge-return identities establish
the representation of that exterior. This includes the original
peripheral paths, rather than just their eigenvalues. Every group
relation is a rational identity in the face matrices on the integral
component \(X_S\). An identity holding on the complete open analytic
germ therefore holds in its function field. One elementary way to
check this last step is to take the field norm of a putatively nonzero
rational difference to \(\mathbb C(a_S)\). Its norm is nonzero, but
would vanish on an open base polydisk, a contradiction. Regularity
of the face maps extends the identity throughout (8).

The same argument applies to any polynomial relation among the
resulting representation coordinates. Hence a relation holds on the
complete germ if and only if it holds on the whole rational image.
The closure of a rational image of an integral variety is irreducible,
which proves the assertion. It also applies after passing to conjugacy
invariants. It does not identify this closure with every component of
the full representation or character variety. \(\square\)

All assertions here are projective. Choosing determinant-one matrix
representatives over the function field could require square roots of
their determinants; none is needed for Proposition 3.

The actual primitive words also give
\[
 e^{V_v}=Q_v^2,\qquad
 e^{U_v}=Q_v^{-\eta_v}
                \prod_{e\ni v}\frac{q_e+a_v}{q_e-a_v}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Here \(\eta_v\in\{-1,0,1\}\) is the retained half-twist carrier.
At \(v\in U\), both multipliers are one. The main meridian remains
parabolic: in a local marked frame \(W_0^{-1}W_1\) is translation
by \(E-1\ne0\). These are representation statements. The completeness
of a positive metric, and the winding integers in its primitive
periods, require the additional argument in the
[marked completion note](research_positive_point_marked_completion.md).

## 4. Avoiding critical points of the primitive-period map

There is also a useful regularity divisor that can be removed without
disconnecting the marked component. Fix arbitrary integers \(n_v\),
\(v\in S\). In any local logarithmic chart put
\[
 t_v=-i\log Q_v,\qquad
 r_{v,e}=\frac12\log\frac{q_e+a_v}{q_e-a_v},\qquad
 F_v=\sum_{e\ni v}r_{v,e}-\frac{i n_v}{2}t_v.
 \tag{17}
\]
Choose the initial logarithms to vanish at \(o\). The derivatives
of (17) are independent of additive logarithmic periods. Direct
differentiation of (1) gives
\[
 \frac{\partial r_{v,e}}{\partial t_v}
       =\frac{1+a_v^2}{2q_e},\qquad
 \frac{\partial r_{v,e}}{\partial t_w}
       =-\frac{a_va_w}{2q_e}\quad(e=vw).
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus the selected Jacobian \(J_n=(\partial F_v/\partial t_w)\)
is rational, with
\[
 (J_n)_{vv}=\sum_{e\ni v}\frac{1+a_v^2}{2q_e}-\frac{i n_v}{2},
 \qquad
 (J_n)_{vw}=\begin{cases}
 -a_va_w/(2q_{vw}),&vw\in E_S,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{19}
\]
At \(o\), it is diagonal with entries \((d_v-i n_v)/2\), where
\(d_v\ge1\) is the original tree degree. It is therefore nonsingular
for every integer choice of \(n\).

**Corollary 4.** Every point of \(\mathcal R_S\) with
\(\det J_n\ne0\) can be connected to \(o\) through points having
that same property. Along such a path the actual primitive multipliers
admit continued logarithms, and the selected map (17) has a local
inverse at every point.

**Proof.** The determinant is a nonzero rational function, regular
on (8), since its value at \(o\) is nonzero. Apply Proposition 2
after deleting its zero divisor. The factors in (16) are nonzero on
(8), so they have logarithms along any parameterized interval. The
holomorphic inverse function theorem applies to (19) locally. \(\square\)

This path does not in general hold \(F_v\) constant. In particular it
is not a path of solutions of the fixed primitive filling equations
\(F_v=-\pi i\). Its endpoint logarithms can differ from independently
prescribed endpoint logarithms by integral periods. Corollary 4 does
not discard that difference.

## 5. What this resolves for short partial fillings

For a proposed short partial filling, the first question is whether its
marked discrete faithful representation has a lift to the original
coordinates (1). If such a lift is supplied, (5) tests whether it lies
on the marked complete component. If it also satisfies (8) and the
Jacobian condition, the preceding results provide a path of regular
marked representation and period charts from completeness to that
lift. The complex path can pass through tetrahedra with negative or
zero imaginary shape, provided the shapes themselves stay nondegenerate.

There is no proof here that every hyperbolic short partial filling has
such a lift. There is also no assertion that a regular path preserves
the original integration cycle or prevents cancellation of its geometric
contribution. Those are separate from the algebraic component and
period-map regularity proved above. On the positive domain, the stronger
metric and full-period conclusions already follow from the marked
completion theorem; this note adds complex continuation outside that
domain without extending those metric conclusions.

The independent-square-class argument for unrestricted edge parameters
already appears in Section 7 of
[the all-circle characteristic note](actual_all_circle_geometric_characteristic.md).
The additional issue here is the repeated roots created by fixing
\(a_U=0\), their marked component, and the regular continuation of
the literal original face construction. No new general claim about
multiquadratic extensions is intended.
