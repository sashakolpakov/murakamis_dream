# The complete value from a fixed number of nearby ordinary-color jets

Private written proof, 23 November 2026.

A single final row of an actual color recurrence reconstructs the
original complete value from quantum derivatives of only \(d\) colors
strictly below \(N\). The number of derivatives is fixed by the
operator, and the observation row has polynomial norm.
No full color orbit, cyclotomic candidate or arithmetic coefficient
hypothesis is needed for this local observation.

The input jets can have unknown exponential size. This is a reduction
of the actual data and their observation cost, not a geometric lower
bound or a new short-filling volume theorem.

## 1. Original source and the terminal divisor

Use the actual \(Q\)-only reduced color functions \(\mathcal C_r(Q)\)
and a specified all-level recurrence of [M]:
\[
 P=\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_j(Q,Y)T^j,\qquad TY=QYT,\qquad d\ge1,
\]
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{d}a_j(Q,Q^m)\mathcal C_{m+j}(Q)=0,
 \qquad m\ge1,\qquad a_d\ne0.
 \tag{1}
\]
The fixed coefficients are Laurent polynomials after one recorded
denominator clearing. Complex coefficients are allowed.
The actual reduced, zero-framed closing is
\[
 Q_N=e^{2\pi i/N},\qquad K_N=\mathcal C_N(Q_N).
 \tag{2}
\]
In [O]'s half-root convention \(q_N=e^{\pi i/N}\), the same
ordinary root value is \(\mathcal C_r(Q_N)=C_r(q_N)\).
Every original crossing phase, pivot and framing stays in these
color functions; no closed scalar is used to normalize the row.

In the Laurent ring factor the leading coefficient maximally as
\[
 a_d(Q,Y)=(1-Q^dY)^\mu b_d(Q,Y),\qquad
 B(Q)=b_d(Q,Q^{-d}).
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\mu\ge0\) is finite and \(B\ne0\). Indeed the substitution
\(Z=Q^dY\) identifies this Laurent ring with
\(\mathbb C[Q^{\pm1},Z^{\pm1}]\); evaluation at \(Z=1\)
has kernel generated by \(1-Z\). Maximality proves \(B\ne0\).
In particular \(\mu\) is at most the \(Y\)-exponent span of \(a_d\).
Put
\[
                         v=\operatorname{ord}_{Q=1}B(Q)\ge0.
 \tag{4}
\]
All these quantities are fixed by the recorded operator.

## 2. Exact observation and polynomial stability

At fixed \(N>d\) vary the quantum parameter by
\[
 Q=Q_N(1+\zeta/N).
 \tag{5}
\]
The color dimensions are held fixed during differentiation. Define
the actual below-complete quantum jets and the known row coefficients
by
\[
 J_{j,\ell,N}=[\zeta^\ell]\,
    \mathcal C_{N-d+j}\!\left(Q_N(1+\zeta/N)\right),
 \quad 0\le j<d,\quad 0\le\ell\le\mu,
\]
\[
 A_{j,r,N}=[\zeta^r]\,
 a_j\!\left(Q_N(1+\zeta/N),
            [Q_N(1+\zeta/N)]^{N-d}\right).
 \tag{6}
\]
These are honest derivatives of integral-color functions at fixed
color, including their off-root pivot and framing.
They are not fixed-\(Q_N\) meridian derivatives of the root ADO state.

**Theorem.** For every sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 K_N=\frac{(-1)^{\mu+1}}{B(Q_N)}
       \sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}
                   A_{j,\mu-\ell,N}J_{j,\ell,N}.
 \quad} \tag{7}
\]
The total absolute row weight satisfies
\[
 \frac1{|B(Q_N)|}
       \sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\mu}|A_{j,\mu-\ell,N}|
                            \le C N^v .
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus only \(d(\mu+1)\) scalar jets are supplied, and every color
\(N-d,\ldots,N-1\) is strictly between zero and \(N\).
There is no parity or primality restriction.

For arbitrary approximate data \(\widetilde J_{j,\ell,N}\), define
\(G_N\) by the same explicit row (7). Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 |G_N-K_N|\le C N^v
       \max_{j,\ell}|\widetilde J_{j,\ell,N}-J_{j,\ell,N}|.
 \quad} \tag{9}
\]
The approximations need not be coefficients of a common lifted
section. The assertion compares them with the specified actual jets.
Constants may depend on the knot, its fixed filling, and the chosen
fixed recurrence. No uniform bound over growing words is asserted.

## 3. Proof of the exact row and its cost

Use row \(m=N-d\) in the generic identity (1), before root evaluation.
At its leading entry,
\[
 a_d(Q,Q^{N-d})=(1-Q^N)^\mu b_d(Q,Q^{N-d}).
 \tag{10}
\]
Under (5), \(Q_N^N=1\) gives
\[
 1-Q^N=1-(1+\zeta/N)^N=-\zeta+O(\zeta^2).
 \tag{11}
\]
The coefficient of \(\zeta\) is exactly \(-1\) at every level.
Also \(Q_N^{N-d}=Q_N^{-d}\), so the last factor in (10)
has value exactly \(B(Q_N)\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\) that value is nonzero.
The coefficient of \(\zeta^\mu\) in the last term of (1) is
therefore
\[
                         (-1)^\mu B(Q_N)K_N.
 \tag{12}
\]
No positive-order jet of the complete color occurs in (12).
The other terms give the convolution in (7), including all lower
ordinary-color jets. This proves (7) with its displayed sign.

For the quantitative claim, a monomial \(Q^aY^b\) in any fixed
\(a_j\) becomes \(Q^{E_N}\), where \(E_N=a+b(N-d)=O(N)\).
Its scaled Taylor coefficient is
\[
 [\zeta^r]Q^{E_N}
           =Q_N^{E_N}\binom{E_N}{r}N^{-r}.
 \tag{13}
\]
For each fixed \(r\le\mu\), this is bounded independently of \(N\),
also for negative integral \(E_N\). Finite sums prove
\(|A_{j,r,N}|\le C\).

Write \(B(Q)=(Q-1)^vB_0(Q)\), \(B_0(1)\ne0\).
Laurent polynomials are holomorphic at one, so
\[
 |B(Q_N)|\ge c|Q_N-1|^v\ge c'N^{-v}
 \tag{14}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Here \(|Q_N-1|=2\sin(\pi/N)\ge4/N\).
Equations (13)--(14) prove (8), and the triangle inequality proves (9).
No companion-product inversion or algebraic-number estimate is used.

The earlier root-normal coordinate is retained if desired:
\[
 \delta=1-Q^N=1-(1+\zeta/N)^N,\qquad
 \zeta=N[(1-\delta)^{1/N}-1].
 \tag{15}
\]
Both substitutions have uniformly bounded fixed Taylor coefficients,
with linear coefficient \(-1\). Their triangular fixed-depth jet
changes and inverses are uniformly bounded. The gain in (7) is the
selection of a fixed nearby color block, not a coordinate relabeling.

## 4. What is now isolated, and prior coverage

The ordinary root estimate [O] is uniform throughout \(1\le r<N\).
For a fixed \(b\)-strand knot presentation it gives
\[
 |\mathcal C_r(Q_N)|\le\sin(\pi/N)^{1-b}
                                      \le(N/2)^{b-1}.
 \tag{16}
\]
If \(\mu=0\), (7)--(8) consequently imply
\(|K_N|\le C N^{v+b-1}\).
More generally the \(\ell=0\) part of (7) is polynomially bounded.
Any positive exponential size in this row must therefore enter through
the finitely many \(\ell\ge1\) quantum jets.
Equation (16) alone does not bound those derivatives.

The stronger necessary boundary-column condition is already proved
in [P, Section 2]: positive complete growth forces
\(a_j(Q,Q^{-j})\equiv0\) for every \(j\), equivalently the actual
Laurent operator has a retained right factor \(1-Y\), up to sign.
It is prior coverage, not an addition here.

The full ordinary-orbit reconstruction [J] already gives a polynomial
row from quantum jets in all noncomplete residue classes, using genuine
high colors up to \(HN-1\). Formula (7) instead uses one recurrence row
and only \(d\) colors below \(N\); its derivative depth depends on the
terminal factor of that specified recurrence.

This is different input from the earlier quantitative Mellin theorem.
That theorem controls error from an approximate whole source equation,
its prefix and pole lattice. Here those hypotheses are replaced by
approximations to the actual near-\(N\) jets themselves.
Their construction and geometric asymptotics are not supplied.
The row is complex and signed: large packet norm does not give
a lower bound for its observed sum.
No nonzero geometric coefficient or volume theorem follows.
