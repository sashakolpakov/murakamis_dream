# The own-three upper wedge with the complete first factor

Private research proof candidate, 8 November 2026. The positive
upper-row block isolated in
[the finite wedge theorem](research_mixed_next_own_three_upper_physical_wedge.md)
can be attached to the complete first conditional kernel, not only
its below-half part. The first upper physical rows are reduced to
their original half and last alternatives. The last alternative
has a slightly different moving-row multiplier; its possible
decrease is bounded explicitly and paid by the original next
half-row certificate.

The complete next own-three kernel is still not estimated: its
physical rows above the wedge remain. No numerical evaluation
or frozen-source edit is used.

## 1. Literal statement and source inputs

Retain exactly the actual ordinary-next own-three domain,
heights, original weights, complete cap, following plane
\(\Lambda_{123}(z)\), and positive finite sum
\(\mathcal U_{0,N}^{\mathrm{wedge}}\) of equations (1)--(4)
in the finite wedge theorem. Thus \(x,u\in P=[11/50,9/20]\),
\(q\in[0,11/50]\), the first and shared heights are negative,
and \(-.065<h_z<.068\). Both first and next incoming carriers
remain present. Write
\[
\delta_a=-2\pi h_a,\quad R=e^{2\pi h_y}\in(13/25,1),
\quad Y=Re^{-2\pi iq},\quad Z=e^{-2\pi iz}.
\tag{1}
\]
The next own flag is zero, so there is no outgoing halfweight.

**Theorem.** At all sufficiently large levels of both parities,
uniformly for all real ordinary colors and the actual independent
targets above,
\[
\boxed{\quad
|U_{0,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(Y)|\,
\mathcal U_{0,N}^{\mathrm{wedge}}(Y,Z;q)
\le C N^B e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11}\}} .
\quad}\tag{2}
\]
Here \(C_{1,N}\) is the complete original sum \(0\le i<N\).
The next positive sum still has exactly
\[
\lceil N/2\rceil\le j\le\lfloor N(18/25-q)\rfloor.
\tag{3}
\]
Arbitrary subsets obey the same bound. The same estimate applies
to the actual central first incoming integral, with its original
measure \(+N\,da\). Fixed-base signed partial sums have normalized
local mixed jets with gap \(10^{-12}\), with the same conventions
as the finite wedge theorem.

We use the first assertion of
[the own-four upper-row theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_own_four_upper_rows.md),
Section 2. Its precise domain is the present
\(x\in P,\ q\in[0,.22]\), with \(-.072<h_a<0\) and
\(-.102<h_y<-.001\). It bounds the entire positive first
upper physical block, including its last index, by a polynomial
times
\[
e^{N\{\max(E_1(1/2;a,y),\delta_a)+\tau\}}
\tag{4}
\]
for any fixed tolerance \(\tau>0\). The proof preserves the
incoming carrier, every numerator zero, and both parities.
The first half alternative in (4) is already a controlling
profile in the finite wedge theorem. Only the last alternative
requires a new comparison.

## 2. First last row paired with the actual next half row

Let \(B_U(x)\) be the original complete cap plane, and define
\[
\mathcal H(q)
=B_U(x)+\delta_a+E_0(1/2;y,z)-\Re g(y).
\tag{5}
\]
The outgoing type-three allocation is zero. At \(q_0=11/50\),
the actual first last-row certificate and the actual next
own-three half-row certificate give
\[
\mathcal H(q_0)<\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/2000.
\tag{6}
\]
Precisely, these are the all-typed first last-row and both-own-type
half-row comparisons assembled in
[ordinary source assembly, Sections 1--2](research_mixed_three_row_source_assembly.md).
The original cap plane cancels the same first incoming marginal,
and the full shared weight is split into its two original halves.
The first last-row certificate is used only for its strict sign;
the explicit \(1/2000\) in (6) belongs to the next half-row
certificate. No uniform numerical margin is assigned to the
first last-row certificate.

The half-row derivative is independent of the own flag and of
the outgoing height:
\[
\mathcal H'(q)=
\frac12\log(1+R^2+2R\cos2\pi q)
 -\log(1+R^2-2R\cos2\pi q).
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed \(\Re g(1/2)=0\), and any change of the own denominator
or outgoing allocation is constant in \(q\) at this row.
Thus the proof in
[the first-last reduction, Section 4](research_mixed_next_lower_left_first_last_four_reduction.md)
applies verbatim to this derivative. It gives positivity for
\(q\le16/75\), and a lower bound \(-1/15\) thereafter.
The remaining interval has length \(1/150\), so
\[
\mathcal H(q)<\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/18000
\quad(0\le q\le q_0).
\tag{8}
\]
This extends just that half-profile calculation to own type three,
using its actual old certificates in (6). It is not an extension
of the other own-four main or low-cut claims.

There is a stronger bound for \(R\le7/10\). Expression (7)
is then positive on the entire interval. It increases with
\(\cos2\pi q>9/50\); at \(c=9/50\) the sufficient difference is
\[
1+R^2+\frac9{25}R-
       \left(1+R^2-\frac9{25}R\right)^2>0.
\tag{9}
\]
This is strictly concave on \(1/2\le R\le7/10\).
Its endpoint values are \(2851/10000\) and \(52339/250000\).
Hence
\[
R\le7/10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal H(q)<\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/2000
\quad(0\le q\le q_0).
\tag{10}
\]

## 3. Moving the last-row alternative with the next physical index

For the original physical rate \(E_0\), set
\[
\mathcal L(q,v)
=B_U(x)+\delta_a+E_0(1-v;y,z)-\Re g(y).
\tag{11}
\]
Use the same path as the finite wedge theorem:
\[
v-q=d\ge7/25,\qquad
q\ \hbox{increases to}\ q_e=1/2-d\le11/50.
\tag{12}
\]
At \(q_e\), (11) is exactly (5). Direct differentiation of
the physical integral, including the incoming carrier, gives
\[
\boxed{\quad
\exp\frac d{dq}\mathcal L(q,q+d)
=\frac{R\{2\sin(\pi v)\}^2}
 {|1-Y|^2\,|1-Ze^{2\pi iv}|}.
\quad}\tag{13}
\]
In particular the first numerator factor from the previous
wedge theorem is absent, and the own shared denominator has
power two rather than three. Equation (13) integrates through
any outgoing numerator zero with its original logarithmic limit.

The outgoing modulus is less than two by the exact bound (13)
of the finite wedge theorem. For \(D_c(R)=1+R^2-2cR\),
\[
\frac d{dR}\frac{R}{D_c(R)}
=\frac{1-R^2}{D_c(R)^2}\ge0.
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently the following lower bounds hold:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
q\text{ interval}&c<\cos2\pi q&R/|1-Y|^2&
                         \{2\sin(\pi v)\}^2\\ \hline
[0,1/10]&4/5&>1&>7/3\\
[1/10,3/20]&7/12&>3/4&>17/5\\
[3/20,9/50]&21/50&>3/5&>19/5\\
[9/50,21/100]&37/150&>975/1901&>39/10\\
[21/100,11/50]&37/200&>260/539&>399/100 .
\end{array}\tag{15}
\]
The first three radial bounds follow by (14) from
\[
\frac{325}{274}>1,\qquad
\frac{1950}{2489}>\frac34,\qquad
\frac{325}{521}>\frac35.
\]
The fourth uses
\[
\cos(21\pi/50)=\sin(2\pi/25)
>\sin(1/4)>37/150
\]
and the exact denominator \(D_{37/150}(13/25)=1901/1875\).
The last uses \(D_{37/200}(13/25)=539/500\).
The earlier sine-chord bounds were proved in the wedge theorem.
For the final one, \(v\ge49/100\), so
\[
\{2\sin(\pi v)\}^2\ge2+2\cos(\pi/50)>399/100,
\]
using \(\pi/50<11/175\) and the quadratic cosine lower bound.

After division by the outgoing modulus, the first four bounds
in (15) make (13) greater than
\[
7/6,\quad51/40,\quad57/50,\quad7605/7604,
\]
respectively. Thus the action increases through \(q=21/100\).
On the final interval, (13) remains greater than
\[
\frac{260}{539}\frac{399}{200}=\frac{5187}{5390}.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(\log(1+\xi)<\xi\), the total possible decrease on
an interval of length at most \(1/100\) is less than
\[
\frac{203}{518700}<\frac1{2500}.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(R\le7/10\), combine (10) and (17):
\[
\mathcal L(q,v)
<\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/2000+1/2500
=\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/10000 .
\tag{18}
\]

For \(R\ge7/10\) no debit is needed. On the only potentially
adverse interval \(q\ge9/50\), (14) and
\(\cos2\pi q>37/200\) give
\[
\frac{R}{|1-Y|^2}>
\frac{7/10}{149/100-(37/100)(7/10)}
=\frac{700}{1231}.
\]
The sine chord there exceeds \(39/10\), so (13) is greater
than \(1365/1231>1\). Earlier intervals were already paid
by (15). Equations (8),(12) therefore give
\[
\mathcal L(q,v)<\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/18000
\qquad(R\ge7/10).
\tag{19}
\]
The uniform first-last comparison on the whole wedge follows
from (18)--(19).

## 4. Complete first factor and exact remaining rows

Split the literal first kernel at \(\lceil N/2\rceil\).
Its complete lower part, with all canonical middle currents,
is covered by the finite wedge theorem. For its upper part
use the positive estimate (4). The half alternative is covered
by that theorem's real-half profile, and the last alternative
by (18)--(19). Every next row retains the exact positive
finite estimate, endpoint convention and outgoing zeros of
that theorem. Choose all upper-product tolerances below the
unused explicit margins, and sum the finitely many groups.
This proves (2), with no assertion that the modulus of a
coherent sum bounds its individual terms.

The complete cap's all-real-color estimate, the original forced
\(d_N\) bound through \(q=0\), and the factorial seam continuation
are unchanged. The original upper-row theorem includes both
true index ends and both parities. There is no new factor of
\(N\) in the finite products; the original \(+N\) is inserted
only for an actual incoming integral. Fixed-base local mixed
jets follow exactly as in the wedge theorem, preserving all
index sets and the original holomorphic normalization.

The remaining upper physical rows of the next own-three factor are
\[
\lfloor N(18/25-q)\rfloor<j<N.
\tag{20}
\]
They are not estimated by this note. The products of the first
upper physical block with the next own-three below-half block
also remain outside this theorem; the own-four first-last
reduction is not a proof of those own-three combinations.
The result does not change
any Fourier selection, remove a cardinal correction, estimate
the other incoming paths, or prove a full next transfer.
