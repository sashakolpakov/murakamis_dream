# The complete selected first four/four source on the early interval

Private written proof, 10 November 2026. The complete original selected
first four/four product has a strict gap on the early rectangle at its
fixed selected height. The main/main comparison is the separate early
central theorem. Here every other cap profile, ordinary real row,
alias and joining current is retained and bounded. The outgoing
ordinary halfweight is unchanged.

## 1. Actual reference, exact source and conclusion

Fix an actual positive three/four word of length at least three
beginning with four and four. Retain the coupled reference, branches
and calibration of the
[selected next-four finite reduction](research_mixed_selected_four_four_finite_reduction.md).
In particular the actual heights satisfy
\[
 h_e\in[-.051,-.019],\qquad h_s,d\in[-.102,-.001].
\tag{1}
\]
We sometimes enlarge the latter interval to \([-.108,0]\) for
an analytic estimate, without changing the reference. Put
\[
 H=1/100,\quad a=x+iH,\quad b=z+id,\qquad
 x\in[1/10,11/50],\quad z\in P=[11/50,9/20].
\tag{2}
\]
For \(q_N=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(q_N;q_N)_j\),
\(p_j(X)=(q_NX;q_N)_j\), \(X=e^{-2\pi ia}\),
\(Y=e^{-2\pi ib}\), keep
\[
\begin{split}
 U_{1,N}(w,X)&=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}
                 \frac{H_N(w,q_N^{-i})p_i(X)}{P_i^2},\\
 C_{1,N}(X,Y)&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}R_{j,N},\qquad
 R_{j,N}=X^{-j}\frac{p_j(X)p_j(Y)}{P_j^3},\\
 \mathcal K_N(w;X,Y)&=U_{1,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(X)X^{-N}.
\end{split}
\tag{3}
\]
The coefficient \(H_N\) is the complete original endpoint source.
There is one whole selected Fourier factor at \(X\). The outgoing
allocation is the separate modulus \(|d_N(Y)|^{1/2}\), not a
holomorphic square root. No new scalar is inserted.

Use the original bare phases
\[
\begin{split}
 \Psi(e,a)&=S(e)+g(e+a)-g(a)-g(e)-\frac{i\pi}{2}(e-e^2),\\
 Q_4(s;a,b)&=g(s+a)+g(s+b)-3g(s)-g(a)-g(b)+2\pi ias,\\
 \mathcal F_4&=\Psi+Q_4-g(a)-\tfrac12g(b),\\
 M&=\Re\mathcal F_4(e_*,s_*;a_*,b_*),\quad
 p=(\mathcal F_4)_b(e_*,s_*;a_*,b_*),\\
 \Lambda(z)&=M+\Re p\,(z-\Re b_*).
\end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
The larger selected Fourier upper rate on the early interval is
\[
                         W(x)=-\Re g(a)-2\pi H>3/40.
\tag{5}
\]
The other upper alternative is zero, so (5) retains both alternatives
of the same exact polynomial. This is the scalar branch comparison
in the [early central theorem](research_mixed_selected_four_four_early_central.md),
not a decomposition of \(d_N(X)\).

**Theorem 1.** For every \(\tau>0\), both parities and every fixed
ordinary-color derivative order \(k\), a sufficiently small fixed
ordinary-color disk about \(-2\) gives
\[
 |\partial_w^k\mathcal K_N(w;X,Y)|\,|d_N(Y)|^{1/2}
 \le C_{\tau,k}N^{A_{\tau,k}}
                  e^{N\{\Lambda(z)-1/2000+\tau\}}
\tag{6}
\]
uniformly on (2) for all sufficiently large \(N\), on a smaller
disk for derivatives. The same zeroth-order rate holds on the whole
real ordinary-color interval \([-2,2]\).
Every index in (3), every cap correction and every ordinary contour
term is included. This is a fixed-height two-factor upper bound;
it makes no assertion about changing-height outer joins or the
whole word.

## 3. The ordinary real cuts and the left cap cut

Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 r=e^{2\pi H},\quad D=|1-r e^{-2\pi ix}|,\quad
 N_e=|1-rR e^{-2\pi i(u+x)}|,\quad R=e^{2\pi\Im e},\\
 N_s=|q-r e^{-2\pi i(v+x)}|,\qquad q=e^{-2\pi\Im s}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{14}
\]
Exact differentiation, retaining the chirps and whole first weight,
gives
\[
 \partial_x\Re\{\Psi(e,a)+Q_4(s;a,b)-g(a)-\tfrac12g(b)\}
                           =\log\frac{N_eN_s}{D^3}.
\tag{15}
\]
The selected constant and outgoing half allocation have no
additional \(x\)-derivative. Throughout the comparisons below,
\[
 1<r<43/40,\qquad q\ge1,\qquad
 \frac{D^2}{r}<\frac{49}{30},\quad D^2<\frac95,\quad
 \frac{D^6}{r^2}<\frac{5058907}{1080000}<\frac{569}{120}.
\tag{16}
\]
The elementary radius and denominator bounds are proved in the
early central source; they use \(\cos2\pi x>14/75\).
Here \(q\ge1\) covers both the actual main height and real cuts.

On the main cap restricted by (9), \(R>18/25\) and
\(u+x\in[.37,.65]\). The written cosine bound
\(\cos2\pi(u+x)<-7/12\) gives
\[
 N_e^2>1+\frac{324}{625}r^2+\frac{21}{25}r,\qquad
                         \frac{N_e^2}{r}>\frac94.
\tag{17}
\]
This is the cap-only bound in Section 5 of the cited early
four/three source, with the same restricted fixed private interval.
At the ordinary half row,
\[
                    N_s^2/r\ge178/75.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus the squared numerator of (15), divided by \(r^2\),
exceeds \(267/50>569/120\); its derivative is positive.

At the ordinary left cut \(v=1/8\), split at \(x=.16\).
For \(x\le.16\), \(\cos2\pi x>1/2\), so
\[
 D^2/r<101/100,\qquad D^6/r^2<(101/100)^3(43/40)<6/5.
\tag{19}
\]
Also \(v+x\ge.225\) and \(v+x\le.285\), whence
\(\cos2\pi(v+x)<1/6\): use
\(\cos(.45\pi)=\sin(.05\pi)<11/70<1/6\).
Since this is a real cut, \(q=1\), and hence \(N_s^2/r>5/3\).
Together with (17) this pays (19).
For \(x\ge.16\), \(v+x\in[.285,.345]\), so
\[
 \cos2\pi(v+x)<-1/5,\qquad N_s^2/r>12/5.
\tag{20}
\]
One exact witness is
\(\sin(.07\pi)>\sin(21/100)>
21/100-(21/100)^3/6>1/5\).
The squared product in (15) now exceeds \(27/5>569/120\).
Both ranges therefore have positive derivative.

The fixed private restriction in (9) allows pointwise transport
to \(x_0\) before maximizing. Applying (11) yields
\[
                         A_0+B_L+W,\ A_0+B_R+W
                                  \le\Lambda(z)-1/2000.
\tag{21}
\]

For the left cap cut \(e=.27\), the original written cosine
guard \(\cos2\pi(e+x)<-2/3\) gives
\[
                            N_e^2/r>10/3.
\tag{22}
\]
Every ordinary private point or real cut in (10) has
\(v+x\in[.225,.72]\), whose cosine is less than \(1/6\).
Consequently, using \(q\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{N_s^2}{r}>
       \frac{q^2}{r}+r-\frac q3\ge\frac{5q}{3}\ge\frac53.
\tag{23}
\]
The squared product exceeds \(50/9>569/120\), proving
positive derivative in (15) throughout this whole private set.
Transport and (11) give
\[
                         A_L+B_j+W\le\Lambda(z)-1/2000
                              \qquad(j=0,L,R).
\tag{24}
\]
The two alias profiles and the right cut follow from (9).
This proves every regular-cap comparison with (10).

## 4. True cap ends remain separate

For (8) the derivative is not (15). At every ordinary main

\[
 \partial_x\{E_*+\Re Q_4(s;a,b)-\tfrac12\Re g(b)+W\}

\tag{25}
\]
Indeed (23) gives \(N_s^2>5/3>25/16\), while (16) gives
\(D^2<9/5\). Thus \(N_s/D^2>25/36>3/5\);
\(\log(3/5)>-3/5\) follows from the positive exponential
series. The early interval has length \(3/25\), so transporting
backwards from \(x_0\) costs less than \(9/125\).

against the main cap. The remaining reserve is greater than

                         E_*+B_j+W<\Lambda(z)-1/2000
                              \qquad(j=0,L,R).
\tag{26}
\]
Only the literal endpoint ceiling was transported. No endpoint row
has been replaced by a regular main packet.

We have now paid the full list

 A_i+B_j+W\le\Lambda(z)-1/2000,\qquad
 i\in\{0,L,R,-,+,*\},\quad j\in\{0,L,R\},\quad A_*=E_*.
\tag{27}
\]
No separate last-row comparison will be needed: Section 6 reduces
the entire high physical block to \(B_R\).

## 5. All ordinary central contour terms

Use the exact original factorial interpolation with half-grid cuts
tending to \(1/8,1/2\), lower height \(-1/5\), and upper
height \(2\). These heights are fixed before \(N\). All real
factorial arguments stay in compact regular strips: in particular
\[
 \Re(s+a)\in[.225,.72],\qquad
 \Re(s+b)\in[.345,.95].
\tag{28}
\]
The [exact factorial theorem](exact_factorial_interpolation_and_complex_contours.md)
permits any fixed compact imaginary-height interval. The same
cotangent residue identity described in the
[asymmetric-contour source](research_mixed_horizontal_asymmetric_contour.md),
Section 4, therefore retains exactly the central physical rows,
its \(+N\) zero-mode density, both horizontal aliases and all
four vertical alias halves. No interpolation pole is crossed.

Here are the required own-four argument bounds, derived afresh.
Use the original regular-strip argument
\(\mathcal A(t,h)=\arg(1-e^{2\pi h-2\pi it})\), continued
from height zero for \(0<t<1\).
For \(s=v+iy\), \(1/8\le v\le1/2\), write
\[
 I=\Im(Q_4)_s=
 \mathcal A(v+x,y+H)+\mathcal A(v+z,y+d)
                  -3\mathcal A(v,y)+2\pi x,
\tag{29}
\]
on the original lift. Since \(x\le z\), there are three ordered
cases. The all-height upper bounds, after division by \(\pi\),
are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 (v+x,v+z)&\text{upper bound}\\ \hline
 (<1/2,<1/2)&2-4v-2z\le53/50\\
 (<1/2,\ge1/2)&1-2v\le3/4\\
 (\ge1/2,\ge1/2)&2x\le11/25 .
\end{array}
\tag{30}
\]
For \(y\le0\), use
\(\mathcal A(v,y)\le\pi(1/2-v)\). The three lower bounds
are respectively
\[
 -3/2+3v+2x\ge-37/40,\quad
 -1/2+v+2x-2z\ge-43/40,\quad
 1/2-v-2z\ge-9/10.
\tag{31}
\]
For \(y\ge0\), the own argument is at most
\(\pi(1-2v)\). When \(v+x<1/2\), its positive first
height gives
\(\mathcal A(v+x,y+H)\ge\pi(1/2-v-x)\).
The corresponding lower bounds are
\[
 -5/2+5v+x\ge-71/40,\quad
 -3/2+3v+x-2z\ge-77/40,\quad
 -1+2v-2z\ge-33/20 .
\tag{32}
\]
These are the elementary radius-argument inequalities of the
original alias source, with the incoming chirp retained.
Half arguments are included by continuity. In particular
\[
 I\le(53/50)\pi,\qquad
 I\ge-(43/40)\pi\ (y\le0),\qquad
 I\ge-(77/40)\pi\ (y\ge0).
\tag{33}
\]

Since \(\partial_y\Re Q_4=-I\), integration from the actual
main height \(h_s\in[-.108,0]\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \Re Q_4(v-i/5)-2\pi/5
   &\le\Re Q_4(v+ih_s)-47\pi/250,\\
 \Re Q_4(v+2i)-4\pi
   &\le\Re Q_4(v+ih_s)-339\pi/10000
    <\Re Q_4(v+ih_s)-1/10.
\end{split}
\tag{34}
\]
For the first line the height distance is at most \(1/5\).
For the second, the cost below zero is at most
\((43/40)(27/250)\pi\), and the cost above zero is
\((77/40)2\pi\). Subtracting \(4\pi\) gives the stated
reserve. This uses only \(\pi>3\).

At either real cut the upper weighted vertical rate has derivative
\(-I-2\pi\le-3\pi/40\) for \(y\ge0\), and the lower
weighted rate has derivative
\(-I+2\pi\ge47\pi/50\) for \(y\le0\).
Thus every vertical alias half is bounded by its real-cut endpoint.
For a zero-mode join from \(0\) to \(h_s\), the curvature
proof in Section 2 of the early four/four central theorem remains
valid for every index height in \([-.108,0]\): only its
absolute-height bound enters the cotangent estimates. In particular
\(\Re(Q_4)_{ss}<0\). Holomorphy gives vertical convexity,
so each such join is bounded by its main and real endpoints.

Equations (27), (34) pay this entire central inventory after
multiplication by the complete cap and original weights. The cap
aliases have already been bounded at the same private real point;
no double alias or joining term is discarded. The changed upper
height changes only a legal contour and its explicit Fourier

## 6. Every physical row and the full weighted product

Partition the exact ordinary sum into
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 0\le j\le\lfloor N/8\rfloor,&\text{low},\\
 \lfloor N/8\rfloor+1\le j\le\lceil N/2\rceil-1,
                                      &\text{central},\\
 \lceil N/2\rceil\le j<N,&\text{high}.
 \end{array}
\tag{35}
\]
The preceding section treats the central block, with the appropriate
half-grid cuts. The
[exact low-row ratio theorem](research_mixed_first_attached_low_row_transport.md)
applies to the actual heights (1)--(2), including \(d\ge-.102\).
Its ratio is greater than \(e^{1/200}\) for every step up to
\(N/5\), so the absolute low block in (35) is below 201 times
its final row. That row has continuous allocated rate \(B_L\),
up to the original rounding tolerance. This pays every low row,
including \(j=0\), by (27).

For the high block the exact drift is zero:
\[
                 2\pi\{(-H)_++d_+\}=0.
\tag{36}
\]
The [all-angle high-row theorem](research_mixed_all_angle_high_row_sign_class.md),
Theorem 2 and Corollary 3, applies directly, with own flag one,
to the selected targets (2). Its hypotheses concern the target
height signs and angles, not a new actual reference. It proves
\[
 \sum_{j=\lceil N/2\rceil}^{N-1}|R_{j,N}|
 \le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
           e^{N\{E_1(1/2;a,b)+\tau\}}.
\tag{37}
\]
Here
\[
 E_1(t;a,b)=\int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(a+u)}|\,du
 +\int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(b+u)}|\,du
                         -3J(t)-2\pi Ht
\]
is the full original absolute row rate. At \(t=1/2\), adding
the outgoing rate \(-\Re g(b)/2\) gives exactly \(B_R\).
The source proof is zero-safe on every physical index, including
all wrapped and revived rows and the literal stop \(N-1\).
It is a comparison of continuous rates, not division by a
possibly zero finite half row. Multiplying (37) by the entire
cap and full selected factor, (27) pays the high block.

Finally the Fourier theorem bounds the whole
\(d_N(X)X^{-N}\) by the larger rate (5); no forcing term is
omitted. Since the actual \(d<0\), the outgoing allocation
has its original upper rate \(-\Re g(b)/2\). All factorial
errors, half-grid displacements, finite counts, both \(+N\)
current densities and polynomial symbols consume one prescribed
total \(\tau\). Adding the three exact blocks proves (6)
at complete color and uniformly on the whole real ordinary-color
interval, using the all-real cap envelope.

The exact cap has degree less than \(N\) in its ordinary color;
all other factors are independent of that color. Uniform interval
polynomial continuation therefore supplies a fixed disk at any
prescribed additional rate allowance. On a smaller disk Cauchy's
formula gives all fixed derivatives. This uses the all-real bound
for the literal product, not continuation of a bound at one color.
If a color-independent factor vanishes the inequality is immediate;
no division or choice of square root is needed.

Both parities, the original attached next-input case at length three,
all subsequent positive three/four types, and the original outgoing
affine term are unchanged. The theorem is only the complete
fixed-height source (3). It neither deforms an unestimated outer
join nor proves whole-word noncancellation, knot identification or
a finite-exception result for short partial fillings.
