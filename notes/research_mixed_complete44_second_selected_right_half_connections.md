# Right-half connections while the second coordinate is selected

Private written deduction, 11 November 2026. For an actual positive
three/four word beginning with \(443\) or \(444\), this note pays the
first-coordinate paths from the old right plateau face to the real
half point. The second coordinate stays on its selected plateau at
height \(1/100\), and the third stays on its original unselected
plateau. Both first Fourier choices, the complete third factor and
its outgoing allocation are retained. The subsequent real interval
from \(1/2\) to \(7/8\) is not included.

## 1. Literal source and calibrated statement

Use the exact source and reference of the
[second-selected far-first-arc theorem](research_mixed_complete44_second_selected_far_first_arc.md),
equations (1)--(7). In particular \(m\ge4\), the first three flags
are \((1,1,\beta)\), \(\beta\in\{0,1\}\), and
\[
 a_*=a_0+ic,\quad .246<a_0<.350,\quad -.078<c<-.020,
 \qquad P=[11/50,9/20],\qquad H=1/100.
 \tag{1}
\]
The cap index height satisfies \(-.051<h_e<-.019\). The first
ordinary index \(s_2^*\) satisfies
\[
                  -.102<h_s:=\Im s_2^*<-.001.
 \tag{2}
\]
This is the actual reduced-disk/ordinary-image intersection in
equation (3) of the second-selection reduction, not a restriction
on an independently chosen reference. It holds also at \(m=4\).
Put
\[
 y=x+iH,\quad x\in P,\qquad
 z=v+i\Im r_3,\quad v\in P,
 \qquad X=e^{-2\pi ia},\quad Y=e^{-2\pi iy},\quad Z=e^{-2\pi iz}.
 \tag{3}
\]
With \(q_N=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(q_N;q_N)_j\), and
\(p_j(X)=(q_NX;q_N)_j\), retain
\[
 U_{1,N}(w,X)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}H_N(w,q_N^{-i})p_i(X)/P_i^2,
 \qquad
 C_{\epsilon,N}(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
                  X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)/P_j^{2+\epsilon}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The modulus under consideration is precisely
\[
 \mathcal B_{\sigma,\beta,N}(a,y,z)=
 |U_{1,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)C_{\beta,N}(Y,Z)
       d_N(X)X^{-\sigma N}d_N(Y)Y^{-N}|\,|d_N(Z)|^{\beta/2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The last factor equals one when \(\beta=0\), including at a zero
of \(d_N\). Let \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\) be exactly the affine action
in equation (6) of the far-first-arc theorem. At the actual reference
its constant is
\[
 \Re\{\mathcal T_1(r_1,-2)+Q_1(s_2^*;r_1,r_2)
       +Q_\beta(s_3^*;r_2,r_3)-g(r_1)-g(r_2)-\beta g(r_3)/2\},
 \tag{6}
\]
and its sole target tangent is the original third-coordinate
marginal. The asymmetric chirp in
\[
 Q_\epsilon(s;a,b)=g(s+a)+g(s+b)-(2+\epsilon)g(s)
                         -g(a)-g(b)+2\pi ias
 \tag{7}
\]
is unchanged.

Set \(h_0=c\), \(h_1=H\), and orient
\[
 \mathcal P_\sigma:\quad
 9/20+ih_\sigma\longrightarrow1/2+ih_\sigma
                         \longrightarrow1/2.
 \tag{8}
\]

**Theorem 1.** For every \(\tau>0\), both parities, both
\(\sigma\in\{0,1\}\), and both \(\beta\), uniformly on (3) and
\(a\in\mathcal P_\sigma\),
\[
 \mathcal B_{\sigma,\beta,N}(a,y,z)
 \le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
        \exp N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/12800+\tau\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The assertion holds for every real ordinary color \(w\in[-2,2]\).
At an arbitrary separately reserved rate allowance it holds on a
fixed ordinary-color disk about \(-2\), with fixed derivatives on
a smaller disk. Integration along either increasing horizontal and
its indicated vertical, with the original \(+N\,da\) measure,
preserves the upper rate and the original exterior scalar.

The proof also retains a profilewise bound at the real half point;
it does not infer such a bound from a cancelling finite sum.

## 2. Common full cap and the old right-face comparisons

Use the five bare cap profiles with private real interval
\([.27,.49]\): the actual line at height \(h_e\), the real cuts
\(.27,.49\), and the lines at heights \(-.055,.04\), with penalties
\(.11\pi,.08\pi\). Their phase is the original
\[
 \Psi(e,a)=S(e)+g(e+a)-g(a)-g(e)-\frac{i\pi}{2}(e-e^2).
 \tag{10}
\]
Include the constant true-end ceiling
\[
                E_*:=3/5+2\pi H+\log4/100<69/100.
 \tag{11}
\]
The upper-target cap envelope and the full source check in Section 2
of the half-face theorem license precisely these six alternatives
for \(9/20\le\Re a\le1/2\), \(c\le\Im a\le H\). Compact
physical tails reduce to the two real cuts; the two actual index-end
groups are bounded by (11). All cap joins and aliases, revived rows,
ordinary colors and parity endpoints are included. This cap assertion
does not depend on the second target's height.

For the third factor keep the four original bare profiles
\(R_j^3(y,z)\), \(j\in\{0,L,R,1\}\), of the second-selection
reduction. Keep both second-weight rates
\[
              W_2^{\rm fac}=-\Re g(y)-2\pi H,
              \qquad W_2^{\rm force}=0.
 \tag{12}
\]
They and the outgoing rate \(-\beta\Re g(z)/2\) are constant
during every first-coordinate motion below.

At the old face \(\Re a=9/20\), each of the six cap alternatives
paired with any of the first ordinary main, left-cut, half-row or
full last-row profiles, each \(R_j^3\), and each (12) is paid
against \(\Lambda_{123}\) with common gap
\[
                              d_0=1/3200.
 \tag{13}
\]
For \(\sigma=0\), multiply the profilewise second-selected
completion by the original allocated cap plane. The incoming
halfweight and opposite cap marginal cancel, leaving the whole
first weight and exactly (6). The cap completion pays every one
of its profiles separately. Its bare plane is \(>149/200\),
whereas (11) is \(<69/100\); the same halfweight-convexity argument
therefore also pays this enlarged constant true-end ceiling.

For \(\sigma=1\), use the double-selected completion and its
underlying complete profile reduction. Its raw gap is \(3/1000\).
The first ordinary non-main profiles are dominated by its main
profile before multiplication, and the cap's other profiles are
dominated by the main or left-cut profiles before finite summation.
The true-end ceiling (11) is below the original cap main maximum
by more than \(53/500\), as in Section 2 of the selected right-half
proof. Thus (13) is available profilewise, with this extra reserve
for its true-end terms. These implications use the individual

bounds alone.

The old real-tail reduction is valid with both outer heights
changed to \(H\), as stated explicitly in the second-selection
reduction. Consequently (13) holds for each physical first-ordinary
row \(t\in[1/2,1]\). By uniform continuity of the integrated
zero-safe rates, fix \(0<\varepsilon<1/100\) so that the same

The common reference and profile families are compact. Put
\(r_0=1/2-\varepsilon\). Any further decrease of this fixed

chosen arbitrarily sufficiently close to \(1/2\), subject also to
any separately proved compact critical-point inclusion. All these
choices precede \(N\to\infty\).

## 3. The central main interval extends horizontally

The original first ordinary main line is
\(s=t+ih_s\), \(1/8\le t\le r_0\). At the old face, every
maximizer of a main or real-cut cap profile paired with this line
has \(t>11/50\), also when the cap is replaced by (11).

Here is the additional height-domain check. The next-four endpoint
budget proves a positive \(t\)-derivative at \(t=.22\) with
incoming height \(H\) and actual outgoing height. At this index,
the two shifted real coordinates belong to \([.44,.67]\), with
negative cosines. Raising the outgoing height to \(H\) strictly
increases its numerator modulus, and changes neither the chirp nor
the other factors of this derivative. If the first height is then
lowered from \(H\) to \(c\), its numerator divided by its radius
also increases: it has the form \(|r^{-1}-\rho e^{i\theta}|\)
with \(\cos\theta<0\). Thus the same positive barrier holds in
both cases. The index curvature proof used in Section 3 of the
selected right-half theorem is unchanged. Writing
\[
 U=\Re\cot\pi s,\quad C_y=\Re\cot\pi(s+y),\quad
 u_0=\cot\pi t,
 \tag{14}
\]
it gives \(U>u_0/2\), while, whenever \(C_y>0\),
\[
 C_y\le\cot\pi(t+x)
       =\frac{u_0p_x-1}{u_0+p_x}<u_0/2,
                 \qquad p_x=\cot\pi x<4/3.
 \tag{15}
\]
The first inequality follows directly from the cotangent formula
and is valid for either sign of \(\Im(s+y)\). If \(C_y\le0\)
there is nothing to prove. Hence \(3U-C_y>0\) throughout the
private interval and its zero-mode joining heights.

For first height \(H\), the exact old-face derivative test in
equations (12)--(17) of the selected right-half proof applies
unchanged: \(q=e^{-2\pi h_s}<19/10\), \(53/50<r=e^{2\pi H}<43/40\),
and the numerator/denominator comparison has its positive witness
\(17513/250000\). The outgoing height occurs in neither numerator
of the derivative with respect to the first real coordinate.
Both first alternatives therefore have a negative derivative at
every such old-face private maximizer.

For first height \(c\), set \(r=e^{2\pi c}\). Then \(3/5<r<1\):
\(.156\pi<1/2<\log(5/3)\). At an old-face maximizer let
\(D=|1-re^{-.9\pi i}|\), and let \(N_e,N_s\) be the two
normalized numerator moduli in that same derivative. The bounds
\(t>.22\), \(q<2\), and the cap main/cut radius at most one give
\[
 N_e^2<1+r^2+2r/5<D^2,\qquad
 N_s^2<4+r^2+2r,\qquad D^2>1+r^2+19r/10.
 \tag{16}
\]
The polynomial
\[
 (1+r^2+19r/10)^2-(4+r^2+2r)
 =r^4+19r^3/5+461r^2/100+9r/5-3
 \tag{17}
\]
has positive nonconstant coefficients and at \(r=3/5\) equals
\(69/100\). Thus \(N_s<D^2\) and \(N_eN_s<D^3\).
The unselected first rate is \(-\Re g(a)\) through this interval,
so its joint derivative is \(\log(N_eN_s/D^3)<0\).
For a constant true cap end it is \(\log(N_s/D^2)<0\).

These old-face derivative signs propagate by the original joint
real concavity. Put \(A=\Re\cot\pi a\ge0\),
\(d_e=-\Re\cot\pi(e+a)>0\), and
\(d_s=-\Re\cot\pi(s+a)>0\). For the first forced alternative
the negative real Hessian in the private cap coordinate, private
ordinary coordinate and first real target is
\[
 \pi\left[
 \operatorname{diag}(-\Re S''/\pi+\Re\cot\pi e,\,3U-C_y,\,2A)
 +d_e(1,0,1)^T(1,0,1)+d_s(0,1,1)^T(0,1,1)\right].
 \tag{18}
\]
It is positive definite, including at the half face, by the cap
curvature, (14)--(15), and the two rank-one terms. The factorial
alternative adds \(\pi A\) to its last diagonal. A fixed cap cut
deletes its private row and column. A true-end constant deletes
the cap terms altogether; its remaining Hessian is nonnegative.
All chirp Hessian terms are purely imaginary. Private first-order
optimality at a maximizing boundary point has the correct
nonpositive supporting tangent. Consequently the old maximum
cannot increase to the right.

For the positive first-height true-end constant, the forced
derivative need only be bounded by \(\log2<1\), exactly as in
Section 4 of the selected right-half proof. The possible increase
over length \(1/20\) is less than \(1/20\), paid by its separate
\(53/500\) reserve. Thus all main/cut cap profiles and true ends
times the first ordinary main line retain (13).

The two cap aliases also reduce to the same-real-coordinate cap
main. Equation (20) of the selected right-half proof gives extra
gaps \(>1/4\) and \(>1/50\). It remains valid for every first
height \(h\in[c,H]\): for a shifted real part above \(1/2\),
\(\Arg(1-e^{2\pi(\eta+h)-2\pi i(u+\Re a)})\) decreases as
\(h\) increases. Its lower bound at \(H\) is therefore still valid,
and its upper bound zero is height independent. The original
coefficient term and alias penalties do not change. No new cap
sheet or alternative phase is used.

## 4. Physical rows and the complete central contour

For a physical first ordinary index \(t\), retain
\[
 E(t;a,y)=I_{\Re a,e^{2\pi\Im a}}(t)
             +I_{x,e^{2\pi H}}(t)-3J(t)-2\pi(\Im a)t,
 \quad I_{b,r}(t)=\int_0^t\log|1-re^{-2\pi i(b+v)}|\,dv.
 \tag{19}
\]
On \(9/20\le\Re a\le1/2\), \(t\in[1/8,1]\), its incoming
numerator \(N_t\) obeys \(N_t\le D\). At first height \(c\),
every regular cap profile has \(N_e<D^2\); at first height \(H\),
it has \(N_e<D\). These are respectively (15)--(16) of the
unselected and (22)--(23) of the selected right-half proofs.
Their estimates include both cap aliases. The joint derivatives
are \(\log(N_eN_t/D^3)\) for the negative-height unselected case,
and \(\log(N_eN_t/D^k)\), \(k=2,3\), for the selected alternatives.
For true ends omit \(N_e\) and reduce the denominator exponent by
one. Every derivative is nonpositive. These formulas contain no
outgoing height. They transport every old high-row and cut
comparison, including the fixed right-cut collar, with gap \(d_0/2\).

For \(0<t\le1/8\), the ratio proof in equation (25) of the
selected right-half theorem works for any positive outgoing radius.
The first numerator divided by its radius exceeds one, the second
has squared modulus greater than \(24/25\), and
\(2\sin(\pi/8)<4/5\). Hence \(E_t>0\) uniformly. All low rows
are bounded by the left cut, including the actual endpoint.

Use the unchanged half-grid cuts tending to \(1/8,r_0\), lower
height \(-1/5\), upper height \(5/4\), and interpolant
\[
 T_N(s)=e^{2\pi iNas}
 \frac{\widehat P_N(N(s+a))\widehat P_N(N(s+y))}
      {\widehat P_N(Ns)^3\widehat P_N(Na)\widehat P_N(Ny)}.
 \tag{20}
\]
All real factorial arguments lie in compact original regular
strips: \(\Re(s+a)\le1-\varepsilon+o(1)\) and
\(\Re(s+y)<.95+o(1)\). The exact cotangent interpolation gives
the \(+N\) main current, its two zero-mode joins, two horizontal
aliases and all four weighted vertical halves. The all-height
argument bounds are
\[
                  -33\pi/20<\Im Q_s<131\pi/100.
 \tag{21}
\]
Their derivation in the two right-half proofs uses only real
coordinates, so applies at the new outgoing height. The lower and
upper aliases are below the same main point by \(69\pi/500\) and
\(2593\pi/10000\). Each weighted vertical half decreases away
from its real cut. On each unweighted join, (14)--(15) and
\(\Re\cot\pi(s+a)<0\) give \(\Re Q_{ss}<0\), so the height
rate is convex and bounded by its main/cut endpoints. Every one
of these comparisons is pointwise before multiplication by the
cap and by a third-factor profile.

The cap source is already complete. Factor 3 uses its original
four-profile finite reduction on its unchanged selected-input and
actual-output domain. Thus every simultaneous correction is
included. Uniform factorial symbols, zero-safe physical numerator
bounds, half-grid displacements and all finite row counts cost
only polynomial factors and an arbitrary prescribed rate allowance.
This proves the two horizontal bounds, with raw gap \(d_0/2\).

## 5. Fixed-profile convexity pays both half-face verticals

Now let \(a_h=1/2+ih\), \(c\le h\le H\). Use the same private
contours and profile sets, independent of \(h\). At this face
\(\Re g(a_h)=0\). The full unselected first-weight rate is zero
for \(c\le h\le0\), and after multiplication by \(X_h^{-N}\)
the full selected first-weight rate is zero for \(0\le h\le H\).
Both forcing alternatives have been retained in these upper bounds.

Fix any regular cap private point \(e\) in its five sets and any
first ordinary point \(s\) on the main line, a join, or any alias
side. Their real parts satisfy
\[
 .77\le\Re(e+a_h)\le.99,\qquad
                  5/8\le\Re(s+a_h)\le1-\varepsilon+o(1).
 \tag{22}
\]
With \(C(t)=\Re\cot\pi t\), exact differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{d^2}{dh^2}\{\Re\Psi(e,a_h)+\Re Q_1(s;a_h,y)}
          =-\pi C(e+a_h)-\pi C(s+a_h)>0.
 \tag{23}
\]
The two incoming base terms have \(C(a_h)=0\); the chirp has
zero second derivative. All alias penalties are independent of
\(h\). For a true-end constant delete the first cotangent in
(23); the remaining expression is still positive. Hence each
central raw profile, including all joins and weighted sides,
is convex in \(h\). Its two endpoints have just been paid with
raw gap \(d_0/2\), so the same gap holds throughout \([c,H]\).

For the low physical rows \(0\le t\le1/8\) and the right-cut
collar \(r_0\le t<1/2\), direct differentiation of (19) gives
\[
                         E_{hh}=-\pi C(1/2+t+ih)\ge0.
 \tag{24}
\]
The formula holds for every \(0\le t<1/2\). Adding a fixed cap
profile preserves convexity. Thus both these physical blocks are
bounded by their already paid height endpoints. The limit at
\(t=1/2\) is continuous; it is also included in the following
high-row argument.

For high rows \(1/2\le t\le1\), do not assert convexity across
the numerator wall. Below zero the original postwall disk branch
gives
\[
 E_h=-\Arg(1-e^{-2\pi i(t-1/2+ih)})-2\pi t\le-\pi.
 \tag{25}
\]
Every regular cap profile has derivative in \((0,\pi)\) at
this face, by its prewall argument, and a constant true end has
derivative zero. Therefore each cap-plus-high-row rate is
nonincreasing on \([c,0]\). Above zero use the exact reciprocal
modulus identity for the incoming numerator; its factor
\(e^{2\pi ht}\) cancels the chirp in (19). The remaining disk
expression has derivative equal to the nonnegative postwall
argument at height \(-h\). Each cap-plus-row rate is consequently
nondecreasing on \([0,H]\). The outgoing numerator contributes no
\(h\)-derivative, regardless of its positive height. Limits at
\(h=0\) and the wall are continuous integrated rates. The high
rows are therefore paid by \(c\) or \(H\), respectively.

These arguments prove the common raw endpoint bound for every
cap and every first ordinary current or physical block. In
particular, at \(h=0\) it holds pointwise with each third-factor
profile and each (12), with raw gap \(d_0/2\). The cap upper alias
still has the same-index extra reserve \(>1/50\) from Section 3.
This profile corollary is available for a future real-arc comparison;
no sign along that arc is asserted here.

The real-half main interval can also be extended down to \(2/25\)
for this profile assertion. Indeed, for \(2/25\le t\le1/8\),
\(s=t+ih_s\), and \(a_h=1/2+ih\), put
\(q=e^{-2\pi h}>9/10\) and \(\rho=e^{2\pi h_s}>1/2\), with
\(\rho<1\). The exact derivative, including its incoming chirp,
has exponential modulus ratio
\[
 e^{\Re Q_s}=
 \frac{|q-\rho e^{-2\pi i(1/2+t)}|\,
       |1-\rho e^{2\pi H}e^{-2\pi i(x+t)}|}
      {|1-\rho e^{-2\pi it}|^3}>1.
 \tag{26}
\]
The first squared numerator is greater than
\(q^2+\rho^2>53/50\). The second exceeds one because
\(x+t\in[.30,.575]\) has negative cosine. The denominator base
has modulus less than one because
\(\rho<1<2\cos(2\pi t)\). The radius bounds follow from the
original witnesses in the next-four endpoint budget. Thus this
extra main segment increases to its \(1/8\) endpoint. The low
physical rates on \([0,1/8]\) likewise increase, by the ratio
proof already used in Section 4, valid at every \(c\le h\le H\).

**Corollary 2 (the allocated real-half profiles).** Let
\(A_\ell(0)\) denote any of the five cap profiles or the constant
\(E_*\) at \(a=1/2\). Set
\[
 M_2(y)=\max_{2/25\le t\le r_0}\Re Q_1(t+ih_s;1/2,y),
 \qquad E_0(t;y)=E(t;1/2,y).
 \tag{27}
\]
For every third profile \(j\), either \(\nu\) in (12), and
each \(B\) equal to \(M_2(y)\), an individual \(E_0(t;y)\)
with \(t\in[0,1/8]\cup[r_0,1]\), or one of the retained raw first-ordinary
join/alias/cut rates,
\[
 A_\ell(0)+B+R_j^3(y,z)+W_2^\nu
              -\frac{\beta}{2}\Re g(z)
 \le\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/6400.
 \tag{28}
\]
In particular it pays the physical rate \(E_0(2/25;y)\).
For every same-index pairing the upper cap alias has the
additional \(1/50\) loss relative to that cap main point.
This statement preserves the original three-factor affine
calibration and is stronger than a bound on their signed product.
It does not bound the individual real central rows
\(1/8<t<r_0\); that block remains a coherent contour integral.

## 6. Finite conclusion and precise remaining region

Partition the first ordinary sum into the low block, the exact
central contour (20), and the high block. Sections 3--5 control
each term and all cap and third-factor terms. Split the uniform
symbol, rounding and continuation tolerances in advance. The raw
gap \(d_0/2=1/6400\) leaves the stated \(1/12800\) and an arbitrary
final \(\tau\). The original negative-root factorial convention,
full Fourier forcing, \(+N\) zero mode, finite endpoint rows and
both midpoint parities remain unchanged.

All cap bounds hold for all real ordinary colors. The exact
integer-weighted source has degree at most \(N-1\) in that color.
The original interval/Chebyshev continuation gives fixed disks
with any prescribed small exponential allowance; Cauchy's formula
on smaller disks gives fixed derivatives. Color-independent zeros
are retained, and no Fourier polynomial or modulus halfweight is
divided out. A fixed horizontal or vertical integration length,
the density \(N\), and the original exterior scalar contribute
only their original polynomial and unit factors. This proves (9).

For \(m=4\), the third conditional index is still ordinary. Its
outgoing marginal and halfweight meet the original terminal
transform. For greater length they meet the next ordinary factor.
The full first and second weights in (5) have already closed their
two incidences; the third coordinate has only the stated half.

The result completes the specified first right connections only
as far as the real point \(1/2\), with the second coordinate
selected. The real interval \([1/2,7/8]\), complementary first
paths, other second-coordinate pieces and second-period completion
remain separate. No whole-word or knot conclusion is inferred.
