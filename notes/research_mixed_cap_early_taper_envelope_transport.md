## 2. The own-four real-private profile dominates both true ends

**Proposition 1.** With the actual reference (1)--(2),
\[
 A^{\rm real}_1(x+ih)>\frac{13}{20}
 \quad\text{for } .1\le x\le.22,\quad c\le h\le0.     \tag{4}
\]

*Proof.* First keep the outgoing variable equal to \(z_*\).
Private vertical convexity on the original endpoint rectangle,
proved by the full cap Hessian theorem, and the stationarity in
(2) imply
\[
                   \Re\Psi_1(b,z_*)\ge\Re T_1(z_*)>1.
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
The vertical segment from \(b+iv_*\) to \(b\) stays in
that proved rectangle; no height reset of the outgoing variable
is used in (5).

At fixed outgoing height \(c<0\), the bare marginal is
\(\Psi_{1,z}(b,z)=g'(b+z)-g'(z)\).
For \(x\le y\le a_*\), we have \(.1\le y<.35\).
Writing \(r=e^{2\pi c}<1\), its numerator modulus is at most
two, while
\[
                  |1-re^{-2\pi iy}|>1/2.                \tag{6}
\]
For positive cosine, minimize over the radial variable and use
\(\sin(2\pi y)\ge\sin(.2\pi)>1/2\); when the cosine
is nonpositive the modulus is at least one. Thus
\(\Re\Psi_{1,z}<\log4<7/5\). Integrating horizontally
back from \(a_*\), a distance less than \(1/4\), gives
\[
                 \Re\Psi_1(b,x+ic)>1-\frac14\frac75
                                      =\frac{13}{20}.   \tag{7}
\]

Finally raise the outgoing height from \(c\) to any \(h\le0\).
At the fixed real private point \(b\), let
\(N=1-re^{-2\pi i(b+x)}\), \(D=1-re^{-2\pi ix}\),
now with \(0<r\le1\). Direct multiplication yields
\[
 \Im(N\overline D)
   =2r\sin\pi b\{\cos\pi(2x+b)-r\cos\pi b\}<0.      \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(.533<2x+b<.803\), while \(0<b<1/2\).
Both original factors have positive real part: for \(N\),
\(.433<b+x<.583\) makes its angular cosine negative;
for \(D\), use \(r\le1\) and \(x>0\).
Their original argument difference therefore belongs to
\((-\pi,\pi)\), and (8) proves
\(\Im(g'(b+z)-g'(z))<0\). The derivative of
\(\Re\Psi_1(b,x+ih)\) with respect to \(h\) is the
negative of this quantity. Raising the height increases the bare
phase. Equation (7) proves (4). \(\square\)

## 3. Complete finite envelopes and their allowed derivative modes

Let \(c_0>0\) be the uniform angular-loss constant of the early
real-row source theorem. For own four replace it, if necessary, by
\(c_1=\min(c_0,7/400)\). Since \(1-\cos\theta\le2\),
(10) absorbs the true-end exponent into the main exponent uniformly
for all real ordinary colors. Thus the whole original finite cap has
the single bare envelope
\[
                A^{\rm real}_1(z)-c_1(1-\cos\theta).
                                                               \tag{11}
\]
The inequality includes the arbitrary additive source tolerance,
polynomial factors, both parities and fixed ordinary-color jets
of the original finite-source theorem.

On the compact early lower-half tube, the exact absolute
[Fourier weight theorem](research_mixed_fourier_weight_continuation.md),
Section 5, gives the outgoing halfweight rate
\(-\tfrac12\Re g(z)\). In fact its relative condition holds
there because \(-\Re g(z)>0\), including the real boundary.
This is the unselected sector \(\sigma=0\) of the original
selection expansion; no selected carrier \(X^{-N}\) is omitted.
No finite Fourier weight is divided out. With \(\kappa=3/2\),
the resulting gauged allocated envelope is
\[
 \mathcal F_1(x,h;\theta)
  = A^{\rm real}_1(x+ih)-\frac12\Re g(x+ih)
                       -\kappa h-c_1(1-\cos\theta).     \tag{12}
\]

For own three keep both exponents:
\[
 \mathcal F_0(x,h;\theta)
 =\max\{A^{\rm real}_0(x+ih)-c_0(1-\cos\theta),
                          E_{\rm end}(h)\}-\kappa h.   \tag{13}
\]
This avoids any unnecessary dominance assumption for its true ends.

Consider the two derivative modes of the original early minimum
contour: a fixed height, or the affine height
\(h=c(x-.1)/.12\). Keep the same actual \(c\) and the
same switch in the neighboring ordinary half. The completed
[real-private half-stencil](research_mixed_cap_real_private_half_stencil.md)
and [correlated right-cut theorem](research_mixed_cap_correlated_right_half.md)
give, for every fixed private real coordinate,
\[
 \frac d{dx}\left\{
  \Re\Psi_\alpha(e,z)-\frac\alpha2\Re g(z)-\kappa h
                 \right\}>\frac1{600}                 \tag{14}
\]
for own four in either mode. For own three the corresponding
uniform lower bound is \(1/20\). In the fixed-height mode the
stronger bounds in the half-stencil theorem apply on the full
containing height interval.

Integration of (14), followed by the maximum over the unchanged
compact private interval, preserves its slope bound. No smoothness
of the maximizing private coordinate is needed. Therefore (12)
strictly increases, with slope at least \(1/600\), along each
admissible piece of such transport.

For the second exponent in (13), its gauged derivative is zero
in the fixed-height mode. In the moving-height mode it equals
\[
 \begin{cases}
 (2\pi-\kappa)c/.12,&c\ge0,\\
 -\kappa c/.12,&c\le0,
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{15}
\]
which is nonnegative. Thus the full maximum (13) is nondecreasing.
It need not have a uniform positive slope when the endpoint
exponent is active. Piecewise admissible paths follow by adding
the integrated comparisons; ties of the envelopes cause no loss.
