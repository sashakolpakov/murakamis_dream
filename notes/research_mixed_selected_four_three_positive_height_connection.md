# The complete selected first-four/three positive-height connection

Private written corollary, 10 November 2026. The complete real-face
estimate supplies the missing endpoint of the already proved convex
first-height envelope. Thus the selected first-four/three source is
paid on the full vertical segment from height zero to \(7/625\), with
the next coordinate at its actual height. This is only a connection
estimate, not a whole-period replacement.

## 1. Literal source and statement

Fix an actual positive three/four open word of length \(m\ge3\)
beginning with four and three. Use the actual reference and the
original phases and affine rate \(\Lambda(z)\) of the
[complete selected early source](research_mixed_selected_four_three_early_complete_source.md).
In particular the actual private heights satisfy
\[
 -.051<h_e<-.019,\qquad -.065<h_s,d<.068.
\]
Set
\[
 H=7/625,\qquad a=1/10+ih,\quad 0\le h\le H,\qquad
 b=z+id,\quad z\in[11/50,9/20].
\tag{1}
\]
Retain the literal source
\[
 \mathcal K_N^1(w;a,b)
 =U_{1,N}(w,X)C_{0,N}(X,Y)d_N(X)X^{-N},
 \qquad X=e^{-2\pi ia},\quad Y=e^{-2\pi ib}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both finite kernels are the complete original ones. There is no
outgoing type-three halfweight and no change of outside scalar.

**Corollary 1.** For every \(\tau>0\), uniformly on (1), both
parities, every integer \(N\ge2\), and every real ordinary color
\(w\in[-2,2]\),
\[
 |\mathcal K_N^1(w;a,b)|
 \le C_\tau N^{A_\tau}
          e^{N\{\Lambda(z)-1/2000+\tau\}}.
\tag{3}
\]
It holds on a sufficiently small fixed ordinary-color disk about
\(-2\) with any prescribed total allowance, and with fixed
derivatives on smaller disks. It also pays the literal oriented
vertical integral, with unchanged outside scalar.

## 2. The same complete envelope at both endpoints

Apply Theorem 1 of the
[positive-join convex-envelope theorem](research_mixed_first_positive_join_convex_envelope.md)
with own-type flag \(\beta=0\). Its envelope
\(\mathcal E_0(h,b)=A(h)+B_0(h,b)+W(h)\) retains:
the five cap profile families and the affine true-end rate
\(3/5+2\pi h+\log4/100\); the ordinary main line, two real
cuts, both zero-mode joins, aliases at \(\pm1/5\), and all four
vertical halves; every physical rate on \([1/2,9/10]\);
and the last carrier \(2\pi d_+\). The low rows are bounded by
the retained cut at \(3/20\).
The whole selected Fourier rate is exactly
\[
                       W(h)=\max\{-\Re g(a)-2\pi h,0\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The theorem proves the finite-source bound by this envelope and
its convexity on \([0,H]\), for the same actual heights in (1).
It neither divides by a Fourier factor nor assumes convexity of
the finite modulus.

At height \(H\), Sections 3--8 of the complete selected early
source give individual cap-profile times ordinary-current and
physical-rate comparisons, with reserve \(1/2000\). Hence they
pay this entire envelope:
\[
                         \mathcal E_0(H,b)\le\Lambda(z)-1/2000.
\tag{5}
\]
This is the raw-profile implication explicitly recorded in
Section 5 of the convex-envelope theorem.

At height zero, Proposition 2 of the
[complete four/three real-face theorem](research_mixed_selected_four_three_zero_height_face.md)
uses the same aliases, joins, cap profiles and end constant.
It pays every physical-tail rate, hence in particular the entire
interval and carrier retained above. It also pays both Fourier
upper alternatives. Therefore
\[
                         \mathcal E_0(0,b)\le\Lambda(z)-1/1000.
\tag{6}
\]
The factor \(X^{-N}\) has unit modulus at this endpoint, so the
literal source still agrees with (2). No endpoint estimate for a
different Fourier alternative is substituted into convexity.

With \(\theta=h/H\), the already proved same-envelope convexity gives
\[
 \mathcal E_0(h,b)
 \le\Lambda(z)-(1-\theta)/1000-\theta/2000
 \le\Lambda(z)-1/2000.
\tag{7}
\]
The finite-source upper bound proves (3). Its whole-real-color
estimate and degree less than \(N\) give fixed ordinary disks
and jets exactly as in the convex-envelope theorem. Finally
\(da=i\,dh\) and the fixed segment length change only the
prefactor of its oriented integral.

The second height and all markings remain fixed throughout. The
real first-coordinate interval, the negative unselected connection,
the right outer boundary, later coordinate departures and any
whole-period or nonzero assembly require their separate estimates.
No knot or finite-exception conclusion is asserted.
