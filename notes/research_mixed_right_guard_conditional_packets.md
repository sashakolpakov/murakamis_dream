# Full conditional sources through the right outer half-interval

Research proof, 4 November 2026. This separate source extension leaves
completed certificates and frozen manuscripts unchanged. Its outward
certificate and independent written/code review have passed. No outer
supporting-plane comparison is asserted here.

The full original conditional sum has a nonzero packet when one outer
real argument runs from .45 to .50 and the other lies in [.22,.45].
The parameter rectangles include all original and selected graph heights.
This supplies a coherent source where an absolute bound on individual
index rows need not be below the geometric supporting plane.

## 1. Independent domains and original logarithmic roots

Put
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_0&=[-.030,.020],&H_1&=[-.057,.0141],\\
 P_j&=[.22,.45]+iH_j,&R_j&=[.45,.50]+iH_j
                         \qquad(j=0,1).
 \end{split}
 \tag{1}
\]
For each \(\eta,\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\), the two ordered products are
\(R_\eta\times P_\epsilon\) and \(P_\eta\times R_\epsilon\).
The second argument is the own leg of the type \(3+\epsilon\) block.
Use the unchanged primitive, logarithmic lift and phase
\[
 g(z)=\frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-2\pi iz})-\pi^2/6}{2\pi i},\qquad
 Q_\epsilon(s;a,b)=g(s+a)+g(s+b)-(2+\epsilon)g(s)+2\pi ias.
 \tag{2}
\]
Every logarithm in this proof lies in its original regular strip
\(0<\Re z<1\).

**Lemma 1.** On each of the eight closed products, the original equation
\(Q_{\epsilon,s}=0\) has a unique root in
\[
 D=[.10,.49]+i[-.17,.17],
 \tag{3}
\]
which extends holomorphically to a neighborhood of the product.
On the full index contour \(K=[.10,.49]+i[-.20,.20]\),
\(-\Re Q_{\epsilon,ss}>1/8\).

The curvature estimate is exactly the majorant already proved in the
[transition-source theorem](research_mixed_transition_conditional_rectangles.md),
not an extrapolation of its root. Write
\[
 c(x,y)=\Re\cot\pi(x+iy)
       =\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{\cosh(2\pi y)-\cos(2\pi x)}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For \(.10\le x\le.28\), every positive shifted cotangent is at most
\(\max\{0,c(x+.22,d)\}\), where \(d\) is the distance of zero from
its shifted height interval. The minimum real argument remains at
least .32; a positive cotangent decreases in its real argument there
and in its absolute height. Increasing the parameter's upper real
endpoint from .45 to .50 introduces only shifted arguments less than
.99. Those at or above .50 have nonpositive real cotangent and do not
increase this majorant. The typed height intervals are unchanged.
Thus the four previously certified two-variable curvature majorants
apply without alteration. For \(.28\le x\le.49\), both shifted
cotangents are nonpositive and the previous own-index lower bound
applies as well. The checker validates that certificate and its
provenance before reusing these two bounds.

Root existence is checked afresh on the smaller rectangle
\([.10,.49]+i[-.17,.15]\). The four inequalities are
\[
 \Re Q_s(.10+iy)>1/50,\quad \Re Q_s(.49+iy)<-1/50,\quad
 \Im Q_s(x-.17i)>1/50,\quad \Im Q_s(x+.15i)<-1/50.
 \tag{5}
\]
There are 32 faces, each covered by 128 exact slabs. The two parameter
contributions are harmonic separately; on each slab their extrema
reduce to the four parameter sides, each covered by 32 intervals.
Poincaré--Miranda gives an original logarithmic zero. Negative real
second derivative on the convex rectangle gives injectivity and
simplicity. The implicit function theorem, uniqueness and compact
strict margins prove holomorphy before any parameter maximum principle
is applied.

## 2. The full original finite source

Let \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(p_j=(r;r)_j\), and retain the same
canonical finite function as in the transition theorem:
\[
 \mathcal L_{\epsilon,N}(a,b)=
 \widehat P_N(Na)\widehat P_N(Nb)
 \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \frac{e^{2\pi iaj}(r^{Na+1};r)_j(r^{Nb+1};r)_j}
      {p_j^{2+\epsilon}}.
 \tag{6}
\]
All original rows remain present, including rows revived when the
parameters leave their physical grids.

**Theorem 2.** Uniformly on each product (1), for both parities,
\[
 \mathcal L_{\epsilon,N}(a,b)=
 N^{(1-\epsilon)/2}
 \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\,A_\epsilon(s_*;a,b)}
      {\sqrt{-Q_{\epsilon,ss}(s_*;a,b)}}
 e^{NQ_\epsilon(s_*;a,b)}(1+O(N^{-1})).
 \tag{7}
\]
The original factorial symbol and Gaussian branch are unchanged,
holomorphic and nonzero. The assertion holds to every fixed asymptotic
order, with all fixed derivatives on smaller neighborhoods.

Here are all the source estimates, including the parts not determined
by the root. The 128 distinguished-boundary products have exact binary
covers by rational affine Newton witnesses. Every accepted cell checks
the original logarithmic equation, its self-map and
\[
                    .20<\Re s_*<.46,\qquad |\Im s_*|<.17.
 \tag{8}
\]
With \(M=\Re Q_\epsilon(s_*;a,b)\), the checks retain both real cuts,
all four periodic last-row branches, and both horizontal aliases:
\[
 \begin{split}
 M-\Re Q_\epsilon(.10)&>10^{-4},\qquad
 M-\Re Q_\epsilon(.49)>10^{-4},\\
 M-H_\epsilon(1)&>10^{-4},\\
 M-\Re Q_\epsilon(x\pm .20i)+.40\pi&>10^{-3}
                            \qquad(.10\le x\le.49).
 \end{split}
 \tag{9}
\]
For the last inequality each of eight real-index intervals is bounded
by its midpoint concavity tangent, using Lemma 1 and an outward bound
on \(\Re Q_s\) over the entire parameter cell. This controls the whole
horizontal interval, rather than just its midpoint.

The exact right-tail polynomial stays below one up to its two
quadratic vertices. Consequently the full right real tail is bounded
by the larger of its rates at .49 and 1. The left real tail increases:
\[
 \Re Q_s(s)\ge
 2\log\frac{\sin(.22\pi)}{\sin(.10\pi)}-.08\pi>1,
                                      \qquad0<s\le.10.
 \tag{10}
\]
Here the shifted real sine arguments are in [.22,.60], whose sine
minimum is \(\sin(.22\pi)\). The exact height term, including the
original chirp, is \(\pi(\Im b-\Im a)>-.08\pi\). The additional
four-type denominator increases the lower bound since
\(2\sin(\pi s)<1\). The zero-uniform finite-product bounds from the
transition theorem retain the last rows and any physical zeros in
these tails.

The finite cotangent trace has its original \(+N\) zero mode.
Both horizontal aliases in (9) are kept. Every half-grid vertical
current has penalty \(2\pi|\Im s|\); imaginary-height convexity
from Lemma 1 bounds it by the real and horizontal endpoints already
in (9). The zero-mode joins at the selected height have a gap at
least \((.03)^2/16\), by (8) and the same curvature bound. Strict
margins absorb half-grid rounding. This is the exact source contour
from the transition proof, with fresh bounds on the larger products;
there is no discarded seam or changed finite interpolant.

The summand has power \(N^{-\epsilon/2}\). Its \(+N\) cotangent
coefficient and one Gaussian give the power in (7). Normalize the
finite-source error by the holomorphic nonzero leading expression.
Lemma 1 makes this a holomorphic function on the whole parameter
product. Two successive maximum-modulus principles extend the
boundary source estimates throughout it. Strict compact margins
supply neighborhoods, derivatives and the higher orders.

For the original Laurent kernel
\(C_{\epsilon,N}=\mathcal L_{\epsilon,N}/
[\widehat P_N(Na)\widehat P_N(Nb)]\), the phase is
\(Q_\epsilon(s_*)-g(a)-g(b)\) and the power is
\(N^{-(1+\epsilon)/2}\), with the identical nonzero symbol convention.

## 3. Joining to the plateau and scope

The roots and full packets glue with the transition theorem. More
precisely they define a holomorphic packet on a neighborhood of each
product
\[
 \begin{split}
 ([.22,.50]+iH_\eta)\times([.22,.45]+iH_\epsilon),\quad\text{or}\quad
 ([.22,.45]+iH_\eta)\times([.22,.50]+iH_\epsilon).
 \end{split}
 \tag{11}
\]
On the common slice with real coordinate .45, both original roots
lie in (3); injectivity identifies them. The implicit function theorem
identifies the germs on an open overlap. The normalized primitive and
factorial symbols coincide there, and \(-\Re Q_{ss}>0\) fixes the same
Gaussian branch. A finite subcover gives a uniform packet.

These products contain every right-taper target with the other angle
in the plateau, including selected heights. They do not assert that
the selected action is below the geometric tangent plane, or control
two simultaneous right-taper coordinates. Those remain additional
outer-integral questions. In particular no global mixed-word
asymptotic follows from this source extension alone.
