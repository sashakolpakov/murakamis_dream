# The selected first left join over the early second interval

Private written deduction, 11 November 2026. The entire first
height join at real coordinate \(1/10\), with the first Fourier
term selected, is paid when the second coordinate is in
\([1/10,11/50]\) at its actual negative height and the next
own type is four. The original cap, both conditional sums
and all Fourier weights are retained.

The argument first calibrates the same main/cut profiles at
the old second face by convexity in the first height. It then
uses the newly proved shared-coordinate transport uniformly
through that height interval. It asserts neither a complete
selected first coefficient nor a full second-period theorem.

## 1. Exact source and result

Use precisely the actual \(444\), \(m\ge4\) references, phases,
ordinary color and outgoing incidence of
[the selected horizontal theorem](research_mixed_complete44_first_selected_early_second_horizontal.md).
Put
\[
 H=1/100,\quad P=[11/50,9/20],\quad E=[1/10,11/50],
\]
\[
 a=1/10+ih,\quad 0\le h\le H,\qquad
 y=q+ic_2,\quad q\in E,\qquad z=u+ic_3,\quad u\in P.
 \tag{1}
\]
All second and third outer/private heights are the same actual
values in \([-.102,-.001]\); the cap private height is in
\([-.051,-.019]\). At \(m=4\) vertex three is ordinary with
its original attached following leg.

With \(X=e^{-2\pi ia}\), \(Y=e^{-2\pi iy}\), \(Z=e^{-2\pi iz}\),
retain the exact density
\[
 \mathscr K_N^+(w;a,y,z)=
 U_{1,N}(w,X)d_N(X)X^{-N}C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(Y)C_{1,N}(Y,Z).
 \tag{2}
\]
The functions and their finite summation ranges are those in
equations (2)--(3) of the horizontal theorem. In particular
every row \(0\le j<N\), both parities, the complete \(H_N\),
and the forcing in \(d_N\) are unchanged.
Carry \(|d_N(Z)|^{1/2}\) as a nonnegative outside multiplier,
without division. Let \(\Lambda_{123}(z)\) be the same original
affine action as in equation (4) of that theorem.

**Theorem.** For every \(\tau>0\), uniformly on (1), a
sufficiently small fixed ordinary-color disk about \(-2\) gives
\[
 |\mathscr K_N^+(w;a,y,z)|\,|d_N(Z)|^{1/2}
 \le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
          e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-1/2000+\tau\}}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Both level parities and all sufficiently large \(N\) are included.
One may take a fixed gap \(1/4000\). Fixed ordinary-color
derivatives have the same rate on a smaller disk. Integrating
the literal oriented join \(1/10\to1/10+iH\), with the
original \(N\,da\), changes only its polynomial factor.

## 2. Calibrate one common old-face profile envelope

Set \(y_0=11/50+ic_2\). Let \(A(h)\) be the complete cap
envelope of the positive-join source, equation (5):
\[
\begin{split}
 A(h)=\max\{&
 \max_{.27\le e_R\le.49}\Re\Psi(e_R+ih_e,a),\
 \Re\Psi(.27,a),\ \Re\Psi(.49,a),\\
 &\max_{e_R}\Re\Psi(e_R-.055i,a)-.11\pi,\
 \max_{e_R}\Re\Psi(e_R+.04i,a)-.08\pi,\
 \tfrac35+2\pi h+\tfrac{\log4}{100}\}.
\end{split}
 \tag{4}
\]
This is a bound for the entire cap on the independent first-height
rectangle. In particular its separate true-end term is retained.
The cap bound depends on \(a,w\), not on the following target \(y\).

Define the following *core* envelope at the old second face:
\[
 B(h;y_0)=\max\left\{
   \max_{1/8\le v\le1/2}\Re Q(v+ih_s;a,y_0),\
   \Re Q(1/8;a,y_0),\ \Re Q(1/2;a,y_0)\right\}.
 \tag{5}
\]
It is used here only to calibrate its constituent profiles;
it is not presumed to be a complete ordinary-kernel bound.
Keep the two full selected Fourier rates
\[
 W(h)=\max\{-\Re g(a)-2\pi h,\ 0\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
At \(h=0\), Proposition 2 of the zero-height face theorem gives,
for every cap and core profile before maximization,
\[
 A(0)+B(0;y_0)+W(0)-\tfrac12\Re g(y_0)
                    <\Lambda_{12}(11/50)-1/1000 .
 \tag{7}
\]
At \(h=H\), equation (27) of the complete early selected-four
source gives the same expression with bound
\(\Lambda_{12}(11/50)-1/2000\). This includes the true cap ends
and both upper alternatives for the original \(d_N(X)X^{-N}\).
The two sources use the same actual reference and affine constant.

The convexity needed between these endpoints is proved
profilewise. On \(a=1/10+ih\), the exact first cotangent
satisfies
\[
 C_a(h)=\Re\cot\pi(1/10+ih)>25/9\qquad(0\le h\le H).
 \tag{8}
\]
For every private point in (4)--(5),
\[
 \Re\cot\pi(e+a)\le3/2,\qquad
 \Re\cot\pi(s+a)\le3/2.
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed their real parts are respectively in \([.37,.59]\)
and \([.225,.6]\), and the bound
\(\Re\cot\pi(t+i\eta)\le3/2\) holds for every real
\(\eta\) and \(1/5\le t<1\).
These are exactly the elementary inequalities (13)--(14)
of the positive-join proof. They do not restrict the second
target or change a private height.

The original phases consequently give
\[
 \partial_h^2\Re\Psi(e,a)
     =\pi\{C_a-\Re\cot\pi(e+a)\}>0,\qquad
 \partial_h^2\Re Q(s;a,y_0)
     =\pi\{C_a-\Re\cot\pi(s+a)\}>0 .
 \tag{10}
\]
All fixed penalties and the incoming chirp have zero second
height derivative. The true-end term in (4) is affine, and
\(-\Re g(a)-2\pi h\) is convex. Thus \(A,B,W\), and their
sum, are convex. From their endpoint inequalities,
\[
 A(h)+B(h;y_0)+W(h)-\tfrac12\Re g(y_0)
          \le\Lambda_{12}(11/50)-1/2000
                    \qquad(0\le h\le H).
 \tag{11}
\]
This compares actual majorant profiles. It does not infer
convexity of a finite modulus or infer a profile bound from
cancellation in a signed product.

Attach each next own-four main, real-cut or last profile at
\(y_0,z\). Its original ordinary supporting plane and the
two shared halfweights cancel the first-pair outgoing marginal
exactly as in the horizontal theorem, equations (7)--(8).
Therefore (11) gives its old-face three-factor profile bound
with gap \(1/2000\), at every height in (1).

## 3. Why the new full-source transport is uniform in the height

This step checks the proof of the horizontal theorem, rather
than applying its printed fixed-\(H\) conclusion outside its
domain. For \(0\le h\le H\),
\[
                         15/16<T=e^{-2\pi h}\le1.
 \tag{12}
\]
The lower and upper main clippings in its Section 2 use
only (12). Every one of the five rational force comparisons
there therefore holds at the present height, including zero.
The next main clipping is unchanged. The direct \(q\)-derivative
of a paired profile contains no first-height parameter:
\[
 \frac{|1-R\rho_s e^{-2\pi i(q+s_R)}|
             |T_t-R e^{-2\pi i(q+t_R)}|}
      {|1-Re^{-2\pi iq}|^3},
 \quad
 R=e^{2\pi c_2},\quad
 \rho_s=e^{2\pi\Im s},\quad T_t=e^{-2\pi\Im t}.
 \tag{13}
\]
All main/cut transports in its Section 3 thus remain valid.
In particular the first real-half comparison is direct and
uses \(V^2>D^3\); no sign of a half-to-main vertical derivative
has been assumed. Its Section 4 pays the next last carrier
by the same \(R\le7/10\) transport and \(R\ge7/10\) next-low
comparison. Equations (11)--(13) consequently pay every
controlling profile pair on the entire early interval \(E\).

Here is the complete finite inventory at the changed first
height. The positive low-block force (21)--(22) of the
horizontal proof uses only (12); it still bounds all low
rows by the real cut. The first high block has
\[
                 2\pi\{(-h)_++(c_2)_+\}=0
 \tag{14}
\]
at every such height. The all-angle high-row theorem therefore
bounds all its rows through \(N-1\) by the half profile.

For the first middle block use the same exact interpolation
and cuts \(1/8,1/2\), with aliases at \(-1/5,+4\).
In the new argument estimates (24)--(28) of the horizontal
proof, the only use of the particular positive first height is
\(\sigma+h\ge0\) for \(\sigma\ge0\).
That inequality includes \(h=0\). Thus uniformly in (1),
\[
 I<29\pi/20,\qquad
 I>-37\pi/40\ (\sigma\le0),\qquad
 I>-2533\pi/1400\ (\sigma\ge0).
 \tag{15}
\]
Every weighted side reduces to its real cut; both complete
horizontal aliases retain the original sampling penalties
and the losses stated in that proof. For the zero-mode joining
curvature (30)--(31) there, a positive incoming shifted cotangent
obeys \(\Re\cot\pi(s+a)\le\cot\pi(\Re s+1/10)\)
independently of the sign of \(\Im s+h\). If that shifted
cotangent is nonpositive, discarding it only improves the
curvature inequality. The same proof therefore holds uniformly here.
Thus both unweighted joins reduce to their main and real
endpoints.

## 4. Exact scope

The entire selected-first join at \(x=1/10\), with its original
orientation and \(N\,da\), is now controlled after multiplying
by the complete next own-four kernel and the full second
unselected Fourier polynomial. Together with the selected
horizontal theorem this pays the selected path from \(1/10\)
to \(1/10+i/100\) and then to \(9/20+i/100\), for early
second targets at their actual negative height.

The real first interval from zero to \(1/10\), the right
first connection and real arcs, the whole selected first
coefficient and the full \(R_{44,N}\) still require their
own estimates at those early second targets. No later
coordinate has been moved, and no whole second period,
