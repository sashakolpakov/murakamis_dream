# The coideal graph Gram has an octahedral volume action

24 November 2026. Written research proof.

The actual corrected coideal graph has an explicit positive Gram on
every tail channel. Its complete finite product gives a uniform
large-level profile, including the endpoint channels. That profile is
the volume of a specified convex ideal octahedron. The graph and its
projector therefore have sharp whole-space exponential costs.

The original marked row also simplifies in this graph. Its observed
channel has Gram value exactly \(N\), rather than the maximal volume
rate. These are source-specific metric and boundary calculations;
they do not bound the original scalar below or identify a global
geometric cycle after an arbitrary short component.

## 1. The actual graph and its prescribed metrics

Use the complete balanced physical modules in K and H. Put
\[
 N\ge2,\quad d=N-1,\quad b=2k\ge2,\quad
 q=e^{\pi i/N},\quad Q=q^2,\quad x=-q^{-1},\quad
 F_a=(Q;Q)_a,\quad F_0=1,\quad F_d=N,\quad c=1+q.
 \tag{1}
\]
All missing physical endpoint arrows remain zero. On an ordered
tensor power let
\(B_c=\mathsf F-(\mathsf E-cI)\mathsf K^{-1}\), with the actual
coproducts and generator order in K. Write
\(W=V_N^{\otimes(b-1)}\), \(T=B_c^{(b-1)}\), and
\(\mathsf B=B_c^{(b)}\) on \(V_N\otimes W\).

K gives \(p_N(T)=p_N(\mathsf B)=0\), where
\[
 p_N(z)=2q^{N/2}T_N(z/(2\sqrt q)),\qquad
 \mu_j=Q^j+qQ^{-j}\quad(0\le j<N).
 \tag{2}
\]
The \(N\) roots are distinct at every level. Indeed a collision of
\(s+q/s\) and \(t+q/t\), for distinct \(N\)-th roots \(s,t\), would
force \(st=q\), contrary to \(q^N=-1\). Every root occurs in the tail.
Let \(P_j\) be its full tail spectral idempotent and prescribe
\[
 G=\sum_jP_j^*P_j,\qquad
 \|v\|_G^2=v^*Gv,\qquad
 \|u\|_{\rm mix}^2=u^*(I\otimes G)u.
 \tag{3}
\]
The star is the physical Euclidean adjoint. K's finite cyclic average
gives the same \(G\), with \(N^{-1}I\le G\le e^{C_bN}I\).
The tail eigenspaces are orthogonal in \(G\). Adjoint symbols below
always refer to the displayed domain and range metrics.

Let \(D_a=D_a(c;T)\) be the leading continuants:
\[
 D_{-1}=0,\quad D_0=I,\qquad
 D_{a+1}=(c+qQ^aT)D_a-q(1-Q^a)^2D_{a-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
If \(E_d\) is the trailing continuant, K proves
\[
 L=\sum_{a=0}^d|a\rangle\otimes q^{-a}D_a/F_a,\qquad
 M=\sum_{a=0}^d\langle a|\otimes D_a/F_a,\qquad
 \Pi=\frac{L E_d M}{p_N'(c)},\quad p_N'(c)=\frac{2N}{1-q}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Here \(L:W\to V_N\otimes W\), \(M:V_N\otimes W\to W\), and
\(\Pi\) is the actual projector onto \(\ker(\mathsf B-cI)\).
Until the graph pullback is introduced, their adjoints and operator
norms use \(G\) on \(W\) and \(I\otimes G\) on \(V_N\otimes W\).

We will use the following exact tail-density identity in metric \(G\):
\[
 E_d=q^dD_d^{\dagger_G},\qquad
 D_d^{\dagger_G}D_d=N^2I.
 \tag{6}
\]
Here is a direct proof. Each root and \(c\) obey
\(\overline\mu=q^{-1}\mu\), \(\bar c=q^{-1}c\).
For a scalar tail root, multiplying conjugated diagonal entries
\(c+qQ^a\mu\) by \(q\) reverses \(a\mapsto d-a\).
The conjugated edge product multiplied by \(q^2\) is
\(q(1-Q^{-a})^2\), the reversed edge indexed \(N-a\).
Thus \(E_d(c;\mu)=q^d\overline{D_d(c;\mu)}\).
K's determinant identity \(D_dE_d=q^dN^2I\) gives
\(|D_d(c;\mu)|=N\). Orthogonality of the tail eigenspaces proves (6).

Define the actual scalar graph weight by
\[
 g_N(\mu)=\sum_{a=0}^d\left|\frac{D_a(c;\mu)}{F_a}\right|^2,
 \qquad g_N(T)=\sum_jg_N(\mu_j)P_j.
 \tag{7}
\]
On each tail eigenspace the maps in (5) are one scalar column and
row tensored with the identity. Consequently
\[
 L^\dagger L=g_N(T),\qquad MM^\dagger=g_N(T),\qquad
 g_N(\mu)\ge\frac{|p_N'(c)|}{N}=\csc\frac\pi{2N}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The last bound is Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(ML=p_N'(c)D_d/(q^dN^2)\), using (6).
Thus \(G_{\rm graph}=Gg_N(T)\) is a specified positive Hermitian
Gram, and \(L:(W,G_{\rm graph})\to(V_N\otimes W,I\otimes G)\)
is an exact isometry. No frame has been discarded.

## 2. One finite product on every tail channel

For the unique \(j\) in (2), set
\[
 A=[\lfloor N/2\rfloor-j]_N\in\{0,\ldots,d\},\qquad B=d-A,
 \qquad \mathscr F_{N,j}(z)=\sum_{a=0}^dD_a(c;\mu_j)z^a/F_a.
 \tag{9}
\]
Then the entire scalar graph column is generated by
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathscr F_{N,j}(z)=(-qQ^jz;Q)_A(-q^2Q^{-j}z;Q)_B,
 \qquad
 g_N(\mu_j)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
                     |\mathscr F_{N,j}(e^{i\vartheta})|^2d\vartheta.
 \quad}                                                    \tag{10}
\]
The second identity is ordinary finite Fourier Parseval.
For the first, recurrence (4) gives, modulo \(z^N\),
\[
 (1-z)(1-qz)\mathscr F(z)
       =(1+q\mu_jz+qQz^2)\mathscr F(Qz).
 \tag{11}
\]
The coefficient of each new degree \(1\le a<N\) is multiplied
by \(1-Q^a\ne0\); the constant term one therefore determines
the whole degree-\(d\) polynomial. In the proposed product the two
end factors are \(\{1,q\}\): their order is \(q,1\) for even \(N\),
and \(1,q\) for odd \(N\). Its quotient at \(z,Qz\) is precisely
the quotient in (11), proving (10) without an infinite product.

In particular, for even \(N\) this product is
\[
 \prod_{l=1}^A(1-q^{-(2l-1)}z)
       \prod_{l=1}^B(1-q^{-2l}z);
 \tag{12}
\]
for odd \(N\) the odd and even strings exchange places.
This formula keeps both parity-dependent half-grid marks.

## 3. A uniform profile on the whole finite spectrum

Put
\[
 t=A/N,\quad \psi=\pi\min(t,1-t),\quad
 \eta=\arcsin(\sin\psi/\sqrt2),\quad
 \Lambda(u)=-\int_0^u\log(2\sin v)\,dv\quad(0\le u\le\pi),
\]
\[
 \mathcal V_\psi
       =2\{2\Lambda(\eta)+\Lambda(\psi-\eta)-\Lambda(\psi+\eta)\}.
 \tag{13}
\]
At \(\psi=0\) use the continuous value \(\mathcal V_0=0\).
There is a universal constant \(C\) such that
\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 \left|\log g_N(\mu_j)-\frac N\pi\mathcal V_{\psi_j}\right|
                           \le C\log N
 \qquad(N\ge2, 0\le j<N).\qquad}                         \tag{14}
\]
No channel cutoff or nondegenerate-endpoint assumption enters this bound.

Here are uniform estimates paying the logarithmic zeros. Write
\(f(v)=\log|1-e^{iv}|\), a periodic integrable function of mean zero.
For a grid string of length \(r\le N\), with spacing \(2\pi/N\)
and either endpoint or half-grid samples, its sum is bounded above
by \(N/(2\pi)\) times its arc integral plus \(C\log N\), uniformly
in the arc and its rotation. Remove the at-most-two cells nearest
each logarithmic zero. On the other cells the total oscillation is
bounded by \(\int|f'|=O(\log N)\). A removed sample is at most
\(-\log N+C\), whereas its cell average is at least
\(-\log N-C\), proving the upper estimate even at an exact zero.
If all samples and their arcs stay at distance at least \(\kappa/N\)
from a zero, the same estimate is two-sided, with constant depending
only on \(\kappa>0\). An endpoint cell changes the normalized arc integral
by at most \(C\log N\). These observations also cover (12)'s half shifts.

The corresponding limiting energy on the circle is
\[
 \mathcal E_t(\vartheta)=\frac1\pi\left\{
 \int_0^{2\pi t}f(\vartheta-v)dv+
 \int_0^{2\pi(1-t)}f(\vartheta-v)dv\right\}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Replacing \(B/N\) by \(1-t\) costs just the endpoint cell above.
Thus globally
\(\log|\mathscr F_{N,j}(e^{i\vartheta})|^2
 \le N\mathcal E_t(\vartheta)+C\log N\).
The function is unchanged by \(t\mapsto1-t\). For \(0<t<1\),
endpoint differentiation gives
\[
 \mathcal E_t'(\vartheta)=\frac1\pi
  \log\frac{1-\cos\vartheta}{|\cos\vartheta-\cos(2\psi)|}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Its only ordinary critical points satisfy
\(\cos\vartheta=\cos^2\psi\), hence are \(\vartheta=\pm2\eta\).
The negative angle is the unique maximum, the positive angle the
minimum. At the logarithmic cusps the derivative diverges with the
same sign on both sides, so there is no further extremum.
Using \(\Lambda(\pi-u)=-\Lambda(u)\) in (15) proves
\[
                 \max\mathcal E_t=\mathcal E_t(-2\eta)
                                      =\mathcal V_\psi/\pi.
 \tag{17}
\]
This proves the upper half of (14) by Parseval.

For its lower half suppose \(1\le A\le N-1\), and put
\(m=\min(A,N-A)\). Every actual factor zero in (12) has angle
in \([\pi/N,\,2\pi-2\psi]\), because
\(\max(A,B)\le N-m\). The preferred angle \(2\pi-2\eta\)
therefore has distance at least
\(\min(2\eta,2(\psi-\eta))\ge\kappa_0\psi\ge\kappa_1/N\)
from every zero and from the corresponding continuum arcs.
Here \(\kappa_2\psi\le\eta\le\psi/\sqrt2\) follows directly from
its definition. The two-sided grid estimate at this angle is
therefore uniform. On an interval of width \(\kappa_3N^{-3}\) about it,
each logarithmic derivative is at most \(CN\); their sum is at most
\(CN^2\). The product logarithm changes by only \(O(1)\).
Integrating this interval in (10) loses only \(O(\log N)\), proving
the lower bound. If \(A=0\), the product is a unit-phase geometric
series with all \(N\) coefficients of modulus one, so \(g_N=N\).
This also proves (14) there. The case \(A=N-1\) was already included.

## 4. The actual convex ideal octahedron

For \(0<\psi\le\pi/2\), consider the one-parameter angle assignment
consisting of two tetrahedra with angles
\((u,\psi-u,\pi-\psi)\) and two with
\((u,\psi,\pi-\psi-u)\), where \(0<u<\psi\).
Their common internal edge has alternating angles \(\pi-\psi,\psi\),
of sum \(2\pi\); all boundary angle sums are \(\psi\) or \(\pi-\psi\).
These are off-critical angle assignments. They need not yet develop
with consistent tetrahedral moduli; that gluing equation is imposed below.
Its volume functional is
\[
 \mathcal W_\psi(u)=
       2\{2\Lambda(u)+\Lambda(\psi-u)-\Lambda(\psi+u)\},\qquad
 \mathcal W_\psi'(u)=
       2\log\frac{\sin(\psi-u)\sin(\psi+u)}{\sin^2u}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The ratio decreases strictly from infinity to zero. Its unique
maximum is \(u=\eta\), since \(2\sin^2\eta=\sin^2\psi\).
Thus this is a concrete strictly concave angle-volume maximization,
not an assignment of volumes to arbitrary signed summands.

The maximizing tetrahedra develop into a convex ideal octahedron.
In the upper half-space boundary take
\[
 0,\ \infty,\ 1,\ v,\ -1,\ -v,\qquad
 v=r e^{i(\pi-\psi)},\quad r=\frac{\sin\eta}{\sin(\psi-\eta)}.
 \tag{19}
\]
The four tetrahedra around \(0\infty\) have alternating shapes
\(v,-1/v,v,-1/v\). The first has angles
\((\eta,\psi-\eta,\pi-\psi)\), by the ordinary sine rule.
The maximizing equation gives
\(r^{-1}=\sin\eta/\sin(\psi+\eta)\), so the second has
angles \((\eta,\psi,\pi-\psi-\eta)\).

For completeness all eight boundary planes support the convex hull.
The equator \((1,v,-1,-v)\) is a strictly convex centrally symmetric
quadrilateral. The four planes through \(\infty\) are its vertical
side planes. For an adjacent equatorial pair \(a,b\), the other face
is the hemisphere through \(0,a,b\). Its boundary circle has equation
\(|z|^2-\ell(z)=0\), with \(\ell\) real linear,
\(\ell(a)=|a|^2\), \(\ell(b)=|b|^2\).
At the two remaining vertices \(-a,-b\) its values are
\(2|a|^2,2|b|^2>0\), in the halfspace containing \(\infty\).
Thus these too are supporting planes. The four positive tetrahedra
fill this convex ideal octahedron, and their boundary dihedral sums
are precisely \(\psi\) or \(\pi-\psi\). Its volume is (13).
This proves the local Rivin interpretation of the actual Gram rate.

The maximizing volume increases with \(\psi\): its derivative is
\(2\log[\sin(\psi+\eta)/\sin(\psi-\eta)]>0\) below \(\pi/2\).
At \(\psi=\pi/2\), \(\eta=\pi/4,r=1\), giving the regular ideal
right-angled octahedron, of volume \(4G_{\rm Cat}\).
Here \(G_{\rm Cat}=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n/(2n+1)^2\), and
\(\Lambda(\pi/4)=G_{\rm Cat}/2\), directly from the sine series.
The limiting volume at \(\psi=0\) is zero.

## 5. Sharp full-graph costs and the retained original row

The label \(j=0\) has \(A=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), hence
\(\psi=\pi/2+O(N^{-1})\). Equations (14), (18) therefore give
\[
 \log\max_jg_N(\mu_j)=\frac{4G_{\rm Cat}}\pi N+O(\log N),\qquad
 \log\|L\|=\log\|M\|=\frac{2G_{\rm Cat}}\pi N+O(\log N).
 \tag{20}
\]
The norms use the two metrics in (3). On a tail channel the column
and row in (5) each have squared norm \(g_N\), while \(E_d\) has
modulus \(N\). Thus the actual full projector satisfies
\[
 \|\Pi\|_{\rm mix}=\sin\frac\pi{2N}\max_jg_N(\mu_j),\qquad
 \log\|\Pi\|_{\rm mix}=\frac{4G_{\rm Cat}}\pi N+O(\log N).
 \tag{21}
\]
This is a sharp mixed-metric operator estimate, not a physical
condition-number identification or an arbitrary-word estimate.

Retain the actual cup and caps from H:
\(p_{ab}=Nx^{-b}\delta_{a+b,d}\),
\(\ell_{ab}=x^a\delta_{a+b,d}/N\), and
\(m_{ab}=x^b/N^2\), with no degree delta on \(m\).
Put \(h_a=x^a\) and \(\mu_*=x+q/x=-q^{-1}-q^2\).
For the one-head tail matrix, including its zero end arrows,
\[
 (hT)_a/x^a=x+q/x-Q^a(xQ+q/x+qc)=\mu_*,
\]
because the bracket vanishes. Recurrence (4), starting from
\(D_0=1,D_1=q(1-Q)\), then gives
\(D_a(c;\mu_*)=q^aF_a\), so on the entire one-head graph
\(mL_2=N^{-2}h\sum_aq^{-a}D_a/F_a=h/N\).
Ordinary caps intertwine the total generators, hence all tail
continuants. Therefore, on the entire \((b-1)\)-head graph input,
\[
 a:=(m_{12}\otimes\ell^{\otimes(k-1)})L
       =(h/N)\otimes\ell^{\otimes(k-1)},\qquad
 \|a\|_{\rm ph,*}=N^{-k/2},\quad aT=\mu_*a,\quad g_N(\mu_*)=N.
 \tag{22}
\]
For odd \(N\) this is \(A=0\); for even \(N\) it is \(A=N-1\),
including \(N=2\). Its geometric profile is asymptotically flat,
although its exact Gram value is \(N\) at every level.

The prescribed last marked cap can be moved to the first only on
the true saturated generic source \(\mathcal S_N\). This is the
existing mark-transport result, with the following regular proof
also covering \(N=2\). Lift a vector of \(\mathcal S_N\) to a generic
invariant tensor. Closing all but any one pair by the regular generic
ordinary caps leaves a scalar times its unique generic cup.
The same fully ordinary closure is that scalar times the same
nonzero generic quantum-dimension bubble, so all these scalars agree.
Every matrix and normalized mark is regular at the root: factorial
indices are \(<N\), the common pivot correction is independent of
the marked pair, and \(mp=1\). A generic mark extension can be
normalized by its cup pairing, which is a unit at the root.
Equality thus specializes, without
dividing the zero root bubble. The root cap formulas are not used
as their generic extensions. No assertion for larger root-only
invariants, selected windows, or omitted normal currents is made.

For an actual regular unmarked braid \(\mathcal R\), let
\(\mathcal R_c=\pi_0\mathcal R L\) and
\(z_0=Nx^{-d}|d\rangle\otimes p^{\otimes(k-1)}\).
K and this legal relocation give the original scalar exactly as
\[
 \mathcal K_N=\chi_N a\mathcal R_cz_0,\qquad
 \|a\|_{G,*}\le N^{-k/2},\qquad
 \|a\|_{G_{\rm graph},*}\le N^{-(k+1)/2}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Indeed \(aP_j\) vanishes except at \(\mu_*\), while
\(\|v\|_G^2=\sum_j\|P_jv\|_{\rm ph}^2\); this proves the first
dual bound from (22). The graph metric adds exactly the factor
\(g_N(\mu_*)=N\) on that observed channel, proving the second.
Consequently
\( |\mathcal K_N|\le|\chi_N|N^{-(k+1)/2}
                    \|\mathcal R_cz_0\|_{G_{\rm graph}}\).
The full marked row and external factor remain. No lower bound on
its value on this particular propagated vector follows.

K already supplies the exact graph/source reduction. P proves
top-mark transport; the argument above makes its \(N=2\) scope
explicit. C already uses finite products, Parseval and Laplace for
a different centered-input family; E already interprets native

are the full coideal spectrum product/profile, its specified local
octahedron, sharp graph costs, and the explicit original graph row.
Its phase-bearing transitions through arbitrary overlapping blocks
and the required geometric word-energy estimate remain unproved.
All results concern complete-root values with full Casimir channels;
no peripheral or moving-\(q\) jet theorem is inferred.
