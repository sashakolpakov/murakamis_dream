# The marked braid-axis germ and its transverse filling coordinate

Private written proof, 19 November 2026.

On the irreducible regular-total chart, character fixedness for a
braid leaves the marked closure multiplier constructed in B. Under
the precise fixed-meridian rigidity input below, that character-fixed
germ is a smooth curve and the multiplier minus one is its coordinate.
Allowing the original meridian eigenvalue gives a smooth surface with
the meridian and the marked axis filling as independent coordinates.
The unmarked trace filling instead has a double zero.

The complete directs are B, the actual braid-axis closure multiplier,
and G, the actual reduced critical scheme. The smoothness and rank
arguments are proved here. The input from G includes its supplied
positive complete gluing certificate; completeness alone is not used
as an unconditional rigidity theorem.

## 1. Precise input and the local tuple quotient

Let \(\beta\) be an \(m\)-head braid, \(m\ge2\), in B's
inverse-Hurwitz convention
\[
 (H_i,H_{i+1})\longmapsto
       (H_{i+1},H_{i+1}H_iH_{i+1}^{-1}),\qquad
 P=H_m\cdots H_1.
 \tag{1}
\]
This convention preserves the displayed product. Fix a tuple
\(H^0\) with the following properties:

* Every \(H_i^0\) is noncentral with trace \(-2\), and the tuple
  is irreducible.
* It is literally closed: \(\beta H^0=H^0\).
* The total \(P^0\) is regular semisimple, with a selected eigenvalue
  \(k_0\ne k_0^{-1}\).
* The germ of the literal closed character scheme at this tuple,
  with the meridian eigenvalue fixed at \(-1\), is a reduced point.

The last condition is scheme-theoretic: its local analytic ring is
\(\mathbb C\), not merely a ring whose reduced zero set is one point.
For the complete geometric tuple, G, section 6, proves precisely this
input after additionally supplying the exterior's positive complete
gluing certificate specified there. G's resolved framing quotient
retains the selected flags, including over local Artin rings. We take
the regular presentation as supplied and use no new stabilization or
normalization assertion.

Put \(M=e^\mu\) and consider, near \((0,H^0)\), the family
\[
 \mathcal T=\{(\mu,H):\det H_i=1,
                  \operatorname{tr}H_i=-M-M^{-1}\text{ for all }i\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Keep the noncentral locus. Each fixed-\(\mu\) factor is a smooth
two-dimensional conjugacy class. Indeed the trace differential on
\(SL_2\) is zero only at scalar matrices, and the centralizer in
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) of a noncentral matrix has dimension one.
Thus its tangent space is exactly
\(\{[X,H_i]:X\in\mathfrak{sl}_2\}\).
An explicit regular representative throughout this meridian family is
\[
                     H_*(\mu)=
                  \begin{pmatrix}-M&1\\0&-M^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Conjugating (3) by two transverse Lie-algebra coordinates gives a
local parametrization of each factor, holomorphically in \(\mu\).
Consequently \(\mathcal T\) is smooth of dimension \(2m+1\), with
smooth fibers of dimension \(2m\).

The chosen eigenline for eigenvalue \(-M\) introduces no hidden
direction here. The matrix \(H_i+MI\) has determinant zero and has a
unit entry near the base, so its kernel is a holomorphic line, also
over local Artin rings. At \(\mu=0\) it is the unique parabolic
eigenline. Choosing the inverse meridian mark replaces \(\mu\) by
\(-\mu\), without changing the fixed-meridian base germ.

Simultaneous projective conjugation is locally free: an irreducible
tuple has only scalar common commutant. The local quotient
\(\mathcal X\) is therefore smooth of dimension
\[
                   n+1=2m-2,\qquad n=2m-3,
 \tag{4}
\]
and its fiber \(X_\mu\) is smooth of dimension \(n\).
Here this quotient can be constructed directly. Choose three linear
slice equations transverse to the three infinitesimal conjugations.
The action map from a neighborhood of the identity in \(PGL_2\)
times that slice is locally biholomorphic. On B's irreducible Gram
chart, a conjugator between two nearby tuples is unique projectively
and tends to the identity: the linear equations for it have a
one-dimensional kernel at the base, generated by \(I\). Normalize
one nonzero scalar coordinate of that kernel. Thus the slice gives
the local character quotient, rather than just a quotient of tangent
spaces. All subsequent statements concern this analytic germ and its
local Artin tests.

The braid induces a holomorphic automorphism of \(\mathcal X\),
preserving both \(\mu\) and \(f=\operatorname{tr}P\).

## 2. Product trace is a submersion before imposing closure

At the base, \(df\) is nonzero on the fixed-meridian fiber \(X_0\).
We prove this without assuming anything about an axis exterior.

For each \(i\), write
\[
 A_i=H_m\cdots H_{i+1},\qquad B_i=H_{i-1}\cdots H_1,
                         P=A_iH_iB_i.
 \tag{5}
\]
Varying only \(H_i\) by \([X,H_i]\) gives
\[
 \delta_i f=\operatorname{tr}(A_i[X,H_i]B_i)
           =\operatorname{tr}([H_i,B_iA_i]X).
 \tag{6}
\]
If every such derivative vanished, nondegeneracy of the trace pairing
on \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) would give
\[
 [H_i,B_iA_i]=0,
 \quad\text{hence}\quad [H_i,B_iPB_i^{-1}]=0
                         \quad\text{for every }i.
 \tag{7}
\]
For \(i=1\), (7) says that \(H_1\) commutes with \(P\).
Inductively, the previous factors then make \(B_i\) commute with
\(P\), and (7) says that \(H_i\) commutes with \(P\) as well.
All meridians would preserve the eigenlines of the regular semisimple
\(P\), contradicting irreducibility.

Thus \(df\ne0\). It descends to the quotient because \(f\) is
conjugation invariant. Shrinking the germ, choose coordinates
\[
                    (\mu,f,y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1})
 \tag{8}
\]
on \(\mathcal X\). Preservation of \(\mu,f\) means that the braid
has the form
\[
                (\mu,f,y)\longmapsto(\mu,f,b(\mu,f,y)).
 \tag{9}
\]
Its character-fixed scheme \(\mathcal Y\) is therefore defined by
exactly the following list of \(n-1\) equations, with no additional
fixed-coordinate equation:
\[
                   F_j=b_j(\mu,f,y)-y_j=0,
                              \quad 1\le j\le n-1.
 \tag{10}
\]
We do not yet assume that these equations have independent
differentials. Write \(Y_0=\mathcal Y\cap\{\mu=0\}\).

## 3. The marked axis coordinate forces full rank

Pass to the selected local eigenvalue branch of the total product,
\[
 k^2-fk+1=0,
 \qquad
 \Pi_+=\frac{P-k^{-1}I}{k-k^{-1}},\qquad \Pi_-=I-\Pi_+.
 \tag{11}
\]
The cover is unramified at the base. B proves, on the
character-fixed scheme and over local Artin rings, that
\[
     (\beta H)_i=C H_iC^{-1},\qquad [C,P]=0,
     \qquad C=c_+\Pi_++c_-\Pi_-.
 \tag{12}
\]
The projective conjugator is unique; a \(GL_2\) representative is
only a local scalar choice. Its two coefficients are units. The ratio
\[
                           z=c_+/c_-
 \tag{13}
\]
is an intrinsic holomorphic unit on \(\mathcal Y\), with \(z=1\)
at the base. The two total eigenvalue marks exchange \(z\) and
\(z^{-1}\).

B also proves the ideal-level statement
\[
   \text{literal closed character scheme inside }\mathcal Y
                                  \ =\ \{z-1=0\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
To recall why this retains nilpotents, the matrix closure equations
are equivalent to the entries of \([C,H_i]\). Their products give
\([C,B_\nu]\) for a trace-Gram matrix basis. The inverse basis map
recovers the nonscalar part of \(C\) from those commutators. In
(12) that part is generated by the single unit multiple \(z-1\).
Conversely every closure difference is divisible by \(z-1\).
This is an equality of ideals, not just of complex zero sets.

**Fixed-meridian theorem.** The germ \(Y_0\) is a smooth curve,
and \(z-1\) is a local coordinate on it. In particular,
\[
                  \mathcal O_{Y_0,H^0}\simeq\mathbb C\{z-1\}.
 \tag{15}
\]

Indeed (10), at \(\mu=0\), defines \(Y_0\) by \(n-1\)
equations in a smooth \(n\)-dimensional ambient germ. Hence
\(\dim T_{H^0}Y_0\ge1\). By the stated rigidity input and (14),
\(Y_0/(z-1)\) is a reduced point. Its tangent space is
\[
                 \ker(dz:T_{H^0}Y_0\longrightarrow\mathbb C)=0.
 \tag{16}
\]
Thus \(\dim T_{H^0}Y_0\le1\). Equality holds, the Jacobian of
(10) has rank \(n-1\), and the holomorphic implicit-function
theorem makes \(Y_0\) smooth and reduced. Equation (16) then makes
\(z-1\) its coordinate. This proves the analytic germ statement,
and consequently its completed local-ring and Artin versions.

**Meridian-family theorem.** The germ \(\mathcal Y\) is a smooth
surface, with local coordinates \((\mu,z-1)\):
\[
                 \mathcal O_{\mathcal Y,(0,H^0)}
                          \simeq\mathbb C\{\mu,z-1\}.
 \tag{17}
\]
The same \(n-1\) Jacobian rows in (10) already have full rank
on the \(\mu=0\) fiber, so they have full rank in the
\((n+1)\)-dimensional total ambient germ. It follows that
\(\mathcal Y\) is smooth of dimension two. Alternatively the
rigidity of its quotient by \((\mu,z-1)\) gives the same rank.
On its tangent space the map \((d\mu,dz)\) is injective, since its
kernel is exactly the zero tangent space of that reduced quotient.
It is therefore an isomorphism. The inverse-function theorem proves
(17). The literal filling \(z=1\) is a smooth meridian curve and
is transverse to the fixed-meridian axis curve.

This proof uses submersivity of \(f\) on the ambient tuple quotient.
It does not assert that \(f\), or the selected \(k\), restricts to
a coordinate on \(Y_0\). Their derivatives along that curve may
vanish; no such derivative was needed in (16).

## 4. The actual group filling and its ramified trace

The mapping-torus group in the convention (1) is
\[
 \langle h_1,\ldots,h_m,t\mid
              t h_i t^{-1}=\beta(h_i)\rangle,
          \qquad [t,p]=1,\quad p=h_m\cdots h_1.
 \tag{18}
\]
Its local representations with irreducible meridian tuple have
stable letter \([C]\) projectively. Choose the unique determinant-one
lift near \(C=I\); the other lift is not silently identified with it.
The ratio (13) and this sign specify that lift. Consequently the
selected local axis character germ at the representation with
\(t=I\) is the germ just proved, and literal filling \(t=I\)
is precisely \(z=1\). No discrete faithful or hyperbolic
representation of the unfilled axis exterior is assumed.

Set \(\eta=\Log z\) with \(\eta=0\) at the base. Since
\(\det C=1\), the retained branch has
\[
 C=e^{\eta/2}\Pi_++e^{-\eta/2}\Pi_-,
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}C=2\cosh(\eta/2).
 \tag{19}
\]
In particular the exact trace equation is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \operatorname{tr}C-2
       =\frac{(z-1)^2}{\sqrt z\,(\sqrt z+1)^2}
       =\frac{\eta^2}{4}+O(\eta^4).
 \quad}\tag{20}
\]
The square root has value one at the base. Its denominator is a
unit. Thus the ideal of trace filling is \(((z-1)^2)\), whereas
the marked literal filling ideal is \((z-1)\). On the fixed-meridian
axis curve the former has length two and the latter length one.
The corresponding quotient rings of the surface (17) retain this
same distinction over the meridian coordinate. Switching the total
eigenline sends \(\eta\) to \(-\eta\) and leaves (20) unchanged.

The result reduces the many literal closure equations to one
transverse marked equation after character reduction, on this
supplied rigid geometric germ. It is classical local information.
The axis coordinate \(\eta\) and the original quantum meridian
\(\mu\) are independent coordinates in (17), not interchangeable
normal derivatives. Neither B's actual source formulas nor G's
rational correspondence identifies the Cartan-weighted quantum
first normal with a residue in \(\eta\). No such identification,
source-cycle coefficient, amplitude estimate, or lower bound is
deduced here. In particular the positive gluing certificate used for
the rigidity input is not replaced by an assertion for every fixed
short partial filling.
