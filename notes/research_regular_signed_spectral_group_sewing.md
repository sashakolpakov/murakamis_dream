## 2. The real local metrics and their force angles

An ordinary face has signed radius r_f=(-1)^gamma_f/L_f and geometric
radius |r_f|. At a MAIN edge the center distance is

    |r_f+r_g|;

equal signs give external tangency and opposite signs give internal
tangency. On a matching edge with spectral parameter delta, the
center distance squared is

    r_f²+r_g²+2r_f r_g cos(2pi delta).                         (2)

The incidence character has zero outer entries. Hence the two finite
outers have equal gamma, so (2) is a real circle intersection metric
on the prescribed branch.

To check the actual angle, normalize one port by its common row sign
so x,y>0. Its middle is either q>0 with T_q>0 or a post-pole
q=-c<-L_end with T_q<0. The latter satisfies
c>max(x,y); its internal tangent distances are positive and nonzero.
With D=(x+y)²-4xy sin²(pi delta), the law of cosines gives

    cos theta_y=
      [y(x-y)+q(x+y cos(2pi delta))]/[(y+q)sqrt D],
    sin theta_y=
      2y cos(pi delta) T_q/[(y+q)sqrt D],                     (3)

and

    exp(-i theta_q)=(T_q-iq cos(pi delta))/
                                  (T_q+iq cos(pi delta)).   (4)

These are exactly the exponentiated source forces from the local
generating relation. The signed q<0 does not invalidate the law of
cosines: (q+y)/(qy)>0 is its actual internal-tangent distance.
In the selected branch all three angles lie in (0,pi) and sum to pi.
The input/output signs sigma_t in (1) orient their placement relative
to the original white triangle order.

At a line corner the same assertions use the explicit finite line
matrices, not an inference from a vanished scalar root. The two finite
angles use the directed normal of the cooriented line as the infinite
center direction. Their sum is pi. At a two-line corner the finite
angle is pi and both line angles are zero.

Thus each local triangle is a rigid configuration up to an
orientation-preserving Euclidean motion, including its coorientations
and selected matching intersection flag. Adjacent triangles have
the same actual edge data because every ordinary signed clock is
shared. They need not place their disks disjointly.

## 3. Edge gluing, including the protected-line signs

Along a finite/finite edge its two distinct ordered centers determine
the Euclidean gluing. Along a MAIN line/finite edge the tangent line
and the finite center determine it. Along a MATCHING line/finite edge
the selected J intersection point is retained as well. This matters
at cos(2pi delta)=0, when the finite center lies on the line:
the selected intersection fixes the remaining possible half-turn.

There is also a lift check. Normalize the reflection of each finite
circle by its global signed curvature, the real lower-left entry
of its determinant-minus-one matrix. An orientation-preserving
Euclidean coordinate change preserves this normalization. Across a
MAIN line/finite edge both local raw products have trace-2. Once the
finite reflection is matched, the opposite central sign for the line
would give trace+2, so it is excluded. Across a MATCHING edge the
two triangles are the halves of the SAME original port; their common
ordered product and selected J flag already fix the same line
reflection, also at trace0.

Thus the edge gluings propagate actual line reflections, not an
unrecorded independent sign at each occurrence of a protected face.
No positive sign is arbitrarily assigned to a zero clock.

The marked line/line edge uv is unique in the original simple sphere.
It will be a boundary edge in the developing disk and is never used
as an underdetermined interior gluing.

## 4. The disk holonomy and the same two marked lines

finite circle, using the exact two-line adapter. A marked triangle

protected, whereas a negative triangle would overload that face.

Develop every local triangle by the unique edge gluing above.

Cycles of the disk dual are generated by these finite-vertex stars.
Consequently development is independent of path. Both marked

Each boundary line fan is connected, so it propagates the SAME

Here is the raw-lift calculation that makes this last assertion
