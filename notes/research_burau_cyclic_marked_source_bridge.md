# The original marked source in the finite cyclic Burau model

Private written derivation, 22 November 2026. The finite cyclic model
below retains the original normalized knot scalar as a specified inverse
matrix element, and hence as an ordinary bordered determinant. Its
unbordered ordinary determinant is one. No asymptotic estimate or volume
lower bound follows from this presentation.

The local quantum-binomial conventions are fixed by direct multiplication.
In particular, the inconsistent printed quantum-integer shorthand in the
Huynh--Lê paper is not used to identify its module with the physical
module. Both crossing comparisons below are full finite matrix identities.

## 1. The source and the exact statement

Put
\[
 N\ge2,\qquad d=N-1,\qquad q=e^{\pi i/N},\qquad Q=q^2,\qquad
 \eta=(-1)^d,\qquad \kappa=q^{d^2/2}.
 \tag{1}
\]
All fractional powers in this note use these displayed exponential lifts.
Let the original physical braid operator on \(b\) heads be
\[
 W=R_{i_1}^{\delta_1}\cdots R_{i_k}^{\delta_k},\qquad
 \delta_j\in\{1,-1\},\qquad w_W=\sum_j\delta_j .
 \tag{2}
\]
These are column operators: the rightmost factor acts first. Assume
the closure is a knot. In the uniform balanced physical basis,
\[
 K|a\rangle=q^{d-2a}|a\rangle,\qquad
 g=\eta K,\qquad P_d=|d\rangle\langle d| .
\]
The original average and zero-framing convention are
\[
 \Phi_b(W)=\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}
       [(I\otimes g^{\otimes(b-1)})W],
 \qquad K_N(W)=\kappa^{w_W}\Phi_b(W).
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the original positive crossing has enhancement
\(\phi_N(R)=\eta\kappa q^d=\kappa^{-1}\); its inverse enhancement
is \(\kappa\). Thus (3) is the original inverse-writhe correction.

Form a sequence of local Burau blocks from the reversed, sign-mirrored
list
\[
 (i_k,-\delta_k),\ (i_{k-1},-\delta_{k-1}),\ldots,
 (i_1,-\delta_1).
 \tag{4}
\]
Use independent operator variables at every occurrence, and multiply
the embedded blocks in the order (4) to obtain \(\rho\).
The finite operator \(C_N\), defined in Section 2, acts on a prescribed
space of dimension \(N^{3k}\). Let \(\mathbf1\) be its constant function,
and let \(\ell_N\) evaluate all its crossing variables at
\[
                       x_j=y_j=Q^{-1},\qquad u_j=1.
 \tag{5}
\]
Then the exact original source formula is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 K_N(W)=Q^{(b-1+w_W)/2}\,
       \ell_N\left(\sum_{r=0}^{k(N-1)}C_N^r\right)\mathbf1 .
 \quad}
 \tag{6}
\]
The exponent of \(Q\) is an integer: a knot braid has
\(w_W\equiv b-1\pmod2\). Formula (4) is part of (6), rather than an
unspecified mirror or braid-order convention.

## 2. The finite root operator and its nilpotence

For each crossing take multiplication and shift operators on Laurent
functions, with \(\tau_x f(x,y,u)=f(Qx,y,u)\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_+&=(u-y\tau_x^{-1})\tau_y^{-1},&
 b_+&=u^2,&c_+&=x\tau_y^{-2}\tau_u^{-1},\\
 a_-&=(\tau_y-x^{-1})\tau_x^{-1}\tau_u,&
 b_-&=u^2,&c_-&=y^{-1}\tau_x^{-1}\tau_u .
\end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
Here \(\tau_xx=Qx\tau_x\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 a_+b_+=b_+a_+,&a_+c_+=Qc_+a_+,&b_+c_+=Q^2c_+b_+,\\
 a_-b_-=Q^2b_-a_-,&c_-a_-=Qa_-c_-,&c_-b_-=Q^2b_-c_- .
\end{array}
 \tag{8}
\]
Operators belonging to distinct crossing occurrences commute.
For later coefficient calculations, let \(\mathcal E_z(P)\) mean:
apply a finite operator polynomial \(P\) to \(1\), then set
\(x_j=y_j=z,\ u_j=1\). Write \(\mathcal E_N=\mathcal E_{Q^d}\).
This evaluation is multiplicative for separated crossing copies,
and need not be multiplicative within one copy. Normal ordering gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E_z(b_+^s c_+^r a_+^l)
   &=Q^{-rl}z^r\prod_{h=0}^{l-1}(1-zQ^{-r-h}),\\
 \mathcal E_z(b_-^s c_-^r a_-^l)
   &=z^{-r}\prod_{h=0}^{l-1}(1-z^{-1}Q^{r+h}).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]
For example these formulas follow by applying \(a_\pm\) successively,
then the indicated shifts in \(c_\pm\); they also give an induction
proof for every nonnegative \(r,s,l\).

At the root, pass to the finite Laurent-function quotient
\[
 \mathcal F_N=\mathbb C[(\mu_N)^{3k}],\qquad
                  \dim\mathcal F_N=N^{3k}.
 \tag{10}
\]
All multiplication and shift \(N\)-th powers are \(I\).
Direct products in (7) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_+^N&=(u^N-y^N\tau_x^{-N})\tau_y^{-N}=0,\\
 a_-^N&=(\tau_y^N-x^{-N})\tau_x^{-N}\tau_u^N=0.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
Equivalently, the two terms in \(a_\pm=U-V\) \(Q^{\mp1}\)-commute,
with \(U^N=I\) and \(V^N=(-1)^{N-1}I\); the primitive-root binomial
identity gives (11), including even and composite \(N\).

Let \(\mathcal S_N\) be the unital subalgebra generated by the specified
triples, and \(J\) its two-sided ideal generated by the \(a_j\).
It is an ideal in this generated subalgebra, not in the full ambient
endomorphism algebra. Relations (8) put every monomial into normal
order. A product of \(k(N-1)+1\) elements of \(J\) is consequently a
linear combination of monomials containing some \(a_j^N\). Hence
\[
                         J^{L}=0,\qquad L=k(N-1)+1.
 \tag{12}
\]

The local blocks are
\[
 S_+=\begin{pmatrix}a_+&b_+\\c_+&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S_-=\begin{pmatrix}0&c_-\\b_-&a_-\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Embed them at their adjacent strand positions. For a square operator
matrix use the column-ordered convention
\[
 \det_Q A=\sum_{\pi}(-Q)^{\operatorname{inv}(\pi)}
                      A_{\pi(1),1}\cdots A_{\pi(r),r}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Delete the first row and column from \(\rho\) to get \(\rho'\), and set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C(t)&=\sum_{\emptyset\ne I\subset\{2,\ldots,b\}}
       (-1)^{|I|-1}t^{|I|}\det_Q((Q\rho)_{I,I}),\\
 C_N&=\left.C(1)\right|_{\mathcal F_N}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]
The variable \(t\) is central. Modulo \(J\), every block is a weighted
transposition. Its product has the one-cycle zero pattern of the knot
permutation. No nonempty proper subset of the \(b\) strands is invariant
under that permutation. Every nonempty principal minor remaining after
the first strand is deleted therefore vanishes by its zero pattern,
independently of the order of its coefficients. Thus every coefficient
of \(C(t)\) belongs to \(J\), and
\[
 C(t)^L=0,\qquad
 [I-C(t)]^{-1}=\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}C(t)^r,\qquad
                         \det(I-C_N)=1.
 \tag{16}
\]
The last determinant is the ordinary determinant on \(\mathcal F_N\).
The first two identities are polynomial identities in \(t\).

For every finite operator polynomial \(P\), all shifts preserve the
Laurent quotient in (10), so
\[
        \left.\mathcal E_N(P)\right|_Q
                    =\ell_N\,\pi_N(P)\,\mathbf1.
 \tag{17}
\]
This statement is about finite polynomials. No infinite inverse is
silently extended by (17).

## 3. The actual coaction kernels

Define the quantum polynomial algebra by
\(z_i z_j=Qz_jz_i\) for \(i<j\). For a right-quantum matrix \(A\)
whose entries commute with the \(z_i\), put
\[
                    \Phi_A(z_i)=\sum_j A_{ij}z_j .
 \tag{18}
\]
Our standard Gaussian \({n\brack l}_u\) is the polynomial with product
\(\prod_{h=1}^l(1-u^{n-l+h})/(1-u^h)\) at generic \(u\), and
its polynomial continuation elsewhere. Its base is \(u\), rather
than the inverse-base integer convention printed elsewhere.

Let \(U_\pm=\mathcal E_N(\Phi_{S_\pm})\). Direct expansion of (18)
using (8)--(9), before root specialization, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 U_+(i,j;l)
 &= {i\brack l}_{Q^{-1}}Q^{j(l-i)}z^j
                         \prod_{h=0}^{l-1}(1-zQ^{-j-h}),\\
 U_-(i,j;l)
 &= {j\brack l}_{Q}Q^{i(j-l)}z^{-i}
                         \prod_{h=0}^{l-1}(1-z^{-1}Q^{i+h}),
 \qquad z=Q^d .
\end{aligned}
 \tag{19}
\]
The respective output indices are \((j+l,i-l)\) and
\((j-l,i+l)\).

Here is the ordering check. In the positive case set \(X=az_1\),
\(Y=bz_2\); then \(YX=Q^{-1}XY\).
Moving \(z_2^{i-l}\) past \(z_1^j\) contributes \(Q^{-j(i-l)}\).
Moving \(a^l\) past \(c^j\) contributes \(Q^{jl}\), which cancels
the \(Q^{-jl}\) in (9). This proves the first formula.
In the negative case \(X=bz_1,Y=az_2\) satisfy \(YX=QXY\).
The factors from moving \(z_2^i\) past \(z_1^{j-l}\), and
from moving \(c^i\) past \(b^{j-l}\), are respectively
\(Q^{-i(j-l)}\) and \(Q^{2i(j-l)}\). This proves the second.

The formulas are polynomial at every root. If \(i,j<N\), the positive
product vanishes when \(j+l\ge N\), and the negative product vanishes
when \(i+l\ge N\). Thus the kernels restrict to the full \(N^2\)
finite head space. More generally, if any one incoming index is
below \(N\), the outgoing index on the same braid strand is below
\(N\) in every nonzero summand. This observation will make the
selected trace finite.

## 4. The full physical phase adapter

For \(0\le a,b\le d\), write \(h_a=d-2a\),
\([n]=(q^n-q^{-n})/(q-q^{-1})\), and \(\varrho=q-q^{-1}\).
The actual balanced physical kernels are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_+(a,b;l)
 &=q^{h_a h_b/2+l(a-b)-l(l+1)/2}\varrho^l
       \frac{[d-a+l]![b+l]!}{[d-a]![b]![l]!},\\
 R_-(a,b;l)
 &=(-1)^l q^{-h_a h_b/2+l(a-b)+l(l+1)/2}\varrho^l
       \frac{[d-b+l]![a+l]!}{[d-b]![a]![l]!}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{20}
\]
Their outputs are \((b+l,a-l)\), \((b-l,a+l)\), with the
corresponding nonnegative indices at most \(d\). These are the finite
universal crossing and its inverse in balanced coordinates. One obtains
the second by inverting the divided-power exponential before the
Cartan factor; the Gaussian convolution gives the inverse without
dividing by any \([N]\). The complete entry proof and its original
Casimir identification are in the physical crossing-adapter source below.

On an auxiliary head let \(K_0e_i=q^{d-2i}e_i\).
Define one explicit map on all \(b\) heads:
\[
 \mathcal T_b e_{n_1,\ldots,n_b}
   =q^{-\frac d2\sum_{h=1}^b h(d-2n_h)}
                      |d-n_1,\ldots,d-n_b\rangle .
 \tag{21}
\]
This is a phase-permutation isomorphism. At the positive local output
\((j+l,i-l)\), its output/input phase is \(q^{d(i-j-l)}\);
at the negative output \((j-l,i+l)\), it is \(q^{d(i-j+l)}\).
Both are independent of the adjacent strand position.

For completeness the factorial conversions establishing the comparison
are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 {i\brack l}_{Q^{-1}}
    &=q^{-l(i-l)}\frac{[i]!}{[l]![i-l]!},&
 {j\brack l}_Q
    &=q^{l(j-l)}\frac{[j]!}{[l]![j-l]!},\\
 \prod_{h<l}(1-Q^{d-j-h})
    &=(-\varrho)^lq^{l(d-j)-l(l-1)/2}
                        \frac{[d-j]!}{[d-j-l]!},\\
 \prod_{h<l}(1-Q^{i+h+1})
    &=(-\varrho)^lq^{l(i+1)+l(l-1)/2}
                        \frac{[i+l]!}{[i]!}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{22}
\]
Use \([N-r]=[r]\) and \(q^d=-q^{-1}\).
Substitution in (19), with \(a=d-i,b=d-j\), gives exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \kappa^{-1}q^{d(i-j-l)}U_+(i,j;l)&=R_-(d-i,d-j;l),\\
 \kappa q^{d(i-j+l)}U_-(i,j;l)&=R_+(d-i,d-j;l).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{23}
\]
All factorials used in these supported identities have index below
\(N\). Consequently, at every adjacent pair,
\[
       \mathcal T_b U_{\varepsilon,h}\mathcal T_b^{-1}
                         =\kappa^\varepsilon R_{h}^{-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{24}
\]
This proof uses every physical matrix entry and retains the
repeated-root contributions of the actual crossings. It is not a
comparison only at distinct Casimir values.

There is also an order issue. For matrices with separated coefficient
copies, (18) gives
\[
                  \Phi_{AB}=\Phi_B\circ\Phi_A.
 \tag{25}
\]
Evaluation of the separated copies respects this composition.
On the invariant finite head space, the deliberately reversed
list (4) and (24) imply
\[
       U_\rho:=\mathcal E_N(\Phi_\rho)
                 =\kappa^{-w_W}\mathcal T_b^{-1}W\mathcal T_b .
 \tag{26}
\]
No topological braid-reversal identity is needed for (26).

## 5. Quantum MacMahon and the finite selected trace

We use the following coefficient identity for a right-quantum matrix
with the convention (18):
\[
 \left[1+\sum_{\emptyset\ne I}(-1)^{|I|}
                         \det_Q(A_{I,I})\right]^{-1}
       =\sum_{n\ge0}\operatorname{tr}
                  (\Phi_A\mid\mathcal P_Q^{(n)}).
 \tag{27}
\]
It is interpreted formally in the degree of \(A\). The identity is
the quantum MacMahon formula. Its primary operator-elimination proof
is audited with parameter \(Q^{-1}\); a proof synopsis fixing both
the coefficient order and the specialization is given in Section 8.
No colored-Jones determinant theorem is used as a source premise.

Apply (27) to \(tQ\rho'\), with central \(t\):
\[
 [1-C(t)]^{-1}
    =\sum_{n\ge0}t^nQ^n
                   \operatorname{tr}(\Phi_{\rho'}\mid\mathcal P_Q^{(n)}).
 \tag{28}
\]
Every coefficient is a finite operator polynomial, so it can first be
specialized to the root representation. The left side is the polynomial
in (16), of \(t\)-degree at most \((b-1)(L-1)\). Applying (17) is
therefore legitimate coefficientwise.

There is a sharper cutoff after applying the specified observation.
Setting \(z_1=0\) is the highest-cut projection \(p_0\).
For an input monomial independent of \(z_1\),
\[
                  p_0\Phi_\rho=\Phi_{\rho'},\qquad
                  p_0\mathcal E_N=\mathcal E_Np_0.
 \tag{29}
\]
In a nonzero diagonal trace summand its first strand label is \(0<N\).
The last assertion of Section 3 propagates the property “label below
\(N\)” along that braid strand. A knot permutation has one cycle, so
the closed diagonal conditions propagate it through every strand.
Every selected trace label is consequently in \(\{0,\ldots,d\}\).
This proves directly that the observed right side of (28) has degree
at most \((b-1)d\), without an infinite Verma trace limit.
At \(t=1\), (28)--(29) yield the finite identity
\[
 \ell_N(I-C_N)^{-1}\mathbf1
   =\sum_{n_2,\ldots,n_b=0}^d
        Q^{n_2+\cdots+n_b}
                   (U_\rho)_{0,n_2,\ldots,n_b}^{0,n_2,\ldots,n_b}.
 \tag{30}
\]
Both sides are now finite. There is no interchange of an infinite
root-level sum with evaluation.

## 6. The marked cut, tail pivot, and framing

The map (21) gives literal operator identities
\[
 \mathcal T_b P_0\mathcal T_b^{-1}=P_d,\qquad
 \mathcal T_b K_{0,h}^{-1}\mathcal T_b^{-1}=K_h .
 \tag{31}
\]
Only the \(b-1\) traced heads carry a pivot. Since
\(Q^{n}=q^d(K_0^{-1})_{nn}\), equations (26) and (30) give
\[
 S_N:=\ell_N(I-C_N)^{-1}\mathbf1
   =q^{d(b-1)}\kappa^{-w_W}
                    \operatorname{Tr}[(P_d\otimes K^{\otimes(b-1)})W].
 \tag{32}
\]
Before imposing that \(W\) is an actual intertwiner, the marked
projector in (32) is retained. For any inserted nonintertwining exterior,
conjugating that projector is the source identity; averaging it is not.

For the actual braid operator \(W\), its \(K\)-weighted partial trace
over the last \(b-1\) heads commutes with the first head's \(E,F,K\).
This follows by taking partial traces of the original coproduct
commutators, with
\(E_{1B}=E_1K_B+E_B\) and \(F_{1B}=F_1+K_1^{-1}F_B\).
The first-head \(K\) has distinct eigenvalues and its adjacent
lowering arrows are nonzero. The partial trace is therefore scalar.
This argument, including the \(F\) trace ordering, is proved in the
prescribed-cut source below and divides by no quantum dimension.
Consequently
\[
       \operatorname{Tr}[(P_d\otimes K^{\otimes(b-1)})W]
                          =\eta^{b-1}\Phi_b(W).
 \tag{33}
\]

Combining (32)--(33), and using \(\eta q^d=\kappa^{-2}\), gives
\[
 S_N=\kappa^{-w_W-2(b-1)}\Phi_b(W),\qquad
 K_N(W)=\kappa^{2(w_W+b-1)}S_N .
 \tag{34}
\]
Finally \(\kappa^2=q^{d^2}=(-1)^Nq\), and \(w_W+b-1\) is even.
Thus
\[
            \kappa^{2(w_W+b-1)}=Q^{(w_W+b-1)/2}.
 \tag{35}
\]
Equations (16), (34), and (35) prove (6), with the prescribed original
closing, all phases and all levels retained.

## 7. Ordinary determinant and exact scope

Set \(D=N^{3k}\), \(A_N=I-C_N\), and
\(\zeta_N=Q^{(b-1+w_W)/2}\).
Since \(\det A_N=1\), the ordinary Schur identity proves
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 K_N(W)=\det
       \begin{pmatrix}
          A_N&\mathbf1\\
          -\zeta_N\ell_N&0
       \end{pmatrix}.
 \quad}
 \tag{36}
\]
All entries and both source vectors are specified independently of
\(K_N(W)\). The bordered matrix has size \(N^{3k}+1\).

The unbordered ordinary determinant is identically one. Its selected
inverse element, or the border in (36), contains the knot source.
Neither nilpotence nor an ordinary bulk determinant approximation
bounds that selected element from below. No Fuglede--Kadison limit,
conditioning estimate, efficient algorithm, or geometric overlap is
proved here.

The theorem concerns the full original closed scalar of a knot braid.
It neither replaces the physical pair algebra by its values at distinct
roots nor claims a new auxiliary-module realization of arbitrary free
boundary polynomials, independent Casimir normal insertions, or
generic-\(q\) derivatives. Such inserted-source comparisons require
their actual operators and marked observations before applying any
reduction. For links with more than one permutation cycle, the single
cut and the nilpotent-minor argument used here do not give this theorem.

## 8. Audit of the formal coefficient identity

Here are the finite identities behind (27). They also explain why its
use at a composite or even root introduces no factorial denominator.
Use a temporary parameter \(r=Q^{-1}\), so
\(z_jz_i=rz_iz_j\) for \(i<j\), and use determinant weights
\((-r)^{-\operatorname{inv}(\pi)}\), exactly (14).
Let \(G(\boldsymbol n)\) be the diagonal coefficient of
\(\prod_i(\sum_j a_{ij}z_j)^{n_i}\). Its constant-term version is
\[
 H(\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol z)
   =z_m^{-n_m}\cdots z_1^{-n_1}
                    \prod_i\left(\sum_j a_{ij}z_j\right)^{n_i}.
\]
Write \(M_iG(\boldsymbol n)=G(\boldsymbol n+\boldsymbol e_i)\).
Put \(B_{ii}=M_i-a_{ii}\), and, for \(i<j\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{ij}&=-r^{n_i+2n_{i+1}+\cdots+2n_{j-1}+n_j}a_{ij},\\
 B_{ji}&=-r^{-n_i-2n_{i+1}-\cdots-2n_{j-1}-n_j}a_{ji}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{37}
\]
Moving \(z_i^{-1}\) and the new linear form through \(H\) proves
\(\sum_jB_{ij}z_jH=0\).
Here is an explicit check of the two determinant steps.
Set
\[
 D_i=r^{2\sum_{h<i}n_h+n_i},\qquad q_{ij}=D_j/D_i.
\]
Then \(B_{ij}=\delta_{ij}M_i-q_{ij}a_{ij}\).
The relations \(M_ir^{n_i}=r^{n_i+1}M_i\) imply the
right-quantum relations for \(B\). In a column containing a diagonal
entry, its \(M_i\) sees exponent \(-1\) or \(1\) in the other
\(q\)-factor, which gives its required column factor \(r\).
In a cross relation a diagonal entry in the top-left or bottom-right
position sees exponent zero in the opposite \(q\)-factor. A diagonal
entry in the top-right or bottom-left position sees exponent \(-2\)
or \(2\) in the other offdiagonal factor; the two cross terms then
cancel. The remaining terms are the corresponding relations for
\(A\), with the common multiplier
\(q_{ij}q_{i'j'}=q_{i'j}q_{ij'}\). This checks also the case of two
diagonal entries.

For any right-quantum matrix \(B\), introduce exterior variables
\(\theta_i^2=0\), \(\theta_k\theta_i=-r^{-1}\theta_i\theta_k\)
for \(i<k\), commuting with its coefficients. The column forms
\(V_j=\sum_i\theta_iB_{ij}\) satisfy
\(V_j^2=0\) and \(V_lV_j=-r^{-1}V_jV_l\) for \(j<l\).
For example the mixed coefficient assertion is exactly
\[
 B_{ij}B_{i'j'}-B_{i'j'}B_{ij}
   =r^{-1}B_{i'j}B_{ij'}-rB_{ij'}B_{i'j}
       \quad(i<i',\ j<j').
\]
Therefore \(V_1\cdots V_{m-1}V_j=0\) for \(j<m\).
Its top exterior coefficient gives the needed cofactor cancellation:
\[
 \sum_i(-r)^{i-m}\det_r(B_i)B_{ij}=0\quad(j<m),
\]
where \(B_i\) deletes row \(i\) and the last column.
For \(j=m\) the same sum is the column-Laplace formula for
\(\det_rB\). Applying these cofactors to the equations
\(\sum_jB_{ij}z_jH=0\) eliminates the first \(m-1\) variables and
gives \(\det_r(B)G=0\). No assertion about swapping columns of an
already swapped right-quantum matrix is used.

Expanding the same determinant, grouping terms by which diagonal
entries supply \(M_i\), gives
\[
 \det_r(B)=\sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}}
                (-1)^{|I|}\det_r(A_{I,I})M_{\bar I}.
 \tag{38}
\]
To see the coefficient order, let \(I\) be the columns supplying
\(a\)-entries, and \(F=\bar I\) the columns supplying \(M_i\).
The contributing permutation \(\pi\) fixes every index of \(F\).
Move its factors \(q_{\pi(j),j}\) to the left. Their total product
is one. Moving them past the selected shifts supplies \(r^{2c}\),
where
\[
 c=\#\{(i,j):i\in F,\ j\in I,\ \pi(j)<i<j\}.
\]
Each fixed index has as many upward as downward crossings, so
\(\operatorname{inv}(\pi)=\operatorname{inv}(\pi|_I)+2c\).
The factor \(r^{2c}\) cancels precisely the extra determinant
weight. The remaining \(a\)-factors retain their ascending column
order and all selected shifts move to the right, proving (38).

Now sum the resulting shift recurrence on all \(\boldsymbol n\ge0\),
formally after scaling \(A\) by a central degree parameter.
Each shifted coordinate contributes its full sum minus its zero
boundary. Inclusion--exclusion yields
\[
 \sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}}(-1)^{|I|}
              \operatorname{Ferm}_r(A_{I,I})\,S_I=0,
 \tag{39}
\]
where \(S_I\) is the diagonal sum with the other occupation indices
zero and \(S_\emptyset=1\).
Induction on the number of variables gives
\(\operatorname{Ferm}_r(A)S_{\{1,\ldots,m\}}=1\).
Since its constant term is one, its formal inverse is unique; this
is (27). The argument uses only Laurent powers of \(r\), finite
coefficient extraction, and formal geometric inversion at constant
term one. Sections 2 and 5 make the subsequently observed root inverse
finite before setting the central parameter to one.

This is the operator-elimination proof of
[Garoufalidis--Lê--Zeilberger, Sections 2.1--2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0303319v3),
with the parameter inversion and source order made explicit.
The entire primary proof was read and checked. No colored-Jones
determinant theorem substitutes for Sections 3--6.

The Newton source supplies the complete original packet and its
enhancement. The physical adapter supplies both full balanced crossings;
the prescribed-cut note supplies the unrestricted marked cut, the
intertwiner-only scalarity step, and the original framing. These source
formulas are restated with the writhe convention (2)--(3).
The direct calculations (7)--(26) and (31)--(35) avoid importing
Huynh--Lê's colored-Jones theorem.

The primary [Huynh--Lê version 1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0503296v1)
supplies the standard operator definitions and the historical source
route. Its definition/coaction/cut proof chain was read in full.
Our Gaussians in (19) are derived from (7)--(9) and the specified
quantum plane. For instance the \(z_1z_2\) coefficient in
\((az_1+bz_2)^2\) is \((1+Q^{-1})ab\); interpreting the printed
inverse-base integer notation literally would instead give \(1+Q\).
Thus no step relies on that ambiguous notation or the resulting
printed module coefficient.

Only written algebra and source comparison were used. No numerical
evaluation, mathematical program, test, build, git action, or frozen
file edit was performed.
