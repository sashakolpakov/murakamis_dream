# The height direction of the following-reference action comparison

Research deduction, 7 November 2026. The selected action of the literal
transformed three/four prefix has a definite height derivative at every
actual ordinary following reference. Its increase with outgoing height
strictly exceeds the height derivative of one proved radius-dependent
remainder ceiling. Consequently lowering the outgoing height worsens
that calibrated margin. This does not decide the sign of the margin,
or obstruct the complete source. No numerical evaluation is used.

## 1. The original pre-Gram phase

Keep the exact polynomial and action of
[the complete transformed prefix](research_mixed_three_four_complete_transformed_prefix.md).
At complete left ordinary color the local selected action is
\[
 \mathcal P(z)=\operatorname{stat}_{a,t}
       [T_0(a)+Q(t;a,z)],\qquad
 Q=g(a+t)+g(t+z)-3g(t)-g(a)-g(z)+2\pi iat .             \tag{1}
\]
Every primitive has its original constant and logarithmic lift.
This is the action before the final Gram contraction. At an actual
reference of a longer word, the existence of this local germ follows
by eliminating the four prefix variables in the original critical
point, as in
[the exact next attachment](research_mixed_three_four_next_conditional_attachment.md),
Section 4. No whole-source packet at that new reference is assumed.

Write
\[
 t=T+i\eta,\quad z=Z+ih,\quad
 \rho=e^{2\pi\eta},\quad r=e^{2\pi h}.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
The two legs of the ordinary four-vertex lie in the original
\(O_4\) enclosure. In particular
\[
 6/25\le T,Z\le9/25,\qquad 1/2<\rho,r<1.              \tag{3}
\]
The real and height bounds are those of
[the actual reference domains](research_mixed_actual_last_row_reference_domains.md);
the radius guard is also recorded in
[the following-four remainder theorem](research_mixed_transformed_following_four_radial_majorant.md),
equation (16). We prove the scalar inequalities below on the larger
closed range \(1/2\le\rho,r\le1\).

For \(0<q<1\), put
\[
 A_r(q)=\operatorname{Arg}(1-r e^{-2\pi iq}),\qquad
 0<r\le1.                                             \tag{4}
\]
These are the original strip arguments. None of the arguments in
(4) vanishes on the ranges used below. Stationary differentiation,
before any estimate, gives
\[
 \mathcal P'(z)=g'(t+z)-g'(z),\qquad
 \partial_h\Re\mathcal P(Z+ih)
                  =A_r(Z)-A_{\rho r}(T+Z).            \tag{5}
\]
The derivative in (5) includes the motion of the critical variables:
their contributions vanish by the original stationary equations.

There is a horizontal curvature bound in this same germ. If \(e,u\)
are the first private and endpoint coordinates, the unreduced action
in (1) is
\[
 E(e,u)+g(e+a)+g(a+t)+g(t+z)-2g(a)-3g(t)-g(z)+2\pi iat,
 \quad
 E=-2J(1/2-u)-3J(u)-J(e-u)-\frac{\pi i}{2}(e-e^2)+\pi ieu.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
Its real Hessian is exactly that of
\[
 B(e,u)+[J(e+a)-J(a)]
       +V_J(a,t)+V_J(t,z)-J(t),\quad
 B=-2J(1/2-u)-3J(u)-J(e-u),\quad
 V_J(v,w)=J(v+w)-J(v)-J(w).                            \tag{7}
\]
All omitted terms have a purely imaginary Hessian. The endpoint,
attached-vertex and pair estimates (5), (9), (10) of
[the original open-word Hessian theorem](research_mixed_open_word_hessian.md)
apply on its \(E\times U\times Q^3\) product. The actual
reference is strictly inside this product. They give
\[
 -\Re\mathcal F''\succ\frac{6\pi}{49}I
 \quad\text{including the retained coordinate }z,
 \qquad -\Re\mathcal P''(z)>\frac{6\pi}{49}.           \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(u,e\) receive the endpoint floor, \(a\) receives
the attached-vertex and first pair costs, \(t\) receives two
pair costs and the positive own cost, and \(z\) receives the
last pair cost \(\pi/8>6\pi/49\). The second assertion is
the complex accretive Schur identity, not a Schur complement of
entrywise real parts. Equation (8) concerns local continuations
whose roots remain in this product; it does not prove that all
independent outgoing points in a rectangle admit such roots.

## 2. An explicit argument inequality on the whole four-leg box

**Lemma 1.** For every \(T,Z\in[6/25,9/25]\) and
\(\rho,r\in[1/2,1]\),
\[
 A_r(Z)-A_{\rho r}(T+Z)
       >\frac{2\pi}{5}\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}+\frac1{400}.  \tag{9}
\]

*Proof.* Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_q A_r(q)
  =\frac{2\pi r(\cos(2\pi q)-r)}
         {1-2r\cos(2\pi q)+r^2},\qquad
 \partial_r A_r(q)
  =\frac{\sin(2\pi q)}{1-2r\cos(2\pi q)+r^2}.        \tag{10}
\]
The summed real part \(T+Z\) lies in \([.48,.72]\),
where its cosine is negative. Hence the left side of (9) is
increasing in \(T\); it suffices to put \(T=6/25\).

First suppose \(6/25\le Z\le13/50\). Then
\(Z+6/25\le1/2\), and the second formula in (10) implies
\(A_{\rho r}(Z+6/25)\le A_r(Z+6/25)\).
The function \(A_r\) decreases throughout \([.24,.5]\),
because its cosine is at most \(\cos(12\pi/25)<1/2\le r\).
Moreover, wherever \(q\in[1/4,1/2]\), (10) gives
\[
                  -\partial_q A_r(q)
                    \ge\frac{2\pi r^2}{1+r^2}.         \tag{11}
\]
The interval \([13/50,12/25]\) is contained in
\([Z,Z+6/25]\). Integrating (11) on this subinterval,
and retaining the nonnegative rest, gives
\[
 A_r(Z)-A_{\rho r}(Z+6/25)
 \ge\frac{11\pi}{25}\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}
 \ge\frac{2\pi}{5}\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}+\frac{\pi}{125}.
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
This is stronger than (9).

Now suppose \(13/50\le Z\le9/25\). The summed real
part is at least one half, so (10) instead gives
\(A_{\rho r}(Z+6/25)\le A_{r/2}(Z+6/25)\).
For every \(0<q_0\le1\), the most negative value of
\(\partial_q A_{q_0}\) occurs at \(q=1/2\), and
has magnitude \(2\pi q_0/(1+q_0)\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{d}{dZ}[A_r(Z)-A_{r/2}(Z+6/25)]
 \le2\pi\left[\frac r{2+r}-\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}\right]
 \le0.                                                   \tag{13}
\]
The last inequality is exactly \(2r\ge1\). Thus it remains
to prove (9) at \(Z=9/25\). Define
\[
 M(r)=A_r(9/25)-A_{r/2}(3/5)
                         -\frac{2\pi}{5}\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}.
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
We will show that \(M\) decreases on \([1/2,1]\).
The elementary bounds
\[
 \cos(7\pi/25)>3/5,\quad \sin(7\pi/25)<4/5,
 \quad \cos(\pi/5)>4/5,\quad \sin(\pi/5)<3/5
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
give
\[
 \frac{d}{dr}[A_r(9/25)-A_{r/2}(3/5)]
 <\frac{4/5}{1+6r/5+r^2}
       +\frac{3/10}{1+4r/5+r^2/4}.                    \tag{16}
\]
For the first two bounds in (15), use
\(7\pi/25<22/25\) and
\(\cos(7\pi/25)>1-(22/25)^2/2=383/625>3/5\).
The sine bound follows by squaring. The other two follow from
\(\cos(\pi/5)=(1+\sqrt5)/4\) and \(\sqrt5>11/5\).

On \([1/2,3/5]\), the right side of (16) is at most
\[
                 \frac{16}{37}+\frac8{39}<\frac23.
\]
On this interval, \(r/(1+r^2)^2\ge8/25\): its derivative
has the sign of \(1-3r^2\), and its two endpoint values
are \(8/25\) and \(375/1156>8/25\).
The derivative of the last term subtracted in (14) is therefore
\[
 \frac{4\pi}{5}\frac r{(1+r^2)^2}
                   >\frac{96}{125}>\frac23.
\]
On \([3/5,1]\), the right side of (16) is at most
\[
                 \frac5{13}+\frac{30}{157}<\frac35,
\]
whereas \(r/(1+r^2)^2\ge1/4\), and the last derivative
is greater than \(3/5\). This proves \(M'(r)<0\)
throughout the whole interval.

At its final endpoint the original arguments are explicit:
\[
 M(1)=\arctan\frac{\sin(\pi/5)}{2+\cos(\pi/5)}
                                               -\frac{3\pi}{50}.
                                                               \tag{17}
\]
We have \(\sin(\pi/5)>7/12\): its square is
\((5-\sqrt5)/8>11/32>49/144\), using \(\sqrt5<9/4\).
The quotient in (17) consequently exceeds \(7/36\).
Using \(\arctan x>x-x^3/3\) and \(\pi<22/7\),
\[
 M(1)>
 \frac7{36}-\frac{(7/36)^3}{3}-\frac{33}{175}
 =\frac{26873}{139968}-\frac{33}{175}>\frac1{400}.
                                                               \tag{18}
\]
For the last comparison,
\(33/175+1/400=107/560\), and
\(26873\cdot560-139968\cdot107=72304>0\).
Equations (12)--(18) prove (9). \(\square\)

## 3. Consequence for a proved remainder ceiling

The complete complementary source has the bound
\[
 \Re\text{(remainder rate)}
 \le\mathfrak C(r)
 \le B(h):=C_+-\frac{\beta(r)}5,\qquad
 C_+=\frac{17539}{9600},\quad
 \beta(r)=\frac12\log\frac2{1+r^2},\quad r=e^{2\pi h},
                                                               \tag{19}
\]
with arbitrary prescribed finite-rate tolerance, by
[the following-four radial majorant](research_mixed_transformed_following_four_radial_majorant.md).
Its literal contour retains the original fixed incoming height;
the source estimate in (19) does not require that height to be the
new critical incoming height. The sharper function is
\[
 \mathfrak C(r)=C_+-\frac{23}{64000}
                     -\frac6{25}\beta(r)+\frac{\beta(r)^2}{20}.
                                                               \tag{20}
\]
For the simpler valid ceiling in (19),
\[
 B'(h)=\frac{2\pi}{5}\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}.
\]
Consequently (5) and Lemma 1 prove the strict local inequality
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \partial_h\,[\Re\mathcal P(Z+ih)-B(h)]>\frac1{400}.
 \quad}                                                        \tag{21}
\]
If a vertical continuation of the same original critical branch
stays in the domains (3) and (8), then integration gives
\[
 [\Re\mathcal P(Z+ih_2)-B(h_2)]
 -[\Re\mathcal P(Z+ih_1)-B(h_1)]
                 >\frac{h_2-h_1}{400}\qquad(h_2>h_1).
                                                               \tag{22}
\]
Also (8) makes this margin strictly concave in the horizontal
coordinate along any admitted horizontal segment. These statements
are genuine directions of the selected action, with its full
constant retained. They do not assert that an arbitrary rectangle
of outgoing parameters admits such a continuation.

In particular the radius improvement alone does not make downward
height transport favorable for this margin. This is a limitation
of that proposed monotonic transport argument, not a proof that
the actual following-reference margin is negative. Nor does (21)
assert the same derivative sign after replacing \(B\) by the
sharper function (20).

The still required signed comparison at the correlated references
of the exact next-attachment theorem is
\[
 \Re\mathcal P(t_2)>\max\{7/4,\mathfrak C(e^{2\pi\Im t_2})\},
                                                               \tag{23}
\]
with a uniform positive reserve on the actual reference family.
The point \(t_2\) is the output of the same original own-four
map whose first input is the attached cap leg; it is not a freely
chosen point of the containing \(O_4\) box. Equations (8), (21)
and (22) do not supply the missing absolute value in (23).
The plateau and taper face calibrations, the following two-sector
constant term, and the nonzero coefficient of that full next
source also remain separate. No new short-filling or knot-family
conclusion is drawn from this derivative theorem.
