# The first right horizontal segment on the lower left interval

Private research proof, 8 November 2026. For a next type-four block,
the complete first incoming horizontal segment from \(9/20+ih_a\)
to \(1/2+ih_a\) is paid on the closed shared interval
\(0\le\Re y\le11/50\). The proof retains every physical row,
both horizontal aliases, four weighted sides and both unweighted
joins of the first conditional sum, as well as the complete next sum.

This note does not estimate the subsequent incoming corner rectangle.
No mathematical numerical evaluation or frozen-source edit is used.

## 1. Original source and reference

Use the actual ordinary-next reference of
[the original right-tail theorem](research_mixed_next_unselected_incoming_right_tail.md),
with flags \((0,1,1)\). Both conditional vertices remain ordinary.
Write
\[
\begin{gathered}
(a_*,s_*,y_*,t_*,z_*)=(r_1,s_2^*,r_2,s_3^*,r_3),\\
A=\Re a_*,\qquad \alpha=\Re y_*,\qquad
.278\le A\le.391,\qquad .24<\alpha<.36,\\

\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The heights and all other geometric data retain the correlations
of that actual reference. In particular the external parameter is
the original \(s_4^*\), not a substituted private terminal coordinate.

Put \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), \(P_j=(r;r)_j\),
\(p_j(V)=(rV;r)_j\), and \(X=e^{-2\pi ia}\),
\(Y=e^{-2\pi iy}\), \(Z=e^{-2\pi iz}\). The exact kernels and
density are
\[
\begin{split}
C_{1,N}(X,Y)&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
       \frac{X^{-j}p_j(X)p_j(Y)}{P_j^3},\\
D_N(a;y,z,w)&=
 U_{0,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}(X,Y)d_N(Y)C_{1,N}(Y,Z).
\end{split}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(U_{0,N}\) is the complete original cap and \(d_N\) is its
original forced Fourier polynomial. There is no selected factor
\(Y^{-N}\).

Retain the original phases
\[
Q(s;a,y)=g(a+s)+g(y+s)-3g(s)-g(a)-g(y)+2\pi ias,
\qquad F=Q-\tfrac12g(y).
\tag{3}
\]
Write \(Q_1(t;y,z)\) for the same expression with
\((s,a,y)=(t,y,z)\), and \(J(v)=\Re g(v)\) on the real interval,
with the original normalization \(g(0)=0\).
Let \(p_L,p,A_*,B_3,\Lambda_{123}\) have exactly their original
definitions in Sections 1--3 of the cited right-tail theorem, and set
\[
B_U(x)=\Re T_0(a_*)-\Re p_L(x-A)-\kappa_0(x-A)^2,
\qquad \kappa_0=1/2500000.
\tag{4}
\]
The complete all-real cap has this supporting rate for
\(.22\le x\le.5\), at its original height. The attached equation
and the old-face supporting identity are
\[
T_0'(a_*)+p_L=0,\qquad
A_*+p(q_0-\alpha)+B_3(q_0,u)\le\Lambda_{123}(z).
\tag{5}
\]
All additive constants and Fourier allocations remain unchanged.

**Theorem.** Uniformly on (1), for every real ordinary color
\(w\in[-2,2]\), and for all sufficiently large integer levels
of both parities,
\[
\boxed{\quad
|D_N(a;y,z,w)|\,|d_N(Z)|^{1/2}
\le C N^B e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11}\}}.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently the same bound, with a possibly different polynomial,
holds for
\[
\left|N\int_{9/20+ih_a}^{1/2+ih_a}D_N(a;y,z,w)\,da\right|
 |d_N(Z)|^{1/2},
\tag{7}
\]
with increasing orientation and the original \(+N\) measure.
No local complex-jet conclusion is needed or asserted here.

## 3. The main profiles and their old-face calibration

At \(q=q_0\), the original right-tail theorem, equations (9)--(10),
proves for every \(1/8\le s_R\le1/2\) at height \(h_s\)
\[
B_U(x)+\Re F(s_R+ih_s;a,q_0+ih_y)
\le A_*+p(q_0-\alpha)-\kappa_0(x-A)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
In the present interval,
\[
\kappa_0(x-A)^2
\ge\frac{3481}{2500000000000}>10^{-9}.
\tag{14}
\]
This is a pointwise main-profile supporting bound. It is not
inferred from a bound on a coherent integral.

At the same face every next main, real low \(1/8\), real half
\(1/2\), and last profile lies below its original allocated plane
\(B_3(q_0,u)\). These are the profile-by-profile conclusions in
[ordinary three-row assembly](research_mixed_three_row_source_assembly.md),
Section 2. For the last profile only its strict sign is used;
no quantitative last-certificate margin is assigned.

The first main extends down to \(c\). Put
\(r_s=e^{2\pi h_s}\in(1/2,1)\), \(R=e^{2\pi h_y}\in(1/2,1)\),
and \(T=e^{-2\pi h_a}\ge1\). Its exact horizontal force is
\[
e^{\Re Q_s(v+ih_s;a,y)}
=\frac{|T-r_s e^{-2\pi i(x+v)}|\,
             |1-Rr_s e^{-2\pi i(q+v)}|}
            {|1-r_s e^{-2\pi iv}|^3}.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(c\le v\le1/8\), \(x+v\in[1/2,5/8]\), so its first
numerator exceeds one. Since \(q+v<1/2\), angular monotonicity gives
\[
|1-Rr_s e^{-2\pi i(q+v)}|
\ge \cos(\pi v)\,|1-r_s e^{-2\pi iv}|.
\tag{16}
\]
To verify the radial part of (16), put \(\theta=2\pi v\).
If \(r_s\le\cos\theta\), shrinking its radius cannot lower
the modulus. Otherwise its smallest possible value is
\(\sin\theta\), while the denominator is at most
\(2\sin(\theta/2)\). Their ratio is at least
\(\cos(\theta/2)\). This proves (16) in both cases.
The own square in (15) is less than \(3/5\) on the present
radius and index intervals, and \(\cos(\pi v)>9/10\).
Thus (15) exceeds one, and the added main strip increases to
\(1/8\).

The [joint-profile theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_joint_main_profiles.md),
Section 3, then raises the first index to \(1/6\) if necessary.
That proof permits first incoming real parts through \(1/2\).
Its upper clipping in Section 6.1 also remains valid here:
for \(5/12\le v\le1/2\), one now has
\(x+v\in[13/15,1]\), improving its incoming cosine lower bound.
The negative height \(h_a+h_s\) keeps the limiting argument at
real part one regular on this main line. Consequently every first
main is bounded by a first main with
\[
1/6\le s_R\le5/12.
\tag{17}
\]
The next main can similarly be clipped to
\(1/6\le t_R\le9/20\), by the joint-profile theorem and
[the first-last comparison](research_mixed_next_lower_left_first_last_four_reduction.md),
Section 2. These changes are at the same \(q\), with every other
factor retained.

## 4. Joint transport of all next alternatives

For the clipped main indices define
\[
\mathcal A(s,t)=B_U(x)+
 \Re\{Q(s;a,y)+Q_1(t;y,z)-g(y)-\tfrac12g(z)\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The exact derivative is
\[
\exp(\partial_q\mathcal A)=
\frac{|1-Rr_s e^{-2\pi i(q+s_R)}|\,
      |e^{-2\pi h_t}-R e^{-2\pi i(q+t_R)}|}
     {|1-R e^{-2\pi iq}|^3}.
\tag{19}
\]
At a real next cut replace \(h_t\) by zero and take
\(t_R=1/8\) or \(1/2\). The chirp is included in the second,
folded numerator. In particular (19) is independent of the first
incoming real part \(x\).

For \(0\le q\le1/5\), Section 4 of the joint-profile theorem,
including its real-face variant, proves that (19) exceeds one.
For \(1/5\le q\le11/50\), the first shifted cosine is at most
\(-13/20\). The next shifted cosine is at most
\(-\cos(2\pi q)<-9/50\), both on the clipped next main and at
either real cut: all these indices lie in \([1/8,1/2]\).
Since \(e^{-2\pi h_t}\ge1\), sufficient squared-modulus bounds are
\[
D=1+R^2-\tfrac9{25}R,\qquad
U=1+\tfrac14R^2+\tfrac{13}{20}R,\qquad
V=1+R^2+\tfrac9{25}R.
\tag{20}
\]
Here the own square is at most \(D\), and the numerator squares
are at least \(U,V\). Equations (25)--(26) of that theorem give
\(U>(23/20)D\), \(V>(20/23)D^2\) on \(1/2\le R\le1\).
Hence \(UV>D^3\), proving positive joint transport to \(q_0\).
Combining (13)--(14) with the next profiles' supporting inequalities
pays every first main paired with a next main or either next real cut,
with deficit greater than \(10^{-9}\).

For a next last profile replace \(Q_1(t;y,z)\) in (18) by
\(\delta_y=-2\pi h_y\). When \(R\le7/10\), Section 2 of
[the next-four last-row theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_next_four_last_row.md)
proves the positive derivative
\[
\log|1-Rr_s e^{-2\pi i(q+s_R)}|
                  -2\log|1-R e^{-2\pi iq}|>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Its proof depends on the clipped first index, not on \(x\).
Equation (13) and the next last-row sign calibrate it at \(q_0\),
again retaining (14). When \(R\ge7/10\), Section 3 of that source
proves the same-point inequality
\[
E_1(1/8;y,z)>\delta_y+1/1100.
\tag{22}
\]
The already paid next-low comparison therefore pays this case too.
Thus the first main is paid with every controlling next profile
at every actual shared depth.

The first real half profile is bounded by the first main-height
half profile, at the same \(q\). Indeed, on
\(s=1/2+i\sigma\), \(h_s\le\sigma\le0\), the original arguments give
\[
\Im Q_s\ge-\pi x-\pi q+2\pi x=\pi(x-q)\ge0.

\]
This is Section 6.4 of the joint-profile theorem; its elementary

## 5. The entire first upper physical block

The exact reflected rate of
[the own-four upper-row theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_own_four_upper_rows.md)
remains
\[
\begin{split}
\Phi(v)&=E_1(1-v;a,y)-E_1(1;a,y)\\
&=3J(v)-\int_0^v\log|T-e^{-2\pi i(x-s)}|\,ds
        -\int_0^v\log|1-R e^{-2\pi i(q-s)}|\,ds .
\end{split}
\tag{24}
\]
Here \(E_1(1;a,y)=\delta_a\); the original chirp has not been
removed. For \(0\le v\le11/50\), the first arc
\([x-v,x]\) is contained in \([0,1/2]\) and its integral is at
least \(J(v)\). The second arc is at least \(2J(v/2)\).
Therefore
\[
\Phi(v)\le2\{J(v)-J(v/2)\}\le0,
\tag{25}
\]
by that theorem's equation (4), since \(11/50<2/9\).

For \(11/50\le v\le1/2\), the first integral between \(v\) and
\(1/2\) has \(|x-s|\le7/25\). With \(T<8/5\), its log modulus
is less than \(3/4\); the other log modulus is less than \(7/10\).
Hence
\[
\Phi(v)-\Phi(1/2)\le\tfrac{29}{20}(1/2-v)+3J(v).
\tag{26}
\]
This upper envelope is convex. It vanishes at \(1/2\), and at
\(11/50\), the original inequality \(-J(11/50)>989/6600\) gives
\[
\tfrac{203}{500}-\tfrac{989}{2200}
                  =-\tfrac{479}{11000}<0.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus every upper real rate is at most its half or last rate.
The endpoint-uniform factorial bound and zero-safe numerator bounds
give the complete positive finite version, including every
\(\lceil N/2\rceil\le j<N\), at both parities, with any prescribed
rate tolerance. The half alternative is paid by Section 4 using
the cap plane; the last alternative is paid by (12) using the
cap disk bound. Both uses concern the same complete cap.

## 6. All first weighted currents

For \(c\le v\le1/2\), write
\[
J_1(v,\sigma)=\Im Q_s(v+i\sigma;a,y).
\]
On the original strip branches, the incoming shifted real part
\(x+v\) lies in \([1/2,1]\), the outgoing one in \([c,.72]\),
and the own argument is nonnegative. The original argument
inequalities used in the right-tail theorem give
\[
\begin{split}
J_1/\pi&\ge-2+4v+\min(0,1-2q-2v)\ge-9/5,\\
J_1/\pi&\le2x+\max(0,1-2q-2v)\le19/10 .
\end{split}
\tag{28}
\]
For the first line, if \(q+v\le1/2\), use \(v\ge1/20\);
otherwise the expression is \(-1+2v-2q\ge-67/50>-9/5\).
These weak bounds suffice; in the regular interior the original
finite-radius inequalities are strict.

Choose the first lower and upper alias heights \(-1/5\) and \(4\).
Relative to the same-index first main at height \(h_s\), (28) gives
\[
\begin{split}
\Re Q(v-i/5)-2\pi/5
 &\le\Re Q(v+ih_s)-\pi/50,\\
\Re Q(v+4i)-8\pi
 &\le\Re Q(v+ih_s)-1541\pi/2500 .
\end{split}
\tag{29}
\]
Indeed \(h_s+1/5\le1/5\) and \(4-h_s<4+51/500\).
Every upper weighted vertical rate
\(\Re Q(v+i\sigma)-2\pi\sigma\) decreases with \(\sigma\ge0\);
every lower weighted rate decreases as the downward height increases.
Their respective derivative upper bounds are \(-\pi/5\) and
\(-\pi/10\). Thus all four weighted half-sides are bounded by
their real-cut profiles. All outside factors, including the whole
next kernel, are held fixed during these index comparisons.

At \(x=v=1/2\), the incoming numerator can meet its first wall.

No nonzero factorial symbol is asserted at the wall, and no

## 7. The two unweighted joins

At the new lower cut \(c=1/20\), the incoming shifted cotangent
has nonpositive real part because \(x+c\in[1/2,11/20]\).
We show on \(h_s\le\sigma\le0\) that any positive outgoing
shifted cotangent is less than \(4/3\) times the own one.
Put
\[
C=\cosh(2\pi\sigma)<5/4,\quad c_0=\cos(2\pi c)>19/20,\quad
s_0=\sin(2\pi c)>3/10,\qquad
K(v,\sigma)=\frac{\sin(2\pi v)}{C-\cos(2\pi v)}.
\tag{30}
\]
The height bound is the original \(\cosh(.204\pi)<5/4\).
If \(Cc_0\le1\), \(K(v,\sigma)\) decreases for \(v\ge c\)
through \(1/2\), so the outgoing term at \(c+q\) is at most
the own term. Adding \(h_y\le0\) only decreases a positive
cotangent on this negative-height join.

If \(Cc_0>1\), the maximum of the positive cotangent over its
real coordinate is \(1/\sqrt{C^2-1}\). Relative to the own term
its ratio is
\[
\frac{C-c_0}{\sqrt{C^2-1}\,s_0}.
\tag{31}
\]
The derivative in \(C\) has the sign of \(Cc_0-1\), so (31)
increases on the present case. At \(C=5/4\) it is less than
\[
\frac{5/4-19/20}{(3/4)(3/10)}=\frac43 .
\tag{32}
\]
Thus in both cases
\[
3\Re\cot\pi(c+i\sigma)
-\Re\cot\pi(a+c+i\sigma)
-\Re\cot\pi(y+c+i\sigma)>0.
\tag{33}
\]
The real index Hessian is negative, so the real phase is convex
in the vertical index direction. This join is bounded by the
maximum of its already paid real and main endpoint profiles.

At the half cut the own real cotangent is zero and both shifted
ones are nonpositive. Weak height convexity suffices, including
the possible equality at \(x=1/2,q=0\). On this join both shifted
heights are negative, so the incoming boundary argument is regular.
Its endpoint profiles are paid in Sections 3--4.

## 8. Literal finite assembly

Use the original canonical row
\[
T_N(s;a,y)=e^{2\pi iNas}
 \Pi_N(a+s)\Pi_N(y+s)E_N(a)E_N(y)E_N(s)^3,
\tag{34}
\]
which equals the first summand in (2) at \(s=j/N\).
With (9), the exact first index groups are
\[
0\le j<L_N,\qquad L_N\le j<M_N,\qquad M_N\le j<N,
\tag{35}
\]
and the middle half-grid boundaries are
\((L_N-1/2)/N\), \((M_N-1/2)/N\).
The original cotangent residue formula contains \(+N\) times
the real middle integral, both horizontal Fourier remainders
and all four weighted half-sides. Deforming only the constant-mode
integral to height \(h_s\) adds its two oriented unweighted joins.
There are no other residues or deleted Fourier frequencies.

For \(q>0\) all denominator arguments are in their original
regular strips. At \(q=0\), the original
[inward-seam factorial theorem](research_factorial_symbol_across_inward_seam.md)
continues \(E_N(y)\) with its unchanged normalization. The shifted
outgoing numerator \(y+s\) remains strictly inside its strip.
The possible incoming numerator wall uses the zero-safe estimate
in Section 6. The original factorial estimates and integrated-rate
continuity give uniform polynomial costs and arbitrarily small
rate tolerances under the \(O(1/N)\) half-grid changes, including
numerator zeros. The even half row, first odd upper row and actual
last index all remain in their exact groups (35).

The [complete next-current theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_complete_currents.md),
its low physical bounds and its own-four upper-row bounds reduce
the entire next sum to the profiles used in Section 4, with every
next current retained. It can be applied with any fixed first
profile attached. Conversely the first comparisons in Sections
6--7 keep the whole next factor fixed. Consequently every
main/half alternative is paid by (14) and Section 4, and every
first low/last alternative is paid by (8), (11)--(12).
This uses absolute finite upper bounds; no positivity or
noncancellation of either coherent kernel is assumed.

Finally
\[
|d_N(Y)|\le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}e^{N\{-\Re g(y)+\tau\}},\qquad
|d_N(Z)|^{1/2}\le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}e^{N\{-\Re g(z)/2+\tau\}},
\tag{36}
\]
on the full closed interval (1), by
[low physical blocks, Section 3](research_mixed_next_lower_left_low_physical_blocks.md).
The first bound retains the complete forced polynomial at the seam;
it is distinct from the factorial continuation in (34).
These are precisely the allocations in (18), with no missing
halfweight or extra selected carrier.

There are finitely many groups. Share their arbitrary finite
tolerances inside the reserve (14) and the larger raw-prefix margins.
The additional sampling factors, finite sums and integrations cost
only powers of \(N\). A common finite gap \(10^{-11}\) therefore
proves (6), and the fixed-length original integration proves (7).
All-real color bounds come from the complete cap estimates.

The theorem removes only this first right horizontal segment for
the next own-four source. It does not pay the following incoming
corner, the first incoming initial/taper pieces, a next own-three
upper block, or the entire paired-left remainder.
