# The actual attachment excess on two complete parameter disks

Private phase/source reduction, 9 November 2026. The original
half-input conditional action is holomorphic on the entire strip
\(0<\Re z<1/2\). This licenses its composition with the actual
cap-initialized \(343\) output on both complete external parameter
disks, without extending any finite outgoing estimate to that larger
phase domain. The correlated attachment excess is harmonic and has
no interior critical point. Its uniform upper bound is therefore an
exact two-circle boundary problem.

No sign for that boundary problem is proved here. In particular no
adequate coherent-action budget, new finite-source packet, or
short-partial-filling theorem is asserted. The original task attachment
was reread. No mathematical numerical evaluation or frozen-source edit
is used.

## 1. The original half-input action on a full strip

Let
\[
\mathcal H=\{z\in\mathbb C:0<\Re z<1/2\},\qquad
g(v)=\frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-2\pi iv})-\pi^2/6}{2\pi i}
                                                        \tag{1}
\]
with the original holomorphic primitive on \(0<\Re v<1\).
Retain the literal own-three conditional phase
\[
Q_0(s;a,z)=g(a+s)+g(z+s)-2g(s)-g(a)-g(z)+2\pi ias .
                                                        \tag{2}
\]
No primitive constant or Fourier carrier is changed.

**Lemma 1.** There is one holomorphic root \(s_{\rm h}(z)\) on
\(\mathcal H\), characterized by
\[
0<\Re s_{\rm h}<1/2,\qquad
\Re\cot(\pi s_{\rm h})>0,\qquad
Q_{0,s}(s_{\rm h};1/2,z)=0.                              \tag{3}
\]
Its conditional action
\[
\mathcal S_{\rm h}(z)=Q_0(s_{\rm h}(z);1/2,z)             \tag{4}
\]
is holomorphic there. On the original actual output rectangle it is
the half-input action of the
[selected outgoing sheet](research_mixed_next_outer_selected_outgoing_sheet.md)
and hence the action normalized by the
[terminal normalization theorem](research_mixed_next_outgoing_action_terminal_normalization.md).
There is no restriction on \(\Im z\) in this phase statement.

*Proof.* Put \(Q=\cot(\pi z)=q+i\beta\). On \(\mathcal H\),
\[
\Re Q=
\frac{\sin(2\pi\Re z)}
     {\cosh(2\pi\Im z)-\cos(2\pi\Re z)}>0.
                                                        \tag{5}
\]
The original cotangent equation at \(a=1/2\) is
\[
F(S,Q)=S^2+QS-1+iQ=0.                                   \tag{6}
\]
For \(S=iv\), \(v\in\mathbb R\),
\[
F(iv,Q)=-v^2-1-\beta(v+1)+iq(v+1).
                                                        \tag{7}
\]
When \(q>0\), a zero would require \(v=-1\), where the real
part is \(-2\). Thus no root is imaginary. At \(Q=0\) the
roots are \(1,-1\). Along the compact path \(tQ\),
\(0\le t\le1\), there is no imaginary root, including at \(t=0\);
the monic quadratic has bounded coefficients, so no root escapes to
infinity. It follows that there is exactly one root in each open
half-plane, counted with multiplicity. Each is simple.
The unique right root \(S_+(Q)\) is globally holomorphic on
\(\Re Q>0\), since its local implicit branches agree by uniqueness.

Neither \(i\) nor \(-i\) is a root:
\[
F(-i,Q)=-2,\qquad F(i,Q)=-2+2iQ\ne0
                                      \quad(\Re Q>0).
                                                        \tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
T=\frac{S_++i}{S_+-i},\qquad
\Im T=\frac{2\Re S_+}{|S_+-i|^2}>0,\qquad
s_{\rm h}=\frac{\Log T}{2\pi i}                         \tag{9}
\]
are well defined and holomorphic, using the principal logarithm
on the upper half-plane. In particular \(0<\Re s_{\rm h}<1/2\).
There is no assertion that \(\Im S_+\) has one sign on this
larger domain.

All of \(s_{\rm h},\,1/2+s_{\rm h},\,z,\,z+s_{\rm h}\)
remain in the original strip \(0<\Re v<1\).
Equation (6) is the original exponentiated index equation, so
\(Q_{0,s}(s_{\rm h};1/2,z)\) is a holomorphic function with
values in \(2\pi i\mathbb Z\) on the connected strip
\(\mathcal H\). The adopted selected-sheet theorem fixes its
value to zero, for example at \(z=1/3\), where its selected
cotangent root is precisely the unique right root above.
The lift is therefore zero throughout \(\mathcal H\).
This proves (3) and holomorphy of (4). Uniqueness of the same
right root identifies the original branch on the actual rectangle.
\(\square\)

Stationary differentiation gives the exact formula
\[
\mathcal S_{\rm h}'(z)
       =g'(z+s_{\rm h}(z))-g'(z).                       \tag{10}
\]
In particular (4) is a phase continuation alone. A finite
conditional kernel or its complete outgoing anchor is not estimated
on all of \(\mathcal H\).

## 2. The correlated ordinary \(343\) family

Use the two original closed reduced disks
\[
\begin{split}
P_3&=\{|b-(7/20-i/100)|\le83/1000\},\\
P_4&=\{|b-(3/10-13i/250)|\le3/50\}.
\end{split}                                             \tag{11}
\]
For the cap-initialized word \(343\), retain the original
seven-variable critical point in the order
\[
(u_L,s_1,t_1,s_2,y,s,z),\qquad y=t_2,\quad s=s_3,\quad z=t_3,
                                                        \tag{12}
\]
with final incidence \(G(z,b)\). The
[cap-phase entrance theorem, Lemma 1](research_mixed_alternating_cap_phase_entrance.md)
and the
[global pre-Gram curve theorem](research_mixed_arbitrary_prefix_pregram_curve.md)
give a unique original-log branch on a neighborhood of each disk
in (11). Its whole typed product has
\[
y(b),s(b),z(b)\in
Q=[23/100,9/20]+i[-3/25,3/25].                         \tag{13}
\]
The strict original image inclusions also give \(z(b)\in O_3\).
For the present purpose (13) already proves the needed
\[
                         z(b)\in\mathcal H.             \tag{14}
\]
Thus no later \(m\ge5\) height-window refinement is needed to
compose (4) on the complete independent disks (11).
The original pre-Gram curve theorem also proves
\[
                              z'(b)\ne0.               \tag{15}
\]
All statements include neighborhoods of the closed disks, by the
strict image margins and compactness.

Let \(H_{343}(b)\) be the original half-chain critical action,
including its complete left cap and final incidence. Define the
holomorphic conditional attachment action on this branch by
\[
\Pi(b)=H_{343}(b)-G(z(b),b)-g(z(b)),\qquad
G(v,b)=g(v+b)-2g(v)-2g(b)+2\pi ivb .                    \tag{16}
\]
The pre-Gram theorem, equation (17), removes exactly the final
incidence. The extra \(-g(z)\) in (16) is exactly the own-three
conditional normalization in
[the next local source, equations (9),(11),(14)](research_mixed_three_four_next_local_source.md).
Consequently
\[
\Pi(b)=\mathcal P_0^+(-2,z(b)),\qquad
\Pi'(b)=\{g'(z(b)+s(b))-g'(z(b))\}z'(b).                \tag{17}
\]
The first equality is the original locally defined action,
continued on the common following sheet; (16) specifies its
absolute value globally, with no additive renormalization.

Let \(\mathfrak M_3\) be the original real complete-inner-three
action. Define
\[
\Delta(b)=\mathcal S_{\rm h}(z(b))-\Pi(b),\qquad
\mathfrak E(b)=\mathfrak M_3+\Re\Delta(b).               \tag{18}
\]
For every actual complete word in the original powers three and
four beginning \(343\), with length \(m\ge5\), its external
leg \(b=s_4\) belongs to the corresponding disk (11).
Uniqueness identifies its first three vertices with (12).
At this actual reference the original local geometric rate is
\(\Lambda=\Re\Pi(b)\). The
[absolute outgoing anchor](research_mixed_next_outgoing_absolute_anchor.md)
and terminal normalization then give
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathfrak E(b)=
\mathfrak M_3+\mathcal E(1/2,Z_*)-\Lambda
=\mathfrak M_3+\Re\mathcal S(1/2,z_*)-\Lambda,
\qquad Z_*=e^{-2\pi iz(b)} .
\quad}                                                   \tag{19}
\]
This equality of the actual attachment excess uses the finite
anchor only at those eligible actual references. Neither a
finite anchor nor an actual complete word is assigned to an
arbitrary independent point of (11).

## 3. Exact two-circle reduction and nonstationarity

**Theorem 2.** The function \(\mathfrak E\) is harmonic on a
neighborhood of each closed disk \(P_\tau\), \(\tau=3,4\).
Its gradient is nowhere zero there after restricting to the
original typed neighborhood. In particular
\[
\boxed{\quad
\max_{b\in P_\tau}\mathfrak E(b)
       =\max_{b\in\partial P_\tau}\mathfrak E(b),
                       \qquad \tau=3,4 .
\quad}                                                   \tag{20}
\]
The critical derivative retaining the full correlation is
\[
\Delta'(b)=
\{g'(z(b)+s_{\rm h}(z(b)))-g'(z(b)+s(b))\}\,z'(b)\ne0.
                                                        \tag{21}
\]

*Proof.* Holomorphy follows from Lemma 1 and (14)--(18).
Equations (10),(17) give the displayed derivative.
Suppose its bracket vanished. Both arguments of \(g'\) lie
in \(0<\Re v<1\). Exponentiating
\(g'(v)=\log(1-e^{-2\pi iv})\) gives
\[
e^{-2\pi i(z+s_{\rm h})}=e^{-2\pi i(z+s)}.
\]
Their real-part difference has modulus less than one.
Their difference is an integer, hence zero, so \(s_{\rm h}=s\).

The actual root \(s\) also satisfies
\[
g'(s+y)+g'(s+z)-2g'(s)+2\pi iy=0,                       \tag{22}
\]
by the original own-three index equation in the half-chain.
Subtracting (22) from the half-input equation (3) gives
\[
g'(s+1/2)-g'(s+y)+2\pi i(1/2-y)=0.
                                                        \tag{23}
\]
Put \(X=e^{-2\pi is}\), \(Y=e^{-2\pi iy}\).
All factors below are nonzero by their original regular strips.
Exponentiating (23) yields
\[
                       -Y\,\frac{1+X}{1-YX}=1,
              \qquad\hbox{and therefore}\qquad Y=-1.    \tag{24}
\]
This forces \(y=1/2\) modulo an integer, contradicting
\(23/100\le\Re y\le9/20\) in (13).
The bracket in (21) is nonzero. Equation (15) completes
the nonvanishing proof. For the real part of a holomorphic
function, a zero real gradient is equivalent to a zero complex
derivative. The harmonic maximum principle now proves (20).
\(\square\)

For an entirely explicit boundary parametrization, let
\[
b_\tau(\vartheta)=b_\tau^0+r_\tau e^{i\vartheta},
\quad
(b_3^0,r_3)=(7/20-i/100,83/1000),\quad
(b_4^0,r_4)=(3/10-13i/250,3/50).
\]
Then the original correlated boundary derivative is
\[
\frac{d}{d\vartheta}\mathfrak E(b_\tau(\vartheta))
=\Re\{i r_\tau e^{i\vartheta}\Delta'(b_\tau(\vartheta))\}.
                                                        \tag{25}
\]
Equations (18),(21),(25) retain all outputs of one and the same
seven-variable branch; they are not independent containing-box
substitutions.

For any proposed common positive coherent gain \(\delta\), the
whole-disk upper bound \(\mathfrak E<\delta\) is equivalent to
the two boundary inequalities
\[
\max_{\vartheta\in[0,2\pi]}
       \mathfrak E(b_\tau(\vartheta))<\delta,
                           \qquad \tau=3,4.             \tag{26}
\]
This whole-disk comparison is sufficient for all eligible actual
references; no necessity on the smaller realized family is claimed.
The equality of the maxima in (20) is proved; the inequalities in
(26) are not. The nonzero derivative in (21) supplies no numerical
margin or sign for them. This is a correlated phase reduction for
the original complete \(343\) family, not a proof of the required
coherent observation bound or of stability under fixing arbitrary
short filling coefficients.
