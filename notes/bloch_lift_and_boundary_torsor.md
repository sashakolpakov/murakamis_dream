# The boundary algebra needs its Bloch-period lift

Research note, 25 October 2026. This identifies a structural datum not
retained by the boundary algebra alone. The construction is classical;
the point here is to state exactly what the proposed quantum period must
lift. No identification with algebraic K_3 is used as a proof premise.

## 1. The five-term relation is an algebraic identity

Let P(F) be the rational vector space generated by [x], x in F minus
{0,1}, with the five-term relations

    [x]+[y]-[xy]
       -[x(1-y)/(1-xy)]-[y(1-x)/(1-xy)]=0               (1.1)

whenever all terms are defined. Work in F^* tensor Q and set

    delta[x]=x wedge (1-x).

Use the usual oriented-shape identifications [1/x]=-[x] and
[1/(1-x)]=[x] as well. Delta respects them rationally by expansion;
the discarded signs are torsion. They record vertex reversal and
cyclic choice of an opposite edge pair.

The map respects (1.1). This can be proved without any dilogarithm:
write the formal multiplicative coordinates as additive symbols
X=log x,Y=log y,A=log(1-x),B=log(1-y),C=log(1-xy).
The five terms of delta are

    X wedge A+Y wedge B-(X+Y) wedge C
      -(X+B-C) wedge (A-C)-(Y+A-C) wedge (B-C)=0.        (1.2)

Expansion proves the equality. The rational Bloch group needed here is
B(F)=ker delta. These definitions discard torsion, so they do not
silently settle the separate integral or flattening questions.

## 2. A uniform boundary formula

Let z_j be the nondegenerate literature shape coordinates of a
one-cusped gluing chart, and z_j''=1-z_j^(-1). Use the independently
audited edge and marked peripheral matrices. In multiplicative
coordinates tensor Q, the edge equations are homogeneous because
their fixed signs disappear. The peripheral rows have pairing -2.

Then the tetrahedral symbol beta=sum_j[z_j] satisfies

    delta beta=-(1/2) m wedge l,                        (2.1)

where m,l are the full peripheral monomials in those same conventions.
If using independently oriented shape coordinates, insert their
orientation signs consistently in both the symplectic form and beta.

Here is the linear-algebra proof, avoiding individual triangulations.
On the formal shape space use Omega=sum_j du_j wedge dv_j, with
u_j=z_j, v_j=z_j'' in the additive notation of F^* tensor Q.
The edge Hamiltonian vectors form an isotropic space E of dimension
r-1. The homogeneous edge solution space is E^perp; its symplectic
quotient is two-dimensional. The Hamiltonian vectors for m,l have
pairing -2. On this quotient,

    Omega_red=-(1/2) dm wedge dl.

For example its value on the m and l Hamiltonian vectors is -2,
whereas (dm wedge dl) on them is 4. This determines the form; its
pullback proves the same identity on E^perp. Alternating bilinearity
therefore gives sum_j u_j wedge v_j=-(1/2)m wedge l for coordinates
in any Q-vector space, including F^* tensor Q.

Finally

    z wedge z''=z wedge(1-z)

in this rational group: the missing sign -1 is torsion and
z wedge z^(-1)=0. This proves (2.1). No angle limit or inverse face
matrix appears. At completeness m=l=1, so beta lies in B(F).

## 3. Volume is already a regulator evaluation

For a complex embedding define

    D(z)=Im Li_2(z)+log|z| Arg(1-z),

with its single-valued continuation. Its differential is

    dD(z)=log|z| dArg(1-z)-log|1-z| dArg z.              (3.1)

The bilinear map

    f wedge g -> log|f| dArg g-log|g| dArg f

sends (1.2) to the differential of the corresponding five-term sum
of D values. That differential is zero. The nondegenerate complex
parameter domain is the complement of finitely many algebraic
divisors in C^2, hence connected. On 0<x,y<1 the sum is zero.
Thus D obeys (1.1) everywhere by this constant-function argument.

Consequently D(beta) is independent of five-term re-presentations.
For a complete positive geometric triangulation the oriented ideal
tetrahedron volume is D(z_j), so

    Vol(M)=D(beta).                                    (3.2)

This is an exact period evaluation, not a definition via a quantum
asymptotic. The logarithmic/Heisenberg refinement also records the
complex period and its branch constants. Those constants must remain
when comparing exact complex quantum states.

For a finite idealized fundamental chain, changing its presentation
by the boundary of five decorated vertices produces precisely a
five-term relation. The symbol map is therefore the natural way to
pass from tetrahedral coordinates to the fundamental-class period.
For an explicit check, normalize those vertices to infinity,0,1,a,b
and use cross-ratio r(infinity,0,1,z)=z. Their alternating boundary is

    [a]-[b]+[b/a]-[(1-b)/(1-a)]
                       +[a(1-b)/(b(1-a))].

Putting a=x,b=xy and applying the two oriented-shape identifications
turns this into (1.1). Thus this chain relation is coordinate-independent
and needs no knot example.
We do not infer a global quantum-state identity merely from this
classical chain relation.

## 4. Why the unpointed boundary module is too small

Equation (2.1) specifies the boundary symbol. Its Steinberg realization
splits the corresponding cyclic boundary algebra on the gluing field.
The finite edge reduction constructs that splitting module directly.
This is the boundary layer of the theory.

The choice beta is a *lift* of that boundary symbol. Two lifts with
the same boundary can differ by an element of B(F), and their
regulator values need not agree. In particular the boundary m=l=1
does not force the volume to vanish. An unpointed splitting of the
boundary algebra has forgotten the datum that can carry this period.

The candidate intrinsic object must therefore retain

    (marked boundary Heisenberg algebra,
     its pointed splitting module,
     a Bloch/Heisenberg period lift beta).

The phrase K_2 versus K_3 describes this degree distinction: the
Steinberg symbols belong to the boundary-symbol problem, whereas the
Bloch/regulator lift carries the next period datum. No theorem
identifying a specific integral Bloch group with K_3 is required
for the elementary argument above.

## 5. The quantum comparison target

The pointed Steinberg module has an exact algebraic factorization;
its multiplicity spaces have a basis-free categorical pentagon.
The Heisenberg cover fixes their finite normalization data, and the
rational Faddeev identity supplies the continuous dilogarithm period.

The missing theorem is a compatible global regulator construction
taking the idealized fundamental class to this *pointed period*
object, with the literature integration cycle and normalization.
It must identify the quantum five-term coherence with the classical
period lift, and compare its normalized size across finite levels
with (3.2). This is one uniform target. More boundary-matrix examples
cannot replace it, and an appeal to Hilbert 90 for the unpointed
module alone does not establish it.
