# The horizontal terminal plane loses too much at four canonical suffixes

Research result, 6 November 2026. The prescribed four-case diagnostic
certifies a positive value greater than \(1/100\) for the joint
**horizontal-envelope majorant**, at a legal target for every selected
height \(0\le H\le.014\). This excludes that proposed shortcut
for these canonical references. It does not contradict a negative
comparison for the complete original terminal source.

## 1. The exact lower comparison

The [fixed protocol and proof](research_mixed_terminal_horizontal_envelope_diagnostic.md)
define a canonical endpoint-plus-ordinary suffix with terminal flag
\(\epsilon\), preceding ordinary flag one, and incoming flag
\(\beta\). The fixed external inputs are the original disk centers
\[
 b_0=.35-.01i,\qquad b_1=.30-.052i.
 \tag{1}
\]
All four zeros \((e_*,\xi_*,t_*,a_*,s_*)\) are certified using
the original five gradient equations, prior canonical existence,
and the global accretivity constant \(6\pi/49\). The localized
coordinate squares have radius \(10^{-30}\). They lie in the
original convex product and the requisite smaller typed domains.
For terminal own type four, the oriented incoming floor and the
adjacent inverse feasibility are also retained. These are genuine
canonical references; finite-word realization is not asserted.

Write \(a_*=A+ic\) and
\[
 p_T=(P_\epsilon-g/2)'(a_*)
     =g'(a_*+t_*)-\tfrac32g'(a_*)+2\pi it_*.
 \tag{2}
\]
The existing selected-terminal theorem bounds the complete terminal
factor with its halfweight by its original horizontal plane. After
joining the preceding ordinary factor and the other halfweight,
let \(D_{0,1}\) denote the main factorial profile of that particular
majorant, with the original total reference action and incoming
complex tangent subtracted. At the legal target
\((b,s,a)=(b_*,s_*,A+iH)\), the protocol proves
\[
 D_{0,1}(b_*,s_*,A+iH)\ge L(H)
          :=(H-c)\Im p_T-\pi H.
 \tag{3}
\]
This follows from vertical convexity of
\(\Re\{Q_1(s_*;b_*,a)-g(a)/2\}\) and exact shared-leg
stationarity. In particular the positive imaginary terminal tangent
has not been dropped from the comparison.

The certified conservative lower bounds are:

| Incoming type \(3+\beta\) | Terminal type \(3+\epsilon\) | \(L(0)>\) | \(L(.01)>\) | \(\min_{0\le H\le.014}L(H)>\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3 | .04007 | .01876 | .01023 |
| 3 | 4 | .04391 | .02190 | .01309 |
| 4 | 3 | .04717 | .02620 | .01782 |
| 4 | 4 | .05081 | .02910 | .02042 |

The last column follows from the exact endpoint minimum
\(\min\{L(0),L(.014)\}\), since \(L\) is affine in height.
Every displayed decimal is a rational number, rounded down from the
saved exact lower endpoints. In particular, in each of the four cases,
\[
 \inf_{0\le H\le.014}
 \sup_{\text{legal plateau targets}}D_{0,1}>1/100.
 \tag{4}
\]
The trial in (3) may depend on \(H\), as the target contour does;
its real parts and all other heights remain the fixed reference ones.

The tested object is an upper envelope for the source. A positive
value of that envelope gives neither a positive lower bound for the
original selected source nor a competing source saddle. There is no
claim that all references have this obstruction, and no claim that
these fixed canonical suffixes are realized by finite outside words.
The result does show that this envelope cannot prove a theorem on
the whole canonical input domain, even with a different height in
the allowed interval.

Section 5, or a rigorously stronger terminal transport bound retaining
the missing coupled decrease. The 378-profile source comparison has
