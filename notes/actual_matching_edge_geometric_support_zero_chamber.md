## 1. A strict failed triangle has a deficient normalized degree

Let (a0,a1,a2) be an original prefix triple in a generic regular frame.
Assume zero is strictly inside its real convex hull, and
Im(a0 conjugate(a2))!=0. Multiplying all entries by one nonzero scalar,
put a0=1 and arg a2=theta in (-pi,pi), theta!=0. The strict positive
relation among the three diagonals implies

    a1=-b0-b2 a2, b0,b2>0.

Conversely such a relation makes zero an interior point of the triangle.
Thus the argument beta of a1 lies strictly between pi and pi+theta,
with these arguments lifted to the same interval of length less than pi.

The actual wire exponentials and their canonical logarithms are

    X1=-a0/a1, X2=-a1/a2,
    xj=Log_(0,2pi)(Xj)/(2pi i), 0<Re xj<1.

If theta<0, the two wire arguments are pi-beta and beta-theta-pi.
They are positive and their sum is -theta. If theta>0, the arguments
are 3pi-beta and pi+beta-theta, with sum 4pi-theta. Hence

    theta<0: 0<Re(x1+x2)=-theta/(2pi)<1/2,
    theta>0: 3/2<Re(x1+x2)=2-theta/(2pi)<2.         (1)

Use the ORIGINAL degree adapters even though the complete channel
is no longer in their physical support. Define

    low:  sigma=x1+x2, r=x1;
    high: sigma=2-x1-x2, r=1-x2.

In both cases

    0<Re sigma<1/2, 0<Re r<Re sigma.               (2)

No wire endpoint has been crossed. It is the complete-channel total
degree bound, rather than a magnetic-row endpoint, that fails.

## 2. The two exceptional ends share this same complex degree

The prescribed-pair theorem chooses the polarization line to leave
the white-normal polyhedron through the interiors of the two shaded
facets at one matching edge. For a generic transverse line, the
induced affine map on each triangular facet is nonsingular. Its
strict barycentric relation therefore gives the strict convex-hull
hypothesis of section 1, not merely a boundary relation.

The input and output triples at this edge have the SAME outer
prefix diagonals a0,a2. They consequently have the same theta and
the same adapter in (1). Their canonical wire sums have equal real
parts, and their exponentials are both a0/a2. Their imaginary parts
therefore agree as well. They share an EXACT complex normalized
total sigma. Write their two normalized magnetic rows as r,s. Then

    0<Re r,Re s<Re sigma<1/2.                     (3)

The generic family can additionally avoid Re(r-s)=0. The relevant
target-total ratio is the holomorphic ratio of the two middle prefix
diagonals. It is nonconstant: otherwise the two transported first
meridians would be proportional as linear trace functions on every
rank-one projector, hence equal after using their common trace -2.
The actual nontrivial complete collar does not fix that meridian.
A nonconstant holomorphic ratio cannot take only positive-real
values on the open prescribed-pair family. Its real seam is thus a
proper excluded set, compatible with all the existing strict margins.

## 3. The actual target chart has exactly the opposite support defect

First suppose the original adapter is low. Write
O=exp(2pi i sigma), R=exp(2pi i r), S=exp(2pi i s).
The original local prefix traces are (-1/R,-1/S,1/O).
Undoing their actual inverse-turn relation gives target exponentials

    T_target=R/S, V_input=1/S, U_output=O/S.        (4)

Their canonical physical logarithms have total 1+r-s and rows
1-s, sigma-s. If Re r<Re s, this target is low. If Re r>Re s,
apply its ORIGINAL high adapter: the normalized total is 1-r+s
and the rows are 1-r, sigma-r. For an original high adapter,
the exponentials are instead V_input=O/S,U_output=1/S; this
only reverses the target input/output order in the same calculation.

In a fixed component choose m,M from r,s by their smaller and larger
real parts. This does not take a nonholomorphic minimum inside an
action: the choice is fixed on that component. The normalized
ordered target chart is

    delta=1-sigma,
    nu=sigma-M, lambda=m,
    tau=nu+lambda+delta=1+m-M,
    u=nu, v=nu+delta=1-M.                          (5)

It follows immediately from (3) that

    1/2<Re delta<1,
    Re nu>0, Re lambda>0, Re(nu+lambda+delta)<1,
    1/2<Re tau<1,
    0<Re u<Re tau/2<Re v<Re tau.                 (6)

For the two midpoint inequalities, the positive differences are

    Re(tau/2-u)=[1+Re(r+s)-2Re sigma]/2>0,
    Re(v-tau/2)=[1-Re(r+s)]/2>0.

Thus EVERY inequality of the proved exceptional-kernel tube is
satisfied except its bandwidth condition Re delta<alpha<=1/2.
This description is uniform in bridge width and uses the actual
typed turn and degree adapters, not a freely selected target frame.
