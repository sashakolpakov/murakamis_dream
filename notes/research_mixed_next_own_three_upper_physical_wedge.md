# A finite own-three upper block transported to the half row

Private research proof candidate, 8 November 2026. A fixed positive
portion of the original own-three upper physical block is controlled
jointly with the complete below-half first conditional factor. The
comparison moves the shared real coordinate and the reflected physical
index together until the latter reaches the original half row. It uses
the already proved paired half-row calibration, with the full shared
Fourier weight retained.

This is an estimate for actual finite rows, not an inference from the
critical branch or from a bound for a coherent whole sum. The other
upper physical rows and the first upper-block alternatives remain
separate. No numerical evaluation or frozen-source edit is used.

## 1. Original source and retained indices

Use precisely the actual ordinary-next own-three references and
allocations of the
[joint profile theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_joint_main_profiles.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=x+ih_a,\quad x\in P=[11/50,9/20],\qquad
y=q+ih_y,\quad 0\le q\le11/50,\\
z=u+ih_z,\quad u\in P,\qquad
-.102<h_y,h_s<-.001,\quad -.065<h_z<.068 .
\end{gathered}\tag{1}
\]
The first height \(h_a\) and all other heights retain their original
reference correlations. Set
\[
X=e^{-2\pi ia},\quad Y=e^{-2\pi iy},\quad Z=e^{-2\pi iz},
\qquad R=|Y|=e^{2\pi h_y}\in(13/25,1).
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, the outgoing guard proved in
[the three-twentieths companion](research_mixed_next_own_three_seam_action_three_twentieths.md),
equation (1), gives
\[
|Z|<43/28.
\tag{3}
\]
No independent containing-box point replaces an actual reference.

For \(r=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), write \(P_j=(r;r)_j\) and
\(p_j(W)=(rW;r)_j\). Define the literal first below-half sum
and the next positive upper block by
\[
\begin{split}
C_{1,N}^{<1/2}(X,Y)
&=\sum_{0\le i<\lceil N/2\rceil}
       \frac{X^{-i}p_i(X)p_i(Y)}{P_i^3},\\
\mathcal U_{0,N}^{\mathrm{wedge}}(Y,Z;q)
&=\sum_{\lceil N/2\rceil\le j\le
                 \lfloor N(18/25-q)\rfloor}
       \left|\frac{Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)}{P_j^2}\right|.
\end{split}\tag{4}
\]
An empty range is zero. Each upper index is at most \(N-1\)
for all sufficiently large \(N\). Arbitrary subsets of the
second range obey the same estimate below.

**Theorem.** For all sufficiently large levels of both parities,
uniformly over (1), all real ordinary colors, and the complete original
cap, one has
\[
\boxed{\quad
|U_{0,N}(w,X)C_{1,N}^{<1/2}(X,Y)d_N(Y)|\,
\mathcal U_{0,N}^{\mathrm{wedge}}(Y,Z;q)
\le C N^B e^{N\{\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-11}\}} .
\quad}\tag{5}
\]
Here \(d_N\) is the original forced Fourier polynomial,
\(d_N(r^b)=P_b^{-1}\), and \(\Lambda_{123}\) is the unchanged
actual following plane. The next own type is three, so there is
no outgoing factorial halfweight. Both incident halves of the
shared weight remain in the single factor \(d_N(Y)\).

There is no outside index density in either finite sum in (4).
If the actual first incoming integral is taken, its original
measure is \(+N\,da\); the same exponential bound then holds.
For a signed next partial sum with an index set frozen at a base
parameter, the corresponding fixed normalized local mixed jets
near complete color and actual output have gap \(10^{-12}\).
This is not a derivative assertion for the absolute sum or for
a moving integer cutoff.

## 2. Exact joint transport to a previously paid profile

The first original phase and next physical rate are
\[
\begin{split}
Q_1(s;a,y)&=g(a+s)+g(y+s)-3g(s)-g(a)-g(y)+2\pi ias,\\
E_0(t;y,z)&=
 \int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(y+b)}|\,db
 +\int_0^t\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(z+b)}|\,db\\
&\hspace{12mm}-2J(t)-2\pi h_y t .
\end{split}\tag{6}
\]
Every integrable zero and the incoming carrier are retained.
Let the first profile be a main point at its original height,
clipped to
\[
1/6\le\Re s\le5/12,\qquad \Im s=h_s,
\tag{7}
\]
or one of its original real cuts \(s=1/8,1/2\).
The clipping inequalities in the joint-profile theorem increase the
first phase; they remain valid after attaching a next physical row.
Put \(S_s=e^{2\pi is}\), and let \(B_U(x)\) denote the original
complete cap supporting plane. The joint upper rate is
\[
\mathcal B_s(q,v)
=B_U(x)+\Re Q_1(s;a,y)+E_0(1-v;y,z)-\Re g(y).
\tag{8}
\]
The domain of (4) is exactly
\[
q+7/25\le v\le1/2.
\tag{9}
\]
Keep \(d=v-q\) fixed, and increase \(q\) and \(v\) together.
The endpoint is
\[
q_e=1/2-d=q+1/2-v,\qquad v_e=1/2,\qquad
q\le q_e\le11/50.
\tag{10}
\]
All heights, first profiles and outgoing targets remain fixed.
The original following plane has no target-\(q\) dependence.

Differentiating (6), the incoming shifted numerator cancels
because \(q+t\) is constant when \(t=1-v\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{\quad
\exp\frac d{dq}\mathcal B_s(q,q+d)
=
\frac{R\,|1-Y/S_s|\,\{2\sin(\pi v)\}^2}
 {|1-Y|^3\,|1-Ze^{2\pi iv}|}.
\quad}\tag{11}
\]
In particular the factor \(R\) comes from the original incoming
carrier. It cannot be omitted. Equation (11) holds away from an
outgoing numerator zero and integrates through it: its logarithmic
singularity has positive sign and is integrable.

At the endpoint (10), (8) is precisely the original first
main/real-cut profile paired with the real next half row.
The joint-profile theorem pays each such profile, with
the same complete cap plane and full shared allocation, by
\[
\mathcal B_s(q_e,1/2)<\Lambda_{123}(z)-10^{-10}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is a phase-by-phase input, not a consequence obtained by
taking a term out of a signed sum.

## 3. Uniform positive derivative on the entire moving path

First, throughout (9),
\[
|u-v|\le7/25.
\]
Thus \(\cos(2\pi(u-v))>-1/5\), by
\(\sin(3\pi/50)<1/5\). With (3),
\[
|1-Ze^{2\pi iv}|^2
<1+(43/28)^2+(2/5)(43/28)
=\frac{15573}{3920}<4.
\tag{13}
\]

We next bound \(R/|1-Y|^3\), retaining its radial correlation.
For any fixed \(0<c<1\), the function
\[
f_c(R)=\frac{R}{(1+R^2-2cR)^{3/2}}
\]
has derivative with the sign of \(1-2R^2+cR\).
On \(13/25\le R\le1\) this sign can change only from positive
to negative. Therefore its minimum is at an endpoint.
The following four bounds follow from that endpoint test:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
q\text{ interval}&\cos(2\pi q)>c&R/|1-Y|^3\\ \hline
[0,1/10]&4/5&>5/3\\
[1/10,3/20]&7/12&>9/10\\
[3/20,9/50]&21/50&>2/3\\
[9/50,11/50]&37/200&>23/50 .
\end{array}\tag{14}
\]
Here are explicit endpoint witnesses. At \(R=13/25\), the four
denominators before the power \(3/2\) are respectively
\[
\frac{274}{625}<\frac9{20},\quad
\frac{2489}{3750}<\frac23,\quad
\frac{521}{625}<\frac{21}{25},\quad
\frac{539}{500}<\frac{13}{12}.
\]
After squaring the desired bounds, the resulting comparisons are
\[
\frac{10816}{3645}>\frac{25}{9},\quad
\frac{4563}{5000}>\frac{81}{100},\quad
\frac{4225}{9261}>\frac49,\quad
\frac{169\cdot1728}{625\cdot2197}>\frac{529}{2500}.
\]
The cross-product difference in the last comparison, after
canceling \(625\), is \(5915>0\).
At \(R=1\) the denominators are
\(2/5,5/6,29/25,163/100\). For the last two use
\(29/25<6/5\) and \(163/100<5/3\), giving squared lower
bounds \(125/216>4/9\) and \(27/125>529/2500\).
The first two are stronger still.
The cosine witnesses are elementary: the pentagon value proves
the first; the second is the original \(3\pi/10\) bound;
the third follows from
\(\sin(7\pi/50)>\sin(7/16)>21/50\);
the fourth follows from
\(\sin(3\pi/50)>\sin(3/16)>37/200\).
Both latter sine bounds follow already from \(s-s^3/6\).

The remaining two numerator factors have the following bounds
on the same four intervals:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
q\text{ interval}&|1-Y/S_s|&
                       \{2\sin(\pi v)\}^2\\ \hline
[0,1/10]&>7/10&>7/3\\
[1/10,3/20]&>1&>17/5\\
[3/20,9/50]&>1&>19/5\\
[9/50,11/50]&>23/20&>39/10 .
\end{array}\tag{15}
\]
For the first column, radial minimization on the clipped main
gives at least the chord sine at real coordinate \(1/6\)
when its shifted cosine is positive, and at least one otherwise.
For the real low cut the corresponding minimum is
\(\sin(\pi/4)>7/10\); the real half cut is larger than one.
When \(q\ge1/10\), the main shifted cosine is nonpositive.
For the real low cut it is less than \(1/6\), and \(R>1/3\)
then makes its modulus greater than one. The half cut is
again larger than one.

For \(q\ge9/50\), every clipped main has
\[
q+\Re s\in[26/75,191/300]\subset[1/3,2/3].
\]
Its shifted cosine is at most \(-1/2\).
Since \(e^{2\pi h_s}>1/2\),
\[
|1-Y/S_s|^2>1+R^2/4+R/2
>\frac{3319}{2500}>\left(\frac{23}{20}\right)^2.
\]
For the real low cut the cosine is at most
\(-\sin(11\pi/100)<-1/3\); the cubic sine lower bound at
\(11/32\) verifies this witness. Its squared modulus therefore
exceeds \(1+R^2+2R/3>19/12>(23/20)^2\).
For the real half cut use \(\cos(2\pi q)>3/20\), giving
\(1+R^2+3R/10>7/5>(23/20)^2\).

For the second column of (15), use \(v\ge q+7/25\).
The respective minimum real coordinates are
\(7/25,19/50,43/100,23/50\).
Their squared sine chords are
\[
2+2\sin(3\pi/50),\quad
2+2\cos(6\pi/25),\quad
2+2\cos(7\pi/50),\quad
2+2\cos(2\pi/25).
\]
The first is greater than \(7/3\). The other three follow from
\(\cos t\ge1-t^2/2\) and respectively
\(t<132/175,11/25,44/175\).

Combining (13)--(15), the four lower bounds for (11) are
\[
\frac{49}{36},\qquad
\frac{153}{100},\qquad
\frac{19}{15},\qquad
\frac{20631}{20000}>1.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus (8) increases along the complete path (10). Its value at
the original physical row is no larger than (12).

## 4. Exact finite sums, current inventory and limits of the result

The original endpoint-uniform sine-factorial estimates and the
zero-safe numerator upper estimates imply, for every fixed tolerance
\(\tau>0\),
\[
\left|\frac{Y^{-j}p_j(Y)p_j(Z)}{P_j^2}\right|
\le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
             e^{N\{E_0(j/N;y,z)+\tau\}},
\tag{17}
\]
uniformly over the entire range in (4). Outgoing numerator zeros
can be handled by a fixed logarithmic cutoff before taking the
uniform upper Riemann sum, exactly as in
[the upper-row source](research_mixed_next_lower_left_own_four_upper_rows.md).
No lower bound for a vanishing numerator is used.
The actual endpoint \(j/N\) determines \(v\) and \(q_e\) exactly;
no rounding to a different row is needed in (10). At \(q=11/50\)
the range has only the half row for even \(N\), and is empty
for odd \(N\). In either case it is covered by (12).

The first low positive block is bounded by its real \(1/8\)
profile by
[the low-block theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_low_physical_blocks.md).
The remaining first indices below \(\lceil N/2\rceil\) use exactly
the original canonical middle source in
[the complete-current theorem](research_mixed_next_lower_left_complete_currents.md):
the \(+N\) main density, both horizontal aliases, all four
weighted side pieces and both unweighted joins. Each of these
is bounded by a first main, real low or real half profile at
the same \(q\). The next row, independent of the first index,
stays attached during these comparisons. Equations (11)--(16)
are applied only to the resulting nonpositive-height or real
profiles, never to an uncalibrated positive-height current.

The complete cap is bounded by its original all-real-color plane
\(B_U(x)\). The full shared weight uses its distinct forced bound
\[
|d_N(Y)|\le C_\tau N^{B_\tau}
               e^{N\{-\Re g(y)+\tau\}}
\]
through \(q=0\), from the low-block theorem. The original canonical
first row there uses
[the factorial seam continuation](research_factorial_symbol_across_inward_seam.md);
no relative asymptotic for \(d_N\) is asserted.
Take finite tolerances smaller than the unused reserve in (12),
sum at most \(N\) next rows, and retain all polynomial factors.
This proves (5). A fixed small enlargement of the first real
cuts absorbs their half-grid shifts inside the strict profile
reserves. No artificial endpoint density is inserted.

For fixed-base local derivatives, freeze all index sets, paths
and reference labels. Each signed partial sum is an original
holomorphic finite source. Uniform zero-safe upper continuity,
the strict reserve in (12), and the already proved fixed-current
neighborhoods give a sufficiently small fixed parameter tube.
Near a boundary of (9), the same strict reserve absorbs a small
enlargement of that inequality before Cauchy's estimate is applied.
Fixed real-color derivatives have only polynomial cost, as for
the complete cap. This yields the normalized local-jet assertion
after the stated smaller loss.

The theorem leaves the next rows \(j/N>18/25-q\), the first
upper physical block and its last-row alternatives, and the
other first-incoming intervals outside its scope. It does not
bound a whole next own-three kernel or the remaining signed
outgoing contour. It also makes no claim about a coefficient
or contour contribution of the transported algebraic critical
branch.
