# The compact source generator is a polar-fibre boundary class

Research proof, 27 October 2026. The fixed compact source has a precise
localization obstruction: deleting just one polar fibre makes its
selected two-form exact. A map through that absolute chart therefore
kills the source generator. Its nonzero image must retain an explicit
relative divisor class or the equivalent residue co-source.

This is an obstruction in the actual compact source, not a
manufactured alternative knot state. It also gives a direct algebraic
residue formula for the normalized trace, including the fixed-order
actual A-period. It does not prove that the geometric Gaussian
pushforward preserves that residue.

## 1. One polar fibre leaves an affine plane

Use the fixed surface of
[the compact coherent theorem](compact_coherent_source_period.md).
Normalize its Segre coordinates as
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&t\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 a+t=1,\quad bc=t(1-t).
\]
In the usual chart,
\[
 t=\frac{wz}{1+wz},\quad
 b=\frac{z}{1+wz},\quad c=\frac{w}{1+wz}.
\]
Thus
\[
 X=\{bc=t(1-t)\},\qquad
 \omega_0=\frac{dw\wedge dz}{(1+wz)^2}.
\]
Let
\[
 L_\infty=\{b=0,\ t=1\}\simeq\mathbb A^1_c.
\]
It is a smooth Cartier divisor: near it, \(t\) is a unit and
\(t-1=-bc/t\). Geometrically it is the whole fibre \(w=\infty\)
of the projection \(X\to\mathbb P^1_w\).

Its complement is exactly an affine plane. The maps
\[
 U=X\setminus L_\infty\simeq\mathbb A^2_{w,b},\qquad
 t=wb,\quad c=w(1-wb)                              \tag{1}
\]
are inverse: \(w=t/b\) where \(b\ne0\), and
\(w=c/(1-t)\) where \(t\ne1\). These two opens cover \(U\).
On it,
\[
 \omega_0=dw\wedge db=d(w\,db).                    \tag{2}
\]
The selected class therefore restricts to zero in \(H^2_{\rm dR}(U)\).

Deleting both fibres \(t=0,1\) gives the still more familiar chart
\[
 X^\circ\simeq
   (\mathbb A^1\setminus\{0,1\})_t\times\mathbb G_{m,b},
 \qquad c=t(1-t)/b,
\]
on which
\[
 \omega_0=dt\wedge d\log b=d(t\,d\log b).           \tag{3}
\]
This chart has nontrivial absolute top cohomology, generated by
\(d\log t\wedge d\log b\) and
\(d\log(1-t)\wedge d\log b\); nevertheless the actual compact source
class is zero there. A nonzero chart cohomology group alone is
therefore not sufficient.
These bases follow directly by Laurent expansion in \(b\) and
partial fractions in \(t\): all nonzero \(b\)-weights and all
rational terms except the two simple logarithmic poles have
algebraic primitives.

## 2. The exact relative complex retaining the generator

Define the divisor-support complex directly by
\[
 C^k_{L_\infty}(X)=
       \Omega^k(X)\oplus\Omega^{k-1}(U),\qquad
 D(\alpha,\beta)=(d\alpha,\alpha|_U-d\beta).       \tag{4}
\]
Its projection to \(\Omega^\bullet(X)\) is a chain map.
The standard exact sequence of this explicit cone contains
\[
 H^1_{\rm dR}(U)\longrightarrow
 H^2(C^\bullet_{L_\infty}(X))\longrightarrow
 H^2_{\rm dR}(X)\longrightarrow H^2_{\rm dR}(U).
\]
Both outer groups vanish because \(U=\mathbb A^2\). One elementary
proof is polynomial radial contraction: a closed positive-degree
homogeneous form has primitive obtained by contracting with the
Euler field and dividing by its positive total degree.
Consequently the middle projection is an isomorphism.

The compact generator has the actual lift
\[
 (\omega_0,w\,db)\in C^2_{L_\infty}(X).            \tag{5}
\]
It is a cycle by (2). At \(L_\infty\), its primitive is
\[
 w\,db=t\,\frac{db}{b},
\]
whose residue is exactly one. Thus (5) supplies a concrete
generator of the divisor-support line, not just its dimension.

On the real compact sphere, \(b=\sqrt{t(1-t)}e^{-i\phi}\).
For the cap \(0\le t\le1-\varepsilon\), Stokes gives
\[
 \int\omega_0=\int_{\partial}t\,d\log b
             =-2\pi i(1-\varepsilon).
\]
Taking the limit recovers the selected period \(-2\pi i\).
The removed compact-cycle point is restored by a clockwise
meridian of \(L_\infty\). This is the boundary datum lost by
passing from \(X\) to the absolute affine chart.

For an explicit two-fibre version, the deleted divisor has four
components \(L_{0,b},L_{0,c},L_{1,b},L_{1,c}\).
The residues of \(d\log t,d\log(1-t),d\log b\) are respectively
\[
 (1,1,0,0),\quad(0,0,1,1),\quad(1,0,1,0).
\]
The primitive \(t\,d\log b\) has residue vector \((0,0,1,0)\).
It is not in the span of those three vectors. This records
directly why the source boundary class remains nontrivial even
though its top form becomes exact on \(X^\circ\).

## 3. A direct residue formula for every actual coherent trace

Let the coherent matrix size be \(N=n+1\). In the affine coordinates
(1), its symbol is the polynomial
\[
 f_A(b,w)=
   \sum_{i,j=0}^n A_{ij}\binom nj
                    w^i b^j(1-wb)^{n-j}.         \tag{6}
\]
Hence the normalized top form has a primitive on the affine chart
which does not require its trace as input:
\[
 \beta_A=
 -\frac1{2\pi i}
  \left[\int_0^w f_A(b,s)\,ds\right]db,\qquad
 d\beta_A=\omega_A.                              \tag{7}
\]
The integral is ordinary polynomial antiderivation.
Then the original compact trace is exactly
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Tr}A}{N}
       =-2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}_{L_\infty}\beta_A.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]

Here is an algebraic verification. Near the divisor use \(b,c\)
with \(t\) the root of \(t(1-t)=bc\) tending to one. Substitution
\(w=t/b\) makes (7) a finite-pole Laurent one-form
\(F(b,c)\,db\). Since its derivative is regular on \(X\), every
negative Laurent coefficient of \(F\) is constant in \(c\).
In particular its residue is constant along \(L_\infty\).
For \(A=E_{ij}\), torus scaling
\((b,w)\mapsto(\lambda b,\lambda^{-1}w)\) gives the form weight
\(\lambda^{j-i}\). A constant residue can therefore be nonzero
only if \(i=j\). In that case the residue is
\[
 -\frac1{2\pi i}\binom ni
     \sum_{k=0}^{n-i}
           \frac{(-1)^k\binom{n-i}k}{i+k+1}
   =-\frac1{2\pi iN}.
\]
The equality follows by integrating
\(s^i(1-s)^{n-i}\) on \([0,1]\), or by repeated integration by
parts. Linearity proves (8).

Any other regular algebraic primitive on \(U\), equivalently a
rational primitive on \(X\) with poles only on \(L_\infty\), has
the same residue. Its difference from (7) is closed, hence exact
on \(\mathbb A^2\); an exact rational differential has zero
logarithmic residue. Allowing extra poles inside \(U\) would
invalidate this assertion.

For the fixed-order actual A-period of the compact theorem, apply
(7) at companion matrix size \(r\), with
\(R_N=v_0\ell H_N\). Its selected value becomes the concrete boundary
period
\[
 C_N(q)=-2\pi i\,
             \operatorname{Res}_{L_\infty}(r\,\beta_{R_N(q)}).
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
Its normal polar coefficients have zero residue because their
traces vanish. Thus the existing pole removal preserves this
specified boundary functional exactly, without a nonvanishing
assumption. Formula (9) does not estimate the growing coefficient
product \(H_N\).

### The entire finite boundary complex is explicit

There is more than the residue formula. With
\(J_-e_j=je_{j-1}\), the complete polar part of (7) is
\[
 \operatorname{PP}_{L_\infty}\beta_A
   =-\frac1{2\pi i}
      \sum_{k=0}^n\frac{n!}{(n+k+1)!}
             \operatorname{Tr}(J_-^kA)\,
                         b^{-k-1}db.             \tag{10}
\]
To prove this, the negative coefficients are constant along \(c\),
as above, so evaluate them at \(c=0,t=1,w=1/b\).
The \(E_{ij}\) contribution is
\[
 -\frac1{2\pi i}b^{j-i-1}\binom nj
             \int_0^1s^i(1-s)^{n-j}ds
   =-\frac1{2\pi i}b^{j-i-1}
             \frac{n!\,i!}{j!(n-j+i+1)!}.
\]
For a pole write \(i=j+k\); summing that lower subdiagonal
gives precisely \(\operatorname{Tr}(J_-^kA)\), proving (10).

The modes \(b^{-k-1}db\), \(k\ge1\), are exact in the punctured
formal neighborhood:
\[
 b^{-k-1}db=d(-b^{-k}/k).
\]
Thus the finite normal-pole complex
\[
 \bigoplus_{k=1}^n F\,b^{-k}
       \xrightarrow{\,d\,}
 \bigoplus_{k=0}^n F\,b^{-k-1}db
\]
has exactly its one-dimensional residue cokernel over the
coefficient field \(F\).

In particular, for each actual one-cut diagonal array and every
diagonal Green weight, all \(\operatorname{Tr}(J_-^kA)\), \(k>0\),
vanish. Its primitive has only a logarithmic polar part even
when the color size grows. For the fixed-order companion
representation the number of polar modes is at most its fixed
matrix size, independently of the color level.

There is a stronger canonical statement for that companion source.
Slide the actual source to its regular right representative
\[
 R_N^{\rm right}=H_Nv_0\ell,\qquad \ell=e_r^t.
\]
Since \(J_-\) lowers the index, \(\ell J_-^k=0\) for every \(k>0\).
Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(J_-^kR_N^{\rm right})=0\ (k>0),\qquad
 \operatorname{PP}\beta_{R_N^{\rm right}}
       =-\frac{C_N(q)}{2\pi i\,r}\frac{db}{b}.     \tag{11}
\]
The scaled actual A-form \(r\,\omega_{R_N^{\rm right}}\)
therefore has exactly its one logarithmic boundary coefficient
\(-C_N(q)/(2\pi i)\). This representative is also regular in
the quantum normal. The exact source slide from
\(R_N^{\rm left}=v_0\ell H_N\) removes both apparent quantum-normal
poles and higher boundary-pole modes without normalizing by
\(C_N\).

This is a finite explicit boundary model of the selected source.
It is a linear current/cohomology model, not an algebra quotient
of the star product. Projection to these boundary moments before
composing the whole word would discard matrix data needed by
later factors. The source and exact star product remain until
the final residue.

## 4. The precise obstruction to an absolute Gaussian comparison

Suppose a proposed source-to-geometric map is a chain map on the
whole compact de Rham complex and factors through restriction to
\(\Omega^\bullet(U)\). Its induced map on the selected \(H^2\)
generator is zero, by (2). The same is true if it factors through
the two-polar-fibre chart (3). It cannot simultaneously send that
class to a nonzero geometric period.

There are two possible ways to avoid this obstruction: retain a
map of the actual support/relative complex (4), including its
residue co-source, or prove a different global continuation that
does not discard the polar fibre. A local stationary Gaussian
only gives neither assertion. In particular one cannot claim both
that all affine total derivatives are killed and that their
specific primitive (2) retains a nonzero absolute class.

For an actual full-word form the primitive (7) can have growing
degree. A local formal Gaussian may not be defined on its entire
normal or asymptotic class. Proving that extension, or carrying
the missing boundary instead, is part of the comparison theorem.
This statement does not assume that the existing partial Gaussian
already extends to every global rational primitive.

The new concrete test is therefore whether the proposed geometric
current map preserves the residue in (8)--(9). Exact currents
coming from forms globally exact on \(X\) have zero such residue.
The selected source generator has residue
\(-1/(2\pi i)\), and must not be discarded with those currents.
This is an explicit divisor-source condition for the actual
source, beyond a choice of normalization at an interior saddle.

## 5. Global physical Gauss coordinates require their period torsor

The local physical identity in the compact theorem is correct:
\[
 A=e^{2\pi it},\quad p=1/w=b/t,\qquad
 d\log A\wedge d\log p=2\pi i\,\omega_0.
\]
It does not define a global algebraic map to the Gauss torus.
Already on a generic \(b=\text{constant}\) curve, an algebraic
function \(A\) satisfying \(d\log A=2\pi i\,dt\) cannot exist
on any finite algebraic cover. At a place over \(t=\infty\),
the right side has pole order greater than one; a logarithmic
derivative of an algebraic meromorphic function has only simple
poles. The clock must remain a period of its exponential
connection, with its marked logarithmic coordinate.

Globally the analytic divisor \(A=1\) is also not just \(t=0,1\):
it has every fibre \(t\in\mathbb Z\). The two endpoints describe
the actual real compact cycle; replacing them by a global finite
algebraic divisor silently changes that analytic map.

Finally, a globally regular class-preserving map from \(X\) to a
split framing surface with
\(\Omega_{\rm cut}=d(\tau\,d\log A)\) would make \(\omega_0\)
exact, contradicting its compact period. The cotangent-torsor
transition
\[
 w'=1/w,\qquad b'=w-w^2b,\qquad
 \lambda=-b\,dw,\quad\lambda'=\lambda+d\log w
\]
exhibits exactly the missing Chern twist. On the compact sphere
\(\lambda\) is the \(O(1)\) Chern connection and
\(c_1=-[\omega_0/(2\pi i)]\). A twisted affine framing object or
the corresponding relative/logarithmic period data can retain
this class; an unmarked split chart cannot.

The physical half-form scale checks exactly in this convention.
For \(\Omega_{\rm phys}=2\pi i\,\omega_0\) and
\(h_N=2\pi i/N\), the curvature \(\Omega_{\rm phys}/h_N\)
is \(N\omega_0\), the curvature of \(O(N)\) with connection
\(N\lambda\). The holomorphic half-form is
\(K_{\mathbb P^1}^{1/2}=O(-1)\), so the corrected section space is
\[
 H^0(\mathbb P^1,O(N)\otimes O(-1))
       =H^0(\mathbb P^1,O(N-1)),
 \qquad \dim=N.
\]
This explains the exact shift in the coherent head calculus.
It does not identify its source connection with the internal
framing connection of the knot.

Neither this Chern twist nor the boundary residue is already an
identification with the knot's internal geometric framing bundle.
The local coefficient map, its residues and the selected regulator
action still have to be transported through the actual word.
