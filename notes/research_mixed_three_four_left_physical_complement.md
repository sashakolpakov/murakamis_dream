\]

\[

## 3. The physical private complement keeps its entire coefficient

For physical private \(e=j/N\), retain the whole-index upper rate
from [the original private-tail theorem](research_mixed_terminal_four_private_tails.md):
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal R(a;t,e)&=Q_a(t)-2J(t)+\lambda(e)-J(e)+I_t(e),\\
 Q_a(t)&=\int_0^t\log|e^{-2\pi h}-e^{-2\pi i(x+s)}|\,ds,\\
 I_t(e)&=\int_0^e\log|1-e^{-2\pi i(t+s)}|\,ds,
 \qquad\lambda(e)=\Re S(e).
\end{split}                                             \tag{7}
\]
Its original finite bound is uniform through \(e=0,1\),
with an arbitrary prescribed rate tolerance and polynomial costs.
It uses the entire \(H_N\), including every Newton summand and
every ordinary derivative.

For \(e\le.27\), \(t\le.15\), one has
\(t\le t+e\le.42<.5\), and
\[
 e^{\mathcal R_t}
 =\frac{|R-e^{-2\pi i(x+t)}|\,2\sin\pi(t+e)}
             {(2\sin\pi t)^3}
 \ge\frac{|R-e^{-2\pi i(x+t)}|}{(2\sin\pi t)^2}.        \tag{8}
\]
If \(t\le.1\), then \(x+t\in(.1,.32]\).
The first modulus is greater than \(1/2\): use the sine
minimum while its angular cosine is positive, and the bound one
when it is nonpositive. Also
\[
 (2\sin\pi t)^2\le(3-\sqrt5)/2<2/5,
\]
where \(\sqrt5>11/5\). Thus the ratio in (8) exceeds \(5/4\).
If \(.1\le t\le.15\), then \(x+t\in[.2,.37]\),
whose cosine is less than \(1/3\). Since \(R>4/5\),
\[
 |R-e^{-2\pi i(x+t)}|^2
 >R^2-2R/3+1>83/75>1.
\]
The old guard \(2\sin(.15\pi)<23/25\) now gives a ratio
greater than \(625/529>9/8\). Consequently
\[
                       \mathcal R_t>\log(9/8)            \tag{9}
\]
throughout this complete lower-private region.
Transport first to \(t=.15\), then use the old lower-private
derivative \(R_e>13/100\) to reach \(e=.27\).
The original anchor identity gives
\[
 \mathcal R(a;t,e)\le\Re\Phi(a;.15,.27)
    -\log(9/8)(.15-t)-13(.27-e)/100.                    \tag{10}
\]

For \(e\ge.49\), first transport \(e\) to \(.49\).
At real \(t\), the old bounds
\(\lambda'-J'<-6/5\) on \([.49,.5]\),
\(\lambda'-J'\le-\log4\) above \(.5\), and

       -(.15-t)/2000-(e-.49)/2.                        \tag{11}
\]
The logarithmic potentials extend continuously through product
zeros. Integrating on their complementary intervals proves the
