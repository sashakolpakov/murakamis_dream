\title{Marked geometry of strict Conway compositions}
\author{Alexander Kolpakov}\date{25 November 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We associate a hyperbolic link exterior to a finite construction from
crossing-circle tangles by stacking and quarter-turns. The hypothesis is
an explicit system of strict linear inequalities on one real variable
for each stage of the construction. A variational theorem then supplies
the parameters of ideal pyramids and prisms. We prove that these cells
assemble to a convex right-angled polyhedron, and that two copies of it,
with specified face identifications, give the prescribed link exterior.
We calculate the meridian and disk longitude at every crossing circle,
including their signs and primitive lattice periods. The same face maps
extend holomorphically near the complete metric. For all independently
sufficiently long signed fillings, their logarithmic filling equations
have a unique nearby solution. Its classical critical value is the
volume of the filled exterior. The argument concerns hyperbolic geometry;
the additional estimates needed for a quantum-invariant volume limit
are stated separately at the end.
\end{abstract}

\section{The fixed diagram and the geometric statements}\label{sm:intro}

The construction keeps track of a diagram, rather than just an unmarked
isotopy class of tangles. This is necessary because the crossing disks
specify the curves along which we shall fill. A \emph{crossing circle} is
an auxiliary unknotted component bounding a disk punctured twice by the
main strands. Begin with a two-strand crossing box, remove its full
twists, and turn the box clockwise through a quarter-turn; call the
result $h$. The crossing circle and its disk move with the box.
A construction is a finite parenthesized expression in distinct copies
of $h$, ordered products, and clockwise quarter-turns $\Turn$.
The leaves of this expression are its elementary crossing-circle boxes.
The unturned pure box has two parallel vertical strands. Consequently,
in the endpoint convention below, a pure leaf $h$ joins $0$ to $1$
and $2$ to $3$. An odd retained half twist changes this pairing to
$0$ with $2$ and $1$ with $3$. The crossing disk meets each of the
two strands once.

The four endpoints of a box are numbered $0,1,2,3$ cyclically, starting
at the bottom left. In the stack $FG$, with $F$ below $G$, join
$F_2$ to $G_1$ and $F_3$ to $G_0$. Its exposed tuple is
$(F_0,F_1,G_2,G_3)$. A turn replaces a tuple by
$(C_1,C_2,C_3,C_0)$ and moves the entire box and all its disks.
Cylindrical closure $C$ joins endpoints $0$ to $3$ and $1$ to $2$.
Our closed diagrams are $C(FG)$ with arbitrary such $F,G$ subject to
the following condition. Figure~\ref{sm:endpoint-figure} records the endpoint convention.

\begin{definition}\label{sm:strict}
Assign a real \emph{output variable} to each open subexpression.
These variables are also called wires: they are the labels on the
edges of the expression tree, not individual strands in the link
diagram. A leaf has output $a\in(0,1/2)$, and a turn changes an
output $b$ to $1-b$. If $x,y$ are the outputs of the two factors
of a product and $z$ is its output, require
\begin{equation}\label{sm:gate-domain}
             \max(x,y)<z<x+y<1.
\end{equation}
at every product below the closing root. The two factors at the root
have complementary outputs $b,1-b$; there is no independent output
variable or product term at that root. The turn and closing relations
are affine. Together with the inequalities above they define a
relatively open convex polytope in their affine constraint space,
called the \emph{wire polytope}. A \emph{strict state} is a point of
this polytope, and the expression is \emph{strict} if the polytope is
nonempty.
\end{definition}

Strictness is therefore a finite feasibility condition on the chosen
construction. For example, the expression
$C((h_1h_2)(h_3h_4))$ has a strict state: give each leaf the value
$3/10$ and each of the two products the value $1/2$. The two closing
outputs are complementary, and $3/10<1/2<3/5<1$ at both products.
The geometry uses the maximizing state, which need not be this sample
state. A single leaf, or a turned leaf, may also be one of the two
closing factors when the complementary constraints permit it.

Fix $\eps_i\in\{-1,0,1\}$ at the $H$ leaves of the expression $E$. It specifies
the retained signed half twist; $\eps_i=0$ is the pure disk pairing.
Let $Y_E^\eps$ be the exterior in $S^3$ of the resulting augmented
link, retaining all $H$ crossing circles and every main component.
The cell volumes will be expressed in terms of the functions
\begin{align}
 J(u)&=\int_0^u\log(2\sin\pi t)\,\dd t\quad(0\le u\le1),
       &J(1-u)&=-J(u),\label{sm:J}\\
 g(a)&=J(2a)-4J(a),\label{sm:g}\\
 m(x,y,z)&=J(x+y)+J(z)-J(x)-J(y)\notag\\
 &\hspace{13mm}-J(z-x)-J(z-y)-J(x+y-z).\label{sm:m}
\end{align}
Let $\mathcal F$ be the sum of $g$ over leaves and $m$ over retained
products, and let $F_0$ denote its maximum. The variational theorem
of \cite{Newton} supplies the unique strict optimizer and its
negative definite Hessian in any independent coordinates on the affine
constraint space. We state its precise conclusions in
Section~\ref{sm:variational}. In the geometric construction, a leaf
contributes a pyramid of volume $\pi g$, and a product contributes a
prism of volume $\pi m$. The stationary equations are exactly the
conditions for their quadrilateral faces to be isometric.

We use \emph{exterior} for the complement of an open tubular
neighborhood of a link, and use the same notation for its interior
with complete cusp ends. Each crossing disk fixes a meridian
$\mu_i$ and a disk longitude $\lambda_i$ on the auxiliary boundary
torus. A pair is \emph{primitive} if it is a basis of the integral
first homology of that torus. On a complete cusp, holonomy acts on a
horosphere by translations. We orient our basis so that its
longitude-to-meridian ratio has positive imaginary part.

\begin{theorem}[Complete marked exterior]\label{sm:complete}
For every fixed strict expression and retained half-twist pattern,
$Y_E^\eps$ has a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric assembled
from two copies of a convex right-angled ideal polyhedron. Its volume is
\begin{equation}\label{sm:complete-volume}
                     \Vol(Y_E^\eps)=2\pi F_0.
\end{equation}
At each crossing circle the original meridian $\mu_i$ and disk
longitude $\lambda_i$ are a primitive ordered basis. Their complete
longitude-to-meridian translation ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{sm:tau}
 \tau_i=\frac{2\ii c_i}{1-\eps_i\ii c_i},\qquad
 c_i=\cot(\pi a_i)>0,\qquad \Ima\tau_i>0.
\end{equation}
All original main components have complete cusp ends.
\end{theorem}

The primitive basis fixes the filling convention. For a subset
$I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,H\}$ and integers $p_i$, fill precisely the
auxiliary slopes $\mu_i+p_i\lambda_i$, $i\in I$. Denote the original
filled exterior by $Y_E^\eps(\mathbf p;I)$. Cutting the original disk,
rotating through $p_i$ full turns and removing its crossing circle
inserts the physical power $\eps_i-2p_i$ in that same disk frame.
The disjoint disk neighborhoods show directly that the ambient manifold
is still $S^3$. We never fill a main cusp.

For the deformation statement we also fix the logarithm convention.
A \emph{framed representation} consists of a homomorphism to
$\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ and a continuously chosen common
fixed point of each peripheral subgroup on the sphere at infinity.
Send that point to $\infty$. A peripheral element then acts as
$z\mapsto a z+b$; its \emph{similarity logarithm} is the branch of
$\Log a$ that vanishes at the complete structure. We write $U_i$
for the meridian logarithm and $V_i$ for the longitude logarithm.
These are twice the corresponding eigenvalue logarithms in a centered
$\operatorname{SL}_2$ lift. The factor two will be important in the
filling and volume formulas. We parametrize the meridian deformation
by $U_i=-2\pi\ii\chi_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Primitive signed fillings]\label{sm:fillings}
There is a holomorphic classical potential $\Phi_\eps(\boldsymbol\chi)$,
with $\Phi_\eps(0)=F_0$, and a framed representation of the original
group near its oriented complete holonomy, whose primitive similarity
logarithms are
\begin{equation}\label{sm:UV}
       U_i=-2\pi\ii\chi_i,\qquad V_i=-2\partial_i\Phi_\eps.
\end{equation}
Every main peripheral group remains parabolic. Set $\chi_i=0$ outside
$I$. There is a bound $B_{E,\eps,I}$ such that, whenever all
$|p_i|>B_{E,\eps,I}$, the restricted action
\begin{equation}\label{sm:filled-action}
 S_{\eps,\mathbf p}(\boldsymbol\chi)=\Phi_\eps(\boldsymbol\chi)
       +\frac{\pi\ii}{2}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{(\chi_i-1)^2}{p_i}
\end{equation}
has a unique critical point in a fixed neighborhood of zero. The
representation there completes to a smooth complete finite-volume
hyperbolic metric on the original $Y_E^\eps(\mathbf p;I)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{sm:filled-volume}
 \Vol\bigl(Y_E^\eps(\mathbf p;I)\bigr)
                   =2\pi\Rea S_{\eps,\mathbf p}(\boldsymbol\chi_*).
\end{equation}
The signs and relative sizes of the filling integers are independent.
If $I$ contains all auxiliaries and the strand pairing of the specified diagram has one
component, the right side is the volume of that knot complement.
\end{theorem}

Here and below a holomorphic potential is a function on a sufficiently
small neighborhood of zero; its value and logarithm branches are fixed
by the complete structure. Uniqueness means uniqueness in that fixed
neighborhood. Section~\ref{sm:potential} defines the potential directly
from the cell functions. A maximum over the finitely many $\eps,I$ gives one bound
for a fixed strict expression if desired. An empty filling subset
recovers Theorem~\ref{sm:complete}.

The classical geometry of fully augmented links and their half-twist
partners is well established; see \cite{Purcell}. Dehn filling and
holomorphic peripheral potentials belong to the framework of
\cite{NZ}. The work here is the ordered individual-edge assembly and
its marked continuation for strict expressions, with the original
manifold and the absolute normalization proved explicitly.

\section{Variational input and local cells}\label{sm:variational}

Before constructing the cells, we record exactly what is supplied by
the variational calculation. For an open expression $E$ with prescribed
output $b$, its \emph{profile} $f_E(b)$ is the maximum of its sum of
leaf and product terms over all its remaining variables.
We use the following conclusions of the strict-profile
companion \cite[Lemma~5.2, Theorem~6.4, Lemma~7.1 and
Proposition~7.2]{Newton}.
For each nonempty strict fixed-output domain, the maximum over the
internal wires is strict, unique and nondegenerate. Its profile $f_E(b)$
is real analytic on that domain. The analogous closed statement holds
on Definition~\ref{sm:strict}'s reduced affine constraint space.
The possible intervals of output values are denoted by $L,H,A$, as follows. The table gives the interval for a product of two given types:
\begin{equation}\label{sm:types}
 L=(0,1/2),\quad H=(1/2,1),\quad A=(0,1),\qquad
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
    &L&H&A\\\hline
 L&A&H&A\\ H&H&\varnothing&H\\ A&A&H&A
 \end{array}.
\end{equation}
Every point of each indicated interval is attained. A leaf has type
$L$; a turn exchanges $L,H$ and preserves $A$. The nonempty closing
pairs are $AA,AL,AH,LH$ and their reversals.

For an optimized output define
\begin{equation}\label{sm:profile-data}
 B=\cot\pi b,\quad \kap=e^{-f_E'(b)},\qquad
 X^2=\frac{1+B^2}{1+\kap},\quad Y^2=\kap X^2,
 \quad X,Y>0.
\end{equation}
Then $X^2+Y^2-B^2=1$, and the imported profile identities give
\begin{equation}\label{sm:profile-types}
 L:(B,X,Y)=(B,B,1),\qquad H:(B,X,Y)=(-Y,1,Y),
 \qquad A:-Y<B<X.
\end{equation}
A turn sends $(B,X,Y)$ to $(-B,Y,X)$. At a stationary product the
two children have a common $X$, and, writing $S=B_1+B_2>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{sm:profile-product}
       B_o=\frac{B_1B_2+X^2-1}{S},\qquad
       Y_o=\frac{Y_1Y_2}{S}.
\end{equation}
The number $\kap$ will be the cross ratio of the quadrilateral left
exposed by the cell assembly. Thus these profile identities control
the shape of the next gluing face. The exposed quadrilateral is an
internal cut face in the construction; the physical boundary of a
four-ended tangle is a different surface.
