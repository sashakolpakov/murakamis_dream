\title{Asymptotics of signed Newton coefficients near the quarter index}
\author{Alexander Kolpakov}\date{25 November 2026}
\begin{document}\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study a finite coefficient in a root-of-unity tangle evaluation
whose normalized values satisfy a second-order recurrence. At one
quarter of the level, the two characteristic roots have equal modulus
but are distinct. We prove that the coefficient has one nonzero
holomorphic asymptotic expression on a fixed neighborhood extending
across this point. The proof uses the finite sum at its upper endpoint
to determine the initial values of both recurrence components. An
analytic change of basis makes their coupling exponentially small,
and an integral estimate shows that neither the second component nor
this small coupling changes the leading term over the chosen interval.
We then interpolate the selected product with exponentially small
error on the integer grid. This fixes its action and amplitude,
including their absolute normalization at both level parities. We also
give a local Gaussian formula for finite tangle sums made from these
coefficients. Contributions outside that local summation window are
not estimated here.
\end{abstract}

\section{The finite coefficient and the question}\label{qi:intro}

Rigorous asymptotics for finite-order recurrences form an established discrete WKB theory; see Costin and Costin \cite{CostinCostin} for a general treatment with smooth coefficients. We give the analytic change of basis used here directly. The additional
step is to determine which solution describes the given finite sum:
we do this from its endpoint values and retain the resulting two
components throughout the transport argument.

Equal characteristic moduli do not by themselves determine an asymptotic of a finite recurrence. Below the equality point a locally smaller branch can become larger; an initially small component can then grow. We prove a local statement in which the coefficient at a higher index, the accumulated ratio of the two
solutions, and the error caused by their coupling are controlled
together. Comparing only the eigenvalue moduli at the target index
would not give this information.

Fix an integer $N\ge2$, and put
\[
 d=N-1,\quad M=\lfloor d/2\rfloor,\quad q=e^{\pi\ii/N},
 \quad Q=q^2,\quad \kappa=q^{d^2/2},\quad
 F_j=(Q;Q)_j=\prod_{a=1}^j(1-Q^a).
\]
Complex powers of $q,Q$ mean their displayed exponential lifts.
Let $G_N$ be the holomorphic interpolation of the sine factorial
constructed in \cite[Lemma 2.1]{Conway}. We use its exact shift
identity, its specified normalization on integer arguments, and its
uniform holomorphic bulk expansion. These properties, including the
choice of logarithm in that expansion, are the input from that lemma.
Set
\[
 \widehat F_N(z)=e^{\pi\ii z(z+1)/(2N)-\pi\ii z/2}G_N(z).
\]
Thus $G_N(j)=\prod_{a=1}^j2\sin(\pi a/N)$ and
$\widehat F_N(z+1)=(1-Q^{z+1})\widehat F_N(z)$ exactly.
The discrete index is $r$, with scaled value $x=r/N$. The color
deficit is $h=Nt$; physical deficits are integers, but $t$ will also
be allowed to vary in a fixed complex disk about zero. For $r/N$
near $1/4$, define
\begin{align}
 A_{h,r}
 &=\sum_{v=r}^{M}
 \frac{Q^{-vr}F_{2v}}
 {F_v\widehat F_N(v-h)\widehat F_N(v+h)F_{v-r}},\notag\\
 D_{h,r}
 &=\frac{Q^{-r^2}F_{2r}}
 {F_r\widehat F_N(r-h)\widehat F_N(r+h)},\qquad
 U_{h,r}=A_{h,r}/D_{h,r}.\label{qi:source}
\end{align}
Only the two color-shifted factorials are continued. For physical integer $0\le h\le r$ this is the normalization of the signed Newton coefficient obtained from the circle and crossing identities of \cite[Proposition 4.1 and equation (4.8)]{Newton}. Section~\ref{qi:signed} writes that coefficient explicitly and checks the normalization. For a fixed small complex disk in $t$, all factorial arguments in \eqref{qi:source} remain regular. Hence the finite coefficient is holomorphic and even in $t$.

Here is its exact recurrence. With $e=1/N$, $x=r/N$, $Y=e^{2\pi\ii x}$, $H=e^{2\pi\ii t}$ and
$\operatorname{den}(x)=(1-QY/H)(1-QYH)$,
\begin{align}
 U_r&=p(x,t,e)U_{r+1}+q_c(x,t,e)U_{r+2},\label{qi:recurrence}\\
 p&=-\frac{Q^{-2}Y^{-1}[(H+H^{-1})Q^3Y^2+Q+1]}
                {\operatorname{den}(x)},\notag\\
 q_c&=-\frac{Q^{-4}Y^{-2}(1-Q^2Y)(1-Q^3Y^2)(1+Q^2Y)}
                {\operatorname{den}(x)\operatorname{den}(x+e)}.\notag
\end{align}
The upper endpoint is
\begin{equation}\label{qi:wall}
 U_M=1,\qquad q_c((M-1)/N,t,1/N)=0,\qquad U_{M-1}=p((M-1)/N,t,1/N).
\end{equation}
For odd $N$ the factor $1-Q^3Y^2$ vanishes in that row; for even $N$ the factor is $1+Q^2Y$. No value $D_{h,M+1}$ is introduced.

Here is a direct verification at complex color. Let $a_v$ be the
$v$th summand defining $A_{h,r}$ and write $z=Q^v$. Then
\[
 \frac{a_{v+1}}{a_v}
 =\frac{Y^{-1}(1-Qz^2)(1+Qz)}
 {(1-Qz/H)(1-QHz)(1-Qz/Y)}.
\]
For $b(z)=(1-z/H)(1-Hz)(1-z/Y)/z^2$, expansion of
$b(Qz)a_{v+1}/a_v-b(z)$ gives
\[
 \left(\frac1{YQ^2}-1\right)z^{-2}
 +\left(\frac1{YQ}+H+H^{-1}+\frac1Y\right)z^{-1}
 -\frac1{YQ}-1-\frac{H+H^{-1}}Y.
\]
Summing this forward difference kills the lower endpoint, where
$z=Y$, and the upper endpoint, whose numerator contains $F_{2M+2}=0$.
The shift $r\mapsto r+1$ multiplies $a_v$ by
$z^{-1}-Y^{-1}$. Expressing the two displayed moments through the
shifted sums gives
\begin{multline}\label{qi:unnormalized-recurrence}
 QY^2(1-Q^2Y)A_{h,r+2}
 +Y[(H+H^{-1})Q^3Y^2+Q+1]A_{h,r+1}\\
 -Q(QY+1)(QY^2-1)A_{h,r}=0.
\end{multline}
Division by the nonzero normalizing factors $D_{h,r}$ proves
\eqref{qi:recurrence}. The calculation uses the exact factorial shift,
so it proves the identity for complex $h$ as well as for integer colors.

Let $p_0=p(x,t,0)$ and $q_0=q_c(x,t,0)$, and label the two characteristic roots of
$\mu^2-p_0\mu-q_0=0$ by
\begin{equation}\label{qi:roots}
 \mu_s(x,0)=-e^{-2\pi\ii x},\qquad
 \mu_f(x,0)=e^{-2\pi\ii x}\cot^2(\pi x).
\end{equation}
The subscript $s$ labels the branch selected by the upper endpoint;
$f$ labels the other branch. These labels are continued analytically
and are not reassigned when their moduli change order. At
$x_0=1/4$ the roots are $\ii$ and $-\ii$. They extend as distinct nonzero holomorphic functions of $(x,t)$ on a fixed neighborhood.
Use the logarithm of $\mu_s$ continued from its value zero at
$(x,t)=(1/2,0)$; in fact $\mu_s(1/2,t)=1$ for all small $t$.
Define, by continuation from the high-index domain,
\begin{align}
 S(x,t)&=\int_x^{1/2}\Log\mu_s(u,t)\dd u,\notag\\
 \nu(x,t)&=
 \frac{p_1+q_1/\mu_s-q_0(\partial_x\mu_s)/\mu_s^2}
                   {1+q_0/\mu_s^2},\notag\\
 C(x,t)&=\sqrt{\mu_s(x,t)}
       \exp\!\int_x^{1/2}\frac{\nu(u,t)}{\mu_s(u,t)}\dd u.\label{qi:SC}
\end{align}
Here $p_1=\partial_ep(x,t,0)$ and $q_1=\partial_eq_c(x,t,0)$.
The square root is one at $1/2$. The denominator
$1-\mu_f/\mu_s$ is nonzero, including at $x=1/4$.

\begin{theorem}[The finite coefficient near the quarter index]\label{qi:main}
There are fixed constants $h_0,a_0,t_0,c>0$ and, for all sufficiently large $N$ of either parity, a nonzero holomorphic function $I_N(x,t)$ on
\[
 |\Re x-1/4|<h_0,\qquad |\Im x|<a_0,\qquad |t|<t_0,
\]
such that on a smaller real index interval, uniformly for all physical integers $r$ there and all complex $|t|<t_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{qi:grid}
 U_{Nt,r}=I_N(r/N,t)(1+O(e^{-cN})).
\end{equation}
On a smaller fixed joint complex domain,
\begin{equation}\label{qi:complex}
 I_N(x,t)=C(x,t)e^{NS(x,t)}(1+O(N^{-1})).
\end{equation}
Every fixed derivative of the normalized remainder satisfies the same bound on smaller domains. The first estimate concerns the original finite coefficient on its integer index set. The second concerns the explicitly constructed holomorphic interpolation. All constants are independent of $N$.
\end{theorem}

Lemma~\ref{qi:high-index} proves the needed starting value directly
from the upper endpoint. Above quarter the unwanted ratio has modulus
strictly less than one. That proof cannot be applied below quarter,
where $|\mu_f/\mu_s|$ can exceed one. The matrix change of basis and
accumulated-ratio estimate in
Sections~\ref{qi:diagonalization}--\ref{qi:source-proof} supply the
additional control needed to cross the equality point.
