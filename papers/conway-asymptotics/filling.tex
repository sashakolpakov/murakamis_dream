\section{Performing the twists and identifying the volume}\label{ca:filling}

The filled diagram has the crossing power $R^{n_\ell}$ at disk
$\ell$, with $n_\ell=\epsilon_\ell-2p_\ell$. We expand that entire
power in ordinary colored circles. This permits us to use the pure
source of Theorem~\ref{ca:strict-pure} while evaluating exactly the
same knot. The calculation includes positive and negative, odd and
even powers.

\subsection{Fourier orthogonality with repeated-root derivatives}

Let $T_N$ be the Chebyshev polynomial determined by
$T_N(\cos\theta)=\cos(N\theta)$. The monic polynomial
$\mathscr P_N(\sigma)=2T_N(\sigma/2)+2$ has roots $c_k$, where
\[
 w_k=q^{2k+1},\qquad c_k=w_k+w_k^{-1},\qquad 0\le k\le d.
\]
The crossing is represented in the polynomial quotient
$\mathbb C[\sigma]/(\mathscr P_N)$. This retains the multiplicities
of the roots. For instance, at $N=3$,
$\mathscr P_3(\sigma)=(\sigma-1)^2(\sigma+2)$. A class is
specified by $f(1),f'(1),f(-2)$, rather than just its values at the
two distinct roots. Algebraically the component at the double root
contains an element $u$ with $u^2=0$, and
$f(1+u)=f(1)+f'(1)u$. Dropping the derivative would discard part
of the crossing operator.

In this quotient the normalized crossing $r_N=\kappa^{-1}R$ has
\begin{equation}\label{ca:crossing-data}
 r_N(c_k)=(-1)^kq^{k(k+1)},\qquad
 (w\partial_w)r_N(w+w^{-1})\big|_{w=w_k}
      =-\tfrac12(d-2k)r_N(c_k).
\end{equation}
At a double root the value and derivative are both needed to specify
a polynomial modulo $\mathscr P_N$. The second formula records that
derivative in the coordinate $w$: it equals
$(w_k-w_k^{-1})r_N'(c_k)$, and $w_k-w_k^{-1}\ne0$ at a
double root. At the odd-level simple root
$w_k=-1$ it is zero and imposes no extra condition. These data are
proved in \cite[Proposition 5.1]{Finite} and
\cite[equation (4.8)]{Newton}. Since every value is nonzero, the
crossing is invertible in the quotient and every integer power is
defined.

Let $f_{N,n}$ be the unique representative of degree at most $d$ of $r_N^n$.
Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 h_k=(-1)^{nk}q^{nk(k+1)},\qquad
 d_k=-\frac n2(d-2k)h_k,\\
 H_a=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^d h_kw_k^{-a},\qquad
 D_a=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^d d_kw_k^{-a}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Here $d_k$ is the value of $(w\partial_w)r_N^n$ at $w_k$; the
unsubscripted $d=N-1$ still denotes the level shift. All sums in this
subsection run from $0$ to $N-1$ unless another range is shown.
For $a\ge1$, define the polynomial $\mathsf C_a$ by
$\mathsf C_a(w+w^{-1})=w^a+w^{-a}$, and write
$f_{N,n}=a_0+\sum_{a=1}^d a_a\mathsf C_a$.
These $a_a$ are scalar polynomial coefficients, distinct from the
function $a_0(t)$ in Theorem~\ref{ca:strict-pure}. Discrete Fourier inversion on $w_k^N=-1$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{ca:Hermite-inverse}
 a_0=H_0,\qquad
 a_a=\frac{D_a+(N-a)H_a}{N}\quad(1\le a<N).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the Fourier transforms of the value and derivative equations
are
$H_a=a_a-a_{N-a}$ and
$D_a=a\,a_a+(N-a)a_{N-a}$. When $N$ is even and $a=N/2$, the first equation is identically zero and the second still recovers the coefficient. At odd $N$ the simple node has only its value. These equations therefore recover the whole quotient, not just its distinct-root values.

Let $U_j$ be the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind,
$U_j(\cos\theta)=\sin((j+1)\theta)/\sin\theta$, and put $U_{-1}=0$.
Since $\mathsf C_a=U_a(\sigma/2)-U_{a-2}(\sigma/2)$ for $a\ge1$,
set $a_N=a_{N+1}=0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{ca:beta}
 \beta_j^{n/2}=(-1)^j(a_j-a_{j+2}),\qquad0\le j<N.
\end{equation}
The superscript $n/2$ is a label, rather than an exponent; odd $n$
is allowed. A circle of color $j+1$ surrounding the two strands
represents $L_{j+1}=(-1)^jU_j(\sigma/2)$ by
\cite[Proposition 4.1]{Newton}. Consequently the operator identity is
\begin{equation}\label{ca:crossing-split}
 R^n=\kappa^n\sum_{j=0}^{d}\beta_j^{n/2}L_{j+1},
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^{d}|\beta_j^{n/2}|
       \le 2\sqrt N(1+|n|/\sqrt{12}).
\end{equation}
The norm bound follows from the ordinary Hermitian inner product on
arrays in $\mathbb C^N$. It does not require a positive inner product
for the tangle representation. For any real shift $\alpha$,
put $u_k=e^{2\pi\ii(k+\alpha)/N}$. The finite character sum gives
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}u_k^{a-b}=\mathbf1_{a=b}
                    \qquad(0\le a,b<N).
\]
Thus every complex array $(f_k)_{k=0}^{N-1}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{ca:shifted-parseval}
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat f_a&=\frac1N\sum_k f_ku_k^{-a},&
 f_k&=\sum_a\widehat f_a u_k^a,\\
 \sum_a|\widehat f_a|^2&=\frac1N\sum_k|f_k|^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both identities follow by expanding the sums and cancelling all unequal
characters. Taking $\alpha=1/2$ gives $u_k=w_k$, and therefore
\[
 \|H\|_2^2=1,\qquad
 \|D\|_2^2=\frac{n^2}{4N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(d-2k)^2
           =\frac{n^2(N^2-1)}{12}.
\]
In \eqref{ca:Hermite-inverse} the $H$ part is a diagonal
contraction, while the $D$ part is divided by $N$ and its zeroth
coordinate is omitted. Thus
$\|a\|_2\le1+|n|/\sqrt{12}$. Here $\|a\|_2^2=\sum_{j=0}^d|a_j|^2$. The truncated shift
$(a_j)\mapsto(a_{j+2})$ has operator norm at most one, so
$\|\beta\|_2\le2\|a\|_2$; Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\eqref{ca:crossing-split}. This includes the derivative data and
both parities. The identity is then inserted at each disk in the
diagram, without interchanging distinct boxes.

\subsection{The midpoint identity and all its Fourier modes}

Let $C_N(t)$ be the restricted, even holomorphic sum of
Theorem~\ref{ca:strict-pure}, with one variable for the moment, and let
$\mathcal F_N(z)=\sum_{|h|\le H_N^{\rm cut}}C_N(h/N)e^{2\pi\ii hz}$.
Put
\[
 z_k=\frac{k+1/2}{N}-\frac12,\qquad
 U_N(z)=e^{\pi\ii nNz^2}\sinh(2\pi\ii z)\mathcal F_N(z).
\]
On the actual midpoint grid, for every integer $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{ca:chirp}
 h_k=\kappa^{-n/2}e^{\pi\ii nNz_k^2},\qquad
 d_k=nNz_kh_k.
\end{equation}
For example, the first equality follows by writing
$(-1)^k=e^{-\pi\ii k}$ at integer $k$ and using
$-k+k(k+1)/N=Nz_k^2-d^2/(4N)$. This fixes the fractional power
$\kappa^{n/2}=e^{\pi\ii n d^2/(4N)}$ without a branch convention.

Substitute \eqref{ca:chirp} into \eqref{ca:Hermite-inverse}--\eqref{ca:beta}. Pairing the $h$ and $-h$ terms gives
\begin{equation}\label{ca:midpoint}
 \sum_{h=0}^{H_N^{\rm cut}}\beta_{N-h-1}^{n/2}C_N(h/N)
 =\frac{(-1)^d\kappa^{-n/2}}{2\pi\ii N^2}
      \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}U_N'(z_k).
\end{equation}
To see the endpoint multiplicity directly, for $h\ge1$ the summand before this pairing is
\[
 \frac{2(-1)^d\kappa^{-n/2}}{N^2}
 \sum_k e^{\pi\ii nNz_k^2+2\pi\ii h z_k}
 \{(nNz_k+h)\sinh(2\pi\ii z_k)+\cosh(2\pi\ii z_k)\}.
\]
For $h=0$ it is half this expression. The symmetric Fourier polynomial has two terms at $h\ne0$ and one at zero, exactly as required by \eqref{ca:midpoint}. The term $nNz_k$ comes from the derivative data of the crossing.
It is present in the derivative of the Gaussian in $U_N$, so the
midpoint identity retains it automatically.

Both endpoint values of $U_N$ vanish, and
$U_N'(1/2)=U_N'(-1/2)$. Its periodically extended derivative is continuous and piecewise smooth. Two integrations by parts give an absolutely summable Fourier series for each fixed $N$. Midpoint selection and a further integration by parts yield
\begin{equation}\label{ca:charge-one}
 (-1)^d\kappa^{-n/2}
 \sum_{m\ne0}(-1)^{md}m
 \int_{-1/2}^{1/2}
 e^{\pi\ii nNz^2-2\pi\ii mNz}
 \sinh(2\pi\ii z)\mathcal F_N(z)\dd z.
\end{equation}
The integers $m$ here are Fourier indices, also called charges.
The index zero is absent because the endpoint values of $U_N$ vanish.
The series converges at each fixed $N$. A uniform bound as $N$ grows
requires the estimates in \cite{Filling}, not merely this convergence.

Apply this identity in all $b$ color variables and restore
$\kappa^{\sum n_\ell}$ from \eqref{ca:crossing-split}. Change $m_\ell$ to $-\ell_\ell$ and use $\sinh(2\pi\ii z)=\ii\sin(2\pi z)$. The resulting filled evaluation of the restricted sum is
\begin{equation}\label{ca:generic-period}
 (-1)^{bd}\mathcal I_N(\mathbf n),
\end{equation}
where, with $I=[-1/2,1/2]$,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_N(\mathbf n)
 ={}&\kappa^{\frac12\sum n_\ell}(-\ii)^b
 \sum_{\ell\in(\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\})^b}
       \left(\prod_j\ell_j\right)(-1)^{(N+1)\sum_j\ell_j}\notag\\
 &\quad\times\int_{I^b}\mathcal F_N(z)
       \prod_j\sin(2\pi z_j)
       e^{N\{\pi\ii\sum_jn_jz_j^2+
                         2\pi\ii\sum_j\ell_jz_j\}}\dd z.\label{ca:period}
\end{align}
This is precisely the normalization in \cite[equation (1.6)]{Filling}; the definition \eqref{ca:period} fixes it independently of that equation number.

Here is the elementary rate comparison used for the physical remainder.
\begin{lemma}[Subexponential costs]\label{ca:subexponential-cost}
Fix $\beta\in\mathbb R$. Suppose $B_N,C_N\ge0$, $E_N\in\mathbb C$
and, for every $\eta>0$, eventually
$B_N\le e^{N(\beta+\eta)}$ and $C_N\le e^{N\eta}$.
If $|E_N|\le C_NB_N$, then for every $\eta>0$ eventually
$|E_N|\le e^{N(\beta+\eta)}$.
The hypothesis on $C_N$ holds for any bound
$C_N\le C_0N^m$, with fixed $C_0\ge0$ and real $m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the two assumed bounds with $\eta/2$ and multiply them.
For $C_0>0$, $\log C_0+m\log N=o(N)$ proves the polynomial assertion.
For $C_0=0$ the cost vanishes. Neither step divides by a source
budget or takes its logarithm, so zero budgets cause no exception.
\end{proof}

Here is the error array to which the lemma applies. If
$j\in\{0,\ldots,d\}^b$, the actual circle colors are $j_\ell+1$
and their deficits are $h_\ell=d-j_\ell$. Define
\[
 D_N(j)=\mathcal A_N(\mathbf h)-\widetilde C_N(\mathbf h),\qquad
 \widetilde C_N(\mathbf h)=
 \begin{cases}
 C_N(\mathbf h/N),&0\le h_\ell\le H_N^{\rm cut}\text{ for all }\ell,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Choose $\gamma_*>0$ smaller than both the local source-error gap
and the gap for deficits outside this cube. The latter follows from
Theorem~\ref{ca:strict-pure}, with the cutoff reduced if necessary.
Then $|D_N(j)|\le B_N=e^{N(F_0-\gamma_*)}$ for all large $N$. After removing the common
phases of modulus one, its filled error is the coherent sum
\begin{equation}\label{ca:coherent-error}
\begin{aligned}
 E_N&=\sum_j\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^b
                   \beta_{j_\ell}^{n_\ell/2}\right)D_N(j),\\
 |E_N|&\le B_N\prod_{\ell=1}^b
                   \sum_{k=0}^d|\beta_k^{n_\ell/2}|.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second line follows by the triangle inequality and factoring the
finite sum of products. The source bound is shared by all color tuples;
no color is chosen independently before closing the diagram.

Combining \eqref{ca:coherent-error} with the coefficient bound
\eqref{ca:crossing-split}, the replacement of the full evaluation
by the restricted sum changes its filled value by at most
\begin{equation}\label{ca:filled-error}
 \prod_j2\sqrt N(1+|n_j|/\sqrt{12})\ e^{N(F_0-\gamma_*)}
\end{equation}
The error array is evaluated only at its nonnegative integer deficits.
Its proof uses no even extension of the full source. Evenness has been
used only to form $\mathcal F_N$ from $C_N$. For fixed $\mathbf n$,
the displayed prefactor is polynomial in $N$, and hence does not alter
the strict exponential gap.

\subsection{The nonzero leading term of the filled invariant}

Apply \cite[Theorem 1.1]{Filling} to $\mathcal F_N$.
The trigonometric polynomial called $C_N$ in that paper is
$\mathcal F_N$ here; it is not the earlier sum over internal indices.
Its separate degree is at most $d$. Proposition~\ref{ca:Fourier-theorem}
verifies the local expansion and the bound on the rest of the torus.
The remaining translation of notation is
\[
 \alpha=3/2+b/2,\qquad \varepsilon_N=1,\qquad
 A_N=\Psi_0,\qquad a_N=B,\qquad c_j=\pi/X_j.
\]
The phase $\Psi_0$ is real on the real polydisk, and its Hessian
is $-2\diag(c_1,\ldots,c_b)$ as required. The global bound holds
on a fixed complex strip, with polynomial factors absorbed into half
its exponential gap. The cited theorem estimates the whole series
\eqref{ca:period}. In particular, it controls the infinitely many
Fourier indices, pairs their endpoint contributions before estimating
them, and shows that the contributions with all $|\ell_j|=1$
add with a nonzero coefficient.

Its nonzero leading expression, multiplied by \eqref{ca:generic-period}, has parity factor
$(-1)^{bd+(N+1)b}=1$. Its power of $N$ is
$\alpha-b/2=3/2$. The local phase and amplitude are independent of $N$ here, so the theorem's finite critical action is exactly $S_{\mathbf n}$, not merely a sequence converging to it. It gives
\eqref{ca:asymptotic}--\eqref{ca:D} for the restricted sum. Also
$F_0-\Re S_{\mathbf n}=O(\sum n_j^{-2})$ with no ratio constraint.
Increase a fixed threshold on the independent $|n_j|$ until this loss is smaller than $\gamma_*/2$. Then \eqref{ca:filled-error} is exponentially below the nonzero leading term. Thus it is absorbed into the relative $O_{\mathbf p}(N^{-1})$
error, giving the same asymptotic for the full knot evaluation.

Nonvanishing is worth making explicit. The amplitude $B$ is nonzero
near zero. If a coordinate of $z_{\mathbf n}$ were zero, separate
evenness of $\Psi_0$ would make the corresponding derivative of
$T_{\mathbf n}$ equal to $2\pi\ii$, contrary to criticality.
The coordinates are small, so none is another zero of
$\sin(2\pi z_j)$. The Gaussian Hessian is nonsingular by the filling
theorem. Every factor in \eqref{ca:D} is therefore nonzero.

\subsection{The critical action is the knot-complement volume}

It remains to identify this action with the marked geometric filling.
We use the classical shear defined at every strict leaf. For $0<x<1/2$ and $v=g_t(x,t)$ put
\[
 \chi=t-\frac{\epsilon}{2\pi\ii}v,\qquad
 f_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(x,\chi)
        =g(x,t)-\frac{\epsilon}{4\pi\ii}v^2.
\]
At zero its change of parameter has derivative
$1-\epsilon\ii\cot(\pi x)\ne0$, so it is locally invertible.
Direct differentiation gives
$\dd f_\epsilon^{\rm cl}=g_x\,\dd x+v\,\dd\chi$.
These are exactly the mixed-pyramid potential and the
primitive retained-half-twist shear of
\cite[Section 6, equations (6.1)--(6.5)]{StrictGeometry};
the normalization is $J(0)=0$ and $f_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(x,0)=g(x,0)$.
Their construction is classical and includes $x=1/4$.

Sum these functions and the unchanged gate actions and eliminate
the stationary wires. Denote the resulting classical action by
$\Phi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}$. The differential just computed shows that
its wire derivatives are precisely the pure wire derivatives at
the changed colors. Hence the pure stationary wires are also
stationary for this sheared action. Its Hessian in those wires
is invertible near zero, so they are the same local branch.
The envelope rule and the local formula give the exact identities
\begin{align}
 \chi_j&=t_j-\frac{\epsilon_j}{2\pi\ii}
                             \partial_{t_j}\Phi_0(t),\notag\\
 \Phi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(\chi)
   &=\Phi_0(t)
       -\sum_j\frac{\epsilon_j}{4\pi\ii}
                         [\partial_{t_j}\Phi_0(t)]^2.\label{ca:global-shear}
\end{align}
The coordinate change is locally invertible, since
\[
 D_t\chi(0)=\diag(1-\epsilon_j\ii X_j)_{j=1}^b
\]
has no zero diagonal entry. The right side of
\eqref{ca:global-shear} is an identity of functions, not merely of their
derivatives. It fixes the additive constant and every
color-dependent term. By its construction it is the cell potential
of \cite[Theorem 1.3]{StrictGeometry}, for the same original
expression, disk framings and retained half twists.

Define the Legendre transform of the sheared action in the same way
as before: choose $\chi(z)$ satisfying
$\nabla_\chi\Phi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(\chi)+2\pi\ii z=0$, and put
$\Psi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(z)=\Phi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(\chi(z))+
2\pi\ii\chi(z)\cdot z$. Its derivative variable is unchanged by
the shear. Substituting \eqref{ca:global-shear} gives
\begin{equation}\label{ca:Legendre-shear}
 \Psi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(z)=\Psi_0(z)+\pi\ii\sum_j\epsilon_jz_j^2,
 \qquad \chi=t+\epsilon z,
 \quad \nabla\Phi_0(t)=-2\pi\ii z.
\end{equation}
Let $z=z_{\mathbf n}$ be the charge-one critical point. Its equation is
$\xi(z)+\mathbf n z+\mathbf1=0$, componentwise. By evenness put
$z_g=-z$ and $t_g=-\xi(z)$; then
$t_g=\mathbf1-\mathbf n z_g$. Therefore
\[
 \chi=t_g+\epsilon z_g=\mathbf1+2\mathbf p z_g.
\]
Substitution into the Legendre expression gives exactly
\begin{align}
 S_{\mathbf n}
 &=\Psi_0(z_g)+\pi\ii\sum_jn_j z_{g,j}^2
                        -2\pi\ii\sum_j z_{g,j}\notag\\
 &=\Phi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}(\chi)
       +\frac{\pi\ii}{2}\sum_j\frac{(\chi_j-1)^2}{p_j}.
 \label{ca:classical-action}
\end{align}
For clarity, the peripheral variables in the geometry theorem are
logarithms of the complex dilation multipliers of the cusp
holonomies along $\mu_j$ and $\lambda_j$. A similarity acts as
$u\mapsto a u+b$ on a horospherical plane, and its similarity
logarithm means a chosen branch of $\log a$. Both branches vanish
at the complete metric. With the primitive meridian and disk-longitude
basis fixed in the introduction, those logarithms are
\[
 U_j=-2\pi\ii\chi_j,\qquad
 V_j=-2\partial_j\Phi_\epsilon^{\rm cl}
       =4\pi\ii z_{g,j}.
\]
Consequently $U_j+p_jV_j=-2\pi\ii$ exactly, with the integer
$p_j$ that specifies the filling. This is one full turn along the
primitive filled slope; the sign is fixed by the orientation of our
meridian. These equations also show that $\chi$ is critical
for the final expression in \eqref{ca:classical-action}.
The critical-point equations give
$z_{\mathbf n,j}=O(|n_j|^{-1})$, uniformly as the independent
$|n_j|$ become large. Since $\xi(z)=O(\|z\|)$, the formula $\chi=t_g+\epsilon z_g$
shows that $\chi$ tends to zero independently of the ratios
of the filling integers. Local uniqueness in
\cite[Theorem 1.3]{StrictGeometry} therefore identifies it with
the primitive filling branch through the oriented complete metric.
No uniqueness of the full character variety is used.
The geometry theorem proves that this branch satisfies the
representation relations of the augmented link and extends smoothly
over its filled auxiliary cusps. The main cusps remain complete.
Its comparison of the oriented cells with the metric fundamental
chain fixes the volume itself, including the additive constant. Its conclusion is the absolute equality
$\Vol(S^3\setminus K_{\epsilon,\mathbf p})
=2\pi\Re S_{\mathbf n}$.
The knot test in Section~\ref{ca:intro} is what makes this
filled main link a knot.

Take $P_0$ larger than the geometric threshold and large enough that
$|n_j|=|\epsilon_j-2p_j|$ exceeds the source and whole-period thresholds. The equality holds for every fixed signed tuple beyond it. Finally the framing factor and all powers of $\kappa$ have modulus one; the Gaussian coefficient is nonzero. Taking logarithms proves \eqref{ca:volume} and completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{ca:main}.
