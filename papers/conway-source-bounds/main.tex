\title{Spectral estimates for Conway tangle sources}

\begin{abstract}
We bound the exact colored source of a fixed strict Conway composition,
uniformly over its physical circle colors, retained half twists and all
sufficiently large integer levels. The upper exponential rate is the
maximum of its positive complete-color action. A color a fixed fraction
below complete color gives a strict loss, as does the entire difference
between the full source and its selected core. The proof retains the
full root algebra, including first derivatives at repeated spectral
roots. An exact Fourier formula controls a character before a turn;
a positive comparison with shared indices controls regions without a
character. Two supporting-plane inequalities, proved by outward rational
arithmetic, propagate this comparison. We also prove the uniform signed
recurrence estimates for the local core coefficients. The result includes
strict compositions with an elementary closing leaf and the isolated
three-leaf closure. A separate corollary treats the strict two-leaf
closure. These are source bounds before filling, not a filled
invariant or volume asymptotic.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source and the result}\label{cs:intro}

A circle surrounding two strands gives a polynomial operator before it
is filled by twists. Stacking tangles multiplies these operators, a
quarter turn acts by a specified linear map, and cylindrical closure
takes a specified linear functional. The spectral polynomial has
repeated roots: values alone do not determine the operator. Its first
derivatives are needed both in the original finite algebra and in the
cancellation that bounds a small physical color.

Separate absolute bounds at every intermediate operation are inadequate.
A turn can have an exponential norm cost which need not be the intrinsic
rate of that subtree. We instead alternate two estimates. A character
followed by a turn admits a Fourier estimate with its exact endpoint
currents. A region without a character admits a positive comparison
whose shared indices determine the correct cost. The variational
identities for those two operations retain their additive constants.

\subsection{Constructions and their complete action}
The diagrams use ordered products of two-strand boxes, their clockwise
quarter turns and cylindrical closure. Each elementary box is
\(h_i=\Turn(R^{\epsilon_i}L_{j_i})\), where \(L_{j_i}\) is its circle
operator, \(1\le j_i\le N\), and \(\epsilon_i\in\{0,1,-1\}\) specifies
no retained crossing or one crossing of the indicated sign. Every box
occurs once. The construction and its number \(H\ge3\) of boxes are
fixed before the level \(N\) tends to infinity. A physical color is an
integer dimension; it is not a continuous Fourier parameter.

Strictness is a finite condition on this syntax. The types \(L,H,A\)
denote output intervals \((0,1/2),(1/2,1),(0,1)\), respectively.
An elementary box has type \(L\). A turn interchanges \(L,H\) and
preserves \(A\). With either input order the product table is
\[
 LL,LA,AA\longmapsto A,\qquad
 LH,HA\longmapsto H,\qquad HH\longmapsto\varnothing.
\]
At closure the allowed pairs are \(AA,AL,AH,LH\) and their reversals.
A presentation passing this test is called \emph{strict}. The empty
type means absence of a strict real state, not necessarily a zero
finite operator. The type letter \(H\) is unrelated to the box count.

Write \(Z_N(\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\epsilon)\) for the original scalar,
with the single reduced cut and normalization of \cite{Newton}.
Section~\ref{cs:finite} specifies the finite operators and each exact
core--alias split. Let \(Z_N^{\core}\) be the same contraction with
every box replaced by its core. Thus these are also the full and core
sources formerly denoted by \(\mathcal A_N,\mathcal C_N\).
Let \(P\) be the limiting pure state polytope, retaining every shared
wire index and the turn and closing relations. The closing product has
coefficient one. With the explicit continuous functions \(g,m\) in
\eqref{cs:g}--\eqref{cs:m}, its complete action is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:F0}
 F_0=\max_{y\in P}
 \left\{\sum_{i=1}^{H}g(a_i(y))+
             \sum_{v=1}^{H-2}m(x_v(y),y_v(y),z_v(y))\right\}.
\end{equation}
The strict profile theorem \cite{Profiles} gives a unique interior
maximum on the primitive state space. Its value is also the rate of
\(Z_N(\mathbf N,\mathbf0)\), which is positive for every sufficiently
large integer \(N\).

\begin{theorem}[Whole-source and color bounds]\label{cs:main}
Fix a strict presentation with \(H\ge3\). Uniformly over all its
physical colors and retained signs,
\begin{equation}\label{cs:whole}
 |Z_N(\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\epsilon)|\le\exp\{N(F_0+o(1))\}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed \(\delta>0\) there are \(\gamma_\delta>0,N_\delta\)
such that
\begin{equation}\label{cs:color-gap}
 \max_i(N-j_i)\ge\delta N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |Z_N(\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\epsilon)|
 \le\exp\{N(F_0-\gamma_\delta)\},\qquad N\ge N_\delta.
\end{equation}
There are also \(\gamma>0,N_0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{cs:alias-gap}
 |Z_N-Z_N^{\core}|\le\exp\{N(F_0-\gamma)\},\qquad N\ge N_0.
\end{equation}
All sufficiently large integer levels are included, with no parity or
primality restriction. Constants depend on the fixed presentation;
no bound for constructions growing with \(N\) is asserted.
\end{theorem}

This includes the earlier completed-root grammar: start with a product
of two elementary boxes, allow turns, products of completed boxes and
single-box attachments, and close two completed boxes. The strict
class also allows an elementary closing leaf. Corollary~\ref{cs:two-leaf-source}
handles the strict two-leaf closure separately, with rate \(2G_0\),
where \(G_0=g(1/4)\). The final alias gap
follows from the full physical-color bound, not by identifying high
Newton indices with complementary colors. A crossing can mix both
Newton ranges.

\subsection{The proof and its inputs}
The exact finite quotient, turn, pairing, product and circle formulas
are \cite[Proposition 3.2, Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 4.1]{Newton}.
The crossing values and first derivatives are also stated in
\cite[Proposition 5.1]{Finite}. We prove the needed complete-node
conditioning directly in Section~\ref{cs:conditioning}, including
all high-index normals. No moving-meridian or circle-tree estimate is
an input.

Section~\ref{cs:local} retains the local signed recurrence estimate for
applications that require individual core coefficients. It is independent
of the whole-source proof below, and asserts no polynomial error at
colliding roots.
Sections~\ref{cs:two-leaf}--\ref{cs:attachment} prove two scalar
supporting-plane inequalities; Section~\ref{cs:arithmetic} supplies
their full rational certificates. Their parameter hypotheses are
stated explicitly. The comparison for arbitrary arrays in
Section~\ref{cs:probe} uses those scalar inequalities, not a
completed-root source theorem applied outside its grammar.

The finite strict-state, reduced-junction and pure boundary-profile
results of \cite{Profiles} identify the component decomposition,
stationary indices and absolute rate constants. The completed-root
boundary inequalities in \cite[Lemma 6.1]{Newton} are used only for
the completed pure references specified below. Neither companion's
quantum core asymptotic is an input. The Fourier and component proofs
in Sections~\ref{cs:fourier}--\ref{cs:assembly} then establish the
whole-source theorem, including the three-leaf reference at the
endpoint of the two-leaf comparison.

A later filling theorem still needs a normalized nonzero core
asymptotic, the exact oscillatory filling transformation and a marked
geometric identification of the selected action. None follows from
an upper source bound alone.

\section{Exact finite coefficients and their supports}\label{cs:finite}

Put $d=N-1$, $M=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$, $q=e^{\pi\ii/N}$ and $Q=q^2$. Throughout this paper
\[
 S_r=\prod_{a=1}^r2\sin(\pi a/N),\qquad S_0=1,
 \qquad c_a=q^{2a+1}+q^{-2a-1}.
\]
Thus $S_r$ is a product, not a single sine. The root algebra is
\[
 \mathscr A_N=\mathbb C[z]/(P_N(z)),\qquad
 P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2=\prod_{a=0}^{d}(z-c_a),
\]
where $T_N(\cos t)=\cos Nt$. Repeated entries in the displayed product remain repeated roots. Equality in $\mathscr A_N$ preserves values and first derivatives at its double roots; at odd $N$ the simple root $-2$ also remains.
The normalized Newton basis is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:basis}
 e_r(z)=S_r^{-2}\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}(c_a-z),\qquad 0\le r\le d.
\end{equation}
The physical clockwise turn $\Turn$ and normalized cylindrical closure $\phi$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{cs:turn-trace}
 \Turn e_r=e_{d-r},\qquad \phi(e_i e_j)=\mathbf1_{i+j=d},\qquad
 \phi(F)=(-1)^dN^2[z^d](F\bmod P_N).
\end{equation}
Coefficient reversal makes the turn adjoint for this pairing:
\begin{equation}\label{cs:adjoint}
 \phi((\Turn F)G)=\phi(F\Turn G),\qquad
 \phi((\Turn F)(\Turn G))=\phi(FG).
\end{equation}
It is a linear involution, not an algebra homomorphism.
A stack is multiplication. For $i+j\le d$ its exact coefficients are
\begin{equation}\label{cs:product}
 e_i e_j=\sum_{k=\max(i,j)}^{i+j}
 \frac{S_{i+j}S_k}{S_iS_jS_{k-i}S_{k-j}S_{i+j-k}}e_k.
\end{equation}
For $i+j\ge N$ the product is zero in the full algebra, with multiplicities. All nonzero coefficients in \eqref{cs:product} are positive. We call one use of this multiplication a \emph{gate}. Its exact finite support is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:support}
 i,j\ge0,\qquad i+j\le d,\qquad \max(i,j)\le k\le i+j.
\end{equation}
At a closing gate the trace forces $i+j=d$, and its coefficient is one. This is an exact constraint before any scaling by $N$.

\subsection{The core and the alias}
Let $U_0(z/2)=1$, $U_1(z/2)=z$, and $U_{r+1}(z/2)=zU_r(z/2)-U_{r-1}(z/2)$. The ordinary circle is $L_j=(-1)^{j-1}U_{j-1}(z/2)$, and the elementary turned piece is $h_j=\Turn L_j$. Put $h_0=0$. For a high color $j=N-h$, $0\le h\le M$, its exact split is
\begin{align}
 h_{N-h}&=C_{N-h}+A_{N-h},\\
 C_{N-h}&=\sum_{v=h}^{M}
 \frac{N^2S_{2v}}{S_v^2S_{v-h}S_{v+h}}e_v,
 &A_{N-h}&=(-1)^N h_h.\label{cs:split}
\end{align}
For the remaining physical colors $1\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor$, set $C_j=0$ and $A_j=h_j$. The low-color formula is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:low-color}
 h_j=\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{j-1+r}
 \frac{S_r^2S_{j+r}}{S_{2r+1}S_{j-r-1}}e_{d-r}.
\end{equation}
There is no overlap or omitted color in this definition. The high and low \emph{colors} should be distinguished from the high and low \emph{Newton indices}: the high-color core has indices at most $M$, while an alias before the retained half twist in \eqref{cs:low-color} has indices $d-r\ge N/2$.

Let $\kappa=q^{d^2/2}$, with $q^a=e^{\pi\ii a/N}$ for real $a$. A retained half twist acts by
\begin{equation}\label{cs:half-twist}
 \Theta_\epsilon e_v=\kappa^\epsilon(-1)^v
 \sum_{r=0}^v q^{\epsilon v(r+1)}
 \frac{S_v}{S_rS_{v-r}}e_r,\qquad \epsilon=\pm1,
 \qquad \Theta_0=1.
\end{equation}
Define $C_j^\epsilon=\Theta_\epsilon C_j$ and $A_j^\epsilon=\Theta_\epsilon A_j$. The core still has indices at most $M$, but a half-twisted alias can occupy every index from $0$ to $d$. Thus the alias must be estimated on the entire gate support \eqref{cs:support}. The phases in \eqref{cs:half-twist} are retained until after the finite sum has been formed.

The transformation is exactly
\(\Theta_\epsilon F=\Turn(R^\epsilon\Turn F)\). The physical crossing has
\begin{equation}\label{cs:circle-crossing}
 R(c_k)=\kappa(-1)^kq^{k(k+1)},\qquad
 \frac{R'(c_k)}{R(c_k)}
 =-\frac{d-2k}{4\ii\sin((2k+1)\pi/N)}.
\end{equation}
The derivative is required only at double nodes; at the odd simple
node its value suffices. The inverse crossing is the inverse of this
class in the full quotient.

Equations \eqref{cs:turn-trace}--\eqref{cs:half-twist} are the physical identities imported above. Their derivation in \cite{Newton} is in the full quotient, not merely at the distinct roots. Consequently the estimates below concern the original normal data too; no semisimple replacement of a circle is made.

\subsection{Continuous actions and elementary bounds}\label{cs:actions}
Define the continuous function
\begin{equation}\label{cs:J}
 J(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi u)\dd u,\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $J(1-t)=-J(t)$ and $J(0)=J(1/2)=J(1)=0$. The leaf and gate actions are
\begin{align}
 g(x)&=J(2x)-4J(x),&0\le x\le\tfrac12,\label{cs:g}\\
 m(x,y,z)&=J(x+y)+J(z)-J(x)-J(y)\notag\\[-2mm]
 &\quad-J(z-x)-J(z-y)-J(x+y-z).\label{cs:m}
\end{align}
The full limiting gate polytope is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:D}
 D=\{x,y\ge0:\ \max(x,y)\le z\le x+y\le1\}.
\end{equation}
It includes inputs larger than $1/2$. The two majorant profiles will be
\begin{equation}\label{cs:profiles}
 \widetilde g(x)=g(\max(x,1/4))\quad(0\le x\le1/2),
 \qquad k(x)=-2J(x/2)\quad(0\le x\le1).
\end{equation}

For reference, the uniform sine-factorial estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:factorial}
 \log S_r=NJ(r/N)+O(\log N),\qquad 0\le r<N.
\end{equation}
To see endpoint uniformity, subtract $\log t+\log(1-t)$ from the integrand of $J$. The remainder extends with a bounded derivative to $[0,1]$, so its Riemann sum has bounded error. The two removed sums are logarithms of ordinary factorials; integral comparison, or Stirling's inequalities, bounds their errors by $O(\log N)$ uniformly in the endpoint. This includes $r=0$. Applied to \eqref{cs:product}, it gives the exponent $Nm(i/N,j/N,k/N)$ with a uniform $O(\log N)$ error.

All three profiles $\widetilde g,k,m$ are concave on their indicated domains. Indeed
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g'(x)&=2\log\cot\pi x,&g''(x)&<0,\\
 k'(x)&=-\log(2\sin(\pi x/2)),&
 k''(x)&=-\frac\pi2\cot(\pi x/2)<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The derivative of $\widetilde g$ is zero on its plateau and joins $g'$ continuously at $1/4$. For the gate put $p=z-x$, $q_1=z-y$, $r=x+y-z$, $w=1-x-y$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{cs:tetrahedra}
 \pi m=V(y,x,w)+V(p,q_1,2r+w)+V(p+q_1,r,r+w),
 \qquad V(a,b,c)=-\pi[J(a)+J(b)+J(c)].
\end{equation}
Each triple is nonnegative and sums to one. On such a triangle $V$ is concave: its two-variable negative Hessian is a positive multiple of
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cot\pi a+\cot\pi c&\cot\pi c\\\cot\pi c&\cot\pi b+\cot\pi c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, whose diagonal entries are positive and whose determinant is one. Continuity handles the boundary. Identity \eqref{cs:tetrahedra}, obtained by $J(1-t)=-J(t)$, proves concavity on the full $D$.
