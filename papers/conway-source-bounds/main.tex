\title{Spectral estimates for Conway tangle sources}
\author{Alexander Kolpakov}

\begin{abstract}
We study a finite polynomial expression associated with a Conway tangle
construction before twists are inserted in its crossing circles. Each
circle has an integer color between $1$ and the level $N$. We prove that
the absolute value of the expression is bounded by the exponential rate
of its positive complete-color sum. The estimate is uniform in all circle
colors and in the retained half twists. If any color stays a fixed
fraction below $N$, the exponential rate is strictly smaller. The same
strict bound holds for the terms omitted by the core decomposition defined below. The proof combines a Fourier formula for a colored circle
with positive estimates for the intervening multiplication trees. It
keeps both values and first derivatives at repeated spectral roots.
Two concave optimization inequalities supply the strict losses; their
constants are proved by rational interval arithmetic. We also give a
separate recurrence estimate for individual leaf coefficients. These
results bound the finite expressions used in a filling argument; they do
not by themselves give an asymptotic formula for a filled knot invariant.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source and the result}\label{cs:intro}

A Conway construction builds a tangle by stacking two-strand pieces and
turning them through a quarter turn. At a fixed level $N$, these operations
have a finite algebraic description. A circle surrounding two strands
acts by a polynomial, stacking is multiplication in a polynomial quotient,
and a quarter turn is a linear involution. A linear functional closes the
diagram. We call the resulting scalar a \emph{source}: it is the expression
to which one later applies a filling transformation to insert full twists.
The present paper estimates this scalar before filling.

The difficulty is to obtain an upper bound which respects cancellation.
Taking a separate absolute bound for every turn gives too large an
exponential rate. We use a Fourier identity when a colored-circle factor
is present. Between such factors, we keep all shared multiplication
indices and compare with a positive sum. The comparison must also retain
first derivatives at repeated roots of the quotient polynomial. Replacing
the quotient by its distinct spectral values would lose precisely the
terms used in the Fourier cancellation.

\subsection{Constructions and their complete action}
Fix an expression built from ordered products, clockwise quarter turns
and one closing operation. Denote the turn by $\Turn$ and closure by
$\phi$. Each elementary two-strand box is
\(B_i=h_{j_i}^{\epsilon_i}=\Turn(R^{\epsilon_i}L_{j_i})\), where $R$ is the positive crossing operator and \(L_{j_i}\) is its circle
operator, \(1\le j_i\le N\), and \(\epsilon_i\in\{0,1,-1\}\) specifies
no retained crossing or one crossing of the indicated sign. Every box
occurs once. The construction and its number \(H\ge3\) of boxes are
fixed before the level \(N\) tends to infinity. The color $j_i$ is the
integer dimension label on the $i$th circle. The \emph{complete color} is
$j_i=N$. Write $\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_H)$,
$\boldsymbol\epsilon=(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_H)$ and
$\mathbf N=(N,\ldots,N)$.

Here is the real feasibility condition on the expression. Give every
elementary box a label in $(0,1/2)$. A turn replaces a label $x$ by $1-x$.
At a multiplication, the input labels $x,y$ and output label $z$ satisfy
\[
 \max(x,y)<z<x+y<1.
\]
At closure the two input labels sum to $1$; there is no free output
label. The presentation is \emph{strict} if such labels exist. These are
the interiors of the normalized Newton-index constraints derived in
Section~\ref{cs:finite}. They are conditions on the expression, not on
its chosen circle colors.

Strictness can be checked without solving for all the labels.
The types \(L,H,A\) denote the possible output intervals
\((0,1/2),(1/2,1),(0,1)\), respectively.
An elementary box has type \(L\). A turn interchanges \(L,H\) and
preserves \(A\). With either input order the product table is
\[
 LL,LA,AA\longmapsto A,\qquad
 LH,HA\longmapsto H,\qquad HH\longmapsto\varnothing.
\]
At closure the allowed pairs are \(AA,AL,AH,LH\) and their reversals.
A presentation admits strict labels exactly when it passes this test
\cite[Lemma 5.2]{Newton}. An empty type means that no strict labels
exist; it does not assert that the finite operator vanishes. The type
letter \(H\) and the number $H$ of boxes play different roles.

For example, consider the closure $\phi(B_1(B_2B_3))$. Labels
$a_1=2/5$, $a_2=a_3=7/20$ and $z=3/5$ give
\[
 \max(a_2,a_3)<z<a_2+a_3<1,\qquad a_1+z=1.
\]
Thus this three-leaf presentation is strict, although one side of the
closing product is a single elementary box.

Write \(Z_N(\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\epsilon)\) for the scalar obtained by
evaluating the chosen expression: use $B_i$ at its leaves, multiplication
at a stack, $\Turn$ at a turn, and $\phi$ at closure. The normalization
of $\phi$ is given in \eqref{cs:turn-trace}; it is the single reduced cut
of \cite{Newton}.
Section~\ref{cs:finite} specifies the finite operators and each exact
core--alias split. Let \(Z_N^{\core}\) be the same contraction with
every box replaced by the summand $C_{j_i}^{\epsilon_i}$ defined there.
We call this summand the \emph{core}; the complementary summand is the
\emph{alias}. These names refer to an exact algebraic decomposition,
not to an asymptotic approximation.

Let \(P\) be the closure of the set of strict labelings. A single label
is used at both ends of each shared edge. Turns and closure impose the
relations just described. With the functions \(g,m\) in
\eqref{cs:g}--\eqref{cs:m}, its complete action is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:F0}
 F_0=\max_{y\in P}
 \left\{\sum_{i=1}^{H}g(a_i(y))+
             \sum_{v=1}^{H-2}m(x_v(y),y_v(y),z_v(y))\right\}.
\end{equation}
There is one term $g$ for each elementary box and one term $m$ for
each multiplication other than the closing product. The latter has
coefficient one. The strict profile theorem \cite[Theorem 6.4]{Newton}
gives a unique interior maximum after the turn and closing relations
are imposed. In particular,
$N^{-1}\log Z_N(\mathbf N,\mathbf0)\to F_0$; this complete-color,
untwisted sum is positive for every sufficiently large integer $N$.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-source and color bounds]\label{cs:main}
Fix a strict presentation with \(H\ge3\). Uniformly for
$\mathbf j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}^H$ and
$\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\{0,1,-1\}^H$,
\begin{equation}\label{cs:whole}
 |Z_N(\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\epsilon)|\le\exp\{N(F_0+o(1))\}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed \(\delta>0\) there are \(\gamma_\delta>0,N_\delta\)
such that
\begin{equation}\label{cs:color-gap}
 \max_i(N-j_i)\ge\delta N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |Z_N(\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\epsilon)|
 \le\exp\{N(F_0-\gamma_\delta)\},\qquad N\ge N_\delta.
\end{equation}
There are also \(\gamma>0,N_0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{cs:alias-gap}
 |Z_N-Z_N^{\core}|\le\exp\{N(F_0-\gamma)\},\qquad N\ge N_0.
\end{equation}
All sufficiently large integer levels are included, with no parity or
primality restriction. Constants depend on the fixed presentation;
no bound for constructions growing with \(N\) is asserted.
\end{theorem}

The second estimate is needed when a filling transformation sums over
many colors: it excludes every region where a circle color is separated
from $N$ by a fixed fraction. The third estimate allows the core to
replace the full source at the leading exponential rate. Its proof
uses the color bound. A high Newton index alone would not suffice,
because a half twist mixes Newton indices.
Corollary~\ref{cs:two-leaf-source} treats the strict two-leaf closure
separately, with rate \(2G_0\), where \(G_0=g(1/4)\).

\subsection{The proof and its inputs}
The exact finite quotient, turn, pairing, product and circle formulas
are \cite[Proposition 3.2, Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 4.1]{Newton}.
The crossing values and first derivatives are also stated in
\cite[Proposition 5.1]{Finite}. We prove the needed complete-node
conditioning directly in Section~\ref{cs:conditioning}, including
all derivative data at high Newton indices.

Section~\ref{cs:local} bounds individual twisted core coefficients by a
recurrence. This result is useful when the coefficients themselves are
needed, but is not an input to Theorem~\ref{cs:main}. A reader following
only that theorem may pass directly to Section~\ref{cs:two-leaf}.
Sections~\ref{cs:two-leaf}--\ref{cs:attachment} prove two scalar
supporting-plane inequalities; Section~\ref{cs:arithmetic} supplies
their rational certificates. These inequalities compare a leaf with
the two possible coefficient bounds, and propagate the resulting strict
loss through a multiplication tree.

The finite strict-state, reduced-junction and boundary-profile
results of \cite{Newton} give the tree decomposition and the maximized
action at each boundary. Some comparisons use the following smaller
class of auxiliary complete-color expressions. Begin with a product
$hh$ of two leaves, where $h=h_N$. Allow turns, products of two such
expressions and attachment of one leaf on either side:
\[
 E::=hh\mid\Turn E\mid E_1E_2\mid hE\mid Eh.
\]
We call these expressions \emph{completed comparison expressions}.
The boundary inequalities of \cite[Lemma 9.5]{Newton} apply to its
descendants at the maximum of a closure with two completed sides. In Section~\ref{cs:probe} we explain why each comparison
needed for a general strict presentation lies in this class, or is
the explicit three-leaf example above. We do not use a core asymptotic
from either companion. The Fourier and multiplication-tree proofs
in Sections~\ref{cs:fourier}--\ref{cs:assembly} then establish the
whole-source theorem.

A filling theorem still needs a nonzero core asymptotic, the oscillatory
filling transformation and a geometric interpretation of the selected
action. None follows from an upper source bound alone.

\section{Exact finite coefficients and their supports}\label{cs:finite}

Put $d=N-1$, $M=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$, $q=e^{\pi\ii/N}$ and $Q=q^2$. Throughout this paper
\[
 S_r=\prod_{a=1}^r2\sin(\pi a/N),\qquad S_0=1,
 \qquad c_a=q^{2a+1}+q^{-2a-1}.
\]
Thus $S_r$ is a product, not a single sine. The finite polynomial algebra is
\[
 \mathscr A_N=\mathbb C[z]/(P_N(z)),\qquad
 P_N(z)=2T_N(z/2)+2=\prod_{a=0}^{d}(z-c_a),
\]
where $T_N(\cos t)=\cos Nt$. Every class has a unique representative
of degree at most $d$; we call it the \emph{canonical representative}.
Repeated entries in the displayed product remain repeated roots.
Equality in $\mathscr A_N$ means equality of values and first derivatives
at its double roots, and of the value at the additional simple root
$-2$ when $N$ is odd.
The normalized Newton basis is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:basis}
 e_r(z)=S_r^{-2}\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}(c_a-z),\qquad 0\le r\le d.
\end{equation}
Write $[e_r]F$ for the coefficient of $e_r$ in this basis. The clockwise turn $\Turn$ and normalized cylindrical closure $\phi$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{cs:turn-trace}
 \Turn e_r=e_{d-r},\qquad \phi(e_i e_j)=\mathbf1_{i+j=d},\qquad
 \phi(F)=(-1)^dN^2[z^d](F\bmod P_N).
\end{equation}
Coefficient reversal makes the turn adjoint for this pairing:
\begin{equation}\label{cs:adjoint}
 \phi((\Turn F)G)=\phi(F\Turn G),\qquad
 \phi((\Turn F)(\Turn G))=\phi(FG).
\end{equation}
It is a linear involution, not an algebra homomorphism.
A stack is multiplication. For $i+j\le d$ its exact coefficients are
\begin{equation}\label{cs:product}
 e_i e_j=\sum_{k=\max(i,j)}^{i+j}
 \frac{S_{i+j}S_k}{S_iS_jS_{k-i}S_{k-j}S_{i+j-k}}e_k.
\end{equation}
For $i+j\ge N$ the product is zero in the full algebra, with multiplicities. All nonzero coefficients in \eqref{cs:product} are positive. We call one use of this multiplication a \emph{gate}. Its exact finite support is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:support}
 i,j\ge0,\qquad i+j\le d,\qquad \max(i,j)\le k\le i+j.
\end{equation}
At a closing product $\phi$ forces $i+j=d$, and its coefficient is one. This is an exact constraint before any scaling by $N$.

\subsection{The core and the alias}
A colored circle is represented by a signed Chebyshev polynomial.
We occasionally call it a \emph{circle character}; only its polynomial
formula is used below. Let $U_0(z/2)=1$, $U_1(z/2)=z$, and $U_{r+1}(z/2)=zU_r(z/2)-U_{r-1}(z/2)$. The ordinary circle is $L_j=(-1)^{j-1}U_{j-1}(z/2)$, and the elementary turned piece is $h_j=\Turn L_j$. Put $h_0=0$. For a high color $j=N-h$, $0\le h\le M$, its exact split is
\begin{align}
 h_{N-h}&=C_{N-h}+A_{N-h},\\
 C_{N-h}&=\sum_{v=h}^{M}
 \frac{N^2S_{2v}}{S_v^2S_{v-h}S_{v+h}}e_v,
 &A_{N-h}&=(-1)^N h_h.\label{cs:split}
\end{align}
For the remaining circle colors $1\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor$, set $C_j=0$ and $A_j=h_j$. The low-color formula is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:low-color}
 h_j=\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{j-1+r}
 \frac{S_r^2S_{j+r}}{S_{2r+1}S_{j-r-1}}e_{d-r}.
\end{equation}
There is no overlap or omitted color in this definition. The high and low \emph{colors} should be distinguished from the high and low \emph{Newton indices}: the high-color core has indices at most $M$, while an alias before the retained half twist in \eqref{cs:low-color} has indices $d-r\ge N/2$.

Let $\kappa=q^{d^2/2}$, with $q^a=e^{\pi\ii a/N}$ for real $a$. A retained half twist acts by
\begin{equation}\label{cs:half-twist}
 \Theta_\epsilon e_v=\kappa^\epsilon(-1)^v
 \sum_{r=0}^v q^{\epsilon v(r+1)}
 \frac{S_v}{S_rS_{v-r}}e_r,\qquad \epsilon=\pm1,
 \qquad \Theta_0=1.
\end{equation}
Set
\[
 h_j^\epsilon=\Theta_\epsilon h_j=\Turn(R^\epsilon L_j),\qquad
 C_j^\epsilon=\Theta_\epsilon C_j,\qquad
 A_j^\epsilon=\Theta_\epsilon A_j.
\]
Thus $h_j^\epsilon=C_j^\epsilon+A_j^\epsilon$ exactly in $\mathscr A_N$. The core still has indices at most $M$, but a half-twisted alias can occupy every index from $0$ to $d$. Thus the alias must be estimated on the entire gate support \eqref{cs:support}. The phases in \eqref{cs:half-twist} are retained until after the finite sum has been formed.

The transformation is exactly
\(\Theta_\epsilon F=\Turn(R^\epsilon\Turn F)\). The physical crossing has
\begin{equation}\label{cs:circle-crossing}
 R(c_k)=\kappa(-1)^kq^{k(k+1)},\qquad
 \frac{R'(c_k)}{R(c_k)}
 =-\frac{d-2k}{4\ii\sin((2k+1)\pi/N)}.
\end{equation}
The derivative is required only at double nodes; at the odd simple
node its value suffices. The inverse crossing is the inverse of this
class in the full quotient.

These formulas are identities in the full quotient, as derived in
\cite{Newton}; they hold for derivative data as well as values. A small
example explains the distinction. Since $L_1=1$, we have $h_1=e_d$.
This is a nonzero class, but it vanishes at every distinct spectral
root. Its derivatives at the double roots cannot all vanish. Any
estimate which discarded those derivatives would therefore replace a
nonzero colored box by zero.

\subsection{Continuous actions and elementary bounds}\label{cs:actions}
Define the continuous function
\begin{equation}\label{cs:J}
 J(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi u)\dd u,\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $J(1-t)=-J(t)$ and $J(0)=J(1/2)=J(1)=0$. The leaf and gate actions are
\begin{align}
 g(x)&=J(2x)-4J(x),&0\le x\le\tfrac12,\label{cs:g}\\
 m(x,y,z)&=J(x+y)+J(z)-J(x)-J(y)\notag\\[-2mm]
 &\quad-J(z-x)-J(z-y)-J(x+y-z).\label{cs:m}
\end{align}
The full limiting gate polytope is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:D}
 D=\{x,y\ge0:\ \max(x,y)\le z\le x+y\le1\}.
\end{equation}
It includes inputs larger than $1/2$. The two majorant profiles will be
\begin{equation}\label{cs:profiles}
 \widetilde g(x)=g(\max(x,1/4))\quad(0\le x\le1/2),
 \qquad k(x)=-2J(x/2)\quad(0\le x\le1).
\end{equation}

For reference, the uniform sine-factorial estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{cs:factorial}
 \log S_r=NJ(r/N)+O(\log N),\qquad 0\le r<N.
\end{equation}
To see endpoint uniformity, subtract $\log t+\log(1-t)$ from the integrand of $J$. The remainder extends with a bounded derivative to $[0,1]$, so its Riemann sum has bounded error. The two removed sums are logarithms of ordinary factorials; integral comparison, or Stirling's inequalities, bounds their errors by $O(\log N)$ uniformly in the endpoint. This includes $r=0$. Applied to \eqref{cs:product}, it gives the exponent $Nm(i/N,j/N,k/N)$ with a uniform $O(\log N)$ error.

All three profiles $\widetilde g,k,m$ are concave on their indicated domains. Indeed
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g'(x)&=2\log\cot\pi x,&g''(x)&<0,\\
 k'(x)&=-\log(2\sin(\pi x/2)),&
 k''(x)&=-\frac\pi2\cot(\pi x/2)<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The derivative of $\widetilde g$ is zero on its plateau and joins $g'$ continuously at $1/4$. For the gate put $p=z-x$, $q_1=z-y$, $r=x+y-z$, $w=1-x-y$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{cs:tetrahedra}
 \pi m=V(y,x,w)+V(p,q_1,2r+w)+V(p+q_1,r,r+w),
 \qquad V(a,b,c)=-\pi[J(a)+J(b)+J(c)].
\end{equation}
Each triple is nonnegative and sums to one. On such a triangle $V$ is concave: its two-variable negative Hessian is a positive multiple of
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cot\pi a+\cot\pi c&\cot\pi c\\\cot\pi c&\cot\pi b+\cot\pi c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, whose diagonal entries are positive and whose determinant is one. Continuity handles the boundary. Identity \eqref{cs:tetrahedra}, obtained by $J(1-t)=-J(t)$, proves concavity on the full $D$.
