\chapter*{Abstract}
We study the volume conjecture for knots assembled from planar tangles.
For each of the constructions specified here, sufficiently long signed
twists give
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|\langle K\rangle_N|
       =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K),
\]
where $\langle K\rangle_N$ is the reduced Kashaev invariant, with
unknot value one. The knot and its twists are fixed while $N$ tends
to infinity through all integers, including even and composite levels.
For a fixed construction this covers a set of integer twist assignments
of natural density one; it is not a counting statement about all knots.

The first class consists of strict ordered Conway constructions.
Its proof is the same pure-source proof used in the research paper:
full integer crossing powers are expanded before any asymptotic
estimate. A second class starts from a triangulated sphere
with paired triangles. Explicit additions of terminal tangles make
its circle-packing inequalities strict. The additions preserve the
chosen strand connections but can change the knot. For an unchanged
diagram, separate theorems assume weak balance or a shared-weight
certificate; these hypotheses are stated with each result.

For a finite ordered tree of crossing circles, let $d_v$ be the degree
of vertex $v$ and $n_v$ its signed crossing power. Explicit sufficient
conditions include
\[
 \sum_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2}\le2^{-25},
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 d_v\ge2^{28}\ \text{or}\ |n_v|\ge2^{27}
       \quad\text{at every vertex}.
\]
The second test is imposed separately at each vertex; its degree
alternative permits short or zero powers. In every theorem the strand
connections must give one knot, including components that might close
inside a tangle.

The proofs separate exact finite algebra, asymptotic estimates, and
geometry. Linear corridors are contracted before estimates. Fourier
orthogonality bounds the coefficient vectors with their repeated-root
derivatives, while Schur complements describe elimination at a stationary
point. A nonzero local Gaussian contribution is then combined with a
bound for the entire complementary sum. Finally the ordered face maps
and primitive peripheral curves identify the action with the volume
of the knot complement. The main cusp remains complete.

The opening chapters explain the transported Gram pairing, shifted
Fourier orthogonality, the full Hermite coefficient bound and the
stationary Schur complement. The subsequent arguments state the
different hypotheses needed by each volume family.
