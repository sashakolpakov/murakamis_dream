\chapter*{Abstract}
We prove the volume conjecture for a specified recursive family of arborescent knot diagrams with sufficiently many twists in each designated region. The construction starts with two-strand tangles surrounded by auxiliary crossing circles. It uses vertical stacking, quarter-turns, and attachment of one new elementary tangle. Two boxes, each built from at least two elementary pieces by the stated rule, are joined by a cylindrical closure. We define these operations geometrically before introducing their notation. After choosing a parity pattern that gives one main component, filling the auxiliary circles produces the knots of the theorem.

For each fixed construction, every sufficiently large tuple of independently signed filling integers gives a hyperbolic knot whose reduced Kashaev invariant has exponential growth equal to its knot complement volume. The integers are fixed before the root-of-unity level tends to infinity, and the limit includes both parities and composite levels. Only auxiliary circles are filled; the knot cusp remains unfilled.

The proof starts from the finite matrices defining the invariant. Their polynomial algebra retains derivative data at repeated roots. Positive multiplication coefficients give an angle maximization problem, and explicit ideal hyperbolic cells realize its complete value on the actual augmented link. Rigorous rational inequalities control the signed complementary color terms throughout the recursive construction. A holomorphic asymptotic for the remaining finite sum is then inserted into the exact filling transform. The contour argument retains its boundary contributions and all Fourier modes, and its leading action is identified with the volume after filling. Detailed appendices and an executable certificate companion support the proof. The theorem concerns the stated construction class; it does not classify all arborescent knots.
