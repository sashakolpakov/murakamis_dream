\chapter{The exact certificate arithmetic}\label{app:certificate}

The two source comparisons in \cite[Lemmas 4.1 and 5.1]{Bounds}
are inequalities on continuous domains. The terms called aliases there
are the complementary Newton terms in the finite tangle expansion.
Their comparison with the positive part reduces to rational interval
arithmetic, using the concavity established in that paper. Concavity bounds an alias action from above by a supporting tangent plane. A feasible pure point bounds the pure maximum from below. Subtracting these bounds leaves a function affine in the domain variables and slope parameters, so its minimum occurs among finitely many vertices and corners. This appendix explains the reduction, the exact cover of the parameter domain, and the error bounds for the transcendental functions.

\section{From concavity to a finite certificate}
For clarity, the supporting-plane argument has the following form.
Let $P_\lambda$ be a pure concave objective and $H_\lambda$ its
competing alias objective on a compact polytope $D$. Their dependence
on a real slope vector $\lambda$ is affine. A feasible pure point $p$
gives $\max P_\lambda\ge P_\lambda(p)$. At a strict tangent point $v$,
concavity gives
\[
 H_\lambda(w)\le H_\lambda(v)
             +\nabla H_\lambda(v)\cdot(w-v),\qquad w\in D.
\]
Thus a positive lower bound on the difference between the pure value
and this affine majorant proves a gap between the two maxima. For fixed
$p,v$, it suffices to check the vertices of $D$ and the corners of each
slope rectangle. The actual inequalities are (4.5) and (5.5) of
\cite{Bounds}, with gaps $1/100$ and $1/1000$ on their stated parameter
domains. This appendix explains how their entries are enclosed, rather
than assuming numerical values of the maximizing points.

The two certificates consist of rational points and exact coverage data. The two-leaf block table has sixteen closed slope intervals, one rational feasible pure point and two strict alias tangent points per interval. The attachment table is a recursively subdivided dyadic cover of $[-2,0]^2$ with 55 leaves, one pure point and one alias tangent point per leaf. The verifier checks feasibility and coverage before evaluating an inequality. It then checks every slope corner and every vertex of the appropriate product polytope.

The certificate proves the inequality independently of how its candidate points were found. A feasible pure value is a lower bound for the pure maximum. A tangent to a concave alias action is a global upper bound, including at boundary points and on the core plateau. The difference is affine in the slope parameters and the product-domain point. Corner and vertex checks therefore prove the continuum inequality. Numerical optimization may suggest the points; only their rational supporting inequalities enter the proof.

\section{Rational bounds for the elementary functions}
Every interval endpoint is an integer multiple of $10^{-40}$. Addition and subtraction are exact on this grid. Products and quotients use floor and ceiling integer division in the outward directions, with sign cases handled explicitly. A denominator interval must avoid zero. Intersections and monotone range reductions are checked, rather than inferred from floating-point values.

The constant $\pi$ is enclosed by Machin's formula
\[
 \pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239).
\]
Forty terms of each alternating arctangent series, with the next-term remainder, suffice for the recorded precision. Sine arguments are reflected into $(0,\pi/2]$. The Taylor polynomial through degree 41 has error at most the next degree-43 term there. Cosine is evaluated as the sine of the complementary angle.

For a positive logarithm argument, scale by an explicitly recorded power of two. On the resulting range let $z=(x-1)/(x+1)$, with the verifier checking $0\le z<1/2$. Then
\[
 \log x=2\sum_{j=0}^{79}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R,
 \qquad 0\le R\le\frac{2z^{161}}{161(1-z^2)}.
\]
The same series at $z=1/3$ encloses $\log2$. Negative logarithms are handled by reciprocal and sign, with outward intervals preserved.

\section{The integrated sine product}
For $0<t<1/2$, Euler's sine product gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-J-series}
 J(t)=t\log(2\pi t)-t
 -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2n)t^{2n+1}}{n(2n+1)}.
\end{equation}
To justify termwise integration, expand $\log(1-t^2/k^2)$ on compact subintervals below one, where the absolute double series converges; the initial logarithm is integrable at zero. The remaining terms all have the same sign. Use $\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$, and for $2\le n\le20$ the rational bounds
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{64}k^{-2n}\le\zeta(2n)
 \le\sum_{k=1}^{64}k^{-2n}+\frac{64^{1-2n}}{2n-1}.
\]
Since $\zeta(2n)<2$, the tail after $n=20$ in \eqref{eq:C-J-series} is bounded by
\[
 \frac{2t^{43}}{21\cdot43(1-t^2)}.
\]
The exact identities $J(0)=J(1/2)=J(1)=0$ and $J(1-t)=-J(t)$ reduce every action argument to this range. Thus every action and derivative used by the certificates has a proved rational enclosure.

\section{Gradients and coverage}
For the certificate notation, put
\[
 g_0(y)=g(y,0)=J(2y)-4J(y),\qquad k(x)=-2J(x/2),
\]
where $J(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sin\pi s)\,\dd s$ on the real
interval and by the continued branch used in the strict window.
The function $m(x,y,z)$ is the multiplication action in
Chapter~\ref{ch:conway-current}. The precise block and attachment
comparisons are the supporting inequalities used in the
whole-source proof of \cite{Bounds}.
The derivatives evaluated by the verifier are
\begin{align*}
 g_0'(y)&=2\log\cot\pi y,& k'(x)&=-\log(2\sin(\pi x/2)),\\
 m_x&=\log\frac{\sin\pi(x+y)\sin\pi(z-x)}{\sin\pi x\sin\pi(x+y-z)},&
 m_y&=\log\frac{\sin\pi(x+y)\sin\pi(z-y)}{\sin\pi y\sin\pi(x+y-z)},\\
 m_z&=\log\frac{\sin\pi z\sin\pi(x+y-z)}{\sin\pi(z-x)\sin\pi(z-y)}.
\end{align*}
Every tangent point is strict, and the core tangent point in the block certificate lies above $1/4$, so these derivatives are finite and select the intended branch. The entire action remains concave even where a maximizer could lie on the boundary; differentiability is needed only at the chosen tangent point.

For the square certificate, each nonterminal rectangle is split into its four dyadic children. The verifier checks equality with these children, their endpoints and their depth. Thus the 55 leaves cover the square exactly. It does not infer coverage from a list of sampled centers or from an approximate total area. The 880 corner/vertex inequalities are then enclosed from below. The exact lower endpoints are retained in the certificate records; outward error can only weaken the lower bound.

\section{The role of verification}
A successful replay establishes the finite supporting inequalities, their exact domains, the recorded rational margins and consistency of the immutable data. The pure complex continuation and the finite-lattice contour are proved in Chapter~\ref{ch:conway-current}; the whole filling theorem has its separate argument. Optional finite matrix calculations check identities at selected levels. They do not replace the arguments that hold at every level.
