\chapter{Classical face maps and the absolute volume}
\label{app:marked-calibration}

This appendix records the local geometric calculations used in the
circle-tree construction of Sections~\ref{sec:ft}--\ref{sec:fg}. They determine the
original peripheral curves, the quadratic change of their classical
potential under a retained half twist, and the absolute volume on the
same filled fundamental chain. The calculations start with ideal cells
and their labeled faces; their matrix coordinates are independent of
the quantum level.

All matrices below are projective: a nonzero scalar multiple represents
the same M\"obius map. A crossing disk has two separately labeled shaded
triangles, meeting at its ideal apex. A quadrilateral used to attach
cells is an auxiliary cut face; it is not the physical four-punctured
boundary of a tangle box. Peripheral logarithms are logarithms of
similarity multipliers, hence twice the corresponding eigenvalue logs.
Only auxiliary cusps are filled. The main cusps remain complete.

\section{Local formulas and the two assembly inputs}

The cell and collar calculations below use \(0<x<1/2\). They do not
assert a global assembly theorem for a diagram. For a general strict
Conway expression, the complete metric and its marked continuation are
supplied by \cite[Theorems~1.2 and~1.3]{StrictGeometry}, which retain
the original face labels and primitive peripheral curves.

For circle trees, Section~\ref{sec:ft} proves the ordered projection
cut and Section~\ref{sec:fa} constructs its positive octahedra. Each
tree edge has two regular ideal octahedra at completeness, with local
parameter \(x=1/4\). The formulas below apply at that value. The
circle-tree argument supplies its own metric and logarithmic period
lifts; Proposition~\ref{prop:app-same-chain-volume} then compares its
retained ideal chain with the completed metric.

\section{The classical leaf potential and its face maps}

Fix
\[
 J(u)=\int_0^u\operatorname{Log}(2\sin\pi s)\,\dd s,
 \qquad J(1-u)=-J(u),\qquad J(0)=J(1/2)=J(1)=0,
\]
and continue from the indicated real branch. For \(0<x<1/2\) and
small \(t\), put
\[
 g(x,t)=J(2x)-2J(x)-J(x-t)-J(x+t),\qquad
 v=g_t=\operatorname{Log}\frac{\sin\pi(x-t)}{\sin\pi(x+t)}.
\]
There is a direct geometric normalization of this function. The mixed
leaf pyramid has ordered base
\((1,E/T,-1/T,-E)\), where \(E=e^{2\pi\ii x}\) and
\(T=e^{2\pi\ii t}\). For small real \(t\), its two tetrahedra have
angle triples
\[
 (x-t,1/2-x,1/2+t),\qquad
 (1/2-t,x+t,1/2-x).
\]
The ideal-tetrahedron volume formula
\(V(a,b,c)=-\pi[J(a)+J(b)+J(c)]\), for \(a+b+c=1\),
therefore gives the pyramid volume \(\pi g(x,t)\): the complementary
terms cancel, and
\(J(2x)=2J(x)-2J(1/2-x)\).
This fixes the additive normalization before deformation.

For a retained sign \(\eps\in\{0,1,-1\}\), define a classical shear
and its potential by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-shear}
 \chi=t-\frac{\eps v}{2\pi\ii},\qquad
 f_\eps(x,\chi)=g(x,t)-\frac{\eps v^2}{4\pi\ii},\qquad
 \dd f_\eps=g_x\,\dd x+v\,\dd\chi.
\end{equation}
The last identity follows by differentiation. At \(t=0\), the derivative
of the first equation in \(t\) is
\(1-\eps\ii\cot\pi x\ne0\), so the hidden parameter is uniquely
holomorphic near that point. Also \(f_\eps(x,0)=g(x,0)\).
The endpoint normalization has an elementary independent check:
\(J(1/2+t)=-J(1/2-t)\) gives \(g(1/2,t)=0\), and the selected
logarithm has \(v(1/2,t)=0\). Thus the continued shear has
\(t=\chi\) and \(f_\eps(1/2,\chi)=0\). No color-dependent constant
is introduced.

Normalize the ordered quadrilateral to \((0,\infty,1,R)\). Its two
projection charts have apices \(Z,Z/E\), where
\[
 Z=\frac{1+E}{1+T},\qquad
 R=\frac{T(1+E)^2}{E(1+T)^2},\qquad
 Z^2-(1+E)Z+ER=0.
\]
The four white transitions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-white-matrices}
\begin{aligned}
 W_0&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&E\end{pmatrix},&
 W_1&=\begin{pmatrix}1&E-1\\0&E\end{pmatrix},\\
 W_2&=\begin{pmatrix}E-R&R(1-E)\\E-1&1-ER\end{pmatrix},&
 W_3&=\begin{pmatrix}R&0\\1-E&ER\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At an isolated leaf, \(W_0,W_2\) are actual white-face maps.
The other two are completion transitions: an adjacent cell must supply
the indicated white face before they can be used as its pairing.
The pure shaded map and the two odd cross-copy maps are
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-ER\\1&-1-E\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}R&-R\\1&ER-1-E\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}R/E-1-1/E&R\\-1&R\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their ordered vertex assignments follow by substitution; the apex
equation verifies the remaining flag. A second projection chart uses
reciprocal phase variables, not complex conjugates of variable
parameters. The resulting identities are consequently holomorphic.

For example, writing \(\overline S\) for the same expression with
\(E\) replaced by \(E^{-1}\), the pure edge words are
\[
 W_0S=\overline S W_0,\qquad W_2S=\overline S W_2,\qquad
 W_3S=\overline S W_1.
\]
The odd words are
\[
 W_0^{-1}BW_2^{-1}A=I,\qquad
 W_2^{-1}BW_0^{-1}A=I,\qquad
 W_1^{-1}BW_3^{-1}A=I.
\]
They hold projectively for independent nearby \(E,R\); reverse cycles
give their inverses. A turn and a closing are represented by
\[
 T_Q(z)=\frac{R-1}{z-1},\qquad C_Q(z)=\frac{z-R}{z-1}.
\]
These local identities check the entire maps, including translations.
To obtain a representation of a closed exterior one must also check
that every white transition used is an actual face map and that the
listed edges exhaust the original cut. The circle-tree proof makes
those checks in Sections~\ref{sec:ft}--\ref{sec:fa}; the strict Conway
companion makes them with its individual-edge labels. A local matrix
identity alone is not a substitute for either assembly argument.

\section{The original primitive peripheral curves}

Put
\[
 \rho=\frac{Z}{1+E-Z},\qquad Q=\frac{E-Z}{Z-1}.
\]
Here \(Q\) is a local multiplier parameter, unrelated to a
finite-level root of unity. The disk longitude and three meridians,
traced in the unchanged two-rectangle collar, are
\[
 \lambda=W_2^{-1}W_0,\qquad
 \mu_0=S^{-1},\qquad
 \mu_-=A^{-1}W_0,\qquad \mu_+=W_0^{-1}B.
\]
The longitude follows the twice-punctured disk. The pure meridian
crosses its shaded identification once, in the reverse direction.
The negative retained half twist crosses \(W_0\) and then \(A^{-1}\);
the positive one crosses \(B\) and then \(W_0^{-1}\). These are paths
in the original disk collar, as in~\cite[Section~5]{StrictGeometry}.
A physical turn transports the entire marked collar.

Their similarity multipliers are respectively
\(Q^2,\rho,\rho Q,\rho/Q\), in the selected flag-at-infinity frame.
At completeness conjugate back to the original leaf coordinate with
\[
 M(z)=\frac{1+E}{2}\frac{z-1}{z-E},\qquad
 R=\frac{(1+E)^2}{4E}.
\]
Substitution gives the translations
\[
 \mu_0=E-1,\qquad \lambda=-2(E+1),\qquad
 \mu_-=-2,\qquad \mu_+=2E.
\]
For \(E=e^{2\pi\ii x}\), the collar consists of two rectangles of
total area \(4\sin(2\pi x)\), while
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Im}
       \bigl(\overline{E-1}\,[-2(E+1)]\bigr)\right|
                  =4\sin(2\pi x).
\]
Thus the two paths span the full cusp lattice, rather than a proper
sublattice. The odd paths have the same area and are primitive too.
As translations on the respective marked tori,
\(\mu_\eps=\mu_0-\eps\lambda/2\). This compares half-twist partners;
it does not make half a longitude an integral curve on the pure torus.
The longitude-to-meridian ratio is therefore
\[
 \tau_\eps=\frac{2\ii c}{1-\eps\ii c},\qquad
 c=\cot\pi x>0,\qquad \operatorname{Im}\tau_\eps>0.
\]

The explicit apex gives \(\rho=e^{-2\pi\ii t}\) and \(Q=e^{-v}\).
Continue the multiplier logarithms from zero at completeness. The
actual collar words then give
\[
 U=-2\pi\ii t-\frac{\eps V}{2}=-2\pi\ii\chi,
 \qquad V=-2v.
\]
This is the geometric meaning of \eqref{eq:app-shear}. When internal
cell parameters have been eliminated at their classical stationary
point, the envelope rule cancels their covectors. With \(\Phi_\eps\)
denoting that classical critical value, it gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-primitive}
 U_\ell=-2\pi\ii\chi_\ell,\qquad
 V_\ell=-2\partial_\ell\Phi_\eps,\qquad
 G=2\pi\ii\Phi_\eps,\qquad
 \dd G=\frac12\sum_\ell V_\ell\,\dd U_\ell.
\end{equation}
For strict Conway assemblies the existence of this classical germ is
part of~\cite[Theorem~1.3]{StrictGeometry}. In the circle-tree angular
coordinates the corresponding primitive is computed directly in
Section~\ref{sec:fg-6}. In either description the shear
\(U^0\mapsto U^0-\eps V/2\) changes the primitive by
\(-\eps V^2/8\), since differentiation gives
\(\tfrac12 V\,\dd U\). Its complete imaginary constant is the actual
complete cell volume. No comparison with a signed quantum action is
needed for this statement.

\section{The absolute volume of the retained chain}

Triangulate the original cut cells with their orientations and cusp
flags. Let \(\mathcal V(\rho)\) be the signed sum of the resulting
ideal-tetrahedron volumes. Differentiate the Bloch--Wigner formula
\[
 \dd D(z)=\log|z|\,\dd\arg(1-z)
                       -\log|1-z|\,\dd\arg z.
\]
Across an internal face, the flags agree and the two orientations are
opposite. These terms cancel; the edge-log sums remain fixed.
On a boundary torus, cut along the recorded primitive pair
\((\mu,\lambda)\). Opposite sides of its parallelogram cancel except
for the eigenline scale factors. In the similarity-log convention
\(U=2u,V=2v\), the surviving term is
\[
 2\bigl(\operatorname{Re}v\,\dd\operatorname{Im}u
                  -\operatorname{Re}u\,\dd\operatorname{Im}v\bigr).
\]
The sign uses the boundary orientation with
\(\operatorname{Im}(\lambda/\mu)>0\). This is the local boundary
calculation of~\cite[equation~(7.3)]{MarkedFillings}; it applies to
the retained positive chains here because their actual face maps and
primitive collars have just been fixed. Main cusps have zero logs.
Consequently
\[
 \dd\mathcal V
  =\frac12\sum_\ell\left(
       \operatorname{Re}V_\ell\,\dd\operatorname{Im}U_\ell
       -\operatorname{Re}U_\ell\,\dd\operatorname{Im}V_\ell\right).
\]
For any primitive \(G\) with the differential in
\eqref{eq:app-primitive}, this is the differential of
\(\operatorname{Im}G-\tfrac12\sum\operatorname{Re}U_\ell
                                      \operatorname{Im}V_\ell\).
Fix \(\operatorname{Im}G\) at the complete point to its actual cell
volume. On the same connected marked branch we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-absolute-regulator}
 \mathcal V(\rho)=\operatorname{Im}G
       -\frac12\sum_\ell
                     \operatorname{Re}U_\ell\operatorname{Im}V_\ell.
\end{equation}
The normalization is absolute because the chain and its complete value
have been retained. Equal differentials on unrelated components would
not establish this equality.

\section{Primitive completion and comparison with the metric}

Near a complete finite-volume metric, the general completion theorem
\cite[Proposition~6.1]{MarkedFillings} applies to an actual representation
of the original group in a fixed framed neighborhood of the complete
holonomy, with the original primitive bases and small multiplier logs.
If a primitive
integral combination is \(2\pi\ii\) or \(-2\pi\ii\) at each cusp to be
filled, and the remaining cusps are parabolic, it constructs the smooth
complete metric on precisely those topological fillings. The framed
neighborhood is chosen before the filling slopes. The companion theorem
supplies completion, not the existence of the representation or the
solution of its filling equations.

We recall the part of the proof that is also used along the longer
circle-tree paths. Send the peripheral fixed points to \(0,\infty\).
If the logarithmic link development is a global Euclidean plane, its
two periods form a real rank-two lattice. They must be identified with
the recorded \(U,V\), not merely with them modulo \(2\pi\ii\).
Near completeness the second fixed point escapes every bounded
fundamental cross section. The logarithmic increments of fixed
generator paths tend to zero and hence are the uniquely small logs.
Along a connected positive deformation their differences from \(U,V\)
are continuous integers times \(2\pi\ii\), so remain zero. Section
\ref{sec:fg-4} proves these link and period properties for the
circle-tree path even when a total meridian log is not small.

Writing the logarithmic link coordinate as \(f=\operatorname{Log}z\), put
\[
 r=\frac{y}{|z|},\qquad R=\operatorname{arsinh}(1/r),\qquad
 \theta=\operatorname{Im}f,\qquad
 X=\operatorname{Re}f+\tfrac12\log(1+r^2).
\]
The upper-half-space metric becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-smooth-tube}
 \dd s^2=\dd R^2+\cosh^2R\,\dd X^2+
                                \sinh^2R\,\dd\theta^2.
\end{equation}
An actual period \(L\) acts by
\((X,\theta)\mapsto(X+\operatorname{Re}L,
                       \theta+\operatorname{Im}L)\).
The ends constructed here contain an epigraph \(y>A|z|\): on the compact
link quotient the initial height-to-\(|z|\) ratio is bounded above.
Thus they contain a whole punctured tube \(0<R<R_0\).
If the original primitive curve \(\mu+p\lambda\) has period
\(-2\pi\ii\), and the complementary period has nonzero real part,
adding the axis \(R=0\) gives a smooth solid torus with exactly that
meridian. Its affine holonomy is the identity. A period
\(-2\pi\ii c\), \(0<c<1\), gives the corresponding cone tube;
smooth filling and descent to the filled group are asserted at \(c=1\).
Thus the circle-tree application uses its actual positive metric and
period lifts, rather than importing a small-total-log hypothesis.

\begin{proposition}[Absolute comparison on the filled chain]
\label{prop:app-same-chain-volume}
Let the retained oriented ideal chain be the fundamental chain of the
original exterior. Suppose its holonomy also develops an oriented
hyperbolic metric on that exterior, with horoball ends at the unfilled
cusps and the primitive logarithmic tube completion above at the filled
cusps. Use the same peripheral flags for the chain and the metric.
Then its signed ideal volume is the volume of the completed metric:
\[
                  \mathcal V(\rho)=\Vol(M_{\mathrm{filled}}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use one finite boundary triangulation for both developments.
At a filled end put the peripheral axis at horizontal coordinate zero
and its selected endpoint at infinity. On a compact fundamental cross
section the conical ideal map has the form
\((a(w),Yb(w))\), where \(b>0\). Choose a real function \(\eta\)
whose deck increments are the real peripheral periods. Then
\((0,Ye^{\eta(w)})\) is an equivariant axis map, and
\[
 H_s(w,Y)=\left(sa(w),
     Y\exp\bigl((1-s)\eta(w)+s\log b(w)\bigr)\right),
                  \qquad 0\le s\le1,
\]
is a face-compatible equivariant comparison. Its horizontal coordinates
and derivatives are bounded on that cross section. Its height is
comparable to \(Y\), and its height derivatives are \(O(Y)\).
The boundary sweep therefore has hyperbolic volume \(O(Y^{-2})\),
by the form \(y^{-3}\,dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dy\), and tends to zero.

At smooth filling the primitive meridian has zero real period, so the
axis assignment extends over its meridian disk. Extend the same boundary
triangulation through the added solid torus with all new vertices on the
axis. Every added tetrahedron has zero signed volume. Unfilled horoball
ends have the same vanishing boundary comparison. Stokes on finite
truncations and then \(Y\to\infty\) identify the original chain with
the completed metric fundamental class, exactly as in
\cite[Section~7.3]{MarkedFillings}. Hence
\[
                  \mathcal V(\rho)=\Vol(M_{\mathrm{filled}}).
\]
This proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

For an intermediate cone angle the comparison is with the metric off
its axis; the axis has zero three-dimensional volume. This is the
absolute comparison used in Section~\ref{sec:fg-6}. It depends on the
same original chain, its actual primitive completion, and the retained
logarithmic lifts. It does not require small total peripheral logs, a
new choice of volume constant, or a quantum source normalization.
