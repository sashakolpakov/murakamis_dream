\chapter{A local corollary and a vertexwise geometric criterion}
\label{ch:local-circle-messages}

The maximum of the vertex ratios gives a convenient sufficient test
that does not depend on the number of vertices. We prove it by showing
that every vertex then satisfies the degree-or-twist alternative of
the directed theorem in Chapter~\ref{ch:directed-circle-messages}.
We then give a weaker geometric condition that completes the metric
without asserting the quantum limit.

Keep the ordered circle-tree construction of
Section~\ref{sec:circle-the-knot-construction}, with at least one edge.
Each vertex has its original crossing circle, degree \(d_v\ge1\),
and fixed signed power \(n_v=\epsilon_v-2p_v\). Cyclic orders,
quarter-turn frames and primitive disk markings are retained. The
strand test must give one main knot, including no component
closed inside an intermediate tangle. The tree and powers are fixed
before the quantum level tends to infinity.

\section{The local test is a directed corollary}

Use the same physical normalization throughout:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-normalizations}
\begin{aligned}
 q_N&=e^{\pi i/N},& K_N(K)&=F_N(1),\\
 H_N(s)&=\frac{\sin\pi s}{N\sin(\pi s/N)},&
 A_N(s)&=q_N^{a(D)s^2/2},\\
 a(D)&=-w(D)-\sum_v n_v.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The removable value of \(H_N\) at zero is one; \(w(D)\) is the
oriented writhe of the original filled diagram. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-criterion}
 \kappa=\max_v\frac{d_v}{d_v^2+n_v^2}\le2^{-120}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[The local ratio supplies the directed alternative]
\label{lem:local-implies-directed}
Under \eqref{eq:local-criterion}, every vertex satisfies
\[
                 d_v\ge2^{28}\quad\hbox{or}\quad |n_v|\ge2^{27}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If both inequalities failed at a vertex, then
\(d_v^2+n_v^2<2^{56}+2^{54}<2^{57}\). Since \(d_v\ge1\),
its local ratio would exceed \(2^{-57}>2^{-120}\), a contradiction.
This is the implication used in the local-ratio corollary of the
directed proof; it does not bound a sum over vertices.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A local degree and twist condition]
\label{thm:local-circle-volume}
For the fixed marked knot diagram above, suppose
\eqref{eq:local-criterion}. Then the filled knot complement
is hyperbolic. For every fixed \(0\le R<1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-profile}
 \frac{F_N(q_N^s)}{F_N(1)A_N(s)H_N(s)}
       =1+O_{\mathcal T,\mathbf n,R}(N^{-1}),\qquad |s|\le R,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-volume}
 \frac{2\pi}{N}\log|F_N(q_N^s)|
          \longrightarrow\Vol(S^3\setminus K)
\end{equation}
uniformly on that disk. These values are nonzero at all sufficiently
large integer levels, including both parities and composite levels.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-implies-directed} supplies the degree-or-twist
hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:directed-degree-twist}. That theorem
uses the same ordered diagram, knot test and scalars
\eqref{eq:local-normalizations}, and gives every displayed conclusion.
No directed condition is added as an independent assumption here.
\end{proof}

The sufficient threshold has no factor involving the vertex count. Error constants and the
initial level can still depend on the entire fixed tree and its
powers. Its conclusion is the centered profile
\(1+O(N^{-1})\); the explicit first correction is proved separately in
\eqref{eq:circle-21}, under the summed condition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-quantitative}.

\section{The common finite boundary polynomial}

The directed proof contracts the exact finite source in any order
pointing toward a final vertex. Let \(K_N\) denote the full kernel
\eqref{dk:eq-13}, and let \(\mathcal D_{n_v,\sigma_v,N}^{(t)}\)
be the original full crossing trace acting in \(-2\cos t\), with
\(\sigma_v\in\{0,s,-s\}\) prescribed by the orientation type.
The edge scalar $\gamma_{e_v}(s)$ is the turn factor prescribed by
the orientation of that attachment; its possible values are
$1,q_N^{s^2/2},q_N^{-s^2/2}$. We call the polynomial contributed
by a subtree its message. At a nonfinal vertex its exact recursion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-recursion}
 M_{v,N}(u)=\gamma_{e_v}(s)
 \mathcal D_{n_v,\sigma_v,N}^{(t)}
 \left[K_N(t,u;s)\prod_{w\text{ child of }v}M_{w,N}(t)\right].
\end{equation}
The quotient trace acts on the whole product, including high indices
and repeated-root derivatives. Only the kernel depends on \(u\), so
the resulting polynomial has degree at most \(N-1\) in
\(-2\cos u\), independently of subtree size. The exact finite
regrouping and its normalization are proved once in
Section~\ref{dk:sec-3}. The directed argument keeps
\(M_{v,N}(0)\) as its exact scalar and estimates the normalized
polynomial; it does not change the physical closing or commute tangles.

\section{A separate vertexwise metric criterion}

The geometry can be completed under a substantially weaker condition.
We state that result separately, so its conclusion is not confused
with the quantum estimate.

\begin{theorem}[Positive completion from vertexwise data]
\label{thm:vertexwise-geometry}
For the same ordered construction, suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertexwise-criterion}
 L_v=\sqrt{d_v^2+n_v^2}\ge630\qquad\text{for every }v.
\end{equation}
Then the charge equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertexwise-charge}
 \Psi_v(\boldsymbol\tau)+\frac{in_v}{2\pi}\tau_v=ic,
 \qquad \Psi=\sum_{vw}\psi(\tau_v,\tau_w),\qquad 0\le c\le1
\end{equation}
have a unique solution in $|\tau_v|\le10^{-2}$. It is holomorphic
in $c$ near the closed unit disk and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertexwise-size-sign}
 |\tau_v(c)|\le
 \frac{2\pi c}{L_v-d_v/39000},\qquad
 \Im\tau_v(c)<0\quad(c>0).
\end{equation}
The auxiliary ends of the positive cell metric complete to cone solid tori of angle $2\pi c$,
and to smooth solid tori at $c=1$. The main cusp remains complete.
When the component test gives a knot, the endpoint is its complete
finite-volume hyperbolic complement.
\end{theorem}

The sign in \eqref{eq:vertexwise-size-sign} is proved, rather than
inferred from the smallness of the parameters. Put
\[
 F(t,u)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(\tan(t/2)\cos(u/2)),\qquad E=F-t.
\]
On the radius $10^{-2}$ bidisk, the explicit derivatives give
$|E_t|,|E_u|<1/39000$. Since $E(0,u)=0$, write $E=tH$
with $H$ holomorphic and $|H|<1/39000$. The charge equation is
\[
 \left(d_v-in_v+\sum_{w\sim v}H(\tau_v,\tau_w)\right)\tau_v
       =-2\pi ic.
\]
Its denominator has strictly positive real part. This gives the
sign and a contraction whose row bound is $2/39000$, independent
of the tree size. The proof, including a finite variant with
different radii at different vertices, is in
Section~\ref{sec:vg-derivatives}.

The original primitive periods are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertexwise-periods}
 U_v=-2\pi\Psi_v-i\epsilon_v\tau_v,\qquad
 V_v=2i\tau_v,\qquad U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi ic.
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:quantitative-geometry} proves that these are
the full logarithmic periods of the primitive curves along the
positive path. At the initial
cusp their integral ambiguities vanish; continuity preserves that
choice. At $c=1$ the primitive filling slope therefore makes one
full turn. The conclusion concerns the original marked exterior,
even if a total meridian logarithm has moved far from zero.

The geometric criterion is weaker than either hypothesis used to
prove the invariant volume limit. It constructs the metric and its
classical action, but it does not estimate the finite invariant or
prove that its leading contribution survives cancellation. The local
corollary at the start of this chapter uses the directed analytic
proof for those additional steps.
