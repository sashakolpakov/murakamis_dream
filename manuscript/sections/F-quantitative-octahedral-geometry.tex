\chapter{The ordered octahedral geometry and the filled cusp}\label{app:quantitative-geometry}

This appendix supplies the geometric proof for
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-quantitative}. It begins with the ordered tangle diagram, identifies the two balls
obtained by its projection and disk cuts, and assigns hyperbolic cells
to those balls. The resulting identification keeps the specified link
components and peripheral curves. Section~\ref{sec:ft}
constructs the two-octahedron block at every physical tree edge and
tracks the primitive peripheral curves. Section~\ref{sec:fa} gives
positive tetrahedral shapes and the complete face maps.
Section~\ref{sec:fg} completes the original fillings and calibrates
their volume.

An ideal vertex lies at the sphere at infinity of hyperbolic space.
An ordered ideal tetrahedron is specified by its cross ratio; a
positive shape has positive imaginary part in the chosen orientation.
A logarithmic peripheral period records the full change of a continued
logarithm along a marked cusp curve. It is not reduced modulo $2\pi i$.
This distinction matters here: each local angular parameter remains
small, but the primitive meridian collects contributions from all
incident edge blocks. Its total logarithm reaches one full turn at
the smooth filling endpoint.

The shaded faces below come from cutting the original twice-punctured
crossing disks; the white faces come from the projection regions.
Their labels determine the gluing maps and the original cyclic order.
The word ``pants'' denotes the two-triangle thrice-punctured sphere
obtained by joining the projection copies of a disk-side face. It
always retains its three labeled punctures. The local M\"obius maps
$C$ and $T$ realize the closing and turn operations in the displayed
coordinate charts. The symbol $R$ in those charts is a normalized
ideal vertex parameter, distinguished from the crossing operator
by its definition in Section~\ref{sec:fa-1}.

\section{The ordered octahedral decomposition}\label{sec:ft}

A crossing circle surrounds two strands of a link and bounds a disk punctured by them. Cutting along the projection regions and these disks opens the augmented link exterior into two balls. For the ordered tangle constructions considered here, each ball can be assembled from octahedra, with one octahedron for each edge of the tangle-operation tree. The proof identifies every boundary vertex and face, so it also identifies the original meridians and disk longitudes after the pieces are joined.

The tangles have two strings and four ordered endpoints. Their operations are vertical stacking, quarter-turns in the projection plane, and the specified cylindrical closure. Every product junction in the reduced operation tree carries an original crossing circle; there are no junctions without a circle. The tree, the order of its branches, and all endpoint frames are retained throughout. Faces created by opening crossing disks are called shaded faces; the pieces of the projection regions are called white faces.

This is the topological input for the \hyperref[ch:quantitative-circle-trees]{quantitative volume theorem}. The \hyperref[sec:fa]{angular cell calculation} provides positive shapes for its octahedra, and the \hyperref[sec:fg]{completion theorem} uses the path supplied by the \hyperref[sec:qe]{analytic filling criterion}. The present proof concerns the augmented exterior before filling.

\subsection{The ordered tangle diagram and its cut}\label{sec:ft-1}

At each vertex \(v\) of a finite tree put an original two-strand crossing-circle tangle \(P_v\). It retains the crossing \(R^{\epsilon_v}\), where \(\epsilon_v\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and its auxiliary component bounds the original twice-punctured disk. Every edge is a quarter-turn between two ordered product junctions. There are no product junctions without their own original circle. Terminal circles are included as vertices of degree one. The tree has at least one edge. Write \(T\) for the quarter-turn, \(C\) for the cylindrical closure, and \(R\) for one positive braid crossing in the original ordered terminal frame.

The product order, the four endpoint frames, every rotation of a child ball, and every original disk orientation are part of the data. A representative recursive description has
\begin{equation}
W_v=P_v\prod_{w\text{ child of }v}T(W_w).
\label{eq:ft-1}
\end{equation}
The product here is in its specified order. At the closing sphere one may use the collar move to expose a root circle. A double turn then leaves its half-turn decoration on the affected subtree; it is not replaced by the scalar identity \(T^2=1\). The relevant closed-turn and cyclic collar isotopies are proved in
\cite[Lemmas~3.1--3.2]{ConwayReductions}. Their four collar sectors, and any reversal of the child order, are retained. Thus \eqref{eq:ft-1}, with those transported frames, describes the original all-circle ordered operation tree. It does not introduce a commutativity move on open two-tangles.

To relate the two ways of drawing the closure, use the following
rotation of its collar:
\begin{equation}
C\!\left(h_0T(E)\right)
 =C\!\left((TP_0)T(E)\right)
 \simeq C\!\left(P_0\,T^2(E)\right)
 =C\!\left(P_0\,\tau(E)\right).
\label{eq:ft-1a}
\end{equation}
Here \(\tau\) is the half-turn of the ball. If \(E=L_1\cdots L_k\), then \(\tau(E)=\tau(L_k)\cdots\tau(L_1)\), and \(\tau(TW)=T\tau(W)\). Thus this collar rotation reverses the displayed root order and transports every child frame; it does not suppress this rotation. The two-leaf case of \eqref{eq:ft-1a} is the base used below.

More generally the crossing-circle box can occur between two ordered lists of child factors in a nonroot junction. Keep that position:
\begin{equation}
W_v=\left(\prod_{w\in I_v^-}T(W_w)\right)P_v
          \left(\prod_{w\in I_v^+}T(W_w)\right).
\label{eq:ft-1b}
\end{equation}
The lists record the cut at the parent edge in the original cyclic order. The left-insertion version of the calculation below handles this form without moving \(P_v\) through a child. Reassociation of a stack and transport of genuine turn-chain rotations put the specified circle-only tree in this form. At the root only the cyclic closed-collar move is used to choose its cutting position.

Write \(E\) for the number of tree edges, \(V=E+1\) for the number of circles, and \(d_v\) for the number of edges incident to circle \(v\). The exterior admits the following labeled cut:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  There is one pair of octahedral balls per original tree edge.
\item
  At a circle \(v\), its incident edge blocks are joined in the original cyclic order. There are \(d_v\) disk-side pages, and exactly one of their cyclic seams retains the original disk identification and its \(\epsilon_v\) crossing. All other page seams are the untwisted labeled identity seams supplied by the ordered insertion.
\item
  Cutting open those distinguished disk seams and the projection faces gives two balls. Each is a tree of \(E\) octahedra attached along \(E-1\) triangular faces. Its remaining boundary has exactly the two original shaded triangles of every crossing circle and the original white face cycles.
\end{itemize}

At this stage an octahedron is a labeled topological cell. Positive
shapes are supplied in Section~\ref{sec:fa}. The topological induction
uses attachment along a triangular disk, so it does not require the
union of several cells to be convex.

\subsection{How endpoint pairings determine the cut}\label{sec:ft-2}

We specify the projection cut by its endpoint pairings. For the turned elementary leaf \(h_v=T(P_v)\), label its four physical half-segments
\begin{equation}
(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3)
 =(\text{lower left},\text{lower right},
   \text{upper right},\text{upper left}).
\label{eq:ft-2}
\end{equation}
A quarter-turn cyclically shifts this tuple. A lower factor \(F\) followed by an upper factor \(G\) joins \(F_2\) to \(G_1\) and \(F_3\) to \(G_0\), leaving exposed \((F_0,F_1,G_2,G_3)\). Cylindrical closure joins positions 0 to 3 and 1 to 2. These are empty joining strips in the projection plane.

One ideal vertex is assigned to each paired main segment and one vertex \(O_v\) to each original crossing-circle semicircle. The two shaded triangles of a leaf are
\begin{equation}
(O_v,v_1,v_2),\qquad (O_v,v_3,v_0),
\label{eq:ft-3}
\end{equation}
where a half-segment denotes the main-segment vertex containing it.
Around \(O_v\) the order is \((v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3)\). At a main-segment
vertex, concatenate the two shaded corner sectors in the order of the
planar joining strip. Walking along the remaining sectors gives every
white face boundary. Thus the matching records the full oriented
boundary graph, including its cyclic orders, rather than only face or
component counts.

This endpoint construction records the reversible projection/disk cut. Drilling a boundary-parallel auxiliary semicircle and then opening its recorded half disk restores a ball. The disjoint leaf boxes permit this for all circles. White faces reglue by their projection labels. The two shaded faces reglue by the original disk map, including the retained half twist. The proof of that cut uses no quantum source identity.

For the next local calculation it is useful to use an inverse turn:
\begin{equation}
P_v=T^{-1}(h_v),\qquad
 \text{its exposed tuple is }(v_3,v_0,v_1,v_2).
\label{eq:ft-4}
\end{equation}
This is an exact physical identity by the definition of \(h_v\). It avoids using \(T^2=1\) on an arbitrary physical tangle.

\subsection{One terminal insertion is exactly one octahedron}\label{sec:ft-3}

Suppose the unturned \(P_v\) occurs at the beginning of its specified local stack. In the current full diagram, let \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) be the half-segments matched to \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) outside that box. Insert a new terminal child \(h_w\) immediately after \(P_v\). The specified product rule changes precisely the following two matches:
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{before}&\text{after}\\ \hline
 v_1\! -\! x_1&v_1\! -\! w_1,\quad w_2\! -\! x_1\\
 v_2\! -\! x_2&v_2\! -\! w_0,\quad w_3\! -\! x_2.
 \end{array}
\label{eq:ft-5}
\end{equation}
All other half-segment matches are unchanged. This remains true if \(x_1\) or \(x_2\) is in a nested factor or outside the local ball: the replacement only uses the two exposed output strips of \(P_v\).

Name the old two vertices \(C=(v_1,x_1)\), \(B=(v_2,x_2)\), and name the new internal vertices \(D=(v_1,w_1)\), \(A=(v_2,w_0)\). The outside vertices \(C\) and \(B\) are now represented respectively by \((w_2,x_1)\) and \((w_3,x_2)\). Attach the octahedron with vertices
\begin{equation}
(O_v,O_w,A,B,C,D).
\label{eq:ft-6}
\end{equation}
and equatorial cycle \((A,B,C,D)\). Its four shaded triangles are
\begin{equation}
(O_v,B,C),\quad (O_v,D,A),\quad
 (O_w,D,C),\quad (O_w,B,A).
\label{eq:ft-7}
\end{equation}
Glue the first one to the old shaded face \((O_v,C,B)\). The two orientations are opposite, as required for joining oriented balls. The old face disappears. The second triangle in \eqref{eq:ft-7} is exactly the new \(v\) face in \eqref{eq:ft-3}, and the last two are exactly the new \(w\) faces. The other shaded face of \(v\) is untouched.

The same gluing determines all four white faces. At each of
\(B,C,O_v\), delete the two identified shaded sectors and concatenate
the remaining sectors. This is exactly the planar strip substitution
\eqref{eq:ft-5}. The vertices \(A,D\) carry the two new joining strips.
Consequently the white pieces assemble into the prescribed white
regions with their cyclic orders. This single boundary calculation
checks both kinds of face at once.

Perform the same attachment in the other projection copy. This adds exactly one two-octahedron edge block and introduces one new original circle. It neither identifies two old circles nor inserts a circle at the attachment seam.

The calculation also proves the inverse operation: remove a terminal edge block, restore the deleted triangular face, and undo \eqref{eq:ft-5}. The original diagram loses that terminal child and otherwise retains its strand connections and order.

There is also an explicit left-insertion version, needed for \eqref{eq:ft-1b}. Inserting \(h_w\) immediately before \(P_v\) changes
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|c}
 v_3\! -\! x_3&v_3\! -\! w_3,\quad w_0\! -\! x_3\\
 v_0\! -\! x_0&v_0\! -\! w_2,\quad w_1\! -\! x_0.
 \end{array}
\label{eq:ft-7a}
\end{equation}
This is the same octahedral attachment on the other shaded face \((O_v,v_3,v_0)\). The two formulas are direct applications of the same lower-output/upper-input stack rule. They retain the physical rotation of each tangle and all its endpoint labels.

\subsection{Why this covers every ordered tree}\label{sec:ft-4}

Start with one tree edge. The two-leaf matching gives exactly the octahedral boundary \eqref{eq:ft-7}, in both projection copies. It is the two-circle block with the endpoint-frame rotation already
recorded. The preceding disk and projection cuts identify its boundary
faces directly.

For a general tree, choose a terminal vertex as root and start with its unique incident edge. Expose that root by the closed-collar moves from section~\ref{sec:ft-1}, retaining all transported frames and orders. The initial root then has no further children to insert; at the other endpoint the only existing edge is its parent edge. At each vertex add the children on the right of \(P_v\) in reverse final order, always immediately after \(P_v\). Add the children on its left in their final order, always immediately before \(P_v\). This is a construction of the specified word, not a permutation of an existing word: successive insertions produce exactly the two ordered lists in \eqref{eq:ft-1b}, including the special case \eqref{eq:ft-1}. A new child initially has only its own crossing-circle tangle; its children are subsequently inserted inside its own ball. Its quarter-turn carries the whole local insertion, including the two projection sides and all four endpoint labels. Any previously recorded half-turn or input/output reversal similarly carries the complete insertion and its order. It does not remove the frame decoration.

Induction now gives exactly the original recursive word and its full boundary ribbon graph. Every step glues a ball along a triangular disk, so each projection copy stays a ball. There are \(E-1\) attachments after the initial octahedron. The shaded face count is
\begin{equation}
4E-2(E-1)=2V.
\label{eq:ft-8}
\end{equation}
The count is only a consistency check: the insertion proof has already identified each of these two faces with its particular original circle and each main-segment vertex with its specified pair.

The boundary identification extends over each ball. Since it respects
every original face pairing, it descends to the required homeomorphism
of exteriors. In particular it preserves the main arcs, the individual
crossing circles, their disk sides, and the collar paths used for the
peripheral marking.

The extra triangular interfaces provide the promised pages. In the whole exterior an upper interface triangle and its lower partner form a two-triangle, thrice-punctured sphere. The ends are the same original circle and its two physical main punctures. Its compactification is a twice-punctured spanning disk for that circle. These pages are obtained from the explicit cell subdivision and the preceding homeomorphism; we did not assume that a single original disk cut somehow creates all \(d_v\) pages. The insertion order places the pages consecutively in exactly the original cyclic order at that circle. There are \(d_v-1\) new seams there, plus the original distinguished disk seam.

\subsection{Retained half twists and reversed disk frames}\label{sec:ft-5}

Perform the projection construction first with the disk cuts open.
A retained crossing changes the disk regluing, while the open-cut
endpoint matching stays fixed. At each \(v\), keep the distinguished
original seam through all insertions. Its signed disk map is used once,
after the untwisted page attachments; the newly created seams carry
identity maps.

For \(\epsilon_v=0\) the original pure disk map pairs the two sides within the same projection copy. For \(\epsilon_v=+1\) or \(-1\) use its odd pairings: the punctures exchange and the two projection copies are permuted as in the original A,B disk maps. The signs +1 and -1 have their distinct primitive meridian words even though their endpoint permutation has the same parity. The explicit collar maps and their copy exchange are calculated in
\cite[Section~3.2]{CircleTreeGeometry}. A full twist changes the peripheral marking and must not be discarded simply because its endpoint permutation is trivial.

A physical rotation or reflection of a disk frame transports its puncture labels, its two pages, and its chosen meridian and longitude together. Apply the insertion table in that transported frame. In particular a reversal that exchanges the projection copies is applied to both halves of every incident block. There is no use of an arbitrary puncture permutation justified only by the rigidity of an abstract thrice-punctured sphere.

These conventions cover the original signed and reversed diagrams. The local geometric block may be relabeled by these same rotations. Its shape formula is not used to determine the topology.

\subsection{Exact primitive peripheral concatenation}\label{sec:ft-6}

Fix one auxiliary circle \(v\) and orient its original disk-framed longitude \(\lambda_v\) and meridian \(\mu_v\) as in the original marked leaf theorem. After the page cut, a small cusp torus of \(v\) consists of \(d_v\) annular sectors, one from each incident edge block. Every page boundary on this torus is the same primitive disk-longitude class. A small meridian crosses these sectors once in their cyclic order. The insertion \eqref{eq:ft-5} subdivides one such transverse passage into two consecutive passages; it neither winds the meridian a second time nor changes its longitude class.

This can also be read as a face-word statement. Each insertion replaces one original disk-face passage by two passages separated by the new triangular interface. In the final word all internal interface changes of coordinates occur in inverse consecutive pairs. After eliminating them, the retained word is the original disk meridian. Its ordered factors are one endpoint passage for each incident block, followed at the distinguished seam by the single original half-twist correction. This is a statement about the marked loop before evaluating its holonomy.

Use the \hyperref[sec:fa]{local angular chart}, with common oriented longitude variable \(\tau_v\) at all pages of this circle. Its incoming/outgoing edge-ratio tables give exactly the inverse seam maps with the just-established puncture matching. In one edge block its small pure meridional increment is
\begin{equation}
u_{v,e}=-2\pi\,\partial_{\tau_v}\psi_e,
 \qquad V_v=2i\tau_v.
\label{eq:ft-9}
\end{equation}
This is the local primitive word, not an arbitrary logarithm of its multiplier. The longitude conjugates to the same element on all pages. Whenever it is loxodromic, its two fixed points are therefore carried to the same two marked points. In a coordinate with one at infinity and the other at \(q\), integrate \(d\log(z-q)\). The coordinate changes telescope in the concatenated path; the integral is the sum of the local increments \eqref{eq:ft-9}. At the complete value it is the continuous limit of this equality.

The original disk words fix the half-twist correction unambiguously. In the notation of the marked-leaf theorem, the pure meridian has multiplier \(\rho\), and the two odd primitive words have multipliers \(\rho Q\) and \(\rho/Q\). Write \(\nu\) for the local leaf response, so \(Q=e^{-\nu}=e^{i\tau_v}\) and \(\nu=-i\tau_v\). The physical \(R^{-1}\) tangle uses the first word and \(R\) uses the second. Thus the single retained crossing contributes \(\epsilon_v\nu=-i\epsilon_v\tau_v\), or equivalently \(-\epsilon_vV_v/2\). Consequently the continued primitive logs are
\begin{equation}
U_v=-2\pi\sum_{e\sim v}\partial_{\tau_v}\psi_e
                   -i\epsilon_v\tau_v,
 \qquad V_v=2i\tau_v.
\label{eq:ft-10}
\end{equation}
These primitive words, with both crossing signs, are calculated in
\cite[Sections~2.1 and~3.2]{CircleTreeGeometry}.
The two-rectangle cusp lattice there certifies their primitivity. Our page insertion preserves those same paths; it does not choose a new rational meridian slope.

In particular the integral in \eqref{eq:ft-10} is not reset to a principal logarithm after several pages. Each summand follows its local small branch, and the concatenated loop retains their full sum. This is precisely where a large total logarithm can occur while every individual block stays in its small positive chart.

Along the continuous first-charge equations, with charge \(c\in[0,1]\),
\begin{equation}
\partial_{\tau_v}\Psi
       +\frac{i n_v}{2\pi}\tau_v=ic,
 \qquad \Psi=\sum_e\psi_e,
 \qquad n_v=\epsilon_v-2p_v,
\label{eq:ft-11}
\end{equation}
\eqref{eq:ft-10} gives the exact original primitive filling word
\begin{equation}
U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi i c.
\label{eq:ft-12}
\end{equation}
At \(c=1\) this is one primitive turn. This calculation identifies the word and its continued logarithm. The \hyperref[sec:fg]{completion proof} verifies the positive-cell and nonsingular cusp-link conditions along the path and deduces the smooth complete filling.

\subsection{From the cut to the geometric filling}\label{sec:ft-7}

This proof applies to the ordered circle-only tree, with all its inherited frames and disk collars. It does not assert such a cut for an arbitrary scalar tensor network, for arbitrary branching of six independent boundary slots, or for a graph with cycles. It uses neither commutativity of pair-algebra polynomials nor equality of source actions. No auxiliary component is filled or deleted in the topological identification.

The explicit terminal substitution \eqref{eq:ft-5}, its ribbon-face realization \eqref{eq:ft-7}, and the primitive path concatenation supply the ordered topological identification. The \hyperref[sec:fa]{local angular charts} give the positive cells and their full complex seam ratios. The \hyperref[sec:qe]{analytic criterion} constructs the charge path, and the \hyperref[sec:fg]{geometric completion} proves that its endpoint is a smooth complete metric on the stated filled topology.

\section{Positive angular coordinates on each edge block}\label{sec:fa}

Each edge in an ordered tree of crossing circles contributes two octahedra to the cut link exterior, one in each projection copy. This section gives their vertices and tetrahedron shapes as explicit functions of the two endpoint longitude parameters. The shapes remain positive on a fixed complex disk, independently of the size of the tree. The two-triangle disk boundaries depend only on their own circle parameter, which is what allows incident blocks with different other endpoints to be joined.

The \hyperref[sec:ft]{ordered decomposition} identifies these cells and all their labels with the original link exterior. The \hyperref[sec:fg]{completion proof} then follows their logarithmic periods to the smooth filling. These are the geometric inputs to the \hyperref[ch:quantitative-circle-trees]{quantitative volume theorem}, whose angular path is supplied by the \hyperref[sec:qe]{analytic criterion}.

\subsection{The two-leaf model and its coordinate normalization}\label{sec:fa-1}

Start with the specified two-leaf closure \(C(h_0T(h_1))\). At a pure leaf use the original coordinates below. Here \(T\) is the quarter-turn, \(C\) is the cylindrical closure, and \(h_j\) is the turned two-strand piece with its original crossing circle.
\begin{equation}
Q=(0,\infty,1,R),\qquad
 Z=\frac{1+E}{1+H},\qquad
 E=e^{2\pi ix},\quad H=e^{2\pi it},
 \quad R=\frac{H(1+E)^2}{E(1+H)^2}.
\label{eq:fa-1}
\end{equation}
The parameter \(R\) in this display is the fourth normalized ideal vertex of the quadrilateral. The second projection copy has apex \(Z/E\), not a conjugation of the free parameters. The original turn and root closing maps are
\begin{equation}
T(z)=\frac{R_1-1}{z-1},\qquad
 C(z)=\frac{z-R}{z-1},\qquad R_1=1-R.
\label{eq:fa-2}
\end{equation}
These are the ordered turn and closing maps of
\cite[Section~3, equation~(3.2)]{CircleTreeGeometry}.
In particular both maps must be used when placing the second leaf. Applying only the turn would put the two complete apices at the same point.

The complete two-leaf wire is \(x=1/4\). In each projection ball, the two ideal pyramids are joined along their ordered quadrilateral bases. Their six outer vertices form an octahedron. This is a specified local cut statement, not a deduction from its volume.

\subsection{Solving in the two angular longitude variables}\label{sec:fa-2}

In this section \(\tau_v\) is an angular longitude parameter, with
\(V_v=2i\tau_v\). It is distinct from a Euclidean cusp shape, which
is also often denoted by \(\tau\). The chart is centered at the
complete cusp \(\tau_v=0\).

Write \(\tau_0=\tau\), \(\tau_1=\upsilon\), and prescribe the disk longitude logarithms
\begin{equation}
V_j=2i\tau_j.
\label{eq:fa-3}
\end{equation}
The pure leaf relation gives \(v_j=-i\tau_j\), where \(v_j=\log[\sin\pi(x-t_j)/\sin\pi(x+t_j)]\). On the branch through completeness, put
\begin{equation}
y=\tan(\tau/2),\qquad z=\tan(\upsilon/2),\qquad
 k=(1+y^2+z^2)^{-1/2},\qquad k(0,0)=1.
\label{eq:fa-4}
\end{equation}
Then the exact solution of the two-leaf stationarity equations is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \tan(\pi x)&=k,\\
 \tan(\pi t_0)&=iky,&
 \tan(\pi t_1)&=ikz,\\
 E&=\frac{1+ik}{1-ik},&
 H_0&=\frac{1-ky}{1+ky},&
 H_1&=\frac{1-kz}{1+kz},\\
 R&=\frac{1+z^2}{2+y^2+z^2},& R_1&=1-R,\\
 Z_j&=\frac{1+E}{1+H_j}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fa-5}
\end{equation}
Indeed the logarithmic ratio equation first gives \(\tan\pi t_j=ik\tan(\tau_j/2)\). Its ordered metric is
\begin{equation}
\kappa_j=\frac{k^2(1+\tan^2(\tau_j/2))}
                       {1-k^2\tan^2(\tau_j/2)}.
\label{eq:fa-6}
\end{equation}
The wire equation is \(\kappa_0\kappa_1=1\). With \(a=y^2+z^2\), it factors as \((k^2+1)((1+a)k^2-1)=0\); the complete branch is \eqref{eq:fa-4}. Thus \(\kappa_0=(1+y^2)/(1+z^2)\), which yields \eqref{eq:fa-5}. No meridian-color approximation was used.

The two octahedra have the same equator \(q=(0,\infty,1,R)\), and apices
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & A & B\\ \hline
 +&Z_0&\displaystyle\frac{RZ_1}{Z_1-R_1}\\[2mm]
 -&Z_0/E&\displaystyle\frac{RZ_1}{Z_1-ER_1}.
\end{array}
\label{eq:fa-7}
\end{equation}
For example, composing \eqref{eq:fa-2} gives \(C(T(Z_1))=RZ_1/(Z_1-R_1)\). Replacing the second-copy apex by \(Z_1/E\) gives the last entry. At zero the two rows are
\begin{equation}
(A_+,B_+)=((1+i)/2,(1-i)/2),\qquad
 (A_-,B_-)=((1-i)/2,(1+i)/2).
\label{eq:fa-8}
\end{equation}
The corresponding complete cells are the regular octahedra in their opposite labeled orientations.

\subsection{Uniform positive tetrahedra on an explicit complex bidisk}\label{sec:fa-3}

We use the orientation of upper half-space for which
\((0,\infty,1,w)\) is positive when \(\Im w>0\). The positive
geometric orders are \((B_+,A_+,q_j,q_{j+1})\) in the plus copy
and \((B_-,A_-,q_{j+1},q_j)\) in the minus copy, with indices
modulo four. To compute the numerical shape, reverse the first two
entries of either ordered tuple and use the edge-ratio convention
\begin{equation}
[a,b,c,d]=\frac{(a-c)(b-d)}{(a-d)(b-c)}.
\label{eq:fa-9}
\end{equation}
The reversal is essential: this edge-ratio formula has a different
vertex convention from the positively ordered tetrahedron model.
All eight complete shapes are \(i\). This triangulates the two-pyramid union, with the internal diagonal joining its two apices.

There is a uniform positive chart on
\begin{equation}
|\tau|,|\upsilon|\le 10^{-2}.
\label{eq:fa-10}
\end{equation}
Here are deliberately loose explicit bounds. The elementary sine and cosine series give \(|y|,|z|<0.005001\). Hence \(|y^2+z^2|<0.000051\), \(|k-1|<0.000026\), \(|E-i|<0.00006\), \(|H_j-1|<0.0102\), and \(|R-1/2|<0.000026\). Substitution in \eqref{eq:fa-5} gives \(|Z_j-(1+i)/2|<0.004\). In each denominator in the B column of \eqref{eq:fa-7}, the complete modulus is \(1/2\), and its perturbation is less than \(0.0041\). The quotient estimate therefore bounds the motion of each finite vertex in \eqref{eq:fa-7} by \(0.02\); the remaining finite equatorial vertices are fixed or move by less than \(0.000026\).

Every finite pair of distinct complete vertices occurring in \eqref{eq:fa-9} has separation at least \(1/2\). Each difference factor therefore changes by relative size less than \(0.08\). Comparing both the numerator and denominator products with one bounds the cross-ratio quotient change by
\begin{equation}
\frac{4(0.08)+2(0.08)^2}{(1-0.08)^2}<0.4.
\label{eq:fa-11}
\end{equation}
For a factor involving infinity there are fewer factors and the same bound suffices. Thus every ordered shape lies in \(|w-i|<0.4\), and in particular has positive imaginary part. All denominators and ideal vertices remain distinct. The internal diagonal has total angle \(2\pi\): its product relation telescopes exactly, and its integer angle branch is fixed by \eqref{eq:fa-8}.

The result concerns the geometric tetrahedra with the orders just
specified. The parameters \((x,t_j)\) need not stay in their real
angle domain. For complex angular parameters the equatorial quadrilateral need not be planar. The triangular octahedral boundary and the positive tetrahedral subdivision are the retained geometric objects.

\subsection{The doubled disk boundaries depend only on their longitude}\label{sec:fa-4}

The local white matrices in a leaf frame are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 W_0&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&E\end{pmatrix},&
 W_1&=\begin{pmatrix}1&E-1\\0&E\end{pmatrix},\\
 W_2&=\begin{pmatrix}E-R&R(1-E)\\E-1&1-ER\end{pmatrix},&
 W_3&=\begin{pmatrix}R&0\\1-E&ER\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fa-12}
\end{equation}
The relation \(Z^2-(1+E)Z+ER=0\) is retained. Put
\begin{equation}
Q=\frac{E-Z}{Z-1}=e^{-v}=e^{i\tau}.
\label{eq:fa-13}
\end{equation}
The incoming shaded triangle has cyclic vertices \((Z,\infty,1)\), and the outgoing one has \((Z,R,0)\). A disk cut doubles each of them across its adjacent white faces, producing the two-triangle pants boundary. In the plus chart, transport the opposite triangle through the appropriate \(W_j^{-1}\).

If an oriented edge has endpoints \((a,b)\) and the third vertices on its two sides are \(c,d\), measure its complex ratio by \eqref{eq:fa-9}. Direct substitution gives the following cyclic tables:
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{incoming edge}&(Z,\infty)&(\infty,1)&(1,Z)\\ \hline
\text{ratio}&-Q^{-1}&-Q&-Q^{-1}\\[1mm]
\text{outgoing edge}&(Z,R)&(R,0)&(0,Z)\\ \hline
\text{ratio}&-Q&-Q^{-1}&-Q.
\end{array}
\label{eq:fa-14}
\end{equation}
For example the first incoming ratio is \((1-Z)/(E-Z)=-Q^{-1}\). Across its base edge, the opposite apex is \(W_1^{-1}(Z/E)=Z-E+1\), giving \(-Q\). For the outgoing base, its opposite apex is \(W_3^{-1}(Z/E)=ER/(2E-Z)\), giving \(-Q^{-1}\). The remaining entries follow in the same way from \eqref{eq:fa-12}.

The important feature of the table is its dependence on \(Q\) alone.
The far endpoint of the block has disappeared, and both angles and
complex shears are retained. The two main puncture products are one;
the third is \(Q^{\pm2}\). In the recorded disk orientation the
longitude multiplier is \(Q^2=e^{2i\tau}\).

Consequently an outgoing disk-side boundary of one local block and an incoming boundary of another with the same \(\tau\) have inverse edge ratios under the original puncture matching. Normalize one triangle by its three ordered vertices. The other triangle's position is then fixed by \eqref{eq:fa-14}, so the whole two-triangle transition matches. Their edge shears cancel and their interior-angle branches, continued from \(\pi\), sum to \(2\pi\). This is the local geometric seam calculation required for joining edge blocks. It does not assert an arbitrary reordering of the three punctures.

\subsection{Local logarithmic increments and the edge action}\label{sec:fa-5}

Define the edge potential \(\psi\) by its complete value
\(\psi(0,0)=v_{\rm oct}/\pi\), and by
\begin{equation}
\partial_\tau\psi=it_0
   =-\frac1\pi\operatorname{arsinh}
                   (\tan(\tau/2)\cos(\upsilon/2)),
\label{eq:fa-15}
\end{equation}
with the branch through zero and the corresponding formula after
interchanging the endpoints. The two mixed derivatives agree, so these
formulas determine a holomorphic potential. They also have a direct
geometric meaning. The pure meridian map has multiplier
\(\rho=H_0^{-1}\); hence its logarithm is
\(2\operatorname{artanh}(ky)\), by \eqref{eq:fa-5}.
The identity
\(\operatorname{artanh}(ky)=
\operatorname{arsinh}(y/\sqrt{1+z^2})\)
shows that this equals \(-2\pi\partial_\tau\psi\).
The two-leaf action introduced earlier has the same derivatives and
complete value, and is therefore this same function.

The local pure meridional increment is therefore
\begin{equation}
-2\pi it_0=-2\pi\partial_\tau\psi.
\label{eq:fa-16}
\end{equation}
It is a small log-link increment in this fixed local chart. The ordered disk-page proof in Section~\ref{sec:ft-6} concatenates these local paths in their original order. The total pure meridian period is therefore the sum of \eqref{eq:fa-16}. A single retained half twist of sign \(\epsilon_v\) adds \(-\epsilon_vV_v/2\), in the original primitive basis. Consequently
\begin{equation}
U_v=-2\pi\sum_{e\sim v}\partial_{\tau_v}\psi_e
                         -i\epsilon_v\tau_v,
 \qquad V_v=2i\tau_v.
\label{eq:fa-17}
\end{equation}
The direct first-charge saddle with \(n_v=\epsilon_v-2p_v\) makes \eqref{eq:fa-17} exactly \(U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi i\). The integer is fixed by concatenating the local increments and continuing the periods from the complete cusp, even when the total is no longer a small logarithm. Section~\ref{sec:fg-4} establishes that period statement without resetting a principal log.

\subsection{Assembly in the original ordered exterior}\label{sec:fa-6}

The \hyperref[sec:ft]{ordered decomposition} constructs the following finite assembly. Associate the two octahedra above to each physical edge of the circle tree. At a circle vertex, list its incident edges in the cyclic order and join the corresponding outgoing and incoming disk-side pants cyclically. Insert the prescribed retained half twist at its specified disk page. Table \eqref{eq:fa-14} supplies geometric seam matching for a common angular variable at that circle. The complete model has two octahedra per edge and \(D\) meridional sectors at a degree-\(D\) circle.

The topological proof cuts each original circle neighborhood into ordered disk pages and uses terminal-vertex insertion to identify the two-leaf block at each physical quarter-turn, including its four marked main segments. The two-circle endpoint pairs are interlaced by the quarter-turn. The proof retains that matching and every left or right insertion in the original product order. It also proves that the primitive meridian crosses the pages once in their cyclic order, with only the single recorded half-twist shear. These facts justify the paths used in \eqref{eq:fa-17}.

At a distinguished odd seam one uses the original puncture-and-copy exchange, rather than an arbitrary permutation of the three pants punctures. The original matrices and their three full projective edge-return words are given in Section~\ref{sec:fg-3}. Table \eqref{eq:fa-14} transports that specified map between incident blocks with a common longitude parameter.

Together, \eqref{eq:fa-10}, \eqref{eq:fa-14}, and the period equations provide the positive cells and link data used by that completion. The \hyperref[sec:qe]{analytic criterion} keeps every angular parameter below \(0.00111\), within the radius \(0.01\) proved here. The complete conclusion and its precise class of diagrams are stated in the \hyperref[ch:quantitative-circle-trees]{quantitative volume theorem}.

\section{Completing the prescribed fillings and fixing the volume constant}\label{sec:fg}

The octahedral cells of an ordered tree of crossing circles stay positive while their angular longitude parameters vary in a fixed small disk. This section proves that the parameter path selected by the analytic filling equations completes those cells to the filled link exterior. It follows the full logarithmic period of each primitive filling curve, so the completed meridian makes exactly one turn at the endpoint. It then identifies the absolute volume with the continued critical action.

The \hyperref[sec:ft]{ordered decomposition} supplies the original topology and primitive curves. The \hyperref[sec:fa]{angular charts} supply the positive cells and their exact face maps. The \hyperref[sec:qe]{analytic criterion} supplies the path under the explicit bound in \eqref{eq:fg-4}. Together these establish the geometric part of the \hyperref[ch:quantitative-circle-trees]{quantitative volume theorem}. Only the original crossing circles are filled; the main knot cusp remains complete and unfilled.

\subsection{Exact topological and local inputs}\label{sec:fg-1}

Fix a finite circle-only Conway tree with at least two circle vertices. Every edge is a physical quarter-turn and every vertex is an original crossing circle. Keep its ribbon orders, projection-copy labels, half twists and primitive disk curves.

The \hyperref[sec:ft]{ordered cut theorem} supplies three marked facts.

\begin{itemize}
\item
  The original projection and disk-page cuts give two octahedra per edge, with the six vertices and face maps of the specified two-leaf \hyperref[sec:fa]{angular chart}. Any transported corner permutations and projection-copy changes are retained.
\item
  At each original circle, outgoing and incoming pages are joined in their cyclic order. The retained half twist occurs at its distinguished original seam. The face maps keep their full three-point labels.
\item
  The disk longitude is the boundary loop with period \(V_v=2i\tau_v\). The original meridian concatenates the local meridional page paths, with the single half-longitude shear \(-\epsilon_vV_v/2\). These are the original primitive curves.
\end{itemize}

Physical \(T^{-1}\), \(T\), and retained planar half-turns are not identified by source involutivity. Reversing a disk frame reverses both its paths and its peripheral coordinate. All formulas below use the fixed original disk basis.

The local positive eight-tetrahedron chart is valid on
\begin{equation}
|\tau_v|,|\tau_w|\le10^{-2}.
\label{eq:fg-1}
\end{equation}
Its incoming pants edge ratios are \((-Q^{-1},-Q,-Q^{-1})\), and its outgoing ratios are their inverses, where \(Q=e^{i\tau_v}\). Thus the marked seam cancels the full complex shears, and its two interior-angle branches sum to \(2\pi\). This includes both projection copies. At zero these are the two regular octahedra in the two-leaf cut.

\subsection{The deformation path and its peripheral logarithms}\label{sec:fg-2}

Let \(F_0\) be the complete positive action, normalized so that \(2\pi F_0\) is the volume of the original augmented exterior. There are two regular octahedra per tree edge at completeness. Let
\begin{equation}
\Psi(\boldsymbol\tau)=\sum_{\{v,w\}}\psi(\tau_v,\tau_w),
 \qquad \Psi(0)=F_0,
 \qquad
 \psi_{\tau_v}=-\frac1\pi\operatorname{arsinh}
          \bigl(\tan(\tau_v/2)\cos(\tau_w/2)\bigr).
\label{eq:fg-2}
\end{equation}
The local action is separately even, with its complete branch and constant. Fix the original signs \(\epsilon_v\in\{0,\pm1\}\), integral fillings \(p_v\), and powers \(n_v=\epsilon_v-2p_v\). The \hyperref[sec:qe]{analytic criterion} supplies an analytic first-charge path \(\boldsymbol\tau(c)\), \(0\le c\le1\), with
\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\tau_v}+\frac{i n_v\tau_v}{2\pi}-ic=0,\qquad
 \boldsymbol\tau(0)=0,\qquad
 |\tau_v(c)|<10^{-2},\qquad
 \Im\tau_v(c)<0\quad(c>0).
\label{eq:fg-3}
\end{equation}
Write \(D_v\) for the circle-tree degree. The sufficient condition and pointwise bound are
\begin{equation}
\sum_v\frac{D_v}{D_v^2+n_v^2}\le2^{-25},\qquad
 |\tau_v(c)|\le
 \frac{2\pi c}{(1-1/50)\sqrt{D_v^2+n_v^2}},
\label{eq:fg-4}
\end{equation}
The common bound is less than \(0.00111\), inside \eqref{eq:fg-1}. The geometric proof only uses the explicit path properties \eqref{eq:fg-3}.

In a pure local block the small meridional increment is \(-2\pi\psi_{\tau_v}\). The ordered cut theorem consequently gives the original peripheral logarithms
\begin{equation}
U_v=-2\pi\Psi_{\tau_v}-i\epsilon_v\tau_v,
                      \qquad V_v=2i\tau_v.
\label{eq:fg-5}
\end{equation}
Their exponentials are the holonomy multipliers. Section~\ref{sec:fg-4} proves that the chosen lifts are also the log-link periods. The stationary equation gives
\begin{equation}
U_v+p_vV_v=-2\pi ic.
\label{eq:fg-6}
\end{equation}
The primitive basis is not reset when \(U_v\) approaches \(-2\pi i\).

\subsection{Positive metric on the cut-and-glued exterior}\label{sec:fg-3}

Realize every local block by its positive tetrahedra and perform the original identifications in Section~\ref{sec:fg-1}. Within a block the rational face identities retain the full edge return map. At a page seam the inverse ratios give both zero shear and angle \(2\pi\), not only equality of multiplier moduli. Their branches are continued from the complete cells.

At the distinguished odd seam use the original cross-copy maps, rather than the pure inverse-ratio identification. In the local leaf frame of the angular chart they are
\begin{equation}
A=\begin{pmatrix}R&-R\\1&ER-1-E\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}R/E-1-1/E&R\\-1&R\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:fg-6a}
\end{equation}
They send respectively \((Z,\infty,1)\) to \((Z/E,R,0)\) and \((Z,R,0)\) to \((Z/E,\infty,1)\). Thus they specify all three ideal vertices and the exchange of projection copies. With the four original white maps \(W_j\), direct multiplication gives the full projective edge returns
\begin{equation}
W_0^{-1}BW_2^{-1}A=I,\qquad
 W_2^{-1}BW_0^{-1}A=I,\qquad
 W_1^{-1}BW_3^{-1}A=I.
\label{eq:fg-6b}
\end{equation}
The three products are projective identities under
\(Z^2-(1+E)Z+ER=0\). To use them for different incident blocks,
normalize one shaded triangle by its three ordered vertices. The
edge-ratio table then determines the second triangle from the common
\(Q=e^{i\tau_v}\) alone. Hence the whole odd seam, including its
specified puncture exchange, is independent of the far endpoints.
Its edge return is the identity by \eqref{eq:fg-6b}, and its angle
branch is \(2\pi\) by positivity and continuation. Both crossing signs
keep their distinct primitive words and the correction in
\eqref{eq:fg-5}.

This gives a nonsingular local hyperbolic metric at every finite point of the original exterior. Face pairings are isometries, edge links have angle \(2\pi\) and identity return, and a finite subdivision vertex has a nonsingular spherical metric on its topological sphere link. Its complete local spherical development is the ordinary sphere. No global injectivity of the developing map is assumed.

The horospherical triangles form a nonsingular similarity structure on each topological cusp torus. Main puncture holonomies are the nontrivial parabolics of the local pants and remain so in the fixed local charts. The primitive main meridian is one of these local words. Its commutant is the translation group at its flag. Hence each main torus has a complete Euclidean translation link.

For \(c>0\), \eqref{eq:fg-3} and \eqref{eq:fg-5} give
\begin{equation}
\Re V_v=-2\Im\tau_v>0.
\label{eq:fg-7}
\end{equation}
The auxiliary peripheral group has a nonunit dilation and a second common fixed point \(q_v\). Its nonsingular similarity development misses \(q_v\). Indeed \((z-q_v)\partial_z\) is a globally invariant vector field; each possible zero has index \(+1\), whereas the total index on a torus is zero.

On the simply connected link cover put
\begin{equation}
f_v=\log(\operatorname{dev}_v-q_v).
\label{eq:fg-8}
\end{equation}
Its real derivative is invertible. The metric \(|df_v|^2\) descends to a nonsingular Euclidean metric on the compact torus, and is complete. Geodesic path lifting makes \(f_v\) a covering of the Euclidean plane; since the plane is simply connected, it is a diffeomorphism. Its periods form an injective real rank-two lattice.

\subsection{The periods throughout the charge path}\label{sec:fg-4}

Let \(P_{\mu_v},P_{\lambda_v}\) be the periods of \eqref{eq:fg-8} on the retained primitive paths. Their exponentials are the peripheral multipliers, so
\begin{equation}
P_{\mu_v}-U_v,\quad P_{\lambda_v}-V_v
                                         \in2\pi i\mathbb Z.
\label{eq:fg-9}
\end{equation}
These differences are continuous for \(0<c\le1\). The link remains nonsingular, its development avoids the second fixed point, and the paths and flags are retained. Choose a logarithm at one basepoint and transport it continuously; rebasing adds a constant and does not change a period.

Fix the integers at the complete end of the path. Choose a continuous
peripheral frame with the selected flag at infinity, and write the
longitude as \(z\mapsto a(c)z+b(c)\), where \(a(c)=e^{V_v(c)}\).
At completeness \(a(0)=1\) and \(b(0)\ne0\). For \(c>0\),
\eqref{eq:fg-7} gives \(a(c)\ne1\), so the second fixed point is
\(q_v(c)=b(c)/(1-a(c))\), which escapes to infinity as \(c\downarrow0\).

For this fixed finite tree, the developed fixed-generator paths remain in one bounded region and have bounded Euclidean lengths as \(c\downarrow0\). They use finitely many triangles and transition maps, all continuous in the complete charts. If their images lie in \(|z|\le B\) and their lengths are at most \(L\), their logarithmic increments obey
\begin{equation}
|P_\gamma(c)|\le\frac{L}{|q_v(c)|-B}\longrightarrow0.
\label{eq:fg-9a}
\end{equation}
Meanwhile \eqref{eq:fg-5} gives \(U_v,V_v\to0\). Each integer difference in \eqref{eq:fg-9} is therefore zero for sufficiently small \(c\), and hence on all of \((0,1]\). This fixes the winding without importing a small-neighborhood completion theorem or resetting principal logs at the endpoint.

Equivalently, each page increment in \eqref{eq:fg-5} is small in its local two-leaf chart. Changes of a pants frame add logarithmic constants at the ends of a page path; those constants telescope around the circle. Its meridian accumulates the sum of the increments, even when high degree makes that sum close to \(-2\pi i\). The preceding continuity argument fixes the possible integer.

The slope period is thus \(-2\pi ic\). Its complementary primitive curve is the original longitude: the determinant of \((\mu_v+p_v\lambda_v,\lambda_v)\) in the original basis is one. Its positive core translation length is
\begin{equation}
\ell_v=\Re V_v=-2\Im\tau_v.
\label{eq:fg-10}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cone completion and the smooth endpoint}\label{sec:fg-5}

The geometric end contains all sufficiently high vertical rays over its similarity triangles. Send its fixed points to zero and infinity, put \(r=y/|z|\), and use
\begin{equation}
R=\operatorname{arsinh}(1/r),\quad
 \theta=\Im f_v,\quad
 X=\Re f_v+\tfrac12\log(1+r^2).
\label{eq:fg-11}
\end{equation}
The metric is exactly
\begin{equation}
dR^2+\cosh^2R\,dX^2+\sinh^2R\,d\theta^2.
\label{eq:fg-12}
\end{equation}
Compactness of the similarity link shows that the end contains a whole punctured tube \(0<R<R_0\). Its primitive meridian period is \(-2\pi ic\), and its independent longitude has positive real part \eqref{eq:fg-10}. Adding the axis produces a cone solid torus of angle \(2\pi c\), with the specified topological meridian. At \(c=1\) it is an ordinary smooth solid torus.

Main cusps remain complete horoball quotients, and the remaining middle is compact. Thus the \(c=1\) metric is complete and has finite volume on the original filled topology. The complete universal local development covers simply connected hyperbolic space, so the resulting representation is the geometric holonomy. When the original component test gives one main component, the metric is on that original knot complement. Its main cusp is unfilled.

The intermediate cone description makes no discreteness claim for \(0<c<1\). It records the positive metric and its link completion; smoothness is asserted at \(c=1\).

\subsection{Absolute action and the primitive normalization}\label{sec:fg-6}

We calibrate volume on the family of cells already constructed. There
are two parts: the regulator gives its differential, and the complete
cell sum fixes the additive constant. The comparison with the filled
metric must use the same oriented fundamental chain.

Put \(U_v^0=-2\pi\Psi_{\tau_v}\) and
\begin{equation}
G^0=2\pi i\left(\Psi-\sum_v\tau_v\Psi_{\tau_v}\right).
\label{eq:fg-13}
\end{equation}
Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
dG^0=\tfrac12\sum_vV_v\,dU_v^0.
\label{eq:fg-14}
\end{equation}
The half twist changes \(U_v^0\) to \(U_v=U_v^0-\epsilon_vV_v/2\). Its marked primitive is therefore
\begin{equation}
G=G^0-\sum_v\frac{\epsilon_vV_v^2}{8}
 =2\pi i\left(\Psi-\sum_v\tau_v\Psi_{\tau_v}\right)
                          +\sum_v\frac{\epsilon_v\tau_v^2}{2}.
\label{eq:fg-15}
\end{equation}
This is the Legendre transform of the circle-edge action
\eqref{eq:qe-9}, whose derivatives are identified with the local
periods in \eqref{eq:fa-15}--\eqref{eq:fa-17}. Its complete constant
is retained. In particular \(\Im G(0)=2\pi F_0\).

The original boundary fundamental-chain differential, applied to these positive blocks and their marked paths, gives
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Vol}(\rho)
                  =\Im G-\tfrac12\sum_v\Re U_v\Im V_v.
\label{eq:fg-16}
\end{equation}
The constant is fixed at the complete assembly. Every local shape stays in a simply connected positive neighborhood of \(i\), and Section~\ref{sec:fg-4} retains the period lifts. No volume period is reset. The positive tetrahedral volume sum also shows directly that the volume tends to \(2\pi F_0\) as \(c\downarrow0\).

We now compare the representation volume in \eqref{eq:fg-16}
with the volume of the metric just constructed. Both are computed from the same oriented cell chain:
the eight positive tetrahedra at each original tree edge, with the
face identifications of Section~\ref{sec:ft}. To make the comparison
at an auxiliary end explicit, use the same finite boundary
triangulation and send its two fixed points to zero and infinity.
Here \(w\) ranges over a fixed fundamental cross section and
\(Y\) is a height parameter tending to infinity. Write the ideal
conical map as \((a(w),Yb(w))\), where \(a(w)\in\mathbb C\)
and \(b(w)>0\). The logarithmic link coordinate
\eqref{eq:fg-8} supplies a real function \(\eta\) whose deck increments
are the real peripheral periods, so \((0,Ye^{\eta(w)})\) is an
equivariant axis-valued comparison map. The homotopy
\[
 H_s(w,Y)=\left(sa(w),
     Y\exp\bigl((1-s)\eta(w)+s\log b(w)\bigr)\right),
                 \qquad 0\le s\le1,
\]
uses one shared assignment on every boundary face. It is equivariant
because \(a,b,e^\eta\) transform respectively by the peripheral
complex multiplier, its modulus, and its modulus. On the compact
fundamental cross section its horizontal derivatives are bounded,
its height is comparable to \(Y\), and its height derivatives are
\(O(Y)\). Thus the hyperbolic volume form
\(y^{-3}dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dy\) bounds the volume of this boundary
homotopy by \(O(Y^{-2})\), which tends to zero.

For each fixed \(0<c\le1\), the same comparison applies to the cone
tube of Section~\ref{sec:fg-5}. It compares the metric away from the
axis, which contributes no three-dimensional volume; it does not
assert smooth filling or descent to the filled group when \(c<1\). At \(c=1\), its meridian is the primitive curve
\(\mu_v+p_v\lambda_v\), with period \(-2\pi i\). Its real period
vanishes, so the axis assignment extends over that meridian disk;
extend the same boundary triangulation through the solid torus
with its added vertices on the axis. All added tetrahedra have zero
volume. The main cusp is unchanged, and its horoball comparison
also has vanishing boundary volume. Consequently the original representation chain and the completed
metric have the same absolute volume:
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Vol}(\rho)=\operatorname{Vol}_c.
\label{eq:fg-16a}
\end{equation} This is Proposition~\ref{prop:app-same-chain-volume} applied to this
original fundamental chain; it requires the primitive
completion and the nonsingular links already proved here, rather
than a small bound on the total peripheral logarithms.

Define the stationary charge action
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C(c)=\operatorname*{stat}_{\boldsymbol\tau}
 \left[\Psi+\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_vn_v\tau_v^2
                                  -ic\sum_v\tau_v\right].
\label{eq:fg-17}
\end{equation}
At its critical point, set \(\Phi=G/(2\pi i)\). Substitution of \eqref{eq:fg-3} gives the exact cancellation
\begin{equation}
\Phi-\mathcal C(c)=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\sum_vp_v\tau_v^2,
 \qquad
 \tfrac12\Re U_v\Im V_v=p_v\Im(\tau_v^2).
\label{eq:fg-18}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Vol}_c
                                     =2\pi\Re\mathcal C(c).
\label{eq:fg-19}
\end{equation}
The primitive normalization has an independent differential check:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dc}\,2\pi\Re\mathcal C(c)
                                  =2\pi\sum_v\Im\tau_v.
\label{eq:fg-20}
\end{equation}
The core is represented by the complementary primitive curve \(\lambda_v\), its length is \eqref{eq:fg-10}, and its cone angle is \(2\pi c\). The finite-cell Schläfli differential \(-\tfrac12\sum_v\ell_v\,d(2\pi c)\) gives \eqref{eq:fg-20}. This checks the factor and sign; \eqref{eq:fg-19} already follows from the marked regulator and the complete anchor, without cone rigidity.

At \(c=1\), \eqref{eq:fg-19} identifies the analytic first-charge rate with the original smooth filled volume. The ordered topological theorem and the analytic criterion supply the topology and path \eqref{eq:fg-3} used in this proof. The finite-source dominance and its exact normalization are proved in the \hyperref[sec:qe]{analytic theorem}.

\subsection{Scope of the completion}\label{sec:fg-7}

The metric argument permits large global meridian logs while every edge block remains uniformly close to its regular geometry. It does not require a globally small framed-representation norm.

The conclusion applies to the ordered all-circle trees of Section~\ref{sec:fg-1} under \eqref{eq:fg-4}, or more generally whenever their analytic path satisfies \eqref{eq:fg-3}. It does not assert that every bounded crossing power gives a hyperbolic filling. The degree-weighted bound permits some small powers because the resulting local angular changes remain small while the primitive meridian is assembled from many sectors.
