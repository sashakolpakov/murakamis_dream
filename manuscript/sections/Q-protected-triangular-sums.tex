\chapter{Protected triangular sums of certified diagrams}
\label{app:protected-triangular-sums}

This appendix proves a constructive closure theorem for full source
certificates. The inputs are actual marked simple-sphere diagrams whose
phase-uniform local envelopes admit an unabridged finite shared-radius
certificate. The output is their literal protected triangular sum. Its
full source has a strict complementary gap at both level parities, and
its independently sufficiently long fixed signed fillings satisfy the
volume limit for the actual new knot.

A shared-radius certificate uses one positive weight for each ordinary
white face, with the same weight at every occurrence of that face.
The join below identifies one ordinary face of each input. Homogeneity
lets us normalize both input certificates at that face, while an exact
local inequality accounts for its single output coefficient target.
If the inputs satisfy weak balance, the output does too. The more
general certificate hypothesis also yields marked examples outside
weak balance.

\begin{theorem}[Closure under protected triangular sums]\label{pts:closure}
Let $D_1,\ldots,D_k$, $k\ge2$, be marked matched spheres as defined in
Section~\ref{pts:sec-1}. Suppose each satisfies the certificate
hypothesis~\eqref{pts:eq-5}, with gap $\gamma_j>0$. Any fixed finite
assembly using the operation of Section~\ref{pts:sec-2} has such a
certificate with gap at least $\min_j\gamma_j$.

Fix a half-twist pattern whose strand connections in the assembled
diagram form one knot, with no additional closed main component.
All independently sufficiently long fixed signed primitive fillings
then satisfy the full reduced-invariant volume limit~\eqref{pts:eq-22}.
The threshold may depend on this entire fixed diagram and pattern.
Section~\ref{pts:sec-7} gives arbitrarily large examples whose marked
complete packings fail weak balance, starting from a seventeen-region
finite-cover certificate.
\end{theorem}

\section{The diagrams, local envelopes, and exact certificate
hypothesis}\label{pts:sec-1}

An input \(D\) is a fixed connected oriented simple triangulated white
sphere, a perfect matching of its dual triangles, the literal
inverse-cut diagram in \(S^3\), its cyclic crossing orders and disk
framings, and a marked nonmatching edge \(uv\). The two protected face
variables are exactly \(L_u=L_v=0\). Every other vertex has the original
target \(d=N-1\). Write \(R_D\) for their number, which is also the
number of matching regions.

The white sphere is the combinatorial record of the two reflected
polyhedral pieces obtained by cutting the augmented diagram. A matched
pair of white triangles records one crossing circle and its
twice-punctured disk. The inverse-cut construction retains the order of
the four adjacent white faces and the disk framing; these are part of
the data, not choices made after the gluing. Let \(Y_D\) denote the
complete augmented exterior in \(S^3\), before filling its
crossing-circle cusps and with the main strand components retained. Its
positive convex cell realization is supplied by the simple-sphere
construction in Appendix~\ref{app:balanced-terminal-fans}, equivalently \href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/actual_balanced_terminal_fan_volume.html}{the
terminal-fan theorem, §§2--4}. The literal diagram realization and
primitive disk curves are those of
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/actual_morse_geometric_contribution.html}{the general Morse
geometric theorem, §§1--2}, also given in Appendix~\ref{app:morse-geometric-contribution}.

Use the source-normalized complete triangle potential \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-1}
Q(w_1,w_2,w_3)=
 \max_{\substack{\alpha_i\ge0\\\sum\alpha_i=1/2\\
                  \alpha_i=0\ \text{if}\ w_i=0}}
 \left\{\sum_i f(\alpha_i)+\sum_{w_i>0}\alpha_i\log w_i\right\},
 \quad
 f(t)=\frac{\Lambda(\pi t)+\Lambda(\pi/2-\pi t)}{\pi}.
\end{equation} Here \(\Lambda(s)=-\int_0^s\log|2\sin t|\,dt\) is the Lobachevsky
function; only \(0\le t\le1/2\) occurs in \(f(t)\). Zero coordinates are
deleted from the supported simplex. Thus, for example,
\(Q(0,0,B)=\tfrac12\log B\) exactly. At an ordered quartet
\((x,b,c,y)\), the input and output triangles are \((x,b,y)\) and
\((x,y,c)\). Put \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-2}
\begin{split}
 A_C&=Q(x,b,y)+Q(x,c,y),\\
 A_L&=\max\{\log c,Q(x,b,y)+\tfrac12\log c\},\\
 A_R&=\max\{\log b,Q(x,c,y)+\tfrac12\log b\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation} left is absent when \(c\) is protected; right is absent when \(b\) is
protected. These are the proved bounds for the actual local path pieces,
uniform in their real spectral parameter and all face phases. central
includes its original odd central row. The physical reflected order,
rather than an arbitrary relabeling of a matrix, supplies right.

For each locally allowed label string \(\omega\), define \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-3}
G_{D,\omega}(q)
       =\sum_e A_{e,\omega_e}(e^q)-\sum_{f\ne u,v}q_f,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_{D,\omega}=\inf_{q\in\mathbb R^{R_D}}G_{D,\omega}(q).
\end{equation} Protected coordinates are kept zero in this notation. Complete
simple-sphere geometry gives a finite positive stationary vector, modulo
a common scale, and \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-4}
\mathcal C_{D,C}=\Phi_D
        =\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(Y_D)}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Certificate hypothesis.} There is \(\gamma_D>0\) such that for
every locally allowed string \(\omega\ne C\), a positive shared ordinary
vector \(L^{D,\omega}\) satisfies \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-5}
G_{D,\omega}(\log L^{D,\omega})
                  \le\Phi_D-\gamma_D.
\end{equation} Different strings may use different vectors. Each vector is shared by
all incidences of its face; there is no independently chosen weight for
a triangle corner or crossing incidence.

A finite branch cover using maxima of the allowed local rates is
sufficient for \eqref{pts:eq-5}, by assigning each string to a branch that covers it.
There are finitely many strings for a fixed input. Conversely, one
witness per string is a finite cover. This is a hypothesis about the
original phase-uniform envelopes, not merely a bound for a signed
coefficient at a selected spectrum.

The cover must be unabridged: it covers every locally allowed noncentral
string, including one whose final shared coefficient might vanish
identically for a separate reason. A certificate that omits strings by a
global support or cancellation argument is not automatically an input to
this theorem. No transfer of such an omission is assumed.
Protected-overloaded labels are the only automatic omissions used here.

Multiplying all ordinary weights by \(t>0\) increases each triangle
potential by \(\tfrac12\log t\), and each local rate by \(\log t\).
Since there are \(R_D\) rates and \(R_D\) ordinary targets, every \(G_{D,\omega}\) is
invariant under \(q\mapsto q+t\mathbf1\). Thus a whole witness may
always be normalized at any specified ordinary face.

\section{The oriented protected triangular
sum}\label{pts:sec-2}

Choose a triangle incident to the marked edge in each of two inputs.
Identify their three boundary vertices so that the protected pair is
\(u,v\), the ordinary third vertex is \(B\), and their matched boundary
sides agree. Record the orientations as \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-6}
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &\text{removed triangle}&\text{original quartet}\\ \hline
 D_1&(u,v,B)&(u,v,c,B)\\
 D_2&(u,B,v)&(u,a,v,B).
 \end{array}
\end{equation} The two removed faces have opposite orientations. The ordinary
vertices \(a,c\) are distinct after relabeling the interiors. If a
reflection of an entire input is needed to achieve these conventions,
transport its complete cyclic orders, source labels and crossing signs
with that reflection. The envelope functions and absolute volume are
unchanged.

Delete the two faces, glue their boundary triangles, remove their two
matching pairs, and pair their former partners. The retained triangles
are \[
                  (u,a,B),\qquad(u,B,c),
\] so the merged crossing has the exact quartet \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-7}
(u,a,c,B).
\end{equation} Keep every other oriented face, matching edge and local order.

The two punctured spheres are triangulated disks with disjoint interiors
and no additional common vertices. Their union is a simple oriented
triangulated sphere. The merged matching edge \(uB\) has four distinct
incident vertices; all remaining matching pairs were already valid. The
edge \(uv\) is retained and remains nonmatching. Every triangle is
paired once. The count is \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-8}
R=R_1+R_2-1,\qquad
 |V|=R+2,\quad |F|=2R,\quad |E|=3R.
\end{equation} Only \(u,v,B\) are identified; every other ordinary coordinate in one
input is distinct from every such coordinate in the other.

The original inverse-cut construction therefore realizes the new data as
an augmented link in \(S^3\), with its own primitive disk curves. This
is an operation on recorded diagrams, not only an abstract tensor
contraction. A knot-producing pattern can be obtained directly. The
untwisted physical strands form projection circles, and a box joins the
two circles that pass through its disk. Their adjacency graph is
connected because the original four-valent diagram is connected. Choose
a spanning tree, switch precisely its boxes to the odd terminal pairing,
and leave all others even. Each tree edge joins two distinct current
components; induction leaves a single main component. Either odd sign is
allowed. This is the marked construction of
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/research_matched_sphere_morse_source.html}{the inverse-cut source
theorem, §3}. The theorem permits any parity choice passing the
new diagram's original one-component test, with no internally closed
extra main component. It does not assert that arbitrary old parity
choices can be merged without that test.

\section{The complete action and the original target
correction}\label{pts:sec-3}

Put \(q_B=\log B\), \(Q_a=Q(0,a,B)\), and \(Q_c=Q(0,c,B)\). The two old
complete bridge rates and the new one are \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-9}
C_1=\tfrac12q_B+Q_c,\qquad
 C_2=Q_a+\tfrac12q_B,\qquad
 X=Q_a+Q_c=C_1+C_2-q_B.
\end{equation}

Every other complete triangle rate is retained. On the other hand,
the combined ordinary target at \(B\) is counted once, rather than once
in each input. Write \(P_i=G_{D_i,C}\) and
\(P_{\mathrm{new}}=G_{\mathrm{new},C}\). Thus \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-10}
P_{\mathrm{new}}(q_1,q_2)
                   =P_1(q_1)+P_2(q_2)
\end{equation} whenever the two displayed vectors agree at \(B\). This is an
identity of the full shared functions, not an inequality between
separately optimized local terms.

Normalize both complete minimizers by \(q_B=0\). They then combine to a
minimizer of \eqref{pts:eq-10}; conversely \eqref{pts:eq-10} is bounded below by the sum of their
minima. Hence \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-11}
\Phi=\Phi_1+\Phi_2.
\end{equation} This also proves that the original complete curvatures on each piece
are unchanged after their common rescaling. The complete simple-sphere
theorem identifies \(2\pi\Phi\) with the actual new augmented volume. It
does not identify independently filled input manifolds with the filled
output manifold.

\section{Every genuine sector at the merged
crossing}\label{pts:sec-4}

The variational formula \eqref{pts:eq-1}, using the simplex vertex at \(B\), gives \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-12}
Q_a,Q_c\ge\tfrac12q_B.
\end{equation} They are strictly larger because \(a,c,B\) are ordinary and positive.
No positivity of \(q_B\) itself is needed.

At the old first crossing only left survives among the one-sided labels,
and at the old second only right survives among them: \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-13}
L_1=\max\{q_c,\tfrac12q_B+\tfrac12q_c\},\qquad
 R_2=\max\{q_a,\tfrac12q_B+\tfrac12q_a\}.
\end{equation} The new local rates are \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-14}
L=\max\{q_c,Q_a+\tfrac12q_c\},\qquad
 R=\max\{q_a,Q_c+\tfrac12q_a\}.
\end{equation} Together \eqref{pts:eq-9} and \eqref{pts:eq-12} give all three required cases: \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-15}
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{new label}&\text{input labels}&\text{rate comparison}\\ \hline
 C&(C,C)&X=C_1+C_2-q_B\\
 L&(L,C)&L\le L_1+C_2-q_B\\
 R&(C,R)&R\le C_1+R_2-q_B .
 \end{array}
\end{equation} For example \[
 L_1+C_2-q_B
  =\max\{q_c+Q_a-\tfrac12q_B,\ Q_a+\tfrac12q_c\}
  \ge\max\{q_c,Q_a+\tfrac12q_c\}=L.
\] The right case is identical with \(a,c\) exchanged. The inequality
retains the mixed branch of the genuine one-sided rate; it is not a
replacement by a pure-middle monomial.

For a whole output string \(\omega\), leave every other local label on
its original input and use \eqref{pts:eq-15} at the merged crossing. This defines
input strings \(\omega_1,\omega_2\). They are locally allowed; no
protected-overloaded side was introduced. If the output is noncentral,
at least one input string is noncentral. The one target correction in
\eqref{pts:eq-10} now gives \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-16}
G_{\mathrm{new},\omega}(q_1,q_2)
             \le G_{1,\omega_1}(q_1)+G_{2,\omega_2}(q_2),
                    \qquad (q_1)_B=(q_2)_B.
\end{equation}

For a noncentral input choose its witness \eqref{pts:eq-5}; for a central input
choose its complete minimizer. Rescale each whole vector to \(B=1\).
Homogeneity changes none of these actions. The vectors now agree on
every shared coordinate: \(u,v\) are zero and \(B=1\). Hence they form
one legal shared Cauchy vector for the new source. Equations \eqref{pts:eq-11} and
\eqref{pts:eq-16} prove \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-17}
G_{\mathrm{new},\omega}\le
       \Phi-\min(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)
                   \qquad(\omega\ne C).
\end{equation} If both input strings are noncentral their losses add. Per-sector
witnesses are essential to this simple statement: the other input can
use its complete minimizer when its induced string is central. There is
no claim that one input witness vector works for every case
simultaneously.

Equivalently, taking infima after the \(B=1\) normalization gives \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-18}
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{new},\omega}
       \le\mathcal C_{1,\omega_1}+\mathcal C_{2,\omega_2}.
\end{equation} No existence of a noncentral capacity minimizer is needed.

\section{The full finite source and the volume
limit}\label{pts:sec-5}

Set \(q_N=e^{\pi i/N}\), \(d=N-1\). The local polynomial \(H_{V_N}\) is
the original homogeneous face numerator of \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-19a}
V_N(\sigma;\Omega)=\chi_N(\sigma)(\sigma-\Omega)^{-1},
 \qquad \chi_N(\sigma)=2T_N(\sigma/2)+2,
\end{equation} where \(\Omega\) is the original two-strand Casimir and the right
side denotes its degree-\(d\) polynomial numerator, including at the
repeated roots. \(T_N\) is the Chebyshev polynomial. In its face
expansion, a recurrence path from index \(j\) to \(i\) has middle powers
\(b^ic^{d-j}\). If \(h=\lfloor d/2\rfloor\), central means \(i\le h\) and
\(d-j\le h\), left means \(d-j>h\), and right means \(i>h\). These
supports are disjoint and exhaustive. This fixes the source
normalization and the meaning of every label used above.

The original full numerator is \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-19}
S_{N,\mathrm{full}}(\boldsymbol\sigma)
 =[\prod_{f\ne u,v}L_f^{N-1}]\,
       \left.\prod_eH_{V_N}(L_x,L_b,L_c,L_y;\sigma_e)
       \right|_{L_u=L_v=0}.
\end{equation} The actual Morse identity includes the unrestricted physical mark,
all cup and cap factors, the even coset and the face measure. No
additional mark or factor \(N\) is introduced at the triangular sum.
Each local polynomial has degree at most \(N-1\) in its spectral
variable. The C/L/R partition and rates \eqref{pts:eq-2} are valid at both parities
and uniformly over all face phases and \(\sigma_e\in[-2,2]\). The
literal coefficient identity is
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/research_matched_sphere_morse_source.html}{the general Morse source
theorem, §§5--6}; the phase-uniform estimates \eqref{pts:eq-2} are the cofactor
bounds used in \href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/general_actual_sector_cover_filling_volume.html}{the
finite-cover theorem} and \href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/general_weak_balance_volume.html}{weak
balance, §§4--6}.

Use the vector supplied by \eqref{pts:eq-17} for each genuine noncentral string.
Cauchy's coefficient inequality, with the original targets \(N-1\),
gives its rate at most \(\Phi-\gamma\), where
\(\gamma=\min(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)>0\). Replacing the normalized targets
by one costs only a fixed logarithmic term divided by \(N\). The finite
number of factors, polynomial prefactors and local uniform errors
contribute \(o(N)\) for the fixed new diagram. There are only finitely
many strings. Therefore \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-20}
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
 \log\|S_{N,\mathrm{full}}-S_{N,C}\|_{[-2,2]^R}
                    \le\Phi-\gamma.
\end{equation} The limit superior includes even, odd and composite levels. This
proves a bound for the entire complementary polynomial, not merely a
fixed endpoint slice.

Apply the finite Hermite functional of Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-filling-bound}, equivalently
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/actual_uniform_filling_supremum_bound.html}{the uniform filling
bound}, to \eqref{pts:eq-20}. It inserts the original power \(R^{n_e}\), including
the normal data at double spectral roots. More precisely, the original
reduced ribbon contraction is \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-20a}
Z_N(\boldsymbol n)=
       \left(\bigotimes_e\mathcal L_N^{n_e}\right)S_{N,\mathrm{full}},
 \qquad
 \left\|\bigotimes_e\mathcal L_N^{n_e}\right\|
       \le\prod_e(1+|n_e|/2).
\end{equation} For fixed integer powers this bound has zero exponential rate, so the
complementary filled contribution has rate at most \(\Phi-\gamma\).

Appendix~\ref{app:morse-geometric-contribution}, equivalently \href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/actual_morse_cross_filling_period.html}{the
Morse period theorem} and
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/actual_morse_geometric_contribution.html}{its geometric
identification}, supplies the nonzero full all-central period for this
original matched sphere. Its selected action \(S_{\boldsymbol p}\) obeys
\begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-21}
\Re S_{\boldsymbol p}\longrightarrow\Phi
 \quad\text{as every }|p_e|\longrightarrow\infty
 \quad\text{independently},\qquad
 2\pi\Re S_{\boldsymbol p}
       =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K_{\boldsymbol p}).
\end{equation} The latter equality uses the new diagram's complete positive cells,
its original meridians and longitudes, the primitive equations
\(U_e+p_eV_e=-2\pi i\), the main cusp left complete, and the same-chain
volume calibration. It is not inferred from \eqref{pts:eq-11} or from a formal
critical value.

Choose the independent long-filling threshold so that the selected real
action is larger than \(\Phi-\gamma/2\), and so that the original
primitive completion and nonzero all-charge asymptotic apply. The
complementary contribution is then exponentially smaller. The full
reduced zero-framed invariant satisfies \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-22}
\boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}
       \log|\langle K_{\boldsymbol p}\rangle_N|
   =\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\setminus K_{\boldsymbol p}).}
\end{equation} Here \(\langle K\rangle_N\) is the original reduced, zero-framed
complete-color Kashaev invariant, with unknot value one. Conversion from
\(Z_N\) retains the original framing/component character, which has
modulus one at complete color; for a knot its enhanced component sign is
one. The full one-cut modified convention retains its additional factor
\(i^{N+3}/N\), whose exponential rate is zero. The knot pattern and
every signed integer \(p_e\), with \(n_e=\epsilon_e-2p_e\), are fixed
before \(N\) tends to infinity. No ratio bound between the large
fillings or uniform threshold over growing diagrams is asserted.

\section{Arbitrarily many blocks and weak balance as an
input}\label{pts:sec-6}

Take any finite number \(k\ge2\) of certified marked inputs. Repeatedly
perform the operation of Section~\ref{pts:sec-2} at the retained common marked edge
\(uv\), identifying the chosen ordinary third vertex of the next block
with the ordinary third vertex on the selected incident triangle of the
current sphere. The previous ordinary coordinates are retained; only the
two chosen third vertices are identified. At each step use fresh
interior vertices.

After every step the sphere is simple and \(uv\) is still nonmatching,
so it has two incident triangles available for the next step. Thus the
construction has no upper bound on \(k\). Induction on \eqref{pts:eq-8}, \eqref{pts:eq-11} and
\eqref{pts:eq-17} gives \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-23}
R_{\mathrm{new}}=\sum_{j=1}^kR_j-(k-1),\qquad
 \Phi_{\mathrm{new}}=\sum_{j=1}^k\Phi_j,\qquad
 \gamma_{\mathrm{new}}\ge\min_{1\le j\le k}\gamma_j.
\end{equation} The exact witnesses are obtained recursively. At the next attachment
one may rescale the entire previous witness to normalize its new joining
face; protected zeros remain zero and all existing incidence
identifications remain intact. One does not normalize already shared
faces independently. The limiting statement always fixes this finite
assembled diagram first.

If each input packing is weak-balanced, the new complete packing retains
all their unmerged local comparisons. At the new crossing, \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-24}
X=Q_a+Q_c\ge C_1\ge\log c,\qquad
 X\ge C_2\ge\log a,
\end{equation} where the first comparisons use \eqref{pts:eq-12}. They are in fact strict at this
merged crossing. Thus protected triangular sums of weak-balanced inputs
are themselves weak-balanced. The existing power-flattening theorem
supplies their unabridged certificates. This gives a direct constructive
family, with unrestricted finite numbers of certified pieces and
arbitrary cycles already present inside those pieces.

More generally the theorem uses \eqref{pts:eq-5}, which includes refined per-sector
or finite-branch shared-radius certificates. It does not assert that the
result lies outside all previously known knot presentations. Its
contribution is the closure proof retaining the literal matching and the
single original target at every face.

\section{Arbitrarily large certified marked diagrams outside weak
balance}\label{pts:sec-7}

The closure is not confined to weak-balanced inputs. This statement
concerns the recorded diagram and mark: a knot might have another
presentation or mark satisfying a different criterion.

First recall why weak balance implies an anchored witness at the same
mark. At the complete vector \(q^*\), let
\(P(q)=\sum_eX_e(q)-\sum_fq_f\), where \(X_e=A_C\), and put \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-25}
H_e=\max\{X_e,q_b,q_c\},
\end{equation} omitting protected logarithms. The entropy proof of
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/general_weak_balance_volume.html}{weak balance, §§3--5} gives
\(h_*>0\), a finite \(\mathcal E\ge0\), and, for \(q(t)=(1-t)q^*\),
\(0<t<1\), \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-26}
P(q(t))\le\Phi+\tfrac12t^2\mathcal E,\qquad
 X_e(q(t))-A_s(q(t))\ge t h_*
                   \quad\text{for every surviving side }s.
\end{equation} The positivity of \(h_*\) uses the overloaded triangle, which has at
least two ordinary corners: the matched outer edge cannot be the
protected nonmatching edge. Since \(A_L\ge q_c\) and \(A_R\ge q_b\),
\eqref{pts:eq-26} implies \(H_e(q(t))=X_e(q(t))\). Thus every fixed surviving anchor
satisfies \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-27}
A_{a,s}(q(t))+\sum_{e\ne a}H_e(q(t))-\sum_fq_f(t)
 \le\Phi+\tfrac12t^2\mathcal E-t h_*<\Phi
\end{equation} for a sufficiently small positive \(t\). This proves the anchored
implication without treating equality in the original balance test as a
strict loss.

Now take the original width-three source with word \begin{equation}\label{pts:eq-28}
(1,3,0,2,4,1,3,0,2,4,1,3,0,2,4,1,3),\qquad
 \{u,v\}=\{0,16\}.
\end{equation} Its construction and rational certificates are given in
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/actual_shared_radius_certificate_separation.html}{the separation
theorem}. The exact data are
\href{https://github.com/sashakolpakov/murakamis_dream/blob/main/experiments/actual_sector_cover_certificate.json}{the
forty-branch certificate} and
\href{https://github.com/sashakolpakov/murakamis_dream/blob/main/experiments/actual_shared_radius_anchor_obstruction_certificate.json}{the
anchor certificate}; their verifiers are
\href{https://github.com/sashakolpakov/murakamis_dream/blob/main/experiments/audit_actual_sector_cover.py}{the cover verifier}
and
\href{https://github.com/sashakolpakov/murakamis_dream/blob/main/experiments/audit_actual_shared_radius_anchor_obstruction.py}{the
anchor verifier}. They retain the original compiler, mark, local orders,
and all ordinary targets.

There are seventeen matching regions. Put \(J=\{2,4,7,12\}\). The first
fifteen cover branches prescribe right on a nonempty subset of \(J\),
allow central/left on its complement in \(J\), and allow all surviving
labels elsewhere. In the remaining case no region in \(J\) is right;
choose any surviving one-sided anchor, allow central/left at the other
regions of \(J\), and all surviving labels elsewhere. There are
twenty-five such anchor branches. Every locally allowed noncentral
string is covered by this combinatorial division, whether or not its
global coefficient vanishes. Thus this is exactly the unabridged
hypothesis \eqref{pts:eq-5}. The rational bounds prove a uniform gap greater than
\(0.04\).

The same data locate a weak-balance failure away from a usable joining

\widetilde G(q)=A_{12,R}(q)+

bounded by \(X_e\); no replacement by \(H_e\) at an unmixed spectator is
required. The certificate gives the flow entropy greater than
\(19.95975965735\) and a complete-action upper bound less than

If all four crossings in \(J\) were weak-balanced at the complete

are face labels, while $e^{q_{11}}$ and $e^{q_{14}}$ are their positive
coefficient weights. The last inequality tests the simplex vertex at

It would give \(\widetilde G(q^*)\le P(q^*)=\Phi_{17}\), contradicting

localization only needs the non-strict comparison at completeness; the
flattening in \eqref{pts:eq-26}--\eqref{pts:eq-27} explains the broader anchored implication.

The two marked-edge triangles belong to crossings 12 and 15. At crossing

Attach any fixed number \(r\ge1\) of these tetrahedral inputs, always

              \Phi_r=\Phi_{17}+4r f(1/4).

\section{Scope and related
constructions}\label{pts:sec-8}

Belted sums and their volume behavior form part of the geometric
background. Morgan and collaborators~\cite{MorganEtAlBeltedSums}
study the geometric, combinatorial and diagrammatic classification of
these decompositions. Van der Veen~\cite{VanDerVeenWhiteheadChains}
proves the volume conjecture for the infinite family of Whitehead chains.
No identification of the present ordered
protected operation with those marked constructions is used here. In
particular classical volume additivity itself is not claimed as a new
phenomenon. The statement proved here is closure of the original
phase-uniform source certificates, with every shared target retained,
and its consequence for independently long fixed fillings of the recorded
diagrams.

An arbitrary block need not satisfy \eqref{pts:eq-5}: the
\href{https://sashakolpakov.github.io/murakamis_dream/notes/actual_singleton_sector_envelope_obstruction.html}{fifty-four-region
example} has a genuine fixed-sector envelope capacity exceeding its
complete action by more than \(1/5\). The present proof does not assert
an exact scalar factorization of finite spectral kernels. It proves an
inequality for every genuine sector with its full real spectrum and all
face phases, and therefore retains the normal data required by the
finite filling functional.

The certificate hypothesis cannot be weakened here to an arbitrary
closed-source cancellation estimate, or to a cap estimate with other
spectra fixed. Such estimates need not provide witnesses for \eqref{pts:eq-16}.
Likewise globally omitted strings require an independent support
transfer argument. The theorem changes diagrams by an explicit
operation; it is not a claim that the original input knot type is
preserved.
