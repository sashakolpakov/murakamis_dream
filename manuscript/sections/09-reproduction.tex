\chapter{From complex asymptotics to volume limits}
\label{ch:reproduction}

A volume proof must connect three statements about the same finite
invariant. The original contraction has an exact formula; its entire
remainder is smaller than a nonzero leading contribution; and the real
part of that contribution's action is the volume of the specified
filled manifold. This chapter proves the elementary last analytic
implication and locates its hypotheses in the preceding arguments.
It also separates the written proofs from the repository's finite
certificates and supplementary checks.

\section{From a complex leading term to a logarithmic limit}
\label{sec:proof-dependencies}

We use the ordinary complex modulus; the invariant and its leading
coefficient may have arbitrary phases. It is convenient to express an
upper exponential bound without taking a logarithm at a zero term.
For a real sequence $B_N$ that is eventually nonnegative, write
\[
 \operatorname{UR}_b(B)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall\eta>0\ \exists N_0\ \forall N\ge N_0,\qquad
 B_N\le e^{N(b+\eta)}.
\]
This is an upper-rate bound, not an assertion that a limit exists.
With the extended convention $\log0=-\infty$, it is equivalent to
$\limsup N^{-1}\log B_N\le b$. Indeed an eventual exponential bound
gives the corresponding logarithmic bound; conversely the definition
of the limit superior gives the exponential inequality for each
positive $\eta$. Using $\operatorname{UR}$ as the hypothesis keeps
zero budgets and zero remainder terms within the statement.

\begin{theorem}[A nonzero leading term with a strict remainder gap]
\label{prop:volume-rate-transfer}
Let $(K_N)$ be a complex sequence indexed by positive integer levels.
For the knot application it will be the reduced invariant of one fixed
knot.
Suppose that, for all sufficiently large $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complex-packet-decomposition}
 K_N=u_N\bigl(N^\rho a_N e^{NS_N}+E_N\bigr),
 \qquad |u_N|=1,
\end{equation}
where $\rho\in\RR$ is fixed, $a_N,S_N\in\CC$ satisfy
\[
                a_N\longrightarrow a\ne0,
                \qquad S_N\longrightarrow S.
\]
Assume the bound is on the whole remainder $E_N$, and that for some
real $b<\Re S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entire-remainder-gap}
                  \operatorname{UR}_b(|E|)
\end{equation}
holds. Equivalently, with the extended logarithmic convention,
$\limsup N^{-1}\log|E_N|<\Re S$.
Then $K_N\ne0$ for all sufficiently large $N$, and the ordinary
all-level limit exists:
\[
               \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|K_N|=\Re S.
\]
If, for this same knot complement, a geometric argument proves
$2\pi\Re S=V=\Vol(S^3\setminus K)$, then
\[
                \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N|=V.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $G_N=N^\rho a_Ne^{NS_N}$. Since $a_N\to a\ne0$,
there are constants $0<c<C$ with $c\le|a_N|\le C$ for all
sufficiently large $N$. In particular $G_N\ne0$, and
\[
 \frac1N\log|G_N|
 =\rho\frac{\log N}{N}+\frac{\log|a_N|}{N}+\Re S_N
 \longrightarrow\Re S.
\]
Choose $0<\eta<(\Re S-b)/3$. For all sufficiently large $N$,
\[
       |E_N|\le e^{N(b+\eta)},\qquad
       |G_N|\ge e^{N(\Re S-\eta)}.
\]
Thus $|E_N/G_N|\le e^{-N(\Re S-b-2\eta)}$.
This tends exponentially to zero and is eventually smaller than
$1/2$. The triangle and reverse triangle inequalities give
\[
 1-|E_N/G_N|\le|1+E_N/G_N|\le1+|E_N/G_N|.
\]
Consequently $K_N\ne0$, and
$N^{-1}\log|1+E_N/G_N|\to0$. Taking moduli and logarithms in
$K_N=u_NG_N(1+E_N/G_N)$ proves the claimed ordinary limit.
The geometric identity then gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

A relative error $1+O(N^{-1})$ in a saddle expansion belongs to
$a_N$. It has the same exponential rate as the leading term and
does not satisfy a strict remainder gap by itself. Likewise,
$S_N\to S$ is sufficient for the logarithmic limit, but it does not
justify replacing $e^{NS_N}$ by $e^{NS}$ in a relative asymptotic.
The coefficient $a\ne0$ is the coefficient after all contributions
with the leading action have been combined. A nonzero individual
Gaussian would not establish that assertion if other equally large
terms could cancel it.

\section{Nonnegative costs and upper exponential rates}

An estimate for a source remainder often acquires a level-dependent
factor when the filling operators are applied. The following
comparison is valid even when the factor or the source budget
vanishes.

\begin{lemma}[A subexponential cost preserves an upper rate]
\label{lem:subexponential-remainder-transfer}
Let $B_N,C_N\ge0$ eventually, let $b\in\RR$, and suppose
$\operatorname{UR}_b(B)$ and $\operatorname{UR}_0(C)$.
If $|E_N|\le C_NB_N$ eventually, then $\operatorname{UR}_b(|E|)$.
In the extended-logarithm notation, this is the implication
\[
 \limsup N^{-1}\log B_N\le b,\quad
 \limsup N^{-1}\log C_N\le0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \limsup N^{-1}\log|E_N|\le b.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every $\eta>0$, apply the two upper-rate bounds with
$\eta/2$. Eventually
$B_N\le e^{N(b+\eta/2)}$ and $C_N\le e^{N\eta/2}$.
These inequalities also hold at their zero values. Hence
$|E_N|\le e^{N(b+\eta)}$, which is the required upper-rate
bound. No division by $B_N$
or $C_N$ is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial filling costs]
\label{cor:polynomial-remainder-transfer}
Suppose $B_N\ge0$ and $|E_N|\le C_NB_N$ for all sufficiently
large $N$. Suppose also that
\[
 \operatorname{UR}_b(B),\qquad
 0\le C_N\le C_0N^m
\]
eventually, with fixed $b,m\in\RR$ and $C_0\ge0$.
Then $\operatorname{UR}_b(|E|)$. Equivalently, in the extended
logarithmic convention,
\[
                 \limsup N^{-1}\log|E_N|\le b.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $C_0=0$, the remainder is eventually zero. Otherwise
$N^{-1}\log C_N\le(\log C_0+m\log N)/N$ wherever $C_N>0$,
and the bound at $C_N=0$ is automatic. Apply the lemma.
\end{proof}

These statements transfer an upper bound. They assert no lower
bound for a complex contraction and introduce no assumption of
nonvanishing. Fourier orthogonality can supply a cost estimate;
it does not supply the leading coefficient in
\eqref{eq:complex-packet-decomposition}.

\section{Where the strict Conway proof supplies each hypothesis}

Chapter~\ref{ch:conway-current} uses one source from beginning to
end. Its finite normalization is the reduced invariant with unknot
value one, including the writhe correction. Turns and the final
closing relation are solved exactly before a lattice is chosen.
The remaining cell volume is one.

Theorem~\ref{ca:strict-pure} gives the restricted holomorphic finite sum,
its nonzero local amplitude and the physical-source comparison
\eqref{ca:source-error}. The same theorem bounds every macroscopic
color deficit. Proposition~\ref{ca:Fourier-theorem} proves both
the local Fourier expansion and the gap on the remainder of the
spectral cube. These are distinct hypotheses of the whole
oscillatory filling theorem imported there.

For fixed signed crossing powers $\mathbf n$, the filling
coefficient bound is
\[
 C_N=\prod_j2\sqrt N(1+|n_j|/\sqrt{12}).
\]
It is a nonnegative polynomial cost. The source budget in
\eqref{ca:filled-error} is
$B_N=e^{N(F_0-\gamma_*)}$, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:polynomial-remainder-transfer} gives upper rate
$F_0-\gamma_*$ for the filled physical remainder. The whole filling
theorem gives a nonzero leading amplitude and a critical action
$S_{\mathbf n}$ satisfying
$F_0-\Re S_{\mathbf n}=O(\sum_jn_j^{-2})$.
Increasing the fixed twist threshold makes
$\Re S_{\mathbf n}>F_0-\gamma_*$.

Thus \eqref{ca:asymptotic} has the form
\eqref{eq:complex-packet-decomposition}, with
$\rho=3/2$, $S_N=S_{\mathbf n}$ and a coefficient converging to
$D(\mathbf n)\ne0$. The framing and crossing phases have modulus
one. The primitive geometric comparison proves
$2\pi\Re S_{\mathbf n}=\Vol(S^3\setminus K_{\epsilon,\mathbf p})$
for the same filled diagram. Theorem~\ref{prop:volume-rate-transfer}
then gives the volume limit and eventual nonvanishing.

The written dependency chain is therefore
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{exact finite algebra and primitive coordinates}\\
 \longrightarrow\ \text{strict pure window and physical remainder}\\
 \longrightarrow\ \text{local Fourier expansion and global gap}\\
 \longrightarrow\ \text{whole filling sum with nonzero coefficient}\\
 \longrightarrow\ \text{primitive metric and absolute volume}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The source bounds, whole-sum theorem and metric comparison are named
companion inputs in Chapter~\ref{ch:conway-current}. This display
does not turn their citations into proofs. Their stated hypotheses
and written arguments remain part of the dependency chain.

\section{The independent tree and planar arguments}

The circle-tree arguments use the same full finite kernel, with two
different remainder estimates. The summed estimate is proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:quantitative-sums}; the directed estimate is proved
in Appendix~\ref{app:directed-circle-messages}. The local ratio test
implies the directed degree-or-twist condition by
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-implies-directed}, so its volume conclusion is
a corollary, with no separate quantum induction. The vertexwise
geometric completion in Appendix~\ref{app:local-messages} remains
a separate result. Both quantum proofs use the primitive metric
comparison of Appendix~\ref{app:quantitative-geometry}. These
arguments concern their stated circle-tree construction, rather
than a change of construction class inside the strict proof.

For weak balance, the shared spectral polynomial is split as
$S_N=C_N+D_N$, with $C_N$ central at every crossing region.
Equation~\eqref{wb:remainder} bounds the supremum norm of the entire
$D_N$ on the real spectral cube.
Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-filling-bound} bounds its filled image by
\[
 |E_N|\le\prod_e(1+|n_e|/2)\,\|D_N\|_{[-2,2]^R}.
\]
This is a fixed cost for fixed powers.
Appendix~\ref{app:morse-geometric-contribution} gives the nonzero
central contribution and its filled geometric action. Only after
both estimates are proved is the filling threshold enlarged until
the action drop is smaller than the remainder gap.

There is no circular use of the full decoration conclusion.
The geometric-contribution proof uses the packing construction
and finite Morse source from
Appendix~\ref{app:morse-preliminaries}. The decoration theorem is a
later application, not an input to that proof. Weak balance supplies the complementary-source
bound separately. Protected sums and ordinary triangular insertion
supply their own complete certificates. For a general unchanged
matched sphere, the geometric contribution alone leaves the
complementary-source estimate open.

\section{Written proofs, certificates and checks}

The analytic and geometric arguments are written proofs. Exact
rational certificates are finite parts of specified inequality
proofs: the text reduces the required continuum statements to their
feasibility, coverage and supporting-plane conditions. Optional
finite matrix or numerical checks have a different role. They may
detect an indexing or normalization error; they do not prove a
contour deformation, a metric completion or a limit.

From the repository root, the finite Conway identities can be
checked separately with
\begin{verbatim}
python3 -I papers/conway-asymptotics/check.py
\end{verbatim}
The checker compares Newton-polynomial powers with full
Fourier--Hermite interpolation and the exact midpoint formula.
The all-level finite identities are proved in
Section~\ref{ca:filling}; sampling levels is not their proof.

The exact certificate runner is
\begin{verbatim}
python3 repro/verify.py --output repro/results
\end{verbatim}
It replays the two-leaf and attachment certificates used in the
source estimates, and the optional stronger endpoint margin.
Appendix~\ref{app:certificate} states the mathematical
inequalities and their reduction to finite rational checks.
Candidate generation and numerical optimization are not run by
verification.

The collection has one runner for the finite algebra and constants
used by its supporting papers:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 repro/check_paper_algebra.py \\
  --output repro/results/paper-collection/finite
\end{verbatim}
Individual paper READMEs give narrower commands. These programs check
the identities and certificates specified by each suite. Their finite
examples do not replace the uniform estimates and geometric arguments
in the corresponding chapters.

The dependency record can be checked with
\begin{verbatim}
python3 repro/check_manuscript_logic.py \
  --output repro/results/manuscript-logic
\end{verbatim}
This checks an authored map: premise roles, references, level
scope and circular dependencies. It does not prove the statements
assigned to its vertices. The Lean code proves the explicitly matched Gram identities,
shifted Parseval and Hermite coefficient bound, the linearized stationary
equations, the complex packet theorem, and the whole-color remainder
assembly. It also contains the finite polynomial and supported-graph
lemmas used in the appendices. The analytic contour arguments and
geometric calibration are the written inputs named in those statements.
The replay command is
\begin{verbatim}
python3 repro/check_lean.py --output repro/results/volume-lean
\end{verbatim}
Compilation and axiom inspection do not establish the analytic
source estimates or the geometric hypotheses of an application.

\section{Building the reference}

The TeX source is in \sourcefile{manuscript/}, with native figures
in \sourcefile{manuscript/figures/}. Build it with
\begin{verbatim}
python3 tools/build_manuscript.py
\end{verbatim}
The output is \sourcefile{manuscript/build/main.pdf}.
The build manifest records its input hashes and typesetting
checks; it is not a mathematical certificate. The standalone
Conway paper is built from the shared source with
\begin{verbatim}
python3 tools/build_papers.py conway-asymptotics
\end{verbatim}

The illustrated guide is built separately:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 tools/build_manuscript_guide.py
\end{verbatim}
Documentation builds do not execute research scripts. The guide
also describes exploratory methods, whose open steps are
distinguished from the volume theorems retained here.
