\chapter{Proof inputs and reproduction}\label{ch:reproduction}

The volume theorems combine written analytic and geometric arguments with finite checks of different kinds. The long-twist proof reduces two local inequalities to exact rational certificates supplied with the paper. The quantitative circle-tree proof establishes its uniform bounds analytically; separate finite checks verify its edge formulas, face identifications and peripheral identities. This chapter distinguishes those roles and records the scope of each theorem.

\section{The implication that finishes a volume proof}\label{sec:proof-dependencies}

It is useful to separate three questions. Does the exact invariant contain
the contribution computed by stationary phase, with the stated scalar
normalization? Is the sum of all its other contributions smaller at the
exponential scale? Is the real part of the selected action the volume of
the intended knot complement? An affirmative answer to one of these
questions does not answer the other two.

The final analytic step has the following precise form. Its hypotheses
refer to the entire remainder, including all other charges and contour
terms. Throughout this statement, the diagram and every filling integer
are fixed. We use $\log 0=-\infty$ in an upper bound for a remainder.

\begin{proposition}[Transfer from a contribution to the invariant]
\label{prop:volume-rate-transfer}
Suppose that for all sufficiently large integers $N$ the exact invariant
has the decomposition
\[
 K_N=u_N(G_N+E_N),\qquad G_N=a_Ne^{N\mathcal A},
\]
where $\mathcal A\in\CC$ is independent of $N$, and $u_N,a_N$ are
nonzero with
\[
 \frac1N\log|u_N|\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \frac1N\log|a_N|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Assume there are real numbers $\Phi$ and $\gamma>0$ such that
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|E_N|\le\Phi-\gamma,
 \qquad \Re\mathcal A>\Phi-\gamma.
\]
Then $K_N$ is nonzero for all sufficiently large $N$, and
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log|K_N|=\Re\mathcal A.
\]
If a separate geometric argument proves
$2\pi\Re\mathcal A=\Vol(S^3\setminus K)$ for this same knot,
the volume conjecture follows for $K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $\Delta=\Re\mathcal A-(\Phi-\gamma)>0$ and choose
$0<\eta<\Delta/3$. The remainder bound and the limit for $a_N$
give, for all sufficiently large $N$,
\[
 |E_N|\le e^{N(\Phi-\gamma+\eta)},\qquad
 |G_N|\ge e^{N(\Re\mathcal A-\eta)}.
\]
Consequently $|E_N/G_N|\le e^{-N(\Delta-2\eta)}$. In particular
this ratio is eventually smaller than $1/2$. The triangle and reverse
triangle inequalities bound $|1+E_N/G_N|$ between
$1-e^{-N(\Delta-2\eta)}$ and $1+e^{-N(\Delta-2\eta)}$.
It is nonzero and its logarithm divided by $N$ tends to zero. Taking
absolute values and logarithms in
$K_N=u_Na_Ne^{N\mathcal A}(1+E_N/G_N)$ proves the assertion.
The last conclusion uses the stated geometric identity, which is not
an assumption needed for the preceding analytic limit.
\end{proof}

A relative correction such as $1+O(N^{-1})$ to the first saddle
belongs to $a_N$. It is not put into $E_N$: such a correction has the
same exponential rate as the first saddle. The strict remainder bound
is reserved for the other contributions.

The location of each hypothesis matters. For the recursive tangle
theorem, Chapter~\ref{ch:source} gives the exact invariant and its
normalization. Chapter~\ref{ch:alias} bounds the complementary color
terms. Chapter~\ref{ch:packet} and Appendix~\ref{app:selected-source}
construct the local holomorphic approximation. Chapter~\ref{ch:filling}
and Section~\ref{app:filling-contours} control the rest of the Fourier
period, prove that the leading coefficient is nonzero, and compare the
charge actions. Chapter~\ref{ch:marked} and
Appendix~\ref{app:marked-calibration} identify that action with the
volume of the original primitive filling. The local approximation
alone supplies neither the global remainder bound nor the metric
identification.

For weak balance, write the exact shared spectral polynomial as
$S_N=C_N+D_N$, where $C_N$ uses the central contribution at every
crossing region. Equation~\eqref{wb:remainder} bounds the supremum
norm of $D_N$ on the full real spectral cube. Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-filling-bound}
then bounds its image under the actual filling functional by
\[
 |E_N|\le\prod_e(1+|n_e|/2)\,\|D_N\|_{[-2,2]^R}.
\]
The product is independent of $N$. Appendix~\ref{app:morse-geometric-contribution}
gives the nonzero asymptotic of the filled central contribution and
its geometric action. Only after these two independent estimates have
been established do we increase the fixed twist threshold so that
the drop from $\Phi$ to $\Re\mathcal A$ is smaller than the remainder
gap. Proposition~\ref{prop:volume-rate-transfer} then applies.

There is no circular use of the planar decoration theorem here.
Appendix~\ref{app:morse-geometric-contribution} uses the complete
packing construction and finite Morse source from
Appendix~\ref{app:balanced-terminal-fans}; it does not assume that
appendix's full-invariant volume conclusion. The complementary-source
estimate under weak balance is proved separately in
Appendix~\ref{app:weak-balance}. For a general unchanged matched sphere
that estimate remains an additional requirement.

\section{The scope of the dependency argument}

For the weak-balance and protected-sum results, the order of the
quantifiers is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{fix an admissible diagram and half twists};\qquad
 \exists T\ \forall\boldsymbol p,\\
 \min_e|p_e|>T\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|K_N|
      =\Vol(S^3\setminus K_{\boldsymbol p}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The diagram must satisfy the particular theorem's packing or certificate
hypothesis, and its strand connections must give one knot. Each tuple
$\boldsymbol p$ is fixed inside the limit. In the protected-sum theorem
the number of pieces is also fixed before $T$ is chosen. The assertion
does not supply one threshold for every diagram or allow the diagram
or filling integers to vary with $N$.

The dependency map in \sourcefile{repro/manuscript_logic.json} records
the interfaces used by Appendices N--R. Each full-volume conclusion
must name its exact invariant, nonzero contribution, full remainder
bound, specified completed metric, absolute action-volume identity,
and final rate-transfer argument. The general unchanged-sphere route
retains its unresolved remainder requirement. It is separate from the
completed weak-balance, protected-sum and ordinary-boundary routes.

The following command checks that the recorded premises have the right
roles, retain their assumptions and level scope, refer to existing
statements, and contain no circular dependency:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 repro/check_manuscript_logic.py \
  --output repro/results/manuscript-logic
\end{verbatim}
It also deliberately introduces ten defects and checks that each is
rejected. These include removing the remainder premise, replacing it
by the central contribution, importing an open estimate, and using an
odd-level estimate in an all-level conclusion. The map is supplied by
the author; its consistency is not a proof of the mathematics at its
vertices. The recorded source hashes identify the texts used by the
check. Internal analytic and geometric lemmas still require their
written proofs.

For ordinary-boundary insertion, the map additionally records the
conditional disk minimum and the small-boundary certificate hypothesis.
The scalar comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:triangular-boundary-insertion}
has a Lean proof. It distinguishes a wholly central inserted block from
a block with a central attaching label but noncentral terms elsewhere.
The duplicated target is twice the boundary logarithm in the doubled
Lean normalization; it is not twice the removed triangle potential.
The conditional packing and the existence of the shared witnesses remain
separate written inputs to those declarations.

\section{Finite steps checked in Lean}

The directory \sourcefile{formal/} contains a small formalization using
Lean's standard library. It checks the exact even- and odd-level degree
cutoffs, polynomial divided differences without division at a spectral
root, the double-root Hermite identity after clearing denominators, and
the finite tensor-product subtraction used when node values vanish.
The formalization also constructs synthetic division on coefficient
lists and the remainder modulo a split monic polynomial
$p(X)=\prod_i(X-r_i)$. It proves the reconstruction identity
coefficient by coefficient, the degree bound on the remainder, and
the formula for its top coefficient as an iterated divided difference.
Repeated nodes are allowed; no inverse of $r_i-r_j$ is introduced.
The constructed remainder and functional are also proved linear over
the coefficient ring. Coefficient-equivalent input lists, including
arbitrary trailing zeros, give the same output. Adding a multiple of
the constructed monic polynomial leaves the remainder and functional
unchanged. These statements establish independence of the polynomial
representative; they do not yet identify the functional with the
original crossing matrix.
The product module constructs multiplication of the coefficient lists
and proves that reducing either factor leaves the final remainder and
functional unchanged. It derives associative reduced multiplication,
reduction after every step of a finite product, and the product rule
for the value and first derivative at a repeated root.
Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-remainder-products} states these facts
in polynomial notation. In particular the multiplication is proved
compatible with reduction; that compatibility is not a formal hypothesis.
The next module proves uniqueness of the remainder among all
representatives of the required degree, allowing arbitrary trailing
zeros in the coefficient lists. It then proves invariance under every
permutation of the root list, including repeated roots, and gives a
characterization of the coefficient functional by additivity,
annihilation of monic multiples, and normalization in degree below the
modulus. Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-polynomial-remainder} gives the
corresponding mathematical statement and proof. To apply that
characterization to a proposed crossing functional, its hypotheses
must still be established for the actual operator.
The resolvent module now proves one such link directly:
if \(p(w)=0\) and \(V_w(X)=(p(X)-p(w))/(X-w)\), then the
top-coefficient functional satisfies \(\ell_p(fV_w)=f(w)\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:resolvent-reproduction} gives the proof and
applies it in the commutative algebra generated by one local Casimir.
Its actual annihilating relation and crossing polynomial remain written
source inputs. No commutativity between different boxes is used.
The face-grid module proves that the ordinary residue conditions and
the total-degree bound force exactly the prescribed monomial, with
both protected exponents zero. Proposition~\ref{mcg:face-grid-selection}
adds the elementary character average and explains the shifted
even-level grids. The physical grid and its turn normalization are
still supplied by the written diagram calculation.
The protected-sum module constructs the three central/left/right rate
functions and their map to the two input sectors. It proves the local
comparison, cancels the duplicated joining-face target, and preserves
the smaller input gap. Appendix~\ref{app:protected-triangular-sums}
proves that the diagrams and their shared witnesses supply these
formal hypotheses. The triangulations, analytic estimates, and volume
identity themselves are not formalized.
The formal regrading module checks the coefficient substitution
$u=hs$ used in Appendix~\ref{app:directed-all-orders}. It recovers
every coefficient from its new pair of exponents and proves
injectivity. It also proves that agreement through order $h^L$
recovers exactly the coefficients of total degree at most $L$.
The all-orders expansion, its uniform analytic remainder, and the
identification of the longitude remain separate inputs.
It also checks the scalar inequalities that compare the entropy gain
with its quadratic cost and preserve a remainder gap after a small
decrease in the action. From the repository root, run
\begin{verbatim}
python3 repro/check_proof_logic.py --output repro/results/proof-logic
\end{verbatim}
The project pins its Lean version. The runner compiles the declarations
and then separately inspects every theorem's logical assumptions.
The accepted foundational axioms are propositional extensionality,
classical choice and quotient soundness; an admitted proof step is
rejected.

These are formal proofs of the stated finite implications. Their
applications still require the hypotheses proved in the manuscript.
For example, the scalar entropy comparison does not prove the Hessian
formula, and the divided-difference identity does not identify the
original knot operator. The monic-remainder calculation does not prove
the Chebyshev factorization or its root multiplicities; these data must
still be supplied for the actual spectral polynomial. The exact diagram adapter, uniform analytic
estimates, geometric realization, primitive completion and
Proposition~\ref{prop:volume-rate-transfer} remain written proofs.
The file \sourcefile{repro/MANUSCRIPT_AUDIT.md} records the current
proof checks and the boundaries of this initial formalization.

\section{Three different kinds of evidence}
The manuscript contains analytic proofs, exact finite certificates, and optional finite checks. They have different logical roles. The matrix identities, support and strictness arguments, geometric incidence, complex estimates and filling analysis are proved in the text. The rational certificates are finite parts of the proof: the continuum inequalities reduce to their exact feasibility, coverage and supporting-plane checks. Finite matrix computations and numerical asymptotic comparisons provide independent checks of formulas and normalizations, but do not establish a theorem by extrapolation.

The chapters and appendices give the proofs with their named dependencies on earlier finite-source and geometric lemmas. References to those technical companions remain proof inputs; the PDF does not reproduce every calculation in the research library. The repository also supplies the exact certificate data and verification programs. Those programs check the finite inequalities described below; they do not formally verify the analytic or geometric arguments.

\section{The scope of the conclusions}
Every volume theorem fixes the diagram, its endpoint order, disk
framings, parity pattern and integer powers before $N$ tends to
infinity. The limit includes both parity sequences and composite
levels. Only auxiliary crossing circles are filled; the main knot
cusp remains complete. The component test must give one knot without
an internally closed extra main component.

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies to the completed expressions of
Definition~\ref{def:grammar}. Each signed filling integer must exceed
a construction-dependent threshold in absolute value; there is no
restriction on ratios between the large integers. The circle-tree
results use their own sufficient conditions: the summed inequality of
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-quantitative}, the local inequality of
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-circle-volume}, or the directed certificate of
Chapter~\ref{ch:directed-circle-messages}. The mixed condition
$d_v\ge2^{28}$ or $|n_v|\ge2^{27}$ at each vertex is a consequence
of the last certificate. These are separate proofs, not relaxations
of the completed-box hypothesis inside the first proof.

The planar theorem of Chapter~\ref{ch:balanced-terminal-fans} applies
a specified finite decoration to a simple matched sphere before
performing its independently long signed fillings. The decoration
preserves the chosen endpoint pairing and can change the knot. It
does not give the full volume theorem for every unchanged sphere.
For unchanged diagrams, Appendix~\ref{app:morse-geometric-contribution}
proves a narrower statement: the specified geometric contribution
of the original exact invariant has a nonzero asymptotic at the
actual filled volume rate. This contribution is not a separate
topological invariant, and the theorem does not bound the remaining
signed contribution.

The meridian-profile and preferred-longitude results retain their
stated source classes. Chapter~\ref{ch:directed-all-orders} proves the
full expansion required for the selected-factor conclusion for
every directed certificate. That conclusion is an implication for
an existing nonzero fixed all-level annihilator; it neither proves
recurrence existence nor identifies the entire A-polynomial or
recurrence ideal. Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-filling-bound} proves a
finite functional estimate. Its application to an additional volume
class requires a separate exponential source estimate.

None of these sufficient conditions is a classification of all
arborescent knots or all short fillings. Broader net-twist and closure
statements in the linked companions keep their own reduction and
long-surviving-weight hypotheses. The geometric arguments retain the
original manifold: scalar equalities in $\cH_N$ are not used as tangle
isotopies, and the continued main boundary word becomes the preferred
longitude of the smooth filled knot at the endpoint of the specified
geometric path.

\section{Uniform bounds for inserting twists}
Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-filling-bound} proves a level-independent
bound for each original filling functional, measured against the
supremum of the shared spectral polynomial. Its finite identities
can be checked with one command:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 repro/run_research_checks.py filling-supremum
\end{verbatim}
This group requires SymPy and NumPy. It checks the derivative
interpolation and Chebyshev residues exactly, then compares the
functional with independently constructed signed crossing matrices
over finite fields. The shared exponential estimate remains a
separate hypothesis; these checks add no new volume class.
